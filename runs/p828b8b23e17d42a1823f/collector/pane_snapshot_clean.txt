## Proof

**Graph model.** Let $G$ be a graph on $n$ vertices (specialists), edges = partn
erships. The conditions state:
- **(i)** Adjacent vertices have **0** common neighbors ($\lambda = 0$).
- **(ii)** Non-adjacent vertices have exactly **2** common neighbors ($\mu = 2$)
.

### Step 1: The graph must be regular

Let $A$ be the adjacency matrix, $D = \operatorname{diag}(d_1, \dots, d_n)$ the
degree matrix, $J$ the all-ones matrix, $\mathbf{1}$ the all-ones vector, and $\
mathbf{d} = (d_1, \dots, d_n)^T$.

The entry $(A^2)_{ij}$ counts common neighbors of $i, j$. By the two conditions:

$$A^2 = D + 0 \cdot A + 2(J - I - A) = D - 2I - 2A + 2J$$

$$\boxed{A^2 + 2A = D - 2I + 2J} \tag{1}$$

**Multiplying (1) by $\mathbf{1}$:** Since $A\mathbf{1} = \mathbf{d}$ and $J\mat
hbf{1} = n\mathbf{1}$:

$$A\mathbf{d} + 2\mathbf{d} = \mathbf{d} - 2\mathbf{1} + 2n\mathbf{1} \implies \
boxed{A\mathbf{d} = (2n-2)\mathbf{1} - \mathbf{d}} \tag{2}$$

**Computing $A^2\mathbf{d}$ two ways.**

*Way 1:* Apply $A$ to (2): $A^2\mathbf{d} = (2n-2)A\mathbf{1} - A\mathbf{d} = (2
n-2)\mathbf{d} - ((2n-2)\mathbf{1} - \mathbf{d}) = (2n-1)\mathbf{d} - (2n-2)\mat
hbf{1}$.

*Way 2:* From (1): $A^2\mathbf{d} = (D - 2I + 2J - 2A)\mathbf{d} = D\mathbf{d} -
 2\mathbf{d} + 2(\sum d_i)\mathbf{1} - 2A\mathbf{d}$.

Using (2) ($A\mathbf{d} = (2n-2)\mathbf{1} - \mathbf{d}$) and $\sum d_i = 2|E|$:

$$A^2\mathbf{d} = D\mathbf{d} - 2\mathbf{d} + 4|E|\mathbf{1} - 2(2n-2)\mathbf{1}
 + 2\mathbf{d} = D\mathbf{d} + (4|E| - 4n + 4)\mathbf{1}$$

**Equating** the two expressions:

$$(2n-1)\mathbf{d} - (2n-2)\mathbf{1} = D\mathbf{d} + (4|E| - 4n + 4)\mathbf{1}$
$

Component-wise (noting $(D\mathbf{d})_i = d_i^2$):

$$d_i^2 - (2n-1)d_i + \big(2n - 2 + 4|E| - 4n + 4\big) = 0$$

$$\boxed{d_i^2 - (2n-1)\,d_i + \big(4|E| - 2n + 2\big) = 0} \tag{3}$$

**Every degree is a root of the same quadratic**, so degrees take **at most two
values** $a, b$ with:

$$a + b = 2n - 1 \tag{4}$$

**Contradiction if biregular:** Since $a, b \leq n-1$ (maximum possible degree),
 we get $a + b \leq 2(n-1) = 2n - 2 < 2n - 1$. This contradicts (4).

Therefore **$G$ is $k$-regular** for some $k$, i.e., $a = b = k$ and $2k = 2n -
1$ is impossible unless the quadratic has a double root. With all degrees equal
to $k$, equation (2) gives $k^2 \mathbf{1} = (2n - 2 - k)\mathbf{1}$, so:

$$\boxed{n = \frac{k^2 + k + 2}{2}} \tag{5}$$

### Step 2: Eigenvalue integrality forces $k - 1$ to be a perfect square

$G$ is a strongly regular graph $\operatorname{srg}(n, k, 0, 2)$. Its eigenvalue
s are:

$$\theta = k, \qquad r, s = \frac{(\lambda - \mu) \pm \sqrt{(\lambda-\mu)^2 + 4(
k-\mu)}}{2} = \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{4 + 4(k-2)}}{2} = -1 \pm \sqrt{k-1}$$

Let $m = \sqrt{k-1}$. The multiplicities of $r = -1 + m$ and $s = -1 - m$ are:

$$f = \frac{n-1}{2} - \frac{2k + (n-1)(\lambda - \mu)}{2\sqrt{(\lambda-\mu)^2 +
4(k-\mu)}}$$

Computing the numerator: $2k + (n-1)(\lambda - \mu) = 2k - 2(n-1)$. Using (5), $
n - 1 = \frac{k(k+1)}{2}$, so:

$$2k - 2 \cdot \frac{k(k+1)}{2} = 2k - k(k+1) = k(1 - k) = -k(k-1)$$

The denominator is $2 \cdot 2\sqrt{k-1} = 4\sqrt{k-1}$. Thus:

$$f = \frac{n-1}{2} + \frac{k(k-1)}{4\sqrt{k-1}} = \frac{n-1}{2} + \frac{k\sqrt{
k-1}}{4}$$

For $f$ to be an integer, $k\sqrt{k-1}$ must be rational, hence $\sqrt{k-1}$ mus
t be rational, hence **$k - 1$ must be a perfect square**. (The alternative—nume
rator zero, giving $f = g = (n-1)/2$—requires $k = n-1$, i.e., $G$ complete, con
tradicting $\lambda = 0$ for $n > 2$.)

### Step 3: Find the smallest valid $n > 4$

Setting $k - 1 = m^2$ (so $k = m^2 + 1$) and using (5):

$$n = \frac{(m^2+1)^2 + (m^2+1) + 2}{2} = \frac{m^4 + 3m^2 + 4}{2}$$

| $m$ | $k$ | $n$ | $n > 4$? | Eigenvalues | Multiplicities | Exists? |
|-----|-----|-----|-----------|-------------|----------------|---------|
| 0 | 1 | 2 | No | — | — | — |
| 1 | 2 | 4 | No | $0, -2$ | $1, 3$ | Yes ($C_4$) but $n \not> 4$ |
| 2 | 5 | **16** | **Yes** | $1, -3$ | $10, 5$ | **Yes** (Clebsch graph) |

**Verification of multiplicities for $k=5$, $n=16$:** $f = \frac{15}{2} + \frac{
5 \cdot 2}{4} = 7.5 + 2.5 = 10$, $g = 15 - 10 = 5$. Check: $1 + 10 + 5 = 16$ ✓,
and $5 + 10(1) + 5(-3) = 0$ ✓ (trace).

**No intermediate $k$ works:** $k = 3$ ($n = 7$) and $k = 4$ ($n = 11$) give irr
ational eigenvalues $-1 \pm \sqrt{2}$ and $-1 \pm \sqrt{3}$ with non-integer mul
tiplicities, so no such strongly regular graphs exist.

### Step 4: The Clebsch graph realizes $\operatorname{srg}(16, 5, 0, 2)$

**Construction.** Vertices = all even-cardinality subsets of $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$: $\
binom{5}{0} + \binom{5}{2} + \binom{5}{4} = 1 + 10 + 5 = 16$ vertices. Two verti
ces $A, B$ are adjacent iff $|A \triangle B| = 4$ (symmetric difference has size
 4).

**Regularity ($k = 5$):**
- $\emptyset$: adjacent to all 4-element subsets → degree 5.
- A 2-subset $\{i,j\}$: adjacent to 2-subsets disjoint from it ($\binom{3}{2} =
3$) plus 4-subsets sharing exactly 1 element ($2$) → degree 5.
- A 4-subset: adjacent to $\emptyset$ ($1$) plus 2-subsets sharing exactly 1 ele
ment ($2$) → degree $1 + 2 + 2 = 5$. ✓

**$\lambda = 0$ (adjacent → 0 common neighbors):** Take $\emptyset$ and $\{1,2,3
,4\}$ (adjacent since $|\triangle| = 4$). Neighbors of $\emptyset$ = all 4-subse
ts. Neighbors of $\{1,2,3,4\}$ = $\emptyset$ and 2-subsets $\{i,5\}$ for $i \in
\{1,2,3,4\}$. Intersection: $\emptyset$ is not a neighbor of itself; no 4-subset
 is a neighbor of $\{1,2,3,4\}$ (would need $|B \cap \{1,2,3,4\}| = 2$ with $B$
a 4-subset, but only 1 element lies outside). Common neighbors = $\emptyset$. ✓

**$\mu = 2$ (non-adjacent → 2 common neighbors):** Take $\{1,2\}$ and $\{1,3\}$
(non-adjacent since $|\triangle| = 2 \neq 4$). Neighbors of $\{1,2\}$: $\{3,4\},
 \{3,5\}, \{4,5\}, \{1,3,4,5\}, \{2,3,4,5\}$. Neighbors of $\{1,3\}$: $\{2,4\},
\{2,5\}, \{4,5\}, \{1,2,4,5\}, \{2,3,4,5\}$. Common: $\{4,5\}$ and $\{2,3,4,5\}$
 → exactly 2. ✓

### Conclusion

The graph must be regular (biregularity is impossible since $a + b = 2n - 1 > 2(
n-1)$). The parameters force $k - 1$ to be a perfect square. The smallest $k$ yi
elding $n > 4$ is $k = 5$, giving $n = 16$, realized by the Clebsch graph.

$$\boxed{n = 16}$$
