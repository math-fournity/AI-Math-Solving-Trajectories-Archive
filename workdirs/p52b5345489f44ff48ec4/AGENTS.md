# Problem

In a vast logistics hub, a heavy-lifting robot moves across a floor tiled with a seamless grid of regular hexagons. The robot is programmed to execute "vector-thrusts." A vector-thrust of magnitude $n$ is defined as choosing any one of the six directions parallel to the sides of the hexagons and moving precisely $n$ hexagonal cells in that straight line.

The robot is tasked with a specific calibration sequence consisting of $N$ consecutive thrusts. It begins with a thrust of magnitude $N$, followed immediately by a thrust of magnitude $N-1$, then $N-2$, continuing this pattern until it completes a final thrust of magnitude $1$. Each thrust can be oriented in any of the six directions, independent of the previous choices.

At the end of the final thrust of magnitude 1, the robot must be located exactly at the center of the hexagonal cell where it first began the sequence.

Determine the sum of the smallest 10 positive integers $N$ for which such a closed-loop sequence is mathematically possible.
