## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $A$ commutes with $\sqrt{AA^T}$.

**Step 1: Simplify $AA^T$ using skew-symmetry.**

Since $A^T = -A$,
$$AA^T = A(-A) = -A^2.$$

**Step 2: Show $A$ commutes with $AA^T$.**

Since $AA^T = -A^2$ is a polynomial in $A$, clearly $A$ commutes with it:
$$A(AA^T) = A(-A^2) = -A^3 = (-A^2)A = (AA^T)A.$$

**Step 3: Show $\sqrt{AA^T}$ is a polynomial in $AA^T$.**

$AA^T$ is positive definite symmetric, so it is diagonalizable with positive eig
envalues. Let its *distinct* eigenvalues be $\mu_1, \dots, \mu_k$ (all $> 0$). B
y polynomial interpolation (Lagrange), there exists a polynomial $p$ such that
$$p(\mu_i) = \sqrt{\mu_i}, \quad i = 1, \dots, k.$$

By the spectral mapping theorem for diagonalizable matrices, applying $p$ to $AA
^T = O\Lambda O^T$ gives
$$p(AA^T) = O\, p(\Lambda)\, O^T = O\, \text{diag}(p(\lambda_1),\dots,p(\lambda_
n))\, O^T = O\sqrt{\Lambda}\,O^T = \sqrt{AA^T}.$$

Hence $\sqrt{AA^T} = p(AA^T)$ for some polynomial $p$.

**Step 4: Conclude commutativity.**

Since $A$ commutes with $AA^T$ (Step 2), $A$ commutes with every power $(AA^T)^m
$, and therefore with any polynomial in $AA^T$:
$$A\,\sqrt{AA^T} = A\,p(AA^T) = p(AA^T)\,A = \sqrt{AA^T}\,A.$$

**Conclusion:** $A\sqrt{AA^T} = \sqrt{AA^T}\,A$. The commutativity holds because
 $AA^T = -A^2$ is a polynomial in $A$, so $A$ commutes with $AA^T$, and $\sqrt{A
A^T}$ is itself a polynomial in $AA^T$ (by spectral interpolation on the positiv
e eigenvalues), hence $A$ commutes with $\sqrt{AA^T}$ as well.
