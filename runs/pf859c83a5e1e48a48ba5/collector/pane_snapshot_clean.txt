## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. If $V \subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$ is an affine variety whose coordin
ate ring $A(V)$ is a UFD, then every closed subvariety $W \subseteq V$ of codime
nsion $1$ is defined by a single equation on $V$.

---

### Setup and correspondence

Let $k$ be the ground field and let $A(V) = k[x_1,\dots,x_n]/I(V)$ be the coordi
nate ring of $V$. Since $A(V)$ is a UFD, it is in particular an integral domain,
 so $V$ is irreducible.

Closed subvarieties of $V$ correspond bijectively to prime ideals of $A(V)$: a c
losed subvariety $W \subseteq V$ corresponds to the prime ideal
$$\mathfrak{p} = I(W)/I(V) \subseteq A(V).$$
Under this correspondence, the codimension of $W$ in $V$ equals the height of $\
mathfrak{p}$:
$$\operatorname{codim}(W, V) = \operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}).$$
Thus $\operatorname{codim}(W,V)=1$ translates to: $\mathfrak{p}$ is a prime idea
l of $A(V)$ of height $1$.

Saying "$W$ is defined by a single equation on $V$" means $\mathfrak{p}$ is a pr
incipal ideal, i.e., $\mathfrak{p} = (f)$ for some $f \in A(V)$, so that $W = V(
f) \cap V$.

So the claim reduces to the following algebraic theorem.

---

### Key theorem

**Theorem.** *In a UFD $R$, every prime ideal of height $1$ is principal.*

**Proof of the theorem.** Let $R$ be a UFD and let $\mathfrak{p} \subseteq R$ be
 a prime ideal with $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) = 1$.

If $R$ is a field, there is no prime ideal of height $1$, so assume $R$ is not a
 field. Then $\mathfrak{p} \neq (0)$, since $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) = 1
 > 0 = \operatorname{ht}((0))$. Pick any nonzero element $a \in \mathfrak{p}$.

Since $R$ is a UFD, $a$ admits a factorization into irreducibles:
$$a = u \cdot \pi_1 \cdot \pi_2 \cdots \pi_r,$$
where $u$ is a unit and each $\pi_i$ is irreducible. In a UFD, every irreducible
 element is prime, so each $(\pi_i)$ is a prime ideal.

Since $\mathfrak{p}$ is prime and $a = u\,\pi_1\cdots\pi_r \in \mathfrak{p}$, at
 least one factor $\pi_j \in \mathfrak{p}$. Hence
$$(\pi_j) \subseteq \mathfrak{p}.$$

Now $(\pi_j)$ is a nonzero prime ideal (nonzero because $\pi_j \neq 0$ and $R$ i
s a domain; prime because $\pi_j$ is prime). Therefore $\operatorname{ht}(\pi_j)
 \geq 1$. But $(\pi_j) \subseteq \mathfrak{p}$ gives $\operatorname{ht}(\pi_j) \
leq \operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) = 1$. So $\operatorname{ht}(\pi_j) = 1 = \op
eratorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})$.

Since $(\pi_j) \subseteq \mathfrak{p}$ and both are prime ideals of the same hei
ght $1$, we conclude $(\pi_j) = \mathfrak{p}$. Thus $\mathfrak{p}$ is principal,
 generated by the single irreducible element $\pi_j$. $\blacksquare$

---

### Applying the theorem

Applying the theorem with $R = A(V)$ (a UFD by hypothesis) and $\mathfrak{p} = I
(W)/I(V)$ (a height-$1$ prime because $\operatorname{codim}(W,V)=1$), we obtain
$$\mathfrak{p} = (f)$$
for some $f \in A(V)$. Lifting $f$ to a polynomial $\tilde{f} \in k[x_1,\dots,x_
n]$, the subvariety $W$ is exactly the zero locus of $\tilde{f}$ restricted to $
V$:
$$W = V(\tilde{f}) \cap V = \{x \in V : \tilde{f}(x) = 0\}.$$

Therefore $W$ is defined by the single equation $\tilde{f} = 0$ on $V$.

---

### Conclusion

The statement is **true**: for an affine variety $V \subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$ whos
e coordinate ring $A(V)$ is a UFD, every closed subvariety of codimension $1$ is
 the zero set of a single equation on $V$. This is a direct consequence of the f
act that in a UFD, every height-$1$ prime ideal is principal — a property that f
ails in general Noetherian domains (where one only has Krull's principal ideal t
heorem giving the reverse inequality $\operatorname{ht}(f) \leq 1$), and the UFD
 hypothesis is precisely what upgrades the height-$1$ prime to being principal.
