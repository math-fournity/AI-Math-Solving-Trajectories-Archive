# 4-Torsion Points of $E: y^2 = x^3 - 5$ over $\mathbb{Q}$

## Step 1: Determine the rational 2-torsion

A point $P \in E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$ is a 2-torsion point ($2P = \mathcal{O}
$) if and only if $P = -P$. For a Weierstrass curve $y^2 = x^3 + ax + b$, the in
verse of $(x, y)$ is $(x, -y)$, so $P = -P$ forces $y = 0$ (or $P = \mathcal{O}$
).

Setting $y = 0$ in $y^2 = x^3 - 5$ gives $x^3 = 5$. The rational solutions of $x
^3 = 5$ are none, since $5$ is not a perfect cube in $\mathbb{Q}$. Therefore the
 only rational 2-torsion point is the point at infinity:

$$E(\mathbb{Q})[2] = \{\mathcal{O}\}.$$

## Step 2: Relate 4-torsion to 2-torsion

The 4-torsion subgroup is
$$E(\mathbb{Q})[4] = \{P \in E(\mathbb{Q}) : 4P = \mathcal{O}\}.$$

Every element of $E(\mathbb{Q})[4]$ is either a 2-torsion point (order dividing
2) or a point of exact order 4.

Suppose $P \in E(\mathbb{Q})$ has exact order 4. Then $2P \neq \mathcal{O}$ but
$4P = 2(2P) = \mathcal{O}$, so $2P$ is a **non-trivial** 2-torsion point. Since
$P$ is rational, $2P$ is also rational (the group law is defined over $\mathbb{Q
}$). Hence $2P \in E(\mathbb{Q})[2] \setminus \{\mathcal{O}\}$.

But from Step 1, $E(\mathbb{Q})[2] = \{\mathcal{O}\}$, so $E(\mathbb{Q})[2] \set
minus \{\mathcal{O}\} = \emptyset$. This is a contradiction.

Therefore **no point of exact order 4 exists in $E(\mathbb{Q})$**.

## Step 3: Conclusion

Since every element of $E(\mathbb{Q})[4]$ has order dividing 4, and the only pos
sible orders are 1, 2, or 4, but:
- order 4 is impossible (Step 2),
- order 2 is impossible (Step 1, since $E(\mathbb{Q})[2] = \{\mathcal{O}\}$),
- order 1 gives only $\mathcal{O}$,

we conclude:

$$\boxed{E(\mathbb{Q})[4] = \{\mathcal{O}\}.}$$

The only 4-torsion point of $E: y^2 = x^3 - 5$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is the point at
 infinity $\mathcal{O}$.
