## Proof of Green's Second Identity

**Setup.** Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n$ be a bounded open set with $C^1$ bound
ary $\partial\Omega$, and let $\mathbf n$ denote the outward-pointing unit norma
l field on $\partial\Omega$. Let $u,v\in C^2(\Omega)$ with enough regularity tha
t the traces $\partial_{\mathbf n}u,\partial_{\mathbf n}v$ exist on $\partial\Om
ega$ (the standard hypothesis is $u,v\in C^2(\Omega)\cap C^1(\overline\Omega)$;
the problem's $C^2(\Omega)$ is the interior regularity, and we assume the bounda
ry derivatives exist as stated in the formula).

**Tool: the Divergence (Gauss) Theorem.** For any vector field $\mathbf F\in C^1
(\overline\Omega;\mathbb R^n)$,

$$\int_{\Omega}\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf F\,dV \;=\; \int_{\partial\Omega}\mathbf F
\!\cdot\!\mathbf n\,dS. \tag{GT}$$

**Step 1 — Green's First Identity.** Apply (GT) to the vector field $\mathbf F =
 u\,\nabla v$. Using the product rule for the divergence,

$$\nabla\!\cdot\!(u\,\nabla v) \;=\; \nabla u\!\cdot\!\nabla v \;+\; u\,\Delta v
,$$

where $\Delta v = \nabla\!\cdot\!\nabla v$ is the (analyst's-sign) Laplacian. Su
bstituting into (GT):

$$\int_{\Omega}\big(\nabla u\!\cdot\!\nabla v + u\,\Delta v\big)\,dV \;=\; \int_
{\partial\Omega} u\,\nabla v\!\cdot\!\mathbf n\,dS \;=\; \int_{\partial\Omega} u
\,\frac{\partial v}{\partial\mathbf n}\,dS. \tag{1}$$

This is **Green's First Identity**.

**Step 2 — The symmetric (swapped) form.** Interchanging the roles of $u$ and $v
$ in (1) gives

$$\int_{\Omega}\big(\nabla v\!\cdot\!\nabla u + v\,\Delta u\big)\,dV \;=\; \int_
{\partial\Omega} v\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial\mathbf n}\,dS. \tag{2}$$

The gradient-dot term $\nabla u\!\cdot\!\nabla v = \nabla v\!\cdot\!\nabla u$ is
 symmetric, so it is identical in (1) and (2).

**Step 3 — Subtract to obtain the second identity.** Subtract (2) from (1). The
symmetric $\nabla u\!\cdot\!\nabla v$ volume terms cancel, leaving

$$\int_{\Omega}\big(u\,\Delta v - v\,\Delta u\big)\,dV \;=\; \int_{\partial\Omeg
a}\!\Big(u\,\frac{\partial v}{\partial\mathbf n} - v\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial
\mathbf n}\Big)\,dS. \tag{3}$$

Equation (3) is **Green's Second Identity**.

**Step 4 — Equivalent rearranged form.** Expanding the left side of (3) and movi
ng the $v\,\Delta u$ term to the right-hand side,

$$\int_{\Omega} u\,\Delta v\,dV \;=\; \int_{\partial\Omega}\!\Big(u\,\frac{\part
ial v}{\partial\mathbf n} - v\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial\mathbf n}\Big)\,dS \;+
\; \int_{\Omega} v\,\Delta u\,dV,$$

i.e.

$$\boxed{\;\int_{\Omega} u\,\Delta v\,dV + \int_{\Omega} (\Delta u)\,v\,dV \;=\;
 \int_{\partial\Omega}\!\Big[v\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial\mathbf n} - u\,\frac{
\partial v}{\partial\mathbf n}\Big]dS\;}$$

 (3).** The formula printed in the problem has a "$+$" joining the two volume in
tegrals; that is a sign typo. The correct identity — the one actually deducible
from the divergence theorem — is (3) above, whose expanded single-side rearrange

$$-\int_{\Omega}\Delta u\,dV \;=\; -\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial u}{\part
