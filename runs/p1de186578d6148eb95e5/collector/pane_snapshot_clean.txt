## Proof

Let $X$ be a Hilbert space and $\varphi \in X'$ a non-zero continuous linear fun
ctional. Since $X$ is a Hilbert space, by the **Riesz representation theorem** t
here exists a unique vector $u \in X$, $u \neq 0$, such that
$$\varphi(x) = \langle x, u \rangle \quad \text{for all } x \in X.$$

Hence
$$D = \{x \in X : \langle x, u\rangle = 2\}.$$
Since $u \neq 0$, $D$ is non-empty (e.g. $x_0 = \tfrac{2}{\|u\|^2}u \in D$). Mor
eover $D$ is an affine hyperplane: it does **not** contain the origin (since $\l
angle 0,u\rangle = 0 \neq 2$).

Fix any $x_0 \in D$. Then every $x \in D$ can be written uniquely as $x = x_0 +
v$ with $v \in \ker\varphi = \{w : \langle w, u\rangle = 0\} = u^\perp$. Thus
$$D = x_0 + u^\perp.$$

The orthogonal complement of $D$ is
$$D^\perp = \{y \in X : \langle y, x\rangle = 0 \text{ for all } x \in D\}.$$

Take $y \in D^\perp$. For every $v \in u^\perp$ we have $x_0 + v \in D$, so
$$0 = \langle y, x_0 + v\rangle = \langle y, x_0\rangle + \langle y, v\rangle.$$
Since this holds for all $v \in u^\perp$, in particular it holds for $v$ and $-v
$, forcing
$$\langle y, v\rangle = 0 \quad \text{for all } v \in u^\perp.$$
Therefore $y \in (u^\perp)^\perp$. As $u^\perp$ is a closed subspace of a Hilber
t space, $(u^\perp)^\perp = \overline{\operatorname{span}\{u\}} = \operatorname{
span}\{u\}$. So $y = \lambda u$ for some scalar $\lambda$.

Now using $\langle y, x\rangle = 0$ for any $x \in D$ (all such $x$ satisfy $\la
ngle x, u\rangle = 2$):
$$0 = \langle \lambda u, x\rangle = \lambda \langle u, x\rangle = \lambda \cdot
2 = 2\lambda.$$
Hence $\lambda = 0$, and consequently $y = 0$.

Conversely, $0 \in D^\perp$ trivially. Therefore
$$D^\perp = \{0\}.$$

**Remark.** Geometrically, $D$ is an affine hyperplane not passing through the o
rigin. It contains a full translate of the closed subspace $u^\perp$ (which forc
es any vector in $D^\perp$ to lie in $\operatorname{span}\{u\}$), but it also co
ntains vectors with a fixed nonzero $\langle\cdot,u\rangle$-component equal to $
2$, which forces that remaining scalar to vanish. The only vector orthogonal to
the entire affine hyperplane is the zero vector.

### PROOF COMPLETE
