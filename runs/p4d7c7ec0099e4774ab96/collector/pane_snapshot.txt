## Solution

We evaluate
$$I = \int_\Gamma \frac{3z-2}{z^2-z}\,dz,$$
where $\Gamma$ is a simple, closed, positively oriented contour enclosing both $
z=0$ and $z=1$.

**Step 1: Partial fraction decomposition.**

The denominator factors as
$$z^2 - z = z(z-1).$$
We write
$$\frac{3z-2}{z(z-1)} = \frac{A}{z} + \frac{B}{z-1}.$$
Multiplying through by $z(z-1)$:
$$3z - 2 = A(z-1) + Bz.$$

- Setting $z = 0$: $-2 = -A$, so $A = 2$.
- Setting $z = 1$: $1 = B$, so $B = 1$.

Hence
$$\frac{3z-2}{z^2-z} = \frac{2}{z} + \frac{1}{z-1}.$$

**Step 2: Apply Cauchy's integral formula.**

Since $\Gamma$ is positively oriented and encloses both $0$ and $1$, and the int
egrand's singularities are exactly at $0$ and $1$ (both simple poles lying insid
e $\Gamma$), by Cauchy's integral formula:

$$\int_\Gamma \frac{1}{z}\,dz = 2\pi i, \qquad \int_\Gamma \frac{1}{z-1}\,dz = 2
\pi i.$$

**Step 3: Combine.**

$$I = \int_\Gamma \frac{2}{z}\,dz + \int_\Gamma \frac{1}{z-1}\,dz = 2(2\pi i) +
(2\pi i) = 4\pi i + 2\pi i = 6\pi i.$$

**Verification via residues.** The residues of $\frac{3z-2}{z(z-1)}$ are:
- At $z=0$: $\lim_{z\to 0} z\cdot\frac{3z-2}{z(z-1)} = \frac{-2}{-1} = 2$.
- At $z=1$: $\lim_{z\to 1} (z-1)\cdot\frac{3z-2}{z(z-1)} = \frac{1}{1} = 1$.

Sum of residues $= 2 + 1 = 3$. By the residue theorem,
$$I = 2\pi i \cdot 3 = 6\pi i.$$

Both methods agree.

$$\boxed{\int_\Gamma \frac{3z-2}{z^2-z}\,dz = 6\pi i}$$
