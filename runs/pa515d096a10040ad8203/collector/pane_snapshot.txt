## Solution

Let $B_t$ be standard Brownian motion with $B_0=0$, and define
$$T = \inf\{t \ge 1 : B_t = 0\}.$$

We compute the law of $T$.

**Step 1: Reduction via the Markov property.**

Write $T = 1 + \tau$ where
$$\tau = \inf\{s \ge 0 : B_{1+s} = 0\}.$$
Conditioning on $B_1 = x$ (with $x \neq 0$ a.s. since $B_1 \sim N(0,1)$), the pr
ocess $\{B_{1+s}-x\}_{s\ge 0}$ is a standard Brownian motion independent of $\ma
thcal{F}_1$. Thus $\tau$ is the first hitting time of $-x$ by a standard Brownia
n motion starting at $0$, equivalently the first hitting time of $0$ by Brownian
 motion starting at $x$. By symmetry in $|x|$, the conditional density of $\tau$
 given $B_1 = x$ is the classical Lévy hitting-time density:
$$f_\tau(s \mid x) = \frac{|x|}{\sqrt{2\pi s^3}}\,e^{-x^2/(2s)}, \qquad s > 0.$$

**Step 2: Mix over $B_1 \sim N(0,1)$.**

The (unconditional) density of $T$ at $t = 1+u$ with $u > 0$ is
$$f_T(1+u) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_\tau(u \mid x)\,\varphi(x)\,dx
= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{|x|}{\sqrt{2\pi u^3}}\,e^{-x^2/(2u)}\,\frac{1}{\
sqrt{2\pi}}\,e^{-x^2/2}\,dx.$$

Combining the exponents: $\frac{x^2}{2u}+\frac{x^2}{2} = \frac{1+u}{2u}\,x^2$. S
etting $a = \frac{1+u}{2u}$,
$$f_T(1+u) = \frac{1}{2\pi\,u^{3/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x|\,e^{-a x^2}\,dx.$
$

The integral evaluates by symmetry:
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x|\,e^{-a x^2}\,dx = 2\int_0^\infty x\,e^{-a x^2}\,dx
= 2\cdot\frac{1}{2a} = \frac{1}{a} = \frac{2u}{1+u}.$$

Therefore
$$f_T(1+u) = \frac{1}{2\pi\,u^{3/2}}\cdot\frac{2u}{1+u} = \frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{u}\,
(1+u)}.$$

**Step 3: State the law.**

Substituting back $u = t-1$ (so $1+u = t$):

$$\boxed{\,f_T(t) = \frac{1}{\pi\,t\,\sqrt{t-1}}, \qquad t > 1,\,}$$
and $f_T(t) = 0$ for $t \le 1$.

**Step 4: Verification that this is a probability density.**

We check $\int_1^\infty f_T(t)\,dt = 1$. With $u = t-1$ and then $u = v^2$ ($du
= 2v\,dv$):
$$\int_0^\infty \frac{du}{\pi\sqrt{u}(1+u)} = \int_0^\infty \frac{2v\,dv}{\pi\,v
\,(1+v^2)} = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\infty \frac{dv}{1+v^2} = \frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\fr
ac{\pi}{2} = 1. \checkmark$$

**Step 5: No atoms.**

Since $B_1 \neq 0$ a.s. and Brownian motion started from any $x \neq 0$ hits $0$
 at a strictly positive, continuously distributed time (Lévy distribution has no
 atoms), $T$ has no atoms. In particular $T > 1$ a.s. (since $B_1 \neq 0$ a.s.)
and $T < \infty$ a.s. (by recurrence of 1D Brownian motion). Hence $T$ is purely
 absolutely continuous.

**Conclusion.** The random variable $T = \inf\{t \ge 1 : B_t = 0\}$ is absolutel
y continuous on $(1,\infty)$ with density
$$f_T(t) = \frac{1}{\pi\,t\,\sqrt{t-1}}, \quad t > 1,$$
and $f_T(t)=0$ for $t\le 1$. Equivalently, with $U = T-1 \ge 0$, $U$ has density
 $\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{u}(1+u)}$ on $(0,\infty)$, which integrates to $1$ as verifi
ed above.
