## Solution

We evaluate
$$I=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{(ix+b)^2}\,dx,\qquad x,b\in\mathbb R.$$

**Convergence.** Expanding the exponent,
$$(ix+b)^2=-x^{2}+2ibx+b^{2},$$
so
$$\bigl|e^{(ix+b)^2}\bigr|=e^{b^{2}}e^{-x^{2}},$$
which is integrable on $\mathbb R$. Hence $I$ converges absolutely.

**Reduction to a shifted Gaussian.** Using the expansion above,
$$I=e^{b^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-x^{2}+2ibx}\,dx.$$
Complete the square in the exponent:
$$-x^{2}+2ibx=-(x-ib)^{2}-b^{2}.$$
Therefore
$$I=e^{b^{2}}\cdot e^{-b^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-(x-ib)^{2}}\,dx=\int_{-
\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-(x-ib)^{2}}\,dx.$$

Set $w=x-ib$ (so $dw=dx$). The path of integration becomes the horizontal line $
\operatorname{Im}w=-b$:
$$I=\int_{\mathbb R-ib}e^{-w^{2}}\,dw.$$

**Contour shift to the real axis.** The function $e^{-w^{2}}$ is entire. Conside
r the rectangle with vertices $-R-ib,\ R-ib,\ R,\ -R$ (traversed positively). By
 Cauchy's theorem the integral of $e^{-w^{2}}$ around it is $0$.

On the vertical side $w=R+iy$, $y\in[-b,0]$:
$$\bigl|e^{-w^{2}}\bigr|=\bigl|e^{-(R+iy)^{2}}\bigr|=e^{-R^{2}+y^{2}}\le e^{-R^{
2}+b^{2}}\xrightarrow[R\to\infty]{}0,$$
and similarly on the side $w=-R+iy$ we have $|e^{-w^{2}}|=e^{-R^{2}+y^{2}}\to 0$
. Since the side lengths are bounded (equal to $|b|$), both vertical integrals v
anish as $R\to\infty$.

Hence
$$\int_{\mathbb R-ib}e^{-w^{2}}\,dw=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-w^{2}}\,dw.$$

**The standard Gaussian.** It is classical that
$$\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-w^{2}}\,dw=\sqrt{\pi}.$$

(For completeness: this follows from squaring the integral and passing to polar
coordinates,
$$\Bigl(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-t^{2}}dt\Bigr)^{2}=\int_{\mathbb R^{2}}e^{-(u
^{2}+v^{2})}\,du\,dv=2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}re^{-r^{2}}dr=\pi.)$$

**Conclusion.** Combining everything,
$$I=\int_{\mathbb R-ib}e^{-w^{2}}\,dw=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-w^{2}}\,dw=\sq
rt{\pi}.$$

Notably the value is independent of the real constant $b$.

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{(ix+b)^{2}}\,dx=\sqrt{\pi}}$$
