## Proof

Let $R$ be a ring, $\mathbf{a}\trianglelefteq R$ an ideal, and $\pi:R\to R/\math
bf{a}$ the canonical quotient map. The pullback (contraction) map on spectra is
$$
\pi^*:\mathrm{Spec}(R/\mathbf{a})\longrightarrow \mathrm{Spec}(R),\qquad \mathfr
ak{q}\longmapsto \pi^{-1}(\mathfrak{q}).
$$

We claim
$$
\operatorname{im}(\pi^*) \;=\; V(\mathbf{a}) \;=\; \{\,\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Sp
ec}(R):\mathbf{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\,\}.
$$

---

### ($\subseteq$) Every prime in the image contains $\mathbf{a}$.

Let $\mathfrak{q}\in\mathrm{Spec}(R/\mathbf{a})$ and set $\mathfrak{p}=\pi^{-1}(
\mathfrak{q})$. Since $\pi$ is a ring homomorphism and $\mathfrak{q}$ is prime,
the preimage $\mathfrak{p}$ is prime, so $\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Spec}(R)$.

For every $a\in\mathbf{a}$, we have $\pi(a)=a+\mathbf{a}=\bar 0\in R/\mathbf{a}$
. Since $\mathfrak{q}$ is an ideal, $\bar 0\in\mathfrak{q}$, hence $\pi(a)\in\ma
thfrak{q}$, i.e. $a\in\pi^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})=\mathfrak{p}$. Thus $\mathbf{a}\sub
seteq\mathfrak{p}$, proving $\mathfrak{p}\in V(\mathbf{a})$.

---

### ($\supseteq$) Every prime containing $\mathbf{a}$ lies in the image.

Let $\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Spec}(R)$ with $\mathbf{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}$. De
fine
$$
\mathfrak{q} \;:=\; \pi(\mathfrak{p}) \;=\; \{\,a+\mathbf{a}\in R/\mathbf{a}:a\i
n\mathfrak{p}\,\} \;=\; \mathfrak{p}/\mathbf{a}.
$$

Because $\mathbf{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}$, the correspondence theorem for ideals
 gives a bijection between ideals of $R$ containing $\mathbf{a}$ and ideals of $
R/\mathbf{a}$, sending $\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\mathfrak{p}/\mathbf{a}$. Under this
bijection, prime ideals correspond to prime ideals (the quotient $R/\mathfrak{p}
\cong(R/\mathbf{a})/(\mathfrak{p}/\mathbf{a})$ is a domain iff $\mathfrak{p}$ is
 prime). Hence $\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{p}/\mathbf{a}\in\mathrm{Spec}(R/\mathbf{a
})$.

Now compute the contraction:
$$
(\pi^*)^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})\;=\;\pi^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}/\mathbf{a})\;=\;\{\,a\in R:
\pi(a)\in\mathfrak{p}/\mathbf{a}\,\}\;=\;\{\,a\in R:a+\mathbf{a}\in\mathfrak{p}/
\mathbf{a}\,\}.
$$

Since $a+\mathbf{a}\in\mathfrak{p}/\mathbf{a}$ iff $a\in\mathfrak{p}$ (using $\m
athbf{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}$), we get $\pi^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})=\mathfrak{p}$, i
.e. $\pi^*(\mathfrak{q})=\mathfrak{p}$. Therefore $\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{
im}(\pi^*)$.

---

### Conclusion

Both inclusions are established, so
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{im}(\pi^*) \;=\; V(\mathbf{a}) \;=\; \{\,\mathfrak{p}\in\ma
thrm{Spec}(R):\mathbf{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\,\}.}
$$

Equivalently, $\pi^*$ realizes $\mathrm{Spec}(R/\mathbf{a})$ as the closed subse
t $V(\mathbf{a})$ of $\mathrm{Spec}(R)$, and in fact $\pi^*$ is a homeomorphism
of $\mathrm{Spec}(R/\mathbf{a})$ onto $V(\mathbf{a})$ (with the subspace topolog
y), since $\pi$ is surjective.
