## Solution to the Neumann Problem on the Disk

We solve $\nabla^2 u = 0$ in the disk $0 \le r < a$ with $u_r(a,\theta) = f(\the
ta)$.

### Separation of variables in polar coordinates

Laplace's equation in polar coordinates is
$$u_{rr} + \frac{1}{r}u_r + \frac{1}{r^2}u_{\theta\theta} = 0.$$

Seeking solutions $u = R(r)\Theta(\theta)$ with $\Theta$ $2\pi$-periodic gives $
\Theta_n(\theta) \in \{\cos n\theta,\,\sin n\theta\}$ and $R_n(r) = r^n$ (reject
ing $r^{-n}$ and $\log r$ since they are singular at $r=0$). The general solutio
n regular at the origin is therefore

$$u(r,\theta) = A_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} r^n\bigl(A_n\cos n\theta + B_n\sin n\t
heta\bigr).$$

(The constant $A_0$ reflects the fact that the Neumann problem determines $u$ on
ly up to an additive constant.)

### Applying the boundary condition

Differentiating,
$$u_r(r,\theta) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n\,r^{n-1}\bigl(A_n\cos n\theta + B_n\sin
n\theta\bigr).$$

At $r = a$:
$$f(\theta) = u_r(a,\theta) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n\,a^{n-1}\bigl(A_n\cos n\thet
a + B_n\sin n\theta\bigr). \tag{$\ast$}$$

Expand $f$ in a Fourier series:
$$f(\theta) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl(a_n\cos n\theta + b_n\sin
 n\theta\bigr),$$
where
$$a_0 = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(\theta)\,d\theta,\quad a_n = \frac{1}{\pi}\
int_0^{2\pi} f(\theta)\cos n\theta\,d\theta,\quad b_n = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\p
i} f(\theta)\sin n\theta\,d\theta.$$

Comparing coefficients with $(\ast)$:

- **Constant term:** The right-hand side of $(\ast)$ has **no constant term** (t
he sum starts at $n=1$). Hence we must have $a_0/2 = 0$, i.e.
$$a_0 = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(\theta)\,d\theta = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\q
uad \int_0^{2\pi} f(\theta)\,d\theta = 0.$$

- **For $n \ge 1$:** matching gives
$$n\,a^{n-1} A_n = a_n,\qquad n\,a^{n-1} B_n = b_n,$$
so
$$A_n = \frac{a_n}{n\,a^{n-1}},\qquad B_n = \frac{b_n}{n\,a^{n-1}}.$$

### The solution

Provided $\int_0^{2\pi} f(\theta)\,d\theta = 0$, the solution is

$$\boxed{\,u(r,\theta) = A_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{r^n}{n\,a^{n-1}}\bigl(a
_n\cos n\theta + b_n\sin n\theta\bigr)\,}$$

with $A_0$ arbitrary (reflecting uniqueness only up to a constant), and $a_n, b_
n$ the Fourier coefficients of $f$ as above.

### Why the compatibility condition is necessary

There are two complementary ways to see that $\int_0^{2\pi} f\,d\theta = 0$ is n
ecessary.

**(1) Via the divergence theorem (global argument).** Since $\nabla^2 u = 0$ in
$D = \{r < a\}$, integrating over the disk and applying the divergence theorem:
$$0 = \iint_D \nabla^2 u\,dA = \oint_{\partial D} \frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\
,ds = \int_0^{2\pi} u_r(a,\theta)\,a\,d\theta = a\int_0^{2\pi} f(\theta)\,d\thet
a.$$
Since $a > 0$, this forces $\int_0^{2\pi} f(\theta)\,d\theta = 0$. Physically, $
u_r(a,\theta)$ is the flux density through the boundary; for a harmonic function
 (no sources inside) the net flux through a closed curve must vanish.

**(2) Via the Fourier expansion (local argument).** As shown above, the radial d
erivative $u_r(a,\theta)$ of any function regular at the origin is a Fourier ser
ies containing **only** modes $\cos n\theta, \sin n\theta$ with $n \ge 1$. The $
n=0$ (constant) mode is absent because differentiating $A_0$ kills it and differ
entiating $r^n$ with $n\ge 1$ never produces a constant. Therefore $f$ — being e
qual to such a series — cannot have a nonzero mean (constant Fourier component).
 The condition $a_0 = 0$ is precisely $\int f\,d\theta = 0$.

Both arguments show the condition is **necessary**; the explicit construction ab
ove shows it is also **sufficient** (every $f$ with zero mean is realized by the
 series). Hence the compatibility condition $\int_0^{2\pi} f(\theta)\,d\theta =
0$ is exactly the necessary and sufficient condition for the Neumann problem to
be solvable, with the solution unique up to the additive constant $A_0$.
