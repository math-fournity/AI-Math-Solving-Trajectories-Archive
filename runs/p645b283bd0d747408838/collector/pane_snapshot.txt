## Proof

Let the entries of $X$ be
$$X = \begin{pmatrix} x_1 & x_3 \\ x_3 & x_2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad x_1,x_2\overs
et{\text{iid}}{\sim}\mathcal N(0,1),\quad x_3\sim\mathcal N(0,\tfrac12),$$
with $x_1,x_2,x_3$ mutually independent.

### Step 1. Eigenvalue gap of a $2\times 2$ symmetric matrix

For a real symmetric matrix $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ b & c\end{pmatrix}$, the e
igenvalues are
$$\lambda_{\pm} = \frac{a+c}{2}\pm\sqrt{\left(\frac{a-c}{2}\right)^2+b^2},$$
so the ordered gap ($\lambda_2\ge\lambda_1$) is
$$\lambda_2-\lambda_1 = 2\sqrt{\left(\frac{a-c}{2}\right)^2+b^2}=\sqrt{(a-c)^2+4
b^2}.$$

Substituting $a=x_1,\;c=x_2,\;b=x_3$:
$$\boxed{\;\lambda_2-\lambda_1 = \sqrt{(x_1-x_2)^2+4x_3^2}\;} \tag{1}$$

### Step 2. Reduce to independent standard normals

- **The difference term.** Since $x_1,x_2$ are independent $\mathcal N(0,1)$,
 $$x_1-x_2\sim\mathcal N(0,\,1+1)=\mathcal N(0,2).$$
 Hence $Z_1:=\dfrac{x_1-x_2}{\sqrt 2}\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$, and $(x_1-x_2)^2 = 2Z
_1^2$.

- **The off-diagonal term.** Since $x_3\sim\mathcal N(0,\tfrac12)$,
 $$Z_2:=\sqrt 2\,x_3\sim\mathcal N(0,1),$$
 and $4x_3^2 = 4\cdot\tfrac12 Z_2^2 = 2Z_2^2$.

- **Independence.** $Z_1$ is a function of $(x_1,x_2)$ and $Z_2$ is a function o
f $x_3$. Since $x_1,x_2,x_3$ are mutually independent, $Z_1$ and $Z_2$ are indep
endent standard normals.

### Step 3. Express the gap as a scaled chi distribution

Plugging into (1):
$$\lambda_2-\lambda_1 = \sqrt{2Z_1^2+2Z_2^2}=\sqrt 2\,\sqrt{Z_1^2+Z_2^2}.$$

Since $Z_1,Z_2\overset{\text{iid}}{\sim}\mathcal N(0,1)$,
$$Z_1^2+Z_2^2\sim\chi^2(2),\qquad \sqrt{Z_1^2+Z_2^2}\sim\chi_2\;\text{(chi distr
ibution, 2 d.f.)}.$$

Therefore
$$\lambda_2-\lambda_1 \;=\; \sqrt 2\,\chi_2 \;=\; \sqrt{2\,\chi^2(2)}.$$

Equivalently,
$$\frac{(\lambda_2-\lambda_1)^2}{2}\sim\chi^2(2),\qquad\text{or}\qquad(\lambda_2
-\lambda_1)^2\sim 2\,\chi^2(2)=\operatorname{Exp}\!\left(\text{rate}=\tfrac14\ri
ght).$$

### Step 4. Density (Rayleigh form)

The chi distribution with 2 degrees of freedom is the **Rayleigh distribution**
with scale $\sigma=1$, having density $f_R(r)=r\,e^{-r^2/2}$ for $r\ge 0$.

Set $Y=\lambda_2-\lambda_1=\sqrt 2\,R$, so $R=Y/\sqrt 2$ and $dR/dY=1/\sqrt 2$.
By the change-of-variables formula, for $y\ge 0$:
$$f_Y(y)=f_R\!\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt 2}\right)\cdot\frac1{\sqrt 2}
=\frac{y}{\sqrt 2}\,e^{-y^2/4}\cdot\frac1{\sqrt 2}
=\frac{y}{2}\,e^{-y^2/4}.$$

This is precisely the **Rayleigh density with scale $\sigma=\sqrt 2$**:
$$\boxed{\;f_{\lambda_2-\lambda_1}(y)=\frac{y}{2}\,e^{-y^2/4},\quad y\ge 0,\;}$$
and $f=0$ for $y<0$.

### Verification of moments (sanity check)

- $\mathbb E[(\lambda_2-\lambda_1)^2]=\mathbb E[2\,\chi^2(2)]=2\cdot 2=4$.
- Directly: $\mathbb E[(x_1-x_2)^2+4x_3^2]=2+4\cdot\tfrac12=2+2=4$. ✓
- $\mathbb E[\lambda_2-\lambda_1]=\sqrt 2\,\mathbb E[\chi_2]=\sqrt 2\cdot\sqrt{\
pi/2}=\sqrt\pi$.
- Directly via the Rayleigh($\sqrt 2$) mean: $\sigma\sqrt{\pi/2}=\sqrt 2\sqrt{\p
i/2}=\sqrt\pi$. ✓

### Conclusion

$\lambda_2-\lambda_1$ follows a **Rayleigh distribution with scale parameter $\s
igma=\sqrt 2$**, equivalently $\sqrt{2\,\chi^2(2)}$ where $\chi^2(2)$ is a chi-s
quared random variable with 2 degrees of freedom. Its density is $f(y)=\dfrac{y}
{2}e^{-y^2/4}$ for $y\ge 0$.
