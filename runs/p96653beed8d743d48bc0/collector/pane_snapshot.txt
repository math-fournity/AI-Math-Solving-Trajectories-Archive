## Solution

**Setting up the recurrence for $S_n$.**

Let $T(n,k)$ denote the $k$-th entry of row $n$ ($0$-indexed, $k = 0, \ldots, n{
-}1$). The rules give:
- $T(n,0) = T(n,n{-}1) = n^2$
- $T(n,k) = T(n{-}1,k{-}1) + T(n{-}1,k)$ for $1 \le k \le n{-}2$

Summing over a row:

$$S_n = 2n^2 + \sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\bigl[T(n{-}1,k{-}1)+T(n{-}1,k)\bigr] = 2n^2 + 2S
_{n-1} - 2(n{-}1)^2$$

since the two shifted sums each equal $S_{n-1}$ minus one boundary term $(n{-}1)
^2$. Simplifying:

$$S_n = 2\,S_{n-1} + 4n - 2, \qquad S_1 = 1.$$

**Closed form.** Solving the linear recurrence: homogeneous part $C\cdot 2^n$; p
articular solution $-4n-6$. From $S_1=1$: $2C - 10 = 1$, so $C = 11/2$. Therefor
e:

$$\boxed{S_n = 11\cdot 2^{n-1} - 4n - 6}.$$

Verification: $S_1=1$, $S_2=8$, $S_3=26$, $S_4=66$, $S_5=150$ — all match the gi
ven triangle.

**Reducing modulo 13.** We need $S_n \equiv 0 \pmod{13}$, i.e.,

$$11\cdot 2^{n-1} \equiv 4n+6 \pmod{13}.$$

Since $11 \equiv -2$, this becomes $-2^n \equiv 4n+6 \pmod{13}$, or equivalently
:

$$2^n \equiv -4n - 6 \pmod{13}.$$

The left side $2^n \bmod 13$ has period $12$ (since $\operatorname{ord}_{13}(2)=
12$). The right side depends on $n \bmod 13$. Since $\gcd(12,13)=1$, the conditi
on is periodic with period $\operatorname{lcm}(12,13)=156$.

**Finding valid residues mod 156.** Write $a = n \bmod 12$, $b = n \bmod 13$. Th
e condition is $2^a \equiv -4b - 6 \pmod{13}$, i.e., $b \equiv -10(2^a + 6) \equ
iv -10\cdot 2^a + 5 \pmod{13}$ (using $4^{-1}\equiv 10$). Computing for each $a
\in \{0,\ldots,11\}$:

| $a$ | $2^a\bmod 13$ | $b\bmod 13$ | $r\bmod 156$ |
|-----|---------------|-------------|--------------|
| 0 | 1 | 8 | 60 |
| 1 | 2 | 11 | 37 |
| 2 | 4 | 4 | 134 |
| 3 | 8 | 3 | 3 |
| 4 | 3 | 1 | 40 |
| 5 | 6 | 10 | 101 |
| 6 | 12 | 2 | 54 |
| 7 | 11 | 12 | 103 |
| 8 | 9 | 6 | 32 |
| 9 | 5 | 7 | 33 |
| 10 | 10 | 9 | 22 |
| 11 | 7 | 0 | 143 |

Each $(a,b)$ pair yields exactly one residue $r \bmod 156$ by CRT. The 12 valid
residues are:

$$3,\ 22,\ 32,\ 33,\ 37,\ 40,\ 54,\ 60,\ 101,\ 103,\ 134,\ 143.$$

**Spot checks:** $S_3 = 26 \equiv 0$ ✓; $S_{22}$: $2^{21}\bmod 13 = 2^9\bmod 13
= 5$, $11\cdot5 - 94 = -39\equiv 0$ ✓; $S_{40}$: $2^{39}\bmod 13 = 2^3\bmod 13=8
$, $11\cdot 8-166=-78\equiv 0$ ✓.

**Counting $n \in [1, 10^6]$.** Since $10^6 = 6410 \times 156 + 40$:

- **Complete periods:** Integers $1$ to $999960$ contain $6410$ full residue sys
tems mod 156, contributing $6410 \times 12 = 76920$ valid values.
- **Remainder:** Integers $999961$ to $1000000$ have residues $1, 2, \ldots, 40
\pmod{156}$. The valid residues among these are $3, 22, 32, 33, 37, 40$ — that's
 **6** values.

**Total:**

$$76920 + 6 = \boxed{76926}.$$
