## Proof

Let
$$S_n \;=\; n\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)^n\frac{1}{m^2}.$$

The $m=1$ term is $(1-1)^n\cdot 1 = 0$ for every $n\ge 1$, so effectively
$$S_n \;=\; \sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{n}{m^2}\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)^n.$$

**Reformulation as a Riemann sum.** Set $x_m := m/n$ (so $\Delta x = 1/n$). Then
$$S_n \;=\; \frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x_m^{\,2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{n\
,x_m}\right)^n \;=\; \frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=2}^{\infty} h_n(x_m),$$
where
$$h_n(x) \;:=\; \frac{1}{x^2}\left(1-\frac{1}{nx}\right)^n,\qquad x>\tfrac{1}{n}
.$$

Since $(1-1/(nx))^n \to e^{-1/x}$ pointwise for $x>0$, we have $h_n(x)\to h(x):=
x^{-2}e^{-1/x}$.

**Dominating bound.** Using $1-t\le e^{-t}$ for $t\ge 0$ (with $t=1/(nx)\le \tfr
ac12$ since $x_m\ge 2/n$),
$$h_n(x_m)\;\le\;\frac{e^{-1/x_m}}{x_m^{\,2}}\;=\;h(x_m).$$

**Tail control ($x\to\infty$).** For $x\ge 1$, $h(x)\le x^{-2}$, hence for any $
M>0$:
$$\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m\,>\,Mn} h_n(x_m)\;\le\;\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m\,>\,Mn}\frac{1}{x
_m^{\,2}}\;=\;\sum_{m\,>\,Mn}\frac{n}{m^2}\;\le\;\frac{1}{M},$$
using $\sum_{m>K}m^{-2}\le 1/K$. So the tail is $O(1/M)$, uniformly in $n$.

**Small-$x$ control ($x\to 0^+$).** Since $h(x)=e^{-1/x}/x^2\to 0$ super-polynom
ially as $x\to 0^+$,
$$\frac{1}{n}\sum_{2\le m\le \varepsilon n} h_n(x_m)\;\le\;\frac{1}{n}\sum_{2\le
 m\le \varepsilon n} h(x_m)\;\le\;\int_{0}^{\varepsilon} h(x)\,dx + o(1)\;\xrigh
tarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}\;0.$$

**Bounded core.** On $[\varepsilon,M]$ the convergence $(1-1/(nx))^n\to e^{-1/x}
$ is uniform, so $h_n\to h$ uniformly there, and
$$\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m:\,x_m\in[\varepsilon,M]} h_n(x_m)\;\longrightarrow\;\int_{\
varepsilon}^{M} h(x)\,dx.$$

**Combining.** For every $\varepsilon,M>0$,
$$\limsup_{n\to\infty} S_n \;\le\; \int_{\varepsilon}^{M} h(x)\,dx \;+\; \frac{1
}{M}\;+\;\int_{0}^{\varepsilon} h(x)\,dx,$$
and the matching lower bound (using $h_n\ge 0$ and uniform convergence on $[\var
epsilon,M]$) gives the same expression for $\liminf$. Letting $\varepsilon\to 0^
+$ and $M\to\infty$,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} S_n \;=\; \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-1/x}}{x^2}\,dx.$$

**Evaluating the integral.** Substitute $u=1/x$, so $du=-x^{-2}\,dx$, i.e. $x^{-
2}dx=-du$; as $x:0^+\to\infty$, $u:\infty\to 0^+$:
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-1/x}}{x^2}\,dx \;=\; \int_{\infty}^{0} e^{-u}(-du)
\;=\; \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-u}\,du \;=\; 1.$$

Therefore
$$\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty} n\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\
right)^n\frac{1}{m^2} \;=\; 1.}$$
