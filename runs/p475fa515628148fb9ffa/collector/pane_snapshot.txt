Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether constant order 3 along the
zero set of a holomorphic function on a domain in ℂⁿ implies a local cube factor
ization with a unit.

**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

We work locally at an arbitrary point $z_0 \in Z(f)$. The local ring $\mathcal{O
}_{\mathbb{C}^n, z_0}$ is a UFD (by the Weierstrass preparation theorem), so we
can factor $f$ uniquely (up to units and reordering) as:

$$f = u \cdot p_1^{a_1} \cdots p_r^{a_r}$$

where $u$ is a unit, each $p_i$ is irreducible with $p_i(z_0)=0$, the $p_i$ are
pairwise non-associate, and $a_i \geq 1$.

We use the standard definition $\operatorname{ord}_z f = \max\{k : f \in \mathfr
ak{m}_z^k\}$, the degree of the lowest nonzero homogeneous term in the Taylor ex
pansion at $z$. Key property: $\operatorname{ord}_z(fg) = \operatorname{ord}_z f
 + \operatorname{ord}_z g$.

---

**Step 1: Each $a_i = 3$.**

Fix an index $i$. The irreducible hypersurface germ $Z(p_i)$ has a nonempty smoo
th locus (the singular locus of an irreducible hypersurface has codimension $\ge
q 2$ in the ambient space, while $Z(p_i)$ has codimension $1$). Moreover, $Z(p_i
) \cap Z(p_j)$ for $j \neq i$ is a proper analytic subset of $Z(p_i)$ (since $p_
i$ is irreducible and $p_j$ is not an associate of $p_i$). Therefore, there exis
t points $z$ arbitrarily close to $z_0$ that lie on $Z(p_i)$, are smooth points
of $Z(p_i)$, and do not lie on any $Z(p_j)$ for $j \neq i$.

At such a point $z$: $p_i$ is a local coordinate, so $\operatorname{ord}_z p_i =
 1$; each $p_j$ ($j \neq i$) is a unit at $z$, so $\operatorname{ord}_z p_j = 0$
; and $u$ is a unit. Thus:

$$\operatorname{ord}_z f = a_i \cdot \operatorname{ord}_z p_i = a_i \cdot 1 = a_
i.$$

By hypothesis $\operatorname{ord}_z f = 3$, so $a_i = 3$.

---

**Step 2: Only one irreducible component passes through $z_0$ (i.e., $r=1$).**

Suppose $r \geq 2$. Since every $p_i$ vanishes at $z_0$, we have $\operatorname{
ord}_{z_0} p_i \geq 1$ for each $i$. Using $a_i = 3$ from Step 1:

$$\operatorname{ord}_{z_0} f = \sum_{i=1}^{r} a_i \cdot \operatorname{ord}_{z_0}
 p_i = 3\sum_{i=1}^{r} \operatorname{ord}_{z_0} p_i \geq 3 \cdot 1 \cdot r \geq
6.$$

But the hypothesis requires $\operatorname{ord}_{z_0} f = 3$. Contradiction. Hen
ce $r = 1$.

---

**Step 3: The unique component is smooth at $z_0$.**

With $r=1$, we have $f = u \cdot p_1^3$ and:

$$\operatorname{ord}_{z_0} f = 3 \cdot \operatorname{ord}_{z_0} p_1 = 3,$$

so $\operatorname{ord}_{z_0} p_1 = 1$. This means $p_1$ has a nonzero linear ter
m at $z_0$, i.e., $\nabla p_1(z_0) \neq 0$. By the holomorphic implicit function
 theorem, $Z(p_1)$ is a smooth complex submanifold near $z_0$.

---

**Step 4: The factorization.**

Set $\psi = p_1$ and $g = u$. Then on a neighborhood of $z_0$:

$$f = \psi^3 \cdot g,$$

where $g = u$ is nonvanishing, and $\operatorname{ord}_{z_0} \psi = 1$. Since $Z
(\psi)$ is smooth near $z_0$, we in fact have $\operatorname{ord}_z \psi = 1$ fo
r all $z \in Z(\psi)$ in this neighborhood.

---

**Conclusion.** The argument applies at every $z_0 \in Z(f)$, yielding the desir
ed local factorization $f = \psi^3 g$ on a neighborhood of each zero, with $g$ n
onvanishing and $\operatorname{ord}_z \psi = 1$.

The essential mechanism is that the constant-order condition is highly rigid: it
 forces each irreducible component to appear with multiplicity exactly $3$ (Step
 1), prevents distinct components from meeting (Step 2, since two components wou
ld give order $\geq 6$ at the intersection), and forces each component to be smo
oth (Step 3). The local UFD property then provides the cube factorization direct
ly.
