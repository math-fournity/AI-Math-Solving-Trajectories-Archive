**Answer: Yes.** Slater's condition is sufficient for strong duality, not necess
ary. Here is an explicit convex program with zero duality gap that violates Slat
er's condition.

---

### Example

Consider the convex program (with variable $x \in \mathbb{R}$):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{minimize} \quad & x \\
\text{subject to} \quad & x^2 \le 0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The objective and constraint are convex, so this is a valid convex program.

---

### Feasible set and primal optimal value

The constraint $x^2 \le 0$ forces $x = 0$ (since $x^2 \ge 0$ for all real $x$, w
ith equality only at $x=0$). Hence the feasible set is the singleton $\{0\}$, an
d the primal optimal value is

$$
p^\* = 0.
$$

---

### Slater's condition fails

Slater's condition (for this problem with one inequality constraint) requires a
**strictly feasible** point: some $x$ with $x^2 < 0$. No real number satisfies t
his. Therefore **Slater's condition is not satisfied**.

---

### Lagrangian dual

Introduce a multiplier $\lambda \ge 0$ for the constraint $x^2 \le 0$. The Lagra
ngian is

$$
L(x, \lambda) = x + \lambda x^2.
$$

The dual function is

$$
g(\lambda) = \inf_{x \in \mathbb{R}} \bigl(x + \lambda x^2\bigr).
$$

- **For $\lambda > 0$:** $x + \lambda x^2$ is a strictly convex quadratic, minim
ized at $x^\* = -\tfrac{1}{2\lambda}$, giving
  $$
  g(\lambda) = -\frac{1}{2\lambda} + \lambda \cdot \frac{1}{4\lambda^2} = -\frac
{1}{4\lambda}.
  $$
- **For $\lambda = 0$:** $g(0) = \inf_x x = -\infty$.

The dual problem is

$$
\text{maximize } g(\lambda) \quad \text{subject to } \lambda \ge 0.
$$

Since $g(\lambda) = -\tfrac{1}{4\lambda} < 0$ for every $\lambda > 0$ and $g(\la
mbda) \to 0$ as $\lambda \to +\infty$, the dual optimal value is

$$
d^\* = \sup_{\lambda > 0} \left(-\frac{1}{4\lambda}\right) = 0,
$$

attained as a supremum in the limit $\lambda \to \infty$ (the dual optimum is no
t attained by any finite $\lambda$, but the supremum equals $0$).

---

### Zero duality gap

$$
p^\* - d^\* = 0 - 0 = 0.
$$

The duality gap is **zero**, yet Slater's condition **fails** (there is no stric
tly feasible point).

---

### Conclusion

We have exhibited a convex program — minimize $x$ subject to $x^2 \le 0$ — with:

- **Zero duality gap** ($p^\* = d^\* = 0$), and
- **Slater's condition violated** (no strictly feasible point exists).

This demonstrates that Slater's condition is sufficient but not necessary for st
rong duality. Therefore, **yes**, such a convex program exists.
