## Solution

**Claim.** The polynomial $f = x^p + y^p - z^p$ is irreducible over $K$ if and o
nly if $\operatorname{char}(K) \neq p$.

---

### Case 1: $\operatorname{char}(K) = p$ — reducible.

By the Frobenius (Freshman's dream), in characteristic $p$:
$$x^p + y^p - z^p = (x + y - z)^p.$$
This is a $p$-th power of the non-constant linear form $x+y-z$, hence reducible.

---

### Case 2: $\operatorname{char}(K) \neq p$ — irreducible.

View $f$ as a polynomial in $z$ over the UFD $K[x,y]$:
$$f = z^p - (x^p + y^p) \in K[x,y][z] = K[x,y,z].$$
This polynomial is monic (hence primitive) in $z$. By Gauss's Lemma, $f$ is irre
ducible in $K[x,y][z]$ if and only if it is irreducible in $K(x,y)[z]$.

We apply **Capelli's theorem** on binomials: for a field $F$, $a \in F$, and $n
\geq 1$, the polynomial $T^n - a$ is irreducible over $F$ if and only if $a \not
in F^q$ for every prime $q \mid n$ (and, when $4 \mid n$, $a \notin -4F^4$).

Here $n = p$ is prime, so the only prime dividing $n$ is $p$ itself, and the "$4
 \mid n$" condition does not apply. Thus:
$$z^p - (x^p+y^p) \text{ is irreducible over } K(x,y) \iff x^p + y^p \notin K(x,
y)^p.$$

**We show $x^p + y^p \notin K(x,y)^p$.** Suppose for contradiction that $x^p + y
^p = g^p$ for some $g \in K(x,y)$. Write $g = r/s$ with $r, s \in K[x,y]$ coprim
e. Then
$$r^p = s^p(x^p + y^p).$$
Since $\gcd(r,s)=1$, any irreducible factor $\pi$ of $s$ divides $s^p$ but not $
r^p$, so $\pi \mid (x^p+y^p)$... but more directly: $\gcd(r^p, s^p) = 1$, and $s
^p \mid r^p$, forcing $s$ to be a unit. Hence $g \in K[x,y]$, and $g^p = x^p + y
^p$ in $K[x,y]$.

Now $x^p + y^p$ is homogeneous of degree $p$, so $g$ must be homogeneous of degr
ee $1$, i.e., $g = ax + by$ for some $a, b \in K$. Expanding:
$$(ax+by)^p = \sum_{k=0}^{p}\binom{p}{k}a^k b^{p-k}\, x^k y^{p-k}.$$
Since $\operatorname{char}(K) \neq p$ and $p$ is prime, the binomial coefficient
s $\binom{p}{k}$ for $1 \leq k \leq p-1$ are all nonzero in $K$. For this to equ
al $x^p + y^p$, every cross term ($1 \leq k \leq p-1$) must vanish, requiring $a
^k b^{p-k} = 0$ for all such $k$. This forces $a = 0$ or $b = 0$, but then $g^p
= a^p x^p$ or $b^p y^p$, which cannot equal $x^p + y^p$. Contradiction.

Therefore $x^p + y^p \notin K(x,y)^p$, and by Capelli's theorem, $z^p - (x^p+y^p
)$ is irreducible over $K(x,y)$. By Gauss's Lemma, $f = x^p + y^p - z^p$ is irre
ducible in $K[x,y,z]$.

---

### Conclusion

$$x^p + y^p - z^p \text{ is irreducible over } K \iff \operatorname{char}(K) \ne
q p.$$
