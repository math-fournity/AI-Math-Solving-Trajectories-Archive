# Derivation of the Friedmann Equations

## 1. Setup: The Robertson–Walker Metric

Consider a homogeneous and isotropic universe described by the Friedmann–Lemaîtr
e–Robertson–Walker (FLRW) metric

$$ds^2 = -dt^2 + a(t)^2\left[\frac{dr^2}{1-kr^2} + r^2\left(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\t
heta\,d\phi^2\right)\right],$$

where $a(t)$ is the scale factor and $k\in\{-1,0,+1\}$ is the spatial curvature
parameter. Writing $g_{ij}=a^2\tilde g_{ij}$ with $\tilde g_{ij}$ the unit-curva
ture 3-metric (independent of $t$), the inverse metric is $g^{00}=-1$, $g^{ij}=a
^{-2}\tilde g^{ij}$.

The matter content is a **perfect fluid** comoving with the cosmic flow, $u^\mu=
(1,0,0,0)$, so $u_0=-1$, $u_i=0$. Its stress–energy tensor is

$$T_{\mu\nu}=(\rho+p)\,u_\mu u_\nu + p\,g_{\mu\nu},$$

giving $T_{00}=\rho$, $\;T_{ij}=p\,g_{ij}$, $\;T_{0i}=0$.

Einstein's equation (without cosmological constant) is

$$G_{\mu\nu}\equiv R_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac12 g_{\mu\nu}R = 8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}.$$

## 2. Christoffel Symbols

The non-vanishing Christoffel symbols are:

- $\Gamma^0_{ij}=a\dot a\,\tilde g_{ij}$,
- $\Gamma^i_{0j}=\dfrac{\dot a}{a}\,\delta^i_{\,j}$,
- $\Gamma^i_{jk}=\tilde\Gamma^i_{jk}$ (the spatial Christoffel symbols of $\tild
e g_{ij}$, since the conformal factor $a^2$ cancels).

All symbols with two or more temporal indices in the lower slots vanish because
$g_{00}=-1$ is constant and $g_{0i}=0$.

---

## 3. Ricci Tensor Components

### $R_{00}$

Using $R_{00}=\partial_\mu\Gamma^\mu_{00}-\partial_0\Gamma^\mu_{0\mu}+\Gamma^\mu
_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{00}-\Gamma^\mu_{0\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{0\mu}$ and
 $\Gamma^\mu_{00}=0$:

$$\Gamma^\mu_{0\mu}=3\frac{\dot a}{a},\qquad \partial_0\Gamma^\mu_{0\mu}=3\frac{
\ddot a}{a}-3\frac{\dot a^2}{a^2},\qquad \Gamma^\mu_{0\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{0\
mu}=3\frac{\dot a^2}{a^2}.$$

Hence

$$\boxed{R_{00}=-3\frac{\ddot a}{a}}.$$

### $R_{ij}$

Expanding $R_{ij}=\partial_\mu\Gamma^\mu_{ij}-\partial_j\Gamma^\mu_{i\mu}+\Gamma
^\mu_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{ij}-\Gamma^\mu_{j\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{i\mu}$
 term by term:

- **Term 1:** $\partial_0\Gamma^0_{ij}+\partial_k\Gamma^k_{ij}=(\dot a^2+a\ddot
a)\tilde g_{ij}+\partial_k\tilde\Gamma^k_{ij}$.
- **Term 2:** $-\partial_j\Gamma^\mu_{i\mu}=-\partial_j\tilde\Gamma^k_{ik}$ (sin
ce $\Gamma^0_{i0}=0$).
- **Term 3:** $\Gamma^\mu_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{ij}=3\frac{\dot a}{a}\cdot
 a\dot a\,\tilde g_{ij}+\tilde\Gamma^k_{kl}\tilde\Gamma^l_{ij}=3\dot a^2\tilde g
_{ij}+\tilde\Gamma^k_{kl}\tilde\Gamma^l_{ij}$.
- **Term 4:** $-\Gamma^\mu_{j\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{i\mu}=-2\dot a^2\tilde g_{i
j}-\tilde\Gamma^m_{jl}\tilde\Gamma^l_{im}$ (the two $\dot a^2$ contributions com
e from the mixed $0$–$i$ index pairs).

Collecting the $\tilde g_{ij}$ coefficients: $(\dot a^2+a\ddot a)+3\dot a^2-2\do
t a^2=2\dot a^2+a\ddot a$. The remaining terms are exactly the Ricci tensor $\ti
lde R_{ij}$ of the 3-metric $\tilde g_{ij}$:

$$R_{ij}=(a\ddot a+2\dot a^2)\tilde g_{ij}+\tilde R_{ij}.$$

For a 3-space of constant curvature $k$, $\tilde R_{ij}=2k\,\tilde g_{ij}$. Ther
efore

$$\boxed{R_{ij}=\left(\frac{\ddot a}{a}+2\frac{\dot a^2}{a^2}+\frac{2k}{a^2}\rig
ht)g_{ij}}.$$

## 4. Ricci Scalar

$$R=g^{00}R_{00}+g^{ij}R_{ij}=3\frac{\ddot a}{a}+3\left(\frac{\ddot a}{a}+2\frac
{\dot a^2}{a^2}+\frac{2k}{a^2}\right)=\boxed{6\frac{\ddot a}{a}+6\frac{\dot a^2}
{a^2}+\frac{6k}{a^2}}.$$

---

## 5. Einstein Tensor

### $G_{00}$

$$G_{00}=R_{00}-\tfrac12 g_{00}R=-3\frac{\ddot a}{a}+\frac12\left(6\frac{\ddot a
}{a}+6\frac{\dot a^2}{a^2}+\frac{6k}{a^2}\right)=3\frac{\dot a^2}{a^2}+\frac{3k}
{a^2}.$$

$$\boxed{G_{00}=3\frac{\dot a^2+k}{a^2}}.$$

### $G_{ij}$

$$G_{ij}=R_{ij}-\tfrac12 g_{ij}R=\left[\frac{\ddot a}{a}+2\frac{\dot a^2}{a^2}+\
frac{2k}{a^2}-3\frac{\ddot a}{a}-3\frac{\dot a^2}{a^2}-\frac{3k}{a^2}\right]g_{i
j}.$$

$$\boxed{G_{ij}=-\left(2\frac{\ddot a}{a}+\frac{\dot a^2}{a^2}+\frac{k}{a^2}\rig
ht)g_{ij}}.$$

---

## 6. Imposing Einstein's Equation

### 00-component (First Friedmann Equation)

$$3\frac{\dot a^2+k}{a^2}=8\pi G\,\rho.$$

Defining the Hubble parameter $H\equiv\dot a/a$:

$$\boxed{H^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\,\rho-\frac{k}{a^2}}\qquad\text{(First Friedmann e
quation)}.$$

### $ij$-component (Second Friedmann Equation)

$$-\left(2\frac{\ddot a}{a}+\frac{\dot a^2}{a^2}+\frac{k}{a^2}\right)=8\pi G\,p.
$$

Substituting $\dot a^2/a^2+k/a^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho$ from the first equation:

$$2\frac{\ddot a}{a}+\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho=-8\pi G\,p,$$

$$\boxed{\frac{\ddot a}{a}=-\frac{4\pi G}{3}(\rho+3p)}\qquad\text{(Second Friedm
ann equation)}.$$

---

## 7. Consistency: Energy Conservation

Taking the time derivative of the first Friedmann equation, $\dot a^2=\frac{8\pi
 G}{3}\rho a^2-k$:

$$2\dot a\ddot a=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\dot\rho\,a^2+\frac{16\pi G}{3}\rho a\dot a.$$

Dividing by $2a\dot a$ and using the second Friedmann equation to replace $\ddot
 a/a$:

$$-\frac{4\pi G}{3}(\rho+3p)=\frac{4\pi G}{3}\frac{a\dot\rho}{\dot a}+\frac{8\pi
 G}{3}\rho,$$

which simplifies to

$$\boxed{\dot\rho+3\frac{\dot a}{a}(\rho+p)=0}\qquad\text{(Continuity / energy-c
onservation equation)}.$$

This is precisely $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu 0}=0$, confirming that the two Friedmann eq
uations are mutually consistent and encode local energy–momentum conservation.

---

## 8. Summary

From the FLRW metric and a perfect-fluid stress–energy tensor inserted into $G_{
\mu\nu}=8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}$, the $00$-component yields the **first Friedmann equ
ation**

$$H^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho-\frac{k}{a^2},$$

and the spatial $ij$-components yield the **second Friedmann equation**

$$\frac{\ddot a}{a}=-\frac{4\pi G}{3}(\rho+3p).$$

Together with the continuity equation $\dot\rho+3H(\rho+p)=0$ (which follows fro
m either $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0$ or the compatibility of the two Friedmann equ
ations), these govern the dynamical expansion of a homogeneous, isotropic univer
se filled with a perfect fluid of density $\rho$ and pressure $p$. Including a c
osmological constant $\Lambda$ amounts to the replacements $\rho\to\rho+\rho_\La
mbda$ and $p\to p-\rho_\Lambda$ with $\rho_\Lambda=\Lambda/(8\pi G)$, producing
the generalized form $H^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho-\frac{k}{a^2}+\frac{\Lambda}{3}$.
