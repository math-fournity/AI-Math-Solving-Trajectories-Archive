## Proof

Let $Y = \{f \in C^2[a,b] : f(a) = f(b) = 0\} \subset X$, and consider the opera
tor $D^2 : Y \to C[a,b]$ defined by $D^2 f = f''$. We show $D^2$ is **onto** $C[
a,b]$.

**Construction.** Let $g \in C[a,b]$ be arbitrary. Define
$$A = -\frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b (b-t)\,g(t)\,dt,$$
and set
$$f(x) = \int_a^x (x-t)\,g(t)\,dt + A(x-a).$$

**Verification.**

1. *Smoothness.* Since $g$ is continuous, by the Leibniz rule
$$f'(x) = \int_a^x g(t)\,dt + A, \qquad f''(x) = g(x).$$
Hence $f \in C^2[a,b]$ and $D^2 f = g$.

2. *Boundary condition at $a$.*
$$f(a) = \int_a^a (a-t)\,g(t)\,dt + A\cdot 0 = 0.$$

3. *Boundary condition at $b$.*
$$f(b) = \int_a^b (b-t)\,g(t)\,dt + A(b-a) = \int_a^b (b-t)\,g(t)\,dt - \int_a^b
 (b-t)\,g(t)\,dt = 0.$$

Thus $f \in Y$ and $f'' = g$.

**Conclusion.** For every $g \in C[a,b]$ there exists $f \in Y \subset X$ with $
f'' = g$. Therefore the second derivative operator maps $X$ (restricted to its t
wice-differentiable elements, which is the only subset on which $D^2$ is even de
fined) **onto** $C[a,b]$.

(Remark: the construction also shows $D^2$ is injective on $Y$, since $f''=0$ wi
th $f(a)=f(b)=0$ forces $f \equiv 0$ when $a \neq b$; so $D^2$ is in fact a bije
ction $Y \to C[a,b]$.)
