## Solution

**Step 1: Verify $f$ is a bijection on $[0,1]$.**

We have $f(x)=xe^{x^2-1}$, with $f(0)=0$ and $f(1)=1\cdot e^{0}=1$. Its derivati
ve is
$$f'(x)=e^{x^2-1}+x\cdot 2x\,e^{x^2-1}=e^{x^2-1}(1+2x^2)>0,$$
so $f$ is strictly increasing and continuous, hence a bijection $[0,1]\to[0,1]$.
 Its inverse $f^{-1}$ is well defined.

**Step 2: Compare $f(x)$ and $f^{-1}(x)$ with $x$.**

For $x\in[0,1]$, we have $x^2-1\le 0$, so $e^{x^2-1}\le 1$, giving
$$f(x)=x\,e^{x^2-1}\le x,$$
with equality only at $x=0$ and $x=1$. Thus $f(x)<x$ for all $x\in(0,1)$.

Since $f$ is increasing, $f(x)<x$ implies (applying $f^{-1}$) $x<f^{-1}(x)$. The
refore on $(0,1)$:
$$f(x)<x<f^{-1}(x),$$
so $f^{-1}(x)>f(x)$ and consequently
$$|f(x)-f^{-1}(x)|=f^{-1}(x)-f(x).$$

**Step 3: Use the area identity for the inverse.**

For a strictly increasing bijection $f:[0,1]\to[0,1]$, the area under $f$ plus t
he area under $f^{-1}$ equals the area of the unit square:
$$\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx+\int_0^1 f^{-1}(x)\,dx=1.$$

**Step 4: Compute the integral.**

$$I=\int_0^1\bigl(f^{-1}(x)-f(x)\bigr)\,dx=\int_0^1 f^{-1}(x)\,dx-\int_0^1 f(x)\
,dx.$$

Using the identity from Step 3, $\int_0^1 f^{-1}(x)\,dx=1-\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx$, so
$$I=1-2\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx=1-2\int_0^1 x\,e^{x^2-1}\,dx.$$

Evaluate the remaining integral with $u=x^2-1$, $du=2x\,dx$:
$$\int_0^1 x\,e^{x^2-1}\,dx=\frac12\int_{-1}^{0}e^{u}\,du=\frac12\bigl(1-e^{-1}\
bigr).$$

Therefore
$$I=1-2\cdot\frac12\bigl(1-e^{-1}\bigr)=1-\bigl(1-e^{-1}\bigr)=e^{-1}=\frac{1}{e
}.$$

**Answer:**

$$\boxed{I=\dfrac{1}{e}}$$
