The key tool is the degree formula: $\int_S K \, dA = \deg(N) \cdot 4\pi$.

This formula holds for any compact oriented surface and its Gauss map,
regardless of whether $K = 0$ somewhere. The degree of $N$ is well-defined as

Wait, but we need to be more careful. The degree formula $\int_S K \, dA =
\deg(N) \cdot 4\pi$ is a general result. Let me recall it.

The Gauss-Bonnet theorem says $\int_S K \, dA = 2\pi \chi(S)$. For a compact
connected surface, $\chi(S) = 2 - 2g$ where $g$ is the genus. So $\int_S K \, dA
= 2\pi(2 - 2g) = 4\pi(1 - g)$.

If $S$ is a sphere ($g = 0$), then $\int_S K \, dA = 4\pi$.
