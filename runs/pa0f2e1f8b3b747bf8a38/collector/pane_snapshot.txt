**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

Let $S$ satisfy the hypotheses: $d \geq 2$, $S$ is a finite simplicial complex o
f dimension $2d$, $S$ is simply connected, $\widetilde{H}_i(S;\mathbb{Z}) = 0$ f
or $i \neq d$, and $H_d(S;\mathbb{Z})$ is torsion-free.

**Step 1: $S$ is $(d-1)$-connected.**

We prove by induction that $\pi_i(S) = 0$ for $1 \leq i \leq d-1$.

- **Base case:** $\pi_1(S) = 0$ by hypothesis.
- **Inductive step:** Suppose $\pi_i(S) = 0$ for $i \leq k-1$ where $2 \leq k \l
eq d-1$, so $S$ is $(k-1)$-connected. By the Hurewicz theorem, $\pi_k(S) \cong H
_k(S)$. Since $k < d$, we have $H_k(S) = 0$, so $\pi_k(S) = 0$.

Thus $S$ is $(d-1)$-connected.

**Step 2: $\pi_d(S) \cong H_d(S) \cong \mathbb{Z}^r$.**

Since $S$ is $(d-1)$-connected and $d \geq 2$, the Hurewicz theorem gives $\pi_d
(S) \cong H_d(S;\mathbb{Z})$. Since $H_d(S)$ is torsion-free and finitely genera
ted (as $S$ is a finite complex), $H_d(S) \cong \mathbb{Z}^r$ for some $r \geq 0
$.

**Step 3: Construct a map from a wedge of spheres.**

Choose generators $[\alpha_1], \dots, [\alpha_r]$ of $\pi_d(S) \cong \mathbb{Z}^
r$, represented by maps $\alpha_j : S^d \to S$. Let $W = \bigvee_{j=1}^r S^d$ an
d define $f : W \to S$ by $f|_{S^d_j} = \alpha_j$.

Since $d \geq 2$, the wedge $W$ is simply connected (by van Kampen's theorem app
lied inductively).

**Step 4: $f$ induces an isomorphism on homology.**

We have $H_d(W) \cong \mathbb{Z}^r$ with basis the fundamental classes of the su
mmands, and $H_d(S) \cong \mathbb{Z}^r$ with basis corresponding to the chosen g
enerators of $\pi_d(S)$. Under the Hurewicz isomorphism, the map $f_* : H_d(W) \
to H_d(S)$ sends each summand's fundamental class to the homology class of $\alp
ha_j$, which is exactly the chosen basis. Thus $f_*$ is an isomorphism on $H_d$.

For $i \neq 0, d$: both $\widetilde{H}_i(W)$ and $\widetilde{H}_i(S)$ are zero,
so $f_*$ is trivially an isomorphism. For $i = 0$: both are path-connected, so $
f_*$ is an isomorphism. Hence $f_*$ is an isomorphism on all homology groups.

**Step 5: Apply Whitehead's theorem.**

Both $W$ and $S$ are simply connected CW complexes, and $f : W \to S$ induces an
 isomorphism on all homology groups. By the homology Whitehead theorem (a map be
tween simply connected CW complexes inducing homology isomorphisms is a homotopy
 equivalence), $f$ is a homotopy equivalence.

*(Detailed justification of the homology Whitehead theorem:* Consider the mappin
g cone $C_f = S \cup_f CW$. Since $W$ is simply connected, $CW$ is contractible,
 and by van Kampen, $\pi_1(C_f) = \pi_1(S) *_{\pi_1(W)} \pi_1(CW) = 0$. The long
 exact sequence in homology gives $\widetilde{H}_*(C_f) = 0$. Since $C_f$ is sim
ply connected with trivial reduced homology, by Hurewicz (applied inductively) a
ll homotopy groups of $C_f$ vanish, so $C_f$ is contractible. Hence $f$ is a hom
otopy equivalence.*)

**Conclusion:** $S$ is homotopy equivalent to $W = \bigvee_{j=1}^r S^d$, a wedge
 of $d$-spheres. The dimension $2d$ of $S$ plays no role in the argument — the c
onclusion follows from simple connectivity, $d \geq 2$, homology concentrated in
 degree $d$, and torsion-freeness of $H_d$.
