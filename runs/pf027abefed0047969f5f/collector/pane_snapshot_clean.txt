Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest $k$ such that there exist c
osets $s_i = A_i\text{SO}(3)$ whose simultaneous stabilizer in $\text{SL}(3,\mat

## Setup and Reduction

The stabilizer of $s_i = A_i\text{SO}(3)$ under left multiplication is:
$$\text{Stab}(s_i) = \{g \in \text{SL}(3,\mathbb{R}) : gA_i\text{SO}(3) = A_i\te
xt{SO}(3)\} = A_i\,\text{SO}(3)\,A_i^{-1}.$$

We need $\bigcap_{i=1}^k A_i\,\text{SO}(3)\,A_i^{-1} = \{I\}$.

**Conjugation by $A_1^{-1}$** reduces this to: find the smallest $k$ such that t
here exist $B_2, \ldots, B_k \in \text{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})$ with
$$\text{SO}(3) \cap \bigcap_{i=2}^k B_i\,\text{SO}(3)\,B_i^{-1} = \{I\}.$$

An element $h \in \text{SO}(3)$ lies in $B_i\,\text{SO}(3)\,B_i^{-1}$ iff $B_i^{
-1}hB_i \in \text{SO}(3)$, which (since $h \in \text{SO}(3)$, so $h^T = h^{-1}$)
 is equivalent to $h$ commuting with the positive definite matrix $P_i = (B_i B_
i^T)^{-1}$ (with $\det P_i = 1$).

**Reduced problem:** Find the smallest $k$ such that there exist positive defini
te matrices $P_2, \ldots, P_k$ ($\det = 1$) where the only $h \in \text{SO}(3)$
commuting with all $P_i$ is $h = I$.

## $k = 1$ and $k = 2$ are insufficient

**$k=1$:** No additional forms; the intersection is all of $\text{SO}(3)$. Not t
rivial.

**$k=2$:** One form $P_2$. We need the centralizer $C_{\text{SO}(3)}(P_2) = \{I\
}$. Diagonalize $P_2 = O\,\text{diag}(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)\,O^T$ with
$\lambda_i > 0$, $\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3 = 1$.

- If all $\lambda_i$ are distinct: $h$ commuting with $P_2$ must preserve each 1
-dim eigenspace, so $h = O\,\text{diag}(\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3)\,O^T$
with $\epsilon_i = \pm 1$, $\epsilon_1\epsilon_2\epsilon_3 = 1$. This gives **4
elements** $\{I, g_1, g_2, g_3\}$ (the Klein four-group of 180° rotations).
- If exactly two eigenvalues coincide: the centralizer is isomorphic to $\text{O
}(2)$, which is infinite.
- If all eigenvalues equal ($P_2 = I$): the centralizer is all of $\text{SO}(3)$
.

In every case, $C_{\text{SO}(3)}(P_2) \supsetneq \{I\}$. So $k = 2$ is **insuffi
cient**.

## $k = 3$ is sufficient

Choose $P_2$ with **distinct eigenvalues**. In its eigenbasis, $C_{\text{SO}(3)}
(P_2) = \{I, g_1, g_2, g_3\}$ where:
$$g_1 = \text{diag}(1,-1,-1),\quad g_2 = \text{diag}(-1,1,-1),\quad g_3 = \text{
diag}(-1,-1,1).$$

Now choose $P_3$ (positive definite, $\det = 1$) that commutes with **none** of
$g_1, g_2, g_3$. Take for small $\epsilon > 0$:
$$P_3 = \frac{1}{\alpha}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & \epsilon & \epsilon \\ \epsilon & 1
& \epsilon \\ \epsilon & \epsilon & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \alpha = \big((1+2\ep
silon)(1-\epsilon)^2\big)^{1/3}.$$

This is positive definite for $0 < \epsilon < 1$ (eigenvalues $1+2\epsilon$ and
$1-\epsilon$ with multiplicity 2, all positive), and $\det P_3 = 1$.

**Verification that $g_i$ doesn't commute with $P_3$:** Compute $g_1 P_3$ vs $P_
3 g_1$:
$$g_1 P_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \epsilon & \epsilon \\ -\epsilon & -1 & -\epsilo
n \\ -\epsilon & -\epsilon & -1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad P_3 g_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1
 & -\epsilon & -\epsilon \\ \epsilon & -1 & -\epsilon \\ \epsilon & -\epsilon &
-1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

The $(1,2)$-entries differ ($\epsilon$ vs $-\epsilon$), so $g_1 P_3 \neq P_3 g_1
$. By symmetry, $g_2$ and $g_3$ also fail to commute with $P_3$.

Therefore $C_{\text{SO}(3)}(P_2) \cap C_{\text{SO}(3)}(P_3) = \{I\}$, and the si
multaneous stabilizer is trivial.

## Constructing the original cosets

Set $A_1 = I$ (so $s_1 = \text{SO}(3)$). Choose $A_2 \in \text{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
$ with $(A_2 A_2^T)^{-1} = P_2$ (e.g., $A_2 = P_2^{-1/2}$, which has $\det = 1$)
. Choose $A_3$ similarly with $(A_3 A_3^T)^{-1} = P_3$. Then:
$$\bigcap_{i=1}^3 A_i\,\text{SO}(3)\,A_i^{-1} = \{I\}.$$

## Conclusion

Since $k = 2$ is always insufficient (the centralizer of any single positive def
inite form in $\text{SO}(3)$ is nontrivial) and $k = 3$ is achievable (two gener
ic forms with distinct eigenvalues, chosen so the 180° rotations of the first do
n't commute with the second), the smallest $k$ is:

$$\boxed{k = 3}$$
