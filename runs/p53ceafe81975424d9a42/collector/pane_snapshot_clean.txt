Looking at this problem, I need to find all points P where the median from P to
the midpoint of AB is perpendicular to its isogonal conjugate (the symmedian fro
m P).

## Setup

Place the buoys at **A = (−1, 0)** and **B = (1, 0)**, so the midpoint is **M =
(0, 0)** and |AB| = 2. Let **P = (x, y)** with polar coordinates (r, θ).

Define:
- $\vec{u} = A - P$, $\vec{v} = B - P$ (vectors from P to A, B)
- $p = |PA|$, $q = |PB|$
- Unit vectors: $\hat{u} = \vec{u}/p$, $\hat{v} = \vec{v}/q$

## Isogonal Conjugate (Symmedian) Direction

The **median direction** from P is $\vec{m} \propto \vec{u} + \vec{v} = p\,\hat{
u} + q\,\hat{v}$.

The **angle bisector** at P has direction $\hat{u} + \hat{v}$. Reflecting a dire
ction across the bisector swaps its components in the {$\hat{u}, \hat{v}$} basis
. So the **symmedian direction** is:

$$\vec{s} \propto q\,\hat{u} + p\,\hat{v} = \frac{q}{p}\,\vec{u} + \frac{p}{q}\,
\vec{v}$$

## Perpendicularity Condition

Require $\vec{m} \cdot \vec{s} = 0$:

$$(\vec{u}+\vec{v})\cdot\left(\tfrac{q}{p}\vec{u}+\tfrac{p}{q}\vec{v}\right) = \
frac{q}{p}\,p^2 + \frac{p}{q}\,q^2 + \left(\frac{q}{p}+\frac{p}{q}\right)(\vec{u
}\cdot\vec{v}) = 0$$

$$2pq + \frac{p^2+q^2}{pq}(\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v}) = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \v
ec{u}\cdot\vec{v} = -\frac{2p^2q^2}{p^2+q^2}$$

By the law of cosines with $|AB|=2$: $\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v} = \frac{p^2+q^2-4}{2}$
. Substituting:

$$(p^2+q^2-4)(p^2+q^2) = -4p^2q^2$$

$$(p^2+q^2)^2 - 4(p^2+q^2) + 4p^2q^2 = 0$$

## Converting to Coordinates

With $r^2 = x^2+y^2$:
- $p^2+q^2 = (x+1)^2+y^2+(x-1)^2+y^2 = 2(r^2+1)$
- $p^2q^2 = [(r^2+1)+2x][(r^2+1)-2x] = (r^2+1)^2 - 4x^2$

Substituting:

$$4(r^2+1)^2 - 8(r^2+1) + 4\big[(r^2+1)^2-4x^2\big] = 0$$

$$8(r^2+1)^2 - 8(r^2+1) - 16x^2 = 0$$

$$(r^2+1)^2 - (r^2+1) - 2x^2 = 0$$

$$r^4 + r^2 = 2x^2$$

Using $x = r\cos\theta$ and dividing by $r^2$ (for $r \neq 0$):

$$r^2 + 1 = 2\cos^2\theta \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{r^2 = \cos 2\theta}$$

## Identifying the Curve

This is the **Lemniscate of Bernoulli**, with foci at A and B. It exists where $
\cos 2\theta \geq 0$, i.e., $\theta \in [-\pi/4, \pi/4] \cup [3\pi/4, 5\pi/4]$,
forming two lobes. The curve passes through A = (1,0), B = (−1,0), and M = (0,0)
.

## Computing the Enclosed Area

The total area enclosed by both lobes:

$$A = 2 \times \frac{1}{2}\int_{-\pi/4}^{\pi/4} r^2\,d\theta = \int_{-\pi/4}^{\p
i/4} \cos 2\theta\,d\theta = \left[\frac{\sin 2\theta}{2}\right]_{-\pi/4}^{\pi/4
} = \frac{1}{2}\big(1-(-1)\big) = 1$$

## Result

The patrol region R is the interior of the lemniscate $r^2 = \cos 2\theta$, and
its total area is:

$$\boxed{1 \text{ square nautical mile}}$$
