## Proof

**Goal.** For every integer $n \geq 0$, show
$$S_n \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{n^k}{k!} \;>\; \frac{e^n}{2}.$$

---

**Case $n=0$.** $S_0 = 1 > \tfrac12 = e^0/2$. ✓

---

**Case $n\ge 1$.** Since $e^n = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{n^k}{k!} = S_n + T_n$ w
here $T_n=\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{n^k}{k!}$, the desired inequality $S_n>e^n/
2$ is equivalent to

$$S_n > T_n. \tag{$\star$}$$

**Step 1 – Integral representation.** We claim

$$S_n = \frac{e^n}{n!}\int_n^{\infty} t^n e^{-t}\,dt, \qquad T_n = \frac{e^n}{n!
}\int_0^{n} t^n e^{-t}\,dt. \tag{1}$$

*Proof of (1).* Repeated integration by parts gives
$$\int_{\lambda}^{\infty} t^n e^{-t}\,dt = e^{-\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{n!}{k
!}\lambda^k = n!\,e^{-\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\lambda^k}{k!}.$$
Setting $\lambda = n$ and dividing by $n!$:
$$\frac{1}{n!}\int_n^{\infty} t^n e^{-t}\,dt = e^{-n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{n^k}{k!
} = e^{-n}S_n,$$
so $S_n = \frac{e^n}{n!}\int_n^{\infty}t^n e^{-t}\,dt$. Since $n!=\int_0^{\infty
}t^n e^{-t}\,dt = \int_0^n t^n e^{-t}\,dt + \int_n^{\infty}t^n e^{-t}\,dt$, we g
et $T_n = e^n - S_n = \frac{e^n}{n!}\int_0^n t^n e^{-t}\,dt$. ∎

By (1), $(\star)$ is equivalent to

$$\int_n^{\infty} t^n e^{-t}\,dt \;>\; \int_0^{n} t^n e^{-t}\,dt. \tag{2}$$

**Step 2 – Pointwise comparison.** Substitute $t=n+u$ on the left and $t=n-u$ on
 the right:

$$\text{LHS of (2)} = \int_0^{\infty}(n+u)^n e^{-(n+u)}\,du, \qquad \text{RHS of
 (2)} = \int_0^{n}(n-u)^n e^{-(n-u)}\,du.$$

It suffices to prove, for every $u\in(0,n)$,
$$(n+u)^n e^{-(n+u)} \;>\; (n-u)^n e^{-(n-u)}. \tag{3}$$

Define
$$h(u) \;=\; \ln\!\frac{(n+u)^n e^{-(n+u)}}{(n-u)^n e^{-(n-u)}} \;=\; n\ln\!\fra
c{n+u}{n-u} - 2u, \qquad u\in(0,n).$$

Then $h(0)=0$ and

$$h'(u) \;=\; n\!\left(\frac{1}{n+u}+\frac{1}{n-u}\right) - 2 \;=\; \frac{2n^2}{
n^2-u^2}-2 \;=\; \frac{2u^2}{n^2-u^2} \;>\; 0 \quad\text{for } u\in(0,n).$$

So $h$ is **strictly increasing** on $(0,n)$ with $h(0)=0$; hence $h(u)>0$ for a
ll $u\in(0,n)$, which is exactly (3).

**Step 3 – Conclusion.** Since (3) holds on the open interval $(0,n)$,

$$\int_0^{n}(n+u)^n e^{-(n+u)}\,du \;>\; \int_0^{n}(n-u)^n e^{-(n-u)}\,du,$$

and therefore

$$\int_n^{\infty} t^n e^{-t}\,dt \;\ge\; \int_0^{n}(n+u)^n e^{-(n+u)}\,du \;>\;
\int_0^{n}(n-u)^n e^{-(n-u)}\,du \;=\; \int_0^{n} t^n e^{-t}\,dt,$$

which establishes (2), hence $(\star)$, hence $S_n > e^n/2$. $\blacksquare$
