## Proof

**Claim:** For every bijection $f: \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$, the series $\sum_
{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{f(n)}{n^2}$ diverges (equals $+\infty$). Consequently, for
any $\epsilon > 0$, the number of bijections satisfying $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \fr
ac{f(n)}{n^2} < \epsilon$ is **0**.

### Proof via dyadic decomposition

Partition $\mathbb{N}$ into dyadic blocks $B_k = \{2^k, 2^k+1, \ldots, 2^{k+1}-1
\}$ for $k \geq 0$, where $B_0 = \{1\}$.

**Block $B_0 = \{1\}$:** The single term gives $\frac{f(1)}{1^2} = f(1) \geq 1$.

**Block $B_k$ for $k \geq 1$:** This block contains $|B_k| = 2^k$ consecutive in
tegers. Since $f$ is a bijection, the values $\{f(n) : n \in B_k\}$ are $2^k$ **
distinct** positive integers. The minimum possible sum of $2^k$ distinct positiv
e integers is:
$$\sum_{j=1}^{2^k} j = \frac{2^k(2^k+1)}{2}.$$

For every $n \in B_k$, we have $n \leq 2^{k+1}-1 < 2^{k+1}$, so:
$$\frac{1}{n^2} > \frac{1}{2^{2(k+1)}} = \frac{1}{2^{2k+2}}.$$

Therefore, the contribution of block $B_k$ satisfies:
$$\sum_{n \in B_k} \frac{f(n)}{n^2} \geq \frac{1}{2^{2k+2}} \sum_{n \in B_k} f(n
) \geq \frac{1}{2^{2k+2}} \cdot \frac{2^k(2^k+1)}{2} = \frac{2^k+1}{2^{k+3}} \ge
q \frac{2^k}{2^{k+3}} = \frac{1}{8}.$$

### Summing over all blocks

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{f(n)}{n^2} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \sum_{n \in B_k} \f
rac{f(n)}{n^2} \geq 1 + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{8} = 1 + \infty = +\infty.$
$

Each dyadic block $B_k$ (for $k \geq 1$) contributes at least $\frac{1}{8}$, and
 there are infinitely many such blocks, so the series diverges regardless of the
 choice of bijection $f$.

### Conclusion

Since $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{f(n)}{n^2} = +\infty$ for **every** bijection $
f: \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$, the inequality $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{f(n)}{n
^2} < \epsilon$ is satisfied by **no** bijection, for any finite $\epsilon > 0$.
