Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every loop in the long line
 $L = \omega_1 \times [0,1)$ (with lexicographic order topology) has its image c
ontained in a single fiber $\{r\} \times [0,1)$.

**Answer: False**

**Proof.** I construct an explicit loop in $L$ whose image crosses two distinct
fibers, so it cannot be contained in any single $\{r\} \times [0,1)$.

**Key topological fact.** In the order topology on $L$, the sequence $(0, t)$ co
nverges to $(1, 0)$ as $t \to 1^-$. This is because in the lexicographic order,
$(0, t) < (1, 0)$ for all $t < 1$, and there is no element of $L$ strictly betwe
en $(0, t)$ (for $t$ near $1$) and $(1, 0)$. Thus every open neighborhood of $(1
, 0)$ contains $(0, t)$ for all $t$ sufficiently close to $1$. The same converge
nce holds for any successor ordinal: $(r, t) \to (r+1, 0)$ as $t \to 1^-$.

**Construction of the loop.** Define $f: [0,1] \to L$ by:

$$f(t) = \begin{cases} (0,\; 0.5 + 2t) & t \in [0, \tfrac{1}{4}] \\ (1,\; 2(t -
\tfrac{1}{4})) & t \in [\tfrac{1}{4}, \tfrac{1}{2}] \\ (1,\; 0.5 - 2(t - \tfrac{
1}{2})) & t \in [\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{3}{4}] \\ (0,\; 1 - 2(t - \tfrac{3}{4})) &
 t \in [\tfrac{3}{4}, 1] \end{cases}$$

**Continuity check at the junction points:**

- **At $t = \frac{1}{4}$:** From the left, $f(t) = (0, 0.5 + 2t) \to (0, 1)$, wh
ich converges to $(1, 0)$ in the order topology. From the right, $f(\frac{1}{4})
 = (1, 0)$. Continuous. ✓

- **At $t = \frac{1}{2}$:** Both sides give $(1, 0.5)$. Continuous. ✓

- **At $t = \frac{3}{4}$:** From the left, $f(t) \to (1, 0)$. From the right, $f
(t) = (0, 1 - 2(t - \frac{3}{4})) \to (0, 1)$, which converges to $(1, 0)$ in th
e order topology. Continuous. ✓

- **At $t = 0$ and $t = 1$:** $f(0) = (0, 0.5) = f(1)$, so this is a closed loop
. ✓

**The image spans two fibers.** The image of $f$ contains:
- Points $(0, s)$ for $s \in [0.5, 1)$ — these lie in fiber $\{0\} \times [0,1)$
.
- Points $(1, s)$ for $s \in [0, 0.5]$ — these lie in fiber $\{1\} \times [0,1)$
.

Since the image intersects both $\{0\} \times [0,1)$ and $\{1\} \times [0,1)$, i
t cannot be contained in any single fiber $\{r\} \times [0,1)$.

**Remark.** What *is* true is the weaker statement that every loop in $L$ has **
bounded** image (contained in some initial segment $[0, \alpha) \times [0,1)$ fo
r a countable ordinal $\alpha$). This follows because $S^1$ is Lindelöf, and the
 image of a Lindelöf space under a continuous map into $L$ is bounded (any count
able cofinal subset of $\omega_1$ has a countable supremum, still below $\omega_
1$). However, "bounded" is far weaker than "contained in a single fiber," and th
e counterexample above shows the stronger claim fails.
