## Solution

We minimize $\int_0^1 \left[e^{-(y'-x)} + (1+y)y'\right] dx$ with $y(0)=0$, $y(1
)=\tfrac{1}{2}$.

**Step 1: Simplify the integrand.**

The second term is an exact derivative:
$$(1+y)y' = \frac{d}{dx}\!\left(\frac{(1+y)^2}{2}\right).$$

So its integral depends only on the boundary values of $y$, which are fixed. Min
imizing the full integral is therefore equivalent to minimizing
$$\int_0^1 e^{x - y'}\, dx.$$

**Step 2: Euler–Lagrange equation.**

With $L = e^{x-y'}$ (no explicit dependence on $y$), the Euler–Lagrange equation
 gives
$$\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial y'}\right) = 0, \qquad \frac{\p
artial L}{\partial y'} = -e^{x-y'}.$$

Hence $e^{x-y'} = C$ (constant), so $x - y' = c_0$, giving
$$y' = x - c_0.$$

**Step 3: Integrate and apply boundary conditions.**

$$y = \frac{x^2}{2} - c_0 x + c_1.$$

- $y(0)=0 \Rightarrow c_1 = 0$.
- $y(1)=\tfrac12 \Rightarrow \tfrac12 - c_0 = \tfrac12 \Rightarrow c_0 = 0$.

Thus $y(x) = \dfrac{x^2}{2}$.

**Step 4: Verify minimality.**

The strengthened Legendre condition holds: $\dfrac{\partial^2 L}{\partial y'^2}
= e^{x-y'} > 0$, confirming a minimum.

**Step 5: Evaluate.**

$$y\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{(1/2)^2}{2} = \frac{1}{8}.$$
