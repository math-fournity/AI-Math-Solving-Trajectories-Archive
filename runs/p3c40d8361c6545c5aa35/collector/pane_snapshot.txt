## Proof

We wish to count the number of conjugacy classes of $6\times 6$ complex matrices
 $A$ such that:
1. The characteristic polynomial of $A$ is $(x-5)^6$, and
2. $A$ is **not** conjugate to the diagonal matrix $D = 5I_6$.

**Step 1: Conjugacy over $\mathbb{C}$ is classified by Jordan normal form.**

Over $\mathbb{C}$, every matrix is conjugate to a unique (up to block ordering)
Jordan normal form. Two matrices are conjugate if and only if they have the same
 Jordan normal form (up to permutation of blocks).

**Step 2: The characteristic polynomial $(x-5)^6$ forces a single eigenvalue.**

The characteristic polynomial $(x-5)^6$ has the sole root $\lambda = 5$ with alg
ebraic multiplicity $6$. Hence every Jordan block has eigenvalue $5$, and the su
m of the sizes of all Jordan blocks equals $6$.

**Step 3: Conjugacy classes correspond to partitions of $6$.**

A Jordan form with single eigenvalue $5$ is determined by the multiset of Jordan
 block sizes, which is a partition of $6$. Each distinct partition gives a disti
nct conjugacy class, and every conjugacy class arises this way. Therefore the to
tal number of conjugacy classes with characteristic polynomial $(x-5)^6$ equals
$p(6)$, the number of partitions of $6$.

**Step 4: Enumerate the partitions of $6$.**

The partitions of $6$ are:

| # | Partition | Jordan block structure |
|---|-----------|----------------------|
| 1 | $(6)$ | one $6\times 6$ block |
| 2 | $(5,1)$ | one $5\times 5$, one $1\times 1$ |
| 3 | $(4,2)$ | one $4\times 4$, one $2\times 2$ |
| 4 | $(4,1,1)$ | one $4\times 4$, two $1\times 1$ |
| 5 | $(3,3)$ | two $3\times 3$ blocks |
| 6 | $(3,2,1)$ | one $3\times 3$, one $2\times 2$, one $1\times 1$ |
| 7 | $(3,1,1,1)$ | one $3\times 3$, three $1\times 1$ |
| 8 | $(2,2,2)$ | three $2\times 2$ blocks |
| 9 | $(2,2,1,1)$ | two $2\times 2$, two $1\times 1$ |
| 10 | $(2,1,1,1,1)$ | one $2\times 2$, four $1\times 1$ |
| 11 | $(1,1,1,1,1,1)$ | six $1\times 1$ blocks (diagonal) |

Thus $p(6) = 11$.

**Step 5: Exclude the diagonalizable case.**

A matrix with characteristic polynomial $(x-5)^6$ is diagonalizable if and only
if every Jordan block has size $1$, i.e., the partition is $(1,1,1,1,1,1)$. This
 is the unique conjugacy class of the diagonal matrix $D = 5I_6$. The problem ex
plicitly excludes this case.

**Step 6: Conclusion.**

The number of non-conjugate $6\times 6$ complex matrices with characteristic pol
ynomial $(x-5)^6$ that are **not** diagonalizable to $5I_6$ is:

$$p(6) - 1 = 11 - 1 = \boxed{10}.$$
