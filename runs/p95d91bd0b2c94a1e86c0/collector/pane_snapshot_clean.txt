# Solution: Traveling Salesperson Optimization Problem

## Problem Restatement

Given a complete weighted undirected graph on $n$ vertices with edge weights $d(
i,j) \ge 0$, find a Hamiltonian cycle (a tour visiting every vertex exactly once
 and returning to the start) of minimum total weight.

## Part 1 — NP-Hardness (no polynomial-time exact algorithm is known unless P =
NP)

**Theorem.** TSP-Optimization is NP-hard.

*Proof by reduction from Hamiltonian Cycle (HC).*

Given an unweighted graph $G = (V, E)$ with $|V| = n$, construct a complete weig
hted graph $G'$ on the same vertex set with weights

$$
d(i,j) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } \{i,j\} \in E \\ 2 & \text{if } \{i,j\} \n
otin E. \end{cases}
$$

$G$ has a Hamiltonian cycle $\iff$ $G'$ has a tour of total weight $n$.

- ($\Rightarrow$) A Hamiltonian cycle in $G$ uses $n$ edges all of weight $1$, g
iving a tour of weight $n$ in $G'$.
- ($\Leftarrow$) Any tour in $G'$ uses exactly $n$ edges, each of weight $\ge 1$
. If the tour has weight $n$, every edge has weight $1$, i.e. every edge lies in
 $E$, so the tour is a Hamiltonian cycle of $G$.

Since HC is NP-complete, deciding whether the optimum is $\le n$ is NP-hard, so
optimizing TSP is NP-hard. $\square$

Consequently, **no polynomial-time exact algorithm is known**, and finding one w
ould imply P = NP. The best known exact algorithms run in exponential time.

## Part 2 — Exact Algorithm (Held–Karp Dynamic Programming)

The optimal exact algorithm known is Held–Karp, running in $O(2^n n^2)$ time and
 $O(2^n n)$ space. I give the algorithm and prove its correctness.

### Setup

Label vertices $1, 2, \dots, n$; fix vertex $1$ as the start/end. Let $V' = \{2,
 \dots, n\}$. For a subset $S \subseteq V'$ and a vertex $k \in S$, define

$$
g(S, k) = \text{minimum weight of a path that starts at } 1,\text{ visits exactl
y the vertices of } S,\text{ and ends at } k.
$$

(Here "visits exactly $S$" means the internal vertices are precisely $S$, with $
1$ as the start and $k$ as the end.)

### Recurrence

**Base case** ($|S| = 1$, i.e. $S = \{k\}$):

$$
g(\{k\}, k) = d(1, k).
$$

**Inductive step** ($|S| \ge 2$, $k \in S$):

$$
g(S, k) = \min_{j \in S \setminus \{k\}} \bigl[\, g(S \setminus \{k\},\, j) + d(
j, k) \,\bigr].
$$

**Answer:**

$$
\mathrm{OPT} = \min_{k \in V'} \bigl[\, g(V', k) + d(k, 1) \,\bigr].
$$

### Correctness Proof

**Claim.** For every $S \subseteq V'$ and $k \in S$, $g(S,k)$ as computed by the
 recurrence equals the minimum weight of a path starting at $1$, visiting exactl
y the vertices of $S$, and ending at $k$.

*Proof by strong induction on $|S|$.*

**Base case** $|S| = 1$, $S = \{k\}$: The only path from $1$ visiting exactly $\
{k\}$ and ending at $k$ is the direct edge $1 \to k$, of weight $d(1,k)$. So $g(
\{k\},k) = d(1,k)$ is correct.

**Inductive step**: Assume the claim holds for all subsets of size $< |S|$; prov
e it for $S$ with $|S| \ge 2$ and $k \in S$.

Let $P$ be an optimal path $1 \to \cdots \to k$ visiting exactly $S$. Let $j$ be
 the vertex immediately preceding $k$ in $P$. Then $j \in S \setminus \{k\}$, an
d the prefix $P'$ of $P$ ending at $j$ is a path from $1$ visiting exactly $S \s
etminus \{k\}$ and ending at $j$. Therefore

$$
\mathrm{wt}(P) = \mathrm{wt}(P') + d(j,k) \ge g(S \setminus \{k\}, j) + d(j,k).
$$

Conversely, for any $j \in S \setminus \{k\}$, take an optimal path realizing $g
(S \setminus \{k\}, j)$ (which exists by the inductive hypothesis) and append th
e edge $j \to k$. This yields a valid path from $1$ visiting exactly $S$ and end
ing at $k$, of weight $g(S \setminus \{k\}, j) + d(j,k)$. Hence

$$
g(S,k) \le g(S \setminus \{k\}, j) + d(j,k) \quad \text{for every } j \in S \set
minus \{k\}.
$$

Combining the two inequalities and minimizing over $j$:

$$
g(S,k) = \min_{j \in S \setminus \{k\}} \bigl[\, g(S \setminus \{k\}, j) + d(j,k
) \,\bigr],
$$

which is exactly the recurrence. $\square$

**Theorem (answer correctness).** $\mathrm{OPT} = \min_{k \in V'} [g(V', k) + d(
k,1)]$ is the minimum tour weight.

*Proof.* Every tour is a cycle $1 \to \cdots \to k \to 1$ for some $k \in V'$, w
here the prefix $1 \to \cdots \to k$ visits exactly $V'$. Such a prefix has weig
ht $\ge g(V', k)$, so the tour has weight $\ge g(V',k) + d(k,1) \ge \mathrm{OPT}
$. Conversely, for the minimizing $k$, appending edge $k \to 1$ to an optimal pa
th realizing $g(V',k)$ gives a tour of weight $\mathrm{OPT}$. Hence $\mathrm{OPT
}$ is achievable and is a lower bound, so it is the minimum. $\square$

### Complexity

- Number of states $(S, k)$: $\sum_{m=1}^{n-1} \binom{n-1}{m} m = (n-1) \cdot 2^
{n-2}$, i.e. $O(2^n n)$.
- Each state's transition examines $|S|-1 = O(n)$ predecessors.
- **Time:** $O(2^n n^2)$. **Space:** $O(2^n n)$.

This is exponentially faster than the brute-force $O(n!)$ enumeration of all per
mutations, and it is the best known exact algorithm for general TSP.

## Summary

- TSP-Optimization is **NP-hard** (reduction from Hamiltonian Cycle), so no poly
nomial-time exact algorithm is expected.
- The **Held–Karp dynamic program** solves it exactly in $O(2^n n^2)$ time and $
O(2^n n)$ space, with correctness proved by induction on subset size: the recurr
ence captures exactly the optimal substructure where the last edge into $k$ is r
emoved, leaving an optimal path over $S \setminus \{k\}$ ending at some predeces
sor $j$.
