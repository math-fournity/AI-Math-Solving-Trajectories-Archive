## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, there exists a non-commutative ring $R$ such that $R^m \cong R^n
$ as left $R$-modules for distinct $m, n$.

**Construction.** Let $k$ be a field and let $V$ be an infinite-dimensional vect
or space over $k$ (e.g., $V = k^{(\mathbb{N})}$, the space of finitely-supported
 sequences). Set

$$R = \operatorname{End}_k(V),$$

the ring of $k$-linear endomorphisms of $V$, with multiplication given by compos
ition. We will show that $R \cong R^2$ as left $R$-modules (i.e., $m = 1$, $n =
2$).

**Step 1: $R$ is non-commutative.** Since $V$ is infinite-dimensional, $\dim_k V
 \geq 2$. Take a basis $\{e_i\}_{i \in I}$ of $V$ with at least two elements $e_
1, e_2$. Define $f, g \in R$ by $f(e_1) = e_2$, $f(e_i) = 0$ for $i \neq 1$, and
 $g(e_2) = e_1$, $g(e_i) = 0$ for $i \neq 2$. Then $(f \circ g)(e_2) = f(e_1) =
e_2$ while $(g \circ f)(e_1) = g(e_2) = e_1$, so $f \circ g \neq g \circ f$. Hen
ce $R$ is non-commutative.

**Step 2: A key $k$-linear isomorphism.** Since $V$ is infinite-dimensional, $\d
im_k V = \dim_k V + \dim_k V$, so there exists a $k$-linear isomorphism

$$\psi : V \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} V \oplus V.$$

**Step 3: Identify $R$ with $\operatorname{Hom}_k(V \oplus V, V)$ as left $R$-mo
dules.** The left $R$-module structure on both $\operatorname{Hom}_k(V, V)$ and
$\operatorname{Hom}_k(V \oplus V, V)$ is given by post-composition: for $r \in R
$ and $h$ in either space, $r \cdot h = r \circ h$.

Define

$$\Psi : \operatorname{Hom}_k(V \oplus V,\, V) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom
}_k(V,\, V) = R, \qquad \Psi(g) = g \circ \psi.$$

- **$k$-linear isomorphism:** Pre-composition by the $k$-linear isomorphism $\ps
i$ is a $k$-linear isomorphism on Hom-spaces. ✓
- **$R$-linear:** $\Psi(r \cdot g) = \Psi(r \circ g) = (r \circ g) \circ \psi =
r \circ (g \circ \psi) = r \cdot \Psi(g)$. ✓

So $R \cong \operatorname{Hom}_k(V \oplus V, V)$ as left $R$-modules.

**Step 4: Identify $\operatorname{Hom}_k(V \oplus V, V)$ with $R^2$ as left $R$-
modules.** Let $\iota_1, \iota_2 : V \to V \oplus V$ be the two coordinate inclu
sions. Define

$$\Theta : \operatorname{Hom}_k(V \oplus V,\, V) \longrightarrow \operatorname{H
om}_k(V, V) \oplus \operatorname{Hom}_k(V, V) = R \oplus R = R^2, \qquad \Theta(
g) = (g \circ \iota_1,\; g \circ \iota_2).$$

- **$k$-linear isomorphism:** This is the standard universal property of the dir
ect sum in the first argument of Hom: $\operatorname{Hom}_k(V \oplus V, V) \cong
 \operatorname{Hom}_k(V, V) \oplus \operatorname{Hom}_k(V, V)$. ✓
- **$R$-linear:** $\Theta(r \cdot g) = \Theta(r \circ g) = (r \circ g \circ \iot
a_1,\; r \circ g \circ \iota_2) = (r \circ (g \circ \iota_1),\; r \circ (g \circ
 \iota_2)) = r \cdot (g \circ \iota_1, g \circ \iota_2) = r \cdot \Theta(g)$. ✓

So $\operatorname{Hom}_k(V \oplus V, V) \cong R^2$ as left $R$-modules.

**Step 5: Conclusion.** Composing the two $R$-linear isomorphisms from Steps 3 a
nd 4:

$$R \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; \operatorname{Hom}_k(V \oplus V, V) \;\xrightarro
w{\;\sim\;}\; R^2,$$

we obtain $R \cong R^2$ as left $R$-modules, with $m = 1$ and $n = 2$ distinct.

By induction, $R \cong R^n$ for every $n \geq 1$, so in fact $R^m \cong R^n$ for
 all $m, n \geq 1$.

Therefore, such a non-commutative ring exists. $\blacksquare$
