## Solution

**Contour:** $|z - 1/2| = 3/2$ is a circle centered at $z = 1/2$ with radius $3/
2$. On the real axis it spans from $-1$ to $2$.

**Integrand analysis:** $f(z) = \dfrac{\tan z}{z} = \dfrac{\sin z}{z \cos z}$.

**Singularities:**

1. **At $z = 0$:** Although $1/z$ has a pole here, $\tan z$ has a zero: $\tan z
= z + \frac{z^3}{3} + \cdots$, so
$$\frac{\tan z}{z} = 1 + \frac{z^2}{3} + \cdots$$
   This is analytic at $z=0$ — **not a pole**.

2. **Poles of $\tan z$:** occur where $\cos z = 0$, i.e., $z = \frac{\pi}{2} + k
\pi$, $k \in \mathbb{Z}$. These are all real.

**Which poles lie inside $|z - 1/2| < 3/2$?**

- $k = 0$: $z = \pi/2 \approx 1.571$. Distance: $|\pi/2 - 1/2| = \pi/2 - 1/2 \ap
prox 1.071 < 1.5$. ✅ **Inside.**
- $k = -1$: $z = -\pi/2 \approx -1.571$. Distance: $|-\pi/2 - 1/2| = \pi/2 + 1/2
 \approx 2.071 > 1.5$. ❌ Outside.
- $k = 1$: $z = 3\pi/2 \approx 4.712$. Distance $\approx 4.21 > 1.5$. ❌ Outside
.
- All other $k$ give even larger distances. ❌ Outside.

So the **only** pole inside the contour is $z = \pi/2$.

**Computing the residue at $z = \pi/2$:**

Near $z = \pi/2$, write $z = \pi/2 + w$ with $w \to 0$:
$$\cos(\pi/2 + w) = -\sin w \approx -w, \qquad \sin(\pi/2 + w) = \cos w \approx
1$$
$$\tan(\pi/2 + w) = \frac{\sin(\pi/2+w)}{\cos(\pi/2+w)} \approx \frac{1}{-w} = -
\frac{1}{w}$$

So $\text{Res}(\tan z,\, \pi/2) = -1$.

Since $1/z$ is analytic and nonzero at $z = \pi/2$:
$$\text{Res}\!\left(\frac{\tan z}{z},\, \frac{\pi}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{\pi/2}\c
dot \text{Res}(\tan z,\, \pi/2) = \frac{2}{\pi}\cdot(-1) = -\frac{2}{\pi}$$

**Applying the residue theorem:**
$$\oint_{|z-1/2|=3/2} \frac{\tan z}{z}\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot \text{Res}\!\left(\fra
c{\tan z}{z},\, \frac{\pi}{2}\right) = 2\pi i \cdot \left(-\frac{2}{\pi}\right)
= -4i$$

## Result

$$\boxed{\oint_{|z-1/2|=3/2} \frac{\tan z}{z}\, dz = -4i}$$
