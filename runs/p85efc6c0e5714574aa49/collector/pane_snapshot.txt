Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $\Gamma E$ has full measure
 $2\pi$ on $S^1$.

**Answer: Yes, $\Gamma E$ has measure $2\pi$.**

## Proof

**Setup.** Identify $S^1$ with $[0, 2\pi)$ via $\theta \mapsto e^{i\theta}$, equ
ipped with normalized Lebesgue (arc-length) measure, total measure $2\pi$. The g
roup $\Gamma$ of roots of unity is the torsion subgroup of $S^1$; it is countabl
e and dense. Under the angle parametrization, $\Gamma = \{2\pi r \pmod{2\pi} : r
 \in \mathbb{Q}\}$.

**Step 1: $\Gamma E$ is measurable.** Since $\Gamma$ is countable,
$$\Gamma E = \bigcup_{\gamma \in \Gamma} \gamma E$$
is a countable union of rotations of the measurable set $E$, hence measurable. M
oreover $m(\Gamma E) \geq m(E) > 0$ since $1 \in \Gamma$ gives $E \subseteq \Gam
ma E$.

**Step 2: The complement is $\Gamma$-invariant.** Let $F = S^1 \setminus \Gamma
E$. Then $F$ is measurable. For any $\gamma \in \Gamma$,
$$\gamma F = \gamma(S^1 \setminus \Gamma E) = S^1 \setminus \gamma\Gamma E = S^1
 \setminus \Gamma E = F,$$
since $\gamma\Gamma = \Gamma$ (as $\Gamma$ is a group). So $F$ is $\Gamma$-invar
iant: $\chi_F(\theta + 2\pi r) = \chi_F(\theta)$ for all $r \in \mathbb{Q}$.

**Step 3: A measurable $\Gamma$-invariant set has measure $0$ or $2\pi$.** Consi
der the Fourier coefficients of $\chi_F \in L^2(S^1)$:
$$\hat{\chi}_F(n) = \int_0^{2\pi} \chi_F(\theta)\, e^{-in\theta}\, d\theta, \qqu
ad n \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

By $\Gamma$-invariance, for any rational $p/q$,
$$\hat{\chi}_F(n) = \int_0^{2\pi} \chi_F(\theta + 2\pi p/q)\, e^{-in\theta}\, d\
theta = \int_0^{2\pi} \chi_F(\phi)\, e^{-in(\phi - 2\pi p/q)}\, d\phi = e^{2\pi
i np/q}\, \hat{\chi}_F(n).$$

Thus $(1 - e^{2\pi i np/q})\,\hat{\chi}_F(n) = 0$ for all $p/q \in \mathbb{Q}$.

For $n \neq 0$, choose $q = |n|+1$, $p = 1$. Then $n/q = n/(|n|+1) \notin \mathb
b{Z}$, so $e^{2\pi i n/(|n|+1)} \neq 1$, forcing $\hat{\chi}_F(n) = 0$.

Therefore all nonzero Fourier coefficients of $\chi_F$ vanish, so $\chi_F$ is co
nstant a.e. This means $m(F) \in \{0, 2\pi\}$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.** Since $m(\Gamma E) \geq m(E) > 0$, we have $m(F) = 2\pi
- m(\Gamma E) < 2\pi$. By Step 3, $m(F) = 0$. Hence
$$m(\Gamma E) = 2\pi. \qquad \blacksquare$$
