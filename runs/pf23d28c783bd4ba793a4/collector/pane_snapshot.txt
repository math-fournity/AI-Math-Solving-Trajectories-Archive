## Proof

$D$ is compact, $D$ is Hausdorff, and $f: D \to D$ is a continuous bijection. Th

### Step 2: $f$ maps boundary to boundary and interior to interior

For $p \in \operatorname{int}(D)$, the local homology $H_2(D, D \setminus \{p\})
 \cong \mathbb{Z}$ (a neighborhood of $p$ in $D$ is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^
2$). For $p \in \partial D$, a neighborhood of $p$ in $D$ is homeomorphic to a c
losed half-plane, and $H_2(D, D \setminus \{p\}) = 0$. Since $f$ is a homeomorph

$$f(\operatorname{int}(D)) = \operatorname{int}(D), \qquad f(\partial D) = \part

### Step 3: $f^{-1}$ is smooth on the interior

Let $p \in \operatorname{int}(D)$. Since $f$ is smooth with $df_p$ invertible, t
he inverse function theorem (applied to a smooth extension of $f$ to an open nei

 \operatorname{int}(D)$ (by injectivity of $f$). Hence $f^{-1}$ is smooth at $f(
p)$. Since $f(\operatorname{int}(D)) = \operatorname{int}(D)$, this covers all i
nterior points.

### Step 4: $f^{-1}$ is smooth at boundary points

Let $p \in \partial D$ and $q = f(p) \in \partial D$.

Since $f$ is smooth on $D$, it extends to a smooth map $\tilde{f}: U \to \mathbb

By the inverse function theorem applied at $p \in U'$, there exist open neighbor

$\tilde{f}|_V: V \to W$ is a diffeomorphism, with smooth inverse $\tilde{g}: W \

Now I show $\tilde{g}$ agrees with $f^{-1}$ on a neighborhood of $q$ in $D$. Sin
ce $f^{-1}$ is continuous (Step 1) and $V$ is an open neighborhood of $p = f^{-1

$$f^{-1}(W' \cap D) \subset V.$$

For any $y \in W' \cap D$: let $x' = f^{-1}(y) \in V \cap D$. Then $\tilde{f}(x'

Therefore $f^{-1}$ agrees with the smooth map $\tilde{g}$ on $W' \cap D$, a neig
hborhood of $q$ in $D$. This means $f^{-1}$ is smooth at $q$ (in the manifold-wi

ighborhood of $q$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$).

### Step 5: Conclusion

- $f^{-1}$ is smooth on $\operatorname{int}(D)$ (Step 3).
- $f^{-1}$ is smooth at every point of $\partial D$ (Step 4).

Hence $f^{-1}: D \to D$ is smooth. Since $f$ is smooth, bijective, and has a smo
oth inverse, $f$ is a diffeomorphism of the closed disk. $\blacksquare$
