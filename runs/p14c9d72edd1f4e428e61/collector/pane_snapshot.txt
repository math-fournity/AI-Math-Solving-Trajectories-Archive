## Solution

The error density is $f(x)=\frac{1}{4}e^{-|x|/2}$, which is a **Laplace (double-
exponential) distribution** with location $0$ and scale $b=2$ (since the standar
d form is $\frac{1}{2b}e^{-|x|/b}$).

### Step 1: Mean and variance of a single error

For a Laplace$(0, b)$ distribution:
- Mean: $\mu = E[X] = 0$ (symmetric about 0)
- Variance: $\sigma^2 = \operatorname{Var}(X) = 2b^2 = 2(2)^2 = 8$

### Step 2: Distribution of the total error

Let $S = \sum_{i=1}^{100} X_i$ be the total error (sum of 100 independent errors
). Then
- $E[S] = 100 \cdot 0 = 0$
- $\operatorname{Var}(S) = 100 \cdot 8 = 800$
- $\sigma_S = \sqrt{800} = 20\sqrt{2}$

### Step 3: Apply the Central Limit Theorem

By the CLT, $S \approx N(0, 800)$. We want
$$P(|S| > 50) = P(S > 50) + P(S < -50).$$

Standardizing with $Z = \frac{S - 0}{\sqrt{800}} = \frac{S}{20\sqrt{2}}$:
$$P(|S| > 50) = 2\,P\!\left(Z > \frac{50}{20\sqrt{2}}\right) = 2\,P\!\left(Z > \
frac{5}{2\sqrt{2}}\right) = 2\,P\!\left(Z > \frac{5\sqrt{2}}{4}\right).$$

### Step 4: Numerical evaluation

$$\frac{5\sqrt{2}}{4} = \frac{5 \times 1.41421}{4} \approx 1.7678.$$

Using the standard normal table, $\Phi(1.77) \approx 0.9616$, so
$$P(Z > 1.7678) \approx 1 - 0.9616 = 0.0384.$$

Therefore
$$P(|S| > 50) \approx 2 \times 0.0384 = \boxed{0.077}.$$

(More precisely, $\Phi(1.7678) \approx 0.9614$, giving $P(|S|>50) \approx 0.0772
$.)
