## Proof

Let $X, Y$ be Banach spaces and $T: X \to Y$ a continuous (i.e., bounded) linear
 operator. The hypothesis is:

> (H) For every sequence $(x_n)$ in $X$ with $\|x_n\| = 1$, the sequence $(\|Tx_
n\|)$ does **not** converge to $0$.

We prove that (H) implies $T$ is bounded below, i.e., there exists $C > 0$ with
$\|Tx\| \ge C\|x\|$ for all $x \in X$.

**Key equivalence.** Condition (H) is equivalent to
$$\inf_{\|x\|=1} \|Tx\| > 0. \tag{$\star$}$$

*Proof of equivalence.*

**($\Leftarrow$)** Suppose $\inf_{\|x\|=1}\|Tx\| = c > 0$. For any sequence $(x_
n)$ with $\|x_n\| = 1$, we have $\|Tx_n\| \ge c$ for every $n$, so $\|Tx_n\| \no
t\to 0$. Thus (H) holds.

**($\Rightarrow$)** Suppose, for contradiction, that $\inf_{\|x\|=1}\|Tx\| = 0$.
 Then for each $n \in \mathbb{N}$, by definition of the infimum, there exists $x
_n \in X$ with $\|x_n\| = 1$ and $\|Tx_n\| < 1/n$. This produces a sequence of u
nit vectors with $\|Tx_n\| \to 0$, contradicting (H). Hence $(\star)$ holds.

**From $(\star)$ to bounded below.** Set $C := \inf_{\|x\|=1}\|Tx\|$, which is p
ositive by $(\star)$. For any $x \in X$ with $x \neq 0$, let $u := x/\|x\|$, so
$\|u\| = 1$. By linearity of $T$,
$$Tx = T(\|x\| \cdot u) = \|x\| \, Tu,$$
hence
$$\|Tx\| = \|x\|\,\|Tu\| \ge \|x\| \cdot \inf_{\|v\|=1}\|Tv\| = C\|x\|.$$
For $x = 0$ the inequality $\|Tx\| \ge C\|x\|$ holds trivially. Therefore
$$\|Tx\| \ge C\|x\| \quad \text{for all } x \in X,$$
with $C > 0$, which is precisely the statement that $T$ is bounded below.

**Conclusion.** The answer is **yes**: under the given hypothesis, $T$ must be b
ounded below. The hypothesis that no sequence of unit vectors maps to a sequence
 converging to $0$ is exactly the statement that the infimum of $\|Tx\|$ over th
e unit sphere is strictly positive, which (by linearity and homogeneity) is exac
tly the statement that $T$ is bounded below.
