**Claim.** The topological vector spaces $\mathbb{R}$ (with its usual topology)
and $\mathbb{R}^2$ (with its usual topology) are not homeomorphic.

*Proof.* Both $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{R}^2$ are topological vector spaces over
 $\mathbb{R}$: vector addition and scalar multiplication are continuous in the s
tandard Euclidean topologies.

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a homeomorphism $f : \mathbb{R} \t
o \mathbb{R}^2$. Then the restriction
$$f\big|_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}} : \mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\} \to \mathbb{R}^2\
setminus\{f(0)\}$$
is also a homeomorphism (a restriction of a homeomorphism to a subspace, with th
e image as the codomain, is a homeomorphism).

Now observe:
- $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\} = (-\infty, 0) \cup (0, +\infty)$ is **disconnected*
* (it is the union of two disjoint nonempty open sets).
- $\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{p\}$ for any point $p$ is **path-connected** (hence co
nnected): for any two points $a, b \in \mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{p\}$, one can alwa
ys find a polygonal path from $a$ to $b$ avoiding $p$ (e.g., if the straight seg
ment $[a,b]$ passes through $p$, detour around $p$ via a third point not colline
ar with $a, b, p$).

Connectedness is a topological invariant: homeomorphic spaces are either both co
nnected or both disconnected. This is a contradiction, since $\mathbb{R}\setminu
s\{0\}$ is disconnected while $\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{f(0)\}$ is connected.

Therefore no such homeomorphism exists, and $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{R}^2$ are
not homeomorphic. $\blacksquare$

**No, not all topological vector spaces are homeomorphic to each other.**
