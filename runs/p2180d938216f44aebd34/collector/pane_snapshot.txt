**Answer: Yes.**

We show that condition (2) alone — every nonzero nonunit element of $R$ admits a
 unique factorization into prime elements — already implies that $R$ is a UFD, f

### Step 1: Unique factorization into primes implies $R$ is a UFD.

Recall that in an integral domain, every **prime element** is **irreducible**: i
f $p = ab$ with $p$ prime, then $p \mid ab$ so $p \mid a$ or $p \mid b$; say $p
\mid a$, so $a = pc$, giving $p = pcb$, hence $cb = 1$ (since $R$ is a domain),
so $b$ is a unit.

The hypothesis states that every nonzero nonunit $x \in R$ can be written as
$$x = p_1 p_2 \cdots p_n$$
where each $p_i$ is a prime element, and this factorization is unique up to unit
s and reordering.

Since each $p_i$ is prime, it is irreducible. Thus every nonzero nonunit is a pr
oduct of irreducibles (**existence**), and the factorization is unique up to uni
ts and order (**uniqueness**). This is precisely the definition of a **unique fa
ctorization domain (UFD)**.

### Step 2: UFD implies $R$ is Atomic.

A domain is **atomic** if every nonzero nonunit is a product of irreducible elem
ents. Since prime elements are irreducible, and every nonzero nonunit is a produ
ct of primes (hence a product of irreducibles), $R$ is atomic. $\checkmark$

### Step 3: UFD implies $R$ is a GCD domain.

Let $a, b \in R \setminus \{0\}$. Using the unique prime factorization, write:
$$a = u \prod_{i} p_i^{\alpha_i}, \qquad b = v \prod_{i} p_i^{\beta_i}$$
where $u, v \in U(R)$, the $p_i$ range over a fixed set of representatives of pr
ime elements (up to associates), and $\alpha_i, \beta_i \geq 0$ with only finite
ly many nonzero.

Define:
$$d = \prod_{i} p_i^{\min(\alpha_i, \beta_i)}.$$

**$d$ divides both $a$ and $b$:** Since $\min(\alpha_i, \beta_i) \leq \alpha_i$
and $\leq \beta_i$, we have $d \mid a$ and $d \mid b$.

**$d$ is the greatest common divisor:** Suppose $c \mid a$ and $c \mid b$. Write
 $c = w \prod_i p_i^{\gamma_i}$. Then $c \mid a$ forces $\gamma_i \leq \alpha_i$
 and $c \mid b$ forces $\gamma_i \leq \beta_i$, so $\gamma_i \leq \min(\alpha_i,
 \beta_i)$ for all $i$. Hence $c \mid d$.

Thus $\gcd(a, b)$ exists for every pair of nonzero elements, so $R$ is a **GCD d
omain**. $\checkmark$

### Step 4: Consistency with condition (1).

If $R$ is a UFD with prime elements $\{p_i\}_{i \in I}$, then every nonzero elem

$$x = u \cdot \prod_{i \in I} p_i^{n_i}, \quad u \in U(R), \quad n_i \in \mathbb

with only finitely many $n_i \neq 0$. The map $x \mapsto (n_i)_{i \in I}$ gives
an isomorphism $G^*/U(R) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{(I)}$, which is a **free abelian grou

ually reinforcing and neither introduces any obstruction.

Under the hypothesis that every nonzero nonunit element of $R$ factors uniquely
into prime elements, $R$ is a UFD. Every UFD is both an Atomic domain and a GCD
domain. The additional hypothesis that $G/U(R)$ is free abelian is consistent wi

$R$ is both an Atomic domain and a GCD domain.
