# Representability of the functor $F$

**Claim.** The functor $F:\mathbf{Grp}\to\mathbf{Set}$ defined by
$$F(G)=\{\,\varphi:G_0\to G \mid \varphi(r)=1\text{ for all }r\in R\,\}$$
is representable.

---

## Setup and notation

Let $G_0$ be a group and $R\subseteq G_0$ a subset. Let $\langle\!\langle R\rang
le\!\rangle\trianglelefteq G_0$ denote the **normal closure** of $R$ in $G_0$, i
.e. the smallest normal subgroup of $G_0$ containing $R$:
$$\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle=\bigl\langle\,grg^{-1}\mid g\in G_0,\ r\in
R\,\bigr\rangle.$$

Define the quotient group
$$H:=G_0/\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle,$$
and let $\pi:G_0\twoheadrightarrow H$ be the canonical projection.

---

## Key observation

A homomorphism $\varphi:G_0\to G$ satisfies $\varphi(r)=1$ for every $r\in R$ if
 and only if $R\subseteq\ker\varphi$. Since $\ker\varphi\trianglelefteq G_0$ is
a normal subgroup, this is equivalent to
$$\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle\subseteq\ker\varphi.$$

By the **universal property of the quotient** (first isomorphism theorem for gro
ups), homomorphisms $\varphi:G_0\to G$ with $\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle\
subseteq\ker\varphi$ are in bijection with homomorphisms $\bar\varphi:H\to G$, t
he bijection being
$$\bar\varphi\;\longmapsto\;\bar\varphi\circ\pi.$$

Concretely, every such $\varphi$ factors uniquely through $\pi$:
$$\varphi=\bar\varphi\circ\pi\qquad\text{for a unique }\bar\varphi\in\operatorna
me{Hom}(H,G).$$

Hence, for every group $G$,
$$F(G)\;\cong\;\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Grp}}(H,G).$$

---

## Naturality

The isomorphism $\eta_G:F(G)\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\operatorname{Hom}(H,G)$, give
n by $\eta_G(\varphi)=\bar\varphi$ (the unique factorization through $\pi$), is
natural in $G$.

Indeed, for any group homomorphism $f:G\to G'$, the square
$$\require{AMScd}
\begin{CD}
F(G) @>{\eta_G}>> \operatorname{Hom}(H,G)\\
@V{F(f)VV} @V{f\circ(-)VV}\\
F(G') @>{\eta_{G'}}>> \operatorname{Hom}(H,G')
\end{CD}$$
commutes: for $\varphi\in F(G)$ with factorization $\varphi=\bar\varphi\circ\pi$
,
$$\bigl(f\circ(-)\bigr)\circ\eta_G(\varphi)=f\circ\bar\varphi,$$
while
$$\eta_{G'}\circ F(f)(\varphi)=\eta_{G'}(f\circ\varphi)=\eta_{G'}\bigl((f\circ\b
ar\varphi)\circ\pi\bigr)=f\circ\bar\varphi,$$
since $f\circ\bar\varphi$ is the unique factorization of $f\circ\varphi$ through
 $\pi$. The two agree, so $\eta$ is a natural isomorphism.

---

## Conclusion

We have exhibited a natural isomorphism
$$F\;\cong\;\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Grp}}(H,-),\qquad H=G_0/\langle\!\langle
 R\rangle\!\rangle.$$

By definition, a functor $F:\mathbf{Grp}\to\mathbf{Set}$ is **representable** pr
ecisely when there exists a group $H$ with $F\cong\operatorname{Hom}(H,-)$. Ther
efore:

> **$F$ is representable, represented by the group $H=G_0/\langle\!\langle R\ran
gle\!\rangle$ — the group with presentation $\langle G_0 \mid R\rangle$.**

The representing object is exactly the group obtained from $G_0$ by imposing the
 relations $R$, and the universal element (the identity natural transformation c
omponent) is the canonical projection $\pi:G_0\to H$, viewed as an element of $F
(H)$.
