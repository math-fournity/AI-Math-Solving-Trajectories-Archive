## Proof

**Answer: Yes, $h$ is necessarily in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$.**

**Step 1: $h \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$.**

Since $f, g \in \mathbb{Z}[x] \subset \mathbb{Q}[x]$ and $g$ is monic (in partic
ular $g \neq 0$), the equality $f = gh$ holds in $\mathbb{R}[x] \supset \mathbb{
Q}[x]$. Viewing both sides in the field of fractions $\mathbb{Q}(x)$, we get $h
= f/g \in \mathbb{Q}(x)$. But $h$ is a polynomial (in $\mathbb{R}[x]$), and $f/g
$ as a rational function that is actually a polynomial must lie in $\mathbb{Q}[x
]$ (clearing denominators: if $h \in \mathbb{R}[x]$ and $gh = f \in \mathbb{Q}[x
]$ with $g \in \mathbb{Q}[x]\setminus\{0\}$, then performing polynomial long div
ision of $f$ by $g$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ yields the unique quotient $h$, so $h \in
\mathbb{Q}[x]$). Hence $h \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$.

**Step 2: Apply Gauss's Lemma.**

Recall the *content* $c(P)$ of a nonzero polynomial $P \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ is the
 gcd of its coefficients (taken positive), and $P$ is *primitive* if $c(P)=1$. G
auss's Lemma states that the product of two primitive polynomials in $\mathbb{Z}
[x]$ is primitive.

Since $g$ is monic, its leading coefficient is $1$, so $c(g) = 1$; i.e., **$g$ i
s primitive**.

Write $h \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ in the form
$$h = \tfrac{p}{q}\, h',$$
where $p, q \in \mathbb{Z}$, $q > 0$, $\gcd(p,q)=1$, and $h' \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$
is primitive. (This is the standard content-factorization: factor out the conten
t of a primitive integer representative of $h$.)

From $f = gh = g \cdot \tfrac{p}{q} h'$, multiplying by $q$:
$$q\,f = p\,(g\,h').$$

Since $g$ and $h'$ are both primitive, Gauss's Lemma gives that $g\,h'$ is primi
tive, i.e. $c(g\,h') = 1$. Taking contents of both sides:
$$c(q\,f) = c\bigl(p\,(g\,h')\bigr).$$

Using $c(\lambda P) = |\lambda|\,c(P)$ for $\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}$:
$$q\,c(f) = |p|\cdot c(g\,h') = |p|\cdot 1 = |p|.$$

So $|p| = q\,c(f)$, which means $q \mid |p|$. But $\gcd(p,q)=1$ implies $\gcd(|p
|,q)=1$. The only positive integer $q$ with $q \mid |p|$ and $\gcd(|p|,q)=1$ is
$q = 1$.

**Conclusion.** With $q=1$,
$$h = p\,h' \in \mathbb{Z}[x].$$

Therefore $h$ is necessarily in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$. $\blacksquare$
