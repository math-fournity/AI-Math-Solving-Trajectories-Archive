Let $E$ be a smooth curve: a one-dimensional projective variety such that at eve

Let $\phi : E \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} E'$ be an isomorphism of varieties. We show

**Step 1: Reduction to local rings.** Smoothness of a variety at a point is equi
valent to the regularity of its local ring at that point (the Jacobian criterion
 is precisely the computational form of this equivalence: the rank condition on
the Jacobian is equivalent to $\dim \mathfrak{m}_p/\mathfrak{m}_p^2 = \dim \math
cal{O}_{E,p}$, i.e. to $\mathcal{O}_{E,p}$ being regular). Thus it suffices to s
how that every local ring of $E'$ is regular of dimension $1$.

**Step 2: Isomorphisms preserve local rings.** Let $q \in E'$ be arbitrary and s
et $p = \phi^{-1}(q) \in E$. The isomorphism $\phi$ induces an isomorphism of st
alks
$$\phi^{\sharp}_p : \mathcal{O}_{E',q} \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \mathcal{O}_{E,p},
$$
which is an isomorphism of local rings (it sends the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}
_q$ of $\mathcal{O}_{E',q}$ onto the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}_p$ of $\mathcal
{O}_{E,p}$, since $\phi$ and $\phi^{-1}$ are regular maps carrying $p \leftright
arrow q$).

**Step 3: Regularity and dimension are isomorphism invariants.** A ring isomorph
ism preserves:
- the Krull dimension (chains of prime ideals correspond bijectively), so $\dim
\mathcal{O}_{E',q} = \dim \mathcal{O}_{E,p} = 1$;
- the embedding dimension $\dim_{k}\, \mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$ (the induced
map $\mathfrak{m}_q/\mathfrak{m}_q^2 \to \mathfrak{m}_p/\mathfrak{m}_p^2$ is an
isomorphism of $k$-vector spaces, as $\phi^{\sharp}$ is $k$-linear and carries $
\mathfrak{m}_q$ onto $\mathfrak{m}_p$).

Since $\mathcal{O}_{E,p}$ is regular of dimension $1$ (by smoothness of $E$), we
 have
$$\dim_k \mathfrak{m}_p/\mathfrak{m}_p^2 = \dim \mathcal{O}_{E,p} = 1.$$
By the isomorphism above,
$$\dim_k \mathfrak{m}_q/\mathfrak{m}_q^2 = \dim_k \mathfrak{m}_p/\mathfrak{m}_p^
2 = 1 = \dim \mathcal{O}_{E',q}.$$
Hence $\mathcal{O}_{E',q}$ is a regular local ring of dimension $1$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.** Since $q \in E'$ was arbitrary, every local ring of $E'$
 is regular of dimension $1$. By the Jacobian criterion / regularity–smoothness
equivalence, $E'$ is non-singular (its Jacobian has the required rank $n'-1$ at
every point, where $n'$ is the dimension of the ambient space of $E'$). Therefor
e $E'$ is a smooth curve.

Either way, smoothness is an intrinsic property of the variety, preserved under
