## Proof

**Key observation.** For $x \in (-1, 1)$, we have $x^2 - 1 \neq 0$, so

$$\frac{1 - x^2}{x^2 - 1} = \frac{-(x^2 - 1)}{x^2 - 1} = -1.$$

**Step 1: The equation forces $g \equiv 0$ on $(-1,1)$.**

For every $x \in (-1, 1)$, the argument of $g$ on the right-hand side equals $-1
$, which lies in $[-1, 1]$. Substituting $g(-1) = 0$:

$$g(x) = \frac{x^2 - 1}{2} \cdot g(-1) = \frac{x^2 - 1}{2} \cdot 0 = 0.$$

Hence $g(x) = 0$ for all $x \in (-1, 1)$.

**Step 2: Continuity extends this to the endpoints.**

Since $g$ is continuous on $[-1, 1]$:

$$g(1) = \lim_{x \to 1^{-}} g(x) = \lim_{x \to 1^{-}} 0 = 0,$$

and $g(-1) = 0$ is given. Therefore $g \equiv 0$ on all of $[-1, 1]$.

**Step 3: The zero function is a valid solution.**

We verify $g \equiv 0$ satisfies all the requirements:

- **Continuity:** The zero function is continuous on $[-1, 1]$. ✓
- **Boundary condition:** $g(-1) = 0$. ✓
- **Functional equation for $x \in (-1, 1)$:** The argument $\frac{1-x^2}{x^2-1}
 = -1 \in [-1,1]$, so the right-hand side is $\frac{x^2-1}{2} \cdot g(-1) = \fra
c{x^2-1}{2} \cdot 0 = 0 = g(x)$. ✓
- **At $x = 1$:** $\frac{1-1}{2} \cdot g(-1) = 0 = g(1)$. ✓
- **At $x = -1$:** $\frac{1-1}{2} \cdot g(-1) = 0 = g(-1)$. ✓

**Conclusion.** Such a continuous function exists — in fact it is uniquely deter
mined and equals $g \equiv 0$.
