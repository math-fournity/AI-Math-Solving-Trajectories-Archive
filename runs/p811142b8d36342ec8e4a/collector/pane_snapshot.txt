## Proof

Let $q$ be the conjugate exponent to $p$, i.e. $q = p/(p-1) \in (1,\infty)$.

**Step 1: Define a candidate linear functional on $L^q(\mu)$.**

For each $g \in L^q(\mu)$, the limit $\lim_{n\to\infty} \int f_n g\,d\mu$ exists
 by hypothesis. Define
$$
T(g) := \lim_{n\to\infty} \int f_n g\,d\mu, \qquad g \in L^q(\mu).
$$
Linearity of the integral and of limits shows that $T : L^q(\mu) \to \mathbb{R}$
 (or $\mathbb{C}$) is linear.

**Step 2: $T$ is bounded on $L^q(\mu)$.**

Since $(f_n)$ is bounded in $L^p(\mu)$, set $M := \sup_n \|f_n\|_p < \infty$. By
 Hölder's inequality,
$$
\left|\int f_n g\,d\mu\right| \le \|f_n\|_p\, \|g\|_q \le M\|g\|_q,
$$
for every $n$ and every $g \in L^q(\mu)$. Passing to the limit,
$$
|T(g)| = \lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\int f_n g\,d\mu\right| \le M\|g\|_q.
$$
Hence $T$ is a bounded linear functional on $L^q(\mu)$ with $\|T\| \le M$.

**Step 3: Identify $T$ with an $L^p$ function.**

Since $1 < q < \infty$, the dual of $L^q(\mu)$ is isometrically isomorphic to $L
^p(\mu)$ (this standard duality $(L^q)^* \cong L^p$ for $1<q<\infty$ holds for a
rbitrary measure spaces; the $\sigma$-finiteness hypothesis is more than suffici
ent). Concretely, there exists a unique $f \in L^p(\mu)$ such that
$$
T(g) = \int f g\,d\mu \quad \text{for all } g \in L^q(\mu),
$$
with $\|f\|_p = \|T\| \le M$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Combining the definition of $T$ with the representation from Step 3,
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int f_n g\,d\mu = T(g) = \int f g\,d\mu \quad \text{for every
} g \in L^q(\mu).
$$

Thus $f \in L^p(\mu)$, obtained as the Riesz representative of the bounded funct
ional $T \in (L^q(\mu))^*$, is the desired function. $\blacksquare$
