# Conditions for Existence and Uniqueness of a Strong Solution to the Volatility
 SDE

## Setup

A stochastic volatility model posits an asset price process and a volatility (or
 variance) process $Y(t)$ driven by an SDE of the general form

$$dY(t) = b(t, Y(t))\,dt + \sigma(t, Y(t))\,dW(t), \qquad Y(0) = y_0, \tag{1}$$

on a filtered probability space $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, (\mathcal{F}_t)_{t\ge 0},
 \mathbb{P})$ satisfying the usual conditions, where $W$ is an $(\mathcal{F}_t)$
-Brownian motion, $b:\mathbb{R}_+ \times \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is the drift
, $\sigma:\mathbb{R}_+ \times \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is the diffusion coeffi
cient, and $y_0$ is a deterministic (or $\mathcal{F}_0$-measurable) initial cond
ition.

We give two tiers of sufficient conditions: **(A)** the classical Lipschitz theo
rem, and **(B)** the Yamada–Watanabe theorem, which is the tool actually needed
for the square-root diffusion appearing in the Heston / Cox–Ingersoll–Ross (CIR)
 volatility process.

---

## Theorem A (Classical Existence and Uniqueness — Lipschitz + Linear Growth)

**Statement.** Suppose the coefficients $b$ and $\sigma$ in (1) are **Borel-meas
urable** and satisfy, for all $t \ge 0$ and $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$:

**(A1) Global Lipschitz condition:**
$$|b(t,x) - b(t,y)| + |\sigma(t,x) - \sigma(t,y)| \le L\,|x - y| \tag{2}$$

**(A2) Linear growth condition:**
$$|b(t,x)| + |\sigma(t,x)| \le K(1 + |x|) \tag{3}$$

for constants $L, K > 0$. If $y_0$ is a constant (or $\mathcal{F}_0$-measurable
with $\mathbb{E}[|y_0|^2] < \infty$), then there exists a **unique strong soluti
on** $Y$ to (1) on $[0,\infty)$ that is adapted, has continuous paths, and satis
fies $\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sup_{0\le s\le T}|Y(s)|^2\right] < \infty$ for every $T
 > 0$.

**Proof.**

*Step 1: Picard iteration.* Define $Y^{(0)}(t) = y_0$ and, for $n \ge 0$,

$$Y^{(n+1)}(t) = y_0 + \int_0^t b(s, Y^{(n)}(s))\,ds + \int_0^t \sigma(s, Y^{(n)
}(s))\,dW(s). \tag{4}$$

By induction using (A2) and the BDG inequality, each $Y^{(n)}$ is well-defined,
adapted, continuous, and square-integrable.

*Step 2: A priori bound.* Set $\phi_n(t) = \mathbb{E}[|Y^{(n)}(t)|^2]$. From (4)
, the inequality $(a+b+c)^2 \le 3(a^2+b^2+c^2)$, Jensen's inequality for the tim
e integral, the Itô isometry for the stochastic integral, and (A2):

$$\phi_{n+1}(t) \le 3|y_0|^2 + 3K^2\,t\!\int_0^t(1+\phi_n(s))\,ds + 3K^2\!\int_0
^t(1+\phi_n(s))\,ds.$$

By induction (Gronwall-type argument), one shows $\sup_n \phi_n(t) \le C(1+t^2)$
 for a constant $C$ depending only on $K, |y_0|$, and $t$. Hence $\sup_n \mathbb
{E}[\sup_{0\le s\le T}|Y^{(n)}(s)|^2] < \infty$.

*Step 3: Convergence of the iteration.* Define $\Delta_n(t) = \mathbb{E}[|Y^{(n+
1)}(t) - Y^{(n)}(t)|^2]$. Using (A1), Jensen, and Itô isometry:

$$\Delta_{n+1}(t) \le 2L^2\,t\!\int_0^t \Delta_n(s)\,ds + 2L^2\!\int_0^t \Delta_
n(s)\,ds.$$

With $\Delta_0(t) = \mathbb{E}[|Y^{(1)}(t)-y_0|^2] \le C_0\,t$, induction yields

$$\Delta_n(t) \le \frac{(Ct)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!} \cdot C'$$

for suitable constants. This is summable, so $\sum_n \mathbb{E}[|Y^{(n+1)}(t)-Y^
{(n)}(t)|^2] < \infty$. By the Itô–Nisio / Borel–Cantelli argument (or by Chebys
hev + Borel–Cantelli on $\sup_{s\le T}$), $Y^{(n)}$ converges uniformly on $[0,T
]$, a.s., to a continuous adapted limit $Y$.

*Step 4: $Y$ solves (1).* Passing $n\to\infty$ in (4) under the $L^2$-bound from
 Step 2 (using dominated convergence for the time integral and $L^2$-convergence
 for the stochastic integral) shows $Y$ satisfies (1).

*Step 5: Uniqueness (pathwise).* Let $Y, \tilde{Y}$ be two strong solutions with
 the same $y_0$ and same driving Brownian motion $W$. Set $u(t) = \mathbb{E}[|Y(
t)-\tilde{Y}(t)|^2]$. Then by (A1) and Itô isometry:

$$u(t) \le 2L^2\,t\!\int_0^t u(s)\,ds + 2L^2\!\int_0^t u(s)\,ds \le C_T \!\int_0
^t u(s)\,ds.$$

By Gronwall's lemma, $u(t) \equiv 0$, hence $Y = \tilde{Y}$ a.s. for each $t$; b
y continuity of paths, $Y = \tilde{Y}$ on $[0,T]$ a.s. $\square$

**Application to SV models with Lipschitz coefficients.** The Hull–White model u
ses $dY = \mu Y\,dt + \xi Y\,dW$ (log-normal volatility). Here $b(t,y)=\mu y$, $
\sigma(t,y)=\xi y$ are globally Lipschitz and of linear growth, so Theorem A dir
ectly gives a unique strong positive solution $Y(t) = y_0 \exp((\mu - \tfrac12\x
i^2)t + \xi W(t))$.

## Theorem B (Yamada–Watanabe — for the Square-Root / Heston / CIR Volatility)

The Heston and CIR volatility processes satisfy

$$dY(t) = \kappa(\theta - Y(t))\,dt + \xi\sqrt{Y(t)}\,dW(t), \qquad Y(0) = y_0 \
ge 0, \tag{5}$$

with $\kappa, \theta, \xi > 0$. The diffusion coefficient $\sigma(y) = \xi\sqrt{
y}$ is **not Lipschitz** at $y = 0$, so Theorem A does not apply. The Yamada–Wat
anabe theorem provides the replacement.

**Statement (Yamada–Watanabe, 1971).** Consider the SDE (1) with time-homogeneou
s coefficients $b(y)$, $\sigma(y)$ (the time-inhomogeneous version holds under t
he same pointwise conditions). Assume:

**(B1) Lipschitz drift:** $|b(x) - b(y)| \le L|x - y|$ for all $x, y$.

**(B2) Hölder-type diffusion control:** There exists a strictly increasing, cont
inuous function $\rho: [0,\infty) \to [0,\infty)$ with $\rho(0) = 0$ such that

$$|\sigma(x) - \sigma(y)|^2 \le \rho(|x - y|), \qquad \text{for all } x, y, \tag
{6}$$

and

$$\int_{0+} \frac{du}{\rho(u)} = \infty. \tag{7}$$

**(B3) Linear growth:** $|b(y)| + |\sigma(y)| \le K(1+|y|)$.

Then **pathwise uniqueness** holds for (1). If, in addition, a weak solution exi
sts (which holds under (B3) and weak existence results, e.g., via the Stroock–Va
radhan / Engelbert–Schmidt theorem), then by the Yamada–Watanabe theorem ("pathw
ise uniqueness + weak existence $\Rightarrow$ strong existence and uniqueness in
 law"), there exists a **unique strong solution**.

**Proof sketch (the key argument).**

The heart is proving pathwise uniqueness. Let $Y, \tilde{Y}$ be two solutions dr
iven by the same $W$ with the same $y_0$. Let $\rho_n$ be a sequence of function
s approximating the identity: choose $\rho_n \in C^2$ with $0 \le \rho_n' \le 1/
n$, $\rho_n(u) \to u$ uniformly, and $\rho_n$ supported on a small neighborhood.
 More precisely, one constructs $\varphi_n \in C^2$ with $\varphi_n(x) = |x|$ fo
r $|x| \ge 1/n$, $\varphi_n(x) = 0$ for $x$ near $0$, and $0 \le \varphi_n'' \le
 2/(n\rho(1/n))$.

Apply Itô's formula to $\varphi_n(Y(t) - \tilde{Y}(t))$:

$$\varphi_n(Y(t)-\tilde{Y}(t)) = \int_0^t \varphi_n'(\cdot)\,[b(Y)-b(\tilde{Y})]
\,ds + \int_0^t \varphi_n'(\cdot)\,[\sigma(Y)-\sigma(\tilde{Y})]\,dW + \frac12\i
nt_0^t \varphi_n''(\cdot)\,[\sigma(Y)-\sigma(\tilde{Y})]^2\,ds.$$

The last term is bounded by $\frac12 \cdot \frac{2}{n\rho(1/n)} \cdot \rho(|Y-\t
ilde{Y}|) \cdot t$. Taking expectation and using (B1):

$$\mathbb{E}[\varphi_n(Y(t)-\tilde{Y}(t))] \le L\!\int_0^t \mathbb{E}[|Y(s)-\til
de{Y}(s)|]\,ds + \frac{t}{n\,\rho(1/n)}\,\sup_s \rho(\cdots).$$

The condition (7) — $\int_{0+} du/\rho(u) = \infty$ — is precisely what forces $
1/(n\,\rho(1/n)) \to 0$ as $n\to\infty$ (since otherwise $\rho(1/n) \ge c/n$ wou
ld make the integral $\sum c^{-1} = \infty$ trivially, but the divergence is str
onger: it forces $\rho(u)/u \to 0$ is impossible, hence $n\rho(1/n)\to\infty$).
Letting $n\to\infty$:

$$\mathbb{E}[|Y(t)-\tilde{Y}(t)|] \le L\!\int_0^t \mathbb{E}[|Y(s)-\tilde{Y}(s)|
]\,ds.$$

Gronwall's lemma gives $\mathbb{E}[|Y(t)-\tilde{Y}(t)|] = 0$, hence $Y = \tilde{
Y}$ a.s. — **pathwise uniqueness**.

The Yamada–Watanabe theorem then states: *pathwise uniqueness implies that weak
existence + uniqueness in law $\Rightarrow$ strong existence + pathwise uniquene
ss*, and in fact *pathwise uniqueness alone implies uniqueness in law*. Combined
 with weak existence (guaranteed by (B3) and the Engelbert–Schmidt conditions),
we obtain a **unique strong solution**. $\square$

**Application to the Heston / CIR volatility (5).** Here $b(y) = \kappa(\theta -
 y)$ is globally Lipschitz with constant $L = \kappa$, and

$$|\sigma(x) - \sigma(y)|^2 = \xi^2|\sqrt{x} - \sqrt{y}|^2 \le \xi^2|x - y|,$$

so (6) holds with $\rho(u) = \xi^2 u$. The integral condition (7) becomes $\int_
0^\varepsilon \frac{du}{\xi^2 u} = \infty$, which is satisfied. Linear growth (B
3) is immediate. Therefore:

> **The Heston / CIR volatility SDE (5) admits a unique strong solution on $[0,\
infty)$ for every $y_0 \ge 0$.**

Moreover, the solution is **non-negative** (by the Yamada–Watanabe / going-down
argument: the set $\{Y = 0\}$ is absorbing for the squared diffusion, and the dr
ift $\kappa\theta > 0$ pushes it back up). When the **Feller condition** $2\kapp
a\theta \ge \xi^2$ holds, the solution is **strictly positive** for all $t > 0$
(a.s.) whenever $y_0 > 0$; when $2\kappa\theta < \xi^2$, the solution is instant
aneously reflecting at zero but remains non-negative. In either case, existence
and uniqueness of the strong solution are guaranteed **independently** of the Fe
ller condition — the Feller condition only controls boundary behavior (positivit
y), not existence/uniqueness.

## Summary of Sufficient Conditions

| Condition | Role |
|---|---|
| **Borel measurability** of $b, \sigma$ | Minimum regularity for the integrals
to be defined |
| **Global Lipschitz** (2) | Pathwise uniqueness (Theorem A) |
| **Linear growth** (3) | Non-explosion; $L^2$-boundedness of the solution |
| **Yamada–Watanabe diffusion control** (6)–(7) | Pathwise uniqueness when $\sig
ma$ is only Hölder-$1/2$ (e.g., $\sqrt{y}$) |
| **Lipschitz drift** (B1) | Required in the Yamada–Watanabe setting |
| **Weak existence** (e.g., Engelbert–Schmidt) | Upgrades pathwise uniqueness to
 strong existence via the Yamada–Watanabe theorem |
| **Feller condition** $2\kappa\theta \ge \xi^2$ (CIR/Heston) | Strict positivit
y of the solution; **not** required for existence/uniqueness |

**Conclusion.** The SDE for the volatility process $Y(t)$ admits a unique strong
 solution under either of the following two sets of conditions:

1. **(Lipschitz regime)** The coefficients $b(t,\cdot)$ and $\sigma(t,\cdot)$ ar
e globally Lipschitz and satisfy a linear growth bound. This covers log-normal v
olatility models (Hull–White, SABR in the log-normal limit).

2. **(Yamada–Watanabe regime)** The drift $b$ is Lipschitz, the diffusion $\sigm
a$ satisfies the Hölder-type control $|\sigma(x)-\sigma(y)|^2 \le \rho(|x-y|)$ w
ith $\int_{0+} du/\rho(u) = \infty$, and both satisfy a linear growth bound. Thi
s covers the square-root (Heston / CIR) volatility process, where $\rho(u) = \xi
^2 u$ and the integral diverges logarithmically.

In both cases, linear growth ensures non-explosion, the Lipschitz (or Yamada–Wat
anabe) condition ensures pathwise uniqueness, and the Yamada–Watanabe theorem (p
athwise uniqueness + weak existence $\Rightarrow$ strong existence and uniquenes
s) closes the argument to yield a **unique strong solution**.
