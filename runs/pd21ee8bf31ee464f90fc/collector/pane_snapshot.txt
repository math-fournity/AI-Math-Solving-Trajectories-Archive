**Answer: Yes, such a polynomial exists.**

**Proof.**

Recall the Chinese Remainder Theorem for the PID $\mathbb{R}[X]$: if $f = g_1 \c
dots g_k$ where the $g_i$ are pairwise coprime, then
$$\mathbb{R}[X]/(f) \cong \mathbb{R}[X]/(g_1) \times \cdots \times \mathbb{R}[X]
/(g_k).$$

We produce two coprime factors, each giving quotient $\mathbb{C}$.

**Step 1: Irreducible quadratics over $\mathbb{R}$ give $\mathbb{C}$.** The poly
nomials $X^2+1$ and $X^2+2$ are both irreducible over $\mathbb{R}$ (negative dis
criminant). For any irreducible quadratic $p \in \mathbb{R}[X]$, the quotient $\
mathbb{R}[X]/(p)$ is a field extension of $\mathbb{R}$ of degree $2$. Since $\ma
thbb{C}$ is the unique (up to isomorphism) field extension of $\mathbb{R}$ of de
gree $2$ — indeed $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraically closed and $[\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{
R}]=2$, and there is no other algebraic extension of $\mathbb{R}$ of degree $>1$
 because every real polynomial of odd degree has a real root, forcing the only n
ontrivial finite extension to be quadratic and isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}$ — we h
ave
$$\mathbb{R}[X]/(X^2+1) \cong \mathbb{C}, \qquad \mathbb{R}[X]/(X^2+2) \cong \ma
thbb{C}.$$

(Concretely, $X \mapsto i$ gives an isomorphism $\mathbb{R}[X]/(X^2+1) \xrightar
row{\sim} \mathbb{C}$, and $X \mapsto i\sqrt{2}$ gives $\mathbb{R}[X]/(X^2+2) \x
rightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{C}$.)

**Step 2: The factors are coprime.** $X^2+1$ and $X^2+2$ are distinct irreducibl
e (hence prime) elements of the PID $\mathbb{R}[X]$, so they are coprime: $\gcd(
X^2+1,\, X^2+2) = 1$.

**Step 3: Apply CRT.** Set
$$f(X) = (X^2+1)(X^2+2) = X^4 + 3X^2 + 2 \in \mathbb{R}[X].$$
By the Chinese Remainder Theorem,
$$\mathbb{R}[X]/(f) \cong \mathbb{R}[X]/(X^2+1) \times \mathbb{R}[X]/(X^2+2) \co
ng \mathbb{C} \times \mathbb{C}.$$

Therefore such a polynomial exists; an explicit example is $f(X) = X^4 + 3X^2 +
2$.
