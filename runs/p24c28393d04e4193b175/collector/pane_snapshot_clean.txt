## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. There exists a subsequence $\{f_{n_j}\}$ and a function $f\in L^
\infty([0,1])$ with $\|f\|_\infty \le M$ (where $|f_n|\le M$) such that for ever
y Borel set $B\subseteq[0,1]$,
$$\int_B f_{n_j}\,dx \;\longrightarrow\; \int_B f\,dx.$$

**Step 1. A countable generating algebra.**

Let $\mathcal{A}$ be the algebra of finite unions of half-open intervals $[a,b)$
 with rational endpoints $a,b\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]$ (together with $\{1\}$). Th
is is a countable algebra that generates the Borel $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{B}
([0,1])$.

**Step 2. Diagonal extraction.**

For each $A\in\mathcal{A}$, the sequence $\bigl(\int_A f_n\,dx\bigr)_{n\ge1}$ is
 bounded (by $M\cdot m(A)$), hence has a convergent subsequence. Since $\mathcal
{A}$ is countable, enumerate it as $A_1,A_2,\dots$. By a standard diagonal argum
ent: extract a subsequence along which $\int_{A_1} f_n$ converges; from that ext
ract a further subsequence along which $\int_{A_2} f_n$ converges; and so on, th
en take the diagonal. We obtain a subsequence $\{f_{n_j}\}$ such that
$$\mu_j(A):=\int_A f_{n_j}\,dx \quad\text{converges for every } A\in\mathcal{A}.
$$
Denote the limit by $\mu(A)=\lim_j \mu_j(A)$ for $A\in\mathcal{A}$.

**Step 3. $\mu$ is a (signed) measure on $\mathcal{A}$, absolutely continuous w.
r.t. Lebesgue measure.**

Finite additivity is immediate. For countable additivity: let $A=\bigsqcup_{k=1}
^\infty A_k$ with $A,A_k\in\mathcal{A}$. Since $|\mu_j(A_k)|\le M\,m(A_k)$, the
tails are uniformly controlled:
$$\Bigl|\mu_j(A)-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mu_j(A_k)\Bigr|=\Bigl|\sum_{k>N}\mu_j(A_k)\Bigr|
\le M\sum_{k>N}m(A_k)\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}0\quad\text{uniformly in }j.$$
Taking $j\to\infty$ (with $N$ fixed) then $N\to\infty$ gives $\mu(A)=\sum_{k=1}^
\infty\mu(A_k)$. So $\mu$ is countably additive on $\mathcal{A}$, with $|\mu(A)|
\le M\,m(A)$.

By the Carathéodory extension theorem (and the bound $|\mu|\le M\,m$ guarantees
uniqueness), $\mu$ extends to a finite signed Borel measure $\bar\mu$ with $\bar
\mu\ll m$ and $|d\bar\mu/dm|\le M$ a.e. By Radon–Nikodym, $\bar\mu(B)=\int_B f\,
dx$ for some $f\in L^\infty$ with $\|f\|_\infty\le M$.

**Step 4. Convergence extends to all Borel sets.**

Let $B\in\mathcal{B}([0,1])$ and $\varepsilon>0$. Since $\mathcal{A}$ generates
the Borel $\sigma$-algebra and consists of finite unions of intervals, there exi
sts $A\in\mathcal{A}$ with $m(B\triangle A)<\varepsilon$ (approximate $B$ by a f
inite union of intervals). Then, uniformly in $j$,
$$\Bigl|\int_B f_{n_j}\,dx-\int_A f_{n_j}\,dx\Bigr|\le M\,m(B\triangle A)<M\vare
psilon,$$
and likewise $\bigl|\int_B f\,dx-\int_A f\,dx\bigr|<M\varepsilon$. Since $A\in\m
athcal{A}$, $\int_A f_{n_j}\,dx\to\int_A f\,dx=\mu(A)$. Therefore
$$\limsup_{j\to\infty}\Bigl|\int_B f_{n_j}\,dx-\int_B f\,dx\Bigr|\le 2M\varepsil
on.$$
Letting $\varepsilon\to0$ yields $\int_B f_{n_j}\,dx\to\int_B f\,dx$.

**Conclusion.** The subsequence $\{f_{n_j}\}$ satisfies: for every Borel set $B\
subseteq[0,1]$, the sequence $\int_B f_{n_j}\,dx$ converges (indeed to $\int_B f
\,dx$). $\blacksquare$
