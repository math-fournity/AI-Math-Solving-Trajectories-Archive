## Proof

Set up coordinates with $D$ at the origin, $C = (c, 0)$ on the positive $x$-axis
, and $A = (0, a)$ on the positive $y$-axis, so that $\angle D = \angle CDA = 90
°$ is built in, $|CD| = c$, $|AD| = a$, and $|AC| = d = \sqrt{a^2 + c^2}$.

Let $B = (x, y)$ be the fourth vertex, with $x > c$ and $y > 0$ (convexity, with
 vertices ordered $A, B, C, D$ counterclockwise).

**Define two angles at $C$:**

- $\varphi = \angle ACD$: in right triangle $ACD$ (right-angled at $D$), we have
 $\tan\varphi = \frac{a}{c}$, so $\sin\varphi = \frac{a}{d}$ and $\cos\varphi =
\frac{c}{d}$.

- $\psi = \angle BCD - 180° + \cdots$ — more precisely, let $\psi$ be the angle
that ray $CB$ makes with the positive $x$-axis, i.e. $\psi = \arctan\!\bigl(\fra
c{y}{x-c}\bigr) \in (0, \frac{\pi}{2})$.

Since ray $CD$ points in the negative $x$-direction (angle $180°$), the interior
 angle at $C$ is:
$$\angle BCD = 180° - \psi.$$

The angle $\angle ACB$ (between rays $CA$ at angle $180° - \varphi$ and $CB$ at
angle $\psi$) is:
$$\angle ACB = (180° - \varphi) - \psi = 180° - \varphi - \psi.$$

**The bisector of $\angle ACB$** lies at angle $\frac{(180° - \varphi) + \psi}{2
} = \frac{180° - \varphi + \psi}{2}$ from the positive $x$-axis. This bisector i
s perpendicular to $CD$ (the $x$-axis) iff it is vertical, i.e., iff
$$\frac{180° - \varphi + \psi}{2} = 90° \iff \psi = \varphi.$$

So **it suffices to prove $\psi = \varphi$.**

**Using the two given conditions.** Since $\angle B = \angle C$, we have $\angle
 ABC = \angle BCD = 180° - \psi$.

Apply the **Law of Sines** in $\triangle ABC$:
$$\frac{|AB|}{\sin(\angle ACB)} = \frac{|AC|}{\sin(\angle ABC)}.$$

Substituting $|AB| = 2c$ (given $|AB| = 2|CD| = 2c$), $|AC| = d$, $\angle ACB =
180° - \varphi - \psi$ (so $\sin(\angle ACB) = \sin(\varphi + \psi)$), and $\ang
le ABC = 180° - \psi$ (so $\sin(\angle ABC) = \sin\psi$):

$$\frac{2c}{\sin(\varphi + \psi)} = \frac{d}{\sin\psi}.$$

Cross-multiplying:
$$2c\,\sin\psi = d\,\sin(\varphi + \psi).$$

Expand $\sin(\varphi + \psi) = \sin\varphi\cos\psi + \cos\varphi\sin\psi$, and s
ubstitute $\sin\varphi = \frac{a}{d}$, $\cos\varphi = \frac{c}{d}$:

$$2c\,\sin\psi = d\!\left(\frac{a}{d}\cos\psi + \frac{c}{d}\sin\psi\right) = a\c
os\psi + c\sin\psi.$$

Subtract $c\sin\psi$ from both sides:
$$c\,\sin\psi = a\,\cos\psi \implies \tan\psi = \frac{a}{c}.$$

But $\tan\varphi = \frac{a}{c}$ as well, and both $\varphi, \psi \in (0, \frac{\
pi}{2})$, so $\psi = \varphi$.

**Conclusion.** Since $\psi = \varphi$, the bisector of $\angle ACB$ lies at ang
le $\frac{180° - \varphi + \varphi}{2} = 90°$ from the positive $x$-axis — that
is, it is **vertical**, hence **perpendicular to $CD$** (which lies on the $x$-a
xis). $\blacksquare$
