## Proof

**Problem.** Determine the number of countable non-isomorphic models of the theo
ry $\text{ACF}_p$ (the complete theory of algebraically closed fields of charact
eristic $p$, where $p$ is either a prime or $0$).

**Answer: $\aleph_0$.**

---

**Step 1: The prime field.** Every field of characteristic $p>0$ contains the pr
ime field $\mathbb{F}_p$, and every field of characteristic $0$ contains the pri
me field $\mathbb{Q}$. Denote this prime field by $k_0$.

**Step 2: Models are classified by transcendence degree.** Let $K \models \text{
ACF}_p$. Choose a transcendence basis $T$ of $K$ over $k_0$. Then $K$ is the alg
ebraic closure of $k_0(T)$. A standard result in field theory (Steinitz's theore
m) says:

> Two algebraically closed fields of the same characteristic are isomorphic if a
nd only if they have the same transcendence degree over their common prime field
.

So the isomorphism type of a model of $\text{ACF}_p$ is completely determined by
 $\operatorname{trdeg}(K/k_0) = |T|$.

**Step 3: Countable models.** A model $K \models \text{ACF}_p$ is countable if a
nd only if its transcendence degree over $k_0$ is countable (since the algebraic
 closure of a countable field is countable, and an uncountable transcendence bas
is would make $K$ uncountable). The countable cardinal values for the transcende
nce degree are exactly:
$$0,\; 1,\; 2,\; 3,\; \dots,\; n,\; \dots,\; \aleph_0.$$
Each value gives a distinct isomorphism type, and every countable model realizes
 one of these values.

**Step 4: Counting.** The set of possible transcendence degrees for countable mo
dels is
$$\{0, 1, 2, \dots\} \cup \{\aleph_0\},$$
which has cardinality $\aleph_0$ (countably infinite). Each corresponds to a uni
que isomorphism class, and conversely every isomorphism class of a countable mod
el arises this way.

**Step 5: Consistency with uncountable categoricity.** For uncountable $\kappa$,
 the only possible transcendence degree is $\kappa$ itself (since a countable tr
anscendence degree yields a countable field, and any transcendence degree $<\kap
pa$ but uncountable would give a field of that smaller cardinality). Hence there
 is exactly one model of cardinality $\kappa$ for each uncountable $\kappa$, con
firming $\kappa$-categoricity in all uncountable cardinals (Morley's theorem). T
he countable case is the unique cardinal where multiple isomorphism types appear
, and there are exactly $\aleph_0$ of them.

---

**Conclusion.** The number of countable non-isomorphic models of $\text{ACF}_p$
is $\boxed{\aleph_0}$.
