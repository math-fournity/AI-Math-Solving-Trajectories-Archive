## Proof

We determine the cardinality of
$$S = \{ f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{Q} : f \text{ is continuous and } \lim_{x \t
o -\infty} f(x) = 0 \}.$$

**Step 1: Any continuous $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{Q}$ is constant.**

The domain $\mathbb{R}$ is connected. The continuous image of a connected set is
 connected, so $f(\mathbb{R})$ is a connected subset of $\mathbb{Q}$ (equipped w
ith the subspace topology inherited from $\mathbb{R}$).

We claim the only connected subsets of $\mathbb{Q}$ are the singletons. Indeed,
suppose $A \subseteq \mathbb{Q}$ contains two distinct points $a < b$. Pick any
irrational $r \in (a, b)$ (which exists since irrationals are dense in $\mathbb{
R}$). Then
$$A = \big(A \cap (-\infty, r)\big) \cup \big(A \cap (r, +\infty)\big),$$
and both sets are nonempty (containing $a$ and $b$ respectively), open in $A$, a
nd disjoint. This is a separation of $A$, contradicting connectedness. Hence $A$
 must be a singleton.

Therefore $f(\mathbb{R}) = \{c\}$ for some $c \in \mathbb{Q}$, i.e., $f$ is the
constant function $f(x) = c$.

**Step 2: The limit condition forces $c = 0$.**

For a constant function $f(x) = c$,
$$\lim_{x \to -\infty} f(x) = \lim_{x \to -\infty} c = c.$$
The condition $\lim_{x \to -\infty} f(x) = 0$ thus requires $c = 0$.

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

The only element of $S$ is the identically zero function $f \equiv 0$, which is
indeed continuous, maps $\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{Q}$ (since $0 \in \mathbb{Q}$),
and satisfies the limit condition. Hence

$$|S| = 1.$$
