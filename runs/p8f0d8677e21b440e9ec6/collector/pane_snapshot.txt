## Proof

**Step 1: Apply the Angle Bisector Theorem.**

For any point $P$ not on the lines of the sides, triangle $PAB$ is well-defined
(in the plane through $P, A, B$). The internal angle bisector from $P$ meets $AB
$ at $K$ with:
$$\frac{AK}{KB} = \frac{PA}{PB}$$

Setting $a = PA$, $b = PB$, $c = PC$, $d = PD$, the section formula gives:
$$K = \frac{b\,A + a\,B}{a+b}, \quad L = \frac{c\,B + b\,C}{b+c}, \quad M = \fra
c{d\,C + c\,D}{c+d}, \quad N = \frac{a\,D + d\,A}{d+a}$$

**Step 2: Parallelogram condition.**

$KLMN$ is a parallelogram if and only if the diagonals bisect each other:
$$K + M = L + N$$

**Step 3: Compute $K + M - L - N$.**

Collecting coefficients of each vertex:

| Vertex | Coefficient in $K+M-L-N$ |
|--------|--------------------------|
| $A$ | $\dfrac{a(b-d)}{(a+b)(a+d)}$ |
| $B$ | $\dfrac{b(a-c)}{(a+b)(b+c)}$ |
| $C$ | $\dfrac{c(d-b)}{(c+d)(b+c)}$ |
| $D$ | $\dfrac{d(c-a)}{(c+d)(a+d)}$ |

Since each of $K, L, M, N$ is a convex combination (coefficients sum to 1), the
coefficients in $K+M-L-N$ sum to $1+1-1-1 = 0$. (One can verify directly: groupi
ng the $A$ and $C$ coefficients gives $(b-d)\!\left[\frac{a}{(a+b)(a+d)} - \frac
{c}{(c+d)(b+c)}\right]$ and grouping $B$ and $D$ gives $(a-c)\!\left[\frac{b}{(a
+b)(b+c)} - \frac{d}{(c+d)(a+d)}\right]$, which cancel.)

**Step 4: Reduce to a planar vector equation.**

Set $A$ as the origin. The condition $K+M = L+N$ becomes:
$$p\,B + q\,C + r\,D = 0$$
where $p = \frac{b(a-c)}{(a+b)(b+c)}$, $q = \frac{c(d-b)}{(c+d)(b+c)}$, $r = \fr
ac{d(c-a)}{(c+d)(a+d)}$, and $p + q + r = 0$.

Using $p + q + r = 0$, we eliminate $r = -(p+q)$:
$$p\,B + q\,C - (p+q)\,D = 0 \implies p\,(B - D) + q\,(C - D) = 0$$
$$p\,\vec{DB} + q\,\vec{DC} = 0$$

**Step 5: Use the convexity of $ABCD$.**

Since $ABCD$ is a convex quadrilateral, no three vertices are collinear. In part
icular, $D$, $B$, $C$ are non-collinear, so $\vec{DB}$ and $\vec{DC}$ are **line
arly independent**.

Therefore $p\,\vec{DB} + q\,\vec{DC} = 0$ forces:
$$p = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad q = 0$$

- $p = 0$: Since $b > 0$ and the denominators are positive, this gives $a = c$,
i.e., $PA = PC$.
- $q = 0$: Since $c > 0$ and the denominators are positive, this gives $d = b$,
i.e., $PD = PB$.

(The third coefficient $r$ vanishes automatically since $p+q+r=0$.)

**Step 6: Identify the locus.**

We have proved:
$$KLMN \text{ is a parallelogram} \iff PA = PC \text{ and } PB = PD$$

- $PA = PC$ defines the **perpendicular bisector plane** of diagonal $AC$.
- $PB = PD$ defines the **perpendicular bisector plane** of diagonal $BD$.

Since $ABCD$ is a convex quadrilateral, its diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ intersect at
 an interior point, hence are not parallel. Therefore the two perpendicular bise
ctor planes (with normals $\vec{AC}$ and $\vec{BD}$ respectively) are not parall
el and intersect in a **line**.

This line has direction $\vec{AC} \times \vec{BD}$ (perpendicular to both diagon
als) and passes through the unique point equidistant from $A, C$ and from $B, D$
.

**Conclusion.** The geometrical locus of $P$ is the **line of intersection of th
e perpendicular bisector planes of the two diagonals $AC$ and $BD$** — equivalen
tly, the set of all points $P$ in space satisfying $PA = PC$ and $PB = PD$.
