$$h(h(v) \cdot u) = h(u) \cdot v \quad \text{for all } u, v > 0. \tag{$\star$}$$

**Step 2: Deriving key properties of $h$.**

Setting $v = 1$: $h(cu) = h(u)$.

Applying $h$ to $h(h(v)) = cv$: $h(h(h(v))) = h(cv) = h(v)$. But also $h(h(h(v))

Thus **$h(1) = 1$** and **$h(h(v)) = v$** (h is an involution).

Setting $u = h(w)$ in $(\star)$: $h(h(v) \cdot h(w)) = h(h(w)) \cdot v = wv$. Bu

cdot u) = h(u) \cdot h(t)$. So **$h$ is multiplicative**.
