## Spectrum of the Volterra Operator on $C([0,1])$

**Claim.** $\sigma(A) = \{0\}$. Moreover, $0$ belongs to the residual spectrum:
$A$ is injective, has no nonzero eigenvalues, and its range is not dense.

### Step 1. $A$ is a compact operator.

$A$ is an integral operator on $C([0,1])$ with kernel $K(t,s) = \mathbf{1}_{\{s
\le t\}}$, which is bounded (hence in $L^2([0,1]^2)$). Equivalently, one may ver
ify compactness directly via the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem: if $\|x\|_\infty \le 1$,
 then $\|(Ax)\|_\infty \le 1$ and $(Ax)$ is equicontinuous since $|(Ax)(t)-(Ax)(
t')| \le |t-t'|\cdot\|x\|_\infty$. Hence $A$ maps the unit ball to a relatively
compact set, so $A$ is compact.

For a compact operator on an infinite-dimensional Banach space, the spectrum sat
isfies $\sigma(A) \subseteq \{0\}\cup\{\text{eigenvalues}\}$, and every nonzero
spectral value is an eigenvalue. Also $0\in\sigma(A)$ automatically (since $X$ i
s infinite-dimensional).

### Step 2. No nonzero eigenvalues.

Suppose $Ax = \lambda x$ with $\lambda \ne 0$ and $x \in C([0,1])$. Then
$$\int_0^t x(s)\,ds = \lambda\, x(t), \quad \forall\, t\in[0,1].$$
The left side is $C^1$, so $x$ is $C^1$ and differentiating gives $x(t) = \lambd
a\, x'(t)$, i.e. $x'(t) = \tfrac{1}{\lambda}x(t)$. The general solution is $x(t)
 = C e^{t/\lambda}$. Evaluating the original equation at $t=0$: $0 = \lambda\, x
(0)$, so $x(0)=0$ (since $\lambda\ne 0$), giving $C=0$, hence $x\equiv 0$. Thus
there are **no nonzero eigenvalues**.

By the spectral theorem for compact operators, $\sigma(A)\subseteq\{0\}$. Since
$X$ is infinite-dimensional, $0\in\sigma(A)$. Therefore
$$\boxed{\sigma(A) = \{0\}.}$$

### Step 3. Classification of $0$.

- **Not an eigenvalue (point spectrum):** $Ax=0 \Rightarrow \int_0^t x(s)\,ds =
0$ for all $t$, so $x\equiv 0$. Thus $A$ is injective and $0\notin\sigma_p(A)$.

- **Range is not dense (residual spectrum):** For every $x$, $(Ax)(0) = \int_0^0
 x = 0$, so $\operatorname{Ran}(A)\subseteq \{y\in C([0,1]) : y(0)=0\} =: M$, a
proper closed subspace. Hence $\overline{\operatorname{Ran}(A)}\subseteq M \subs
etneq C([0,1])$, so the range is not dense. Since $A$ is injective but $\operato
rname{Ran}(A)$ is not dense, $0$ belongs to the **residual spectrum** $\sigma_r(
A)$.

(Equivalently, the adjoint $A^*$ on $M([0,1])$ satisfies $A^*\mu = 0$ for $\mu =
 \delta_0$, a nonzero measure, confirming $0\in\sigma_r(A)$.)

### Summary

$$\sigma(A) = \sigma_r(A) = \{0\}, \qquad \sigma_p(A) = \varnothing, \qquad \sig
ma_c(A) = \varnothing.$$

The Volterra operator is quasinilpotent: its spectral radius is $r(A) = 0$, cons
