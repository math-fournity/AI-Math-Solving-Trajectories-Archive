## Determining M₁

**Moore Bound.** For a graph with maximum degree Δ and diameter D, the number of
 vertices is at most:
$$n \leq 1 + \Delta + \Delta(\Delta-1) + \cdots + \Delta(\Delta-1)^{D-1}$$

For Δ = 3, D = 2: n ≤ 1 + 3 + 3·2 = **10**.

**Achievability.** The Petersen graph has 10 vertices, is 3-regular, and has dia
meter 2 (girth 5, so every non-adjacent pair has a common neighbor). It is the u
nique Moore graph for (3,2). So **M₁ = 10**.

## Determining M₂

**Key observation — degree lower bound.** For any vertex v with degree d(v), the
 number of vertices within distance 2 is at most:
$$1 + d(v) + \sum_{u \sim v}(d(u) - 1) \leq 1 + d(v) + d(v)\cdot 2 = 1 + 3\,d(v)
$$

If any vertex has degree 2, then n ≤ 1 + 3·2 = **7**. If any vertex has degree 1
, then n ≤ 4.

**Consequence:** For n ≥ 8, every vertex must have degree exactly 3 (the graph m
ust be 3-regular).

**n = 9 is impossible.** A 3-regular graph on 9 vertices would have degree-sum 9
 × 3 = 27, which is odd — violating the handshake lemma. Since n ≥ 8 forces 3-re
gularity, and 3-regularity on 9 vertices is impossible, **no graph on 9 vertices
 satisfies the conditions**.

**n = 10 is impossible with a triangle.** The only (3,2)-Moore graph is the Pete
rsen graph, which is triangle-free (girth 5). So any triangle-containing graph h
as n ≤ 9, and we just showed n = 9 is impossible.

**n = 8 is achievable.** Construct the following 3-regular graph on vertices {1,
2,3,4,5,6,7,8}:

- Triangle: edges 1–2, 2–3, 1–3
- Matching: edges 1–4, 2–5, 3–6
- Complete bipartite K_{3,2} between {4,5,6} and {7,8}: edges 4–7, 4–8, 5–7, 5–8
, 6–7, 6–8

**Degree check:** Every vertex has degree 3. ✓

**Triangle check:** {1,2,3} is a triangle. ✓

**Diameter 2 verification** — every non-adjacent pair has a common neighbor:

| Pair | Common neighbor | Pair | Common neighbor |
|------|----------------|------|----------------|
| (1,5) | 2 | (3,5) | 2 |
| (1,6) | 3 | (3,7) | 6 |
| (1,7) | 4 | (3,8) | 6 |
| (1,8) | 4 | (4,5) | 7 |
| (2,4) | 1 | (4,6) | 7 |

All remaining pairs are verified similarly — every non-adjacent pair shares a co

Therefore **M₂ = 8**.

## Result

$$M_1 \times M_2 = 10 \times 8 = \boxed{80}$$
