**Proof.**

Place the orthocentre $H$ of $\triangle ABC$ at the origin $O$. In an orthocentr
ic system the three pairwise dot products are equal; denote their common value b
y $k$:
$$\vec A\cdot\vec B \;=\; \vec B\cdot\vec C \;=\; \vec C\cdot\vec A \;=\; k.$$

---

**Parametrising $P$ and $Q$.**

Since $HP\parallel AD$ and $H=O$, the point $P$ is a scalar multiple of $\vec D-
\vec A$; write $P=\lambda(\vec D-\vec A)$. Since $P\in AB$, write $P=\vec A+t(\v
ec B-\vec A)$. Equating and rearranging gives
$$\lambda\,\vec D=(1+\lambda-t)\,\vec A+t\,\vec B. \tag{1}$$

Likewise $Q=\mu(\vec D-\vec C)$ (because $HQ\parallel CD$) and $Q\in BC$, giving
$$\mu\,\vec D=(1-s)\,\vec B+(s+\mu)\,\vec C. \tag{2}$$

---

**Determining $\lambda$ and $\mu$.**

Take the dot product of (1) with $\vec C$. Using $\vec A\cdot\vec C=\vec B\cdot\
vec C=k$:
$$\lambda\,(\vec D\cdot\vec C)=(1+\lambda-t)\,k+t\,k=k(1+\lambda),$$
so $\lambda(\vec D\cdot\vec C-k)=k$, i.e.
$$\lambda=\frac{k}{\vec D\cdot\vec C-k}. \tag{3}$$

Take the dot product of (2) with $\vec A$. Using $\vec B\cdot\vec A=\vec C\cdot\
vec A=k$:
$$\mu\,(\vec D\cdot\vec A)=(1-s)\,k+(s+\mu)\,k=k(1+\mu),$$
so $\mu(\vec D\cdot\vec A-k)=k$, i.e.
$$\mu=\frac{k}{\vec D\cdot\vec A-k}. \tag{4}$$

---

**The orthocentre of $\triangle ACD$.**

Let $H'$ be the orthocentre of $\triangle ACD$. The defining perpendicularities
$AH'\perp CD$, $CH'\perp AD$, $DH'\perp AC$ give (with $H=O$):
$$\vec{H'}\cdot(\vec C-\vec D)=\vec A\cdot(\vec C-\vec D),\qquad \vec{H'}\cdot(\
vec A-\vec D)=\vec C\cdot(\vec A-\vec D).$$

From these, writing $\delta:=\vec{H'}\cdot\vec D$ and using $\vec A\cdot\vec C=k
$:
$$\vec{H'}\cdot\vec C=\delta+k-\vec A\cdot\vec D,\qquad \vec{H'}\cdot\vec A=\del
ta+k-\vec C\cdot\vec D. \tag{5}$$

---

**Verifying $H'$ lies on the perpendicular to $PQ$ through $H$.**

Since $H$ is the origin, the line through $H$ perpendicular to $PQ$ is $\{\,X :
\vec X\cdot(\vec Q-\vec P)=0\,\}$. So it suffices to prove $\vec{H'}\cdot(\vec Q
-\vec P)=0$.

Using $P=\lambda(\vec D-\vec A)$ and $Q=\mu(\vec D-\vec C)$:
$$\vec Q-\vec P=(\mu-\lambda)\vec D-\mu\vec C+\lambda\vec A.$$

Hence, using (5):
$$\vec{H'}\cdot(\vec Q-\vec P)=(\mu-\lambda)\delta-\mu(\delta+k-\vec A\cdot\vec
D)+\lambda(\delta+k-\vec C\cdot\vec D)$$
$$=k(\lambda-\mu)+\mu(\vec A\cdot\vec D)-\lambda(\vec C\cdot\vec D)$$
$$=\lambda\bigl(k-\vec C\cdot\vec D\bigr)+\mu\bigl(\vec A\cdot\vec D-k\bigr).$$

Substituting (3) and (4):
$$\lambda\bigl(k-\vec C\cdot\vec D\bigr)=\frac{k}{\vec C\cdot\vec D-k}\cdot\bigl
(k-\vec C\cdot\vec D\bigr)=-k,$$
$$\mu\bigl(\vec A\cdot\vec D-k\bigr)=\frac{k}{\vec A\cdot\vec D-k}\cdot\bigl(\ve
c A\cdot\vec D-k\bigr)=k.$$

Therefore
$$\vec{H'}\cdot(\vec Q-\vec P)=-k+k=0.$$

So $H'$ lies on the line through $H$ perpendicular to $PQ$; equivalently, the pe
rpendicular from $H$ to $PQ$ passes through the orthocentre of $\triangle ACD$.
$\blacksquare$
