## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** Every $f \in \mathcal{L}^p(A)$ can be extended to a function $g
 \in \mathcal{L}^p(\overline{A})$.

**Setup.** Since $A \in \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$, its closure $\overline{A}$ is
closed, hence also Borel, so both $A$ and $\overline{A}$ are Lebesgue measurable
. We have the disjoint decomposition
$$\overline{A} = A \;\sqcup\; N, \qquad N := \overline{A}\setminus A,$$
and by hypothesis $N$ is a Lebesgue null set. We equip $A$ and $\overline{A}$ wi
th Lebesgue measure (restricted to each), and since $N \subseteq \overline{A}$ i
s Lebesgue null, it remains null under the restriction to $\overline{A}$.

**Construction.** Given $f \in \mathcal{L}^p(A)$, define $g : \overline{A} \to \
mathbb{R}$ by
$$
g(x) = \begin{cases} f(x), & x \in A, \\ 0, & x \in N. \end{cases}
$$

**Measurability.** The set $A$ is Borel (hence Lebesgue measurable in $\overline
{A}$), and $N = \overline{A}\setminus A$ is Lebesgue measurable (it is null). Si
nce $f$ is measurable on $A$ and the constant $0$ is measurable on $N$, the piec
ewise definition yields a measurable function on $\overline{A}$.

**Integrability.** Using $\overline{A} = A \sqcup N$ and $|g|^p = |f|^p \cdot \m
athbf{1}_A + 0 \cdot \mathbf{1}_N$,
$$
\int_{\overline{A}} |g|^p \,d\lambda \;=\; \int_A |f|^p\,d\lambda \;+\; \int_N 0
\,d\lambda \;=\; \int_A |f|^p\,d\lambda \;<\; \infty.
$$
Hence $g \in \mathcal{L}^p(\overline{A})$, and moreover $\|g\|_{\mathcal{L}^p(\o
verline{A})} = \|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^p(A)}$ (the extension is isometric).

**Well-definedness on equivalence classes.** If $\mathcal{L}^p$ is interpreted a
s a space of equivalence classes modulo null sets, the construction is still wel
l-defined: modifying $f$ on a $\lambda$-null subset $Z \subseteq A$ modifies $g$
 only on $Z \subseteq \overline{A}$, which is null in $\overline{A}$. Thus the c
lass $[g]$ depends only on $[f]$, and the map $[f] \mapsto [g]$ is a linear isom
etric embedding $\mathcal{L}^p(A) \hookrightarrow \mathcal{L}^p(\overline{A})$.

**Why the null-set hypothesis is essential.** The argument hinges entirely on $N
$ being null: it guarantees that whatever values we assign on $N$ contribute not
hing to the $L^p$-norm, so the extension is automatic. If $N$ had positive measu
re, arbitrary extension would generally fail (one would need to define $g$ on a
set of positive measure with no information from $f$, and there is no canonical
way to keep $g \in L^p$). The hypothesis $N = \overline{A}\setminus A$ being nul
l is precisely what makes $A$ and $\overline{A}$ indistinguishable from the meas
ure-theoretic standpoint.

**Conclusion.** Under the assumption that $N = \overline{A}\setminus A$ is a Leb
esgue null set, every $f \in \mathcal{L}^p(A)$ extends to $g \in \mathcal{L}^p(\
overline{A})$ (e.g. by zero on $N$), with preservation of the $L^p$-norm.
