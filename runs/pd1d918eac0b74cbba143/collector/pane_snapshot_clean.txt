## Proof

Let $\mathbb{K} = \mathrm{GF}(25) = \mathbb{Z}_5[\alpha]$, where $\alpha^2 = 2$
(so $\alpha = \sqrt{2}$). This is the unique field with 25 elements, a degree-2
extension of $\mathbb{Z}_5$.

**Key fact.** The map $\sigma : \mathbb{K} \to \mathbb{K}$ defined by $\sigma(x)
 = x^5$ is the **Frobenius automorphism** of $\mathbb{K}/\mathbb{Z}_5$. It is a
field automorphism (in particular, bijective), and its fixed field is exactly $\
mathbb{Z}_5$.

**Solving $x^5 = 3$.** Since $\sigma$ is bijective, the equation $\sigma(x) = 3$
 has **exactly one** solution in $\mathbb{K}$.

We check that $x = 3$ is this solution. Indeed, $3 \in \mathbb{Z}_5 \subset \mat
hbb{K}$, and
$$3^5 = 3^{4}\cdot 3 = 1 \cdot 3 = 3 \pmod{5},$$
since $3^4 = 81 \equiv 1 \pmod{5}$ by Fermat's little theorem. Hence $3^5 = 3$ i
n $\mathbb{K}$, so $x=3$ satisfies the equation.

**Uniqueness.** Suppose $y^5 = 3$ for some $y \in \mathbb{K}$. Apply the Frobeni
us again:
$$y^{25} = (y^5)^5 = 3^5 = 3.$$
But $y^{25} = y$ for every $y \in \mathrm{GF}(25)$ (since $|\mathbb{K}^*| = 24$
and $y^{24}=1$ for $y \neq 0$, giving $y^{25}=y$; and $0^{25}=0$). Therefore $y
= 3$.

Alternatively, since $\sigma$ is an automorphism (injective), $\sigma(x) = \sigm
a(3)$ implies $x = 3$ directly.

**Conclusion.** The equation $x^5 = 3$ has exactly one solution in $\mathbb{K}$:

$$\boxed{x = 3}.$$
