**Answer: Yes.** A Riemann integrable function $f: A \subset \mathbb{R}^n \to \m

## Proof (Lebesgue's Criterion, generalized to $\mathbb{R}^n$)

### Setup and Reduction

We work in the standard setting: $A \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is a **bounded, Jordan
-measurable** set (i.e., $\partial A$ has $n$-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero)
, and $f$ is **bounded** on $A$. These conditions are implicit in the definition
 of Riemann integrability on $A$.

**Reduction to a rectangle.** Enclose $A$ in a closed rectangle $R \subset \math
bb{R}^n$. Extend $f$ to $\tilde{f}: R \to \mathbb{R}$ by
$$\tilde{f}(x) = \begin{cases} f(x) & x \in A \\ 0 & x \in R \setminus A. \end{c
ases}$$
Since $\partial A$ has measure zero, $\tilde{f}$ is Riemann integrable on $R$ if
 and only if $f$ is Riemann integrable on $A$, and the discontinuity sets satisf
y: the discontinuities of $\tilde{f}$ in $R$ are contained in (discontinuities o
f $f$ in $\operatorname{int}(A)$) $\cup$ $\partial A$. Since $\partial A$ has me
asure zero, it suffices to prove:

> **Claim:** If $\tilde{f}: R \to \mathbb{R}$ is bounded and Riemann integrable
on the closed rectangle $R$, then $\tilde{f}$ is continuous a.e. on $R$.

### Oscillation

For $x \in R$, define the **oscillation** of $f$ at $x$:
$$\omega_f(x) = \inf_{\delta > 0}\; \sup\bigl\{|f(y) - f(z)| : y, z \in B(x,\del
ta) \cap R\bigr\}.$$

**Key facts:**
- $f$ is continuous at $x$ $\iff$ $\omega_f(x) = 0$.
- $\omega_f$ is upper semicontinuous (as an infimum of decreasing continuous-in-
$\delta$ quantities), so for each $\epsilon > 0$, the set
$$D_\epsilon := \{x \in R : \omega_f(x) \geq \epsilon\}$$
is **closed** (hence compact, being a closed subset of the compact rectangle $R$
).
- The discontinuity set is $D = \bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty} D_{1/k}$, a countable uni
on.

### Key Lemma: $D_\epsilon$ has measure zero for every $\epsilon > 0$

**Proof of Lemma.** Fix $\epsilon > 0$ and an arbitrary $\eta > 0$. Since $f$ is
 Riemann integrable on $R$, there exists a partition $P$ of $R$ into finitely ma
ny subrectangles $\{S_1, \dots, S_N\}$ such that the upper and lower sums satisf
y
$$U(f, P) - L(f, P) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \bigl(\sup_{S_i} f - \inf_{S_i} f\bigr)\ope
ratorname{vol}(S_i) < \epsilon\,\eta.$$

Let $\mathcal{I} = \{i : \operatorname{int}(S_i) \cap D_\epsilon \neq \emptyset\
}$. For each $i \in \mathcal{I}$, pick $x_i \in \operatorname{int}(S_i) \cap D_\
epsilon$. Since $x_i$ is in the **interior** of $S_i$, there exists $\delta_0 >
0$ with $B(x_i, \delta_0) \subset S_i$. Therefore:
$$\sup_{S_i} f - \inf_{S_i} f \;\geq\; \sup_{B(x_i,\delta_0)\cap R} f - \inf_{B(
x_i,\delta_0)\cap R} f \;\geq\; \omega_f(x_i) \;\geq\; \epsilon.$$

Summing over $i \in \mathcal{I}$:
$$\epsilon \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \operatorname{vol}(S_i) \;\leq\; \sum_{i \in
 \mathcal{I}} \bigl(\sup_{S_i} f - \inf_{S_i} f\bigr)\operatorname{vol}(S_i) \;\
leq\; U(f,P) - L(f,P) \;<\; \epsilon\,\eta.$$

Hence $\sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \operatorname{vol}(S_i) < \eta$.

Now, $D_\epsilon$ is covered by:
1. The subrectangles $\{S_i : i \in \mathcal{I}\}$ (total volume $< \eta$), and
2. The union of boundaries $\bigcup_{i=1}^{N} \partial S_i$, which is a finite u
nion of $(n{-}1)$-dimensional rectangles and thus has **$n$-dimensional Lebesgue
 measure zero**.

Therefore $D_\epsilon$ is contained in a set of measure $< \eta$ (plus a measure
-zero set), so $m(D_\epsilon) < \eta$. Since $\eta > 0$ was arbitrary, $m(D_\eps
ilon) = 0$. $\quad\blacksquare$

### Conclusion

The full discontinuity set is
$$D = \bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty} D_{1/k}.$$
Each $D_{1/k}$ has measure zero by the Lemma, and a countable union of measure-z
ero sets has measure zero. Therefore
$$m(D) = 0,$$
i.e., $f$ is continuous almost everywhere on $R$, and hence on $A$ (since the di
scontinuities on $A$ are a subset of those on $R$, minus the measure-zero set $\
partial A$).

### Remark (Converse)

The converse also holds: a bounded function on a Jordan-measurable set that is c
ontinuous a.e. is Riemann integrable. Together, this gives the full **Lebesgue c
riterion** in $\mathbb{R}^n$: *a bounded function on a bounded Jordan-measurable
 set $A \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is Riemann integrable if and only if it is continu
ous almost everywhere on $A$.* This holds for **all** $n \geq 1$; the proof is d
imension-independent because it relies only on (i) the existence of partitions i
nto rectangles, (ii) the measure-zero nature of $(n{-}1)$-dimensional boundaries
, and (iii) countable subadditivity of Lebesgue measure—all of which hold in eve
ry dimension.
