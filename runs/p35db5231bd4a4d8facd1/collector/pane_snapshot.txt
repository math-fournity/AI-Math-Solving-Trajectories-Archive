## Proof

We apply **Rouché's theorem**. Let $g(z) = z^n$ and $h(z) = -f(z)$, so that the
equation $f(z) = z^n$ is equivalent to $g(z) + h(z) = 0$, i.e., finding the zero
s of $g(z) + h(z) = z^n - f(z)$ inside the unit disk.

**Setup.** Both $g(z) = z^n$ and $f(z)$ are analytic on the closed unit disk $\o
verline{B(0,1)}$ (the former is entire; the latter is analytic on a neighborhood
 of $\overline{B(0,1)}$ by hypothesis). Hence $g$ and $h$ are analytic on and in
side the unit circle $|z| = 1$.

**Comparison on the boundary.** On $|z| = 1$:
$$|h(z)| = |f(z)| < 1 = |z|^n = |g(z)|.$$
Thus $|h(z)| < |g(z)|$ holds strictly at every point of the boundary $|z| = 1$.

**Applying Rouché's theorem.** Since $|h| < |g|$ on $|z| = 1$ and both are analy
tic on and inside this contour, Rouché's theorem tells us that $g$ and $g + h$ h
ave the same number of zeros (counting multiplicities) inside the unit disk.

**Counting zeros of $g$.** The function $g(z) = z^n$ has a single zero at $z = 0
$ of multiplicity $n$. So $g$ has exactly $n$ zeros inside $B(0,1)$, counting mu
ltiplicities.

**Conclusion.** Therefore $g(z) + h(z) = z^n - f(z)$ also has exactly $n$ zeros
inside the unit disk, counting multiplicities. These zeros are precisely the sol
utions of $f(z) = z^n$ with $|z| < 1$.

The number of solutions (counting multiplicities) to $f(z) = z^n$ within the uni
t disk is $\boxed{n}$.
