# Problem

In a futuristic circular city, there are exactly 12 solar-powered tram stations positioned at perfectly equal intervals around the city's perimeter, forming the vertices of a regular 12-gon.

The City Planning Committee wants to design "Symmetry Zones." A Symmetry Zone is defined by selecting exactly four of these tram stations to serve as the corners of a quadrilateral district. To qualify as a Symmetry Zone, the district must meet two specific criteria:
1. At least one pair of its boundaries (the straight lines connecting the chosen stations) must be parallel.
2. At least two of its non-parallel boundaries must be of exactly equal length.
3. The district cannot be a parallelogram (all four sides cannot consist of two pairs of equal, parallel lines) unless it also satisfies the properties of a non-rectangular isosceles trapezoid. In geometry, all such four-station configurations that possess at least one axis of symmetry passing through the midpoints of two opposite sides are considered.

Mathematically, how many distinct sets of four stations can be chosen to form an isosceles trapezoid?
