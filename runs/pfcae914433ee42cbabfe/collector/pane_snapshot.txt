The problem asks: What is the probability that a randomly selected number from
[0,1] is algebraic?

This is a standard measure theory problem. The algebraic numbers are countable,
and [0,1] has Lebesgue measure 1. A countable set has measure 0. So the
probability is 0.

Proof

Claim. The probability that a randomly selected number from $[0,1]$ is algebraic
is $0$.

Setup. "Randomly selected from $[0,1]$" means selected according to Lebesgue
measure $\lambda$ on $[0,1]$, the uniform probability measure with $
\lambda([0,1])=1$. The desired probability is $
\lambda(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\cap[0,1])$, where $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ denotes
the set of real algebraic numbers.

Step 1. The algebraic numbers are countable.

A real number $\alpha$ is algebraic if it is a root of some nonzero polynomial
$p(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]$. Clearing denominators, every algebraic real is a root of
a nonzero polynomial with integer coefficients. Let

$$P_n={p(x)=a_dx^d+\cdots+a_1x+a_0\in\mathbb{Z}[x]\setminus{0}:\max_i|a_i|\le n,
\ \deg p\le n}.$$

Each $P_n$ is finite (there are at most $(2n+1)^{n+1}$ choices of coefficients,
minus the zero polynomial). Every nonzero integer polynomial of degree $d$ with
coefficients bounded in absolute value by $n$ belongs to $P_{\max(d,n)}$. Hence

$$\mathbb{Z}[x]\setminus{0}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}P_n$$

is a countable union of finite sets, so $\mathbb{Z}[x]\setminus{0}$ is
countable.

Each nonzero polynomial of degree $d$ has at most $d$ real roots. Therefore the
set of real algebraic numbers is

$$\overline{\mathbb{Q}}=\bigcup_{p\in\mathbb{Z}[x]\setminus{0}}
{x\in\mathbb{R}:p(x)=0},$$

a countable union of finite sets. Thus $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ is countable, and
so is $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\cap[0,1]$.

Step 2. A countable subset of $\mathbb{R}$ has Lebesgue measure zero.

Let $A={a_1,a_2,a_3,\ldots}$ be any countable subset of $\mathbb{R}$. Fix $
\varepsilon>0$. Cover each point $a_k$ by the open interval

$$I_k=\left(a_k-\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{k+2}},,a_k+\frac{\varepsilon}
{2^{k+2}}\right),$$

which has length $\varepsilon/2^{k+1}$. Then $A\subseteq\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}
I_k$, and by countable subadditivity of Lebesgue measure,

$$\lambda(A)\le\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda(I_k)=\sum_{k=1}
^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{k+1}}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<\varepsilon.$$

Since $\varepsilon>0$ was arbitrary, $\lambda(A)=0$.

Step 3. Conclusion.

Applying Step 2 to $A=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\cap[0,1]$, which is countable by
Step 1,

$$\lambda!\left(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\cap[0,1]\right)=0.$$

Therefore the probability that a uniformly random element of $[0,1]$ is
algebraic equals

$$\boxed{0}.$$

Equivalently, a uniformly random real number in $[0,1]$ is transcendental with
probability $1$.
