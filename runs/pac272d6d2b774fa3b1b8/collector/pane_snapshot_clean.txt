# Desingularization of the Tacnode $y^2 = x^4$ by Blowing up $(y, x^2)$

## Setup and the singular point

Let $C \subset \mathbb{A}^2 = \operatorname{Spec} k[x,y]$ be the tacnode curve $
C: F = y^2 - x^4 = 0$. Since $F = (y - x^2)(y + x^2)$, the curve is the union of
 two parabolas $C_1: y = x^2$ and $C_2: y = -x^2$, tangent to each other at the
origin. The singular locus is found from
$$\frac{\partial F}{\partial x} = -4x^3, \qquad \frac{\partial F}{\partial y} =
2y,$$
which both vanish only at $(x,y) = (0,0)$. So $C$ has a unique singular point $p
 = (0,0)$ — a tacnode (two smooth branches with first-order tangency).

## Why the ideal $(y, x^2)$

The coordinate ring of $C$ is $A = k[x,y]/(y^2 - x^4)$. Its normalization is
$$\widetilde{A} \;=\; k[x,y]/(y - x^2) \;\times\; k[x,y]/(y + x^2) \;\cong\; k[t
] \times k[t],$$
with the embedding $A \hookrightarrow \widetilde{A}$ sending $x \mapsto (t, t)$
and $y \mapsto (t^2, -t^2)$. The **conductor ideal** $\mathfrak{c} = \{a \in A :
 a\,\widetilde{A} \subseteq A\}$ is
$$\mathfrak{c} = (y,\, x^2) \subset A.$$
Indeed $y \cdot (1,1) = (y,y) \in A$ and $y \cdot (t, -t) = (t^2 y, -t^2 y)$...
a direct check shows $(y, x^2)$ is the largest ideal of $A$ that is also an idea
l of $\widetilde{A}$; equivalently it is the annihilator of $\widetilde{A}/A$. B
lowing up the conductor is the canonical one-step normalization procedure.

Lifting back to $\mathbb{A}^2$, we blow up the ambient space along $I = (y, x^2)
 \subset k[x,y]$ and take the proper transform of $C$.

## The blowup $\pi: \widetilde{X} = \operatorname{Bl}_{(y,x^2)} \mathbb{A}^2 \to
 \mathbb{A}^2$

The blowup of $\mathbb{A}^2$ along $I = (y, x^2)$ is the closed subscheme of $\m
athbb{A}^2 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ (with homogeneous coordinates $[T_0 : T_1]$) cut
 out by the single equation
$$y\,T_1 \;=\; x^2\,T_0.$$
That is,
$$\widetilde{X} = \operatorname{Proj}\, k[x,y][T_0,T_1]\big/\bigl(y\,T_1 - x^2\,
T_0\bigr) \;\subset\; \mathbb{A}^2 \times \mathbb{P}^1,$$
and $\pi$ is the projection onto $\mathbb{A}^2$. The center $V(y, x^2) = \{(0,0)
\}$ is replaced by the exceptional divisor $E \cong \mathbb{P}^1$.

## Chart computation

### Chart $U_0$: $T_0 \neq 0$, set $t = T_1/T_0$

The blowup relation becomes $y\,t = x^2$, so the chart ring is
$$k[x,y,t]\big/(y\,t - x^2), \qquad \text{i.e. } x^2 = y\,t.$$
The **total transform** of $C$ is obtained by pulling back $y^2 - x^4$:
$$y^2 - x^4 = y^2 - (y\,t)^2 = y^2(1 - t^2).$$
The exceptional divisor is $V(I\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}) = V(y,\, y\,t)
= V(y)$, i.e. $y = 0$ (which forces $x = 0$, with $t$ free — a copy of $\mathbb{
A}^1$). The factor $y^2$ records the exceptional multiplicity $2$. Removing it,
the **proper transform** $\widetilde{C}$ in this chart is
$$1 - t^2 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t = 1 \;\text{ or }\; t = -1,$$
together with $x^2 = y\,t$. Thus:
- $t = 1$: $\;y = x^2$ — the first branch, separated.
- $t = -1$: $\;y = -x^2$ — the second branch, separated.

These two branches are **disjoint** in $U_0$ because $t = 1$ and $t = -1$ are di
stinct values.

### Chart $U_1$: $T_1 \neq 0$, set $s = T_0/T_1$

The relation becomes $y = x^2\,s$, so the chart ring is
$$k[x,y,s]\big/(y - x^2\,s), \qquad \text{i.e. } y = x^2\,s.$$
The total transform:
$$y^2 - x^4 = x^4 s^2 - x^4 = x^4(s^2 - 1).$$
The exceptional divisor is $V(I\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}) = V(x^2\,s,\, x
^2) = V(x^2)$, i.e. $x = 0$ (with $s$ free). Removing the exceptional factor $x^
4$, the proper transform is
$$s^2 - 1 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad s = 1 \;\text{ or }\; s = -1,$$
with $y = x^2\,s$. Again two disjoint branches $y = x^2$ and $y = -x^2$.

The two charts glue consistently: on the overlap $T_0 T_1 \neq 0$ we have $s = 1
/t$, and $t = \pm 1 \leftrightarrow s = \pm 1$, matching the two branches.

## The proper transform is smooth (normalization achieved)

The proper transform $\widetilde{C}$ is the disjoint union of two irreducible co
mponents:
$$\widetilde{C}_1:\; y = x^2, \qquad \widetilde{C}_2:\; y = -x^2,$$
each a smooth parabola isomorphic to $\mathbb{A}^1$. They meet the exceptional d
ivisor $E \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ at two **distinct** points:
- $\widetilde{C}_1$ meets $E$ at $[T_0:T_1] = [1:1]$ (in $U_0$: $t = 1$, $x = y
= 0$),
- $\widetilde{C}_2$ meets $E$ at $[T_0:T_1] = [1:-1]$ (in $U_0$: $t = -1$, $x =
y = 0$).

Since the two branches are separated (they no longer share a point), $\widetilde
{C}$ is **smooth** — every point has a nonsingular local ring. In particular:
- On $\widetilde{C}_1$, the local equation $y - x^2 = 0$ has $\partial/\partial
x = -2x$, $\partial/\partial y = 1 \neq 0$ everywhere — smooth.
- On $\widetilde{C}_2$, the local equation $y + x^2 = 0$ has $\partial/\partial
y = 1 \neq 0$ everywhere — smooth.
- The two components are disjoint, so there is no intersection singularity.

## The map $\pi|_{\widetilde{C}}: \widetilde{C} \to C$ is the normalization

- **Away from the origin**: $\pi$ is an isomorphism off the center $V(y,x^2) = \
{(0,0)\}$, so $\widetilde{C} \setminus \pi^{-1}(p) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} C \set
minus \{p\}$, which is already smooth (the two parabolas only meet at $p$).
- **Over the origin**: $\pi^{-1}(p) \cap \widetilde{C}$ consists of exactly two
points, one on each branch, corresponding to the two tangent directions of the t
acnode.

Thus $\pi|_{\widetilde{C}}: \widetilde{C} \to C$ is finite and birational, $\wid
etilde{C}$ is smooth (hence normal), and the fiber over the singular point has o
ne point per branch — this is precisely the **normalization** $\widetilde{C} \co
ng \operatorname{Spec}\widetilde{A} \cong \mathbb{A}^1 \sqcup \mathbb{A}^1$.

## Conclusion

Blowing up $\mathbb{A}^2$ along the ideal $(y, x^2)$ — the conductor ideal of th
e tacnode — and taking the proper transform of $C: y^2 = x^4$ resolves the singu
larity **in a single step**. The two tangent branches $y = x^2$ and $y = -x^2$,
which met at the origin with first-order contact, are separated: each becomes a
smooth component meeting the exceptional $\mathbb{P}^1$ at a distinct point. The
 resulting proper transform is the disjoint union of two smooth parabolas, i.e.
the normalization of $C$, and the induced map $\widetilde{C} \to C$ is finite, b
irational, and an isomorphism over the smooth locus. The tacnode is desingulariz
ed.
