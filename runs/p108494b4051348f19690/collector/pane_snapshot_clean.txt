**Answer: Yes, it follows that $A \cong B$.**

**Proof.**

The natural isomorphism $\mathcal{C}(-,A) \cong \mathcal{C}(-,B)$ is a natural i
somorphism between the covariant representable functors
$$\mathcal{C}(-,A),\ \mathcal{C}(-,B) : \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{Se
t}.$$

We apply the **Yoneda lemma**. Recall that for any functor $F : \mathcal{C}^{\ma
thrm{op}} \to \mathbf{Set}$ and any object $A \in \mathcal{C}$, the Yoneda lemma
 gives a bijection
$$\mathrm{Nat}(\mathcal{C}(-,A),\, F) \;\cong\; F(A)$$
which is natural in both $A$ and $F$. Under this bijection, a natural transforma
tion $\alpha : \mathcal{C}(-,A) \to F$ is sent to the element $\alpha_A(\mathrm{
id}_A) \in F(A)$.

Apply this with $F = \mathcal{C}(-,B)$. We obtain a bijection
$$\mathrm{Nat}(\mathcal{C}(-,A),\, \mathcal{C}(-,B)) \;\cong\; \mathcal{C}(A,B).
$$

Concretely, a natural transformation $\alpha : \mathcal{C}(-,A) \to \mathcal{C}(
-,B)$ is sent to the morphism
$$f := \alpha_A(\mathrm{id}_A) : A \to B,$$
and conversely every morphism $f : A \to B$ determines a natural transformation
(namely post-composition by $f$):
$$\alpha_X(g) = f \circ g, \qquad g : X \to A.$$

Now we use the fact that this bijection is **natural in $F$** (and hence in $B$)
. The key observation is:

> $\alpha$ is a natural **isomorphism** if and only if the corresponding morphis
m $f = \alpha_A(\mathrm{id}_A) : A \to B$ is an **isomorphism** in $\mathcal{C}$
.

We verify this. Suppose $\alpha : \mathcal{C}(-,A) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \mathc
al{C}(-,B)$ is a natural isomorphism, with inverse $\beta : \mathcal{C}(-,B) \xr
ightarrow{\;\sim\;} \mathcal{C}(-,A)$. Let
$$f = \alpha_A(\mathrm{id}_A) : A \to B, \qquad g = \beta_B(\mathrm{id}_B) : B \
to A.$$

Since $\beta \circ \alpha = \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}(-,A)}$, evaluating at $A$ o
n $\mathrm{id}_A$:
$$\beta_A(\alpha_A(\mathrm{id}_A)) = \mathrm{id}_A \implies \beta_A(f) = \mathrm
{id}_A.$$
By the Yoneda correspondence, $\beta_A(f) = g \circ f$, so
$$g \circ f = \mathrm{id}_A.$$

Similarly, since $\alpha \circ \beta = \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}(-,B)}$, evaluati
ng at $B$ on $\mathrm{id}_B$:
$$\alpha_B(\beta_B(\mathrm{id}_B)) = \mathrm{id}_B \implies \alpha_B(g) = \mathr
m{id}_B.$$
By the Yoneda correspondence, $\alpha_B(g) = f \circ g$, so
$$f \circ g = \mathrm{id}_B.$$

Therefore $f : A \to B$ is an isomorphism with inverse $g : B \to A$, i.e., $A \
cong B$.

Conversely, if $f : A \to B$ is an isomorphism, then post-composition by $f$ def
ines a natural isomorphism $\mathcal{C}(-,A) \cong \mathcal{C}(-,B)$ (with inver
se given by post-composition by $f^{-1}$).

**Conclusion.** The Yoneda embedding $A \mapsto \mathcal{C}(-,A)$ is **fully fai
thful**, so a natural isomorphism $\mathcal{C}(-,A) \cong \mathcal{C}(-,B)$ forc
es $A \cong B$ in $\mathcal{C}$. $\blacksquare$
