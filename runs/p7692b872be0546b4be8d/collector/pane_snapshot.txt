Looking at this problem, I need to find the probability that a random point X in
 the circumcircle of triangle ABC is further from B than from both A and C.

## Setup

Triangle ABC has angles A = 60°, B = 80°, C = 40°, inscribed in its circumcircle
 (the sanctuary). By the inscribed angle theorem, the arcs opposite each vertex
are:
- Arc BC (opposite A) = 2 × 60° = 120°
- Arc CA (opposite B) = 2 × 80° = 160°
- Arc AB (opposite C) = 2 × 40° = 80°

(Check: 120° + 160° + 80° = 360° ✓)

## Key Geometric Insight

The condition "further from B than from A" means X is closer to A than to B, i.e
., X lies on A's side of the **perpendicular bisector of AB**.

The condition "further from B than from C" means X lies on C's side of the **per
pendicular bisector of BC**.

**Critical fact:** Since A, B, C lie on the circumcircle, the perpendicular bise
ctor of any chord passes through the center O. So both bisectors are **diameters
** through O, and each divides the circle into a semicircle. The Zone of Isolati
on is the intersection of two semicircles — a **sector** of the circle.

## Computing the Sector Angle

Place the circumcircle with center O at origin, radius R. Set B at angle 0°.

- B at 0°, C at 120°, A at 280°.

**Perpendicular bisector of AB:** Passes through O and the midpoint of arc AB. T
he midpoint of arc AB (from 280° to 360°) is at 320°. So this bisector is the di
ameter along 320°/140°.

The semicircle containing A (at 280°) spans angles (140°, 320°).

**Perpendicular bisector of BC:** Passes through O and the midpoint of arc BC (f
rom 0° to 120°), which is at 60°. So this bisector is the diameter along 60°/240
°.

The semicircle containing C (at 120°) spans angles (60°, 240°).

## Intersection

Zone of Isolation = (140°, 320°) ∩ (60°, 240°) = **(140°, 240°)**

This is a sector with angular measure **240° − 140° = 100°**.

(Note: 100° = 180° − 80° = 180° − angle B, which is the expected relationship —
the sector opposite to vertex B has measure 180° minus the angle at B.)

## Probability

The sector of angle 100° has area = (100°/360°) × πR² = (5/18)πR².

The total circle area = πR².

$$P = \frac{5/18 \cdot \pi R^2}{\pi R^2} = \frac{5}{18}$$

Since gcd(5, 18) = 1, we have p = 5, q = 18.

$$p \cdot q = 5 \times 18 = \boxed{90}$$
