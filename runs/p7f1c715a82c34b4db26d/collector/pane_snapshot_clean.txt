# Proof

**Answer: Yes.** Every nonreflexive Banach space contains a closed subspace that
 lacks the best approximation property.

---

## Preliminaries

A closed subspace $V \subseteq X$ has the **best approximation property** (is *p
roximinal*) if for every $x \in X$ there exists $v \in V$ with $\|x - v\| = \ope
ratorname{dist}(x, V) := \inf_{w \in V} \|x - w\|$.

We use two standard facts.

**Fact 1 (James' Theorem, 1964).** A Banach space $X$ is reflexive if and only i
f every $f \in X^*$ attains its norm on the closed unit ball $B_X$, i.e., for ev
ery $f \in X^*$ there exists $x \in B_X$ with $|f(x)| = \|f\|$.

(The "reflexive $\Rightarrow$ all functionals attain norm" direction is immediat
e: $B_X$ is weakly compact and $f$ is weakly continuous, so $|f|$ attains its ma
ximum. The converse is the deep part.)

**Fact 2 (Distance to a hyperplane).** For $f \in X^* \setminus \{0\}$ and $H =
\ker f$,
$$\operatorname{dist}(x, H) = \frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|} \quad \text{for all } x \in X
.$$

*Proof of Fact 2.* For any $h \in H$: $\|x - h\| \ge |f(x - h)|/\|f\| = |f(x)|/\
|f\|$, giving $\ge$. For $\le$: given $\varepsilon > 0$, pick $y_\varepsilon$ wi
th $\|y_\varepsilon\| = 1$ and $|f(y_\varepsilon)| > \|f\| - \varepsilon$. Set $
h_\varepsilon = x - \frac{f(x)}{f(y_\varepsilon)}\,y_\varepsilon \in H$. Then $\
|x - h_\varepsilon\| = \frac{|f(x)|}{|f(y_\varepsilon)|} < \frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\| -
 \varepsilon}$. Let $\varepsilon \to 0$. $\square$

---

## Key Proposition

**Proposition.** Let $f \in X^* \setminus \{0\}$ and $H = \ker f$. Then $H$ is p
roximinal if and only if $f$ attains its norm on $B_X$.

*Proof.*

**$(\Rightarrow)$** Suppose $H$ is proximinal. Pick $x_0 \in X$ with $f(x_0) \ne
q 0$. There exists $h \in H$ with $\|x_0 - h\| = \operatorname{dist}(x_0, H) = |
f(x_0)|/\|f\|$ (using Fact 2). Set $u = (x_0 - h)/\|x_0 - h\|$. Then $\|u\| = 1$
 and
$$|f(u)| = \frac{|f(x_0)|}{\|x_0 - h\|} = \frac{|f(x_0)|}{|f(x_0)|/\|f\|} = \|f\
|.$$
So $f$ attains its norm.

**$(\Leftarrow)$** Suppose $f$ attains its norm: there exists $u \in X$, $\|u\|
= 1$, $|f(u)| = \|f\|$. Replacing $u$ by $-u$ if necessary, assume $f(u) = \|f\|
$. For any $x \in X$, set $\alpha = f(x)/\|f\|$ and $h = x - \alpha u$. Then
$$f(h) = f(x) - \alpha\, f(u) = f(x) - \frac{f(x)}{\|f\|}\cdot\|f\| = 0,$$
so $h \in H$, and
$$\|x - h\| = \|\alpha u\| = |\alpha|\,\|u\| = \frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|} = \operatorn
ame{dist}(x, H).$$
Thus $h$ is a best approximation of $x$ in $H$, so $H$ is proximinal. $\square$

---

## Main Proof

Let $X$ be a **nonreflexive** Banach space. By James' Theorem (Fact 1), not ever
y functional attains its norm: there exists $f \in X^* \setminus \{0\}$ such tha
t $|f(x)| < \|f\|$ for all $x \in B_X$.

Set $V = \ker f$. This is a **closed subspace** of $X$ (a closed hyperplane, sin
ce $f$ is continuous and nonzero).

By the Proposition, $V$ is proximinal if and only if $f$ attains its norm. Since
 $f$ does **not** attain its norm, $V$ is **not proximinal**.

Therefore there exists some $x \in X$ that has no best approximation in $V$ — i.
e., $V$ lacks the best approximation property.

---

## Conclusion

Every nonreflexive Banach space $X$ contains a closed subspace $V$ (namely, the
kernel of a continuous linear functional that fails to attain its norm, whose ex
istence is guaranteed by James' theorem) that lacks the best approximation prope
rty. $\blacksquare$
