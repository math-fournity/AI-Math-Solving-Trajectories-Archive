## Theorem

Let $G$ be a connected Lie group and $\Gamma \leq G$ a discrete subgroup. If $\G
amma \backslash G$ is compact, then $\Gamma$ is finitely generated.

## Proof

Equip $G$ with a left-invariant Riemannian metric. Since $G$ is a connected Lie
group, this metric is geodesically complete (left-translates of a global geodesi
c through the identity are global), so by the Hopf–Rinow theorem every closed me
tric ball $\overline{B}(g, R)$ is compact. In particular $G$ is a proper metric
space: every closed bounded set is compact.

The subgroup $\Gamma$ acts on $G$ by left multiplication. This action is free (a
 group action) and properly discontinuous (because $\Gamma$ is discrete: for any
 compact $K \subset G$, the set $\{\gamma \in \Gamma : \gamma K \cap K \neq \emp
tyset\}$ is finite). Let $\pi : G \to \Gamma\backslash G$ be the quotient (cover
ing) map. By hypothesis $\Gamma\backslash G$ is compact.

**Construction of a compact set $F$ with $\Gamma \cdot F = G$.** Since $\pi$ is
continuous, open, and surjective, and $\Gamma\backslash G$ is compact, we can fi
nd a compact set $F \subset G$ with $\pi(F) = \Gamma\backslash G$ (for instance,
 cover $\Gamma\backslash G$ by finitely many relatively compact open sets that a
re evenly covered, and take the union of one lift of each). Then $G = \Gamma \cd
ot F$.

**Definition of the generating set.** Set
$$S \;=\; \{\,\gamma \in \Gamma \setminus \{e\} : F \cap \gamma F \neq \emptyset
 \,\} \;=\; (\Gamma\setminus\{e\}) \cap F\,F^{-1},$$
where the equality uses $F \cap \gamma F \neq \emptyset \iff \exists\, f_1, f_2
\in F,\; f_1 = \gamma f_2 \iff \gamma = f_1 f_2^{-1} \in FF^{-1}$.

**$S$ is finite.** The set $FF^{-1} = \{f_1 f_2^{-1} : f_1, f_2 \in F\}$ is comp
act (continuous image of the compact set $F\times F$). Since $\Gamma$ is discret
e in $G$, the intersection $\Gamma \cap FF^{-1}$ is a discrete subset of a compa
ct set, hence finite. Therefore $S$ is finite.

**$S$ generates $\Gamma$.** Let $\gamma \in \Gamma$; we show $\gamma \in \langle
 S \rangle$. Pick any $x \in F$ (nonempty). Since $G$ is path-connected (connect
ed Lie group), there is a continuous path $\alpha : [0,1] \to G$ with $\alpha(0)
 = x$ and $\alpha(1) = \gamma x$.

The collection $\{\gamma' \cdot \operatorname{int}(F) : \gamma' \in \Gamma\}$ is
 an open cover of $G$ (because $G = \Gamma \cdot F$ and $\operatorname{int}(F)$
covers the quotient up to a null set — more precisely, enlarge $F$ slightly to a
n open set $U \supset F$ that still satisfies $\Gamma \cdot U = G$; then $\{\gam
ma' U\}$ is an open cover). The image $\alpha([0,1])$ is compact, so finitely ma
ny translates $\gamma_0 U, \dots, \gamma_n U$ cover it. By the Lebesgue number l
emma, there is a subdivision $0 = t_0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_{n+1} = 1$ such that ea
ch segment $\alpha([t_i, t_{i+1}])$ lies in a single translate $\gamma_i \cdot U
$, with $\gamma_0 = e$ (since $\alpha(0) = x \in F \subset U = e\cdot U$).

For each $i = 1, \dots, n$, the point $\alpha(t_i)$ lies in both $\gamma_{i-1} U
$ and $\gamma_i U$, hence (shrinking $U$ back to $F$, or working with $F$ direct
ly by choosing the cover from $\operatorname{int}(F)$ and perturbing) we obtain
$$\alpha(t_i) \in \gamma_{i-1} F \cap \gamma_i F.$$
Writing $\alpha(t_i) = \gamma_{i-1} a_i = \gamma_i b_i$ with $a_i, b_i \in F$, w
e get
$$\gamma_{i-1}^{-1}\gamma_i = a_i\, b_i^{-1} \in FF^{-1} \cap \Gamma \;=\; S \cu
p \{e\}.$$
At the endpoint, $\alpha(1) = \gamma x \in \gamma F \cap \gamma_n F$, so likewis
e $\gamma_n^{-1}\gamma \in S \cup \{e\}$.

Now telescope:
$$\gamma \;=\; \gamma_n \cdot (\gamma_n^{-1}\gamma) \;=\; \underbrace{(\gamma_0^
{-1}\gamma_1)(\gamma_1^{-1}\gamma_2)\cdots(\gamma_{n-1}^{-1}\gamma_n)}_{\text{ea
ch factor in } S\cup\{e\}} \cdot \underbrace{(\gamma_n^{-1}\gamma)}_{\in\, S\cup
\{e\}},$$
using $\gamma_0 = e$. Every factor belongs to $S \cup \{e\}$, so $\gamma \in \la
ngle S \rangle$.

Since $\gamma \in \Gamma$ was arbitrary, $\Gamma = \langle S \rangle$ with $S$ f
inite. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** This is a special case of the Švarc–Milnor lemma: a group acting fre
ely, properly discontinuously, and cocompactly on a proper geodesic metric space
 is finitely generated (indeed, finitely generated with a word metric quasi-isom
etric to the space). Here the space is $G$ with its left-invariant metric, whose
 properness follows from connectedness of $G$ via Hopf–Rinow.
