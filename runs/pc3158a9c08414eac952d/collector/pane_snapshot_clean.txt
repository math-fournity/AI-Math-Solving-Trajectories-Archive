## Proof

**Yes.** Under the stated hypotheses, $\alpha \in C^\infty(\overline{M})$.

---

### Setup and notation

Let $(M,g)$ be a smooth, bounded, oriented Riemannian manifold with boundary, $\
dim M = n$. Let $d$ be the exterior derivative and $\delta = (-1)^{n(p+1)+1}\sta
r d\star$ the codifferential on $p$-forms. The Hodge Laplacian is $\Delta = d\de
lta + \delta d$, a second-order elliptic operator on $\Omega^p(M)$.

Let $\nu$ be the outward unit normal along $\partial M$, $\nu^\sharp = g(\nu,\cd
ot)$ the dual $1$-form, and $\iota_\nu$ interior multiplication. We write $\math
bf{t}\alpha$ and $\mathbf{n}\alpha$ for the tangential and normal parts of $\alp
ha$ at the boundary:
$$\mathbf{t}\alpha = \alpha\big|_{\text{tangent directions}}, \qquad \mathbf{n}\
alpha = \alpha\big|_{\text{normal direction}}.$$
Concretely, $\mathbf{t}\alpha = \iota_\nu(\nu^\sharp\wedge\alpha)$ and $\mathbf{
n}\alpha = \nu^\sharp\wedge\iota_\nu\alpha$, so that
$$\iota_\nu\alpha = 0 \iff \mathbf{n}\alpha = 0 \iff \alpha = \mathbf{t}\alpha \
quad\text{on }\partial M,$$
$$\nu^\sharp\wedge\alpha = 0 \iff \mathbf{t}\alpha = 0 \iff \alpha = \mathbf{n}\
alpha \quad\text{on }\partial M.$$

### The two classical elliptic boundary conditions

For the Hodge Laplacian, the two standard *elliptic* boundary conditions (satisf
ying the Agmon–Douglis–Nirenberg complementing / Lopatinski–Shapiro condition) a
re:

| | Absolute | Relative |
|---|---|---|
| Boundary operator $B$ | $\iota_\nu\alpha = 0,\quad \iota_\nu(d\alpha) = 0$ | $
\nu^\sharp\wedge\alpha = 0,\quad \nu^\sharp\wedge(\delta\alpha) = 0$ |

That the pairs $(\Delta, B_{\mathrm{abs}})$ and $(\Delta, B_{\mathrm{rel}})$ def
ine elliptic boundary value problems is classical; it is established in the work
 of Kodaira, Duff, Morrey, and Friedrichs, and systematically in G. Schwarz, *Ho
dge Decomposition—A Method for Solving Boundary Value Problems*, LNM 1607, Sprin
ger, 1995 (see also Morrey, *Multiple Integrals in the Calculus of Variations*,
Ch. 7). The verification of the complementing condition uses the symbol calculus
 for the Hodge Laplacian restricted to the half-space: the boundary conditions s
elect exactly half of the decaying modes of the constant-coefficient frozen prob
lem, which is the content of the Lopatinski condition.

### Reduction on the harmonic kernel

The hypothesis is that $\alpha$ is **harmonic**: $d\alpha = 0$ and $\delta\alpha
 = 0$ (in the weak/distributional sense, since $\alpha\in W^{1,2}$). Consequentl
y the *companion* boundary condition in each pair is automatically satisfied:

- **Absolute:** $\iota_\nu(d\alpha) = \iota_\nu(0) = 0$ automatically; the remai
ning condition is $\iota_\nu\alpha = 0$.
- **Relative:** $\nu^\sharp\wedge(\delta\alpha) = \nu^\sharp\wedge(0) = 0$ autom
atically; the remaining condition is $\nu^\sharp\wedge\alpha = 0$.

Thus, **on the harmonic kernel**, each of the two elliptic boundary conditions c
ollapses to the single scalar boundary condition stated in the problem. A $W^{1,
2}$ harmonic form satisfying $\iota_\nu\alpha=0$ (resp. $\nu^\sharp\wedge\alpha=
0$) on $\partial M$ is therefore a weak solution of the *full* elliptic boundary
 value problem
$$\Delta\alpha = 0,\qquad B_{\mathrm{abs}}\,\alpha = 0 \quad(\text{resp. } B_{\m
athrm{rel}}\,\alpha = 0),$$
with homogeneous interior and boundary data.

### Elliptic regularity (bootstrap)

The interior elliptic regularity of $\Delta$ and the boundary regularity for the
 elliptic BVP $(\Delta, B)$ give the following standard estimate (Schwarz, Thm.
2.2.7 / Morrey, §7.2): for every $k\ge 0$ there is $C_k$ such that
$$\|\alpha\|_{W^{k+2,2}(M)} \le C_k\bigl(\|\Delta\alpha\|_{W^{k,2}(M)} + \|B\alp
ha\|_{W^{k+1/2,2}(\partial M)} + \|\alpha\|_{W^{1,2}(M)}\bigr).$$

Applying this inductively with $\Delta\alpha = 0$ and $B\alpha = 0$ (both in the
 appropriate trace sense, justified by the $W^{1,2}$ hypothesis and the boundary
 condition), we obtain
$$\alpha \in W^{k,2}(M) \quad\text{for every } k\ge 1.$$

### Sobolev embedding

Since $M$ is compact (bounded with smooth boundary), the Sobolev embedding $W^{k
,2}(M)\hookrightarrow C^{l}(\overline M)$ holds for $k > l + n/2$. Taking $k$ ar
bitrarily large yields
$$\alpha \in C^{l}(\overline M) \quad\text{for every } l\ge 0,$$
i.e. $\alpha \in C^\infty(\overline M)$.

### Conclusion

A $W^{1,2}$ harmonic $p$-form on a smooth bounded oriented Riemannian manifold-w
ith-boundary, satisfying either $\iota_\nu\alpha\big|_{\partial M}=0$ (absolute)
 or $\nu^\sharp\wedge\alpha\big|_{\partial M}=0$ (relative), is smooth. The reas
on is that harmonicity makes the companion boundary condition automatic, so the
single stated condition completes the full elliptic absolute/relative boundary v
alue problem, and elliptic regularity bootstraps $W^{1,2}$ to $W^{k,2}$ for all
$k$, whence Sobolev embedding gives $C^\infty$.
