## Proof

**Setup.** Let $M = \mathbb{C}^g/\Gamma$ where $\Gamma \subset \mathbb{C}^g$ is
a lattice acting by translations. A holomorphic vector bundle $E$ of rank $r$ ov
er $M$ is described by a factor of automorphy $J:\Gamma\times\mathbb{C}^g\to GL(
r,\mathbb{C})$ satisfying the cocycle condition
$$J(\gamma'\gamma,\, x) \;=\; J(\gamma',\,\gamma x)\,J(\gamma,\,x), \qquad \gamm
a',\gamma\in\Gamma,\; x\in\mathbb{C}^g.$$
Concretely, $E \cong (\mathbb{C}^g\times\mathbb{C}^r)/\Gamma$ where $\Gamma$ act
s by
$$\gamma\cdot(x,v) \;=\; \bigl(x+\gamma,\; J(\gamma,x)\,v\bigr).$$

**The lift and the induced lattice automorphism.** Since $\mathbb{C}^g$ is the u
niversal cover of $M$, the holomorphic diffeomorphism $f:M\to M$ lifts to a holo
morphic map $\tilde f:\mathbb{C}^g\to\mathbb{C}^g$ with $\pi\circ\tilde f = f\ci
rc\pi$. Because $f$ is a diffeomorphism, $\tilde f$ is a biholomorphism of $\mat
hbb{C}^g$.

For each $\gamma\in\Gamma$ and $x\in\mathbb{C}^g$:
$$\pi\bigl(\tilde f(x+\gamma)\bigr) = f\bigl(\pi(x+\gamma)\bigr) = f\bigl(\pi(x)
\bigr) = \pi\bigl(\tilde f(x)\bigr),$$
so $\tilde f(x+\gamma)-\tilde f(x)\in\Gamma$. By continuity and discreteness of
$\Gamma$, this difference is independent of $x$; denote it $\rho(\gamma)$. Then
$$\tilde f(x+\gamma) = \tilde f(x) + \rho(\gamma), \qquad \rho:\Gamma\to\Gamma.$
$
One checks $\rho$ is a group homomorphism, and since $f$ is a diffeomorphism, $\
rho$ is an automorphism of $\Gamma$. **In general $\rho\neq\mathrm{id}$.**

**Computing the correct factor of automorphy for $f^*E$.** The pullback $\pi^*(f
^*E) = \tilde f^*(\pi^* E)$ is the trivial bundle $\mathbb{C}^g\times\mathbb{C}^
r$ (since $\pi^*E$ is trivial on $\mathbb{C}^g$). A point in the fiber at $x$ is
 $(x, v)$ with $v\in(\pi^*E)_{\tilde f(x)} = \mathbb{C}^r$.

The $\Gamma$-equivariant structure: $\gamma$ must map the fiber at $x$ to the fi
ber at $x+\gamma$. The fiber at $x$ is $(\pi^*E)_{\tilde f(x)}$ and the fiber at
 $x+\gamma$ is $(\pi^*E)_{\tilde f(x+\gamma)} = (\pi^*E)_{\tilde f(x)+\rho(\gamm
a)}$. The action of $\rho(\gamma)$ on $\pi^*E$ transports the fiber at $\tilde f
(x)$ to the fiber at $\tilde f(x)+\rho(\gamma)$ via $J(\rho(\gamma),\,\tilde f(x
))$. Therefore the factor of automorphy for $f^*E$ is:
$$\boxed{J_{f^*E}(\gamma,\,x) \;=\; J\bigl(\rho(\gamma),\;\tilde f(x)\bigr).}$$

**Verification of the cocycle condition.**
$$J_{f^*E}(\gamma'\gamma,\,x) = J\bigl(\rho(\gamma')\rho(\gamma),\,\tilde f(x)\b
igr) = J\bigl(\rho(\gamma'),\,\rho(\gamma)\tilde f(x)\bigr)\,J\bigl(\rho(\gamma)
,\,\tilde f(x)\bigr).$$
Since $\rho(\gamma)\tilde f(x) = \tilde f(x)+\rho(\gamma) = \tilde f(x+\gamma)$,
 this becomes
$$J\bigl(\rho(\gamma'),\,\tilde f(x+\gamma)\bigr)\,J\bigl(\rho(\gamma),\,\tilde
f(x)\bigr) = J_{f^*E}(\gamma',\,\gamma x)\,J_{f^*E}(\gamma,\,x). \quad\checkmark
$$

**The proposed formula fails the cocycle condition when $\rho\neq\mathrm{id}$.**
 The proposed factor of automorphy is $J_f(\gamma,x) = J(\gamma,\,\tilde f(x))$.
 Checking the cocycle condition:
$$J_f(\gamma'\gamma,\,x) = J(\gamma'\gamma,\,\tilde f(x)) = J(\gamma',\,\gamma\,
\tilde f(x))\,J(\gamma,\,\tilde f(x)) = J\bigl(\gamma',\,\tilde f(x)+\gamma\bigr
)\,J_f(\gamma,x).$$
For the cocycle condition to hold we need $J_f(\gamma',\,\gamma x) = J(\gamma',\
,\tilde f(\gamma x)) = J(\gamma',\,\tilde f(x)+\rho(\gamma))$ to equal $J(\gamma
',\,\tilde f(x)+\gamma)$. This requires $\rho(\gamma)=\gamma$ for all $\gamma$,
i.e., $\rho=\mathrm{id}$. **For a general holomorphic diffeomorphism with $\rho\
neq\mathrm{id}$, the proposed $J_f$ does not even satisfy the cocycle condition*
*, so it cannot be a factor of automorphy at all.

**Explicit counterexample.** Let $g=1$, $M=\mathbb{C}/(\mathbb{Z}+\tau\mathbb{Z}
)$ an elliptic curve, and $f:M\to M$ the inversion $f(z)=-z$. The lift is $\tild
e f(z)=-z$, giving $\rho(\gamma)=-\gamma$ (nontrivial). Take $E$ to be a degree-
1 line bundle with classical factor of automorphy (in Appell–Humbert form)
$$J(m+n\tau,\,z) = e^{-2\pi i n z - \pi i n^2\tau}\cdot e^{2\pi i m\theta}$$
for a suitable character parameter $\theta$. The correct pullback factor is $J(-
\gamma,-z)$, which yields a degree-1 bundle with character $\theta\mapsto -\thet
a$ (generally a different line bundle). The proposed formula $J(\gamma,-z)$ give
s
$$J_f(m+n\tau,\,z)=J(m+n\tau,\,-z)=e^{2\pi i n z-\pi i n^2\tau}\,e^{2\pi i m\the
ta},$$
which one checks does **not** satisfy the cocycle condition (the mixed term $e^{
2\pi i n z}$ is incompatible with the lattice relations when $\rho(\gamma)=-\gam
ma\neq\gamma$). Hence $J_f$ is not a valid factor of automorphy, confirming the
formula is wrong.

**When the formula does hold.** The proposed formula $J(\gamma,\tilde f(x))$ is
correct only in the special case $\rho=\mathrm{id}$, which occurs precisely when
 $f$ is a **translation** $f(m)=m+a$. In that case $\tilde f(x)=x+\tilde a$ and
$\rho(\gamma)=\gamma$, so $J_{f^*E}(\gamma,x)=J(\gamma,x+\tilde a)=J(\gamma,\til
de f(x))$, matching the proposed formula.

## Conclusion

**No.** The pullback bundle $f^*(E)$ is **not** characterized by $J_f(\gamma,x)=
J(\gamma,f(x))$ in general. The correct factor of automorphy is
$$J_{f^*E}(\gamma,\,x) \;=\; J\bigl(\rho(\gamma),\,\tilde f(x)\bigr),$$
where $\tilde f:\mathbb{C}^g\to\mathbb{C}^g$ is the lift of $f$ and $\rho\in\mat
hrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$ is the lattice automorphism induced by $f$ (defined by $\tilde
 f(x+\gamma)=\tilde f(x)+\rho(\gamma)$). The proposed formula omits the crucial
substitution $\gamma\mapsto\rho(\gamma)$; when $\rho\neq\mathrm{id}$ it fails to
 satisfy the cocycle condition and therefore is not a valid factor of automorphy
. The formula is correct only for translations ($\rho=\mathrm{id}$).
