## Proof

Let $K$ be an algebraic extension of $\mathbb{Q}$, and let $B$ be a non-singular
 matrix with all entries in $K$. We prove that $\exp(B)$ has at least one entry
not in $K$.

**Arguing by contradiction**, suppose that every entry of $\exp(B)$ also lies in
 $K$.

**Step 1: Eigenvalues of $B$ are algebraic.**
The characteristic polynomial $p_B(x)=\det(xI-B)$ has coefficients in $K$. Its r
oots (the eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n$ of $B$) are algebraic over $K$
. Since $K/\mathbb{Q}$ is algebraic, "algebraic over $K$" implies "algebraic ove
r $\mathbb{Q}$". Hence every $\lambda_i$ is an algebraic number.

**Step 2: Eigenvalues of $\exp(B)$ are algebraic.**
Since all entries of $\exp(B)$ lie in $K$, its characteristic polynomial $p_{\ex
p(B)}(x)$ has coefficients in $K$, so its roots are algebraic over $K$, hence al
gebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Step 3: Eigenvalues of $\exp(B)$ equal $e^{\lambda_i}$.**
Over $\mathbb{C}$, $B$ admits a Jordan normal form $B = PJP^{-1}$, so $\exp(B) =
 P\exp(J)P^{-1}$. Each Jordan block $J_\lambda = \lambda I + N$ (with $N$ nilpot
ent) gives $\exp(J_\lambda) = e^{\lambda}(I + N + \tfrac{N^2}{2!}+\cdots)$, whic
h is upper triangular with $e^{\lambda}$ on the diagonal. Thus the eigenvalues o
f $\exp(B)$ are exactly $e^{\lambda_1},\dots,e^{\lambda_n}$.

**Step 4: Apply the Hermite–Lindemann theorem.**
By Step 2, each $e^{\lambda_i}$ is algebraic. The Hermite–Lindemann theorem (a c
orollary of the Lindemann–Weierstrass theorem) states: *if $\alpha$ is a non-zer
o algebraic number, then $e^{\alpha}$ is transcendental.* Therefore $e^{\lambda_
i}$ algebraic forces $\lambda_i = 0$ for every $i$.

**Step 5: Contradiction.**
All eigenvalues of $B$ being $0$ means
$$\det(B) = \lambda_1\lambda_2\cdots\lambda_n = 0,$$
so $B$ is singular. This contradicts the hypothesis that $B$ is non-singular.

Hence our assumption was false: $\exp(B)$ cannot have all of its entries in $K$.
 Therefore $\exp(B)$ has at least one entry outside the algebraic extension $K$.
