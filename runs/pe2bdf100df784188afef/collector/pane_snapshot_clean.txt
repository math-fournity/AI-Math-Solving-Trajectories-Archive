Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a space $X$ with $H^2(X, \m
athbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and trivial $H^1(X, \mathbb{Z})$ can be a smooth man
ifold.

**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

I exhibit an explicit example: the 2-sphere $X = S^2$.

**$S^2$ is a smooth manifold.** The 2-sphere $S^2 = \{(x,y,z) \in \mathbb{R}^3 :
 x^2+y^2+z^2=1\}$ is a smooth 2-dimensional manifold. It can be covered by two c
oordinate charts (stereographic projection from the north and south poles), with
 smooth transition maps on their overlap. Hence $S^2$ is a smooth manifold.

**Cohomology of $S^2$.** The integral cohomology groups of $S^2$ are well-known
(computed, for instance, via the cellular cochain complex of the standard CW-str
ucture with one 0-cell and one 2-cell):

$$H^k(S^2, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z} & k = 0, \\ 0 & k = 1, \\
\mathbb{Z} & k = 2, \\ 0 & k \geq 3. \end{cases}$$

In particular:
- $H^1(S^2, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$ (trivial first cohomology), and
- $H^2(S^2, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.

**Verification via cellular cohomology.** The CW-structure of $S^2$ consists of
a single 0-cell $e^0$ and a single 2-cell $e^2$, with no 1-cells. The cellular c
ochain complex is:

$$0 \longrightarrow C^0 \cong \mathbb{Z} \stackrel{d^0}{\longrightarrow} C^1 = 0
 \stackrel{d^1}{\longrightarrow} C^2 \cong \mathbb{Z} \stackrel{d^2}{\longrighta
rrow} 0$$

Since $C^1 = 0$, both $d^0$ and $d^1$ are zero maps. Therefore:
- $H^1(S^2, \mathbb{Z}) = \ker d^1 / \operatorname{im} d^0 = 0/0 = 0$,
- $H^2(S^2, \mathbb{Z}) = \ker d^2 / \operatorname{im} d^1 = \mathbb{Z}/0 \cong
\mathbb{Z}$.

**Conclusion.** The 2-sphere $S^2$ is a smooth manifold satisfying $H^2(S^2, \ma
thbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and $H^1(S^2, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$. Therefore, a space wi
th these cohomological properties **can** be a smooth manifold. $\blacksquare$
