## Solution

**Definition.** Let $E$ be a field and $\{K_i\}_{i \in I}$ a collection of subfi
elds of $E$ (the index set $I$ may be finite or infinite). The **composite** (or
 **join**) of the $K_i$, denoted

$$\bigvee_{i \in I} K_i,$$

is the smallest subfield of $E$ containing every $K_i$. Equivalently, it is the
intersection of all subfields of $E$ that contain $\bigcup_{i \in I} K_i$:

$$\bigvee_{i \in I} K_i \;=\; \bigcap_{\substack{L \subseteq E \\ L \text{ a sub
field} \\ \forall i:\, K_i \subseteq L}} L.$$

This intersection is well-defined (it is non-empty since $E$ itself is a qualify
ing subfield, and an arbitrary intersection of subfields sharing the same prime
subfield is again a subfield), and it is manifestly the smallest subfield contai
ning every $K_i$.

---

**Explicit description.** Set $S = \bigcup_{i \in I} K_i \subseteq E$. The compo
site is the field of fractions of the subring of $E$ generated by $S$:

$$\boxed{\;\bigvee_{i \in I} K_i \;=\; \operatorname{Frac}\big\langle S \big\ran
gle_{\text{ring}} \;=\; \left\{\, \frac{a}{b} \;\middle|\; a, b \in \big\langle
S \big\rangle_{\text{ring}},\; b \neq 0 \,\right\} \;\subseteq\; E.\;}$$

Here $\langle S \rangle_{\text{ring}}$ denotes the smallest sub**ring** of $E$ c
ontaining $S$, i.e. the set of all finite $\mathbb{Z}$-linear combinations of fi
nite products of elements of $S$:

$$\big\langle S \big\rangle_{\text{ring}} \;=\; \left\{\, \sum_{\text{finite}} n
_k \cdot x_{k,1} x_{k,2} \cdots x_{k,m_k} \;\middle|\; n_k \in \mathbb{Z},\; x_{
k,j} \in S,\; m_k \ge 0 \,\right\}.$$

Concretely, every element of $\bigvee_i K_i$ is a fraction whose numerator and d
enominator are each *finite* $\mathbb{Z}$-linear combinations of finite products
 of elements drawn from finitely many of the $K_i$.

---

**Proof of the explicit description.**

*Step 1: $\langle S\rangle_{\text{ring}}$ is a subring of $E$ containing every $
K_i$.* It is the intersection of all subrings of $E$ containing $S$, hence a sub
ring; and since each $K_i \subseteq S \subseteq \langle S\rangle_{\text{ring}}$,
 it contains every $K_i$.

*Step 2: $\operatorname{Frac}(\langle S\rangle_{\text{ring}})$ is a subfield of
$E$ containing every $K_i$.* Because $E$ is a field containing $\langle S\rangle
_{\text{ring}}$, the natural map $\operatorname{Frac}(\langle S\rangle_{\text{ri
ng}}) \to E$ sending $a/b \mapsto a\,b^{-1}$ (computed in $E$) is an injective f
ield homomorphism. We identify $\operatorname{Frac}(\langle S\rangle_{\text{ring
}})$ with its image in $E$, making it a subfield of $E$. Since each $K_i$ is a f
ield contained in $\langle S\rangle_{\text{ring}}$, it is contained in this subf
ield. So $\operatorname{Frac}(\langle S\rangle_{\text{ring}})$ is a subfield of
$E$ containing every $K_i$, giving

$$\bigvee_i K_i \;\subseteq\; \operatorname{Frac}\big(\langle S\rangle_{\text{ri
ng}}\big).$$

*Step 3: Reverse inclusion.* Let $L$ be any subfield of $E$ with $K_i \subseteq
L$ for all $i$. Then $S \subseteq L$, and since $L$ is closed under addition, ad
ditive inverses, and multiplication, the whole subring $\langle S\rangle_{\text{
ring}}$ lies in $L$. Since $L$ is a field, it also contains $b^{-1}$ for every n
onzero $b \in \langle S\rangle_{\text{ring}} \subseteq L$, hence contains every
fraction $a/b$ with $a, b \in \langle S\rangle_{\text{ring}}$, $b \neq 0$. Thus
$\operatorname{Frac}(\langle S\rangle_{\text{ring}}) \subseteq L$. Taking the in
tersection over all such $L$ yields

$$\operatorname{Frac}\big(\langle S\rangle_{\text{ring}}\big) \;\subseteq\; \big
cap_L L \;=\; \bigvee_i K_i.$$

Combining Steps 2 and 3 gives equality. $\blacksquare$

---

**Finite-index specialization.** When $I = \{1,\dots,n\}$ is finite, every eleme
nt of $\langle S\rangle_{\text{ring}}$ is a finite $\mathbb{Z}$-linear combinati
on of monomials $x_1^{e_1}\cdots x_n^{e_n}$ with $x_j \in K_j$ and $e_j \ge 0$,
so

$$K_1 \vee \cdots \vee K_n \;=\; \left\{\, \frac{f(x_1,\dots,x_n)}{g(x_1,\dots,x
_n)} \;\middle|\; x_j \in K_j,\; f,g \in \mathbb{Z}[X_1,\dots,X_n],\; g(x_1,\dot
s,x_n)\neq 0 \,\right\}.$$

In particular, for two subfields $K_1, K_2 \subseteq E$:

$$K_1 \vee K_2 \;=\; \left\{\, \frac{\sum_{j} a_j\, b_j}{\sum_{k} c_k\, d_k} \;\
middle|\; a_j, c_k \in K_1,\; b_j, d_k \in K_2,\; \sum_k c_k d_k \neq 0 \,\right
\} \;=\; K_1\!\cdot\! K_2,$$

the latter equality holding when $K_1 \cdot K_2 := \{a\,b : a \in K_1,\, b \in K
_2\}$ is already a field (e.g. when one of $K_1, K_2$ is finite-dimensional over
 the base, or more generally when $K_1 \cdot K_2$ is closed under inverses); in
general $K_1 \vee K_2 = \operatorname{Frac}(K_1 \cdot K_2)$.

---

**Summary.** The composite $\bigvee_{i\in I} K_i$ is the smallest subfield of $E
$ containing all the $K_i$, realized concretely as the field of fractions (taken
 inside $E$) of the subring generated by $\bigcup_i K_i$.
