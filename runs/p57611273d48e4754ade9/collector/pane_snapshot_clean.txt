## Proof

**Step 1: Identify the distribution of the sum.**

Since $\{X_k\}$ are independent Poisson random variables with $X_k \sim \text{Po
isson}(2^k)$, by the additivity of independent Poisson variables,

$$Y_n := \sum_{k=1}^{n} X_k \sim \text{Poisson}\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n} 2^k\right)
 = \text{Poisson}(2^{n+1}-2).$$

Hence $Z_n = \dfrac{Y_n}{2^n}$ where $Y_n \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda_n)$ with $
\lambda_n = 2^{n+1}-2$.

**Step 2: Compute the mean and variance of $Z_n$.**

$$\mathbb{E}[Z_n] = \frac{\lambda_n}{2^n} = \frac{2^{n+1}-2}{2^n} = 2 - 2^{1-n}
\;\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\; 2.$$

$$\mathrm{Var}(Z_n) = \frac{\lambda_n}{2^{2n}} = \frac{2^{n+1}-2}{4^n} = \frac{2
}{2^n} - \frac{2}{4^n} \;\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\; 0.$$

**Step 3: Show convergence in probability to the constant $2$.**

By Chebyshev's inequality, for every $\varepsilon > 0$,

$$\mathbb{P}\!\left(|Z_n - \mathbb{E}[Z_n]| \ge \varepsilon\right) \le \frac{\ma
thrm{Var}(Z_n)}{\varepsilon^2} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.$$

Since $\mathbb{E}[Z_n] \to 2$, the triangle inequality gives

$$\mathbb{P}\!\left(|Z_n - 2| \ge 2\varepsilon\right) \le \mathbb{P}\!\left(|Z_n
 - \mathbb{E}[Z_n]| \ge \varepsilon\right) + \mathbf{1}\{|\mathbb{E}[Z_n]-2| \ge
 \varepsilon\} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.$$

Thus $Z_n \xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}} 2$.

**Step 4: Conclude convergence in distribution.**

Convergence in probability implies convergence in distribution. The limit is the
 degenerate random variable concentrated at $2$, i.e. the Dirac measure $\delta_
2$.

**Conclusion.** $Z_n$ converges in distribution as $n\to\infty$, and the limitin
g distribution is the point mass at $2$:

$$Z_n \xrightarrow{d} \delta_2, \qquad \text{i.e. } Z_n \xrightarrow{d} 2.$$
