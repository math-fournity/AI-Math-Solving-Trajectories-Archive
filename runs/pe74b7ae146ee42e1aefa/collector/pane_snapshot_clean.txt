**Essential spectrum.** $K$ is compact (rank one), so by Weyl's theorem the esse

$$\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(A) = \sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(M) = [-1,1],$$
the latter being the essential range of the identity function $x \mapsto x$ on $

**No residual spectrum.** Since $A$ is self-adjoint, its residual spectrum is em

**Eigenvalues in $(-1,1)$.** Suppose $Au = \lambda u$ with $\lambda\in(-1,1)$. T

$$(\lambda - x)u(x) = \theta c,\qquad c := \int_{-1}^1 u(t)\,dt.$$
If $c = 0$, then $u = 0$ a.e. (since $\lambda - x \neq 0$ a.e. on $(-1,1)$... ac

$(\lambda-x)u(x) = 0$ a.e. implies $u = 0$ a.e. on $(-1,1)\setminus\{\lambda\}$,

(-1,1)$: $|u|^2 \sim \frac{1}{(x-\lambda)^2}$, so $u\notin L_2$. Contradiction.
Thus **no eigenvalues in $(-1,1)$**.

**Endpoints.** For $\lambda = 1$: $(1-x)u(x) = \theta c$ gives $u(x) = \frac{\th
eta c}{1-x}\notin L_2$ (singularity at $x=1$) unless $c=0$, which forces $u=0$.
Similarly $\lambda = -1$. So **no eigenvalues at $\pm 1$**.

Consequently $[-1,1]$ is entirely **continuous spectrum**.

**Eigenvalues outside $[-1,1]$.** Let $\lambda\notin[-1,1]$ and solve $Au = \lam
bda u$:
$$(\lambda - x)u(x) = \theta c,\qquad c = \int_{-1}^1 u(t)\,dt.$$
If $c = 0$ then $u = 0$. For $c\neq 0$,
$$u(x) = \frac{\theta c}{\lambda - x}\in L_2(-1,1)$$
(since $\lambda$ is at positive distance from $[-1,1]$). Substituting back:
$$c = \int_{-1}^1 \frac{\theta c}{\lambda - x}\,dx = \theta c\int_{-1}^1\frac{dx
}{\lambda - x}.$$
Dividing by $c\neq 0$:
$$1 = \theta\int_{-1}^1\frac{dx}{\lambda - x} = \theta\Bigl[\ln(\lambda+1)-\ln(\
lambda-1)\Bigr] = \theta\ln\frac{\lambda+1}{\lambda-1}.$$
(The logarithm is well-defined and real: for $\lambda > 1$ both arguments are po
sitive; for $\lambda < -1$ both are negative, ratio positive.) Hence
$$\frac{\lambda+1}{\lambda-1} = e^{1/\theta}.$$
Solving:
$$\lambda = \frac{e^{1/\theta}+1}{e^{1/\theta}-1} = \frac{e^{1/(2\theta)}+e^{-1/
(2\theta)}}{e^{1/(2\theta)}-e^{-1/(2\theta)}} = \coth\frac{1}{2\theta}.$$

th t < -1$, so $\lambda < -1$. In both cases $\lambda$ lies strictly outside $[-
1,1]$, confirming a genuine eigenvalue. The eigenspace is one-dimensional (spann
