# The Atiyah–Singer Index Theorem

## Setup

Let $M$ be a compact, oriented, smooth manifold (without boundary; the theorem e
xtends to compact manifolds with boundary under suitable elliptic boundary condi
tions, e.g. APS). Let $E$ and $F$ be smooth complex vector bundles over $M$. An
**elliptic complex** (in the simplest case, an elliptic differential operator) i
s a sequence

$$
0 \longrightarrow \Gamma(E^0) \xrightarrow{D_0} \Gamma(E^1) \xrightarrow{D_1} \G
amma(E^2) \longrightarrow \cdots \longrightarrow \Gamma(E^N) \longrightarrow 0
$$

where $E^0,\dots,E^N$ are vector bundles over $M$, the $D_i$ are differential op
erators with $D_{i+1}\circ D_i = 0$, and the associated symbol complex

$$
0 \longrightarrow E^0_x \xrightarrow{\sigma(D_0)(x,\xi)} E^1_x \xrightarrow{\sig
ma(D_1)(x,\xi)} E^2_x \longrightarrow \cdots \longrightarrow E^N_x \longrightarr
ow 0
$$

is exact for every $x\in M$ and every nonzero cotangent vector $\xi\in T^*_xM\se
tminus\{0\}$. The special case $N=1$ is a single **elliptic operator** $D:\Gamma
(E)\to\Gamma(F)$, whose principal symbol $\sigma(D)(x,\xi):E_x\to F_x$ is a line
ar isomorphism for all $\xi\neq 0$.

## The Analytical Index

For an elliptic complex $(E^\bullet, D_\bullet)$ over a compact manifold, ellipt
ic regularity implies that each cohomology group

$$
H^i(E^\bullet,D_\bullet) = \ker D_i / \operatorname{im} D_{i-1}
$$

is finite-dimensional. The **analytical index** is the Euler characteristic of t
his complex:

$$
\operatorname{ind}_{a}(E^\bullet,D_\bullet) \;=\; \sum_{i=0}^{N}(-1)^i \,\dim H^
i(E^\bullet,D_\bullet).
$$

For a single elliptic operator $D:\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(F)$ this reduces to

$$
\operatorname{ind}_{a}(D) \;=\; \dim\ker D \;-\; \dim\operatorname{coker} D,
$$

where $\ker D$ is the space of smooth solutions of $D\psi=0$ and $\operatorname{
coker} D \cong \ker D^{*}$, with $D^{*}$ the formal adjoint defined using Hermit
ian metrics on $E,F$ and a Riemannian volume form on $M$. Finiteness of $\ker D$
 and $\ker D^{*}$ is guaranteed by ellipticity and compactness.

## The Topological Index

The **topological index** is a purely topological quantity constructed from the
symbol class of the elliptic complex. The principal symbols define a class

$$
\operatorname{ch}(D) \in K^{0}(T^{*}M),
$$

the **Chern character of the symbol**, an element of the compactly supported $K$
-theory of the cotangent bundle $T^{*}M$ (the symbol class is the alternating di
fference $\sum_i (-1)^i [\sigma(D_i)]$ in $K$-theory, well-defined because the s
ymbol complex is exact off the zero section).

Let $\pi:T^{*}M\to M$ be the projection. The topological index is defined via th
e **Thom isomorphism** and **integration over the fibre**. Concretely, embed $M$
 into $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ with normal bundle $N$, so that $T^{*}M\oplus N^{*} \cong
 T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}|_M$. Let $i:T^{*}M\hookrightarrow T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}$ be t
he induced embedding and let $j:T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\to T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}/(T^{*
}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus T^{*}M)$ be the collapse map to the Thom space. The com
position

$$
K^{0}(T^{*}M) \xrightarrow{i_{!}} K^{0}_{c}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}) \xrightarrow{\;
\cong\;} K^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}) \xrightarrow{\;\cong\;} \mathbb{Z}
$$

where the last two maps are the Thom isomorphism for the trivial bundle and the
Bott periodicity identification $K^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})\cong\mathbb{Z}$, giv
es a homomorphism

$$
\operatorname{t-ind}:K^{0}(T^{*}M)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}.
$$

The **topological index** of the elliptic complex is

$$
\operatorname{ind}_{t}(E^\bullet,D_\bullet) \;=\; \operatorname{t-ind}\!\big(\op
eratorname{ch}(D)\big).
$$

Equivalently, using the Chern character and the Todd class, one obtains the **co
homological formula**. Let $\pi:T^{*}M\to M$. Then

$$
\operatorname{ind}_{t}(D) \;=\; \int_{T^{*}M} \operatorname{ch}(\sigma(D))\,\wed
ge\, \pi^{*}\!\operatorname{Td}(T_{\mathbb{C}}M),
$$

where $\operatorname{ch}(\sigma(D))$ is the (compactly supported) Chern characte
r form of the symbol class, $\operatorname{Td}(T_{\mathbb{C}}M)$ is the Todd cla
ss of the complexified tangent bundle of $M$, and the integral is over the cotan
gent bundle (equivalently, push-forward to a point). For a single operator this
becomes, after pushing forward along $\pi$,

$$
\operatorname{ind}_{t}(D) \;=\; \int_{M} \operatorname{ch}\!\big(\ker\sigma(D),\
,\operatorname{im}\sigma(D)\big)\,\wedge\,\operatorname{Td}(T_{\mathbb{C}}M),
$$

i.e. the evaluation of the Chern character of the symbol (pulled back to $M$) ag
ainst the Todd class. The construction is independent of the choice of embedding
 $M\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}$ by the functoriality and naturality of $K$-the
ory.

## The Theorem

> **Atiyah–Singer Index Theorem.** *Let $M$ be a compact smooth manifold and let
 $(E^\bullet,D_\bullet)$ be an elliptic complex over $M$. Then the analytical in
dex and the topological index coincide:*
>
> $$\boxed{\;\operatorname{ind}_{a}(E^\bullet,D_\bullet) \;=\; \operatorname{ind
}_{t}(E^\bullet,D_\bullet)\;}$$
>
> *In particular, for every elliptic operator $D:\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(F)$,*
>
> $$\dim\ker D - \dim\operatorname{coker}D \;=\; \int_{T^{*}M}\operatorname{ch}(
\sigma(D))\wedge\pi^{*}\operatorname{Td}(T_{\mathbb{C}}M).$$

## Remarks on consequences and special cases

- **Gauss–Bonnet.** Taking $D=d+d^{*}:\Omega^{\text{even}}\to\Omega^{\text{odd}}
$ on an oriented even-dimensional Riemannian manifold, the index equals the Eule
r characteristic $\chi(M)$, and the right-hand side becomes $\int_{M}e(TM)$, the
 integral of the Euler class — recovering the Gauss–Bonnet–Chern theorem.

- **Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch.** For the Dolbeault complex $\bar\partial$ on a com
plex manifold $X$, the index equals $\sum_i(-1)^i\dim H^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}(E))$ a
nd the right-hand side becomes $\int_{X}\operatorname{ch}(E)\wedge\operatorname{
Td}(TX)$.

- **Hirzebruch signature theorem.** For the signature operator $d+d^{*}$ restric
ted to self-dual forms, the index is the signature $\sigma(M)$, and the right-ha
nd side is $\int_{M}L(TM)$, the $L$-class of Hirzebruch.

- **Spin and Dirac.** On a spin manifold the Dirac operator has index $\hat{A}(M
)$, giving $\int_{M}\hat{A}(TM)\operatorname{ch}(E)$, the formula central to the
 positivity theorems of Lichnerowicz and Atiyah–Singer.

The theorem thus unifies a large family of classical integrality and characteris
tic-class formulae under the single identity $\operatorname{ind}_{a}=\operatorna
me{ind}_{t}$, identifying an analytic quantity (kernel/cokernel dimensions of an
 elliptic operator) with a purely topological one (a characteristic number built
 from the symbol class).
