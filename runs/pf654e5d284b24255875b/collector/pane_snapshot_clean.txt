## Proof

The arc length of a parametric curve $(x(t), y(t))$ for $a \le t \le b$ is

$$L = \int_a^b \sqrt{(x'(t))^2 + (y'(t))^2}\, dt.$$

**Step 1: Compute the derivatives via the Leibniz integral rule.**

Since $x(t) = \int_t^{\infty} \frac{\cos u}{u}\,du$, differentiating with respec
t to the lower limit $t$ (and noting the upper limit is fixed at $\infty$ where
the integrand is well-behaved):

$$x'(t) = -\frac{\cos t}{t}.$$

Similarly,

$$y'(t) = -\frac{\sin t}{t}.$$

**Step 2: Compute the integrand of the arc length.**

$$\bigl(x'(t)\bigr)^2 + \bigl(y'(t)\bigr)^2 = \frac{\cos^2 t}{t^2} + \frac{\sin^
2 t}{t^2} = \frac{\cos^2 t + \sin^2 t}{t^2} = \frac{1}{t^2}.$$

Therefore,

$$\sqrt{\bigl(x'(t)\bigr)^2 + \bigl(y'(t)\bigr)^2} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{t^2}} = \fra
c{1}{t}, \qquad t \in [1,2].$$

**Step 3: Integrate.**

$$L = \int_1^2 \frac{1}{t}\, dt = \bigl[\ln t\bigr]_1^2 = \ln 2 - \ln 1 = \ln 2.
$$

The length of the plane curve is $\boxed{\ln 2}$.
