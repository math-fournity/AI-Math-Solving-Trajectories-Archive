The statement is **false**. Here is a counterexample.

Take $C$ to be any compact Riemann surface (connected, by convention), and let $
L = L' = \mathcal{O}_C$, the trivial line bundle.

- The global sections of the trivial bundle are the holomorphic functions on $C$
. Since $C$ is compact and connected, every holomorphic function is constant, so
$$h^0(C, \mathcal{O}_C) = 1.$$

- Since $L = L' = \mathcal{O}_C$, we have $L \otimes L' = \mathcal{O}_C \otimes
\mathcal{O}_C \cong \mathcal{O}_C$, hence
$$h^0(C, L \otimes L') = h^0(C, \mathcal{O}_C) = 1.$$

- On the other hand,
$$h^0(C, L) + h^0(C, L') = h^0(C, \mathcal{O}_C) + h^0(C, \mathcal{O}_C) = 1 + 1
 = 2.$$

Therefore
$$h^0(C, L \otimes L') = 1 \neq 2 = h^0(C, L) + h^0(C, L'),$$
contradicting the claimed identity.

**Remark.** The identity $h^0(L \otimes L') = h^0(L) + h^0(L')$ has no general r
eason to hold. The global sections of a tensor product are not determined additi
vely by the global sections of the factors; the dimension $h^0$ depends on the g
eometry of how the bundles interact (via degree, the canonical bundle, and Riema
nn–Roch). What *is* true is the Riemann–Roch theorem, which relates $h^0(L) - h^
1(L)$ to $\deg L + 1 - g$, but this concerns a single bundle and its Serre dual,
 not an additive splitting of $h^0$ over tensor products.
