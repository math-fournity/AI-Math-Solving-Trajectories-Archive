## Setup and Reduction

Let $P = x_1 x_2 \cdots x_n$ and $S = x_1 + \cdots + x_n$, so $PS = 100n$.

Suppose exactly $m$ of the $x_i$ are $\geq 2$; call them $a_1, \ldots, a_m$ (eac
h $\geq 2$), and the remaining $n - m$ values equal $1$. Set $Q = a_1 \cdots a_m
$ and $T = a_1 + \cdots + a_m$. Then:
$$P = Q, \qquad S = (n - m) + T,$$
so the equation becomes $Q(n - m + T) = 100n$. Solving for $n$:
$$n(Q - 100) = Q(m - T) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad n = \frac{Q(T - m)}{100 - Q},$
$
where I set $B := T - m = \sum_{i=1}^m (a_i - 1) \geq m \geq 1$.

**Case $Q = 100$:** forces $m = T$, but $T \geq 2m > m$ — contradiction. Impossi
ble.

**Case $Q > 100$:** then $Q - 100 > 0$ and $m - T < 0$, giving $n < 0$. Impossib
le.

**Case $Q < 100$:** This is the only viable case, with $n = \dfrac{Q \cdot B}{10
0 - Q}$, where $B = \sum(a_i - 1)$ and $Q = \prod a_i < 100$.

## Maximizing $B$ for fixed $Q$

Writing $b_i = a_i - 1 \geq 1$, we have $Q = \prod(b_i + 1)$ and $B = \sum b_i$.
 For a fixed product $Q$, the sum $B$ is maximized by using a **single factor**:
 $b_1 + 1 = Q$, giving $B = Q - 1$.

*Proof:* If $Q = q_1 q_2$ with $q_1, q_2 \geq 2$, splitting gives $B = (q_1 - 1)
 + (q_2 - 1) = q_1 + q_2 - 2$, while the single factor gives $B = q_1 q_2 - 1$.
The inequality $q_1 q_2 - 1 \geq q_1 + q_2 - 2$ is equivalent to $(q_1 - 1)(q_2
- 1) \geq 0$, which holds. By induction, any splitting reduces $B$.

Therefore $B \leq Q - 1$, with equality when $m = 1$ (single non-1 value $a_1 =
Q$).

## Optimizing over $Q$

With $B = Q - 1$, we maximize:
$$f(Q) = \frac{Q(Q - 1)}{100 - Q}, \qquad Q \in \{2, 3, \ldots, 99\}.$$

The derivative's numerator is $-Q^2 + 200Q - 100$, whose roots are $Q = 100 \pm
\sqrt{9900} \approx 0.5$ and $\approx 199.5$. Thus $f'(Q) > 0$ for all $Q \in [2
, 99]$, so $f$ is **strictly increasing** on this range.

The maximum occurs at $Q = 99$:
$$n = f(99) = \frac{99 \cdot 98}{1} = 9702.$$
