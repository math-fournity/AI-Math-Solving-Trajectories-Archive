**Answer: No.**

**Proof.** The hypothesis is that for every bounded Borel function $f:\mathbb{R}
\to\mathbb{R}$,
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}^d} f\circ\phi\,d\mu \;=\; \int_{\mathbb{R}^d} f\circ\psi\,d\m
u.$$
This is precisely the statement that the **pushforward measures** of $\mu$ under
 $\phi$ and under $\psi$ coincide:
$$\phi_{\#}\mu \;=\; \psi_{\#}\mu,$$
since by definition $\int f\,d(\phi_{\#}\mu)=\int f\circ\phi\,d\mu$, and bounded
 Borel functions are measure-determining.

Equality of pushforward measures is a much weaker condition than equality of the
 maps $\mu$-a.e. The pushforward only records the *distribution* of the values o
f $\phi$, discarding *which point* maps to *which value*. We exhibit an explicit
 counterexample.

**Counterexample.** Take $d=1$ and let $\mu=\tfrac12(\delta_{-1}+\delta_{1})$, a
 Borel probability measure on $\mathbb{R}$. Define
$$\phi(x)=x,\qquad \psi(x)=-x.$$
Both are measurable (in fact continuous). For any bounded Borel $f:\mathbb{R}\to
\mathbb{R}$,
$$\int f\circ\phi\,d\mu=\tfrac12\bigl(f(-1)+f(1)\bigr),$$
and
$$\int f\circ\psi\,d\mu=\tfrac12\bigl(f(-(-1))+f(-(1))\bigr)=\tfrac12\bigl(f(1)+
f(-1)\bigr).$$
These are equal for every bounded Borel $f$, so the hypothesis is satisfied.

Yet $\phi\neq\psi$ $\mu$-a.e.: indeed $\phi(1)=1\neq -1=\psi(1)$ and $\phi(-1)=-
1\neq 1=\psi(-1)$, so $\phi$ and $\psi$ differ on $\operatorname{supp}\mu=\{-1,1
\}$, a set of full $\mu$-measure. In particular $\mu(\{x:\phi(x)\neq\psi(x)\})=1
$.

Thus the equality of the integrals for all bounded Borel $f$ does **not** force
$\phi=\psi$ $\mu$-a.e. $\qquad\blacksquare$
