We evaluate
$$I = \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{\sin x}{1+\tfrac{1}{2}\cos x}\,dx$$
by substituting $z = e^{ix}$ (so $x$ plays the role of $\theta$), which maps $[0
,2\pi]$ onto the unit circle $|z|=1$ traversed counterclockwise.

### Setting up the contour integral

With $z = e^{ix}$:
$$dx = \frac{dz}{iz},\qquad \cos x = \frac{z+z^{-1}}{2},\qquad \sin x = \frac{z-
z^{-1}}{2i}.$$

The denominator becomes
$$1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\cos x = 1 + \frac{z+z^{-1}}{4} = \frac{z^2+4z+1}{4z}.$$

Hence
$$I = \oint_{|z|=1} \frac{\dfrac{z-z^{-1}}{2i}}{\dfrac{z^2+4z+1}{4z}}\cdot\frac{
dz}{iz}
= \oint_{|z|=1} \frac{2(1-z^2)}{z(z^2+4z+1)}\,dz.$$

### Locating the poles

The integrand $f(z) = \dfrac{2(1-z^2)}{z(z^2+4z+1)}$ has singularities at:

- $z = 0$ (simple pole),
- roots of $z^2+4z+1=0$: $\;z = -2\pm\sqrt{3}$.

Numerically, $z_1 = -2+\sqrt{3}\approx -0.268$ (inside $|z|=1$) and $z_2 = -2-\s
qrt{3}\approx -3.732$ (outside $|z|=1$).

So the poles enclosed by the contour are $z=0$ and $z_1 = -2+\sqrt{3}$.

### Computing the residues

**Residue at $z=0$:**
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z) = \lim_{z\to 0} z\cdot\frac{2(1-z^2)}{z(z^2+4z+1
)} = \frac{2\cdot 1}{1} = 2.$$

**Residue at $z_1 = -2+\sqrt{3}$:** With $z_2 = -2-\sqrt{3}$, factor $z^2+4z+1 =
 (z-z_1)(z-z_2)$, so
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_1} f(z) = \frac{2(1-z_1^2)}{z_1(z_1-z_2)}.$$

Compute each piece:
- $z_1^2 = (-2+\sqrt{3})^2 = 7-4\sqrt{3}$, so $1-z_1^2 = -6+4\sqrt{3}$.
- $z_1 - z_2 = 2\sqrt{3}$.
- $z_1(z_1-z_2) = (-2+\sqrt{3})\cdot 2\sqrt{3} = 6-4\sqrt{3}$.

Therefore
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_1} f(z) = \frac{2(-6+4\sqrt{3})}{6-4\sqrt{3}} = \frac{
-2(6-4\sqrt{3})}{6-4\sqrt{3}} = -2.$$

### Applying the residue theorem

$$I = 2\pi i\Big(\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f + \operatorname{Res}_{z=z_1} f\Big)
= 2\pi i\,(2 + (-2)) = 2\pi i \cdot 0 = 0.$$

### Verification (sanity check)

The antiderivative in $x$ is $-2\ln\!\big(1+\tfrac{1}{2}\cos x\big)$, since
$$\frac{d}{dx}\!\left[-2\ln\!\big(1+\tfrac{1}{2}\cos x\big)\right] = -2\cdot\fra
c{-\tfrac{1}{2}\sin x}{1+\tfrac{1}{2}\cos x} = \frac{\sin x}{1+\tfrac{1}{2}\cos
x}.$$
Evaluating at $0$ and $2\pi$ gives $-2\ln\tfrac{3}{2} - (-2\ln\tfrac{3}{2}) = 0$
, confirming the result.

---

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{\sin x}{1+\tfrac{1}{2}\cos x}\,dx = 0}$
$
