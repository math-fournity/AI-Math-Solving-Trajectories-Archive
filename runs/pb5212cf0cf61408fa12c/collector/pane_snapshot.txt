# Solution

**Claim:** The largest value of $k$ is $\displaystyle\binom{n}{2}-1 = \frac{(n-2
)(n+1)}{2}$.

## Part 1: Peter can always win when $k \le \binom{n}{2}-1$.

Any graph $G$ on $n$ vertices with at most $\binom{n}{2}-1$ edges is **not** the
 complete graph $K_n$, so there exist two non-adjacent vertices. Peter labels th
ese as $A$ and $B$, and places the counter at $A$.

**Peter's strategy.** After each of Baron's moves, suppose the counter rests at
vertex $w$. Peter removes the edge $wB$ if it still exists; if $wB$ does not exi
st, Peter removes any remaining edge incident to $B$ (or any edge if $B$ is alre
ady isolated).

**Proof that Baron never reaches $B$ (by induction on turns).**

*Base case.* At the start, the counter is at $A$ and $AB$ is **not** an edge (by
 Peter's choice). Hence Baron cannot move to $B$ on his first turn.

*Inductive step.* Suppose at the beginning of Baron's turn the counter is at $w$
 and the edge $wB$ does **not** exist in the current graph. Baron moves the coun
ter to some neighbor $w'$ of $w$. Now it is Peter's turn: Peter removes $w'B$ if
 it exists (otherwise some other edge incident to $B$). After Peter's removal, t
he edge $w'B$ does not exist. Thus at the start of Baron's next turn, the counte
r is at $w'$ and $w'B$ does not exist.

By induction, at the start of **every** one of Baron's turns, the edge from the
counter's current position to $B$ is absent. Since moving to $B$ requires traver
sing an edge into $B$, Baron can **never** reach $B$. The graph is finite and ed
ges are only removed, so the game terminates (Baron eventually cannot move or $B
$ becomes isolated). Peter wins. $\checkmark$

## Part 2: Peter cannot always win when $k = \binom{n}{2}$.

When $k = \binom{n}{2}$, the graph is $K_n$. Every pair of vertices is adjacent.
 Whatever $A, B$ Peter chooses, the edge $AB$ exists. Baron moves the counter fr
om $A$ directly to $B$ on his **first** move and wins immediately — Peter has no
 opportunity to remove any edge beforehand.

## Conclusion.

For every $k \le \binom{n}{2}-1$, every graph with $n$ vertices and $k$ edges is
 non-complete, so Peter can find a non-adjacent pair $\{A,B\}$ and execute the s
trategy above to win. For $k = \binom{n}{2}$, the unique graph $K_n$ allows Baro
n to win on his first move regardless of Peter's labeling.

$$\boxed{k = \binom{n}{2} - 1 = \frac{(n-2)(n+1)}{2}}$$
