Let $T = \mathbb{S}^1 \times \mathbb{S}^1$ be the torus, and let $X = T \cup_f D
^2$ be the space obtained by attaching a 2-cell via $f(\zeta) = (\zeta^2, \zeta^
3)$.

**Step 1: Fundamental group of the torus.**

The torus has a standard CW-structure with one 0-cell, two 1-cells (giving gener
ators $a$ and $b$ for the two circle factors), and one 2-cell attached via the c
ommutator $[a,b] = aba^{-1}b^{-1}$. Hence

$$\pi_1(T) = \langle a, b \mid [a,b] \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z},
$$

where $a$ corresponds to the generator of the first $\mathbb{S}^1$ factor and $b
$ to the generator of the second.

**Step 2: Effect of attaching a 2-cell.**

By the standard theorem on attaching cells, attaching a 2-cell via a map $f : \m
athbb{S}^1 \to T$ kills the homotopy class $[f] \in \pi_1(T)$. Precisely, if $f$
 represents the element $w \in \pi_1(T)$, then

$$\pi_1(X) = \pi_1(T) / \langle\!\langle w \rangle\!\rangle.$$

**Step 3: Identify the attaching word.**

The map $f(\zeta) = (\zeta^2, \zeta^3)$ is a loop in $T$. Projecting to the firs
t factor gives $\zeta \mapsto \zeta^2$, which winds twice around the first $\mat
hbb{S}^1$ and thus represents $a^2 \in \pi_1(T)$. Projecting to the second facto
r gives $\zeta \mapsto \zeta^3$, which winds three times around the second $\mat
hbb{S}^1$ and thus represents $b^3$. Since $\pi_1(T)$ is abelian, the combined l
oop represents

$$[f] = a^2 b^3 \in \pi_1(T) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2.$$

**Step 4: Compute the quotient.**

Therefore

$$\pi_1(X) = \langle a, b \mid [a,b],\; a^2 b^3 \rangle = \langle a, b \mid ab =
 ba,\; a^2 b^3 = 1 \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}^2 / \langle (2,3) \rangle.$$

This is the quotient of $\mathbb{Z}^2$ by the subgroup generated by the single v
ector $(2,3)$.

**Step 5: Simplify using a basis change.**

The vector $(2,3)$ is **primitive** in $\mathbb{Z}^2$, i.e., $\gcd(2,3) = 1$. A
primitive vector in $\mathbb{Z}^2$ can be extended to a basis. Concretely, we se
ek $(p,q)$ with

$$\det\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ p & q \end{pmatrix} = 2q - 3p = \pm 1.$$

Taking $(p,q) = (1,2)$ gives $2 \cdot 2 - 3 \cdot 1 = 1$. Thus $\{(2,3),\, (1,2)
\}$ is a basis of $\mathbb{Z}^2$ (the change-of-basis matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 2
& 3 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ has determinant $1$, hence is in $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mat
hbb{Z})$).

In this new basis, the subgroup $\langle(2,3)\rangle$ becomes $\langle(1,0)\rang
le$, so

$$\mathbb{Z}^2 / \langle (2,3) \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}^2 / \langle (1,0) \rangl
e \cong \mathbb{Z}.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\pi_1(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}.}$$

Geometrically: the relation $a^2 b^3 = 1$ together with commutativity leaves a s
ingle free generator (for instance $b$, with $a = b^{-3/2}$ interpreted in the a
belian quotient — equivalently, the new basis element $(1,2)$ survives as the fr
ee generator).
