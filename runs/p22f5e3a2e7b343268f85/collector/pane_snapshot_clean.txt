## Proof that the unit circle $C$ is Jordan-negligible

**Definition.** A bounded set $S\subset\mathbb{R}^2$ is *Jordan-negligible* (has
 Jordan content zero) if for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exist finitely many rec
tangles $R_1,\dots,R_n$ whose union covers $S$ and whose total area satisfies $\
sum_{i=1}^n |R_i|<\varepsilon$.

**Parametrization.** The unit circle is
$$C=\{(\cos t,\sin t):t\in[0,2\pi]\},$$
parametrized by $\gamma(t)=(\cos t,\sin t)$. The coordinate functions are Lipsch
itz with constant $1$:
$$|\cos t-\cos s|\le |t-s|,\qquad |\sin t-\sin s|\le |t-s|,$$
since $|\gamma'(t)|=|(-\sin t,\cos t)|=1$.

**Construction of the cover.** Fix $\varepsilon>0$. Partition $[0,2\pi]$ into $n
$ equal subintervals $I_k=[t_{k-1},t_k]$ of length $\Delta t=2\pi/n$, where $t_k
=2\pi k/n$. Let $A_k=\gamma(I_k)$ be the corresponding arc segment.

For any two points $t,s\in I_k$, the Lipschitz bounds give
$$|\cos t-\cos s|\le \Delta t=\frac{2\pi}{n},\qquad |\sin t-\sin s|\le \Delta t=
\frac{2\pi}{n}.$$
Hence the entire segment $A_k$ lies inside the closed square
$$R_k=\left[\min_{t\in I_k}\cos t,\ \max_{t\in I_k}\cos t\right]\times\left[\min
_{t\in I_k}\sin t,\ \max_{t\in I_k}\sin t\right],$$
and each side of $R_k$ has length at most $2\pi/n$. Therefore
$$|R_k|\le \left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)^2.$$

**Total area estimate.** The $n$ rectangles $R_1,\dots,R_n$ cover $C$, and
$$\sum_{k=1}^{n}|R_k|\le n\cdot\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)^2=\frac{4\pi^2}{n}.$$

**Conclusion.** Choose $n>4\pi^2/\varepsilon$ (e.g. $n=\lceil 4\pi^2/\varepsilon
\rceil+1$). Then
$$\sum_{k=1}^{n}|R_k|<\varepsilon.$$
Since $\varepsilon>0$ was arbitrary, $C$ is Jordan-negligible. $\blacksquare$
