### Step 1: Smoothness of the surface

The surface $S$ is the level set $F(x,y,z) = x^4 + y^4 + z^4 - 1 = 0$. Its gradi
ent is
$$\nabla F = (4x^3,\, 4y^3,\, 4z^3),$$
which vanishes only at the origin. Since $0^4+0^4+0^4 = 0 \neq 1$, the origin is
 not on $S$. Hence $\nabla F \neq 0$ everywhere on $S$, so $S$ is a smooth (clas
s $C^\infty$) closed surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$.

### Step 2: Topology of $S$

The function $g(x,y,z) = x^4 + y^4 + z^4$ is strictly convex (its Hessian is $\o
peratorname{diag}(12x^2, 12y^2, 12z^2) \succeq 0$, and $g$ is strictly convex si
nce $t \mapsto t^4$ is strictly convex). Therefore the sublevel set
$$\Omega = \{(x,y,z) : x^4 + y^4 + z^4 \le 1\}$$
is a compact, strictly convex body in $\mathbb{R}^3$, and $S = \partial\Omega$ i
s its boundary.

The boundary of any compact convex body in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is homeomorphic to the
 2-sphere $S^2$. (Radial projection from any interior point gives a homeomorphis
m to the unit sphere.) Hence
$$S \cong S^2, \qquad \chi(S) = \chi(S^2) = 2.$$

### Step 3: Apply the Gauss–Bonnet theorem

By the global Gauss–Bonnet theorem for a closed, orientable, smooth surface $S$
in $\mathbb{R}^3$:
$$\int_S K\, dA = 2\pi\,\chi(S),$$
where $K$ is the Gaussian curvature and $dA$ is the area element. (There is no b
oundary term since $S$ is closed.)

Substituting $\chi(S) = 2$:
$$\int_S K\, dA = 2\pi \cdot 2 = 4\pi.$$

### Result

$$\boxed{\int_S K\, dA = 4\pi}$$
