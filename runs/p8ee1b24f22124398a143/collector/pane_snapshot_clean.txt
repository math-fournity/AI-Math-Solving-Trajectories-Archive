## Proof

**Yes**, it is possible. We give an explicit example: a smooth cubic curve in $\
mathbb{CP}^2$.

### Setup and Key Identity

Let $h \in \mathbb{C}[X_0, X_1, X_2]$ be a homogeneous polynomial of degree $d =
 3$ defining a **smooth** cubic curve $Z(h) \subset \mathbb{CP}^2$. Such polynom
ials exist (e.g., the Fermat cubic $X_0^3 + X_1^3 + X_2^3 = 0$, which is smooth
since its partial derivatives vanish simultaneously only at the origin, which is
 not a point of $\mathbb{CP}^2$).

**Irreducibility:** A smooth hypersurface in $\mathbb{CP}^n$ with $n \geq 2$ is
automatically irreducible. Indeed, if $h = f \cdot g$ with $\deg f, \deg g \geq
1$, then $Z(f) \cap Z(g) \neq \emptyset$ in $\mathbb{CP}^n$ for $n \geq 2$ (by p
rojective dimension theory: $\dim Z(f) + \dim Z(g) \geq (n-1)+(n-1) \geq n$). An
y point $p \in Z(f) \cap Z(g)$ lies on $Z(h)$ and satisfies $\nabla h(p) = f(p)\
nabla g(p) + g(p)\nabla f(p) = 0$, contradicting smoothness. Hence $Z(h)$ is irr
educible, and $\deg h = 3 > 2$.

### Euler Characteristic of $Z(h)$

A smooth plane curve of degree $d$ in $\mathbb{CP}^2$ has genus $g = \frac{(d-1)
(d-2)}{2}$. For $d = 3$:

$$g = \frac{(3-1)(3-2)}{2} = 1.$$

So $Z(h)$ is an elliptic curve (a torus topologically), with Euler characteristi
c:

$$\chi(Z(h)) = 2 - 2g = 2 - 2 = 0.$$

### Euler Characteristic of the Complement

The Euler characteristic satisfies **additivity** for complex algebraic varietie
s: if $X$ is a variety and $Y \subset X$ is a closed subvariety, then

$$\chi_c(X) = \chi_c(Y) + \chi_c(X \setminus Y),$$

where $\chi_c$ denotes Euler characteristic with compact support. Since $\mathbb
{CP}^2$ and $Z(h)$ are compact, $\chi_c = \chi$ (ordinary Euler characteristic)
for them. The complement $U = \mathbb{CP}^2 \setminus Z(h)$ is a smooth manifold
 of real dimension $4 = 2 \cdot 2$. By Poincaré duality for non-compact orientab
le manifolds of real dimension $r$:

$$\chi_c(U) = (-1)^r \, \chi(U).$$

Since $r = 4$ is even, $\chi_c(U) = \chi(U)$. Therefore:

$$\chi(U) = \chi_c(U) = \chi_c(\mathbb{CP}^2) - \chi_c(Z(h)) = \chi(\mathbb{CP}^
2) - \chi(Z(h)).$$

### Computation

We have $\chi(\mathbb{CP}^2) = 3$ (Betti numbers $1, 0, 1, 0, 1$) and $\chi(Z(h)
) = 0$. Thus:

$$\chi(\mathbb{CP}^2 \setminus Z(h)) = 3 - 0 = 3 \neq 0.$$

### Conclusion

The smooth Fermat cubic $h = X_0^3 + X_1^3 + X_2^3 \in \mathbb{C}[X_0, X_1, X_2]
$ is irreducible, has degree $3 > 2$, and satisfies

$$\chi(\mathbb{CP}^2 \setminus Z(h)) = 3 \neq 0.$$

Therefore, **yes**, such an irreducible hypersurface exists.
