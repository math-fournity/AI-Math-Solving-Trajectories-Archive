## Solution

We evaluate
$$L = \lim_{n\to\infty}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{p_k-1}{p_k} = \lim_{n\to\infty}\prod
_{k=1}^{n}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_k}\right).$$

**Claim.** $L = 0$.

**Proof.** We use Mertens' third theorem, a classical result in analytic number
theory:

$$\prod_{p \le x}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right) \sim \frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\ln x} \qqua
d (x\to\infty),$$

where $\gamma$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant and the product runs over all pr
imes $p\le x$.

Setting $x = p_n$ (the $n$-th prime), the product $\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-1/p_k)$ is
exactly $\prod_{p\le p_n}(1-1/p)$. Hence

$$\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_k}\right) \sim \frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\ln p_n}.$
$

By the prime number theorem, $p_n \sim n\ln n$, so $\ln p_n \to \infty$ as $n\to
\infty$. Since $e^{-\gamma}$ is a positive constant,

$$\frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\ln p_n} \to 0 \qquad (n\to\infty).$$

Therefore

$$L = \lim_{n\to\infty}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_k}\right) = \lim_{n\to\
infty}\frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\ln p_n} = 0.$$

**Remark (self-contained justification via the harmonic series).** For completen
ess, we sketch why the product must vanish without appealing to the full strengt
h of Mertens' theorem. Taking logarithms,

$$\ln\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_k}\right) = \sum_{k=1}^{n}\ln\left(1-\fra
c{1}{p_k}\right).$$

Using $\ln(1-t) \le -t$ for $0<t<1$,

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n}\ln\left(1-\frac{1}{p_k}\right) \le -\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_k}
.$$

A classical consequence of Euler's proof that $\sum_p 1/p$ diverges (which follo
ws from the divergence of $\sum_{m}1/m$ and the Euler product $\sum 1/m = \prod_
p(1-1/p)^{-1}$) gives $\sum_{k=1}^{n}1/p_k \to \infty$. Hence the logarithm of t
he product tends to $-\infty$, so the product itself tends to $0$.

Thus in either approach,

$$\boxed{L = 0.}$$
