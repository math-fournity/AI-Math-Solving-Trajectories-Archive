## Proof

**Step 1: Evaluate the sum $K$.**

Let $a_n = 3^n - 2^n$. The general term is $\dfrac{6^n}{a_n\, a_{n+1}}$. We clai
m the telescoping identity

$$\frac{6^n}{a_n\,a_{n+1}} = \frac{2^n}{a_n} - \frac{2^{n+1}}{a_{n+1}}.$$

Indeed, the right-hand side equals

$$\frac{2^n a_{n+1} - 2^{n+1} a_n}{a_n a_{n+1}} = \frac{2^n(3^{n+1}-2^{n+1}) - 2
^{n+1}(3^n-2^n)}{a_n a_{n+1}}.$$

Expanding the numerator:

$$2^n\cdot 3^{n+1} - 2^{2n+1} - 2^{n+1}\cdot 3^n + 2^{2n+1} = 2^n\cdot 3^{n+1} -
 2^{n+1}\cdot 3^n = 2^n\cdot 3^n(3-2) = 6^n.$$

So the identity holds. Summing from $n=1$ to $N$ telescopes:

$$\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{6^n}{a_n a_{n+1}} = \frac{2^1}{a_1} - \frac{2^{N+1}}{a_{N+
1}} = \frac{2}{1} - \frac{2^{N+1}}{3^{N+1}-2^{N+1}}.$$

As $N\to\infty$,

$$\frac{2^{N+1}}{3^{N+1}-2^{N+1}} = \frac{1}{(3/2)^{N+1}-1}\to 0.$$

Therefore

$$K = 2.$$

**Step 2: Compute the last digit of $(K+6)^{(K+6)!}$.**

With $K=2$, we have $K+6 = 8$ and $(K+6)! = 8! = 40320$.

We need the last digit of $8^{40320}$, i.e., $8^{40320}\bmod 10$.

The powers of $8$ modulo $10$ cycle with period $4$:
$$8^1\equiv 8,\quad 8^2\equiv 4,\quad 8^3\equiv 2,\quad 8^4\equiv 6 \pmod{10},$$
and $8^{k+4}\equiv 8^k\pmod{10}$.

Since $40320 = 4\times 10080$, we have $40320\equiv 0\pmod 4$, so

$$8^{40320}\equiv 8^4\equiv 6\pmod{10}.$$

**The last digit is $\boxed{6}$.**
