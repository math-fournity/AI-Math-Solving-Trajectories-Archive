## Answer

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a Lie algebra (the Lie algebra of the Lie group in questio
n), with basis $\{e_\alpha\}$. The Killing form is the symmetric bilinear form

$$\kappa_{\alpha\beta} = \kappa(e_\alpha, e_\beta) = \operatorname{tr}(\operator
name{ad}e_\alpha \circ \operatorname{ad}e_\beta),$$

i.e. the trace of the product of the adjoint (adjoint representation) endomorphi
sms.

### Condition for non-singularity

The matrix $\kappa_{\alpha\beta}$ is **non-singular** precisely when

$$\det(\kappa_{\alpha\beta}) \neq 0,$$

equivalently, when the Killing form $\kappa$ is a **non-degenerate** bilinear fo
rm on $\mathfrak{g}$. Concretely, non-degeneracy means:

$$\forall\, X \in \mathfrak{g},\quad \bigl(\kappa(X,Y)=0\ \ \forall\, Y\in\mathf
rak{g}\bigr) \implies X = 0.$$

In matrix terms this is exactly $\det(\kappa_{\alpha\beta})\neq 0$, since a dege
nerate form has a nontrivial radical $\operatorname{rad}\kappa = \{X : \kappa(X,
\cdot)\equiv 0\}$, which corresponds to $\ker(\kappa_{\alpha\beta})\neq\{0\}$, i
.e. $\det(\kappa_{\alpha\beta})=0$.

### Why this matters for semi-simplicity

The decisive fact is **Cartan's criterion for semi-simplicity**:

> A Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ over a field of characteristic $0$ is **semi-simp
le** (has no non-zero solvable ideals) **if and only if its Killing form is non-
degenerate**, i.e. $\det(\kappa_{\alpha\beta})\neq 0$.

**Sketch of the argument.**

1. *Non-degenerate $\Rightarrow$ semi-simple.* Let $\mathfrak{r}=\operatorname{r
ad}\kappa$ be the radical of the Killing form. One shows $\mathfrak{r}$ is a sol
vable ideal of $\mathfrak{g}$ (this uses Cartan's solvability criterion: $\kappa
(X,Y)=0$ for all $X,Y$ in an ideal forces that ideal to be solvable). If $\kappa
$ is non-degenerate then $\mathfrak{r}=0$, so the only solvable ideal is $\{0\}$
; hence $\mathfrak{g}$ is semi-simple.

2. *Semi-simple $\Rightarrow$ non-degenerate.* If $\mathfrak{g}$ is semi-simple,
 its only solvable ideal is $\{0\}$. Since $\operatorname{rad}\kappa$ is always
a solvable ideal, it must be $\{0\}$, so $\kappa$ is non-degenerate.

### Importance

The condition $\det(\kappa_{\alpha\beta})\neq 0$ is therefore a single, computab
le algebraic test that completely characterizes semi-simplicity:

- It lets one decide semi-simplicity of a Lie group/Lie algebra by computing one
 determinant, without enumerating all ideals.
- Non-degeneracy of $\kappa$ is the foundation of the structure theory of semi-s
imple Lie algebras: it guarantees the existence of a Cartan subalgebra, the root
-space decomposition, and the non-degenerate pairing between root spaces $\mathf
rak{g}_\alpha$ and $\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}$.
- It underlies the classification of compact/semi-simple Lie groups and the corr
espondence between real forms and signatures of $\kappa$.

In short: **$\kappa_{\alpha\beta}$ is non-singular iff $\det(\kappa_{\alpha\beta
})\neq 0$ (the Killing form is non-degenerate), and by Cartan's criterion this i
s equivalent to the Lie algebra — and hence the corresponding connected Lie grou
p — being semi-simple.**
