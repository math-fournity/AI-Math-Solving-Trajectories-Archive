## Proof

We want to express the Fresnel integrals

$$C(x) = \int_0^x \cos\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}t^2\right)dt, \qquad S(x) = \int_0^x
 \sin\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}t^2\right)dt$$

in terms of the error function $\operatorname{erf}(z)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_0
^z e^{-u^2}\,du$, and find the constant $A$ with

$$C(x)+iS(x)=A\,\operatorname{erf}\!\left[\tfrac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}(1-i)x\right].$$

**Step 1. Combine the two integrals.**

Using Euler's formula,

$$C(x)+iS(x)=\int_0^x\!\left[\cos\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}t^2\right)+i\sin\!\left(\
tfrac{\pi}{2}t^2\right)\right]dt=\int_0^x e^{\,i\pi t^2/2}\,dt.$$

**Step 2. Choose a substitution that turns the exponent into $-u^2$.**

We need $-u^2 = i\pi t^2/2$, i.e. $u^2 = -i\,\pi t^2/2$. Set $u=\alpha t$ with

$$\alpha^2 = -\,\frac{i\pi}{2}.$$

Since $-i = e^{-i\pi/2}$, its square root is $\sqrt{-i}=e^{-i\pi/4}=\frac{1-i}{\
sqrt 2}$. Hence

$$\alpha = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\;\sqrt{-i}=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\cdot\frac{1-i}{\
sqrt 2}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}(1-i).$$

With $u=\alpha t$ (so $dt=du/\alpha$), as $t:0\to x$ we have $u:0\to \alpha x=\f
rac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}(1-i)x$, and

$$\int_0^x e^{\,i\pi t^2/2}\,dt=\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_0^{\alpha x} e^{-u^2}\,du=\
frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\operatorname{erf}(\alpha x).$$

**Step 3. Simplify the prefactor.**

$$\frac{1}{\alpha}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}(1-i)}=\frac{2(1+i)}{\sqrt{\pi}(1-i)(1+i)}
=\frac{2(1+i)}{2\sqrt{\pi}}=\frac{1+i}{\sqrt{\pi}}.$$

Therefore

$$\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{\alpha}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\cdot\frac{1+i}{
\sqrt{\pi}}=\frac{1+i}{2}.$$

**Step 4. Result.**

$$C(x)+iS(x)=\frac{1+i}{2}\,\operatorname{erf}\!\left[\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}(1-i)x
\right].$$

**Consistency check.** As $x\to\infty$, the argument $\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}(1-i)x
$ tends to infinity along the ray $\arg u=-\pi/4$, where $\operatorname{erf}\to
1$. Thus $C(\infty)+iS(\infty)=\frac{1+i}{2}$, which is the classical Fresnel li
mit — confirming the constant.

$$\boxed{\,A=\dfrac{1+i}{2}\,}$$
