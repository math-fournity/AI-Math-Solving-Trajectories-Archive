### Normal subgroups of $S_7$

Since $n = 7 \geq 5$, the only normal subgroups of $S_7$ are $\{e\}$, $A_7$, and
 $S_7$. Any homomorphism $\phi: S_7 \to A_8$ has kernel equal to one of these.

### Case 1: $\ker\phi = S_7$ (trivial homomorphism)

This gives exactly **1** homomorphism.

### Case 2: $\ker\phi = A_7$

 determined by the image of the nontrivial coset (any odd permutation), which mu
st be an element of order 2 in $A_8$.

Each gives a valid homomorphism (well-defined since $g^2 = e$ for the image of t

### Case 3: $\ker\phi = \{e\}$ (injective homomorphism)

This requires $S_7$ to embed as a subgroup of $A_8$ of order $5040$, i.e., index
 $[A_8 : S_7] = 20160/5040 = 4$.

**$A_8$ has no subgroup of index 4.** A subgroup of index 4 would yield a homomo
rphism $A_8 \to S_4$. Since $A_8$ is simple (as $n=8\geq 5$), the kernel is eith
er $\{e\}$ or $A_8$. If $\{e\}$, then $A_8$ embeds into $S_4$, but $|A_8|=20160
> |S_4|=24$—impossible. If $A_8$, the action is trivial (index 1). So no index-4
