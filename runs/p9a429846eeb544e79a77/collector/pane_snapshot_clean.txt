# Lagrange-Charpit Solution of $u_x^2 + u_y^2 + u_z^2 = c^2$

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains only the PDE and the instr
uction to obtain the Lagrange-Charpit conditions and complete solution. No answe
r, solution sketch, or formalization notes are present. Proceeding.

---

## 1. Setup

Let $p = \partial u/\partial x,\; q = \partial u/\partial y,\; r = \partial u/\p
artial z$. The PDE is

$$F(x,y,z,u,p,q,r) \;=\; p^{2}+q^{2}+r^{2}-c^{2} \;=\; 0.$$

---

## 2. Lagrange-Charpit (Charpit) auxiliary equations

For a first-order PDE $F(x,y,z,u,p,q,r)=0$ in three independent variables, the g
eneralized Charpit system is

$$\frac{dx}{F_{p}}=\frac{dy}{F_{q}}=\frac{dz}{F_{r}}=\frac{du}{pF_{p}+qF_{q}+rF_
{r}}=\frac{dp}{-F_{x}-pF_{u}}=\frac{dq}{-F_{y}-qF_{u}}=\frac{dr}{-F_{z}-rF_{u}}.
$$

Computing the partials of $F=p^{2}+q^{2}+r^{2}-c^{2}$:

$$F_{p}=2p,\quad F_{q}=2q,\quad F_{r}=2r,\quad F_{x}=F_{y}=F_{z}=0,\quad F_{u}=0
.$$

Substituting, the **Lagrange-Charpit conditions** are

$$\boxed{\;\frac{dx}{2p}=\frac{dy}{2q}=\frac{dz}{2r}=\frac{du}{2(p^{2}+q^{2}+r^{
2})}=\frac{dp}{0}=\frac{dq}{0}=\frac{dr}{0}\;}$$

(using $p^{2}+q^{2}+r^{2}=c^{2}$ in the $du$-term, so $du/[2c^{2}]$).

---

## 3. Integration of the Charpit system

**From $dp/0=dq/0=dr/0$:** $p,\,q,\,r$ are constant along each characteristic. S
et

$$p=a,\qquad q=b,\qquad r=d,\qquad a^{2}+b^{2}+d^{2}=c^{2},$$

where $a,b,d$ are constants subject to the single constraint above.

**From $dx/(2a)=dy/(2b)=dz/(2d)=du/(2c^{2})$:** introducing a parameter $s$,

$$x=2as+x_{0},\quad y=2bs+y_{0},\quad z=2ds+z_{0},\quad u=2c^{2}s+u_{0}.$$

Eliminating $s$ gives the characteristic strips as straight lines in $(x,y,z)$-s
pace, and along them $u$ varies linearly. Equivalently, since $p,q,r$ are consta
nt, $u$ is affine:

$$u = ax+by+dz+e,$$

with $e$ an integration constant. Direct substitution verifies:

$$u_{x}=a,\;u_{y}=b,\;u_{z}=d\;\Longrightarrow\;a^{2}+b^{2}+d^{2}=c^{2}.\;\check
mark$$

---

## 4. Complete integral

The constraint $a^{2}+b^{2}+d^{2}=c^{2}$ leaves two of $(a,b,d)$ free; together
with $e$ this gives **three arbitrary constants**, exactly the number required f
or a complete integral of a first-order PDE in three independent variables. Solv
ing for $d$,

$$\boxed{\;u \;=\; a\,x+b\,y\;\pm\;\sqrt{c^{2}-a^{2}-b^{2}}\;\,z \;+\; e\;}$$

with $a,\,b,\,e$ arbitrary constants (and $a^{2}+b^{2}\le c^{2}$). This is the *
*complete integral**.

---

## 5. General integral (singular/general solution)

The **general integral** is obtained by the envelope (Cauchy) method: set $e=\ph
i(a,b)$ with $\phi$ an arbitrary function, and eliminate $a,b$ between

$$u=ax+by+\sqrt{c^{2}-a^{2}-b^{2}}\;z+\phi(a,b)$$

and the two envelope conditions

$$x-\frac{a}{\sqrt{c^{2}-a^{2}-b^{2}}}\,z+\phi_{a}(a,b)=0,\qquad y-\frac{b}{\sqr
t{c^{2}-a^{2}-b^{2}}}\,z+\phi_{b}(a,b)=0.$$

The resulting two-parameter family of characteristic surfaces, depending on one
arbitrary function $\phi$, constitutes the **general solution**.

---

## 6. Verification

For the complete integral $u=ax+by+dz+e$ with $a^{2}+b^{2}+d^{2}=c^{2}$:

$$u_{x}^{2}+u_{y}^{2}+u_{z}^{2}=a^{2}+b^{2}+d^{2}=c^{2}.\;\checkmark$$

The Charpit conditions are satisfied since $p,q,r$ are constant (so $dp=dq=dr=0$
) and $F_{x}=F_{y}=F_{z}=F_{u}=0$. $\;\blacksquare$
