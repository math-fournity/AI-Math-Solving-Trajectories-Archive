## Proof

**Claim:** The entire functions $f$ satisfying $\lim_{z\to\infty}\left|\frac{f(z
)}{z}\right|=0$ are exactly the constant functions.

**Step 1: $f$ is a polynomial of degree at most 1.**

Since $\left|\frac{f(z)}{z}\right|\to 0$ as $z\to\infty$, there exists $R>0$ suc
h that for all $|z|>R$,
$$|f(z)|<|z|.$$
On the compact disk $|z|\le R$, the continuous function $|f(z)|$ is bounded by s
ome $M$. Hence for all $z\in\mathbb{C}$,
$$|f(z)|\le \max(M,1)\cdot(1+|z|).$$

Write $f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_n z^n$. By the Cauchy estimate on the circle $|
z|=r$:
$$|a_n|\le \frac{\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|}{r^n}\le \frac{\max(M,1)\cdot(1+r)}{r^n}.$$
For $n\ge 2$, the right-hand side tends to $0$ as $r\to\infty$, so $a_n=0$ for a
ll $n\ge 2$. Thus
$$f(z)=a_0+a_1 z.$$

**Step 2: $a_1=0$.**

Now
$$\left|\frac{f(z)}{z}\right|=\left|\frac{a_0}{z}+a_1\right|\xrightarrow{z\to\in
fty}|a_1|.$$
Since the limit must be $0$, we obtain $a_1=0$.

If $f(z)=a_0$ is constant, then $\left|\frac{f(z)}{z}\right|=\frac{|a_0|}{|z|}\t

**Conclusion:** The entire functions satisfying the given condition are precisel
