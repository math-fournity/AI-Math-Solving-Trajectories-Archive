## Solution

umber of segments $PQ$ (endpoints in $S$, both $\neq X$) whose bisector $m(PQ)$
passes through $X$.

**Upper bound: $|S|\le 5$.** Condition 2 says no point of $S$ lies on three bise
ctors, so $b(X)\le 2$ for every $X\in S$. Condition 1 says every segment $PQ$ ha
s at least one witness $X\in S\setminus\{P,Q\}$ lying on $m(PQ)$, so the total n
umber of (segment, witness) incidences satisfies
$$\sum_{X\in S} b(X) \;\ge\; \binom{|S|}{2}.$$
Combining with $b(X)\le 2$:
$$\binom{n}{2} \;\le\; 2n \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{n(n-1)}{2}\le 2n \;\Longrigh
tarrow\; n-1\le 4 \;\Longrightarrow\; n\le 5.$$

**Excluding $n=2$.** If $S=\{P,Q\}$, then $m(PQ)$ contains neither $P$ nor $Q$ (
since $|PP|=0\neq|PQ|$), so $m(PQ)\cap S=\emptyset$, violating condition 1.

**Excluding $n=4$.** Fix $X\in S$ and consider the other three points $A,B,C$. T
he value $b(X)$ equals the number of equidistant pairs among $\{A,B,C\}$ relativ

 equidistant (if $|XA|=|XB|$ and $|XA|=|XC|$, then $|XB|=|XC|$), giving $b(X)=3$

Hence
$$\sum_{X\in S} b(X) \le 4 \;<\; 6 = \binom{4}{2},$$
contradicting condition 1. So $n=4$ is impossible.

**Construction for $n=3$: equilateral triangle.** Let $S$ be the three vertices

circumcenter, which is not a vertex, so no point of $S$ lies on all three; condi
