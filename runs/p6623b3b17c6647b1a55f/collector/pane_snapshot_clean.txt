# Solution

## Setup

We have the coordinate transformation (position vector in Cartesian basis $\hat{
e}_x, \hat{e}_y$):
$$\mathbf{r} = x\,\hat{e}_x + y\,\hat{e}_y, \quad x = 2u - v,\quad y = u - 2v.$$

## Covariant Basis Vectors and the Metric $g_{ij}$

The covariant basis vectors are $\mathbf{a}_i = \partial\mathbf{r}/\partial u^i$
:

$$\mathbf{a}_1 = \frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial u} = \left(\frac{\partial x}
{\partial u},\frac{\partial y}{\partial u}\right) = (2,\,1),$$
$$\mathbf{a}_2 = \frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial v} = \left(\frac{\partial x}
{\partial v},\frac{\partial y}{\partial v}\right) = (-1,\,-2).$$

The covariant metric tensor $g_{ij} = \mathbf{a}_i\cdot\mathbf{a}_j$:

$$g_{11} = 2^2+1^2 = 5,\quad g_{12}=g_{21}=(2)(-1)+(1)(-2)=-4,\quad g_{22}=(-1)^
2+(-2)^2=5.$$

$$\boxed{g_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}5&-4\\-4&5\end{pmatrix}}.$$

## Contravariant Metric $g^{ij}$

$$\det(g_{ij}) = 5\cdot5-(-4)(-4)=25-16=9,$$

$$\boxed{g^{ij}=(g_{ij})^{-1}=\frac{1}{9}\begin{pmatrix}5&4\\4&5\end{pmatrix}=\b
egin{pmatrix}5/9&4/9\\4/9&5/9\end{pmatrix}}.$$

## Contravariant Basis Vectors (via $g^{ij}$)

$$\mathbf{a}^i = g^{ij}\mathbf{a}_j:$$

$$\mathbf{a}^1 = \tfrac{5}{9}\mathbf{a}_1+\tfrac{4}{9}\mathbf{a}_2 = \tfrac{5}{9
}(2,1)+\tfrac{4}{9}(-1,-2)=\left(\tfrac{10-4}{9},\tfrac{5-8}{9}\right)=\left(\tf
rac{2}{3},-\tfrac{1}{3}\right),$$

$$\mathbf{a}^2 = \tfrac{4}{9}\mathbf{a}_1+\tfrac{5}{9}\mathbf{a}_2 = \tfrac{4}{9
}(2,1)+\tfrac{5}{9}(-1,-2)=\left(\tfrac{8-5}{9},\tfrac{4-10}{9}\right)=\left(\tf
rac{1}{3},-\tfrac{2}{3}\right).$$

$$\boxed{\mathbf{a}^1=\left(\tfrac{2}{3},-\tfrac{1}{3}\right),\quad \mathbf{a}^2
=\left(\tfrac{1}{3},-\tfrac{2}{3}\right)}.$$

## Verification via $\mathbf{a}^i = \nabla x_i$

Solve $x=2u-v,\;y=u-2v$ for $u,v$:

$$2x-y = 2(2u-v)-(u-2v)=3u \;\Longrightarrow\; u=\frac{2x-y}{3},$$
$$x-2y = (2u-v)-2(u-2v)=3v \;\Longrightarrow\; v=\frac{x-2y}{3}.$$

Now compute the gradients:

$$\mathbf{a}^1=\nabla u=\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x},\frac{\partial u}{\p
artial y}\right)=\left(\frac{2}{3},-\frac{1}{3}\right),\quad \mathbf{a}^2=\nabla
 v=\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x},\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\right)=\lef
t(\frac{1}{3},-\frac{2}{3}\right).$$

These **match exactly** the values obtained from $g^{ij}\mathbf{a}_j$. ✓

## Orthogonality Check $\mathbf{a}^i\cdot\mathbf{a}_j=\delta^i{}_j$

$$\mathbf{a}^1\!\cdot\!\mathbf{a}_1=\tfrac{2}{3}(2)+(-\tfrac{1}{3})(1)=\tfrac{4-
1}{3}=1,\quad \mathbf{a}^1\!\cdot\!\mathbf{a}_2=\tfrac{2}{3}(-1)+(-\tfrac{1}{3})
(-2)=\tfrac{-2+2}{3}=0,$$
$$\mathbf{a}^2\!\cdot\!\mathbf{a}_1=\tfrac{1}{3}(2)+(-\tfrac{2}{3})(1)=\tfrac{2-
2}{3}=0,\quad \mathbf{a}^2\!\cdot\!\mathbf{a}_2=\tfrac{1}{3}(-1)+(-\tfrac{2}{3})
(-2)=\tfrac{-1+4}{3}=1.$$

All four give $\delta^i{}_j$. ✓

## Sketch of Coordinate Lines and Basis Vectors

3v$, i.e. lines of slope $\tfrac{1}{2}$ (direction vector $(2,1)$). The covarian
t vector $\mathbf{a}_1=(2,1)$ lies **along** these lines — it is their tangent.
✓

**Family $u=\text{const}$** (parameter $v$ varies): eliminating $u$ gives $y=2x-

mathbf{a}_2=(-1,-2)$ lies **along** these lines — it is their tangent. ✓

## Confirmation of Normality

The defining reciprocal relation $\mathbf{a}^i\cdot\mathbf{a}_j=\delta^i{}_j$ en
codes precisely the geometric fact:

- $\mathbf{a}_j$ is **tangent** to the $u^j=\text{const}$ coordinate line (it is
 $\partial\mathbf{r}/\partial u^j$),
- $\mathbf{a}^i=\nabla u^i$ is **normal** to the surface $u^i=\text{const}$ (the
 gradient is always normal to level surfaces),
- and for $i\neq j$, $\mathbf{a}^i\cdot\mathbf{a}_j=0$ means each contravariant
vector is orthogonal to every covariant vector of a *different* index — i.e. the
 normal to the $u^i=\text{const}$ family is orthogonal to the tangent of the $u^
j=\text{const}$ family ($i\neq j$).

Numerically verified above: $\mathbf{a}^1\perp\mathbf{a}_2$ and $\mathbf{a}^2\pe
rp\mathbf{a}_1$. Thus the covariant basis vectors lie along the tangents to thei
r respective coordinate lines, while the contravariant basis vectors lie along t
he normals to the coordinate lines — exactly as required by $\mathbf{a}^i=\nabla
 x_i$.
