## Proof

**Claim:** The integral holds, i.e., $\int_0^1 |F(\mu, r)|\,dr = 1$ for every $\
mu \in \text{Prob}(\Gamma)$.

**Step 1: Rewrite the cardinality as a sum of indicators.**

Since $\Gamma$ is discrete (hence countable), for each $r \in [0,1]$:
$$|F(\mu, r)| = \bigl|\{t \in \Gamma : \mu(t) > r\}\bigr| = \sum_{t \in \Gamma}
\mathbf{1}\{\mu(t) > r\}.$$

Each term $\mathbf{1}\{\mu(t) > r\}$ is non-negative, so the sum is well-defined
 in $[0, \infty]$.

**Step 2: Finiteness of $|F(\mu,r)|$ for $r > 0$.**

For $r > 0$, if $\mu(t) > r$ then $t$ contributes more than $r$ to the total mas
s. Since $\sum_t \mu(t) = 1$, there can be at most $\lfloor 1/r \rfloor$ such el
ements. Hence $|F(\mu, r)| \leq 1/r < \infty$ for every $r > 0$. (At $r = 0$ the
 set may be countably infinite, but $\{0\}$ has Lebesgue measure zero and does n
ot affect the integral.)

**Step 3: Apply Tonelli's theorem.**

The integrand $(t, r) \mapsto \mathbf{1}\{\mu(t) > r\}$ is non-negative on $\Gam
ma \times [0,1]$, where $\Gamma$ carries counting measure and $[0,1]$ carries Le
besgue measure. By Tonelli's theorem we may interchange the sum and the integral
:

$$\int_0^1 |F(\mu, r)|\,dr = \int_0^1 \sum_{t \in \Gamma} \mathbf{1}\{\mu(t) > r
\}\,dr = \sum_{t \in \Gamma} \int_0^1 \mathbf{1}\{\mu(t) > r\}\,dr.$$

**Step 4: Evaluate the inner integral.**

For each fixed $t \in \Gamma$ with $\mu(t) \geq 0$:
$$\int_0^1 \mathbf{1}\{\mu(t) > r\}\,dr = \bigl|\{r \in [0,1] : r < \mu(t)\}\big
r| = \mu(t),$$
since the set $\{r \in [0,1] : r < \mu(t)\}$ is the interval $[0, \mu(t))$ (empt
y if $\mu(t) = 0$), which has Lebesgue measure $\mu(t)$.

**Step 5: Conclude.**

Substituting back:
$$\int_0^1 |F(\mu, r)|\,dr = \sum_{t \in \Gamma} \mu(t) = 1,$$
where the last equality uses the defining property $\mu \in \text{Prob}(\Gamma)$
, namely $\sum_{t \in \Gamma} \mu(t) = 1$.

Therefore, the integral holds for every probability measure $\mu$ on $\Gamma$.
