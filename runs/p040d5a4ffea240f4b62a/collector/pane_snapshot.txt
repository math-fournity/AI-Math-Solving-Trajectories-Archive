## Proof

**Upper bound.** For any $f \in \mathcal{F}$, the constraint $\max_{[0,1]}|f|=1$
 gives $|f(x)|\le 1$ for all $x\in[0,1]$. Hence
$$\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx \le \int_0^1 1\,dx = 1,\qquad -f(0)\le |f(0)|\le 1,\qquad f(
1)\le 1.$$
Therefore
$$\mathcal{I}(f)=\int_0^1 f - f(0)+f(1)\le 1+1+1=3.$$

**The bound 3 is not attained.** Equality would require $\int_0^1 f = 1$ (so $f\
equiv 1$ a.e., and by continuity $f\equiv 1$), $f(0)=-1$, and $f(1)=1$. But $f\e
quiv 1$ forces $f(0)=1\neq -1$, a contradiction. Thus $\mathcal{I}(f)<3$ for eve
ry $f\in\mathcal{F}$.

**Supremum equals 3.** Construct $f_n\in\mathcal{F}$ for $n\ge 2$ by
$$f_n(x)=\begin{cases}-1+2nx, & 0\le x\le \tfrac{1}{n},\\ 1, & \tfrac{1}{n}\le x
\le 1.\end{cases}$$
This is continuous, $f_n(0)=-1$, $f_n(1)=1$, and $\max|f_n|=1$, so $f_n\in\mathc
al{F}$. Its integral is
$$\int_0^1 f_n = \int_0^{1/n}(-1+2nx)\,dx + \int_{1/n}^1 1\,dx = \Big[-x+nx^2\Bi
g]_0^{1/n} + \Big(1-\tfrac{1}{n}\Big) = \Big(-\tfrac{1}{n}+\tfrac{1}{n}\Big)+1-\
tfrac{1}{n}=1-\tfrac{1}{n}.$$
Hence
$$\mathcal{I}(f_n)=\Big(1-\tfrac{1}{n}\Big)-(-1)+1 = 3-\tfrac{1}{n}\xrightarrow[
n\to\infty]{}3.$$

Combining the upper bound and the approximating sequence,

$$\boxed{\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{I}(f)=3}.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
