## Solution

Let $n = 2015$. We compute $\log_2\!\left(\prod_{a=1}^{n}\prod_{b=1}^{n}(1+e^{2\
pi i ab/n})\right)$.

**Step 1: Evaluate the inner product.** Fix $a$ and let $d = \gcd(a,n)$. Write $
a = d\,a'$, $n = d\,n'$ with $\gcd(a',n')=1$. Then $ab/n = a'b/n'$, and as $b$ r
anges over $1,\dots,n$, the residue $a'b \bmod n'$ runs through all of $\{1,\dot
s,n'\}$ exactly $d$ times. Hence

$$\prod_{b=1}^{n}(1+e^{2\pi i ab/n}) = \left(\prod_{b=1}^{n'}(1+e^{2\pi i b/n'})
\right)^{\!d}.$$

**Step 2: A root-of-unity product.** Since $x^m - 1 = \prod_{k=0}^{m-1}(x - e^{2
\pi i k/m})$, setting $x=-1$ gives

$$\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}(1+e^{2\pi i k/m}) = 1 - (-1)^m.$$

The index $b=m$ gives $e^{2\pi i}=1 = e^{2\pi i\cdot 0/m}$, so $\prod_{b=1}^{m}(
1+e^{2\pi i b/m}) = 1-(-1)^m$: equal to **2** if $m$ is odd, and **0** if $m$ is
 even.

**Step 3: No zero factors.** Since $n = 2015 = 5\times 13\times 31$ is **odd**,
every divisor $d\mid n$ gives $n'=n/d$ odd, so every inner product equals $2^d$
(never $0$). Therefore

$$\prod_{a=1}^{n}\prod_{b=1}^{n}(1+e^{2\pi i ab/n}) = \prod_{a=1}^{n} 2^{\gcd(a,
n)} = 2^{\,\sum_{a=1}^{n}\gcd(a,n)}.$$

Taking $\log_2$:

$$\log_2(\cdots) = \sum_{a=1}^{n}\gcd(a,n) =: S(n).$$

**Step 4: Compute $S(2015)$.** The function $S(n)=\sum_{d\mid n} d\,\varphi(n/d)
$ is multiplicative. For a prime $p$:

$$S(p) = 1\cdot\varphi(p) + p\cdot\varphi(1) = (p-1)+p = 2p-1.$$

Since $2015 = 5\cdot 13\cdot 31$ (distinct primes):

$$S(2015) = S(5)\cdot S(13)\cdot S(31) = (2\cdot5-1)(2\cdot13-1)(2\cdot31-1) = 9
 \times 25 \times 61.$$

$$9\times 25 = 225,\qquad 225 \times 61 = 225\times 60 + 225 = 13500+225 = 13725
.$$

## Answer

$$\boxed{\log_2\!\left(\prod_{a=1}^{2015}\prod_{b=1}^{2015}(1+e^{2\pi iab/2015})
\right) = 13725}$$
