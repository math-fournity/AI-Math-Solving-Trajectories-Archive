## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle I = \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\log x}{\sqrt{x}\,(x+1)^{
2}}\,dx$.

### Step 1: Substitution $x = t^{2}$

Let $x = t^{2}$, so $dx = 2t\,dt$, $\sqrt{x}=t$, $\log x = 2\log t$. The limits
$x:0\to\infty$ become $t:0\to\infty$.

$$I = \int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{2\log t}{t\,(t^{2}+1)^{2}}\cdot 2t\,dt = 4\int_{0}^
{\infty}\frac{\log t}{(t^{2}+1)^{2}}\,dt.$$

### Step 2: Introduce a parameterized integral

Define
$$J(a)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\,a-1}}{(1+t^{2})^{2}}\,dt,\qquad 0<a<4.$$

Then $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\log t}{(1+t^{2})^{2}}\,dt = J'(1)$, s
ince $\frac{\partial}{\partial a}t^{a-1}\big|_{a=1}=\log t$.

### Step 3: Evaluate $J(a)$ via the Beta function

Substitute $t=\tan\theta$, $dt=\sec^{2}\theta\,d\theta$:

$$J(a)=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{\tan^{a-1}\theta}{\sec^{4}\theta}\sec^{2}\theta\,d\
theta=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sin^{a-1}\theta\,\cos^{3-a}\theta\,d\theta.$$

Using the standard identity $\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sin^{p-1}\theta\cos^{q-1}\theta\,d
\theta=\tfrac{1}{2}B\!\left(\tfrac{p}{2},\tfrac{q}{2}\right)$ with $p=a$, $q=4-a
$:

$$J(a)=\frac{1}{2}\,B\!\left(\frac{a}{2},\frac{4-a}{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\,\frac
{\Gamma(a/2)\,\Gamma(2-a/2)}{\Gamma(2)}=\frac{1}{2}\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{a}{2}\r
ight)\Gamma\!\left(2-\frac{a}{2}\right),$$

since $\Gamma(2)=1$.

### Step 4: Differentiate at $a=1$

Using $\Gamma'(x)=\Gamma(x)\,\psi(x)$ and the product rule:

$$J'(a)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\left[\Gamma'\!\left(\frac{a}{2}\right)\Gamma
\!\left(2-\frac{a}{2}\right)-\Gamma\!\left(\frac{a}{2}\right)\Gamma'\!\left(2-\f
rac{a}{2}\right)\right].$$

At $a=1$, using $\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt{\pi}$, $\Gamma(3/2)=\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\pi}$,
$\psi(1/2)=-\gamma-2\ln 2$, and $\psi(3/2)=\psi(1/2)+2=-\gamma-2\ln 2+2$:

$$J'(1)=\frac{1}{4}\Big[\underbrace{\sqrt{\pi}(-\gamma-2\ln 2)}_{\Gamma'(1/2)}\c
dot\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\pi}-\sqrt{\pi}\cdot\underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\pi}(-\ga
mma-2\ln 2+2)}_{\Gamma'(3/2)}\Big].$$

$$J'(1)=\frac{\pi}{8}\Big[(-\gamma-2\ln 2)-(-\gamma-2\ln 2+2)\Big]=\frac{\pi}{8}
\cdot(-2)=-\frac{\pi}{4}.$$

### Step 5: Conclude

$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\log t}{(1+t^{2})^{2}}\,dt = J'(1) = -\frac{\pi}{4},$$

and therefore

$$I = 4\cdot\left(-\frac{\pi}{4}\right) = \boxed{-\pi}.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
