# Derivation of the Covariant Derivative of a Contravariant Vector

## Setup and Definitions

Let $v^i$ be a contravariant vector field defined on a manifold with coordinates
 $x^k$. Under a coordinate transformation $x^i \to x'^i$, a contravariant vector
 transforms as

$$v'^i = \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^j}\, v^j.$$

The **ordinary (partial) derivative** of $v^i$ is $\frac{\partial v^i}{\partial
x^k}$. We first show this is **not** a tensor, which motivates the covariant der
ivative.

## Step 1: The Partial Derivative Is Not a Tensor

Differentiating the transformation law:

$$\frac{\partial v'^i}{\partial x'^k} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x'^k}\!\left(\f
rac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^j}v^j\right) = \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^j}\
frac{\partial v^j}{\partial x'^k} + \frac{\partial^2 x'^i}{\partial x^j\,\partia
l x^l}v^j\frac{\partial x^l}{\partial x'^k}.$$

Using the chain rule on the first term, $\frac{\partial v^j}{\partial x'^k} = \f
rac{\partial x^m}{\partial x'^k}\frac{\partial v^j}{\partial x^m}$:

$$\frac{\partial v'^i}{\partial x'^k} = \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^j}\frac{
\partial x^m}{\partial x'^k}\frac{\partial v^j}{\partial x^m} + \frac{\partial^2
 x'^i}{\partial x^j\,\partial x^l}\frac{\partial x^l}{\partial x'^k}v^j.$$

The **second term** involves the second derivative $\frac{\partial^2 x'^i}{\part
ial x^j\,\partial x^l}$, which does **not** vanish for general (nonlinear) coord
inate transformations. Hence $\frac{\partial v^i}{\partial x^k}$ does **not** tr
ansform as a tensor. We must add a correction term to cancel this extra piece.

## Step 2: Introducing the Christoffel Symbols

Define the **Christoffel symbols of the second kind** (connection coefficients):

$$\Gamma^i_{jk} = \frac{1}{2}g^{il}\!\left(\frac{\partial g_{lj}}{\partial x^k}
+ \frac{\partial g_{lk}}{\partial x^j} - \frac{\partial g_{jk}}{\partial x^l}\ri
ght),$$

where $g_{ij}$ is the metric tensor and $g^{ij}$ its inverse. The key transforma
tion property of the Christoffel symbols is:

$$\Gamma'^i_{jk} = \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^m}\frac{\partial x^p}{\partia
l x'^j}\frac{\partial x^q}{\partial x'^k}\Gamma^m_{pq} + \frac{\partial x'^i}{\p
artial x^m}\frac{\partial^2 x^m}{\partial x'^j\,\partial x'^k}.$$

The **inhomogeneous (second) term** is exactly the kind of second-derivative ter
m that appeared in Step 1 — but with opposite structure. This is precisely what
we need to cancel the non-tensorial piece.

## Step 3: Definition of the Covariant Derivative

The **covariant derivative** of a contravariant vector $v^i$ with respect to $x^
k$ is **defined** as the partial derivative plus a correction term built from th
e connection:

$$\nabla_k v^i \;:=\; \frac{\partial v^i}{\partial x^k} + \Gamma^i_{km}\,v^m.$$

The correction term $\Gamma^i_{km}v^m$ is chosen so that $\nabla_k v^i$ transfor
ms as a genuine **(1,1)-type mixed tensor**.

## Step 4: Verification That $\nabla_k v^i$ Is a Tensor

Compute the transformation of $\nabla'_k v'^i$:

$$\nabla'_k v'^i = \frac{\partial v'^i}{\partial x'^k} + \Gamma'^i_{km}\,v'^m.$$

Substituting the results of Steps 1 and 2:

$$\nabla'_k v'^i = \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^j}\frac{\partial x^m}{\partia
l x'^k}\frac{\partial v^j}{\partial x^m} + \frac{\partial^2 x'^i}{\partial x^j\,
\partial x^l}\frac{\partial x^l}{\partial x'^k}v^j + \left(\frac{\partial x'^i}{
\partial x^p}\frac{\partial x^r}{\partial x'^k}\frac{\partial x^s}{\partial x'^m
}\Gamma^p_{rs} + \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^p}\frac{\partial^2 x^p}{\partia
l x'^k\,\partial x'^m}\right)\frac{\partial x'^m}{\partial x^n}v^n.$$

Simplify the correction-term products using $\frac{\partial x^s}{\partial x'^m}\
frac{\partial x'^m}{\partial x^n} = \delta^s_n$ and $\frac{\partial x^r}{\partia
l x'^k}$ contracts with the outer $\frac{\partial x^m}{\partial x'^k}$-type fact
or:

$$\Gamma'^i_{km}\,v'^m = \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^p}\frac{\partial x^r}{\
partial x'^k}\Gamma^p_{rn}\,v^n + \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^p}\frac{\parti
al^2 x^p}{\partial x'^k\,\partial x'^m}\frac{\partial x'^m}{\partial x^n}v^n.$$

For the second-derivative pieces, use the identity obtained by differentiating $
\frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^p}\frac{\partial x^p}{\partial x'^m} = \delta^i_
m$ with respect to $x'^k$:

$$\frac{\partial^2 x'^i}{\partial x^j\,\partial x^l}\frac{\partial x^l}{\partial
 x'^k}v^j + \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^p}\frac{\partial^2 x^p}{\partial x'^
k\,\partial x'^m}\frac{\partial x'^m}{\partial x^n}v^n = 0.$$

These two inhomogeneous terms **cancel exactly** (one is the "primed-second-deri
vative times unprimed Jacobian" piece, the other is the "unprimed-second-derivat
ive times primed Jacobian" piece; their sum vanishes by the identity above).

After cancellation, relabeling dummy indices ($j\to p$, $m\to r$, $n\to s$):

$$\nabla'_k v'^i = \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^p}\frac{\partial x^r}{\partia
l x'^k}\!\left(\frac{\partial v^p}{\partial x^r} + \Gamma^p_{rs}\,v^s\right) = \
frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^p}\frac{\partial x^r}{\partial x'^k}\,\nabla_r v^
p.$$

This is precisely the transformation law of a **(1,1) mixed tensor** (one contra
variant index $i$, one covariant index $k$). Hence $\nabla_k v^i$ is a genuine t
ensor, as required.

## Step 5: Recovering Special Cases

- **Cartesian coordinates** (flat space, $g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}$): all $\Gamma^i_{k
m}=0$, so $\nabla_k v^i = \frac{\partial v^i}{\partial x^k}$ — the covariant der
ivative reduces to the ordinary partial derivative, as expected.
- **Consistency with the covariant-vector rule**: For a covariant vector $w_i$,
the analogous definition is $\nabla_k w_i = \frac{\partial w_i}{\partial x^k} -
\Gamma^m_{ik}\,w_m$ (minus sign, index lowered). Our result for $v^i$ is the con
travariant counterpart, with the correction index contracted on the **upper** sl
ot via $\Gamma^i_{km}$.

vative (Step 1) and the Christoffel symbols (Step 2), the unique correction term

herefore the covariant derivative of a contravariant vector $v^i$ with respect t

$$\boxed{\nabla_k v^i \;=\; \frac{\partial v^i}{\partial x^k} + \Gamma^i_{km}\,v
