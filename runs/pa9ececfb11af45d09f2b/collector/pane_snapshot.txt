## Proof

We determine the weak limit of $(\mu_n)$, where $\mu_n$ has density
$$f_n(x) = \sqrt{\tfrac{n}{2\pi}}\, e^{-\tfrac{nx^2}{2}}.$$

**Claim.** $\mu_n \Rightarrow \delta_0$ (the Dirac measure at the origin).

---

### Step 1. Identification of $\mu_n$

The function $f_n$ is the density of the Gaussian distribution $N(0, 1/n)$: it i
s non-negative, continuous, and
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} f_n(x)\,d\lambda(x) = \sqrt{\tfrac{n}{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}
} e^{-nx^2/2}\,dx = \sqrt{\tfrac{n}{2\pi}}\cdot\sqrt{\tfrac{2\pi}{n}} = 1,$$
so each $\mu_n$ is a probability measure. Its mean is $0$ and its variance is $\
sigma_n^2 = 1/n$.

---

### Step 2. Characterization of weak convergence via distribution functions

By the portmanteau / Helly–Bray characterization, $\mu_n \Rightarrow \mu$ on $(\
mathbb{R},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))$ iff the distribution functions $F_n(x)=\mu_n
((-\infty,x])$ converge to $F(x)=\mu((-\infty,x])$ at every continuity point $x$
 of $F$.

We show $F_n(x)\to \mathbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}(x)$ for every $x\ne 0$, which is the
distribution function of $\delta_0$ (whose only discontinuity is at $x=0$).

---

### Step 3. Convergence of $F_n$ at continuity points of $\delta_0$

Let $F_n(x)=\mu_n((-\infty,x])=\Phi(\sqrt{n}\,x)$, where $\Phi$ is the standard
normal cdf.

**Case $x<0$.** Then $\sqrt{n}\,x\to -\infty$, so $F_n(x)=\Phi(\sqrt{n}\,x)\to 0
 = \delta_0((-\infty,x])$.

**Case $x>0$.** Then $\sqrt{n}\,x\to +\infty$, so $F_n(x)=\Phi(\sqrt{n}\,x)\to 1
 = \delta_0((-\infty,x])$.

Both cases give convergence at points $x\ne 0$, i.e. at every continuity point o
f the cdf of $\delta_0$.

---

### Step 4. Alternative verification via the portmanteau theorem (bounded contin
uous test functions)

For every bounded continuous $g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$,
$$\int g\,d\mu_n = \int_{\mathbb{R}} g(x)\sqrt{\tfrac{n}{2\pi}}\,e^{-nx^2/2}\,dx
.$$
Substituting $u=\sqrt{n}\,x$,
$$\int g\,d\mu_n = \int_{\mathbb{R}} g\!\left(\tfrac{u}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\tfrac{1
}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-u^2/2}\,du.$$
Since $g$ is continuous, $g(u/\sqrt{n})\to g(0)$ for every $u$, and $|g(u/\sqrt{
n})|\le\|g\|_\infty$ which is $\lambda$-a.e. integrable against the standard nor
mal density. By dominated convergence,
$$\int g\,d\mu_n \to g(0)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-u^2/2}\,du =
 g(0) = \int g\,d\delta_0.$$
By the portmanteau theorem, $\mu_n\Rightarrow\delta_0$.

---

### Step 5. The limit is a probability measure

$\delta_0$ is a probability measure on $(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))$, s
o no mass escapes to infinity — consistent with the fact that $\mu_n(\mathbb{R})
=1$ for all $n$ and $\delta_0(\mathbb{R})=1$.

---

### Conclusion

The sequence $(\mu_n)$ converges weakly to the Dirac measure concentrated at the
 origin:
$$\boxed{\;\mu_n \;\Rightarrow\; \delta_0\;}$$
This is the standard example of an *approximation to the identity*: the Gaussian
 densities $N(0,1/n)$ collapse onto the point mass at $0$ as their variance $1/n
\to 0$.
