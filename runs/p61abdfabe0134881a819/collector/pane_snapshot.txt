## Proof

Let $\lambda(n) = (-1)^{\Omega(n)}$ be the Liouville function (with $\lambda(0)=
1$ since $\Omega(0)=0$ is even). This is **completely multiplicative**: $\lambda
(mn)=\lambda(m)\lambda(n)$.

The hypothesis "$\Omega((a+k)(b+k))$ is even for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$" is equiva
lent to
$$\lambda((a+k)(b+k))=1 \iff \lambda(a+k)\,\lambda(b+k)=1 \iff \lambda(a+k)=\lam
bda(b+k) \quad\forall\, k\in\mathbb{N}. \tag{$*$}$$

### Reduction to a periodicity statement

WLOG $a\le b$; set $d=b-a$. If $d=0$ we are done, so **suppose $d\ge 1$** for co
ntradiction. Let $N=a+\min\mathbb{N}$ (so $N=a$ or $a+1$ depending on convention
). Then $(*)$ gives

$$\lambda(n)=\lambda(n+d) \quad\text{for all } n\ge N. \tag{$**$}$$

By induction, $\lambda(n)=\lambda(n+md)$ for all $m\ge 0$, $n\ge N$. Hence **$\l
ambda$ is constant on each set** $S_r=\{n\ge N : n\equiv r\pmod d\}$.

### The squaring trick

Consider $S_1=\{n\ge N:n\equiv 1\pmod d\}$, which is nonempty (it contains $1+kd
$ for large $k$). For any $n_0\in S_1$:
- $n_0^2\equiv 1\pmod d$ and $n_0^2\ge n_0\ge N$, so $n_0^2\in S_1$.
- Since $\lambda$ is constant on $S_1$: $\lambda(n_0)=\lambda(n_0^2)=\lambda(n_0
)^2=1$.

Therefore $\lambda(n)=1$ for every $n\in S_1$; equivalently, **$\Omega(n)$ is ev
en for all $n\ge N$ with $n\equiv 1\pmod d$**.

### Lemma: infinitely many primes $\equiv 1\pmod d$

*Proof.* For $d=1$ this is Euclid's theorem. For $d\ge 2$, suppose $p_1,\dots,p_
t$ are all such primes. Set $M=d\cdot p_1\cdots p_t\ge 2$ and let $\Phi_d$ be th
e $d$-th cyclotomic polynomial. The integer $\Phi_d(M)$ has $|\Phi_d(M)|>1$, so
it has a prime factor $q$.

Since $\Phi_d(M)\mid M^d-1$ and $\gcd(M,M^d-1)=1$, we have $q\nmid M$, hence $q\
nmid d$ and $q\notin\{p_1,\dots,p_t\}$.

We claim $\operatorname{ord}_q(M)=d$. Since $q\mid\Phi_d(M)\mid M^d-1$, we get $
\operatorname{ord}_q(M)\mid d$. If $e:=\operatorname{ord}_q(M)<d$, then $q\mid M
^e-1$. Writing $M^d-1=(M^e-1)\cdot T$ where $T=1+M^e+M^{2e}+\cdots+M^{d-e}$, we
have $T\equiv d/e\pmod{M^e-1}$, so $q\mid\gcd(M^e-1,T)\mid d/e$. But $(d/e)\mid
d$ and $q\nmid d$ — contradiction. Thus $\operatorname{ord}_q(M)=d$, giving $d\m
id(q-1)$, i.e. $q\equiv 1\pmod d$, contradicting the assumption that $p_1,\dots,
p_t$ were all such primes. $\square$

### Contradiction

By the Lemma, there exists a prime $p\equiv 1\pmod d$ with $p\ge N$. Then $p\in
S_1$, so $\lambda(p)=1$ by the squaring trick. But

$$\lambda(p)=(-1)^{\Omega(p)}=(-1)^1=-1.$$

This contradicts $\lambda(p)=1$.

### Conclusion

The contradiction forces $d=0$, i.e. $\boxed{a=b}$. $\blacksquare$
