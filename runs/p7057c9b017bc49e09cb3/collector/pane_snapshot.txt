teq X$ to a metric space $Y$ cannot always be extended to a continuous $F: X \to

**Counterexample.** Let
- $X = [0,1]$ with the usual (subspace) topology,
- $A = \{0, 1\} \subset X$, which is closed (it is finite, hence compact, hence
closed in the Hausdorff space $[0,1]$),
- $Y = \{0, 1\}$ equipped with the discrete metric $d(a,b) = 1$ for $a \neq b$ (
equivalently, $Y$ as a subspace of $\mathbb{R}$),
- $f: A \to Y$ defined by $f(0) = 0$ and $f(1) = 1$.

**$f$ is continuous.** The domain $A = \{0,1\}$ carries the discrete topology (e
very subset of a finite subset of a $T_1$ space is closed, so every subset is op
en). Any map out of a discrete space is continuous. Hence $f$ is continuous.

**No continuous extension $F: [0,1] \to \{0,1\}$ exists.** Suppose, for contradi
ction, that $F: [0,1] \to \{0,1\}$ is continuous and $F|_A = f$, so $F(0) = 0$ a
nd $F(1) = 1$.

Since $Y = \{0,1\}$ has the discrete topology, both singletons $\{0\}$ and $\{1\
}$ are open (and closed) in $Y$. By continuity of $F$:
- $U := F^{-1}(\{0\})$ is both open and closed in $[0,1]$,
- $V := F^{-1}(\{1\})$ is both open and closed in $[0,1]$.

We have $[0,1] = U \sqcup V$ (disjoint union), with $0 \in U$ and $1 \in V$, so
both $U$ and $V$ are **nonempty**. Thus $\{U, V\}$ is a separation of $[0,1]$: t
wo nonempty, disjoint, open sets whose union is $[0,1]$.

But $[0,1]$ is **connected** (a standard result: it is the continuous image of t
he connected space $\mathbb{R}$ under $t \mapsto$ the appropriate map, or direct
ly, any separation of $[0,1]$ leads to a contradiction via the supremum of one p
art). This contradicts the existence of such a separation.

Therefore no continuous extension $F$ exists. $\square$

**Conclusion.** The extension is not always possible. The obstruction here is to
pological: $[0,1]$ is connected while $Y = \{0,1\}$ is disconnected, and a conti
nuous image of a connected space must be connected — so a continuous map $[0,1]
\to \{0,1\}$ must be constant, yet $f$ takes two distinct values. This shows tha
t even with $A$ closed and $Y$ a (very nice) metric space, continuous extension
can fail.
