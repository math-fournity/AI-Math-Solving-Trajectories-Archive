No, the converse is not true. A bounded complex of finite-dimensional vector spa
ces can have Euler characteristic zero without being exact.

## Counterexample

Consider the following bounded complex $M$ of finite-dimensional $k$-vector spac
es, concentrated in degrees $0$ and $1$:

$$
\cdots \to 0 \to \underbrace{k}_{M_0} \xrightarrow{\;d\;} \underbrace{k}_{M_1} \
to 0 \to \cdots
$$

where the differential $d: M_0 \to M_1$ is the **zero map** $d = 0$.

### Computing the Euler characteristic

$$
\chi(M) = \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} (-1)^n \dim(M_n) = (-1)^0 \dim(k) + (-1)^1 \di
m(k) = 1 - 1 = 0.
$$

So the Euler characteristic is zero.

### Computing the homology

The homology of this complex at each degree is:

- **At degree 0**: $H_0(M) = \ker(d: M_0 \to M_1) \,/\, \operatorname{im}(d: M_{
-1} \to M_0) = \ker(0: k \to k) \,/\, 0 = k \,/\, 0 \cong k \neq 0.$

- **At degree 1**: $H_1(M) = \ker(d: M_1 \to M_2) \,/\, \operatorname{im}(d: M_0
 \to M_1) = k \,/\, \operatorname{im}(0) = k \,/\, 0 \cong k \neq 0.$

Since $H_0(M) \neq 0$ (and $H_1(M) \neq 0$), the complex $M$ is **not exact**.

### Conclusion

We have exhibited a bounded complex of finite-dimensional $k$-vector spaces with
 $\chi(M) = 0$ that is not exact. Therefore the converse of the stated implicati
on is **false**: vanishing of the Euler characteristic does not imply exactness.

### Remark

The Euler characteristic is additive in short exact sequences, which is why exac
tness forces $\chi = 0$: an exact complex splits into short exact sequences $0 \
to \ker d_n \to M_n \to \operatorname{im} d_n \to 0$, giving $\dim M_n = \dim(\k
er d_n) + \dim(\operatorname{im} d_n)$, and the alternating sum telescopes to ze
ro. However, $\chi = 0$ is only a single numerical constraint on the dimensions
$\{\dim M_n\}$, whereas exactness imposes the much stronger requirement that **a
ll** homology groups vanish. The counterexample above shows these are not equiva
lent: the zero differential makes every term contribute full homology, yet the d
imensions still cancel in the alternating sum.
