## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $n = m$.

**Setup.** Let $f: O \to U$ be a bijection between open sets $O \subseteq \mathb
b{R}^m$ and $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$, with both $f$ and $f^{-1}$ differentiabl
e. Fix any point $x \in O$ and let $y = f(x) \in U$.

**Derivatives as linear maps.** Since $f$ is differentiable at $x$, its derivati
ve $Df(x)$ is a linear map $\mathbb{R}^m \to \mathbb{R}^n$, represented (after c
hoosing coordinates) by an $n \times m$ matrix $A$. Since $f^{-1}$ is differenti
able at $y$, its derivative $D(f^{-1})(y)$ is a linear map $\mathbb{R}^n \to \ma
thbb{R}^m$, represented by an $m \times n$ matrix $B$.

**Apply the chain rule.** Because $f$ is a bijection, $f^{-1} \circ f = \mathrm{
id}_O$ and $f \circ f^{-1} = \mathrm{id}_U$. Differentiating both compositions a
t the appropriate points via the chain rule:

$$D(f^{-1})(y) \circ Df(x) = D(\mathrm{id}_O)(x) = I_m, \quad\text{i.e., } BA =
I_m,$$

$$Df(x) \circ D(f^{-1})(y) = D(\mathrm{id}_U)(y) = I_n, \quad\text{i.e., } AB =
I_n.$$

**Rank argument.** From $BA = I_m$ (an $m \times m$ identity), we get $\operator
name{rank}(BA) = m$. Since $\operatorname{rank}(BA) \le \operatorname{rank}(A) \
le \min(m, n)$, we obtain

$$m \le \min(m, n) \implies m \le n.$$

From $AB = I_n$ (an $n \times n$ identity), we get $\operatorname{rank}(AB) = n$
. Since $\operatorname{rank}(AB) \le \operatorname{rank}(A) \le \min(m, n)$, we
obtain

$$n \le \min(m, n) \implies n \le m.$$

**Conclusion.** Combining $m \le n$ and $n \le m$ yields $m = n$. $\blacksquare$
