The implication is **true**. Here is the proof.

---

**Claim.** Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ ($d=2$ or $3$) be a domain with smo
oth boundary $\Gamma$, and let $T>0$. If
$$u \in W^{1,1}\bigl([0,T];\, L^2(\Omega)\bigr),$$
then
$$u \in L^{\infty}\bigl([0,T];\, L^2(\Omega)\bigr).$$

---

**Proof.**

Set $X := L^2(\Omega)$, a Banach space (in fact a Hilbert space). The hypothesis
 is
$$u \in W^{1,1}(0,T; X),$$
i.e., $u \in L^1(0,T;X)$ and the weak derivative $u' \in L^1(0,T;X)$ both exist.

**Step 1. $u$ has an absolutely continuous (hence continuous) representative $[0
,T]\to X$.**

Define
$$v(t) := \int_0^t u'(s)\,ds, \qquad t\in[0,T],$$
where the integral is the Bochner integral. Since $u'\in L^1(0,T;X)$, the map $t
\mapsto v(t)$ is well defined and absolutely continuous (hence continuous) from
$[0,T]$ into $X$. In particular $v\in C([0,T];X)$.

We claim $u - v$ is a.e. constant. Indeed, for every test function $\varphi\in C
_c^\infty(0,T)$ and every $\ell\in X^*$,
$$\langle \ell,\, (u-v)' \text{ (weak)}\rangle = -\int_0^T \langle \ell, u(t)-v(
t)\rangle\, \varphi'(t)\,dt.$$
Since $v' = u'$ (a.e., by the fundamental theorem of calculus for the Bochner in
tegral), $(u-v)' = 0$ in the weak sense. A weakly-differentiable function on the
 connected interval $(0,T)$ with zero weak derivative is a.e. constant (this is
verified by testing against scalar functions and applying the classical scalar r
esult). Hence there exists $c\in X$ such that
$$u(t) = c + \int_0^t u'(s)\,ds \quad \text{for a.e. } t\in[0,T].$$

Define
$$\tilde u(t) := c + \int_0^t u'(s)\,ds, \qquad t\in[0,T].$$
Then $\tilde u \in C([0,T];X)$ (absolutely continuous, in fact) and $\tilde u =
u$ a.e. on $[0,T]$.

**Step 2. Continuity on a compact interval implies boundedness.**

The map
$$t \longmapsto \|\tilde u(t)\|_{X} = \|\tilde u(t)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}$$
is continuous (composition of the continuous map $\tilde u:[0,T]\to X$ with the
continuous norm $\|\cdot\|_X$). Since $[0,T]$ is compact,
$$\sup_{t\in[0,T]} \|\tilde u(t)\|_{L^2(\Omega)} < \infty.$$

Because $u = \tilde u$ a.e.,
$$\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t\in[0,T]} \|u(t)\|_{L^2(\Omega)} = \sup_{t\in[0,T]}
 \|\tilde u(t)\|_{L^2(\Omega)} < \infty,$$
which is precisely
$$u \in L^{\infty}\bigl([0,T];\, L^2(\Omega)\bigr).$$

Moreover, one obtains the quantitative estimate
$$\|u\|_{L^\infty(0,T;X)} \le \|u(0)\|_X + \|u'\|_{L^1(0,T;X)},$$
by the triangle inequality applied to $\tilde u(t) = c + \int_0^t u'(s)\,ds$ (wi
th $c = \tilde u(0)$).

---

**Remark.** The argument uses only that $X$ is a Banach space and that the time
interval is one-dimensional and bounded. The spatial dimension $d\in\{2,3\}$ and
 the smoothness of $\Gamma$ play no role; the result holds for any Banach-space-
valued $W^{1,1}$ function on a bounded interval. The essential mechanism is the
one-dimensional Sobolev embedding
$$W^{1,1}(0,T) \hookrightarrow C([0,T]) \hookrightarrow L^\infty(0,T),$$
lifted to the Bochner (vector-valued) setting.
