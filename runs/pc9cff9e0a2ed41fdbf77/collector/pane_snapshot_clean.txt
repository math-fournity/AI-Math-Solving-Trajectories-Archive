## Proof

**Claim.** Every field extension $K/F$ of degree $m$ embeds (as an $F$-algebra)
into $M_m(F)$.

**Construction (the regular representation).** Since $:F]=m$, the field $K$ is
 an $m$-dimensional vector space over $F$. Fix an $F$-basis $e_1,\dots,e_m$ of $
K$.

For each $\alpha\in K$, define the **left-multiplication map**
$$
L_\alpha : K \longrightarrow K, \qquad L_\alpha(x)=\alpha x .
$$
Because multiplication in $K$ is $F$-bilinear, $L_\alpha$ is $F$-linear, i.e. $L
_\alpha\in \operatorname{End}_F(K)\cong M_m(F)$.

Define
$$
\varphi : K \longrightarrow \operatorname{End}_F(K), \qquad \varphi(\alpha)=L_\a
lpha .
$$

**$\varphi$ is an $F$-algebra homomorphism.**

- *$F$-linearity:* $L_{\alpha+\beta}=L_\alpha+L_\beta$ and $L_{c\alpha}=c\,L_\al
pha$ for $c\in F$ (since $c$ commutes with every element of $K$), and $L_1=\oper
atorname{id}$.
- *Multiplicative:* $L_{\alpha\beta}(x)=\alpha\beta x = L_\alpha(L_\beta(x))$, s
o $\varphi(\alpha\beta)=\varphi(\alpha)\circ\varphi(\beta)$.
- *Unital:* $\varphi(1)=L_1=\operatorname{id}$, the identity endomorphism.

**$\varphi$ is injective.** Its kernel is a two-sided ideal of the field $K$, he
nce $\ker\varphi\in\{0,K\}$. Since $\varphi(1)=\operatorname{id}\neq 0$, we have
 $\ker\varphi\neq K$, so $\ker\varphi=0$.

**Conclusion.** $\varphi:K\hookrightarrow \operatorname{End}_F(K)\cong M_m(F)$ i
s an injective $F$-algebra homomorphism, giving the desired embedding. Concretel
y, the matrix of $L_\alpha$ in the basis $\{e_i\}$ is obtained by writing $\alph
a e_j=\sum_i a_{ij}e_i$; the matrix $(a_{ij})\in M_m(F)$ is the image of $\alpha
$.

Thus **yes**: a field extension of degree $m$ over $F$ always embeds into $M_m(F
)$, via the (left) regular representation.
