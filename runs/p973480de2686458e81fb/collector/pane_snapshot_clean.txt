## Solution

We interpret the problem for **positive integers**, since GCD and LCM are conven
tionally defined on positive integers (the natural domain of these notions). "Di
fference between product and sum" means the absolute value $|ab-(a+b)|$.

Let $a,b$ be distinct positive integers. Write $d=\gcd(a,b)$, so $a=dm,\ b=dn$ w
ith $\gcd(m,n)=1$, $m\ne n$, $m,n\ge 1$. Then $\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)=dmn$, and
$$\gcd(a,b)+\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)=d+dmn=d(1+mn).$$
Also
$$|ab-(a+b)|=\bigl|d^{2}mn-d(m+n)\bigr|=d\,\bigl|dmn-(m+n)\bigr|.$$
The condition becomes
$$\bigl|dmn-(m+n)\bigr|=1+mn. \tag{*}$$

Since $d\ge 1$ and $mn\ge m+n-1$ (because $(m-1)(n-1)\ge 0$), we have $dmn-(m+n)
\ge mn-(m+n)=(m-1)(n-1)-1\ge -1$, while $1+mn>0$. Hence $(*)$ splits into two ca
ses.

**Case 2:** $dmn-(m+n)=-(1+mn)$, i.e. $mn(d+1)=m+n-1$.

Since $d+1\ge 2$, the left side is $\ge 2mn$. But for $m,n\ge 1$ with $m\ne n$ w
e have $2mn\ge m+n>m+n-1$, so $2mn>m+n-1$: no solution exists in this case.

**Case 1:** $dmn-(m+n)=1+mn$, i.e.
$$mn(d-1)=m+n+1. \tag{**}$$

If $d=1$, the left side is $0$ but $m+n+1>0$: impossible. So $d\ge 2$.

*Subcase $m=1$ (by symmetry we may take the smaller one).* Then $(**)$ gives $n(
d-1)=n+2$, i.e. $n(d-2)=2$. Thus $n\mid 2$. With $n\ne m=1$:
- $n=2$: $d-2=1\Rightarrow d=3$, giving $(a,b)=(3,6)$.

*Subcase $m\ge 2$ (hence $n\ge 2$).* Here $mn\ge m+n$ (since $(m-1)(n-1)\ge 1$).
 If $d-1\ge 2$, then $mn(d-1)\ge 2mn\ge 2(m+n)>m+n+1$ (as $m+n\ge 4$), too large
. So $d-1=1$, i.e. $d=2$, and $(**)$ becomes
$$mn=m+n+1\iff (m-1)(n-1)=2.$$
Thus $\{m-1,n-1\}=\{1,2\}$, so $\{m,n\}=\{2,3\}$, with $\gcd(2,3)=1$ ✓. This giv
es $(a,b)=(4,6)$.

No other values of $d$ are possible, so these are all solutions.

**Verification.**
- $\{3,6\}$: product $=18$, sum $=9$, difference $=9$; $\gcd=3$, $\operatorname{
lcm}=6$, sum $=9$. ✓
- $\{4,6\}$: product $=24$, sum $=10$, difference $=14$; $\gcd=2$, $\operatornam
e{lcm}=12$, sum $=14$. ✓

**Answer.** Up to order, the pairs are
$$\boxed{\{3,6\}\ \text{and}\ \{4,6\}}.$$
