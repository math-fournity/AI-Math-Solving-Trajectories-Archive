## Proof

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains no solution, answer, or pr
oof sketch. Proceeding to solve.

**Key identity (telescoping).** For any matrices, we have the expansion

$$\prod_{i=1}^{m} A_i \;-\; \prod_{i=1}^{m} B_i \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{m}\Biggl(\prod
_{j=1}^{i-1} B_j\Biggr)(A_i - B_i)\Biggl(\prod_{j=i+1}^{m} A_j\Biggr),$$

where empty products are identity matrices of the appropriate size.

*Verification by induction.* For $m=1$ this is $A_1 - B_1$, which is trivially c
orrect. Assuming it holds for $m-1$, write

$$\prod_{i=1}^{m} A_i - \prod_{i=1}^{m} B_i = \Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{m-1} A_i\Bigr)
A_m - \Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{m-1} B_i\Bigr) B_m.$$

Adding and subtracting $\bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{m-1} A_i\bigr) B_m$:

$$= \Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{m-1} A_i\Bigr)(A_m - B_m) + \Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{m-1} A_i -
 \prod_{i=1}^{m-1} B_i\Bigr) B_m.$$

The first term is the $i=m$ summand. The second term, by the induction hypothesi
s, expands into the summands for $i=1,\dots,m-1$ (with $B_m$ appended on the rig
ht, which matches $\prod_{j>i} A_j$ being replaced by $B_m$ for those terms — mo
re precisely, applying the induction hypothesis to the $(m-1)$-fold products and
 then right-multiplying by $B_m$ gives exactly the remaining summands with the c
onvention that $A_m$-factors for $j>i$ when $i<m$ include $B_m$... but the clean
est statement uses $A_j$ for $j>i$ throughout). The identity follows. $\square$

**Norm facts.** We use two standard inequalities:

1. **Max-entry vs. Frobenius:** $\|M\|_\infty \le \|M\|_F$, since the largest en
try in absolute value is at most the $\ell_2$-norm of all entries.

2. **Frobenius submultiplicativity:** $\|XY\|_F \le \|X\|_F\,\|Y\|_2 \le \|X\|_F
\,\|Y\|_F$, where the first inequality is standard ($\|XY\|_F \le \|X\|_F\|Y\|_2
$) and the second uses $\|Y\|_2 \le \|Y\|_F$.

**Applying the bound.** By the triangle inequality for $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ and th
e telescoping identity:

$$\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{m} A_i - \prod_{i=1}^{m} B_i\right\|_\infty \;\le\; \sum_{
i=1}^{m} \left\|\Biggl(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1} B_j\Biggr)(A_i - B_i)\Biggl(\prod_{j=i+
1}^{m} A_j\Biggr)\right\|_\infty.$$

For each summand, apply Fact 1 then Fact 2 repeatedly:

$$\left\|\Biggl(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1} B_j\Biggr)(A_i - B_i)\Biggl(\prod_{j=i+1}^{m}
A_j\Biggr)\right\|_\infty \;\le\; \left\|\prod_{j=1}^{i-1} B_j\right\|_F \cdot \
|A_i - B_i\|_F \cdot \left\|\prod_{j=i+1}^{m} A_j\right\|_F.$$

Using Fact 2 iteratively on each product:

$$\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{i-1} B_j\right\|_F \le \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} \|B_j\|_F, \qquad
 \left\|\prod_{j=i+1}^{m} A_j\right\|_F \le \prod_{j=i+1}^{m} \|A_j\|_F.$$

Combining with the hypothesis $\|A_i - B_i\|_F \le \varepsilon$:

$$\boxed{\left\|A_1 A_2 \cdots A_m - B_1 B_2 \cdots B_m\right\|_\infty \;\le\; \
varepsilon \sum_{i=1}^{m}\Biggl(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1} \|B_j\|_F\Biggr)\Biggl(\prod_{
j=i+1}^{m} \|A_j\|_F\Biggr).}$$

**Special case.** If there exists a uniform bound $M > 0$ with $\|A_i\|_F \le M$
 and $\|B_i\|_F \le M$ for all $i$, then each summand is at most $\varepsilon \c
dot M^{i-1} \cdot M^{m-i} = \varepsilon\, M^{m-1}$, giving the cleaner form

$$\|A_1 \cdots A_m - B_1 \cdots B_m\|_\infty \;\le\; m\,\varepsilon\, M^{m-1}.$$

**Remark on tightness.** The telescoping decomposition is the standard perturbat
ion analysis for products, and each step uses tight inequalities ($\|M\|_\infty
\le \|M\|_F$ is tight for rank-1/single-entry matrices; $\|XY\|_F \le \|X\|_F\|Y
\|_F$ is tight up to the spectral-vs-Frobenius gap). Without additional assumpti
ons on the matrix norms, no bound depending only on $\varepsilon$ (and not on th
e $\|A_i\|_F, \|B_i\|_F$) is possible, since for $m \ge 2$ the products can ampl
ify the per-factor perturbation $\varepsilon$ by an arbitrarily large factor.
