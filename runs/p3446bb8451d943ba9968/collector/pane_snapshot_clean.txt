## Proof

**Setup and notation.** Let $m(t)$ be the renewal function and $m^*(\theta) = \i
nt_0^\infty e^{-\theta t}\,dm(t)$ its Laplace–Stieltjes transform (LST). We are
given

$$m^*(\theta) = \frac{1}{\theta\mu} + \frac{\sigma^2 - \mu^2}{2\mu^2} + o(1), \q
quad \theta \to 0^+, \tag{1}$$

and must determine the asymptotic form of $m(t)$ as $t \to \infty$.

---

**Step 1: Relate the LST to the ordinary Laplace transform.**

Since $m(t)$ is non-decreasing with $m(0)=0$ (assuming $F(0)=0$) and $m(t)=O(t)$
, integration by parts gives

$$m^*(\theta) = \bigl[e^{-\theta t}m(t)\bigr]_0^\infty + \theta\int_0^\infty e^{
-\theta t}\,m(t)\,dt = \theta\,\widetilde{m}(\theta), \tag{2}$$

where $\widetilde{m}(\theta)=\int_0^\infty e^{-\theta t}\,m(t)\,dt$ is the ordin
ary Laplace transform of $m(t)$.

---

**Step 2: Identify the inverse of each term.**

Recall the standard Laplace transform pairs (for the ordinary transform $\wideti
lde{f}(\theta)=\int_0^\infty e^{-\theta t}f(t)\,dt$):

$$\mathcal{L}\{t\} = \frac{1}{\theta^2}, \qquad \mathcal{L}\{1\} = \frac{1}{\the
ta}.$$

Via (2), the LST of $f(t)=\frac{t}{\mu}$ is $\theta\cdot\frac{1}{\mu\theta^2}=\f
rac{1}{\mu\theta}$, and the LST of the constant $c$ is $\theta\cdot\frac{c}{\the
ta}=c$.

So the correspondence is:

| Term in $m^*(\theta)$ | Inverse in $m(t)$ |
|---|---|
| $\dfrac{1}{\mu\theta}$ | $\dfrac{t}{\mu}$ |
| constant $B$ | constant $B$ |
| $o(1)$ | $o(1)$ |

---

**Step 3: Justify term-by-term inversion (Tauberian argument).**

*Leading term.* Since $m(t)$ is non-decreasing and $m^*(\theta)\sim\frac{1}{\mu\
theta}$, the **Hardy–Littlewood Tauberian theorem** for Laplace–Stieltjes transf
orms yields

$$m(t) \sim \frac{t}{\mu}, \qquad t\to\infty. \tag{3}$$

*Second-order term.* Define $g(t) = m(t) - \frac{t}{\mu}$. From (1) and (2):

$$\theta\int_0^\infty e^{-\theta t}\,g(t)\,dt = m^*(\theta) - \frac{1}{\mu\theta
} = \frac{\sigma^2-\mu^2}{2\mu^2} + o(1). \tag{4}$$

In renewal theory, under the standard assumptions that the interarrival distribu
tion is **non-lattice** with **finite second moment** ($\sigma^2<\infty$), the *
*key renewal theorem** (Smith's theorem) guarantees that $g(t)=m(t)-\frac{t}{\mu
}$ **converges** to a finite limit as $t\to\infty$. In particular $g$ is bounded
.

Given that $g(t)$ converges, the **Abelian theorem** for Laplace transforms of c
onvergent functions states:

$$\text{If } g(t)\to L \text{ as } t\to\infty, \text{ then } \theta\int_0^\infty
 e^{-\theta t}g(t)\,dt \to L \text{ as } \theta\to 0^+.$$

Conversely, since $g$ is bounded and the Cesàro-type average $\theta\int_0^\inft
y e^{-\theta t}g(t)\,dt$ converges (to the right-hand side of (4)), and $g$ is k
nown to converge (from the renewal theorem), the limit must match:

$$\lim_{t\to\infty} g(t) = \frac{\sigma^2 - \mu^2}{2\mu^2}. \tag{5}$$

---

**Step 4: Combine.**

From (3) and (5):

$$\boxed{m(t) = \frac{t}{\mu} + \frac{\sigma^2 - \mu^2}{2\mu^2} + o(1), \qquad t
\to\infty.}$$

---

**Verification via special cases.**

1. **Poisson process** (exponential interarrivals): $\mu = 1/\lambda$, $\sigma^2
 = 1/\lambda^2 = \mu^2$, so the constant is $\frac{\mu^2-\mu^2}{2\mu^2}=0$, givi
ng $m(t)=\frac{t}{\mu}=\lambda t$ exactly. ✓

2. **Deterministic interarrivals** of period $\mu$: $\sigma^2=0$, constant $=\fr
ac{-\mu^2}{2\mu^2}=-\frac{1}{2}$. Indeed $m(t)=\lfloor t/\mu\rfloor = \frac{t}{\
mu}-\frac{1}{2}+o(1)$, since the fractional part $\{t/\mu\}$ oscillates with mea
n $\frac{1}{2}$. ✓

Both special cases confirm the result.
