**Answer: Yes.**

## Construction

For any prime $p$ and any element $a \in \operatorname{GF}(p)$, define the polyn
omial

$$f_a(x) \;=\; 1 - (x-a)^{p-1}.$$

This is a polynomial (hence an algebraic function) of degree $p-1$ over $\operat
orname{GF}(p)$.

## Proof of correctness

We verify the two required properties directly.

**Case 1: $x = a$.**

$$f_a(a) \;=\; 1 - (a - a)^{p-1} \;=\; 1 - 0^{p-1} \;=\; 1 - 0 \;=\; 1. \quad\ch
eckmark$$

**Case 2: $x \neq a$, i.e. $x \in \operatorname{GF}(p)\setminus\{a\}$.**

Then $x - a \neq 0$, so $x - a \in \operatorname{GF}(p)^{\times}$, the multiplic
ative group of order $p-1$. By **Fermat's little theorem** (equivalently, Lagran
ge's theorem applied to $\operatorname{GF}(p)^{\times}$),

$$(x - a)^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod{p}.$$

Therefore

$$f_a(x) \;=\; 1 - (x-a)^{p-1} \;=\; 1 - 1 \;=\; 0. \quad\checkmark$$

Combining both cases, $f_a$ is the indicator (Kronecker delta) function of the s
ingleton $\{a\}$:

$$f_a(x) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } x = a, \\ 0 & \text{if } x \in \operator
name{GF}(p)\setminus\{a\}. \end{cases}$$

## The role of $p = kn+1$ with $n$ a power of $2$

The construction above is valid for **every** prime $p$; the hypothesis $p = kn+
1$ with $n = 2^m$ is **not required** for existence. However, this hypothesis is
 far from irrelevant — it characterizes the **FFT-friendly (NTT-friendly) primes
**. When $n \mid (p-1)$, the group $\operatorname{GF}(p)^{\times}$ (cyclic of or
der $p-1$) contains a **primitive $n$-th root of unity** $\omega$. This is what
enables the Number-Theoretic Transform over $\operatorname{GF}(p)$, making such
fields the standard choice in computational number theory, error-correcting code
s, and SNARK/cryptography constructions.

In this setting the same delta function can be expressed using the $n$-th-power
structure. Since $p - 1 = nk$, we may factor the exponent:

$$(x-a)^{p-1} = \bigl((x-a)^{n}\bigr)^{k}.$$

The inner power $(x-a)^n$ is computable by $m = \log_2 n$ repeated squarings (ex
ploiting $n = 2^m$), and the map $y \mapsto y^n$ sends $\operatorname{GF}(p)^{\t
imes}$ onto the subgroup of $n$-th-power residues (a subgroup of index $n$, henc
e order $k$). Raising to the $k$-th power then collapses this entire subgroup ba
ck to $1$ (by Lagrange, since the subgroup has order $k$), reproducing $(x-a)^{p
-1} = 1$ for $x \neq a$. Thus the power-of-$2$ structure provides a **computatio
nally efficient** decomposition of the delta function, even though it does not c
hange the algebraic identity itself.

## Concrete example

For the specific instance requested, with target value $a = 23$:

$$\boxed{f_{23}(x) \;=\; 1 - (x - 23)^{p-1}}$$

satisfies $f_{23}(23) = 1$ and $f_{23}(x) = 0$ for every $x \in \operatorname{GF
}(p)$ with $x \neq 23$.

## Uniqueness remark

By Lagrange interpolation, a polynomial of degree $\leq p-1$ is uniquely determi
ned by its values on all $p$ points of $\operatorname{GF}(p)$. Since $f_a$ has d
egree $p-1$ and is specified on all $p$ field elements, it is the **unique** pol
ynomial of degree $\leq p-1$ representing this indicator function. (Any other po
lynomial representing the same function differs by a multiple of $x^p - x$, the
vanishing polynomial of $\operatorname{GF}(p)$.)
