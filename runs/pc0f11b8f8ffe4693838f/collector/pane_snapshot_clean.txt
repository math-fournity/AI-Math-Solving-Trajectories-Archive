**Solution.**

Since the characteristic polynomial of $A$ has no repeated roots, $A$ is diagona
lizable over $\mathbb{C}$ with $n$ distinct eigenvalues. Let

$$A = P\,D\,P^{-1}, \qquad D = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n), \
quad \lambda_i \text{ distinct}.$$

**Step 1: Describe the centralizer of $A$.** A matrix $B$ commutes with $A$ iff
$P^{-1}BP$ commutes with $D$. Writing $C = P^{-1}BP$, the condition $CD = DC$ fo
rces $C$ to be diagonal: indeed, $(CD)_{ij} = c_{ij}\lambda_j$ and $(DC)_{ij} =
\lambda_i c_{ij}$, so $(\lambda_i - \lambda_j)c_{ij} = 0$, giving $c_{ij}=0$ for
 $i\neq j$ since the $\lambda_i$ are distinct. Hence

$$\{B : BA = AB\} = \{P\,\operatorname{diag}(\mu_1,\dots,\mu_n)\,P^{-1} : \mu_i
\in \mathbb{C}\}.$$

**Step 2: Impose "similar to $A$."** A matrix is similar to $A$ iff it is diagon
alizable with exactly the eigenvalues $\{\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n\}$ (each with
 multiplicity one, since they are distinct). A diagonal matrix $\operatorname{di
ag}(\mu_1,\dots,\mu_n)$ is already diagonal, hence diagonalizable, and its eigen
values are precisely $\{\mu_1,\dots,\mu_n\}$. Thus it is similar to $A$ iff

$$\{\mu_1,\dots,\mu_n\} = \{\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n\} \quad\text{(as multisets
)}.$$

Since the $\lambda_i$ are all distinct, this is equivalent to $(\mu_1,\dots,\mu_
n)$ being a permutation of $(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n)$.

**Step 3: Count.** Each of the $n!$ permutations of $(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n)
$ yields a diagonal matrix, and because the $\lambda_i$ are distinct, different
permutations give different diagonal matrices. Every such matrix commutes with $
D$ (hence with $A$) and is similar to $A$. Conversely, every matrix that both co
mmutes with and is similar to $A$ arises this way.

Therefore the number of $n\times n$ complex matrices that are both similar to an
d commute with $A$ is

$$\boxed{n!}.$$
