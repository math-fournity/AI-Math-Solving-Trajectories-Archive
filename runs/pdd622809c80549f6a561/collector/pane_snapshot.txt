**Proof.**

**Step 1: Analyze triangle ABD.**

Since AB = AD = 1, triangle ABD is isosceles with vertex angle ∠BAD = 100°. Thus
$$\angle ABD = \angle ADB = \frac{180° - 100°}{2} = 40°.$$
By the law of cosines:
$$BD^2 = 1 + 1 - 2\cos 100° = 2 - 2\cos 100° = 4\sin^2 50°,$$
so $BD = 2\sin 50°$.

**Step 2: Determine the circle on which C lies.**

The point C satisfies $\angle BCD = 130°$, which is an inscribed angle subtendin
g chord BD. By the inscribed angle theorem, the arc $BD$ **containing** C has me
asure $2 \times 130° = 260°$, so the arc $BD$ **not containing** C has measure $
360° - 260° = 100°$.

If $R$ is the radius of the circle through $B$, $C$, $D$, then the chord length
satisfies:
$$BD = 2R\sin\!\left(\frac{100°}{2}\right) = 2R\sin 50°.$$
Substituting $BD = 2\sin 50°$:
$$2\sin 50° = 2R\sin 50° \implies R = 1.$$

So **C lies on a circle of radius 1 passing through B and D.**

**Step 3: Identify the circle.**

There are exactly two circles of radius 1 passing through B and D. Since $AB = A
D = 1$, point **A is equidistant from B and D at distance 1**, so **A is the cen
ter of one such circle** (with B, D on it).

The other circle of radius 1 through B and D is centered at the point $O'$ which
 is the reflection of A across line $BD$.

**Step 4: Select the correct circle by convexity.**

For quadrilateral ABCD to be convex, the diagonals AC and BD must intersect, whi
ch requires C to lie on the **opposite side** of line BD from A.

- **Circle centered at A:** The arc BD with measure 100° (the arc containing C)
lies on the far side of BD from the center A. So C is on the opposite side of BD

- **Circle centered at $O'$ (reflection of A across BD):** Its arc of measure 10
0° containing C lies on the **same side** of BD as A, making ABCD non-convex. ✗

Therefore C lies on the circle of radius 1 **centered at A**.

**Step 5: Compute AC.**

Since C lies on the circle of radius 1 centered at A:
$$AC = R = 1 \text{ centimeter}.$$
