## Proof

We work with the space $\mathcal{S}$ of (equivalence classes of indistinguishabl
e) real-valued processes $X:\Omega\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}$ such that
$$\|X\| := \mathbb{E}\!\left[\sup_{0\le s\le T}|X_s|^2\right]^{1/2} < \infty,$$
where we assume processes are jointly measurable (so that $\sup_s|X_s|$ is a wel
l-defined random variable). We claim $\mathcal{S}$ is a complete normed space (h
ence a complete metric space under $d(X,Y)=\|X-Y\|$).

**Step 1. $\|\cdot\|$ is a norm.** Clearly it is absolutely homogeneous and sati
sfies the triangle inequality by Minkowski's inequality in $L^2(\Omega)$ applied
 to the random variables $\sup_s|X_s|$ and $\sup_s|Y_s|$:
$$\|X+Y\|^2 = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\sup_s|X_s+Y_s|^2\right] \le \mathbb{E}\!\left[\
big(\sup_s|X_s|+\sup_s|Y_s|\big)^2\right] \le \big(\|X\|+\|Y\|\big)^2.$$
If $\|X\|=0$, then $\mathbb{E}[\sup_s|X_s|^2]=0$, so $\sup_s|X_s|=0$ a.s., i.e.
$X$ is indistinguishable from $0$. Passing to equivalence classes, $\|\cdot\|$ i
s a norm.

**Step 2. Completeness.** Let $(X^n)_{n\ge1}$ be a Cauchy sequence in $\mathcal{
S}$. Extract a subsequence $(n_k)$ with $\|X^{n_{k+1}}-X^{n_k}\| \le 2^{-k}$ for
 all $k$. Set $Y_k := X^{n_{k+1}}-X^{n_k}$, so $\sum_k \|Y_k\| < \infty$.

For each $N$, by Minkowski in $L^2(\Omega)$,
$$\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sup_{s}|Y_{k,s}|\right\|_{L^2(\Omega)} \le \sum_{k=1}^{N
}\|Y_k\| \le \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k}=:M<\infty.$$
Hence $(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sup_s|Y_{k,s}|)_{N}$ is bounded in $L^2(\Omega)$ and incr
easing, so by monotone convergence it converges a.s. and in $L^2$ to $S:=\sum_{k
=1}^{\infty}\sup_s|Y_{k,s}|\in L^2(\Omega)$. In particular $S<\infty$ a.s.

For a.e. $\omega$ with $S(\omega)<\infty$, the series $\sum_k |Y_{k,s}(\omega)|$
 converges uniformly in $s\in[0,T]$. Therefore the telescoping sum
$$X^{n_k}_s(\omega) = X^{n_1}_s(\omega) + \sum_{j=1}^{k-1}Y_{j,s}(\omega)$$
converges uniformly in $s$ to a limit, which we denote $X_s(\omega)$. On the nul
l set where $S=\infty$, set $X_s\equiv 0$. The process $X$ is jointly measurable
 (as an a.s. uniform limit of jointly measurable processes), and
$$\sup_s|X_s| \le \sup_s|X^{n_1}_s| + S < \infty \quad\text{a.s.}$$
Moreover $\sup_s|X^{n_k}_s - X_s| \le \sum_{j\ge k}\sup_s|Y_{j,s}|$, so
$$\|X^{n_k}-X\|^2 = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\sup_s|X^{n_k}_s-X_s|^2\right] \le \mathbb
{E}\!\left[\Big(\sum_{j\ge k}\sup_s|Y_{j,s}|\Big)^2\right] \xrightarrow[k\to\inf
ty]{} 0,$$
since the tail of an $L^2$-convergent series vanishes. Thus $\|X^{n_k}-X\|\to 0$
.

Finally, $\|X\|\le \|X-X^{n_k}\|+\|X^{n_k}\|<\infty$, so $X\in\mathcal{S}$; and
by the Cauchy property,
$$\|X^n-X\|\le \|X^n-X^{n_k}\|+\|X^{n_k}-X\|\to 0,$$
so the entire sequence converges to $X$ in norm.

**Conclusion.** Every Cauchy sequence in $\mathcal{S}$ converges to a limit in $
\mathcal{S}$, so $\mathcal{S}$ is complete. Equivalently, $\mathcal{S}$ is a Ban
ach space (and a complete metric space under the induced metric). $\blacksquare$
