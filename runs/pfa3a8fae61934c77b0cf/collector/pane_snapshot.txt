Looking at this problem, I need to compute $\lim_{\omega\to\infty} u(t,0)$ for t
he given Fourier series solution to a heat equation.

## Step 1: Identify the PDE

The eigenfunctions $\phi_n(x) = \cos\!\big((n-\tfrac12)\pi x\big)$ satisfy $\phi
_n'(0)=0$, $\phi_n(1)=0$, and $-\phi_n'' = \lambda_n \phi_n$ with $\lambda_n = (
n-\tfrac12)^2\pi^2$. Setting $c_n = \frac{4(-1)^n}{(2n-1)\pi}$, I verify that $u
(t,x)$ solves:

$$u_t = u_{xx},\quad u_x(t,0)=0,\quad u(t,1)=\sin\omega t,\quad u(0,x)=0.$$

**Check:** At $x=1$: $\cos((n-\tfrac12)\pi)=0$, so $u(t,1)=\sin\omega t$. ✓ At $
x=0$: $\sin 0=0$, so $u_x(t,0)=0$. ✓ At $t=0$: the bracket gives $\frac{0+\lambd
a_n(1-1)}{\cdots}=0$, so $u(0,x)=0$. ✓

Writing $u = \sin\omega t + \sum a_n(t)\phi_n(x)$, the PDE yields $a_n' + \lambd
a_n a_n = c_n\,\omega\cos\omega t$ with $a_n(0)=0$ (since $1 = -\sum c_n\phi_n$
is the eigenfunction expansion of the constant $1$). Solving via the integrating
 factor reproduces the given formula. ✓

## Step 2: Decompose into steady-periodic + transient

Write $u = u_p + v$, where $u_p$ is the **steady periodic** solution and $v$ is
the **transient**.

Since $\alpha^2 = i\omega$ with $\alpha = (1+i)\sqrt{\omega/2}$, the steady peri
odic solution is:

$$u_p(t,x) = \operatorname{Im}\!\left[\frac{\cosh(\alpha x)}{\cosh\alpha}\,e^{i\
omega t}\right],$$

which satisfies $u_{p,t}=u_{p,xx}$, $u_{p,x}(t,0)=0$, $u_p(t,1)=\sin\omega t$.

The transient $v = u - u_p$ satisfies $v_t = v_{xx}$, $v_x(t,0)=0$, $v(t,1)=0$,
$v(0,x) = -u_p(0,x)$.

## Step 3: Bound $u_p(t,0)$

$$u_p(t,0) = \operatorname{Im}\!\left[\frac{e^{i\omega t}}{\cosh\alpha}\right] \
implies |u_p(t,0)| \le \frac{1}{|\cosh\alpha|}.$$

Using $|\cosh(a+ib)|^2 = \cosh^2 a - \sin^2 b \ge \sinh^2 a$ with $a = \sqrt{\om
ega/2}$:

$$|u_p(t,0)| \le \frac{1}{\sinh(\sqrt{\omega/2})} \le 2\,e^{-\sqrt{\omega/2}} \;
\xrightarrow{\omega\to\infty}\; 0.$$

## Step 4: Bound $v(t,0)$

Expand $v(t,x) = \sum_{n} b_n\, e^{-\lambda_n t}\,\phi_n(x)$, where $b_n = -2\in
t_0^1 u_p(0,\xi)\,\phi_n(\xi)\,d\xi$.

For $\xi \in [0,1]$, using $|\cosh(\alpha\xi)| \le \cosh(\operatorname{Re}\alpha
\cdot\xi) = \cosh(\sqrt{\omega/2}\,\xi)$:

$$|u_p(0,\xi)| \le \frac{\cosh(\sqrt{\omega/2}\,\xi)}{\sinh(\sqrt{\omega/2})} \l
e 4\,e^{-\sqrt{\omega/2}(1-\xi)}$$

for $\omega$ sufficiently large. Therefore:

$$|b_n| \le 2\int_0^1 |u_p(0,\xi)|\,d\xi \le 8\int_0^1 e^{-\sqrt{\omega/2}(1-\xi
)}\,d\xi = \frac{8(1-e^{-\sqrt{\omega/2}})}{\sqrt{\omega/2}} \le \frac{8\sqrt{2}
}{\sqrt{\omega}}.$$

Since $\phi_n(0) = 1$:

$$|v(t,0)| \le \sum_n |b_n|\,e^{-\lambda_n t} \le \frac{8\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\omega}
}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} e^{-(n-1/2)^2\pi^2 t}.$$

For any fixed $t > 0$, the series $S(t) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty e^{-(n-1/2)^2\pi^2 t
}$ converges, so:

$$|v(t,0)| \le \frac{8\sqrt{2}\,S(t)}{\sqrt{\omega}} \;\xrightarrow{\omega\to\in
fty}\; 0.$$

## Step 5: Conclusion

$$|u(t,0)| \le |u_p(t,0)| + |v(t,0)| \le 2\,e^{-\sqrt{\omega/2}} + \frac{8\sqrt{
2}\,S(t)}{\sqrt{\omega}} \;\xrightarrow{\omega\to\infty}\; 0.$$

For $t = 0$: $u(0,0) = 0$ for all $\omega$ (from the initial condition), so the
limit is also $0$.

**Physical interpretation:** The boundary forcing $\sin\omega t$ at $x=1$ oscill
ates infinitely rapidly. The heat equation damps high-frequency signals exponent
ially over distance (by $e^{-\sqrt{\omega/2}\cdot 1}$), so at $x=0$ (distance $1
$ from the driven boundary) the response vanishes.

$$\boxed{\lim_{\omega\to\infty} u(t,0) = 0}$$
