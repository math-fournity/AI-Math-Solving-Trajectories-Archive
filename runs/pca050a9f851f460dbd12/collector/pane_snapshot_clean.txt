## Proof

We wish to determine
$$\sup \big\{\log E[\xi] - E[\log \xi] : \xi \text{ is a random variable with va
lues in } [1,2]\big\}.$$

Since $\log$ is concave, Jensen's inequality gives $\log E[\xi] \geq E[\log \xi]
$, so the quantity is non-negative, and equals $0$ when $\xi$ is deterministic.

**Step 1: Reduce to a fixed mean.** Let $\mu = E[\xi] \in [1,2]$. For $\xi \in [
1,2]$, the concavity of $\log$ means $\log$ lies above the chord joining $(1, \l
og 1) = (1,0)$ and $(2, \log 2)$:
$$\log x \geq (x-1)\log 2, \qquad x \in [1,2].$$
Taking expectations,
$$E[\log \xi] \geq (E[\xi]-1)\log 2 = (\mu - 1)\log 2,$$
with equality iff $\xi \in \{1, 2\}$ a.s. Therefore, for fixed mean $\mu$,
$$\log E[\xi] - E[\log \xi] \leq \log\mu - (\mu-1)\log 2,$$
and the bound is attained by the two-point distribution $P(\xi=1)=2-\mu,\; P(\xi
=2)=\mu-1$.

**Step 2: Maximize over $\mu \in [1,2]$.** Define
$$f(\mu) = \log\mu - (\mu-1)\log 2.$$
Then $f'(\mu) = \frac{1}{\mu} - \log 2$, which vanishes at $\mu^* = \frac{1}{\lo
g 2} \approx 1.4427 \in [1,2]$. Since $f''(\mu) = -1/\mu^2 < 0$, this is the glo
bal maximum on $[1,2]$ (the endpoints give $f(1)=f(2)=0$, while $f(\mu^*)>0$).

**Step 3: Compute the value.**
$$f(\mu^*) = \log\!\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\right) - \left(\frac{1}{\log 2}-1\righ
t)\log 2 = -\log(\log 2) - 1 + \log 2.$$

The supremum is attained by the distribution $P(\xi = 1) = 2 - \frac{1}{\log 2},
\; P(\xi = 2) = \frac{1}{\log 2} - 1$ (both probabilities lie in $[0,1]$ since $
1 < 1/\log 2 < 2$).

$$\boxed{\sup_{1 \leq \xi \leq 2}\big[\log E\,\xi - E\log\xi\big] = \log 2 - 1 -
 \log(\log 2).}$$
