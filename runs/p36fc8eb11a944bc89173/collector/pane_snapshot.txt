Looking at the problem, I need to determine whether the convergence set $P$ of a
 power series always equals the convergence set $P'$ of its term-by-term derivat
ive.

**Answer: No, it is not always true that $P = P'$.**

---

## Proof

We interpret $P' = \{z \in \mathbb{C} \mid \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} n\,a_n\,z^{n-1} \
text{ converges}\}$, the term-by-term derivative of the power series. (The $n=0$
 term vanishes, so the sum effectively starts at $n=1$.)

### Part 1: $P' \subseteq P$ always holds

Suppose $z_0 \in P'$, i.e., $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n\,a_n\,z_0^{n-1}$ converges. S
et $b_n = n\,a_n\,z_0^{n-1}$. By hypothesis $\sum b_n$ converges, so the partial
 sums $B_N = \sum_{n=1}^{N} b_n$ are bounded: $|B_N| \le M$ for some $M$.

For $n \ge 1$, we have $a_n\,z_0^n = \frac{z_0}{n}\,b_n$, so it suffices to show
 $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{b_n}{n}$ converges. By **Abel's summation by parts**
:

$$\sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{b_n}{n} = \frac{B_N}{N} + \sum_{n=1}^{N-1} B_n\!\left(\fr
ac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{n+1}\right).$$

- As $N \to \infty$: $\left|\frac{B_N}{N}\right| \le \frac{M}{N} \to 0$.
- The series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n\!\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{n+1}\right)$ c
onverges **absolutely**, since $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|B_n|\!\left(\frac{1}{n}-\fra
c{1}{n+1}\right) \le M\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\!\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{n+1}\right
) = M < \infty.$

Therefore $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{b_n}{n}$ converges, and

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n\,z_0^n = a_0 + z_0 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{b_n}{n}$$

converges. Hence $z_0 \in P$, proving $P' \subseteq P$.

### Part 2: $P \not\subseteq P'$ in general (counterexample)

Consider $a_0 = 0$ and $a_n = \frac{1}{n}$ for $n \ge 1$. The power series is

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n},$$

with radius of convergence $R = 1$ (by the ratio test: $\left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n
}\right| = \frac{n}{n+1} \to 1$).

**At $z_0 = -1$ (on the boundary $|z|=1$):**

- **Original series:** $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n}$. The terms $\frac{
1}{n} \to 0$ monotonically, so by the **Leibniz alternating series test**, this
converges. Thus $-1 \in P$.

- **Derived series:** $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n \cdot \frac{1}{n} \cdot z^{n-1} = \
sum_{n=1}^{\infty} z^{n-1} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} z^n$. At $z_0 = -1$: $\sum_{n=0
}^{\infty} (-1)^n = 1 - 1 + 1 - 1 + \cdots$. The terms $(-1)^n$ do **not** tend
to $0$, so the series **diverges** (nth-term test). Thus $-1 \notin P'$.

### Conclusion

We have $-1 \in P$ but $-1 \notin P'$, so $P \ne P'$ for this power series. Ther
efore it is **not** always true that $P = P'$.

**Remark.** Inside the open disk of convergence $|z| < R$, both series converge
absolutely; outside $|z| > R$, both diverge. The only possible discrepancy is on
 the boundary $|z| = R$. We have shown $P' \subseteq P$ always, but the reverse
inclusion can fail on the boundary, as the counterexample $\sum \frac{z^n}{n}$ a
t $z = -1$ demonstrates.
