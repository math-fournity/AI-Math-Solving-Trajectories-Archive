## Proofs

### Theorem 1: `cyclic_of_perfect_is_leinster`

**Statement.** If `G` is cyclic of order `n` where `n` is perfect, then `G` is L
einster.

**Proof.** Let `G` be cyclic of order `n` with `n` perfect (i.e., `σ(n) = 2n`, w
here `σ` is the sum-of-divisors function).

Since `G` is cyclic, it is abelian, so every subgroup is normal. The subgroups o
f a cyclic group of order `n` are in bijection with the divisors of `n`: for eac
h `d | n`, there is exactly one subgroup of order `d`. Therefore:

$$\sum_{H \trianglelefteq G} |H| = \sum_{d \mid n} d = \sigma(n) = 2n = 2|G|.$$

Hence `G` is Leinster. $\square$

### Theorem 2: `abelian_is_leinster_iff_cyclic_perfect`

**Statement.** A finite abelian group `G` is Leinster if and only if `G` is cycl
ic and `|G|` is a perfect number. (Leinster, Theorem 2.1.)

**Proof.** Define the *abundancy* $\hat{g}(G) = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{H \le G} |H|$
. Since `G` is abelian, all subgroups are normal, so `G` is Leinster iff $\hat{g
}(G) = 2$.

**Key Lemma (Coprime Decomposition).** If $G = A \times B$ with $\gcd(|A|,|B|)=1
$, then every subgroup of $G$ is of the form $H_A \times H_B$ where $H_A \le A$,
 $H_B \le B$.

*Proof of Lemma.* If $(a,b) \in N \le G$, pick integers $u,v$ with $u|A|+v|B|=1$
. Then $(a,b)^{v|B|} = (a^{v|B|}, b^{v|B|}) = (a^{1-u|A|}, 1) = (a,1) \in N$, an
d similarly $(1,b) \in N$. So $N = (N \cap A) \times (N \cap B)$. $\square$

This gives $\hat{g}(A \times B) = \hat{g}(A) \cdot \hat{g}(B)$ for coprime-order
 abelian groups.

**Key Lemma (Non-cyclic p-groups have abundancy > 2).** If $P$ is a non-cyclic f
inite abelian $p$-group of order $p^a$ ($a \ge 2$), then $\hat{g}(P) > 2$.

*Proof.* Since $P$ is non-cyclic, $P/\Phi(P) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p)^k$ for some $k
 \ge 2$, where $\Phi(P)$ is the Frattini subgroup. The number of subgroups of or
der $p$ in $P$ equals the number of order-$p$ subgroups of $P[p] \cong (\mathbb{
Z}/p)^k$, which is $\frac{p^k - 1}{p - 1} \ge p + 1$. Dually, the number of inde
x-$p$ subgroups (order $p^{a-1}$) is also $\ge p + 1$.

These subgroups — the trivial subgroup, $\ge p+1$ subgroups of order $p$, $\ge p
+1$ subgroups of order $p^{a-1}$, and $P$ itself — are all distinct. Therefore:

$$\sigma(P) \ge 1 + (p+1)\,p + (p+1)\,p^{a-1} + p^a.$$

Dividing by $p^a$:

$$\hat{g}(P) \ge \frac{1}{p^a} + \frac{p+1}{p^{a-1}} + \frac{p+1}{p} + 1 > 1 + \
frac{p+1}{p} + 1 = 2 + \frac{1}{p} > 2. \quad \square$$

**Proof of the theorem.**

($\Leftarrow$) If `G` is cyclic and `|G|` is perfect, this is Theorem 1.

($\Rightarrow$) Suppose `G` is a finite abelian Leinster group. Decompose $G = \
prod_{i=1}^r P_i$ into Sylow $p_i$-subgroups (coprime orders). By the Coprime De
composition Lemma:

$$\hat{g}(G) = \prod_{i=1}^r \hat{g}(P_i) = 2.$$

Each non-trivial $P_i$ satisfies $\hat{g}(P_i) \ge 1 + 1/|P_i| > 1$. If any $P_i
$ is non-cyclic, then $\hat{g}(P_i) > 2$ by the lemma, and since all other non-t
rivial factors have $\hat{g} > 1$, the product exceeds 2 — contradiction. Theref
ore every $P_i$ is cyclic.

A direct product of cyclic groups of pairwise coprime orders is cyclic, so $G$ i
s cyclic. Then $\hat{g}(G) = \sigma(|G|)/|G| = 2$, which means $|G|$ is perfect.
 $\square$

### Theorem 3: `exists_nonabelian_leinster_group`

**Statement.** There exists a non-abelian Leinster group.

**Proof.** We show $G = S_3 \times C_5$ (order 30) is Leinster and non-abelian.

**Normal subgroups of $S_3$** (order 6): $\{e\}$, $A_3$, $S_3$, with orders $1,
3, 6$. Sum $= 10$.

**Subgroups of $C_5$** (order 5, abelian so all normal): $\{0\}$, $C_5$, with or
ders $1, 5$. Sum $= 6$.

Since $\gcd(6, 5) = 1$, by the Coprime Decomposition Lemma (which applies to nor
mal subgroups as well: if $N \trianglelefteq A \times B$ with coprime orders, th
en $N = (N \cap A) \times (N \cap B)$ with each factor normal), every normal sub
group of $G$ is a product of a normal subgroup of $S_3$ and a normal subgroup of
 $C_5$.

$$\sum_{N \trianglelefteq G} |N| = 10 \times 6 = 60 = 2 \times 30 = 2|G|.$$

So $G$ is Leinster. Since $S_3$ is non-abelian, $G = S_3 \times C_5$ is non-abel
ian. $\square$

### Theorem 4: `dihedral_is_leinster_iff_odd_perfect`

**Statement.** The dihedral group $D_n$ (order $2n$) is Leinster if and only if
$n$ is an odd perfect number.

**Proof.** Let $D_n = \langle r, s \mid r^n = s^2 = 1,\; srs = r^{-1}\rangle$, $
|D_n| = 2n$.

**Case 1: $n$ odd.**

*Normal subgroups.* The rotation subgroup $\langle r \rangle$ (order $n$) is nor
mal (index 2). Every subgroup $\langle r^d \rangle$ for $d \mid n$ is characteri
stic in $\langle r \rangle$ (cyclic groups have unique subgroups of each order),
 hence normal in $D_n$. This gives subgroups of orders $n/d$ for each $d \mid n$
.

If $N \trianglelefteq D_n$ contains a reflection, then since all $n$ reflections
 are conjugate when $n$ is odd (they form a single conjugacy class), $N$ contain
s all reflections. The product of two reflections $sr^i \cdot sr^j = r^{j-i}$; s
ince $n$ is odd, the differences $\{j - i\}$ generate all of $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathb
b{Z}$, so $N$ contains all rotations, hence $N = D_n$.

Thus the normal subgroups for $n$ odd are exactly $\{\langle r^d \rangle : d \mi
d n\} \cup \{D_n\}$.

$$\sum_{N \trianglelefteq D_n} |N| = \sum_{d \mid n} \frac{n}{d} + 2n = \sigma(n
) + 2n.$$

$D_n$ is Leinster iff $\sigma(n) + 2n = 4n$ iff $\sigma(n) = 2n$ iff $n$ is perf
ect. Since $n$ is odd, this gives: $D_n$ is Leinster iff $n$ is an odd perfect n
umber. $\checkmark$

**Case 2: $n$ even ($n \ge 2$).**

When $n$ is even, reflections split into two conjugacy classes: $\{s, sr^2, sr^4
, \ldots\}$ and $\{sr, sr^3, sr^5, \ldots\}$. This yields additional normal subg
roups:

- $\langle r^2, s \rangle = \{r^{2k}\} \cup \{sr^{2k}\}$, order $n$, is normal.
(Conjugation: $r \cdot s \cdot r^{-1} = r^2 s \in \langle r^2, s \rangle$; $r \c
dot r^2 \cdot r^{-1} = r^2$; $s$-conjugation is trivial on generators.)
- $\langle r^2, rs \rangle = \{r^{2k}\} \cup \{r^{2k+1}s\}$, order $n$, is norma
l (similar verification).

These are distinct ($s \in \langle r^2, s\rangle$ but $s \notin \langle r^2, rs\
rangle$ for $n \ge 2$), both contain reflections (so are not rotation subgroups)
, and both have order $n < 2n$ (so are not $D_n$). Together with the rotation su
bgroups (summing to $\sigma(n)$) and $D_n$ itself (order $2n$):

$$\sum_{N \trianglelefteq D_n} |N| \ge \sigma(n) + 2n + n + n = \sigma(n) + 4n >
 4n = 2|D_n|,$$

since $\sigma(n) \ge 1 + n > 0$. So $D_n$ is **not** Leinster for $n$ even. $\ch
eckmark$

**Conclusion.** Combining both cases: $D_n$ is Leinster $\iff$ $n$ is odd and $n
$ is perfect $\iff$ $n$ is an odd perfect number. $\square$
