# Hyperbolic Isometry Mapping a Point to the Origin in the Poincaré Disk

## Setup

The Poincaré disk model of hyperbolic geometry is the open unit disk
$$\mathbb{D} = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| < 1\}$$
equipped with the Riemannian metric
$$ds^2 = \frac{4\,|dz|^2}{(1-|z|^2)^2}.$$

The orientation-preserving isometry group of $(\mathbb{D}, ds^2)$ is $\mathrm{PS
U}(1,1)$, realized concretely as follows. A matrix
$$M = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ \bar b & \bar a \end{pmatrix}, \qquad |a|^2 - |b|
^2 = 1,$$
acts on $\mathbb{D}$ by the Möbius transformation
$$f_M(z) = \frac{az + b}{\bar b\, z + \bar a}.$$
The full isometry group is obtained by adjoining the anti-holomorphic reflection
 $z \mapsto \bar z$ (or equivalently composing with complex conjugation).

## Construction

Let $P = p \in \mathbb{D}$ be the given point ($|p|<1$). We seek an isometry $T_
p$ with $T_p(p) = 0$.

**Ansatz.** Use the $\mathrm{SU}(1,1)$ form $T_p(z) = \frac{az+b}{\bar b z + \ba
r a}$ with $|a|^2-|b|^2=1$.

**Condition $T_p(p)=0$.** The numerator must vanish at $z=p$:
$$ap + b = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad b = -ap.$$

**Normalization.** Substitute into the determinant condition:
$$|a|^2 - |b|^2 = |a|^2 - |a|^2|p|^2 = |a|^2(1-|p|^2) = 1,$$
so $|a| = (1-|p|^2)^{-1/2}$. Choosing the phase $a = (1-|p|^2)^{-1/2} > 0$ (real
, positive — a free choice since only $|a|$ is constrained and the overall phase
 cancels in $\mathrm{PSU}(1,1)$) gives
$$a = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|p|^2}}, \qquad b = -\frac{p}{\sqrt{1-|p|^2}}.$$

**Result.** The factors of $(1-|p|^2)^{-1/2}$ cancel between numerator and denom
inator, yielding the clean closed form

$$\boxed{\,T_p(z) \;=\; \frac{z - p}{1 - \bar p\, z}\,}$$

## Verification

1. **Maps $p$ to $0$:** $T_p(p) = \frac{p-p}{1-\bar p\, p} = 0$. ✓

2. **Preserves the disk (maps $\partial\mathbb{D}$ to $\partial\mathbb{D}$).** F
or $|z|=1$ we have $\bar z = 1/z$, hence
$$|1-\bar p\, z|^2 = (1-\bar p z)(1-p\bar z) = 1 - p\bar z - \bar p z + |p|^2\un
derbrace{|z|^2}_{=1},$$
$$|z-p|^2 = (z-p)(\bar z - \bar p) = \underbrace{|z|^2}_{=1} - z\bar p - p\bar z
 + |p|^2.$$
These are identical, so $|T_p(z)| = 1$ whenever $|z|=1$. By the maximum principl
e the open disk maps into itself, and applying the same argument to the inverse
shows it maps $\mathbb{D}$ *onto* $\mathbb{D}$. ✓

3. **Is an isometry.** $T_p$ is a Möbius transformation of the $\mathrm{SU}(1,1)
$ form derived above (with $a=(1-|p|^2)^{-1/2}$, $b=-p(1-|p|^2)^{-1/2}$, satisfy
ing $|a|^2-|b|^2=1$), hence lies in $\mathrm{PSU}(1,1)$, the orientation-preserv
ing isometry group of $(\mathbb{D}, ds^2)$. Concretely, one checks the pullback
of the metric:
$$1 - |T_p(z)|^2 = \frac{(1-|p|^2)(1-|z|^2)}{|1-\bar p z|^2}, \qquad \left|\frac
{dT_p}{dz}\right| = \frac{1-|p|^2}{|1-\bar p z|^2},$$
so
$$\frac{|T_p'(z)|\,|dz|}{1-|T_p(z)|^2} = \frac{(1-|p|^2)/|1-\bar p z|}{(1-|p|^2)
(1-|z|^2)/|1-\bar p z|^2} = \frac{|1-\bar p z|\,|dz|}{1-|z|^2},$$
and the hyperbolic arc-length element transforms as
$$\frac{2\,|dT_p|}{1-|T_p(z)|^2} = \frac{2\,|1-\bar p z|\,|dz|}{1-|z|^2} \cdot \
frac{1-|p|^2}{|1-\bar p z|^2}\cdot\frac{|1-\bar p z|}{1-|p|^2} = \frac{2\,|dz|}{
1-|z|^2}.$$
Thus $ds^2$ is preserved, confirming $T_p$ is a hyperbolic isometry. ✓

4. **Inverse.** $T_p^{-1}(w) = \frac{w+p}{1+\bar p\, w}$ sends $0\mapsto p$, as
expected (the inverse isometry carrying the origin back to $P$). ✓

## Description (geometric interpretation)

$T_p$ is the **hyperbolic translation along the geodesic through $p$ and the ori
gin** that displaces every point by the same hyperbolic distance $d_{\mathbb{H}}
(0,p) = 2\,\mathrm{artanh}|p|$ along the family of geodesics orthogonal to the g
eodesic diameter through $p$. Equivalently:

- It is the unique (up to post-composition with a rotation fixing $0$, i.e. up t
o a phase) orientation-preserving isometry sending $p$ to $0$.
- The full stabilizer of the condition "$p\mapsto 0$" is $T_p$ followed by any r
otation $R_\theta(z)=e^{i\theta}z$ about the origin; the canonical representativ
e is $T_p$ itself (no extra rotation).
- Including orientation-reversing isometries, one also has $z\mapsto T_p(\bar z)
 = \frac{\bar z - p}{1-\bar p\,\bar z}$, etc.

In real coordinates, writing $p = p_1 + i\,p_2$ and $z = x+iy$, the map is the r
estriction to $\mathbb{D}$ of the Möbius transformation
$$T_p(z)=\frac{z-p}{1-\bar p z},$$
a composition of two inversions in circles orthogonal to $\partial\mathbb{D}$ —
the geometric realization of a hyperbolic translation.
