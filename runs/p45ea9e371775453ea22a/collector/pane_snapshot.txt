**Claim:** $\displaystyle \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{g_n(1)}{n!}=0.$

**Proof.** Note first that $g_0(x)=(1+x)^{-3}$ is continuous on $[0,1]$, and
$$|g_0(x)|=\frac{1}{(1+x)^3}\le 1,\qquad x\in[0,1].$$

We prove by induction that for every $n\ge 0$,
$$|g_n(x)|\le \frac{x^n}{n!},\qquad x\in[0,1]. \tag{$\ast$}$$

*Base case* $n=0$: $|g_0(x)|=(1+x)^{-3}\le 1 = \frac{x^0}{0!}$. ✓

*Inductive step:* Assume $|g_{n-1}(y)|\le \frac{y^{\,n-1}}{(n-1)!}$ for $y\in[0,
1]$. Then for $x\in[0,1]$,
$$
|g_n(x)|=\left|\int_0^x g_{n-1}(y)\,dy\right|
\le \int_0^x |g_{n-1}(y)|\,dy
\le \int_0^x \frac{y^{\,n-1}}{(n-1)!}\,dy
= \frac{x^n}{n!}.
$$
This establishes $(\ast)$.

Setting $x=1$ gives
$$
|g_n(1)|\le \frac{1}{n!},
$$
and therefore
$$
\left|\frac{g_n(1)}{n!}\right|\le \frac{1}{(n!)^2}.
$$

Since $(n!)^2\to\infty$ (indeed $n!\ge 1$, and $(n!)^2\ge n! \to \infty$), we ha
ve $\frac{1}{(n!)^2}\to 0$. By the squeeze theorem,
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{g_n(1)}{n!}=0. \qquad\blacksquare
$$
