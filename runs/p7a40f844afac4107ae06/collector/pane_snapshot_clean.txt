$i$.

**Step 1: The centroid is at the origin.** The centroid of a regular tetrahedron
 coincides with the center of its circumscribed sphere. Since the sphere is cent

$$v_1 + v_2 + v_3 + v_4 = 0.$$

**Step 2: All pairwise dot products are equal.** In a regular tetrahedron, all e
dge lengths are equal: $|v_i - v_j|^2$ is the same for all $i \neq j$. Since $|v
_i - v_j|^2 = 2N - 2\,v_i \cdot v_j$, all pairwise dot products $v_i \cdot v_j$
($i \neq j$) share a common value $d$.

**Step 3: Determine $d$ and the divisibility condition.** Expanding $|v_1+v_2+v_
3+v_4|^2 = 0$:
$$0 = \sum_{i=1}^4 |v_i|^2 + 2\sum_{i<j} v_i \cdot v_j = 4N + 12d,$$
so $d = -N/3$. Since each $v_i \cdot v_j$ is an integer, $N$ must be divisible b
y $3$. Set $N = 3M$ where $M$ is a positive integer. Then $d = -M$, and the edge
 length squared is $2N - 2d = 6M + 2M = 8M$.

**Step 4: Reduce to a $3 \times 3$ integer matrix.** Set $v_4 = -(v_1+v_2+v_3)$.
 One verifies:
- $|v_4|^2 = |v_1+v_2+v_3|^2 = 3 \cdot 3M + 2 \cdot 3 \cdot (-M) = 9M - 6M = 3M$

and similarly for $v_4 \cdot v_2, v_4 \cdot v_3$. So it suffices to find three i

$i \neq j$.

Let $A$ be the $3\times 3$ matrix with rows $v_1, v_2, v_3$. Then
$$AA^T = M\begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 & -1 \\ -1 & 3 & -1 \\ -1 & -1 & 3 \end{pmatrix
} = M(4I - J),$$
where $J$ is the all-ones matrix. The matrix $4I - J$ has eigenvalues $4$ (multi
plicity 2) and $4-3=1$ (multiplicity 1), so
$$\det(4I - J) = 16.$$

$$\det(A)^2 = \det(AA^T) = M^3 \cdot 16.$$

square. As $16 = 4^2$ is already a perfect square, this requires $M^3$ to be a p

Therefore $N = 3M = 3k^2$.

$$(1,1,1),\quad (1,-1,-1),\quad (-1,1,-1),\quad (-1,-1,1)$$
