**Setup and parametrization.**

The standard torus $S$ is obtained by rotating the circle $(x-R)^2 + z^2 = r^2$
in the $(x,z)$-plane about the $z$-axis. A standard parametrization is
$$X(u,v) = \big((R+r\cos v)\cos u,\; (R+r\cos v)\sin u,\; r\sin v\big),$$
with $u\in[0,2\pi)$, $v\in[0,2\pi)$. The quantity $R+r\cos v$ is the distance fr
om the axis of revolution, and $v$ is the angle on the generating circle measure
d from the outward radial direction.

**Describing $S^+$ and $S^-$.**

The principal curvatures of a surface of revolution generated by a circle are: t
he curvature of the generating circle, $\kappa_1 = 1/r$ (with sign depending on
which way the normal points relative to the circle's center), and the curvature
of the parallel circle, $\kappa_2 = \cos v/(R+r\cos v)$. Concretely the Gaussian
 curvature is
$$K = \frac{\cos v}{r(R+r\cos v)}.$$
Since $R>r>0$, the denominator $r(R+r\cos v)$ is always positive, so the sign of
 $K$ is the sign of $\cos v$:

- **$S^+$ (where $K>0$):** $\cos v > 0$, i.e. $v\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$. This is the
**outer half** of the torus — the points whose distance from the axis exceeds $R
$ (the part of the generating circle on the side away from the axis). Geometrica
lly, on $S^+$ both principal curvatures have the same sign: the surface is **syn
clastic** (bowl-shaped), like the outside of a donut.

- **$S^-$ (where $K<0$):** $\cos v < 0$, i.e. $v\in(\pi/2,3\pi/2)$. This is the
**inner half** — the points whose distance from the axis is less than $R$ (the p
art of the generating circle on the side toward the axis). Here the principal cu
rvatures have opposite signs: the surface is **anticlastic** (saddle-shaped), li
ke the inside of a donut.

The two circles $v=\pm\pi/2$ (where $\cos v = 0$) are the parabolic curves separ
ating $S^+$ and $S^-$; on them $K=0$.

**The relation $\int_{S^+} K\,dA = -\int_{S^-} K\,dA$.**

This is immediate from the **Gauss–Bonnet theorem**. The torus has genus $1$, he
nce Euler characteristic $\chi(S)=0$. With no boundary,
$$\int_S K\,dA = 2\pi\,\chi(S) = 0.$$
Since $S = S^+ \cup S^- \cup \{\text{parabolic curves}\}$ and the parabolic curv
es have measure zero,
$$\int_{S^+} K\,dA + \int_{S^-} K\,dA = 0,$$
so $\int_{S^+} K\,dA = -\int_{S^-} K\,dA$. This establishes the equality of the
two integrals (up to sign) **without calculation**.

**Showing each equals $4\pi$ in absolute value, without calculation.**

We use the **Gauss map**. The unit normal to $S$ (with the orientation induced b
y $X_u\times X_v$) is
$$N(u,v) = (\cos v\cos u,\; \cos v\sin u,\; \sin v).$$
This is exactly the standard spherical-coordinate parametrization of the unit sp
here $S^2$, with longitude $u$ and latitude $v$. The fundamental fact is:

> The Gaussian curvature is the Jacobian of the Gauss map. Hence the signed area
 $\int_D K\,dA$ equals the signed area of the image $N(D)\subset S^2$, counted w
ith orientation/multiplicity.

Now consider the two halves:

- **On $S^+$:** $u\in[0,2\pi)$, $v\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$. As $(u,v)$ ranges over thi
s domain, $N(u,v)$ runs over the **entire unit sphere exactly once** (latitude f
rom the south pole $v=-\pi/2$ to the north pole $v=\pi/2$, full longitude). More
over $K>0$ here, so the orientation is positive. Therefore
$$\int_{S^+} K\,dA = \operatorname{Area}(S^2) = 4\pi.$$

- **On $S^-$:** $u\in[0,2\pi)$, $v\in(\pi/2,3\pi/2)$. As $(u,v)$ ranges over thi
s domain, $N(u,v)$ again runs over the **entire unit sphere exactly once** (lati
tude from the north pole back around to the south pole). But here $K<0$, so the
Gauss map reverses orientation; the image is covered with **negative** multiplic
ity. Therefore
$$\int_{S^-} K\,dA = -\operatorname{Area}(S^2) = -4\pi.$$

Combining,
$$\int_{S^+} K\,dA = -\int_{S^-} K\,dA = 4\pi.$$

**Why this is "without calculation."** The only ingredients are: (1) the Gauss–B
onnet theorem (a theorem, not a computation) giving the total integral $0$ and h
ence the relation between the two halves; (2) the geometric observation that the
 Gauss map of the torus is the standard parametrization of the sphere, so each h
alf of the torus maps onto the whole sphere once — positively on the outer (sync
lastic) half and negatively on the inner (anticlastic) half. The value $4\pi$ is
 then just the area of the unit sphere, read off geometrically. No explicit inte
gration of $K\,dA$ is performed.
