The term is **precompact** (also called **totally bounded** in the uniform-space
 setting, but in the more general context of Cauchy spaces / filter-merotopic sp
aces the two notions diverge, and "precompact" is the strictly stronger one).

**Reasoning.**

In a Cauchy space $(X, \mathcal{C})$ — a set equipped with a distinguished colle
ction $\mathcal{C}$ of *Cauchy filters* satisfying the usual axioms (every princ
ipal ultrafilter is Cauchy, Cauchy-ness is upward-closed under refinement, and t
he improper filter is excluded) — one defines:

- **Totally bounded:** every ultrafilter on $X$ is Cauchy.
- **Precompact:** every proper filter on $X$ can be refined by (i.e. admits a fi
ner) Cauchy filter.

These are *not* equivalent in this generality. The gap is exactly the ultrafilte
r-refinement step:

- If $X$ is precompact, then in particular every ultrafilter $\mathcal{U}$ (whic
h is a proper filter) can be refined by a Cauchy filter $\mathcal{F} \supseteq \
mathcal{U}$. But an ultrafilter is maximal among proper filters, so $\mathcal{F}
 = \mathcal{U}$; hence $\mathcal{U}$ is Cauchy. Thus **precompact $\Rightarrow$
totally bounded**.

- The converse requires that *every* proper filter, not just ultrafilters, be re
finable to a Cauchy filter. In a uniform space this converse holds: total bounde
dness (every ultrafilter Cauchy) plus the ultrafilter theorem lets one extend an
y proper filter to an ultrafilter, which is then Cauchy and refines the original
 filter. So for **uniform spaces** the two notions coincide, which is why many t
extbooks identify "precompact" with "totally bounded."

- In a general Cauchy space, however, the Cauchy-filter structure need not be co
mpatible with arbitrary ultrafilter extensions in the way uniform entourages gua
rantee. A filter $\mathcal{F}$ may have all its ultrafilter extensions Cauchy (s
o $X$ is totally bounded) yet $\mathcal{F}$ itself may fail to possess a Cauchy
refinement, because the Cauchy collection $\mathcal{C}$ is not required to be cl
osed under the operation of "coarsening back down" from ultrafilters to arbitrar
y subfilters. Hence **totally bounded $\nRightarrow$ precompact** in general.

Therefore the property "every proper filter can be refined by a Cauchy filter" i
s strictly stronger than total boundedness, and the standard name for it is **pr
ecompactness**.
