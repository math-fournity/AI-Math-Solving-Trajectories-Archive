Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum number of distinct individua

**Answer: 11**

## Proof

**Setup.** Each portrait $i$ features a triple $(D_i, B_i, S_i)$ where $D_i$ is
the center Director, $B_i$ is $D_i$'s biological brother (shares at least one bi
ological parent), and $S_i$ is $D_i$'s biological son ($D_i$ is a biological par
ent of $S_i$). The ten $D_i$ are all distinct. We minimize the total number of d
istinct individuals appearing in any portrait.

**Lower bound: 11 is necessary.**

Suppose for contradiction that 10 individuals suffice. Since we need 10 distinct
 directors, every individual must be a director. Then for each $D_i$, the son $S
_i$ must be one of the 10 directors (and $S_i \neq D_i$, since no one is their o
wn son). This defines a function $f:\{D_1,\dots,D_{10}\}\to\{D_1,\dots,D_{10}\}$
 with $f(D_i)=S_i$ and no fixed points, where $D_i$ is a biological parent of $f
(D_i)$.

Since the domain is finite and every element maps to another element, iterating
$f$ must eventually cycle: there exist $D_{i_1}, D_{i_2}, \dots, D_{i_k}$ with $
D_{i_1}$ parent of $D_{i_2}$, $D_{i_2}$ parent of $D_{i_3}$, …, $D_{i_k}$ parent
 of $D_{i_1}$. This makes $D_{i_1}$ a biological ancestor of itself, which is im
possible (it would require $D_{i_1}$ to exist before being born). Contradiction.
 So at least 11 individuals are needed.

**Upper bound: 11 is achievable.**

**Construction.** Introduce a person $P$ who does **not** appear in any portrait
. Let $P$ be a biological parent of all ten directors $D_1, \dots, D_{10}$. This
 makes every pair of directors biological brothers (they all share parent $P$).

For $i = 1, \dots, 9$: let $D_{i+1}$ have parents $D_i$ and $P$. Then:
- $D_i$ is a biological parent of $D_{i+1}$, so $D_{i+1}$ is $D_i$'s biological
son. ✓
- $D_i$ and $D_{i+1}$ share parent $P$, so they are biological brothers. ✓

Introduce one additional person $X$ (the 11th individual) who is a biological ch
ild of $D_{10}$, so $X$ is $D_{10}$'s biological son.

**Assigning portraits:**
- **Portrait $i$** ($i = 1, \dots, 9$): Center $= D_i$, Son (left) $= D_{i+1}$,
Brother (right) $= D_j$ for any $j \notin \{i, i+1\}$ (possible since there are
10 directors). We need $B_i \neq S_i$, i.e., the brother $D_j \neq D_{i+1}$, whi
ch is satisfied. ✓
- **Portrait 10**: Center $= D_{10}$, Son (left) $= X$, Brother (right) $= D_1$
(or any other director). Here $B_{10} = D_1 \neq X = S_{10}$. ✓

**Verification:**
- All 10 directors $D_1, \dots, D_{10}$ are distinct. ✓
- Each director has a biological brother: any two directors share parent $P$. ✓
- Each director has a biological son: $D_i$'s son is $D_{i+1}$ for $i \leq 9$, a
nd $D_{10}$'s son is $X$. ✓
- In each portrait, brother $\neq$ son $\neq$ director. ✓
- Total distinct individuals in portraits: $D_1, \dots, D_{10}, X = 11$. ✓

**Conclusion.** The lower bound of 11 and the matching construction establish th
at the minimum possible number of distinct individuals is $\boxed{11}$.
