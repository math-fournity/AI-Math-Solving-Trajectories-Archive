# Problem

In the digital city of Neo-Byzantium, a master cryptographer is designing a security protocol based on a specific "activation code," which is a fixed integer $V = 2023$.

To generate a secure transmission packet, the cryptographer must choose any positive integer $n$ and multiply it by the activation code to produce a signature value $S = 2023 \cdot n$. This signature $S$ is then converted into a string of high-voltage pulses (1s) and low-voltage gaps (0s) using its standard binary (base-2) representation.

The "Energy Cost" of a transmission, denoted by $k(n)$, is defined as the total number of high-voltage pulses (the count of digits equal to 1) present in the binary form of the signature $S$.

The cryptographer wants to optimize the protocol for maximum energy efficiency. What is the minimum possible Energy Cost $k(n)$ that can be achieved by selecting an optimal positive integer $n$?
