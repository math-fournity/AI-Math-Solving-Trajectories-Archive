## Solution

**Step 1: Evaluate the inner integral.**

For fixed $a \geq 2$ and $b \geq 1$:

$$\int_0^\infty \frac{x^b}{e^{ax}\, b!}\, dx = \frac{1}{b!}\int_0^\infty x^b e^{
-ax}\, dx.$$

Using the standard identity $\int_0^\infty x^b e^{-ax}\, dx = \dfrac{b!}{a^{b+1}
}$ (valid for $a>0$, $b\geq 0$ integer), we get

$$\frac{1}{b!}\cdot\frac{b!}{a^{b+1}} = \frac{1}{a^{b+1}}.$$

**Step 2: Sum over $b$.**

The expression becomes

$$\sum_{a=2}^\infty \sum_{b=1}^\infty \frac{1}{a^{b+1}}.$$

For fixed $a\geq 2$, this is a geometric series. Setting $k=b+1$ (so $k$ runs fr
om $2$ to $\infty$):

$$\sum_{b=1}^\infty \frac{1}{a^{b+1}} = \sum_{k=2}^\infty \frac{1}{a^k} = \frac{
1/a^2}{1-1/a} = \frac{1}{a^2}\cdot\frac{a}{a-1} = \frac{1}{a(a-1)}.$$

**Step 3: Sum over $a$ via telescoping.**

$$\sum_{a=2}^\infty \frac{1}{a(a-1)} = \sum_{a=2}^\infty \left(\frac{1}{a-1}-\fr
ac{1}{a}\right).$$

This telescopes:

$$\left(1-\frac12\right)+\left(\frac12-\frac13\right)+\left(\frac13-\frac14\righ
t)+\cdots = 1.$$

**Justification of interchange.** All terms are non-negative, so Tonelli's theor
em permits exchanging the order of summation/integration freely; the computation
 is rigorous.

**Answer:** $\boxed{1}$
