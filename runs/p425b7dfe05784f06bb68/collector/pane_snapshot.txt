## Solution

We analyze the series $\displaystyle S(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x}{n(1+n^{\de
lta}x^{2})}$ on $\mathbb{R}$, with $\delta\in(0,\infty)$.

### Step 1. Pointwise convergence (necessary)

At $x=0$ every term vanishes. For fixed $x\neq 0$,
$$
\frac{x}{n(1+n^{\delta}x^{2})}\sim \frac{x}{n\cdot n^{\delta}x^{2}}=\frac{1}{n^{
1+\delta}\,x}\qquad(n\to\infty),
$$
and $\sum n^{-(1+\delta)}$ converges since $1+\delta>1$. Hence the series conver
ges pointwise on $\mathbb{R}$ for every $\delta>0$.

### Step 2. Supremum of the general term

Set $f_n(x)=\dfrac{x}{n(1+n^{\delta}x^{2})}$. By symmetry $|f_n(x)|=\dfrac{|x|}{
n(1+n^{\delta}x^{2})}$, so it suffices to maximize $g(t)=\dfrac{t}{n(1+n^{\delta
}t^{2})}$ for $t\ge 0$. Then
$$
g'(t)=\frac{1-n^{\delta}t^{2}}{n(1+n^{\delta}t^{2})^{2}},
$$
which vanishes at $t=n^{-\delta/2}$. The maximum is
$$
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f_n(x)|=g\!\left(n^{-\delta/2}\right)=\frac{n^{-\delta/2}}
{n\bigl(1+n^{\delta}\cdot n^{-\delta}\bigr)}=\frac{n^{-\delta/2}}{2n}=\frac{1}{2
\,n^{1+\delta/2}}.
$$

### Step 3. Weierstrass $M$-test

For $\delta>0$ we have $1+\delta/2>1$, so
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f_n(x)|=\frac12\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac
{1}{n^{1+\delta/2}}<\infty.
$$
By the Weierstrass $M$-test, $S(x)$ converges **uniformly** on $\mathbb{R}$ for
every $\delta>0$.

### Step 4. The boundary $\delta=0$ is excluded

For $\delta=0$ (outside the admissible range, but confirming sharpness), $f_n(x)
=\dfrac{x}{n(1+x^{2})}$, and at $x=1$ the series becomes $\sum \dfrac{1}{2n}$, w
hich diverges; so uniform convergence fails already at the pointwise level. Thus
 the condition $\delta>0$ is exactly the right threshold.

### Conclusion

The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x}{n(1+n^{\delta}x^{2})}$ is u
niformly convergent on $\mathbb{R}$ for **every** $\delta\in(0,\infty)$, and for
 no $\delta\le 0$. Hence the required value is

$$
\boxed{\delta\in(0,\infty)}.
$$
