Looking at this problem, I need to determine the Lipschitz constant of the radia
l retraction $R(x) = x/\max(1, \|x\|)$ when the norm does not come from an inner
 product.

**Answer: The Lipschitz constant is 2.**

## Proof

**Definition.** The radial retraction $R: X \to B$ (where $B = \{x : \|x\| \leq
1\}$) is:
$$R(x) = \begin{cases} x & \text{if } \|x\| \leq 1 \\ \frac{x}{\|x\|} & \text{if
 } \|x\| > 1 \end{cases}$$

### Upper Bound: $R$ is 2-Lipschitz in any normed space

We check three cases for $x, y \in X$.

**Case 1: $\|x\| \leq 1$, $\|y\| \leq 1$.** Then $R(x)=x$, $R(y)=y$, so $\|R(x)-
R(y)\| = \|x-y\| \leq 2\|x-y\|$.

**Case 2: $\|x\| > 1$, $\|y\| > 1$.** We write:
$$x\|y\| - y\|x\| = (x-y)\|y\| + y(\|y\| - \|x\|)$$

By the triangle inequality and reverse triangle inequality ($|\|y\|-\|x\|| \leq
\|x-y\|$):
$$\|x\|y\| - y\|x\|\| \leq \|x-y\|\|y\| + \|y\|\cdot\|x-y\| = 2\|x-y\|\|y\|$$

Therefore:
$$\left\|\frac{x}{\|x\|} - \frac{y}{\|y\|}\right\| = \frac{\|x\|y\| - y\|x\|\|}{
\|x\|\|y\|} \leq \frac{2\|x-y\|}{\|x\|} \leq 2\|x-y\|$$

since $\|x\| > 1$.

**Case 3: $\|x\| \leq 1$, $\|y\| > 1$ (WLOG).** Then:
$$\|R(x) - R(y)\| = \left\|x - \frac{y}{\|y\|}\right\| \leq \|x-y\| + \left\|y -
 \frac{y}{\|y\|}\right\| = \|x-y\| + (\|y\|-1)$$

Since $\|x\| \leq 1$: $\|y\| - 1 \leq \|y\| - \|x\| \leq \|y - x\|$. Hence:
$$\|R(x) - R(y)\| \leq 2\|x-y\|$$

### Lower Bound: The constant 2 is achieved (tight)

Consider $X = \mathbb{R}^2$ with the $\ell^1$ norm $\|(a,b)\|_1 = |a|+|b|$, whic
h does **not** originate from an inner product (the parallelogram law fails: e.g
., $x=(1,0)$, $y=(0,1)$ gives $\|x+y\|^2+\|x-y\|^2 = 4+4 = 8 \neq 4 = 2\|x\|^2+2
\|y\|^2$).

Take $x = (1, 0)$ and $y = (1, \epsilon)$ for $\epsilon > 0$.

- $\|x\|_1 = 1$, $\|y\|_1 = 1 + \epsilon$
- $R(x) = (1, 0)$, $R(y) = \left(\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}, \frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilo
n}\right)$

$$\|R(x) - R(y)\|_1 = \frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon} + \frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}
= \frac{2\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}$$

$$\|x - y\|_1 = \epsilon$$

$$\frac{\|R(x) - R(y)\|_1}{\|x - y\|_1} = \frac{2}{1+\epsilon} \xrightarrow{\eps
ilon \to 0^+} 2$$

So the Lipschitz constant is at least 2.

### Conclusion

The Lipschitz constant of the radial retraction is exactly **2**.

**Remark.** When the norm originates from an inner product (Hilbert space), the
radial retraction is 1-Lipschitz. This follows because for $\|x\|, \|y\| \geq 1$
, setting $\alpha = \|x\|$, $\beta = \|y\|$, $\cos\theta = \langle x,y\rangle/(\
alpha\beta)$:

$$\left\|\frac{x}{\|x\|} - \frac{y}{\|y\|}\right\|^2 = 2 - 2\cos\theta \leq \alp
ha^2 + \beta^2 - 2\alpha\beta\cos\theta = \|x-y\|^2$$

where the inequality reduces to $(\alpha - \beta)^2 \geq 0$. For norms **not** o
riginating from an inner product, the Lipschitz constant is strictly greater tha
n 1, and the tight universal bound is **2**, achieved for instance by the $\ell^
1$ and $\ell^\infty$ norms.
