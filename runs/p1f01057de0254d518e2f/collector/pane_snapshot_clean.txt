## Solution

The answer is **m = 11**.

**Key transformation.** Let the label on edge $e$ be $w(e)\in\{1,\dots,m\}$. The
 condition on every triangle is: its three labels are $\{x,x,y\}$ with $y>x$ (tw
o equal and smaller, one strictly larger). Define $u(e)=m+1-w(e)$, also taking v
alues in $\{1,\dots,m\}$. Then a triangle with $w$-labels $\{x,x,y\}$, $y>x$, be
comes a triangle with $u$-labels $\{m+1-x,\; m+1-x,\; m+1-y\}$, where $m+1-y<m+1
-x$. So in $u$-space every triangle has **two equal largest values and one stric
tly smaller value**. This is exactly the **ultrametric** condition, with the add
itional requirement that no triangle is equilateral (the smallest is strictly sm
aller).

**Geometric remark.** "No 4 coplanar" with $\ge 4$ points implies no 3 are colli
near (any 3 collinear points plus a 4th are coplanar), so every triple forms a g
enuine triangle. Thus the problem is purely combinatorial on $K_{2006}$.

**Ultrametrics ↔ rooted trees.** A standard fact: a function $u$ on pairs satisf
ying "two largest equal in every triangle" is an ultrametric, and every ultramet
ric on a finite set corresponds to a rooted tree (dendrogram) whose leaves are t
he points, where $u(a,b)$ equals the height of the lowest common ancestor $\math
rm{LCA}(a,b)$. Conversely, any rooted tree with a height function on internal no
des (non-decreasing toward the root) gives an ultrametric this way.

**No equilateral triangle ↔ binary tree.** Three points $a,b,c$ form an equilate
ral triangle ($u(a,b)=u(a,c)=u(b,c)$) iff their three pairwise LCAs coincide, i.
e. iff they lie in three distinct child-subtrees of one internal node — equivale
ntly, that node has $\ge 3$ children. Hence:
- "No equilateral triangle" $\iff$ every internal node has at most $2$ children
$\iff$ the tree is **binary** (nodes with $1$ child are redundant and may be con
tracted).

**Strict monotonicity.** For the smallest value in each triangle to be *strictly
* smaller (not all equal), along every root-to-leaf path the heights must be **s
trictly** increasing. Thus the number of distinct $u$-values equals the depth of
 the tree (number of internal nodes on the longest root-to-leaf path), and the m
aximum value $m$ is at least this depth.

**Lower bound.** A binary tree with $n$ leaves has depth $\ge\lceil\log_2 n\rcei
l$. Since $u$ takes values in $\{1,\dots,m\}$, we have $m\ge\text{depth}\ge\lcei
l\log_2 n\rceil$. With $n=2006$ and $2^{10}=1024<2006\le 2048=2^{11}$, we get
$$m\ge\lceil\log_2 2006\rceil=11.$$

**Construction achieving $m=11$.** Take any rooted binary tree with $2006$ leave
s and depth exactly $11$ (e.g. a balanced binary tree: $2006=1024+982$, build a
complete binary tree of depth $10$ with $1024$ leaves, then split $982$ of those
 leaves one more level to obtain $1024-982+2\cdot982=2006$ leaves at depth $\le
11$). Assign to each internal node at depth $d$ (root at depth $11$, leaves at d
epth $0$) the height $h=d$, so heights are $1,2,\dots,11$, strictly increasing t
oward the root. Define
$$u(a,b)=\text{height of }\mathrm{LCA}(a,b)\in\{1,\dots,11\}.$$
This is an ultrametric (two largest equal in every triangle) with no equilateral
 triangle (binary tree), and $\max u=11$. Now set
$$w(e)=12-u(e)\in\{1,\dots,11\}.$$
For any triangle, the $u$-values are $\{a,a,b\}$ with $a>b$ (two largest equal,
smallest strictly smaller, since the tree is binary), so the $w$-values are $\{1
2-a,12-a,12-b\}$ with $12-b>12-a$: two equal and smaller, one strictly larger. T
his satisfies the requirement with $m=11$.

**Conclusion.** The minimum good number is
$$\boxed{m=11}.$$
