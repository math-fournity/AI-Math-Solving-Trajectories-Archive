## Proof

**Setup.** The Legendre function $Q_n(z)$ is defined by
$$Q_n(z) = \frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(1-t^2)^n}{2^n(z-t)^{n+1}}\,dt,\qquad z
\notin[-1,1].$$

We are given the discontinuity across the cut $[-1,1]$:
$$\operatorname{Disc} Q_n(x) \;:=\; Q_n(x+i\epsilon)-Q_n(x-i\epsilon) \;=\; -i\p
i\,P_n(x),\qquad x\in(-1,1).$$

**Step 1 — Conditions for an unsubtracted dispersion relation.**

An unsubtracted (once-subtracted-free) Cauchy dispersion relation
$$f(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\operatorname{Disc}f(t)}{t-z}\,dt$$
is valid provided:

(i) $f$ is analytic in $\mathbb{C}\setminus[-1,1]$ (single branch cut), and

(ii) $f(z)\to 0$ as $|z|\to\infty$ (so the large-circle contribution vanishes an
d no subtraction constant is needed).

For $Q_n(z)$: condition (i) holds by the integral definition (the only singulari
ty of the integrand in $z$ is the pole at $z=t\in[-1,1]$, giving a single branch
 cut). For condition (ii), as $|z|\to\infty$,
$$Q_n(z)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(1-t^2)^n}{2^n(z-t)^{n+1}}\,dt \;\sim\; \
frac{1}{2^{n+1}\,z^{n+1}}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-t^2)^n\,dt \;\longrightarrow\; 0$$
for every $n\ge 0$. Hence the unsubtracted dispersion relation applies to $Q_n$
with **no subtraction term**.

**Step 2 — Apply the dispersion relation.**

Deforming the Cauchy contour to wrap the cut $[-1,1]$ (outer circle $\to 0$ by S
tep 1(ii); small endpoint circles $\to 0$ since the discontinuity is integrable)
, the standard contour analysis gives, for $z\notin[-1,1]$,
$$Q_n(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\operatorname{Disc}Q_n(t)}{t-z}\,dt.
$$

Substituting the given discontinuity $\operatorname{Disc}Q_n(t)=-i\pi\,P_n(t)$:
$$Q_n(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{-i\pi\,P_n(t)}{t-z}\,dt=\frac{-i\pi}
{2\pi i}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{P_n(t)}{t-z}\,dt=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{P_n(t
)}{t-z}\,dt.$$

Therefore
$$\boxed{\,Q_n(z)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{P_n(t)}{z-t}\,dt,\qquad z\notin[
-1,1].\,}$$

**Step 3 — Consistency check (the discontinuity is reproduced).**

Using the Sokhotski–Plemelj identity $\dfrac{1}{x\pm i\epsilon-t}=\operatorname{
PV}\dfrac{1}{x-t}\mp i\pi\delta(x-t)$, for $x\in(-1,1)$:
$$Q_n(x\pm i\epsilon)=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{PV}\!\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{P_n(t)}{x
-t}\,dt \;\mp\; \frac{i\pi}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}P_n(t)\,\delta(x-t)\,dt.$$

Subtracting:
$$Q_n(x+i\epsilon)-Q_n(x-i\epsilon)=-i\pi\,P_n(x),$$
exactly the given discontinuity. The result is self-consistent.

**Conclusion.** The unsubtracted dispersion relation, combined with the disconti
nuity $\operatorname{Disc}Q_n=-i\pi P_n$, yields the Neumann integral representa
tion

$$Q_n(z)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{P_n(t)}{z-t}\,dt,\qquad z\notin[-1,1].$$
