## Proof

**Claim:** $M = R[x]/(x-t)$ is isomorphic to $R$ as an $R$-module.

**Setup.** We have $R = \mathbb{C}[t]$, so $R[x] = \mathbb{C}[t][x] \cong \mathb
b{C}[t,x]$, the polynomial ring in two variables. The module $M$ is the quotient
 of $R[x]$ by the ideal $(x-t)$, viewed as an $R$-module (where $R$ acts by mult
iplication in the natural way, fixing $x$).

**The map.** Define
$$\varphi: R[x] \longrightarrow R, \qquad \varphi\big(f(t,x)\big) = f(t,t),$$
i.e., the evaluation map substituting $x = t$.

**$\varphi$ is $R$-linear.** For $r(t) \in R$ and $f(t,x) \in R[x]$:
$$\varphi\big(r(t)\cdot f(t,x)\big) = \big(r\cdot f\big)(t,t) = r(t)\,f(t,t) = r
(t)\,\varphi\big(f(t,x)\big).$$
Additivity is immediate. Hence $\varphi$ is a homomorphism of $R$-modules.

**$\varphi$ is surjective.** Every $g(t) \in R$ is a constant polynomial in $x$,
 hence lies in $R[x]$, and $\varphi(g(t)) = g(t)$. So $\varphi$ is onto.

**$\ker(\varphi) = (x-t)$.** The containment $(x-t) \subseteq \ker\varphi$ is cl
ear: $\varphi(x - t) = t - t = 0$.

For the reverse containment, let $f(t,x) \in \ker\varphi$, so $f(t,t) = 0$. Sinc
e $R[x]$ is a polynomial ring in $x$ over $R$, we may perform polynomial long di
vision of $f$ by the monic (in $x$) polynomial $x - t$:
$$f(t,x) = (x - t)\,q(t,x) + r(t),$$
where $r(t) \in R$ is the remainder (degree $0$ in $x$, since $x - t$ is monic o
f degree $1$). Substituting $x = t$:
$$0 = f(t,t) = (t-t)\,q(t,t) + r(t) = r(t).$$
Thus $r(t) = 0$ and $f(t,x) = (x-t)\,q(t,x) \in (x-t)$, giving $\ker\varphi \sub
seteq (x-t)$.

**Conclusion.** By the First Isomorphism Theorem for modules,
$$M = R[x]/(x-t) = R[x]/\ker\varphi \;\cong\; \operatorname{im}\varphi = R$$
as $R$-modules. $\\blacksquare$
