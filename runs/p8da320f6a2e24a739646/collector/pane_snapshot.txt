## Solution

**Series representation of the denominator.**

Using the series for $\sinh(z)$:

$$\sinh(z) = z + \frac{z^3}{3!} + \frac{z^5}{5!} + \cdots$$

the denominator becomes

$$2z - \sinh(z) = 2z - z - \frac{z^3}{6} - \frac{z^5}{120} - \cdots = z\!\left(1
 - \frac{z^2}{6} - \frac{z^4}{120} - \cdots\right).$$

So $z = 0$ is a zero of $2z - \sinh(z)$. Factoring out $z$:

$$f(z) = \frac{1}{z\!\left(1 - \frac{z^2}{6} - \frac{z^4}{120} - \cdots\right)}
= \frac{1}{z}\cdot\frac{1}{1 - \frac{z^2}{6} - \frac{z^4}{120} - \cdots}.$$

Since the factor in parentheses equals $1$ at $z=0$ and is analytic and nonzero
there, $z = 0$ is a **simple pole** of $f$.

**Are there other poles inside $|z|=1$? — Rouché's theorem.**

On $|z| = 1$, compare $2z$ with $\sinh(z)$:

$$|2z| = 2, \qquad |\sinh(z)| \leq |z| + \frac{|z|^3}{3!} + \frac{|z|^5}{5!} + \
cdots = \sinh(1) \approx 1.1752 < 2.$$

Hence $|\sinh(z)| < |2z|$ on $|z|=1$. By **Rouché's theorem**, $2z$ and $2z - \s
inh(z)$ have the same number of zeros inside $|z|=1$. Since $2z$ has exactly one
 (simple) zero at $z=0$, the function $2z - \sinh(z)$ has **exactly one zero** i
nside $|z|=1$, namely $z=0$.

Therefore $f(z)$ has exactly **one pole inside $|z|=1$**: the simple pole at $z=
0$.

**Residue at $z=0$.**

$$\operatorname{Res}(f, 0) = \lim_{z\to 0} z\cdot f(z) = \lim_{z\to 0}\frac{z}{2
z - \sinh(z)} = \lim_{z\to 0}\frac{1}{2 - \frac{\sinh z}{z}} = \frac{1}{2 - 1} =
 1.$$

**Applying the residue theorem.**

$$\int_{|z|=1} f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}(f, 0) = 2\pi i \cdot 1
 = \boxed{2\pi i}.$$
