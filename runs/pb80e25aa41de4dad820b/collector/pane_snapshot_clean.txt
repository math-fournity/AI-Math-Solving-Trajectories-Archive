## Solution

We solve $x^{17} \equiv 20 \pmod{1001}$ using the Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT
) and modular exponentiation inversion — a core cryptologic technique (RSA-style
 decryption).

**Factor the modulus.** $1001 = 7 \cdot 11 \cdot 13$, with $7, 11, 13$ pairwise
coprime. By CRT, the congruence is equivalent to the system:
$$x^{17} \equiv 20 \pmod 7,\quad x^{17} \equiv 20 \pmod{11},\quad x^{17} \equiv
20 \pmod{13}.$$

**Key principle.** For a prime $p$ with $p \nmid x$, Fermat gives $x^{p-1} \equi
v 1 \pmod p$, so $x^{17}$ depends on $17 \bmod (p-1)$. If $\gcd(17, p-1)=1$, the
 map $x \mapsto x^{17}$ is a bijection on $(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times$ with i
nverse exponent $d \equiv 17^{-1} \pmod{p-1}$, giving $x \equiv 20^d \pmod p$. T
he case $x \equiv 0$ is checked separately (it gives $0 \not\equiv 20$).

**Mod 7.** $p-1=6$, $17 \equiv 5 \pmod 6$, $\gcd(5,6)=1$, $5^{-1} \equiv 5 \pmod
 6$ (since $5\cdot5=25\equiv1$). With $20 \equiv 6 \equiv -1 \pmod 7$:
$$x \equiv 20^5 \equiv (-1)^5 \equiv -1 \equiv 6 \pmod 7.$$
*Check:* $6^{17}\equiv(-1)^{17}\equiv -1\equiv 6\pmod 7$. ✓

**Mod 11.** $p-1=10$, $17 \equiv 7 \pmod{10}$, $\gcd(7,10)=1$, $7^{-1}\equiv 3 \
pmod{10}$ (since $7\cdot3=21\equiv1$). With $20\equiv 9 \pmod{11}$:
$$x \equiv 20^3 \equiv 9^3 \pmod{11}.\quad 9^2=81\equiv 4,\quad 9^3\equiv 9\cdot
4=36\equiv 3 \pmod{11}.$$
So $x \equiv 3 \pmod{11}$.
*Check:* order of $3$ mod $11$ is $5$; $17\bmod 5 = 2$; $3^2 = 9 \equiv 20 \pmod
{11}$. ✓

**Mod 13.** $p-1=12$, $17 \equiv 5 \pmod{12}$, $\gcd(5,12)=1$, $5^{-1}\equiv 5 \
pmod{12}$ (since $5\cdot5=25\equiv1$). With $20\equiv 7 \pmod{13}$:
$$x \equiv 20^5 \equiv 7^5 \pmod{13}.\quad 7^2=49\equiv 10,\quad 7^4\equiv 10^2=
100\equiv 9,\quad 7^5\equiv 9\cdot7=63\equiv 11 \pmod{13}.$$
So $x \equiv 11 \pmod{13}$.
*Check:* $11\equiv -2$; $(-2)^{17}=-2^{17}$; $2^{12}\equiv1$, so $2^{17}\equiv 2
^5=32\equiv 6$; $(-2)^{17}\equiv -6\equiv 7\equiv 20\pmod{13}$. ✓

**Combine via CRT.** Solve
$$x\equiv 6\pmod 7,\quad x\equiv 3\pmod{11},\quad x\equiv 11\pmod{13}.$$

From the first two: $x = 6 + 7a$, and $6+7a\equiv 3\pmod{11}\Rightarrow 7a\equiv
 -3\equiv 8\pmod{11}$. Since $7^{-1}\equiv 8\pmod{11}$, $a\equiv 8\cdot8=64\equi
v 9\pmod{11}$. So $a=9+11b$ and $x = 6+7(9+11b)=69+77b$.

Now impose the third: $69+77b\equiv 11\pmod{13}$. Since $69\equiv 4$ and $77\equ
iv -1\pmod{13}$: $4-b\equiv 11\Rightarrow -b\equiv 7\Rightarrow b\equiv -7\equiv
 6\pmod{13}$. Thus $b=6+13c$ and
$$x = 69 + 77\cdot 6 = 69 + 462 = 531.$$

**Final verification.** $531 \bmod 7 = 6$, $531 \bmod 11 = 3$, $531 \bmod 13 = 1
1$, all matching the system, and each residue was already checked to satisfy $x^
{17}\equiv 20$ modulo its prime. By CRT, $531^{17}\equiv 20\pmod{1001}$.

**Uniqueness.** Since $\gcd(17, p-1)=1$ for $p\in\{7,11,13\}$, the exponentiatio
n map is bijective on each $(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times$, and $x\equiv0$ is ne
ver a solution. Hence each prime contributes exactly one residue, giving a uniqu
e solution modulo $1001$.

$$\boxed{x = 531}$$
