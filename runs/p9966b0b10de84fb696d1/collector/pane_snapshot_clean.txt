## Proof

**Setup.** Let $m$ be Lebesgue measure on $[0,1]$, $f(x)=x^2$, and $\mu(E)=m(f(E
))$ for measurable $E\subseteq[0,1]$. We seek $\frac{d\mu}{dm}$.

**Step 1: $\mu \ll m$ (absolute continuity).**

The map $f(x)=x^2$ is $C^1$ on $[0,1]$ with $f'(x)=2x\le 2$, so $f$ is Lipschitz
 on $[0,1]$ with constant $2$. A Lipschitz map sends Lebesgue-null sets to Lebes
gue-null sets (it can be covered by intervals whose total measure scales by at m
ost the Lipschitz constant). Hence, whenever $m(E)=0$, we have $m(f(E))=0$, i.e.
 $\mu(E)=0$. Thus $\mu\ll m$, and the Radon–Nikodym derivative $\frac{d\mu}{dm}$
 exists (and is finite, since $\mu([0,1])=m([0,1])=1=m([0,1])$).

**Step 2: Apply the change-of-variables formula.**

The function $f:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ is strictly increasing, $C^1$, and hence absolute
ly continuous, with $f'(x)=2x\ge 0$. By the standard change-of-variables theorem
 for Lebesgue integrals (valid for absolutely continuous, strictly monotone $C^1
$ maps), for every Lebesgue-measurable $E\subseteq[0,1]$:

$$m(f(E))=\int_{f(E)}1\,dm(y)=\int_{E}1\cdot f'(x)\,dm(x)=\int_{E}2x\,dm(x).$$

Therefore
$$\mu(E)=\int_{E}2x\,dm(x)\quad\text{for all measurable }E\subseteq[0,1].$$

**Step 3: Identify the derivative.**

By definition of the Radon–Nikodym derivative, the unique (up to $m$-a.e. equali
ty) measurable function $g$ satisfying $\mu(E)=\int_E g\,dm$ for all measurable
$E$ is $g=\frac{d\mu}{dm}$. From Step 2 this function is

$$\frac{d\mu}{dm}(x)=2x,\qquad x\in[0,1].$$

**Step 4: Verification.**

- For $E=[a,b]\subseteq[0,1]$: $\mu(E)=m([a^2,b^2])=b^2-a^2$, and $\int_a^b 2x\,
dx=x^2\big|_a^b=b^2-a^2$. ✓
- For $E=[0,\tfrac12]$: $\mu(E)=m([0,\tfrac14])=\tfrac14$, and $\int_0^{1/2}2x\,
dx=\tfrac14$. ✓
- For $E=[\tfrac14,\tfrac12]$: $\mu(E)=m([\tfrac1{16},\tfrac14])=\tfrac{3}{16}$,
 and $\int_{1/4}^{1/2}2x\,dx=\tfrac14-\tfrac1{16}=\tfrac{3}{16}$. ✓
- Total mass: $\int_0^1 2x\,dx=1=\mu([0,1])=m([0,1])$. ✓

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\dfrac{d\mu}{dm}(x)=2x,\quad 0\le x\le 1.}$$
