## Proof

**Case $n = 0$.** Here $S^0 = \{+1, -1\}$ and $\mathbb{R}^0 = \{0\}$, so $f \equ
iv 0$ and the conclusion is immediate.

**Assume $n \geq 1$.** Suppose for contradiction that $f(x) \neq 0$ for every $x
 \in S^n$. Define
$$g : S^n \to S^{n-1}, \qquad g(x) = \frac{f(x)}{\|f(x)\|}.$$
Then $g$ is continuous and **odd**:
$$g(-x) = \frac{f(-x)}{\|f(-x)\|} = \frac{-f(x)}{\|f(x)\|} = -g(x).$$

Since $g(-x) = -g(x)$, the map $g$ respects the antipodal identification and des
cends to a continuous map on real projective spaces:
$$\bar{g} : \mathbb{RP}^n \to \mathbb{RP}^{n-1}, \qquad \bar{g}([x]) = [g(x)].$$

---

**Mod-2 cohomology of projective space.** For every $m \geq 0$,
$$H^*(\mathbb{RP}^m;\, \mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2[\alpha_m]\big/\big(\alph
a_m^{m+1}\big), \qquad \alpha_m \in H^1(\mathbb{RP}^m;\,\mathbb{Z}_2),$$
where $\alpha_m$ is the unique nonzero class in $H^1$, classifying the double co
ver $p_m : S^m \to \mathbb{RP}^m$. In particular $\alpha_m^m \neq 0$ (the top cl
ass) while $\alpha_m^{m+1} = 0$.

---

**Claim.** $\bar{g}^*(\alpha_{n-1}) = \alpha_n$.

*Proof of claim.* The pullback $\bar{g}^*(\alpha_{n-1})$ classifies the pullback
 double cover $E \to \mathbb{RP}^n$, where
$$E = \big\{ ([x],\, y) \in \mathbb{RP}^n \times S^{n-1} : \bar{g}([x]) = [y] \b
ig\}.$$
Define $\Phi : S^n \to E$ by $\Phi(x) = ([x],\, g(x))$. This is well-defined and
 continuous. Because $g$ is odd and $g(x) \in S^{n-1}$ (so $g(x) \neq -g(x)$), t
he two points $x$ and $-x$ map to **distinct** points:
$$\Phi(x) = ([x], g(x)), \qquad \Phi(-x) = ([x], -g(x)) \neq \Phi(x).$$
Given any $([x], y) \in E$, we have $[y] = \bar{g}([x]) = [g(x)]$, so $y = \pm g
(x)$; if $y = g(x)$ then $\Phi(x) = ([x],y)$, and if $y = -g(x) = g(-x)$ then $\
Phi(-x) = ([x],y)$. Thus $\Phi$ is a bijection. Since $S^n$ is compact and $E$ i
s Hausdorff, $\Phi$ is a **homeomorphism**.

Therefore the pullback double cover $E \to \mathbb{RP}^n$ is isomorphic to $p_n
: S^n \to \mathbb{RP}^n$, which is classified by $\alpha_n$. Hence $\bar{g}^*(\a
lpha_{n-1}) = \alpha_n$. $\square$

---

**Deriving the contradiction.** Consider the class $\alpha_{n-1}^{\,n} \in H^n(\
mathbb{RP}^{n-1};\,\mathbb{Z}_2)$. Since $n = (n-1)+1$, the defining relation gi
ves
$$\alpha_{n-1}^{\,n} = 0 \quad \text{in } H^n(\mathbb{RP}^{n-1};\,\mathbb{Z}_2).
$$

Applying $\bar{g}^*$ and using the claim:
$$\bar{g}^*\!\big(\alpha_{n-1}^{\,n}\big) = \big(\bar{g}^*\alpha_{n-1}\big)^n =
\alpha_n^{\,n}.$$

But $\alpha_n^{\,n} \in H^n(\mathbb{RP}^n;\,\mathbb{Z}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$ is
 the **top class**, hence $\alpha_n^{\,n} \neq 0$ (since $n < n+1$). Thus
$$0 = \bar{g}^*(0) = \bar{g}^*\!\big(\alpha_{n-1}^{\,n}\big) = \alpha_n^{\,n} \n
eq 0,$$
a contradiction.

---

The contradiction shows that our assumption $f(x) \neq 0$ for all $x \in S^n$ is
 false. Therefore there exists $y \in S^n$ with $f(y) = 0$. $\blacksquare$
