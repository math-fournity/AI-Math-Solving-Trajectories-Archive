## Setup

Let $K_1 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m})$ (quadratic, Galois, $\operatorname{Gal}(K_1/\ma
thbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$) and $K_2 = \mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3})$ (pure cu
bic, **not** Galois). The Galois closure of $K_2$ is
$$L_2 = \mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3}, \zeta_3), \qquad \operatorname{Gal}(L_2/\mathbb{Q})
\cong S_3,$$
of order $6$, acting on the three roots $\{2^{1/3},\, \zeta_3\cdot 2^{1/3},\, \z
eta_3^2\cdot 2^{1/3}\}$ of $x^3 - 2$.

## Splitting types via Frobenius

For a prime $p \nmid 6$ (unramified in $L_2$), the **cycle type** of $\operatorn
ame{Frob}_p \in S_3$ determines the splitting of $p$ in $K_2 = \mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3
})$:

| $\operatorname{Frob}_p$ in $S_3$ | Cycle type | Splitting in $K_2$ |
|---|---|---|
| identity | $1{+}1{+}1$ | splits completely |
| transposition | $1{+}2$ | splits as degree $1$ + degree $2$ |
| **3-cycle** | $3$ | **inert** (single prime, residue degree $3$) |

So **$p$ is inert in $K_2$** $\iff$ $\operatorname{Frob}_p$ is a 3-cycle. The 3-
cycles in $S_3$ are $(123)$ and $(132)$: **2 out of 6 elements**, giving density
 $2/6 = 1/3$.

Likewise, $p$ **splits completely in $K_1$** $\iff$ $\operatorname{Frob}_p = \ma
thrm{id}$ in $\operatorname{Gal}(K_1/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$,
density $1/2$.

## The quadratic subfield of $L_2$

The discriminant of $x^3 - 2$ is $\Delta = -27 \cdot 4 = -108$, so $\sqrt{\Delta
} = 6\sqrt{-3}$. The unique index-$2$ subgroup of $S_3$ is $A_3$, whose fixed fi
eld is
$$L_2^{A_3} = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}).$$
This is the **only** quadratic subfield of $L_2$.

## Case 1: $m \neq -3$

Since the only quadratic subfield of $L_2$ is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$ and $m \ne
q -3$, we have
$$K_1 \cap L_2 = \mathbb{Q}.$$
Both $K_1$ and $L_2$ are Galois over $\mathbb{Q}$ with trivial intersection, so
they are **linearly disjoint**. The compositum $L = K_1 \cdot L_2$ has
$$\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \operatorname{Gal}(K_1/\mathbb{Q}) \tim
es \operatorname{Gal}(L_2/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times S_3, \
qquad |G| = 12.$$

By **Chebotarev's density theorem**, the density of primes with a prescribed Fro
benius conjugacy class $C$ is $|C|/|G|$.

The joint condition is:
- $\operatorname{Frob}_p = \mathrm{id}$ in $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ (splits comp
letely in $K_1$),
- $\operatorname{Frob}_p$ is a 3-cycle in $S_3$ (inert in $K_2$).

The conjugacy class of $(\mathrm{id},\, \text{3-cycle})$ in $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb
{Z} \times S_3$ has size $1 \times 2 = 2$ (since $\{\mathrm{id}\}$ is a singleto
n class and the 3-cycles form a class of size 2). Therefore:

$$\boxed{\delta = \frac{2}{12} = \frac{1}{6}} \qquad (m \neq -3).$$

Equivalently, by linear disjointness the conditions are independent: $\delta = \
frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{6}$.

## Case 2: $m = -3$

Here $K_1 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}) = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3) \subset L_2$, so the com
positum is just $L_2$ itself, with $\operatorname{Gal}(L_2/\mathbb{Q}) = S_3$.

- **$p$ splits completely in $K_1 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$**: $\operatorname{Fro
b}_p$ fixes $K_1$, i.e., $\operatorname{Frob}_p \in \operatorname{Gal}(L_2/K_1)
= A_3 = \{\mathrm{id}, (123), (132)\}$.
- **$p$ is inert in $K_2$**: $\operatorname{Frob}_p$ is a 3-cycle $= \{(123), (1
32)\}$.

The intersection of these two conditions is $\{(123), (132)\}$, a conjugacy clas
s of size $2$ in $S_3$ (order $6$). By Chebotarev:

$$\boxed{\delta = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}} \qquad (m = -3).$$

**Direct verification:** "$p$ splits completely in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$" mean
s $p \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$ (density $1/2$). Among primes $p \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$, the
 cube map $x \mapsto x^3$ on $(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^\times$ has image of inde
x $3$, so $2$ is a cube with probability $1/3$ and **not** a cube with probabili
ty $2/3$. The latter is exactly "inert in $\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3})$." Joint density
$= \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{3} = \frac{1}{3}$. ✓

## Conclusion

The density of primes $p$ that split completely in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m})$ and re
main inert in $\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3})$ is:

$$\delta(m) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{1}{3} & \text{if } m = -3, \\[6pt] \dfrac{1}{
6} & \text{if } m \neq -3 \text{ (} m \neq 0 \text{, square-free).} \end{cases}$
$

The special case $m = -3$ arises because $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}) = \mathbb{Q}(\ze
ta_3)$ is the unique quadratic subfield of the splitting field $L_2 = \mathbb{Q}
(2^{1/3}, \zeta_3)$, making the two splitting conditions **dependent**. For all
other square-free $m$, the fields $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m})$ and $L_2$ are linearly
disjoint over $\mathbb{Q}$, so the conditions are **independent** and the densit
y is the product $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{6}$.
