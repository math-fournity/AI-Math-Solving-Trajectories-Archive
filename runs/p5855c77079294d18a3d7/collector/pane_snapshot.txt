Proof.

Let $\mathrm{Cl}^+(K)$ denote the narrow ideal class group of $K$. The condition
$[\mathfrak{a}] = [\mathfrak{p}^2]$ is equivalent to $[\mathfrak{p}]^2 =
[\mathfrak{a}]$ in $\mathrm{Cl}^+(K)$.

Since $\mathfrak{a}$ belongs to the principal genus, we have $\mathfrak{a} =
\lambda \mathfrak{b}^2$ with $N(\lambda) > 0$, so $[\mathfrak{a}] =
[\mathfrak{b}]^2$.

Now, $[\mathfrak{p}]^2 = [\mathfrak{a}] = [\mathfrak{b}]^2$ holds if and only if
$([\mathfrak{p}][\mathfrak{b}]^{-1})^2 = 1$, i.e., $[\mathfrak{p}] =
[\mathfrak{b}] \cdot t$ for some $t \in \mathrm{Cl}^+(K)[2]$, where $\mathrm{Cl}
^+(K)[2]$ is the (finite) 2-torsion subgroup of $\mathrm{Cl}^+(K)$.

Thus the set of narrow classes $[\mathfrak{p}]$ satisfying $[\mathfrak{p}]^2 =
[\mathfrak{a}]$ is the coset $$ [\mathfrak{b}] \cdot \mathrm{Cl}^+(K)[2] =
{[\mathfrak{b}] \cdot t : t \in \mathrm{Cl}^+(K)[2]}, $$ which is non-empty (it
contains $[\mathfrak{b}]$ itself, corresponding to $t = 1$).

It remains to show that each class in this coset is represented by infinitely
many prime ideals. Let $H^+$ be the narrow Hilbert class field of $K$, i.e., the
maximal abelian extension of $K$ unramified at all finite primes. By Artin
reciprocity, the Artin map gives an isomorphism $$ \mathrm{Cl}^+(K)
\xrightarrow{;\sim;} \mathrm{Gal}(H^+/K), $$ under which the class
$[\mathfrak{p}]$ of an unramified prime $\mathfrak{p}$ maps to its Frobenius
element $\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}} \in \mathrm{Gal}(H^+/K)$.

By the Chebotarev density theorem, every element $\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(H^+/
K)$ is the Frobenius of infinitely many prime ideals of $\mathcal{O}_K$
(unramified in $H^+/K$). Since $\mathrm{Gal}(H^+/K)$ is abelian, each conjugacy
class is a singleton, so each element $\sigma$ — and hence each narrow class $c
\in \mathrm{Cl}^+(K)$ — is represented by infinitely many prime ideals $
\mathfrak{p}$ with $[\mathfrak{p}] = c$.

Applying this to each class $c = [\mathfrak{b}] \cdot t$ in the coset
$[\mathfrak{b}] \cdot \mathrm{Cl}^+(K)[2]$, we obtain infinitely many prime
ideals $\mathfrak{p}$ with $[\mathfrak{p}] = c$, and for each such $
\mathfrak{p}$: $$ [\mathfrak{p}]^2 = ([\mathfrak{b}] \cdot t)^2 =
[\mathfrak{b}]^2 \cdot t^2 = [\mathfrak{b}]^2 = [\mathfrak{a}]. $$

Therefore, there are infinitely many prime ideals $\mathfrak{p} \subset
\mathcal{O}_K$ such that $[\mathfrak{a}] = [\mathfrak{p}^2]$. $\blacksquare$
