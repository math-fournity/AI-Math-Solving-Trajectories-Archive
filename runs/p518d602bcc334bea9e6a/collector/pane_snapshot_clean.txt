## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. A simple algebraic extension $\mathbb{F}(a)$ of degree $n$ over
$\mathbb{F}$ embeds into $M_n(\mathbb{F})$.

**Construction (left-regular representation).**

Since $a$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{F}$ with $\deg_{\mathbb{F}}(a)=n$, the set
$$\mathcal{B}=\{1,\,a,\,a^2,\,\dots,\,a^{n-1}\}$$
is an $\mathbb{F}$-basis of $\mathbb{F}(a)$, so $\mathbb{F}(a)$ is an $n$-dimens
ional $\mathbb{F}$-vector space.

For each $b\in\mathbb{F}(a)$, define the **left-multiplication map**
$$L_b:\mathbb{F}(a)\to\mathbb{F}(a),\qquad L_b(x)=bx.$$
This map is $\mathbb{F}$-linear (multiplication is $\mathbb{F}$-bilinear), so $L
_b\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{F}(a))$. Fixing the basis $\mathcal
{B}$ identifies $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{F}(a))\cong M_n(\mathbb
{F})$, yielding a map
$$\varphi:\mathbb{F}(a)\to M_n(\mathbb{F}),\qquad \varphi(b)=[L_b]_{\mathcal{B}}
.$$

**$\varphi$ is an $\mathbb{F}$-algebra homomorphism.** For $b,c\in\mathbb{F}(a)$
 and $\lambda\in\mathbb{F}$:
- $\varphi(b+c)=\varphi(b)+\varphi(c)$ since $L_{b+c}=L_b+L_c$;
- $\varphi(bc)=\varphi(b)\varphi(c)$ since $L_{bc}(x)=bcx=L_b(L_c(x))$, i.e. $L_
{bc}=L_b\circ L_c$;
- $\varphi(1)=I_n$ since $L_1=\mathrm{id}$;
- $\varphi(\lambda b)=\lambda\varphi(b)$ since $L_{\lambda b}=\lambda L_b$.

So $\varphi$ preserves addition, multiplication, scalar multiplication, and iden
tity.

**$\varphi$ is injective.** Its kernel is a two-sided ideal of the ring $\mathbb
{F}(a)$. But $\mathbb{F}(a)$ is a field, whose only ideals are $\{0\}$ and $\mat
hbb{F}(a)$ itself. Since $\varphi(1)=I_n\neq 0$, the kernel is not all of $\math
bb{F}(a)$, hence $\ker\varphi=\{0\}$.

Therefore $\varphi$ is an injective $\mathbb{F}$-algebra homomorphism, i.e. an *
*embedding** $\mathbb{F}(a)\hookrightarrow M_n(\mathbb{F})$.

**Explicit form.** Writing the minimal polynomial of $a$ as
$$m_a(t)=t^n-c_{n-1}t^{n-1}-\cdots-c_1 t-c_0,\qquad c_i\in\mathbb{F},$$
the relation $a^n=c_{n-1}a^{n-1}+\cdots+c_1 a+c_0$ shows that the matrix of $L_a
$ in the basis $\mathcal{B}$ is the **companion matrix**
$$\varphi(a)=\begin{pmatrix} 0&0&\cdots&0&c_0\\ 1&0&\cdots&0&c_1\\ 0&1&\cdots&0&
c_2\\ \vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\ 0&0&\cdots&1&c_{n-1} \end{pmatrix}\in
 M_n(\mathbb{F}),$$
and $\varphi$ sends $f(a)\mapsto f(\varphi(a))$ for every $f\in\mathbb{F}[t]$. T
he minimal polynomial of this matrix over $\mathbb{F}$ is exactly $m_a(t)$, conf
irming injectivity at the element level as well.

**Remark on hypotheses.** The argument uses only that $\mathbb{F}(a)$ is a finit
e-dimensional $\mathbb{F}$-algebra that is a field; separability of the extensio
n is **not** required. The same regular-representation construction embeds any d
egree-$n$ field extension (simple or not) into $M_n(\mathbb{F})$, and for a simp
le extension the image is precisely the $\mathbb{F}$-subalgebra generated by the
 companion matrix of $m_a$.

**Conclusion.** A simple algebraic extension $\mathbb{F}(a)$ of degree $n$ over
$\mathbb{F}$ **can** be embedded in $M_n(\mathbb{F})$, via the left-regular repr
esentation $b\mapsto L_b$, with $a$ mapping to the companion matrix of its minim
al polynomial.
