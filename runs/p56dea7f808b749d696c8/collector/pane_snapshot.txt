# Proof

**Claim:** No such group exists. If $H$ has finite index in $G$, then for every
$g \in G$, the intersection $H \cap g^{-1}Hg$ has finite index in $G$.

**Proof.**

Suppose $[G : H] = n < \infty$ and let $g \in G$.

**Step 1: $g^{-1}Hg$ has finite index in $G$.**

Conjugation by $g$ is an automorphism of $G$. The map $xH \mapsto (gxg^{-1})(gHg
^{-1})$ gives a bijection between left cosets of $H$ and left cosets of $gHg^{-1
}$, so $[G : gHg^{-1}] = [G : H] = n$. Equivalently, $[G : g^{-1}Hg] = n < \inft
y$.

**Step 2: The intersection of two finite-index subgroups has finite index.**

Let $K = g^{-1}Hg$, so $[G:K] = m = n < \infty$. Consider the action of $G$ on t
he product of coset spaces $G/H \times G/K$ by left multiplication:
$$a \cdot (xH,\, yK) = (axH,\, ayK).$$

The stabilizer of the point $(H, K)$ is
$$\{a \in G : aH = H \text{ and } aK = K\} = H \cap K.$$

By the orbit–stabilizer theorem, $[G : H \cap K]$ equals the size of the orbit o
f $(H, K)$. Since the orbit is a subset of $G/H \times G/K$, which has $nm$ elem
ents, we get
$$[G : H \cap K] \leq nm = n^2 < \infty.$$

**Conclusion.**

Applying Step 2 with $K = g^{-1}Hg$:
$$[G : H \cap g^{-1}Hg] \leq [G:H]\cdot[G:g^{-1}Hg] = n \cdot n = n^2 < \infty.$
$

Therefore $H \cap g^{-1}Hg$ always has **finite** index in $G$ whenever $H$ does
. No group $G$ with the described property exists. $\blacksquare$
