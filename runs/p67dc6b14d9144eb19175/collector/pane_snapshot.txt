## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. Every prime ideal of $\mathcal{O}_K$ lying above $p$ appears amo
ng the factors $\mathfrak{P}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{P}_n$ in the prime decompositio
n of $p\mathcal{O}_K$.

---

**Step 1: Characterize "lying above $p$" in terms of containment.**

Recall that a prime ideal $\mathfrak{P} \subset \mathcal{O}_K$ *lies above $p$*
(equivalently, above the prime ideal $p\mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{Z}$) if
$$\mathfrak{P} \cap \mathbb{Z} = p\mathbb{Z}.$$

- ($\Rightarrow$) If $\mathfrak{P} \cap \mathbb{Z} = p\mathbb{Z}$, then $p \in \
mathfrak{P}$, so $p\mathcal{O}_K \subseteq \mathfrak{P}$; i.e., $\mathfrak{P}$ *
*contains** the ideal $p\mathcal{O}_K$.

- ($\Leftarrow$) Conversely, suppose $\mathfrak{P}$ is a prime ideal of $\mathca
l{O}_K$ with $p\mathcal{O}_K \subseteq \mathfrak{P}$. Then $p \in \mathfrak{P}$,
 so $p\mathbb{Z} \subseteq \mathfrak{P} \cap \mathbb{Z}$. The contraction $\math
frak{P} \cap \mathbb{Z}$ is a prime ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$ (preimage of a prime u
nder the inclusion $\mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{O}_K$). The prime ideals
 of $\mathbb{Z}$ containing $p\mathbb{Z}$ are: $p\mathbb{Z}$ itself, and $\mathb
b{Z}$ (which is not proper). Since $\mathfrak{P} \neq \mathcal{O}_K$, we have $\
mathfrak{P} \cap \mathbb{Z} \neq \mathbb{Z}$, so $\mathfrak{P} \cap \mathbb{Z} =
 p\mathbb{Z}$.

Hence:
$$\{\mathfrak{P} \subset \mathcal{O}_K \text{ prime} : \mathfrak{P} \text{ lies
above } p\} \;=\; \{\mathfrak{P} \subset \mathcal{O}_K \text{ prime} : p\mathcal
{O}_K \subseteq \mathfrak{P}\}.$$

---

**Step 2: The prime ideals containing an ideal in a Dedekind domain are exactly
the factors in its prime factorization.**

The ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$ is a Dedekind domain. In a Dedekind domain,
 every nonzero proper ideal $I$ admits a unique factorization
$$I = \mathfrak{P}_1^{e_1} \cdots \mathfrak{P}_n^{e_n}, \qquad e_i \geq 1,$$
into prime ideals (the problem writes this informally as $p\mathcal{O}_K = \math
frak{P}_1 \cdots \mathfrak{P}_n$; the set of distinct prime factors is the same)
.

- Each $\mathfrak{P}_i$ contains $I$: since $\mathfrak{P}_i^{e_i} \subseteq \mat
hfrak{P}_i$, we have $I = \prod_j \mathfrak{P}_j^{e_j} \subseteq \mathfrak{P}_i^
{e_i} \subseteq \mathfrak{P}_i$.

- Conversely, let $\mathfrak{P}$ be any prime ideal containing $I = \mathfrak{P}
_1^{e_1}\cdots\mathfrak{P}_n^{e_n}$. Since $\mathfrak{P}$ is prime:
$$\mathfrak{P}_1^{e_1}\cdots\mathfrak{P}_n^{e_n} \subseteq \mathfrak{P} \;\Longr
ightarrow\; \mathfrak{P}_i \subseteq \mathfrak{P} \text{ for some } i.$$
In a Dedekind domain, every nonzero prime ideal is maximal, so $\mathfrak{P}_i \
subseteq \mathfrak{P}$ with both nonzero prime (hence maximal) forces $\mathfrak
{P} = \mathfrak{P}_i$.

Therefore:
$$\{\mathfrak{P} \subset \mathcal{O}_K \text{ prime} : I \subseteq \mathfrak{P}\
} = \{\mathfrak{P}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{P}_n\}.$$

---

**Step 3: Combine.**

Applying Step 2 with $I = p\mathcal{O}_K$ and combining with Step 1:

$$\{\mathfrak{P} \subset \mathcal{O}_K \text{ prime} : \mathfrak{P} \text{ lies
above } p\} = \{\mathfrak{P} \subset \mathcal{O}_K \text{ prime} : p\mathcal{O}_
K \subseteq \mathfrak{P}\} = \{\mathfrak{P}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{P}_n\}.$$

Thus every prime ideal of $\mathcal{O}_K$ lying above $p$ is one of the factors
$\mathfrak{P}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{P}_n$ in the prime decomposition of $p\mathcal
{O}_K$, and conversely every factor lies above $p$. $\blacksquare$
