## Proof that $E[V] = e$

**Setup.** Let $X_1, X_2, \dots$ be independent random variables, each uniformly
 distributed on $[0,1]$. Let $S_n = X_1 + \cdots + X_n$ (with $S_0 = 0$), and de
fine
$$V = \min\{n \ge 1 : S_n > 1\}.$$
We show $E[V] = e$.

**Step 1. Tail-sum formula.** Since $V$ takes values in $\{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$, fo
r any non-negative integer-valued random variable,
$$E[V] = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} P(V > n).$$
The event $\{V > n\}$ means that the sum of the first $n$ variables has **not**
yet exceeded $1$, i.e.,
$$P(V > n) = P(S_n \le 1).$$
(For $n=0$, $S_0 = 0 \le 1$, so $P(V > 0) = 1$.)

**Step 2. Geometric volume of a simplex.** The event $S_n \le 1$ with $(X_1, \do
ts, X_n) \in [0,1]^n$ is the region
$$\Delta_n = \{(x_1, \dots, x_n) \in [0,1]^n : x_1 + \cdots + x_n \le 1\}.$$
Since $x_i \ge 0$ and $\sum x_i \le 1$ already forces each $x_i \le 1$, the cons
traint $x_i \in [0,1]$ is automatically satisfied, so $\Delta_n$ is exactly the
standard $n$-simplex. Its $n$-dimensional volume is
$$\text{Vol}(\Delta_n) = \frac{1}{n!}.$$
Because the $X_i$ are uniform on $[0,1]^n$, the probability is the volume:
$$P(S_n \le 1) = \frac{1}{n!}.$$

**Step 3. Summation.** Substituting into the tail-sum formula,
$$E[V] = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} P(S_n \le 1) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n!} = e
.$$

**Conclusion.** The expected number of independent uniform $[0,1]$ variables nee
ded for their sum to exceed $1$ is exactly $e$. $\blacksquare$
