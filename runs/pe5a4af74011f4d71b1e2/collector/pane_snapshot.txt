## Proof

We interpret $(\alpha)^n, (\beta)^n, (\delta)^n$ as the Pochhammer symbols (risi
ng factorials) $(\alpha)_n, (\beta)_n, (\delta)_n$, consistent with the hypergeo
metric-function context. The $n$-th coefficient (up to $1/n!$) of $_2F_1(\alpha,
\beta,\delta,x)$ is $\frac{(\alpha)_n(\beta)_n}{(\delta)_n}$, so the question is
 whether, for every non-negative integer $n$,

$$\frac{(\alpha)_n(\beta)_n}{(\delta)_n} \;>\; \frac{(\alpha+1)_n(\beta+1)_n}{(\
delta+1)_n}. \tag{$\star$}$$

---

**Step 1: Compute the ratio of the two sides.**

Using the telescoping identity $\frac{(c+1)_n}{(c)_n} = \frac{c+n}{c}$, the rati
o of the right-hand side to the left-hand side of $(\star)$ is

$$R \;=\; \frac{(\alpha+1)_n(\beta+1)_n/(\delta+1)_n}{(\alpha)_n(\beta)_n/(\delt
a)_n} \;=\; \frac{(\alpha+n)(\beta+n)\,\delta}{\alpha\,\beta\,(\delta+n)}.$$

Inequality $(\star)$ holds iff $R < 1$, i.e.,

$$(\alpha+n)(\beta+n)\,\delta \;<\; \alpha\,\beta\,(\delta+n). \tag{1}$$

---

**Step 2: Simplify condition (1).**

Expanding both sides:

$$\delta\bigl(\alpha\beta + n\alpha + n\beta + n^2\bigr) \;<\; \alpha\beta\,\del
ta + n\,\alpha\beta.$$

Canceling $\delta\alpha\beta$ from both sides:

$$n\,\delta\alpha + n\,\delta\beta + n^2\delta \;<\; n\,\alpha\beta.$$

For $n > 0$, divide by $n$:

$$\delta(\alpha + \beta + n) \;<\; \alpha\beta. \tag{2}$$

For $n = 0$: both sides of $(\star)$ equal $1$ (since $(c)_0 = 1$), so $(\star)$
 is **false** (equality, not strict inequality).

---

**Step 3: Substitute the given parameters.**

With $\alpha = (K-1)a$, $\beta = K$, $\delta = Ka$:

$$\alpha + \beta = (K-1)a + K = Ka - a + K,$$
$$\alpha\beta = (K-1)a \cdot K = K(K-1)a,$$
$$\delta = Ka.$$

Condition (2) becomes:

$$Ka\,(Ka - a + K + n) \;<\; K(K-1)a.$$

Dividing both sides by $Ka > 0$:

$$Ka - a + K + n \;<\; K - 1,$$

$$n \;<\; K - 1 - Ka + a - K = a - Ka - 1 = a(1 - K) - 1. \tag{3}$$

---

**Step 4: Show condition (3) is never satisfied for $n \ge 0$.**

Since $K > a \ge 1$, we have $K > 1$, hence $1 - K < 0$. Since $a \ge 1$:

$$a(1-K) \;\le\; 1-K \;<\; 0,$$

so

$$a(1-K) - 1 \;<\; -1.$$

Therefore condition (3) requires $n < -1$, which is **impossible for any non-neg
ative integer** $n$.

---

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

- For $n = 0$: both sides equal $1$, so the strict inequality fails (equality ho
lds).
- For every $n \ge 1$: condition (3) fails, so $R > 1$, meaning

$$\frac{(\alpha+1)_n(\beta+1)_n}{(\delta+1)_n} \;>\; \frac{(\alpha)_n(\beta)_n}{
(\delta)_n},$$

i.e., the **reverse** of $(\star)$ holds.

Since every coefficient of $_2F_1(\alpha+1,\beta+1,\delta+1,x)$ (for $n \ge 1$)
strictly exceeds the corresponding coefficient of $_2F_1(\alpha,\beta,\delta,x)$
, and all terms are positive for $x \in (0,1)$, we conclude:

$$\boxed{_2F_1(\alpha,\beta,\delta,x) \;<\; _2F_1(\alpha+1,\beta+1,\delta+1,x) \
quad \text{for all } x \in (0,1).}$$

The proposed inequality **does not hold** for any integer $n \ge 0$; the reverse
 inequality holds for all $n \ge 1$.

**Numerical verification.** Take $K=3,\, a=2$: then $\alpha=4,\,\beta=3,\,\delta
=6$ (satisfying $6>4>3$ and $3>2\ge1$).
- $n=1$: LHS $= 4\cdot3/6 = 2$; RHS $= 5\cdot4/7 = 20/7 \approx 2.857$. LHS $<$
RHS. ✓
- $n=2$: LHS $= (4\cdot5)(3\cdot4)/(6\cdot7) = 240/42 \approx 5.71$; RHS $= (5\c
dot6)(4\cdot5)/(7\cdot8) = 600/56 \approx 10.71$. LHS $<$ RHS. ✓
