## Quadratic Variation of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck Process

**Setup.** The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) process is the unique strong solution of
the linear SDE

$$dX_t = -\theta\, X_t\, dt + \sigma\, dW_t, \qquad X_0 = x,$$

where $\theta > 0$, $\sigma > 0$, and $W_t$ is a standard Brownian motion. In in
tegral form,

$$X_t = x - \theta \int_0^t X_s\, ds + \sigma W_t.$$

**Goal.** Compute the (realized) quadratic variation $[X]_t$.

---

### Proof

**Step 1 — Semimartingale decomposition.** Write

$$X_t = A_t + M_t,$$

where

$$A_t := x - \theta \int_0^t X_s\, ds, \qquad M_t := \sigma W_t.$$

- $A_t$ is a continuous process of **finite variation** on every compact interva
l $[0,t]$, since it is the difference of a constant and an ordinary Riemann inte
gral of a continuous (hence locally bounded) integrand.
- $M_t = \sigma W_t$ is a continuous **local martingale** (in fact a true martin
gale), being a constant multiple of Brownian motion.

Thus $X$ is a continuous semimartingale.

**Step 2 — Quadratic variation of the finite-variation part.** For any continuou
s finite-variation path $A$, the realized quadratic variation vanishes. Indeed,
let $\Pi = \{0 = t_0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_n = t\}$ be a partition with mesh $\|\Pi
\| \to 0$. Then

$$\sum_{i} (A_{t_{i+1}} - A_{t_i})^2 \le \Big(\sup_i |A_{t_{i+1}} - A_{t_i}|\Big
) \sum_i |A_{t_{i+1}} - A_{t_i}| \le \Big(\sup_i |A_{t_{i+1}} - A_{t_i}|\Big) \c
dot V_t(A),$$

where $V_t(A)$ is the total variation of $A$ on $[0,t]$, finite by assumption. S
ince $A$ is continuous, $\sup_i |A_{t_{i+1}} - A_{t_i}| \to 0$ as $\|\Pi\| \to 0
$. Hence

$$[A]_t = 0.$$

**Step 3 — Quadratic variation of the martingale part.** For $M_t = \sigma W_t$,
 the quadratic variation is

$$[M]_t = \sigma^2 [W]_t = \sigma^2 t,$$

using the classical result $[W]_t = t$ for Brownian motion and the scaling $[\si
gma W]_t = \sigma^2 [W]_t$.

**Step 4 — Cross-variation vanishes.** The cross-variation between a continuous
finite-variation process and a continuous semimartingale is zero:

$$[A, M]_t = 0.$$

This follows from the bound

$$\sum_i (A_{t_{i+1}} - A_{t_i})(M_{t_{i+1}} - M_{t_i}) \le \Big(\sup_i |A_{t_{i
+1}} - A_{t_i}|\Big) \sum_i |M_{t_{i+1}} - M_{t_i}|,$$

and the fact that continuous finite-variation paths have $\sup_i |\Delta A| \to
0$ while $\sum |\Delta M|$ is bounded in probability (or more directly, $|[A,M]|
 \le \sqrt{[A]\,[M]} = 0$ by the Kunita–Watanabe inequality once $[A]=0$ is esta
blished).

**Step 5 — Combine via bilinearity.** The quadratic variation bracket is bilinea
r on continuous semimartingales:

$$[X]_t = [A + M]_t = [A]_t + 2[A,M]_t + [M]_t = 0 + 0 + \sigma^2 t.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{[X]_t = \sigma^2\, t.}$$

---

### Interpretation

The quadratic variation of the OU process is **deterministic and linear in $t$**
, depending only on the diffusion coefficient $\sigma$ and **not** on the mean-r
eversion parameter $\theta$ or the initial value $x$. This is expected on genera
l grounds: for any Itô process $dX_t = b_t\, dt + \sigma_t\, dW_t$, the quadrati
c variation is always

$$[X]_t = \int_0^t \sigma_s^2\, ds,$$

independent of the drift. For the OU process the diffusion coefficient $\sigma_t
 \equiv \sigma$ is constant, giving $[X]_t = \sigma^2 t$.
