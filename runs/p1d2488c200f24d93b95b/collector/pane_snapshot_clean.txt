## Proof

**Setup.** Choose a basis of $\Lambda$ so that $\Lambda \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p^2\ma
thbb{Z})^3$ and the bilinear form is $(x,y) = x^T A y \pmod{p^2}$, where $A$ is
a symmetric $3\times 3$ matrix over $\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z}$. Since the form i
s perfect, $\det A$ is a unit in $\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z}$, so $\bar{A} := A \b
mod p$ is a non-degenerate (invertible) symmetric matrix over $\mathbb{F}_p$.

We seek $N = |\{x \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z})^3 : x^T A x \equiv 0 \pmod{p^2}
\}|$.

**Lifting decomposition.** Every $x \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z})^3$ can be wri
tten uniquely as $x = a + p\,b$ with $a, b \in (\mathbb{F}_p)^3$ (identifying $\
{0,\ldots,p-1\}^3$ with $\mathbb{F}_p^3$). Using the symmetry of $A$:

$$x^T A x = (a+pb)^T A (a+pb) = a^T A a + p\,(a^T A b + b^T A a) + p^2\, b^T A b
 \equiv a^T A a + 2p\, a^T A b \pmod{p^2}.$$

Write $A = \bar{A} + p\,\widetilde{A}$ with $\bar{A}, \widetilde{A}$ over $\math
bb{F}_p$. Then $a^T A a = a^T \bar{A}\, a + p\, a^T \widetilde{A}\, a \pmod{p^2}
$. Setting $x^T A x \equiv 0 \pmod{p^2}$ splits into two conditions:

1. **(Mod $p$):** $a^T \bar{A}\, a \equiv 0 \pmod{p}$.
2. **(Mod $p^2$):** If condition 1 holds, write $a^T \bar{A}\, a = p\,d$ in $\ma
thbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z}$ (with $d \in \mathbb{F}_p$). Then we need $d + a^T \widet
ilde{A}\, a + 2\, a^T \bar{A}\, b \equiv 0 \pmod{p}$.

**Counting by cases on $a$.**

*Case 1: $a = 0$.* Then $d = 0$, $a^T \widetilde{A}\, a = 0$, and $a^T \bar{A}\,
 b = 0$, so condition 2 is $0 \equiv 0$, satisfied for all $b$. This gives $p^3$
 solutions.

*Case 2: $a \neq 0$ with $a^T \bar{A}\, a = 0$.* Since $\bar{A}$ is invertible a
nd $a \neq 0$, the linear functional $b \mapsto a^T \bar{A}\, b$ is a nonzero (h
ence surjective) linear map $\mathbb{F}_p^3 \to \mathbb{F}_p$. Therefore conditi
on 2 (a single linear equation in $b$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$) has exactly $p^2$ sol
utions for $b$, regardless of the constant term $d + a^T \widetilde{A}\, a$.

**Counting isotropic vectors over $\mathbb{F}_p$.** Let

$$M = |\{a \in \mathbb{F}_p^3 : a^T \bar{A}\, a = 0\}|.$$

The quadratic form $Q(a) = a^T \bar{A}\, a$ is non-degenerate in $n = 3$ variabl
es over $\mathbb{F}_p$. A standard result on quadratic forms over finite fields
of odd characteristic states:

> For a non-degenerate quadratic form in $n$ variables over $\mathbb{F}_q$ ($q$
odd), the number of zeros is $q^{n-1}$ when $n$ is odd.

(For even $n$ there is a correction term depending on the discriminant, but for
odd $n$ the count is always $q^{n-1}$, independent of the form.)

With $n = 3$ and $q = p$, we get $M = p^2$.

*Brief justification of the formula:* The number of solutions to $Q(x) = t$ is $
q^{n-1} + q^{(n-2)/2}\eta(\Delta)\,G(t)$ for even $n$, and $q^{n-1} + q^{(n-1)/2
}\eta(t)\cdot\eta((-1)^{(n-1)/2}\Delta')$ for odd $n$ and $t \neq 0$, where $\et
a$ is the quadratic character. For $t = 0$ and odd $n$, the character sum vanish
es, leaving exactly $q^{n-1}$.

**Assembling the count.** The total number of isotropic vectors is:

$$N = \underbrace{p^3}_{a=0} + \underbrace{p^2 \cdot (M - 1)}_{a \neq 0,\; Q(a)=
0} = p^3 + p^2(p^2 - 1) = p^3 + p^4 - p^2 = p^2(p^2 + p - 1).$$

**Independence from the form.** The count depends on $A$ only through $M$, which
 depends only on $\bar{A}$ (the reduction mod $p$). Since $M = p^{n-1} = p^2$ fo
r *any* non-degenerate quadratic form in 3 variables over $\mathbb{F}_p$, the re
sult is the same for every perfect symmetric bilinear form on $\Lambda$.

**Verification ($p = 3$).** With $A = I_3$ and $p = 3$: $M = 9$ (1 zero vector +
 8 vectors with all coordinates in $\{1,2\}$, each squaring to 1, summing to 3 ≡
 0). Then $N = 27 + 9 \cdot 8 = 99 = 9 \cdot 11 = 3^2(9 + 3 - 1)$. ✓
