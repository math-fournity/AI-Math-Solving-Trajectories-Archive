## Proof

Let $\alpha = 10 + \sqrt{99}$ and $\beta = 10 - \sqrt{99}$.

**Step 1: Conjugate pair.** Since $\sqrt{99} < \sqrt{100} = 10$, we have $\beta
= 10 - \sqrt{99} > 0$. Also $\sqrt{99} > \sqrt{98.01} = 9.9$, so $\beta < 0.1$.
Thus
$$0 < \beta < 0.1.$$
The numbers $\alpha, \beta$ are roots of $x^2 - 20x + 1 = 0$, with $\alpha + \be
ta = 20$ and $\alpha\beta = 1$.

**Step 2: Integer sequence.** Define $S_n = \alpha^n + \beta^n$. From the recurr
ence relation for $\alpha, \beta$:
$$S_n = 20\,S_{n-1} - S_{n-2}, \quad S_0 = 2,\; S_1 = 20.$$
By induction, every $S_n$ is a **positive integer**.

**Step 3: Fractional part of $\alpha^{2023}$.** We have
$$\alpha^{2023} = S_{2023} - \beta^{2023}.$$
Since $S_{2023}$ is a positive integer and $0 < \beta^{2023} < 1$, we decompose:
$$\alpha^{2023} = (S_{2023} - 1) + (1 - \beta^{2023}).$$
So the **integer part** is $S_{2023} - 1$ and the **fractional part** is $1 - \b
eta^{2023}$.

**Step 4: Bounding $\beta^{2023}$.** From $0 < \beta < 0.1$:
$$0 < \beta^{2023} < (0.1)^{2023} = 10^{-2023}.$$
Therefore
$$1 - 10^{-2023} < 1 - \beta^{2023} < 1.$$

**Step 5: Reading off the digits.** The number $1 - 10^{-2023}$ has decimal expa
nsion
$$1 - 10^{-2023} = 0.\underbrace{99\ldots 9}_{2023}.$$
Since the fractional part $1 - \beta^{2023}$ is **strictly greater** than $0.\un
derbrace{99\ldots 9}_{2023}$ and strictly less than $1$, its decimal expansion b
egins with at least $2023$ consecutive $9$'s:
$$1 - \beta^{2023} = 0.\underbrace{99\ldots 9}_{2023}\ldots$$

(Note: $\beta^{2023}$ is irrational, so $1-\beta^{2023}$ is irrational; its deci
mal expansion is non-terminating and non-repeating, with no ambiguous terminatin
g representation.)

**Conclusion.** The digit at the 2023rd position to the right of the decimal poi
nt in the decimal expansion of $(10+\sqrt{99})^{2023}$ is

$$\boxed{9}.$$
