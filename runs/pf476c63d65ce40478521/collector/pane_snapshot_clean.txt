Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a finite ring with identity
 can be embedded as a subring in a matrix ring over a field of different charact
eristic. I'll work through this purely by reasoning.

## Answer: No, it is not possible.

**Theorem.** Let $R$ be a ring with identity $1_R \neq 0$, and let $F$ be a fiel
d. If there exists an injective ring homomorphism $f: R \to M_n(F)$ (unital or n
ot), then $\operatorname{char}(R) = \operatorname{char}(F)$.

**Proof.**

Let $e := f(1_R) \in M_n(F)$. Since $f$ is a ring homomorphism:

$$e^2 = f(1_R)^2 = f(1_R^2) = f(1_R) = e,$$

so $e$ is idempotent. Since $f$ is injective and $1_R \neq 0$, we have $e \neq 0
$.

**Step 1: The image of $f$ lies in $e\,M_n(F)\,e$.**

For any $r \in R$:
$$f(r) = f(1_R \cdot r) = f(1_R)\,f(r) = e\,f(r),$$
$$f(r) = f(r \cdot 1_R) = f(r)\,f(1_R) = f(r)\,e.$$

Hence $f(R) \subseteq e\,M_n(F)\,e$.

**Step 2: $e\,M_n(F)\,e$ is a ring with identity $e$, isomorphic to $M_k(F)$.**

The set $e\,M_n(F)\,e$ is closed under addition and multiplication, with multipl
icative identity $e$ (since $e \cdot (eme) = e^2me = eme$ and $(eme)\cdot e = em
e^2 = eme$). The map $f: R \to e\,M_n(F)\,e$ is an injective ring homomorphism s
ending $1_R \mapsto e$, so it is a **unital** embedding of $R$ into $e\,M_n(F)\,
e$.

Now, $e$ is a nonzero idempotent in $M_n(F)$, hence a projection of $F^n$ onto a
 $k$-dimensional subspace for some $1 \le k \le n$. Choosing a basis adapted to
the decomposition $F^n = \operatorname{im}(e) \oplus \ker(e)$, the matrix $e$ is
 conjugate to $\begin{pmatrix} I_k & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, and under this c
onjugation:

$$e\,M_n(F)\,e \cong M_k(F).$$

**Step 3: Characteristics must coincide.**

Since $R$ embeds unitally into $M_k(F)$, for any positive integer $m$:

$$m \cdot 1_R = 0 \text{ in } R \iff f(m \cdot 1_R) = m \cdot e = 0 \text{ in }
e\,M_n(F)\,e \iff m \cdot I_k = 0 \text{ in } M_k(F) \iff \operatorname{char}(F)
 \mid m.$$

Therefore $\operatorname{char}(R) = \operatorname{char}(F)$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the two conventions for "subring."** The proof covers both:
- If "subring" requires sharing the identity of the ambient ring, then $f(1_R) =
 I_n$ directly, and $\operatorname{char}(R) = \operatorname{char}(F)$ is immedia
te.
- If "subring" does not require sharing the ambient identity, the argument above
 (via the idempotent $e$ and the corner ring $eM_n(F)e \cong M_k(F)$) still forc
es $\operatorname{char}(R) = \operatorname{char}(F)$.

The finiteness hypothesis on $R$ ensures $\operatorname{char}(R) > 0$ (since the
 additive group of a finite ring is finite), but the theorem holds for arbitrary
 rings with $1 \neq 0$.

**Conclusion:** It is **not possible** for a finite ring with identity to be emb
edded as a subring in a matrix ring over a field whose characteristic differs fr
om that of the ring.
