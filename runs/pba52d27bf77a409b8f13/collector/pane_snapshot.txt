## Proof

**Setup.** Let $\omega$ be the incircle of $\triangle ABC$ with radius $r=1$, ta
ngent to $BC$ at $D$. Let the tangent lengths from $B$ and $C$ be $y$ and $z$ re
spectively, so $BD = y$, $CD = z$, and $BC = y+z$. Place $D$ at the origin with
$BC$ along the $x$-axis, so $I = (0,1)$, $B=(-y,0)$, $C=(z,0)$.

**Finding vertex $A$.** The non-$BC$ tangent from $B$ to $\omega$ has slope $m_B
 = \frac{2y}{y^2-1}$; the non-$BC$ tangent from $C$ has slope $m_C = \frac{-2z}{
z^2-1}$. Intersecting these two lines gives:

$$A = \left(\frac{y-z}{yz-1},\; \frac{2yz}{yz-1}\right).$$

(One verifies the tangent length from $A$ equals $\frac{y+z}{yz-1}$, and the rel
ation $t \cdot y \cdot z = t + y + z$ — equivalent to $\text{Area} = rs$ — is au
tomatically satisfied, confirming consistency.)

**Line $AD$ and its intersection with $\omega$.** Since $D$ is the origin, point
s on line $AD$ are $P = \lambda A$ for $\lambda \in [0,1]$. Substituting into th
e circle equation $x^2 + y^2 - 2y = 0$:

$$\lambda^2\frac{(y-z)^2 + 4y^2z^2}{(yz-1)^2} - \lambda\frac{4yz}{yz-1} = 0.$$

The solutions are $\lambda = 0$ (point $D$) and

$$\lambda_X = \frac{4yz(yz-1)}{(y-z)^2 + 4y^2z^2}.$$

Since $XD = \lambda_X |A|$ and $AX = (1-\lambda_X)|A|$, the ratio $AX:XD = 1:3$
gives $\frac{1-\lambda_X}{\lambda_X} = \frac{1}{3}$, hence $\lambda_X = \frac{3}
{4}$.

**Setting up equations.** Let $u = yz$, $v = y+z$, $w = y-z$, so $w^2 = v^2 - 4u
$.

From $\lambda_X = \frac{3}{4}$:

$$16u(u-1) = 3w^2 + 12u^2 \implies 4u(u-1) = 3w^2. \tag{I}$$

From $XD = \frac{3}{10}BC = \frac{3v}{10}$ and $XD = \frac{3}{4}|A|$ with $|A|^2
 = \frac{w^2+4u^2}{(u-1)^2}$:

$$25(w^2 + 4u^2) = 4v^2(u-1)^2. \tag{II}$$

**Solving.** From (I): $w^2 = \frac{4u(u-1)}{3} - 4u \cdot\ldots$ — more directl
y, $3w^2 = 4u(u-1) - 12u + 12u = 4u^2-4u$, so $w^2 = \frac{4u(u-4)}{3}$, and $v^
2 = w^2 + 4u = \frac{4u(u-4)}{3} + 4u = \frac{4u(u-1)}{3}$.

Substituting both into (II):

$$25\!\left(\frac{4u(u-4)}{3} + 4u^2\right) = 4 \cdot \frac{4u(u-1)}{3} \cdot (u
-1)^2$$

$$100 \cdot \frac{4u^2 - 4u}{3} = \frac{16u(u-1)^3}{3}$$

$$\frac{400u(u-1)}{3} = \frac{16u(u-1)^3}{3}$$

Dividing by $\frac{16u(u-1)}{3}$ (valid since $u > 1$):

$$25 = (u-1)^2 \implies u = 6.$$

**Computing $XD$.** With $u = yz = 6$: $v^2 = \frac{4 \cdot 6 \cdot 5}{3} = 40$,
 so $BC = v = 2\sqrt{10}$.

$$XD = \frac{3}{10} \cdot BC = \frac{3}{10} \cdot 2\sqrt{10} = \frac{3\sqrt{10}}
{5}.$$

**Verification.** With $y = \sqrt{10}+2$, $z = \sqrt{10}-2$ (so $yz=6$, $y+z=2\s
qrt{10}$, $y-z=4$): $A = \left(\frac{4}{5}, \frac{12}{5}\right)$, $X = \frac{3}{
4}A = \left(\frac{3}{5}, \frac{9}{5}\right)$. Check $X \in \omega$: $\frac{9}{25
} + \frac{81}{25} - \frac{18}{5} = \frac{90}{25} - \frac{90}{25} = 0$. ✓ $AX = \
frac{\sqrt{10}}{5}$, $XD = \frac{3\sqrt{10}}{5}$, $BC = 2\sqrt{10}$, giving rati
o $1:3:10$. ✓

$$\boxed{XD = \dfrac{3\sqrt{10}}{5}}$$
