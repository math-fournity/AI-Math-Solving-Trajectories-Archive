## Solution

We solve the heat equation on $[0,\pi]$ with homogeneous Neumann boundary condit
ions via separation of variables (eigenfunction expansion in the Neumann eigenba
sis).

### Step 1. Neumann eigenfunctions on $[0,\pi]$

We seek separated solutions $u(x,t)=X(x)T(t)$ of $\partial_t u = \partial_x^2 u$
 with $X'(0)=X'(\pi)=0$. This gives $X''+\lambda X=0$, $X'(0)=X'(\pi)=0$. The ei
genpairs are

$$\lambda_n = n^2,\qquad X_n(x)=\cos(nx),\qquad n=0,1,2,\dots$$

with $X_0(x)=1$ ($\lambda_0=0$). These form a complete orthogonal system on $[0,
\pi]$. The time factors satisfy $T_n'(t)=-n^2 T_n(t)$, so $T_n(t)=e^{-n^2 t}$ (w
ith $T_0=1$).

### Step 2. General solution

$$u(x,t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n\cos(nx)\,e^{-n^2 t},$$

where the Fourier cosine coefficients of the initial data $g$ on $[0,\pi]$ are

$$a_n=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi} g(x)\cos(nx)\,dx.$$

### Step 3. Compute the coefficients

Since $g(x)=1$ on $[0,\pi/2)$ and $g(x)=0$ on $[\pi/2,\pi]$,

$$a_0=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2}1\,dx=\frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}=1.$$

For $n\ge 1$,

$$a_n=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos(nx)\,dx=\frac{2}{\pi}\left[\frac{\sin(nx)}
{n}\right]_0^{\pi/2}=\frac{2}{\pi n}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2}\right).$$

The values of $\sin(n\pi/2)$ cycle as $1,0,-1,0,1,0,-1,0,\dots$:
- $n$ even: $\sin(n\pi/2)=0$, so $a_n=0$;
- $n=2k+1$ odd: $\sin\!\big((2k+1)\pi/2\big)=(-1)^k$, so $a_{2k+1}=\dfrac{2\,(-1
)^k}{\pi(2k+1)}$.

### Step 4. The solution

Substituting,

$$\boxed{\;u(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\pi n}\sin\!\left(\fra
c{n\pi}{2}\right)\cos(nx)\,e^{-n^2 t}\;}$$

Equivalently, isolating the odd modes ($n=2k+1$, $k=0,1,2,\dots$):

$$u(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k}{2k+1}\cos\!\b
ig((2k+1)x\big)\,e^{-(2k+1)^2 t}.$$

### Step 5. Verification

**PDE.** Each term $\cos(nx)e^{-n^2 t}$ satisfies $\partial_t = -n^2$ and $\part
ial_x^2 = -n^2$, so $\partial_t u=\partial_x^2 u$ termwise. The series converges
 absolutely and uniformly for $t\ge t_0>0$ (and in $L^2$ for $t\ge 0$), justifyi
ng termwise differentiation for $t>0$.

**Boundary conditions.** $\partial_x[\cos(nx)]= -n\sin(nx)$, which vanishes at $
x=0$ and $x=\pi$ for every $n\ge 0$; the constant term has zero derivative. Henc
e $\partial_x u(0,t)=\partial_x u(\pi,t)=0$.

**Initial condition.** At $t=0$ the series becomes the Fourier cosine series of
$g$:
$$\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n\cos(nx)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{k=0
}^\infty\frac{(-1)^k}{2k+1}\cos((2k+1)x).$$
By the standard convergence theorem for cosine series of piecewise-$C^1$ functio
ns, this equals $g(x)$ at every point of continuity of $g$ (i.e. $x\in[0,\pi/2)\
cup(\pi/2,\pi]$) and equals the midpoint value $\tfrac{1}{2}$ at the jump $x=\pi
/2$. In particular $u(x,0)=g(x)$ for $0\le x<\pi/2$ and for $\pi/2<x\le\pi$, whi
ch is the $L^2$ (and pointwise-a.e.) sense in which the initial condition is att
ained. The mean value $\frac{a_0}{2}=\frac{1}{2}$ is also consistent with conser
vation of total heat under Neumann conditions: $\int_0^\pi u\,dx=\int_0^\pi g\,d
x=\frac{\pi}{2}$ for all $t$.

All three conditions (PDE, Neumann BCs, initial data) are satisfied, completing
the solution.
