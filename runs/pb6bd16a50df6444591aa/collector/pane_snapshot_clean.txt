## Solution

**Setup.** Let the two integer roots of $x^2+bx+a=0$ be $p,q$, and the two integ
er roots of $x^2+cx+(a-1)=0$ be $r,s$. By Vieta's formulas:

$$pq = a, \qquad rs = a-1,$$

with $p,q,r,s$ all integers strictly less than $-1$, i.e., $p,q,r,s \le -2$.

**Substitution.** Set $p'=-p,\; q'=-q,\; r'=-r,\; s'=-s$, so $p',q',r',s' \ge 2$
. Then:

$$p'q' = a, \qquad r's' = a-1, \qquad p'q' - r's' = 1.$$

We seek the **smallest** $a = p'q'$ (with $p',q' \ge 2$) such that $a-1 = r's'$
for some $r',s' \ge 2$.

**Checking small values.** The achievable values of $a = p'q'$ (products of two
integers $\ge 2$) are $4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, \ldots$. For each, we need $a-1$ to a
lso factor as a product of two integers $\ge 2$:

| $a$ | $a-1$ | Factorization | Feasible? |
|-----|-------|---------------|-----------|
| 4 | 3 | $1 \times 3$ | No ($1 < 2$) |
| 6 | 5 | $1 \times 5$ | No |
| 8 | 7 | $1 \times 7$ | No |
| **9** | **8** | **$2 \times 4$** | **Yes** ✓ |

Since $3, 5, 7$ are prime, $a-1 \in \{3,5,7\}$ cannot be written as a product of
 two integers $\ge 2$. The first feasible value is $a = 9$.

**Verification.** With $a = 9$:
- First equation: $p = q = -3$ (both $< -1$). Then $a = (-3)(-3) = 9$, $b = -(p+
q) = 6$, giving $x^2 + 6x + 9 = 0$ with double root $-3$. ✓
- Second equation: $r = -2,\; s = -4$ (both $< -1$). Then $a - 1 = (-2)(-4) = 8$
, $c = -(r+s) = 6$, giving $x^2 + 6x + 8 = 0$ with roots $-2$ and $-4$. ✓

All four roots $-3, -3, -2, -4$ are strictly less than $-1$.

**Conclusion.** The smallest value of $a$ is $\boxed{9}$.
