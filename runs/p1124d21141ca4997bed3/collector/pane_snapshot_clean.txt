## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $\{f_n\}$ converges to $f$ in $L^r(X)$ for all $1 \le r < p$.

**Step 1: $f$ is also bounded by $K$ a.e.**

Since $f_n \to f$ in $L^p(X)$, there exists a subsequence $\{f_{n_k}\}$ that con
verges to $f$ pointwise almost everywhere. By hypothesis, $|f_{n_k}(x)| \le K$ f
or a.e. $x$, so passing to the limit gives $|f(x)| \le K$ for a.e. $x$.

**Step 2: A pointwise bound on $|f_n - f|$.**

For a.e. $x$, we have $|f_n(x)| \le K$ and $|f(x)| \le K$, hence by the triangle
 inequality:
$$|f_n(x) - f(x)| \le |f_n(x)| + |f(x)| \le 2K \quad \text{for a.e. } x.$$

**Step 3: Relating the $r$-th and $p$-th powers.**

Let $1 \le r < p$. For any $a$ with $0 \le a \le 2K$, since $r - p < 0$, we have
:
$$a^r = a^p \cdot a^{r-p} \le a^p \cdot (2K)^{r-p},$$
because $a^{r-p} \le (2K)^{r-p}$ (the exponent $r-p$ is negative, so the functio
n $t \mapsto t^{r-p}$ is decreasing, and $a \le 2K$).

Applying this with $a = |f_n(x) - f(x)|$ (valid a.e. by Step 2):
$$|f_n(x) - f(x)|^r \le (2K)^{r-p}\, |f_n(x) - f(x)|^p \quad \text{for a.e. } x.
$$

**Step 4: Integrate and conclude.**

Integrating over $X$:
$$\int_X |f_n - f|^r\, d\mu \le (2K)^{r-p} \int_X |f_n - f|^p\, d\mu.$$

That is,
$$\|f_n - f\|_r^r \le (2K)^{r-p}\, \|f_n - f\|_p^p.$$

Since $f_n \to f$ in $L^p(X)$, the right-hand side tends to $0$. Therefore $\|f_
n - f\|_r^r \to 0$, which gives $\|f_n - f\|_r \to 0$.

This holds for every $1 \le r < p$, completing the proof. $\blacksquare$
