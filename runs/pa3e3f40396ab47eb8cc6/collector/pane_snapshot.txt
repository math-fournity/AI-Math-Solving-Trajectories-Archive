Let $M$ be the midpoint of arc $BC$ not containing $A$. Since $D$ lies on the mi
nor arc $AC$ (which does not contain $B$), the arc $BC$ not containing $D$ is th

C$ not containing $D$, and by the inscribed angle bisector theorem, $DM$ bisects
 $\angle BDC$. The line $DM$ is thus the bisector $\ell$, and $M$ is **fixed** (
independent of $D$).

2\gamma$, measured along the major arc. This is well-defined since $2\beta + 2\g
amma = 2(\pi - \alpha)$ equals the measure of the major arc $BC$. The point $P$
is fixed, depending only on the triangle. (Note: since $\beta < \gamma$, $P$ lie
s between $B$ and $A$ on the major arc, and $P \neq A$.)

**Main claim:** $DM$ bisects $\angle ADP$, i.e., $\angle ADM = \angle MDP$.

If this holds, then reflecting ray $DA$ over line $DM$ yields ray $DP$. Since $E

2\gamma$, so $M$ (midpoint of minor arc $BC$) is at $2\beta + \alpha$, and $D$ i

$$\angle ADM = \frac{2\pi - 2\beta - \alpha}{2} = \frac{\pi - \beta + \gamma}{2}

$$\angle MDP = \frac{2\gamma + \alpha}{2} = \frac{2\gamma + \pi - \beta - \gamma
}{2} = \frac{\pi - \beta + \gamma}{2}$$
