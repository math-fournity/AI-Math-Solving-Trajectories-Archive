Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $\log A$ (for symmetric pos
itive definite $A$) can always be locally written as a power series $\sum c_n (A
 - A_0)^n$ around any SPD matrix $A_0$.

**Answer: No.** It works if and only if $A_0$ is a scalar multiple of the identi
ty.

---

## Proof

### Positive case: $A_0 = \lambda I$ works

When $A_0 = \lambda I$ for some $\lambda > 0$, we write $A = \lambda I + (A - \l
ambda I) = \lambda\!\left(I + \frac{A - \lambda I}{\lambda}\right)$, so

$$\log A = (\log \lambda)\, I + \log\!\left(I + \tfrac{A-\lambda I}{\lambda}\rig
ht) = (\log \lambda)\, I + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}\left(\frac{A
 - \lambda I}{\lambda}\right)^n.$$

This is a power series in $(A - A_0)$ converging when $\rho\!\left(\frac{A-\lamb
da I}{\lambda}\right) < 1$, i.e., when all eigenvalues of $A$ lie in $(0, 2\lamb
da)$. So at scalar matrices, a local power series expansion exists.

### Negative case: $A_0$ with distinct eigenvalues fails

Let $A_0$ be an SPD matrix with at least two distinct eigenvalues. Without loss
of generality (by orthogonal change of basis), take $A_0 = \operatorname{diag}(\
lambda_1, \lambda_2, \dots, \lambda_n)$ with $\lambda_1 \neq \lambda_2$, all $\l
ambda_i > 0$.

**Suppose for contradiction** that there exists a scalar power series $p(z) = \s
um_{k=0}^{\infty} c_k z^k$ with positive radius of convergence such that

$$\log A = p(A - A_0)$$

for all $A$ in some neighborhood of $A_0$ (within the SPD cone).

Restrict to the **commutative subalgebra** of diagonal matrices in the same eige
nbasis as $A_0$. For $A = \operatorname{diag}(a_1, \dots, a_n)$ with each $a_i >
 0$ and $a_i$ near $\lambda_i$:

$$\log A = \operatorname{diag}(\log a_1, \dots, \log a_n),$$

while

$$p(A - A_0) = \operatorname{diag}\!\big(p(a_1 - \lambda_1),\; \dots,\; p(a_n -
\lambda_n)\big).$$

Equating entry-wise:

- From entry $1$: $p(a_1 - \lambda_1) = \log a_1$ for all $a_1$ near $\lambda_1$
. Setting $z = a_1 - \lambda_1$, this gives $p(z) = \log(\lambda_1 + z)$ for $z$
 near $0$.

- From entry $2$: $p(a_2 - \lambda_2) = \log a_2$ for all $a_2$ near $\lambda_2$
. Setting $z = a_2 - \lambda_2$, this gives $p(z) = \log(\lambda_2 + z)$ for $z$
 near $0$.

Since $p$ is a single power series, both identities must hold simultaneously. Bu
t $\log(\lambda_1 + z)$ and $\log(\lambda_2 + z)$ are **different** analytic fun
ctions: they differ already at $z = 0$, since $\log \lambda_1 \neq \log \lambda_
2$ (as $\lambda_1 \neq \lambda_2$ and both are positive). A power series is uniq
uely determined by its value and derivatives at $z=0$; two distinct analytic fun
ctions cannot share the same power series expansion. **Contradiction.**

### Conclusion

A power series $p(A - A_0)$ applies the *same* scalar function $p$ to every eige
nvalue of $(A - A_0)$. But $\log A$ applies $\log$ to the eigenvalues of $A$ its
elf, which are $\lambda_i + (a_i - \lambda_i)$ — a *different* shift for each ei
genvalue when $A_0$ has distinct eigenvalues. No single univariate power series
can encode multiple different shifts simultaneously.

Therefore, the logarithm of a symmetric positive definite matrix **cannot** alwa
ys be expressed locally as a power series. It can be so expressed if and only if
 the center $A_0$ is a scalar matrix $\lambda I$; for any SPD matrix with at lea
st two distinct eigenvalues, no such local power series expansion exists.
