## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. In a separable (real) Hilbert space $H$ there exists a countable
 family $\{w_n\}_{n\in\mathbb N}\subset H$ such that
$$
\|u\|=\sup_{n\in\mathbb N}(u,w_n)\qquad\text{for every }u\in H.
$$

---

### 1. Separability of the closed unit ball

$H$ is a separable metric space (it possesses a countable orthonormal basis, hen
ce is separable). A basic fact: *every subspace of a separable metric space is s
eparable*. Therefore the closed unit ball
$$
B=\{v\in H:\|v\|\le 1\}
$$
is itself separable. Consequently there exists a countable dense subset $\{w_n\}
_{n\in\mathbb N}\subset B$.

We claim this family works.

### 2. Upper bound: $\sup_n (u,w_n)\le \|u\|$

Fix $u\in H$. For every $n$, since $w_n\in B$ we have $\|w_n\|\le 1$. By Cauchy–
Schwarz,
$$
(u,w_n)\le |(u,w_n)|\le \|u\|\,\|w_n\|\le \|u\|\cdot 1=\|u\|.
$$
Taking the supremum over $n$,
$$
\sup_{n\in\mathbb N}(u,w_n)\le \|u\|. \tag{1}
$$

### 3. Lower bound: $\sup_n (u,w_n)\ge \|u\|$

If $u=0$ the equality is trivial. Assume $u\neq 0$ and set
$$
v:=\frac{u}{\|u\|}\in S:=\{x\in H:\|x\|=1\}\subset B.
$$
Since $\{w_n\}$ is dense in $B$ and $v\in B$, there is a subsequence $\{w_{n_k}\
}$ with $w_{n_k}\to v$ in norm. By continuity of the inner product,
$$
(u,w_{n_k})\longrightarrow (u,v)=\Bigl(u,\frac{u}{\|u\|}\Bigr)=\frac{\|u\|^2}{\|
u\|}=\|u\|.
$$
Hence
$$
\sup_{n\in\mathbb N}(u,w_n)\ge \lim_{k\to\infty}(u,w_{n_k})=\|u\|. \tag{2}
$$

### 4. Conclusion

Combining (1) and (2),
$$
\|u\|=\sup_{n\in\mathbb N}(u,w_n)\qquad\text{for all }u\in H.
$$

Thus such a countable family exists: any countable norm-dense subset of the clos
ed unit ball $B$ is an explicit example.

---

**Remark on the hypotheses.** The only ingredient actually used is the *separabi
lity* of $H$, which guarantees that $B$ (a subspace of a separable metric space)
 admits a countable dense subset. The existence of an orthonormal basis and the
Parseval identity $\|u\|^2=\sum_n |(u,e_n)|^2$ are precisely what certifies that
 $H$ is separable, so they enter through separability. The Parseval identity its
elf is not needed in the argument beyond establishing separability.

**Remark on the complex case.** If $H$ is a complex Hilbert space, the expressio
n $\sup_n (u,w_n)$ is not ordered (complex values). The natural complex analogue
 is $\|u\|=\sup_n \operatorname{Re}(u,w_n)$, obtained by the identical construct
ion with a countable dense subset of $B$; the proof above goes through verbatim
with $(u,w_n)$ replaced by $\operatorname{Re}(u,w_n)$, since $|\operatorname{Re}
(u,w_n)|\le\|u\|\,\|w_n\|\le\|u\|$ and $\operatorname{Re}(u,u/\|u\|)=\|u\|$.
