## Proof

We seek a curve $y(x)$ on $[2,6]$ minimizing the arc length
$$L[y] = \int_2^6 \sqrt{1 + (y')^2}\,dx$$
subject to the constraints
$$y(2)=5,\quad y(6)=1,\quad y(4)=3,\quad \int_2^6 y\,dx = 12.$$

**Key observation.** Consider the straight line through the two endpoints $(2,5)
$ and $(6,1)$. Its slope is
$$\frac{1-5}{6-2} = -1,$$
so the line is
$$y = 7 - x.$$

**Check the intermediate point.** At $x=4$: $y = 7-4 = 3$. ✓ The point $(4,3)$ l
ies on this line.

**Check the integral constraint.**
$$\int_2^6 (7-x)\,dx = \left[7x - \tfrac{x^2}{2}\right]_2^6 = (42-18)-(14-2) = 2
4 - 12 = 12.$$ ✓

**Minimum length argument.** Among all curves joining two fixed points, the stra
ight line segment has the shortest length. This is the classical fact underlying
 the calculus of variations: the arc-length functional $\int\sqrt{1+y'^2}\,dx$ h
as Euler–Lagrange equation $\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(\frac{y'}{\sqrt{1+y'^2}}\right)=
0$, giving $y'=\text{const}$, i.e., a straight line. Since the straight line $y=
7-x$ satisfies every one of the imposed constraints (both endpoints, the interio
r point $(4,3)$, and the integral value $12$), it is feasible; and being the glo
bal minimizer of arc length among **all** curves joining $(2,5)$ to $(6,1)$, it
is in particular the minimizer among the constrained subset.

**Length.**
$$L = \sqrt{(6-2)^2 + (1-5)^2} = \sqrt{16+16} = \sqrt{32} = 4\sqrt{2}.$$

**Conclusion.** The curve of minimum length is the straight line
$$\boxed{y = 7 - x},$$
with minimum length $\boxed{4\sqrt{2}}$.
