Proof that $GL_n(F)$ is non-abelian for $n \geq 2$ and any field $F$.

Recall $GL_n(F) = {A \in M_n(F) : \det A \neq 0}$, the group of invertible $n
\times n$ matrices over $F$, with group operation matrix multiplication.

To show $GL_n(F)$ is non-abelian, it suffices to exhibit two elements $A, B \in
GL_n(F)$ with $AB \neq BA$.

Constructing the two matrices.

Since $n \geq 2$, the field $F$ is nontrivial (it contains $0$ and $1$ with $0
\neq 1$), so $1 \in F$ is available. Consider the elementary matrices:

$$A = E_{12}(1) = I_n + e_1 e_2^\top, \qquad B = E_{21}(1) = I_n + e_2 e_1^\top,
$$

where $e_i$ is the $i$-th standard basis column vector. Explicitly, $A$ is the
identity with an extra $1$ in position $(1,2)$, and $B$ is the identity with an
extra $1$ in position $(2,1)$:

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 & \cdots \ 0 & 1 & 0 & \cdots \ 0 & 0 & 1 &
\cdots \ \vdots & & & \ddots \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0
& \cdots \ 1 & 1 & 0 & \cdots \ 0 & 0 & 1 & \cdots \ \vdots & & & \ddots
\end{pmatrix}.$$

Both are invertible. Each is an elementary matrix (a shear), obtained from $I_n$
by adding one row to another. Such operations are invertible (add the row back),
so $A, B \in GL_n(F)$. Concretely, $A^{-1} = I_n - e_1 e_2^\top$ and $B^{-1} =
I_n - e_2 e_1^\top$, confirming $\det A = \det B = 1 \neq 0$.

Computing the products. Using $(e_i e_j^\top)(e_k e_l^\top) = \delta_{jk}, e_i
e_l^\top$:

$$AB = (I + e_1 e_2^\top)(I + e_2 e_1^\top) = I + e_1 e_2^\top + e_2 e_1^\top +
(e_1 e_2^\top)(e_2 e_1^\top).$$

The last term: $(e_1 e_2^\top)(e_2 e_1^\top) = \delta_{2,2}, e_1 e_1^\top = e_1
e_1^\top$. So

$$AB = I + e_1 e_2^\top + e_2 e_1^\top + e_1 e_1^\top.$$

Likewise,

$$BA = (I + e_2 e_1^\top)(I + e_1 e_2^\top) = I + e_2 e_1^\top + e_1 e_2^\top +
(e_2 e_1^\top)(e_1 e_2^\top).$$

The last term: $(e_2 e_1^\top)(e_1 e_2^\top) = \delta_{1,1}, e_2 e_2^\top = e_2
e_2^\top$. So

$$BA = I + e_1 e_2^\top + e_2 e_1^\top + e_2 e_2^\top.$$

$$AB - BA = e_1 e_1^\top - e_2 e_2^\top = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & \cdots \ 0 &
-1 & \cdots \ \vdots & & \ddots \end{pmatrix} \neq 0,$$

in distinct diagonal positions $(1,1)$ and $(2,2)$, and $1 \neq 0$ in $F$).

Therefore $AB \neq BA$, so $GL_n(F)$ is non-abelian. $\blacksquare$
