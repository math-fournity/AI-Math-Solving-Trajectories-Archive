## Solution

**Setup.** Let $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})$. Since $-7 \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$, the ri
ng of integers is
$$\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}\!\left[\tfrac{1+\sqrt{-7}}{2}\right],$$
and the (absolute) discriminant of $K$ is $D_K = -7$.

**Goal.** Determine the Hilbert class field $L$ of $K$ and the minimal polynomia
l over $K$ of a primitive element for $L$.

**Step 1 — Minkowski bound.** For an imaginary quadratic field of discriminant $
D$, the Minkowski bound is
$$M_K = \frac{2}{\pi}\sqrt{|D|}.$$
With $D = -7$:
$$M_K = \frac{2}{\pi}\sqrt{7} \approx \frac{2}{3.14159}\cdot 2.6458 \approx 1.68
4.$$

**Step 2 — Class number computation.** Every integral ideal of $\mathcal{O}_K$ i
s equivalent (in the class group) to an integral ideal of norm $\le M_K$. Since
$M_K < 2$, the only integral ideal with norm $\le 1.684$ is the unit ideal $\mat
hcal{O}_K$ itself (norm $1$). Hence every ideal class is the principal class:
$$\mathrm{Cl}(K) = \{1\}, \qquad h_K = 1.$$

(Equivalently, $-7$ is one of the classical Heegner discriminants, all of which
have class number $1$.)

**Step 3 — The Hilbert class field.** By definition, the Hilbert class field $L$
 of $K$ is the maximal unramified abelian extension of $K$, and class field theo
ry gives
$$[L:K] = h_K = 1.$$
Therefore $L = K$.

**Step 4 — Minimal polynomial of a primitive element.** Since $L = K$, the exten
sion $L/K$ is trivial of degree $1$. A primitive element is any element of $K$;
taking $\alpha = 0 \in K$, its minimal polynomial over $K$ is
$$\boxed{x}.$$
(More generally, for any $\alpha \in K$, the minimal polynomial is the linear po
lynomial $x - \alpha$; the canonical choice $\alpha = 0$ gives $x$.)
