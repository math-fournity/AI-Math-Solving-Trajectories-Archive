## Proof

**Step 1: Convergence.** Near $x=0$, since $1-\cos x = \frac{x^{2}}{2}+O(x^{4})$
, the integrand $\frac{1-\cos x}{x^{2}}$ tends to $\tfrac12$, so there is no sin
gularity. At infinity, $\frac{1-\cos x}{x^{2}}\le \frac{2}{x^{2}}$, which is int
egrable. Hence the integral converges absolutely, and the Cauchy principal value
 coincides with the ordinary value of the integral.

**Step 2: Reduction to the squared sinc integral.** Using $1-\cos x = 2\sin^{2}(
x/2)$,
$$
I:=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos x}{x^{2}}\,dx
   = 2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^{2}(x/2)}{x^{2}}\,dx.
$$
Set $u=x/2$, so $x=2u$ and $dx=2\,du$:
$$
I = 2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^{2}u}{4u^{2}}\cdot 2\,du
  = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^{2}u}{u^{2}}\,du.
$$

**Step 3: Fourier transform of $\dfrac{\sin u}{u}$.** Consider the rectangular f
unction $R(\omega)=\mathbf{1}_{[-1,1]}(\omega)$. Its inverse Fourier transform (
with the convention $\hat f(\omega)=\int f(t)e^{-i\omega t}\,dt$, $\check g(t)=\
frac{1}{2\pi}\int g(\omega)e^{i\omega t}\,d\omega$) is
$$
\check R(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{i\omega t}\,d\omega
          =\frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{e^{it}-e^{-it}}{it}
          =\frac{\sin t}{\pi t}.
$$
Thus $\frac{\sin t}{t}=\pi\,\check R(t)$, i.e. the Fourier transform of $f(t):=\
frac{\sin t}{t}$ is
$$
\hat f(\omega)=\pi\,\mathbf{1}_{[-1,1]}(\omega).
$$

**Step 4: Parseval's identity.** For $f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})$,
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(t)|^{2}\,dt=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\hat
 f(\omega)|^{2}\,d\omega.
$$
Applying this to $f(t)=\frac{\sin t}{t}$ and $\hat f(\omega)=\pi\,\mathbf{1}_{[-
1,1]}(\omega)$:
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^{2}t}{t^{2}}\,dt
   =\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\pi^{2}\,d\omega
   =\frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot 2\pi^{2}
   =\pi.
$$

**Step 5: Conclusion.** Combining Steps 2 and 4,
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos x}{x^{2}}\,dx
   =\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^{2}u}{u^{2}}\,du
   =\pi.
$$

Since the integral converges absolutely, the Cauchy principal value equals this
value:

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{PV}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos x}{x^{2}}\,dx=\pi.}
$$
