## Solution

**Parsing the equivalence relation.** For $f, g \in \mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}}$, we
 have $f \equiv g$ if and only if, for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$:

1. $f(2n) = g(2n)$ — the values at all even-indexed positions are **exactly equa
l**.
2. $f(n) \cdot g(n) > 0$ or $f(n) = 0 = g(n)$ — at every position, $f$ and $g$ h
ave the **same sign** (both positive, both negative, or both zero).

**Verifying this is an equivalence relation.**

- *Reflexivity:* $f(2n)=f(2n)$ and $f(n)^2 > 0$ when $f(n)\neq 0$, else $f(n)=0=
f(n)$. ✓
- *Symmetry:* Both conditions are symmetric in $f,g$. ✓
- *Transitivity:* If $f\equiv g$ and $g\equiv h$, then $f(2n)=g(2n)=h(2n)$, and
at each $n$ the signs of $f(n),g(n),h(n)$ all agree. ✓

**Characterizing an equivalence class.** Condition (1) pins down the exact integ
er value at every even position. Condition (2), applied at odd positions (since
even positions are already pinned exactly), pins down only the **sign** at each
odd position. Therefore the equivalence class of $f$ is completely determined by
:

- The sequence of values at even positions: $(f(0), f(2), f(4), \dots) \in \math
bb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}}$.
- The sequence of signs at odd positions: $(\operatorname{sgn} f(1), \operatorna
me{sgn} f(3), \operatorname{sgn} f(5), \dots) \in \{-1, 0, +1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$.

Two functions are equivalent if and only if these two data agree. Hence the quot
ient set is in bijection with

$$\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}} \times \{-1, 0, +1\}^{\mathbb{N}}.$$

**Computing the cardinality.**

- The even positions $\{0, 2, 4, \dots\}$ and odd positions $\{1, 3, 5, \dots\}$
 are each countably infinite (indexed by $\mathbb{N}$).
- $|\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}}| = \aleph_0^{\aleph_0}$. Since $2 \le \aleph_0 \le 2
^{\aleph_0}$:
$$2^{\aleph_0} \le \aleph_0^{\aleph_0} \le (2^{\aleph_0})^{\aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph
_0 \cdot \aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_0},$$
so $|\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}}| = 2^{\aleph_0}$.
- $|\{-1,0,+1\}^{\mathbb{N}}| = 3^{\aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_0}$ by the same squeezi
ng argument.

Therefore:

$$\left|\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}}/{\equiv}\right| = 2^{\aleph_0} \times 2^{\aleph_
0} = 2^{\aleph_0 + \aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_0}.$$

**Consistency check.** The quotient cannot exceed $|\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}}| = 2
^{\aleph_0}$ (upper bound). The even-position values alone already yield $|\math
bb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}}| = 2^{\aleph_0}$ distinct classes (lower bound). Both bounds
equal $2^{\aleph_0}$, confirming the result.

**Answer.** The power of the quotient set is $\boxed{2^{\aleph_0}}$ — the cardin
ality of the continuum, $\mathfrak{c}$.
