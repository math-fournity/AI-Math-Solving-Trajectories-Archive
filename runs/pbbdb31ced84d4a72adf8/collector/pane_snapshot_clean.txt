## Theorem

For every integer $n \geq 2$, there exists a Pythagorean $n$-tuple, i.e., $n$ po
sitive integers $a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_n$ satisfying
$$a_1^2 + a_2^2 + \cdots + a_{n-1}^2 = a_n^2.$$
For $n \geq 3$, non-trivial (non-degenerate) tuples exist.

## Proof

We give explicit constructions covering all cases.

**Case $n = 2$:** The tuple $(1, 1)$ satisfies $1^2 = 1^2$. (This is the unique
degenerate form, since $a^2 = b^2$ forces $a = b$.)

**Case $n = 3$:** The tuple $(3, 4, 5)$ satisfies $3^2 + 4^2 = 9 + 16 = 25 = 5^2
$.

**Case $n$ odd, $n \geq 5$:** Define the $n$-tuple
$$\bigl(\underbrace{1, 1, \ldots, 1}_{n-2 \text{ ones}},\; \tfrac{n-3}{2},\; \tf
rac{n-1}{2}\bigr).$$
Since $n$ is odd and $n \geq 5$, the values $\frac{n-3}{2}$ and $\frac{n-1}{2}$
are positive integers (with $\frac{n-3}{2} \geq 1$). The left-hand side has $n -
 1$ terms. We verify:
$$\sum_{i=1}^{n-1} a_i^2 = (n-2)\cdot 1^2 + \left(\frac{n-3}{2}\right)^2 = (n-2)
 + \frac{(n-3)^2}{4} = \frac{4(n-2) + (n-3)^2}{4} = \frac{4n - 8 + n^2 - 6n + 9}
{4} = \frac{n^2 - 2n + 1}{4} = \left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)^2 = a_n^2. \quad\check
mark$$

**Case $n$ even, $n \geq 4$:** Define the $n$-tuple
$$\bigl(\underbrace{1, 1, \ldots, 1}_{n-3 \text{ ones}},\; 2,\; \tfrac{n}{2},\;
\tfrac{n}{2}+1\bigr).$$
Since $n$ is even and $n \geq 4$, the value $\frac{n}{2}$ is a positive integer
with $\frac{n}{2} \geq 2$, and $n - 3 \geq 1$ so there is at least one $1$. The

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n-1} a_i^2 = (n-3)\cdot 1^2 + 2^2 + \left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^2 = (
n-3) + 4 + \frac{n^2}{4} = n + 1 + \frac{n^2}{4} = \frac{n^2 + 4n + 4}{4} = \lef
t(\frac{n+2}{2}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)^2 = a_n^2. \quad\checkmark
$$

Pythagorean triple $(p, q, r)$, the $(n+1)$-tuple
$$(p\,a_1,\; \ldots,\; p\,a_{n-1},\; q\,a_n,\; r\,a_n)$$

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(p\,a_i)^2 + (q\,a_n)^2 = p^2\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_i^2
