## Derivation of the General Solution to the Linearized Einstein Equation

### 1. Setup and gauge

In the weak-field limit we write the metric as
$$g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}, \qquad |h_{\mu\nu}| \ll 1,$$
and introduce the **trace-reversed perturbation**
$$\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu} \;=\; h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac{1}{2}\,\eta_{\mu\nu}\,h, \qquad h
 \;=\; \eta^{\alpha\beta}h_{\alpha\beta},$$
so that $h = -\tilde{h}$ with $\tilde h = \eta^{\alpha\beta}\tilde h_{\alpha\bet
a}$. Imposing the **Lorenz (harmonic) gauge**
$$\partial^\mu \tilde h_{\mu\nu} = 0,$$
the linearized Einstein equation reduces to the inhomogeneous wave equation
$$\Box\,\tilde h_{\mu\nu}(x) \;=\; -16\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}(x), \tag{1}$$
where $\Box = \eta^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\alpha\partial_\beta = -\partial_t^2 +
\nabla^2$ (signature $(-,+,+,+)$, units $c=1$).

### 2. The Green function of the d'Alembertian

Let $G(x-y)$ be a Green function of $\Box$, i.e.
$$\Box_x\, G(x-y) \;=\; \delta^{(4)}(x-y) \;=\; \delta(x^0-y^0)\,\delta^{(3)}(\m
athbf{x}-\mathbf{y}). \tag{2}$$
The general (distributional) solution of (2) is a linear combination of the **re
tarded** and **advanced** Green functions. Causality selects the retarded one:
$$\boxed{\,G_{\mathrm{ret}}(x-y) \;=\; -\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\Theta(x^0-y^0)\,\delta\
!\big((x-y)^2\big)\,} \tag{3}$$
where $(x-y)^2 = \eta_{\alpha\beta}(x-y)^\alpha(x-y)^\beta = -(x^0-y^0)^2 + |\ma
thbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|^2$. One may verify (3) satisfies (2): the $\Theta$ enforces
retarded support, and the on-light-cone delta is the characteristic kernel of th
e wave operator in 4D.

### 3. Convolution gives the general solution

Because (1) is linear with constant-coefficient operator $\Box$, the general sol
ution is the sum of a particular solution (convolution of the source with the Gr
een function) and the general solution of the homogeneous equation:
$$\tilde h_{\mu\nu}(x) \;=\; \tilde h_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{(hom)}}(x) \;-\; 16\pi G
 \int d^4y\; G_{\mathrm{ret}}(x-y)\,T_{\mu\nu}(y). \tag{4}$$
Here $\tilde h_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{(hom)}}$ is any solution of $\Box\,\tilde h_{\m
u\nu}^{\mathrm{(hom)}}=0$ subject to the Lorenz gauge $\partial^\mu\tilde h_{\mu
\nu}^{\mathrm{(hom)}}=0$; it represents free gravitational radiation incident fr
om past null infinity. Equation (4) **is the general solution** in Green-functio
n form.

### 4. Reduction to the retarded-potential (spatial) form

Substitute (3) into (4) and drop the homogeneous piece (assuming no incoming rad

$$\tilde h_{\mu\nu}(x) \;=\; -16\pi G\!\int d^4y\,\Big[-\frac{1}{2\pi}\Theta(x^0
-y^0)\,\delta\!\big((x-y)^2\big)\Big]\,T_{\mu\nu}(y)
\;=\; 8G\!\int d^4y\,\Theta(x^0-y^0)\,\delta\!\big((x-y)^2\big)\,T_{\mu\nu}(y).
\tag{5}$$

Write $x^0=t$, $y^0=t'$, $\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}$, and use
$$(x-y)^2 = -(t-t')^2 + |\mathbf{r}|^2,\qquad \delta\!\big((t-t')^2-|\mathbf{r}|
^2\big) = \frac{\delta\!\big(t'-[t-|\mathbf{r}|]\big)+\delta\!\big(t'-[t+|\mathb
f{r}|]\big)}{2|\mathbf{r}|}.$$
The step function $\Theta(t-t')$ kills the advanced root $t'=t+|\mathbf{r}|$ and
 keeps the retarded root $t' = t - |\mathbf{r}|$. Performing the $dt'$ integrati

$$\tilde h_{\mu\nu}(t,\mathbf{x}) \;=\; 8G\int d^3\mathbf{y}\,\frac{1}{2|\mathbf
{x}-\mathbf{y}|}\,T_{\mu\nu}\!\big(t-|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|,\,\mathbf{y}\big).$
$

Hence the **retarded solution** is
$$\boxed{\,\tilde h_{\mu\nu}(t,\mathbf{x}) \;=\; 4G\int d^3\mathbf{y}\;\frac{T_{
\mu\nu}\!\big(t_{\mathrm{ret}},\,\mathbf{y}\big)}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|},\qqua
d t_{\mathrm{ret}} \;=\; t - |\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|\,}. \tag{6}$$

This is the gravitational analogue of the retarded Liénard–Wiechert potential: t
he field at $(t,\mathbf{x})$ is determined by the source on the past light cone,

### 5. Recovering the metric perturbation

From the trace-reversed definition, $h_{\mu\nu} = \tilde h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\e

Therefore
$$\boxed{\,h_{\mu\nu}(t,\mathbf{x}) \;=\; 4G\int d^3\mathbf{y}\;\frac{T_{\mu\nu}

### 6. Consistency checks

- **Gauge preservation.** Differentiating (6): $\partial^\mu\tilde h_{\mu\nu}$ p
ulls $\partial^\mu$ onto $T_{\mu\nu}$ inside the integral. Using $\partial^\mu T
