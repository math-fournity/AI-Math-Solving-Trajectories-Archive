## Proof

**Step 1: Decompose the integral.** For integer $n \geq 1$, substitute $x = k +
t$ on each unit interval $[k, k+1]$, $0 \leq t \leq 1$:

$$I(n) := \int_0^n |\sin(\pi x)|^x\,dx = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} J_k, \qquad J_k := \in
t_0^1 \sin(\pi t)^{k+t}\,dt,$$

using $\sin(\pi t) \geq 0$ on $[0,1]$.

**Step 2: Asymptotic of $J_k$.** Write
$$J_k = \int_0^1 \sin(\pi t)^k \cdot \varphi(t)\,dt, \qquad \varphi(t) := \sin(\
pi t)^t = e^{t\ln\sin(\pi t)}.$$

The function $\varphi$ extends continuously to $[0,1]$ with $\varphi(0) = \lim_{
t\to 0^+} e^{t\ln\sin(\pi t)} = 1$, $\varphi(1/2) = 1$, $\varphi(1) = 0$, and $0
 \leq \varphi \leq 1$.

The weight $\sin(\pi t)^k$ attains its unique maximum $1$ at $t = 1/2$, and conc
entrates there as $k\to\infty$. By the standard Laplace method (or the Watson-ty
pe lemma: a continuous function at the peak point factors out of the leading asy
mptotics),

$$J_k \sim \varphi(1/2)\cdot \int_0^1 \sin(\pi t)^k\,dt = \int_0^1 \sin(\pi t)^k
\,dt.$$

The classical beta-integral gives
$$\int_0^1 \sin(\pi t)^k\,dt = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{k+1
}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{k+2}{2}\right)} \sim \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi k}} \qu
ad (k\to\infty)$$

by Stirling's formula. Therefore
$$J_k \sim \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi k}}. \tag{1}$$

**Step 3: Asymptotic of $I(n)$.** From (1) and $\sum_{k=1}^{n} k^{-1/2} \sim 2\s
qrt{n}$:
$$I(n) = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} J_k \sim \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \frac{1}
{\sqrt{k}} \sim 2\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,\sqrt{n}. \tag{2}$$

**Step 4: Limit comparison.** From (2),
$$a_n = \frac{I(n)}{n^p} \sim 2\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\, n^{1/2 - p}, \qquad \text{
with constant } C := 2\sqrt{2/\pi} > 0.$$

By the limit comparison test ($a_n / n^{1/2-p} \to C \in (0,\infty)$), the serie
s $\sum a_n$ converges if and only if $\sum n^{1/2-p}$ converges, i.e., if and o
nly if
$$\frac{1}{2} - p < -1 \iff p > \frac{3}{2}.$$

At the boundary $p = 3/2$: $a_n \sim C\, n^{-1}$, giving the divergent harmonic
series.

---

**Conclusion.** The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$ converges if and
 only if $\boxed{p > \dfrac{3}{2}}$.
