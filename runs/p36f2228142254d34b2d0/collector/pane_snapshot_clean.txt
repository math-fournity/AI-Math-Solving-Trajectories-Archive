## Proof

**Setup and convention.** Let the Fourier transform be defined by

$$g(w) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,e^{-iwx}\,dx.$$

Differentiating under the integral sign twice with respect to $w$ (valid wheneve
r $f$ decays fast enough that $x^2 f(x)$ is integrable, which is precisely the c
ondition needed for the moment to exist):

$$g''(w) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,\frac{d^2}{dw^2}e^{-iwx}\,dx = \int_{-\
infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,(-ix)^2\,e^{-iwx}\,dx = -\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x^2 f(x)\
,e^{-iwx}\,dx.$$

Evaluating at $w=0$:

$$g''(0) = -\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x^2 f(x)\,dx.$$

**Extracting the second derivative from the given approximation.** We are told t
hat for small $w$,

$$g(w) \approx 1 - w^2.$$

The Taylor expansion of $g$ about $w=0$ is

$$g(w) = g(0) + g'(0)\,w + \tfrac{1}{2}g''(0)\,w^2 + \cdots.$$

Matching term-by-term with $1 - w^2$:

- $g(0) = 1$,
- $g'(0) = 0$,
- $\tfrac{1}{2}g''(0) = -1$, so $g''(0) = -2$.

**Computing the moment.** Substituting into the relation above:

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x^2 f(x)\,dx = -g''(0) = -(-2) = 2.$$

**Conclusion.** Yes, the value can be calculated. The small-$w$ behavior $g(w)\a
pprox 1-w^2$ fixes the Taylor coefficients of $g$ at the origin, and the second
moment of $f$ is determined by $g''(0)$ via the Fourier moment identity. The val
ue is

$$\boxed{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x^2 f(x)\,dx = 2}.$$
