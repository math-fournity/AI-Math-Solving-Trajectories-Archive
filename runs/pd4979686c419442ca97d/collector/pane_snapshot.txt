## Proof

We compute the integral of the 2-form
$$\omega = \frac{x\,dy\wedge dz + y\,dz\wedge dx + z\,dx\wedge dy}{(x^2+y^2+z^2)
^{3/2}}$$
over the unit sphere $S^2$ using the parametrization $f(u,v)=(\sin u\cos v,\;\si
n u\sin v,\;\cos u)$, $(u,v)\in[0,\pi]\times[0,2\pi]$.

**Step 1: Evaluate the denominator on the sphere.**

On the unit sphere, $x^2+y^2+z^2 = \sin^2 u\cos^2 v + \sin^2 u\sin^2 v + \cos^2
u = \sin^2 u + \cos^2 u = 1$. Hence the denominator $(x^2+y^2+z^2)^{3/2}=1$, and
 we integrate the numerator form
$$\tilde\omega = x\,dy\wedge dz + y\,dz\wedge dx + z\,dx\wedge dy.$$

**Step 2: Compute the partial derivatives of $f$.**

$$f_u = (\cos u\cos v,\;\cos u\sin v,\;-\sin u),$$
$$f_v = (-\sin u\sin v,\;\sin u\cos v,\;0).$$

**Step 3: Compute the pullback $f^*\tilde\omega$.**

The pullback of $\tilde\omega$ is
$$f^*\tilde\omega = \Big[x\,\frac{\partial(y,z)}{\partial(u,v)} + y\,\frac{\part
ial(z,x)}{\partial(u,v)} + z\,\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)}\Big]\,du\wedge
 dv.$$

Computing each Jacobian determinant:

$$\frac{\partial(y,z)}{\partial(u,v)} = \begin{vmatrix}\cos u\sin v & \sin u\cos
 v\\ -\sin u & 0\end{vmatrix} = 0 - (-\sin^2 u\cos v) = \sin^2 u\cos v.$$

$$\frac{\partial(z,x)}{\partial(u,v)} = \begin{vmatrix}-\sin u & 0\\ \cos u\cos
v & -\sin u\sin v\end{vmatrix} = \sin^2 u\sin v - 0 = \sin^2 u\sin v.$$

$$\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)} = \begin{vmatrix}\cos u\cos v & -\sin u\si
n v\\ \cos u\sin v & \sin u\cos v\end{vmatrix} = \sin u\cos u\cos^2 v + \sin u\c
os u\sin^2 v = \sin u\cos u.$$

**Step 4: Substitute $x=\sin u\cos v$, $y=\sin u\sin v$, $z=\cos u$.**

$$f^*\tilde\omega = \big[\sin u\cos v\cdot\sin^2 u\cos v + \sin u\sin v\cdot\sin
^2 u\sin v + \cos u\cdot\sin u\cos u\big]\,du\wedge dv$$
$$= \big[\sin^3 u\cos^2 v + \sin^3 u\sin^2 v + \sin u\cos^2 u\big]\,du\wedge dv$
$
$$= \big[\sin^3 u(\cos^2 v+\sin^2 v) + \sin u\cos^2 u\big]\,du\wedge dv$$
$$= \big[\sin^3 u + \sin u\cos^2 u\big]\,du\wedge dv$$
$$= \sin u\,(\sin^2 u+\cos^2 u)\,du\wedge dv = \sin u\,du\wedge dv.$$

**Step 5: Integrate.**

$$\int_{S^2}\omega = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{\pi} \sin u\,du\,dv = \left(\int_0
^{\pi}\sin u\,du\right)\!\left(\int_0^{2\pi}dv\right) = \big[-\cos u\big]_0^{\pi
}\cdot 2\pi = (1+1)\cdot 2\pi = 4\pi.$$

**Result.** The integral of $\omega$ over the unit sphere $S^2$ is

$$\boxed{4\pi}.$$
