## Proof (by counterexample)

Take $m = 1$ (the scalar case). For each $k \geq 2$, define the $k \times 1$ mat
rix $X(k)$ with entries:

$$x_i(k) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } 1 \leq i \leq k-1, \\ \sqrt{k} & \text{i
f } i = k. \end{cases}$$

**Step 1: Verify the convergence condition.**

$$\frac{X'(k)X(k)}{k} = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k} x_i(k)^2 = \frac{(k-1)\cdot 1
+ (\sqrt{k})^2}{k} = \frac{(k-1) + k}{k} = \frac{2k - 1}{k} \xrightarrow{k \to \
infty} 2.$$

Thus $R = 2$, which is positive definite (hence positive semi-definite). The hyp
othesis is satisfied. ✓

**Step 2: Compute the quantity of interest.**

Since $m=1$, we have $X'(k)X(k) = \sum_{j=1}^k x_j(k)^2 = 2k - 1$, and

$$x_i(k)'\bigl(X'(k)X(k)\bigr)^{-1} x_i(k) = \frac{x_i(k)^2}{2k - 1}.$$

The maximum over $i = 1, \ldots, k$ is achieved at $i = k$ (where $x_k(k)^2 = k$
):

$$\max_{i=1,\ldots,k}\; \frac{x_i(k)^2}{2k-1} = \frac{k}{2k - 1} \xrightarrow{k
\to \infty} \frac{1}{2} \neq 0.$$

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

We have constructed a sequence of matrices satisfying $\frac{X'(k)X(k)}{k} \to R
$ with $R$ positive definite, yet

$$\max_{i=1,\ldots,k}\, x_i'(X'(k)X(k))^{-1}x_i \;\longrightarrow\; \frac{1}{2}
\neq 0.$$

Therefore the statement **does not hold** in general. The condition $\frac{X'(k)
X(k)}{k} \to R$ (PSD) alone is insufficient to guarantee that the maximum levera
ge tends to zero; additional regularity conditions (such as bounded regressors o
r moment conditions on the $x_i$) would be required. $\blacksquare$
