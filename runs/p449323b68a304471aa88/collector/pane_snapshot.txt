## Proof

Let $\nu$ be the reflection of $\mu$, i.e. the finite Borel measure defined by $
\nu(A)=\mu(-A)$ for every Borel set $A$. Then for any bounded integrable $f$,

$$
\int_{\mathbb R} f(x+t)\,\mu(dt)=\int_{\mathbb R} f(x-s)\,\nu(ds)=(f*\nu)(x),
$$

so the hypothesis says

$$
f = f*\nu \quad\text{a.e.}\tag{1}
$$

for every real-valued bounded integrable $f$.

**Step 1: Pass to Fourier transforms.** Take $f$ to be any real-valued Schwartz
function. Such $f$ is bounded and integrable, so (1) holds. Since $f\in L^1$ and
 $\nu$ is a finite measure, $f*\nu\in L^1$ with $\|f*\nu\|_1\le \|f\|_1\,\nu(\ma
thbb R)$, and (1) holds as an $L^1$ identity. Taking Fourier transforms (using t
he convention $\hat g(\xi)=\int e^{-i\xi x}g(x)\,dx$) gives

$$
\hat f(\xi)=\hat f(\xi)\,\hat\nu(\xi),\qquad \forall \xi\in\mathbb R.\tag{2}
$$

**Step 2: Determine $\hat\nu$.** For any fixed $\xi_0\in\mathbb R$, choose a rea
l-valued Schwartz function $f$ with $\hat f(\xi_0)\ne 0$ (e.g. a Gaussian times
a cosine, whose transform is a sum of two shifted Gaussians, nonzero at any pres
cribed point). Then (2) at $\xi=\xi_0$ forces

$$
\hat\nu(\xi_0)=1.
$$

Since $\xi_0$ was arbitrary,

$$
\hat\nu(\xi)=1\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb R.\tag{3}
$$

**Step 3: Identify $\mu$.** Compute $\hat\nu$ in terms of $\mu$:

$$
\hat\nu(\xi)=\int e^{-i\xi s}\,d\nu(s)=\int e^{-i\xi(-t)}\,\mu(dt)=\int e^{i\xi
t}\,\mu(dt)=:\varphi_\mu(\xi),
$$

the Fourier–Stieltjes (characteristic) transform of $\mu$. By (3),

$$
\varphi_\mu(\xi)=\int e^{i\xi t}\,\mu(dt)=1\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb R.\
tag{4}
$$

But the Dirac mass $\delta_0$ satisfies

$$
\int e^{i\xi t}\,d\delta_0(t)=e^{i\xi\cdot 0}=1\quad\text{for all }\xi.
$$

The Fourier–Stieltjes transform is injective on finite Borel measures (a standar
d consequence of Stone–Weierstrass: the algebra generated by $\{e^{i\xi t}:\xi\i
n\mathbb R\}$ is dense in $C_0(\mathbb R)$, so equality of transforms forces equ
ality of measures). Hence (4) implies

$$
\mu=\delta_0.
$$

Therefore $\mu(\{0\})=\delta_0(\{0\})=1$. $\blacksquare$
