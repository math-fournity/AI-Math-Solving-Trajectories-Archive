## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $P(z) = z^n$.

Let $P(z) = z^n + a_{n-1}z^{n-1} + \cdots + a_0$ be monic of degree $n$, with $|
P(z)| \le 1$ for all $|z| \le 1$. In particular, $|P(z)| \le 1$ on the unit circ
le $|z| = 1$.

**Coefficient recovery via Fourier integrals.** For each $k$ with $0 \le k \le n
$,

$$a_k = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} P(e^{i\theta})\, e^{-ik\theta}\, d\theta,$$

since $P(e^{i\theta}) = \sum_{j=0}^n a_j e^{ij\theta}$ and the $e^{ij\theta}$ ar
e orthogonal on $[0,2\pi]$.

**Bounding the leading coefficient.** Applying the triangle inequality for integ
rals:

$$|a_n| \le \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \big|P(e^{i\theta})\big|\, d\theta \le \
frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} 1\, d\theta = 1,$$

where we used $|P(e^{i\theta})| \le 1$ on the unit circle. Since $P$ is monic, $
|a_n| = 1$. Therefore both inequalities above are equalities:

1. $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} |P(e^{i\theta})|\, d\theta = 1$, with $|P(e^{i\t
heta})| \le 1$ everywhere. By continuity, $|P(e^{i\theta})| = 1$ for **all** $\t
heta \in [0, 2\pi]$.

2. Equality $|\int f| = \int |f|$ holds in the triangle inequality for integrals
 if and only if the integrand $f(\theta) = P(e^{i\theta})\,e^{-in\theta}$ has co
nstant argument throughout the domain. That is, there exists a fixed $\alpha \in
 \mathbb{R}$ such that

$$P(e^{i\theta})\, e^{-in\theta} = e^{i\alpha}, \qquad \forall\, \theta \in [0,
2\pi].$$

(Here we used that $|P(e^{i\theta})| = 1$, so the modulus of the integrand is id
entically $1$.)

**Conclusion.** The relation above gives

$$P(e^{i\theta}) = e^{i\alpha}\, e^{in\theta} = e^{i\alpha}(e^{i\theta})^n, \qqu
ad \forall\, \theta.$$

Thus $P(z) = e^{i\alpha} z^n$ on the unit circle. Since $P(z) - e^{i\alpha}z^n$
is a polynomial of degree at most $n$ that vanishes on the infinitely many point
s of the unit circle, it is identically zero:

$$P(z) = e^{i\alpha}\, z^n.$$

The monic condition $a_n = 1$ forces $e^{i\alpha} = 1$, so

$$\boxed{P(z) = z^n.}$$

Therefore the answer is **yes**: $P(z) = z^n$ is the unique monic polynomial of
degree $n$ satisfying $|P(z)| \le 1$ for all $|z| \le 1$.

### PROOF COMPLETE
