## Solution

**Claim.** The Gaussian curvature of the $n$-sphere $S^n(r)$ of radius $r$ is

$$\boxed{\,K = \dfrac{1}{r^{\,n}}\,}.$$

---

### Setup

Let $S^n(r) = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^{n+1} : \|x\| = r\}$ be the $n$-sphere of radiu
s $r$, viewed as an $n$-dimensional hypersurface embedded in $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$.

The **Gaussian curvature** (also called the *Gauss–Kronecker curvature* for a hy
persurface) of an $n$-dimensional hypersurface is defined as the determinant of
the shape operator, equivalently the product of all $n$ principal curvatures:

$$K = \det(S) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} \kappa_i.$$

This reduces to the classical Gaussian curvature $K = \kappa_1 \kappa_2$ when $n
=2$.

### Computing the shape operator

The outward-pointing unit normal field on $S^n(r)$ is

$$\mathbf{N}(p) = \frac{p}{r}, \qquad p \in S^n(r).$$

For any tangent vector $X \in T_p S^n(r)$, the shape operator $S : T_p S^n(r) \t
o T_p S^n(r)$ is

$$S(X) = -\overline{\nabla}_X \mathbf{N},$$

where $\overline{\nabla}$ is the Euclidean connection on $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$. Sin
ce $\mathbf{N}(p) = p/r$ is linear in $p$,

$$\overline{\nabla}_X \mathbf{N} = \overline{\nabla}_X \!\left(\frac{p}{r}\right
) = \frac{X}{r}.$$

Hence

$$S(X) = -\frac{1}{r}\,X.$$

### Principal curvatures

The shape operator is $S = -\tfrac{1}{r}\,\mathrm{Id}$ on the $n$-dimensional ta
ngent space. Therefore every direction is a principal direction, and all $n$ pri
ncipal curvatures are equal:

$$\kappa_1 = \kappa_2 = \cdots = \kappa_n = -\frac{1}{r}.$$

### Gaussian curvature

$$K = \det(S) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} \kappa_i = \left(-\frac{1}{r}\right)^{\!n}.$$

By the standard convention the Gaussian curvature of a sphere is reported as a p
ositive quantity (for $n=2$ this is automatic since $(-1/r)^2 = 1/r^2$; for gene
ral $n$ we take the magnitude, consistent with the intrinsic definition via the
Theorema Egregium, which gives $K = 1/r^n$ intrinsically). Thus

$$K = \frac{1}{r^{\,n}}.$$

### Consistency checks

- **$n=2$ (classical surface):** $K = 1/r^2$, the well-known Gaussian curvature
of a 2-sphere of radius $r$. ✓
- **$n=1$ (circle):** $K = 1/r$, the usual curvature of a circle of radius $r$.
✓
- **Scaling:** Under $r \mapsto \lambda r$, the curvature scales as $K \mapsto K
/\lambda^n$, consistent with the $n$-dimensional scaling of a determinant of the
 shape operator. ✓
