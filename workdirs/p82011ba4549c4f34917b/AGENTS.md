# Problem

In a bustling logistical hub, there are $n$ shipping bays and $n$ departure docks ($n > 1$). The hub operates on a strict schedule where each bay is assigned to exactly one dock, forming an $n \times n$ grid of possible connection pairs.

A total of $k$ specific cargo containers are currently staged across various connection pairs in the grid. A logistics manager is tasked with reorganizing the facility by assigning a new priority sequence to the shipping bays (permuting the rows) and a new priority sequence to the departure docks (permuting the columns). 

The goal of this reorganization is to ensure that every one of the $k$ containers ends up in a position $(r, c)$ where the bay's priority index $r$ is less than or equal to the dock's priority index $c$ (mapping them to the upper-triangular portion of the grid, including the main diagonal).

What is the maximum number of containers $k$ for which, regardless of their initial distribution across the $n \times n$ pairs, such a reorganization is always guaranteed to be possible?
