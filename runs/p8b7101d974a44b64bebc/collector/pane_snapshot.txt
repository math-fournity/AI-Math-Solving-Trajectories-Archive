## Solution

**Problem.** Find the transverse vibrations of a rectangular membrane $0\le x\le
 l_1,\;0\le y\le l_2$, fixed around its edge, produced by a transverse concentra
ted impulse $K$ applied at $(x_0,y_0)$, with $0<x_0<l_1,\;0<y_0<l_2$.

### 2. Mathematical model

Let $u(x,y,t)$ be the transverse displacement. A fixed-edge membrane satisfies t
he two-dimensional wave equation

$$u_{tt}=a^{2}\bigl(u_{xx}+u_{yy}\bigr),\qquad 0<x<l_1,\;0<y<l_2,\;t>0,$$

with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions

$$u(0,y,t)=u(l_1,y,t)=u(x,0,t)=u(x,l_2,t)=0,$$

and initial conditions

$$u(x,y,0)=0,\qquad u_t(x,y,0)=K\,\delta(x-x_0)\,\delta(y-y_0).$$

The first condition says the membrane starts from rest (zero displacement); the
second encodes a *concentrated transverse impulse* of total momentum $K$ deliver
ed at $(x_0,y_0)$ at $t=0$. Indeed, integrating the momentum density $\rho\,u_t$
 (with surface density $\rho=1$ in the normalized equation) over the whole membr
ane gives

$$\iint u_t(x,y,0)\,dx\,dy = K\iint\delta(x-x_0)\delta(y-y_0)\,dx\,dy = K,$$

so $K$ is exactly the total transverse impulse.

---

### 3. Eigenfunctions and eigenvalues of the fixed rectangle

Separating variables $u=X(x)Y(y)T(t)$ with the boundary conditions gives the com
plete orthonormal (in $L^2$) system

$$\varphi_{mn}(x,y)=\sin\frac{m\pi x}{l_1}\,\sin\frac{n\pi y}{l_2},\qquad m,n=1,
2,3,\dots$$

with spatial eigenvalues

$$\lambda_{mn}=\pi^{2}\!\left(\frac{m^{2}}{l_1^{2}}+\frac{n^{2}}{l_2^{2}}\right)
$$

and temporal frequencies

$$\omega_{mn}=a\pi\sqrt{\frac{m^{2}}{l_1^{2}}+\frac{n^{2}}{l_2^{2}}}=a\sqrt{\lam
bda_{mn}}.$$

The orthogonality relation is

$$\int_0^{l_1}\!\!\int_0^{l_2}\varphi_{mn}(x,y)\,\varphi_{m'n'}(x,y)\,dx\,dy
=\frac{l_1 l_2}{4}\,\delta_{mm'}\delta_{nn'}.$$

---

### 4. Expansion of the solution

Expand

$$u(x,y,t)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
T_{mn}(t)\,\varphi_{mn}(x,y).$$

Substituting into the wave equation yields $T_{mn}''+\omega_{mn}^{2}T_{mn}=0$, h
ence

$$T_{mn}(t)=A_{mn}\cos\omega_{mn}t+B_{mn}\sin\omega_{mn}t.$$

**From $u(x,y,0)=0$:** $\sum A_{mn}\varphi_{mn}=0$, so $A_{mn}=0$ for all $m,n$.

**From $u_t(x,y,0)=K\delta(x-x_0)\delta(y-y_0)$:**

$$\sum_{m,n}B_{mn}\,\omega_{mn}\,\varphi_{mn}(x,y)=K\,\delta(x-x_0)\delta(y-y_0)
.$$

Multiply both sides by $\varphi_{m'n'}(x,y)$ and integrate over the rectangle. U
sing the orthogonality relation and the sifting property of the delta distributi
on,

$$B_{mn}\,\omega_{mn}\,\frac{l_1 l_2}{4}=K\,\varphi_{mn}(x_0,y_0)
=K\sin\frac{m\pi x_0}{l_1}\sin\frac{n\pi y_0}{l_2}.$$

Therefore

$$B_{mn}=\frac{4K}{l_1 l_2\,\omega_{mn}}\,
\sin\frac{m\pi x_0}{l_1}\,\sin\frac{n\pi y_0}{l_2}.$$

---

### 5. Result

Substituting $A_{mn}=0$ and the expression for $B_{mn}$, the transverse vibratio
ns of the membrane are

$$\boxed{\;u(x,y,t)=\frac{4K}{l_1 l_2}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\frac{\displaystyle\sin\frac{m\pi x_0}{l_1}\,\sin\frac{n\pi y_0}{l_2}\,
\sin\frac{m\pi x}{l_1}\,\sin\frac{n\pi y}{l_2}}
{\displaystyle a\pi\sqrt{\frac{m^{2}}{l_1^{2}}+\frac{n^{2}}{l_2^{2}}}}\,
\sin\!\left(a\pi\sqrt{\frac{m^{2}}{l_1^{2}}+\frac{n^{2}}{l_2^{2}}}\;t\right)\;}$
$

---

### 6. Verification of the properties

- **Boundary conditions.** Every term contains $\sin\frac{m\pi x}{l_1}\sin\frac{
n\pi y}{l_2}$, which vanishes on $x=0,l_1$ and $y=0,l_2$. Hence $u=0$ on the ent
ire fixed edge. ✓

- **Initial displacement.** At $t=0$ every $\sin\omega_{mn}t=0$, so $u(x,y,0)=0$
. ✓

- **Initial velocity / impulse.** Differentiating termwise at $t=0$,
$$u_t(x,y,0)=\sum_{m,n}B_{mn}\omega_{mn}\varphi_{mn}(x,y)
=\frac{4}{l_1 l_2}\sum_{m,n}\varphi_{mn}(x,y)\varphi_{mn}(x_0,y_0)\,K.$$
The series $\frac{4}{l_1 l_2}\sum_{m,n}\varphi_{mn}(x,y)\varphi_{mn}(x_0,y_0)$ i
s precisely the eigenfunction expansion of $\delta(x-x_0)\delta(y-y_0)$ on the r
ectangle (the completeness relation for the Dirichlet Laplacian). Hence $u_t(x,y
,0)=K\,\delta(x-x_0)\delta(y-y_0)$, and integrating gives total transverse impul
se $K$. ✓

- **PDE.** Each term satisfies $u_{tt}=a^2(u_{xx}+u_{yy})$ by construction, sinc
e $\omega_{mn}^2=a^2\lambda_{mn}$. ✓

- **Regularity note.** The series converges in the sense of distributions for th
e initial velocity; for $t>0$ the $\sin\omega_{mn}t$ factor provides the oscilla
tory averaging that makes $u(x,y,t)$ a well-defined (generalized) solution of th
e forced membrane problem, consistent with the physical fact that a point impuls
e excites every mode with amplitude inversely proportional to its frequency.

---

### Physical interpretation

Each pair $(m,n)$ is a natural mode of the fixed membrane with frequency $\omega
_{mn}$. The concentrated impulse at $(x_0,y_0)$ excites every mode; the excitati
on amplitude of mode $(m,n)$ is proportional to the value of that mode's eigenfu
nction at the impact point, $\varphi_{mn}(x_0,y_0)$, and inversely proportional
to the mode frequency $\omega_{mn}$ (heavier / slower modes are driven more stro
ngly by an impulse). The factor $\frac{4}{l_1 l_2}$ is the normalization of the
eigenfunctions. The membrane then vibrates as a superposition of all its standin
g-wave modes, each oscillating harmonically at its own natural frequency.
