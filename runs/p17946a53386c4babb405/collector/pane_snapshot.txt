## Solution

**Setup.** Let $p=(a,b)$ on the circle ($a^2+b^2=1$) and $q=(x,y)$ in the disk (
$x^2+y^2\le 1$). The rectangle $R$ with diagonal $pq$ and sides parallel to the
axes has corners
$$(a,b),\quad (x,b),\quad (x,y),\quad (a,y).$$

**Condition for $R\subseteq \overline{D}$.** Since the closed disk $\overline D=
\{u^2+v^2\le 1\}$ is convex, the rectangle $R$ (the convex hull of its corners)
lies in $\overline D$ iff all four corners lie in $\overline D$. Two corners are
 automatic: $(a,b)\in C\subseteq\overline D$ and $(x,y)\in D\subseteq\overline D
$. The remaining two require
$$x^2+b^2\le 1 \quad\text{and}\quad a^2+y^2\le 1.$$
Using $a^2+b^2=1$, these become $x^2\le a^2$ and $y^2\le b^2$, i.e.
$$|x|\le |a|,\qquad |y|\le |b|.\tag{$\ast$}$$

**Fixing $p$ and computing the valid area for $q$.** Parameterize $p=(\cos\theta
,\sin\theta)$ with $\theta$ uniform on $[0,2\pi)$. Condition $(\ast)$ becomes $|
x|\le|\cos\theta|$ and $|y|\le|\sin\theta|$, so the valid region for $q$ is
$$\{(x,y)\in D:\; |x|\le|\cos\theta|,\; |y|\le|\sin\theta|\}.$$
This is the intersection of the disk with the rectangle $[-|\cos\theta|,|\cos\th
eta|]\times[-|\sin\theta|,|\sin\theta|]$. The four corners of this rectangle are
 $(\pm|\cos\theta|,\pm|\sin\theta|)$, each satisfying $x^2+y^2=\cos^2\theta+\sin
^2\theta=1$, so they all lie on $C$. By convexity of the disk, the entire rectan
gle lies inside $\overline D$. Hence the valid region is exactly this rectangle,
 with area
$$4\,|\cos\theta|\,|\sin\theta| = 2\,|\sin 2\theta|.$$

**Averaging over $q$ and $p$.** For fixed $\theta$, the probability (over $q$ un
iform in the disk of area $\pi$) is $\dfrac{2|\sin 2\theta|}{\pi}$. Averaging ov
er $\theta$ uniform on $[0,2\pi)$ with density $\frac{1}{2\pi}$:
$$P=\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{2|\sin 2\theta|}{\pi}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}\,d\theta=\frac{1
}{\pi^2}\int_0^{2\pi}|\sin 2\theta|\,d\theta.$$
Substituting $u=2\theta$,
$$\int_0^{2\pi}|\sin 2\theta|\,d\theta=\frac12\int_0^{4\pi}|\sin u|\,du=\frac12\
cdot 8=4,$$
since $|\sin u|$ has period $\pi$ and $\int_0^{\pi}\sin u\,du=2$, giving $8$ ove
r $[0,4\pi]$.

Therefore
$$\boxed{P=\dfrac{4}{\pi^2}}.$$
