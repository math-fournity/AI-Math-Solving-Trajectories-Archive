### Part 2: Eliminating $k=1$ (mod 8)

Since $3^m \bmod 8 = 3$ if $m$ odd, $= 1$ if $m$ even, we compute $3^m+3^n+1 \bm
od 8$:

- both $m,n$ even: $1+1+1 = 3$
- one even, one odd: $1+3+1 = 5$ (or $3+1+1=5$)
- both odd: $3+3+1 = 7$

Quadratic residues mod 8 are $\{0,1,4\}$. Since $3,5,7 \notin \{0,1,4\}$, **$k=1
$ is impossible**.

### Part 3: Eliminating $k=2$ (mod 9)

Since $3^m \bmod 9 = 3$ if $m=1$, $= 0$ if $m \ge 2$:

- $m,n \ge 2$: $0+0+2 = 2 \bmod 9$
- exactly one equals $1$: $3+0+2 = 5 \bmod 9$
- $m=n=1$: $3+3+2 = 8 \bmod 9$

Quadratic residues mod 9 are $\{0,1,4,7\}$. Since $2,5,8 \notin \{0,1,4,7\}$, **
$k=2$ is impossible**.

### Part 4: Eliminating $k=5$ and $k=8$ (mod 3)

For $m,n \ge 1$: $3^m + 3^n \equiv 0 \pmod{3}$.

- $k=5$: $5 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$, so $3^m+3^n+5 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$.
- $k=8$: $8 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$, so $3^m+3^n+8 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$.

Quadratic residues mod 3 are $\{0,1\}$. Since $2 \notin \{0,1\}$, **$k=5$ and $k
=8$ are impossible**.

### Part 5: Eliminating $k=9$ (mod 8, after reduction)

$$3^m+3^n+9 = 9\bigl(3^a + 3^b + 1\bigr).$$

- one even, one odd: $5$

None of $3,5,7$ are quadratic residues mod 8. So **$k=9$ is impossible**.
