## Proof

We claim that $\sqrt{I} = I = (xy)$, i.e., the ideal $(xy)$ is already radical i
n $R = \mathbb{Z}[x,y]$.

**Setup.** The ring $R = \mathbb{Z}[x,y]$ is a UFD (since $\mathbb{Z}$ is a UFD
and polynomial rings over UFDs are UFDs). In this UFD, $x$ and $y$ are irreducib
le, hence prime (UFD $\Rightarrow$ irreducibles are prime). They are non-associa
te, so $\gcd(x,y) = 1$.

**Key lemma.** In a UFD, if $p$ is prime and $p \mid f^n$, then $p \mid f$.

*Proof of lemma.* Write $f = u \cdot p_1^{a_1}\cdots p_k^{a_k}$ (unique factoriz
ation). Then $f^n = u^n \cdot p_1^{na_1}\cdots p_k^{na_k}$. If $p \mid f^n$, the
n $p$ (being prime, hence appearing in the factorization of $f^n$) must equal so
me $p_i$ up to associates, so $p \mid f$. $\square$

**Main argument.** We show $(xy) \subseteq \sqrt{(xy)} \subseteq (xy)$.

- *Containment $\supseteq$*: Trivially $(xy) \subseteq \sqrt{(xy)}$, since every
 $f \in (xy)$ satisfies $f^1 \in (xy)$.

- *Containment $\subseteq$*: Let $f \in \sqrt{(xy)}$, so $f^n \in (xy)$ for some
 $n \geq 1$. This means $xy \mid f^n$ in $R$, i.e., $f^n = xy \cdot h$ for some
$h \in R$.

  Since $x \mid f^n$ and $x$ is prime, by the lemma $x \mid f$.
  Since $y \mid f^n$ and $y$ is prime, by the lemma $y \mid f$.

  Now $\gcd(x, y) = 1$ in the UFD $R$ (they are distinct non-associate primes).
A standard property of UFDs: if $\gcd(a,b)=1$ and $a \mid f$, $b \mid f$, then $
ab \mid f$. Applying this with $a = x$, $b = y$, we get $xy \mid f$, i.e., $f \i
n (xy)$.

Combining both containments, $\sqrt{(xy)} = (xy)$.

**Conclusion.** The element $xy$ is squarefree (each prime factor $x$ and $y$ ap
pears with exponent $1$), and in a UFD a principal ideal generated by a squarefr
ee element is radical. Therefore:

$$\boxed{\sqrt{I} = \sqrt{(xy)} = (xy) = I.}$$
