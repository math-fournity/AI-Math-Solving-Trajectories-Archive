## Proof

Let $i \colon X \to Y$ denote the inclusion (embedding) map, which by hypothesis
 is compact: it maps bounded sets in $X$ to relatively compact sets in $Y$.

Let $A \subset X$ be a closed subspace. Since $X$ is a Banach space and $A$ is c
losed, $A$ is itself a Banach space under the restricted norm $\|\cdot\|_X|_A$.
Let $j \colon A \hookrightarrow X$ be the canonical inclusion. Then $j$ is bound
ed (linear and continuous): for every $a \in A$,
$$\|j(a)\|_X = \|a\|_X,$$
so $\|j\| \le 1$.

The embedding of $A$ into $Y$ is the composition
$$i \circ j \colon A \xrightarrow{\;j\;} X \xrightarrow{\;i\;} Y.$$

**Claim:** $i \circ j$ is compact.

*Proof of claim.* Let $B \subset A$ be bounded, say $\sup_{a \in B}\|a\|_X \le M
$. Then $j(B) = B \subset X$ is bounded in $X$ (by the same bound $M$). Since $i
$ is compact, $i(j(B)) = i(B)$ is relatively compact in $Y$. Hence $(i \circ j)(
B)$ is relatively compact in $Y$. $\square$

Thus $i \circ j \colon A \to Y$ is a compact linear map, which is exactly the st
atement that $A$ is compactly embedded in $Y$.

**Answer: Yes, $A$ is compactly embedded in $Y$.**
