## Proof

We must find all $a \in \mathbb{C}$ such that $f(z) = \frac{1}{z} + \frac{1}{(z-
1)^2} + \frac{a}{\sin z}$ has an antiderivative on $U = \{|z|<2\}\setminus\{0,1\
}$.

**Singularities inside $|z|<2$.** The zeros of $\sin z$ are $z = k\pi$, $k\in\ma
thbb{Z}$. Since $\pi > 2$, the only zero of $\sin z$ in $|z|<2$ is $z=0$. Hence
$f$ has isolated singularities in $\{|z|<2\}$ only at $z=0$ and $z=1$, both of w
hich are removed from $U$. There are no other singularities.

**Criterion for existence of an antiderivative.** The domain $U$ is the open dis
k of radius $2$ punctured at $0$ and $1$. Its fundamental group is free of rank
$2$, generated by small positively-oriented loops $\gamma_0$ around $0$ and $\ga
mma_1$ around $1$. A holomorphic function on $U$ has an antiderivative if and on
ly if $\oint_\gamma f\,dz = 0$ for every closed curve $\gamma$ in $U$. By the re
sidue theorem (applied in the full disk, where the only singularities are $0$ an
d $1$), every closed curve integral is an integer linear combination of $2\pi i\
,\mathrm{Res}(f,0)$ and $2\pi i\,\mathrm{Res}(f,1)$. Thus $f$ has an antiderivat
ive on $U$ if and only if

$$\mathrm{Res}(f,0) = 0 \quad\text{and}\quad \mathrm{Res}(f,1) = 0.$$

**Residue at $z=1$.** Near $z=1$: $\frac{1}{z}$ is analytic (residue $0$); $\fra
c{1}{(z-1)^2}$ is a double pole with Laurent expansion $\frac{1}{(z-1)^2}$, whic
h has no $\frac{1}{z-1}$ term, so residue $0$; and $\frac{a}{\sin z}$ is analyti
c at $z=1$ since $\sin 1 \neq 0$. Therefore

$$\mathrm{Res}(f,1) = 0$$

for every $a \in \mathbb{C}$.

**Residue at $z=0$.** Near $z=0$: $\frac{1}{z}$ contributes residue $1$; $\frac{
1}{(z-1)^2}$ is analytic at $0$ (its pole is at $1$), contributing $0$. For $\fr
ac{a}{\sin z}$, using $\sin z = z - \frac{z^3}{6} + \cdots = z\bigl(1 - \frac{z^
2}{6}+\cdots\bigr)$, we get

$$\frac{1}{\sin z} = \frac{1}{z}\cdot\frac{1}{1 - z^2/6 + \cdots} = \frac{1}{z}\
Bigl(1 + \frac{z^2}{6} + \cdots\Bigr) = \frac{1}{z} + \frac{z}{6} + \cdots,$$

so $\mathrm{Res}\!\bigl(\frac{a}{\sin z},\,0\bigr) = a$. Therefore

$$\mathrm{Res}(f,0) = 1 + a.$$

**Conclusion.** The condition $\mathrm{Res}(f,0)=0$ gives $1+a=0$, i.e. $a=-1$,
while $\mathrm{Res}(f,1)=0$ is automatic. Hence $f$ has an antiderivative on $U$
 if and only if

$$\boxed{a = -1}.$$
