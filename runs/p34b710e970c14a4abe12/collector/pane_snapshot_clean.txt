- $a_i \leq M$ for all $i$ (so $\max a_i = M$),
- $\sum_{i=1}^{10} a_i \geq 10$,
- $\sum_{i=1}^{10} a_i^2 \geq 100$.

**Key observation.** For a fixed $M$, feasibility requires both constraints to b
e simultaneously satisfiable. We first note that $\sum a_i \leq 10M$ (since each
 $a_i \leq M$), so $\sum a_i \geq 10$ requires $M \geq 1$.

**Maximizing $\sum a_i^2$ given the constraints.** Fix $M$ and $S = \sum a_i \in
 [10,\, 10M]$. The feasible region $\{x \in \mathbb{R}^{10} : \sum x_i = S,\; x_
i \leq M\}$ is a **bounded** polytope: each $x_i \geq S - 9M \geq 10 - 9M$ (sinc
e the other 9 coordinates are each at most $M$). The function $\sum x_i^2$ is **
convex**, so its maximum over this polytope is attained at a **vertex**.

A vertex of $\{\sum x_i = S,\; x_i \leq M\}$ has exactly 9 coordinates equal to
$M$ and one coordinate equal to $S - 9M$. Therefore:

$$\max \sum a_i^2 = 9M^2 + (S - 9M)^2 \;=:\; g(S).$$

**Optimizing over $S$.** The function $g(S) = 9M^2 + (S-9M)^2$ is a parabola in
$S$ minimized at $S = 9M$. We consider two regimes:

- **If $M < \frac{10}{9}$:** then $9M < 10 \leq S$, so $g$ is increasing on $[10
, 10M]$ and maximized at $S = 10M$: $g(10M) = 9M^2 + M^2 = 10M^2$. Requiring $10
M^2 \geq 100$ gives $M \geq \sqrt{10} \approx 3.16$, which contradicts $M < 10/9
$. No solution in this regime.

- **If $M \geq \frac{10}{9}$:** then $9M \geq 10$, so $g$ is decreasing near $S=
10$ and maximized at the endpoint $S = 10$ (farthest from $9M$): $g(10) = 9M^2 +
 (10 - 9M)^2 = 90M^2 - 180M + 100$.

**Solving the binding inequality.** We need $g(10) \geq 100$:

$$90M^2 - 180M + 100 \geq 100 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; 90M(M - 2) \geq 0.$$

Since $M \geq 1 > 0$, this gives $M \geq 2$.

0 - 18 = -8$. Then:

$$\boxed{M = 2}$$
