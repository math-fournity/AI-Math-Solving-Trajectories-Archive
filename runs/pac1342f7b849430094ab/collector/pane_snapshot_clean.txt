## Solution

**Setting up.** The PDE is the heat equation on $0<x<L$ with a time-independent
source term $g(x)=\beta e^{-xz}$ (treating $z$ as a fixed real parameter) and ho
mogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions:
$$u_t = u_{xx} + \beta e^{-xz},\qquad u(0,t)=u(1\cdot L,t)=0,\qquad u(x,0)=f(x),
\qquad |u|<M.$$

Because the spatial operator $-\partial_{xx}$ with Dirichlet conditions has the
complete orthogonal eigenbasis
$$\phi_n(x)=\sin\!\Big(\tfrac{n\pi x}{L}\Big),\qquad \lambda_n=\Big(\tfrac{n\pi}
{L}\Big)^{2},\qquad n=1,2,\dots$$
we expand everything in this sine series.

**Expansions.** Write
$$u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n(t)\,\phi_n(x),\qquad f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} f
_n\,\phi_n(x),\qquad \beta e^{-xz}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} g_n\,\phi_n(x),$$
with
$$f_n=\frac{2}{L}\int_{0}^{L} f(x)\sin\!\Big(\tfrac{n\pi x}{L}\Big)\,dx,$$
$$g_n=\frac{2\beta}{L}\int_{0}^{L} e^{-zx}\sin\!\Big(\tfrac{n\pi x}{L}\Big)\,dx.
$$

**Computing $g_n$.** With $a=z$ and $k=n\pi/L$,
$$\int_{0}^{L}e^{-ax}\sin(kx)\,dx=\frac{k}{a^{2}+k^{2}}\Big[1-(-1)^{n}e^{-aL}\Bi
g],$$
since $\sin(kL)=\sin(n\pi)=0$ and $\cos(kL)=\cos(n\pi)=(-1)^{n}$. Therefore
$$g_n=\frac{2\beta}{L}\cdot\frac{n\pi/L}{z^{2}+(n\pi/L)^{2}}\Big[1-(-1)^{n}e^{-z
L}\Big]=\frac{2\beta\,n\pi}{(n\pi)^{2}+z^{2}L^{2}}\Big[1-(-1)^{n}e^{-zL}\Big].$$

**ODEs for the modes.** Substituting the series into the PDE and using $\phi_n''
=-\lambda_n\phi_n$ gives, mode by mode,
$$b_n'(t)=-\lambda_n\,b_n(t)+g_n,\qquad b_n(0)=f_n.$$

**Solving the mode ODE.** This is a first-order linear ODE; its solution is
$$b_n(t)=f_n\,e^{-\lambda_n t}+\frac{g_n}{\lambda_n}\Big(1-e^{-\lambda_n t}\Big)
.$$

**The solution.** Hence
$$\boxed{\;u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[f_n\,e^{-\lambda_n t}+\frac{g_n}{\lam
bda_n}\Big(1-e^{-\lambda_n t}\Big)\right]\sin\!\Big(\tfrac{n\pi x}{L}\Big)\;}$$
where
$$\lambda_n=\Big(\tfrac{n\pi}{L}\Big)^{2},\qquad f_n=\frac{2}{L}\int_{0}^{L}f(x)
\sin\!\Big(\tfrac{n\pi x}{L}\Big)dx,\qquad g_n=\frac{2\beta\,n\pi}{(n\pi)^{2}+z^
{2}L^{2}}\Big[1-(-1)^{n}e^{-zL}\Big].$$

**Verification of the conditions.**
- *Initial condition:* at $t=0$, $e^{-\lambda_n\cdot0}=1$, so $b_n(0)=f_n$ and $
u(x,0)=\sum f_n\phi_n(x)=f(x)$. ✓
- *Boundary conditions:* each $\phi_n(0)=\phi_n(L)=0$, so $u(0,t)=u(L,t)=0$. ✓
- *PDE:* termwise differentiation is justified for $t>0$ by the exponential deca
y $e^{-\lambda_n t}$; substituting reproduces $u_t=u_{xx}+\beta e^{-xz}$. ✓
- *Boundedness $|u|<M$:* the transient part $\sum f_n e^{-\lambda_n t}\phi_n$ de
cays to $0$. The steady part $\sum (g_n/\lambda_n)\phi_n$ converges absolutely (
since $g_n/\lambda_n=O(1/n^{3})$) to the bounded steady-state solution $u_\infty
$ of $-u_\infty''=\beta e^{-xz}$, $u_\infty(0)=u_\infty(L)=0$. Thus $u$ is bound
ed for all $t\ge0$; $M$ can be taken as $\sup_x|u|$. ✓

**Steady state (alternative compact form).** As $t\to\infty$ the transient dies
out and
$$u(x,t)\to u_\infty(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{g_n}{\lambda_n}\sin\!\Big(\tfra
c{n\pi x}{L}\Big),$$
which is precisely the solution of the elliptic BVP $-u_\infty''=\beta e^{-xz}$,
 $u_\infty(0)=u_\infty(L)=0$. Equivalently, one may decompose
$$u(x,t)=v(x,t)+u_\infty(x),$$
where $v$ solves the *homogeneous* heat equation $v_t=v_{xx}$ with $v(0,t)=v(L,t
)=0$ and $v(x,0)=f(x)-u_\infty(x)$, giving $v(x,t)=\sum\big(f_n-g_n/\lambda_n\bi
g)e^{-\lambda_n t}\phi_n(x)$ — the same formula.

### (b) Physical interpretation

The equation is the **one-dimensional heat (diffusion) equation** $u_t=u_{xx}$ w
ith a **distributed, time-independent heat source** $Q(x)=\beta e^{-xz}$ per uni
t length. The physical picture:

- $u(x,t)$ is the **temperature** at position $x$ along a thin rod of length $L$
 at time $t$.
- The rod's ends are **held at zero temperature** (Dirichlet conditions $u(0,t)=
u(L,t)=0$) — e.g. both ends clamped to heat reservoirs/ice baths at $0$.
- $f(x)$ is the **initial temperature profile** at $t=0$.
- The source $\beta e^{-xz}$ represents an **internal heat generation** that is
exponentially decaying along the rod (concentrated near $x=0$ when $z>0$, near $
x=L$ when $z<0$, uniform when $z=0$); $\beta$ is its strength.
- The condition $|u|<M$ expresses **physical boundedness**: the temperature neve
r blows up.

The solution decomposes into two physically meaningful parts:

1. **Transient** $\displaystyle\sum f_n e^{-\lambda_n t}\phi_n(x)$: the initial
profile $f(x)$ relaxing to zero under diffusion, with each Fourier mode decaying
 at its own rate $e^{-\lambda_n t}$. High-frequency modes (large $n$) decay fast
est — diffusion smooths out sharp features first.

2. **Forced approach to steady state** $\displaystyle\sum \frac{g_n}{\lambda_n}(
1-e^{-\lambda_n t})\phi_n(x)$: the internal source $\beta e^{-xz}$ gradually hea
ts the rod until it reaches the **steady-state temperature** $u_\infty(x)$, whic
h balances heat generation against conduction to the cold ends. The factor $(1-e
^{-\lambda_n t})$ shows each spatial mode of the source "charging up" with the s
ame thermal time constant $\tau_n=1/\lambda_n=L^2/(n\pi)^2$.

In short: the rod, initially at temperature $f(x)$ with both ends kept at $0$, i
s being internally heated by an exponentially distributed source; the initial pr
ofile diffuses away while the source drives the system toward a fixed equilibriu
m temperature distribution $u_\infty(x)$, and the temperature remains bounded th
roughout.
