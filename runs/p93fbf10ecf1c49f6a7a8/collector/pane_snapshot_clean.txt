## Proof

**Setup and notation.** We work in a category where the following data is given:
- $\alpha_1: X_1 \to Y$, $\alpha_2: X_2 \to Y$
- $\beta_1: X_1 \to Z$, $\beta_2: X_2 \to Z$
- $f: Y \to Z$ with $f \circ \alpha_i = \beta_i$ for $i=1,2$
- Pullback $X_1 \times_Y X_2$ with projections $\pi^Y_1, \pi^Y_2$ satisfying $\a
lpha_1 \circ \pi^Y_1 = \alpha_2 \circ \pi^Y_2$
- Pullback $X_1 \times_Z X_2$ with projections $\pi^Z_1, \pi^Z_2$ satisfying $\b
eta_1 \circ \pi^Z_1 = \beta_2 \circ \pi^Z_2$

**Goal.** Show there is a unique $h: X_1 \times_Y X_2 \to X_1 \times_Z X_2$ with
 $\pi^Z_i \circ h = \pi^Y_i$ for $i=1,2$.

**Step 1: The pair $(\pi^Y_1, \pi^Y_2)$ is a cone over the $Z$-cospan.**

By the pullback property of $X_1 \times_Y X_2$:
$$\alpha_1 \circ \pi^Y_1 = \alpha_2 \circ \pi^Y_2.$$

Composing both sides with $f$:
$$f \circ \alpha_1 \circ \pi^Y_1 = f \circ \alpha_2 \circ \pi^Y_2.$$

Using the hypothesis $f \circ \alpha_i = \beta_i$:
$$\beta_1 \circ \pi^Y_1 = \beta_2 \circ \pi^Y_2.$$

This is exactly the compatibility condition required for the pair of maps $(\pi^
Y_1: X_1\times_Y X_2 \to X_1,\; \pi^Y_2: X_1\times_Y X_2 \to X_2)$ to form a con
e over the cospan $X_1 \xrightarrow{\beta_1} Z \xleftarrow{\beta_2} X_2$.

**Step 2: Universal property of $X_1 \times_Z X_2$ yields a unique $h$.**

The pullback $X_1 \times_Z X_2$ is universal among such cones: for any object $T
$ equipped with maps $p_1: T \to X_1$, $p_2: T \to X_2$ satisfying $\beta_1 \cir
c p_1 = \beta_2 \circ p_2$, there exists a **unique** map $u: T \to X_1 \times_Z
 X_2$ with $\pi^Z_1 \circ u = p_1$ and $\pi^Z_2 \circ u = p_2$.

Applying this to $T = X_1 \times_Y X_2$, $p_1 = \pi^Y_1$, $p_2 = \pi^Y_2$ (which
 satisfy the compatibility by Step 1), we obtain a **unique** map
$$h: X_1 \times_Y X_2 \to X_1 \times_Z X_2$$
such that
$$\pi^Z_1 \circ h = \pi^Y_1 \quad\text{and}\quad \pi^Z_2 \circ h = \pi^Y_2.$$

**Conclusion.** Yes. The equality $f \circ \alpha_1 \circ \pi^Y_1 = f \circ \alp
ha_2 \circ \pi^Y_2$ (equivalently $\beta_1 \circ \pi^Y_1 = \beta_2 \circ \pi^Y_2
$) is precisely the cone condition for the $Z$-pullback, so the universal proper
ty of $X_1 \times_Z X_2$ guarantees a unique $h$ with $\pi^Z_i \circ h = \pi^Y_i
$ for $i = 1, 2$. $\blacksquare$
