## Proof

**Step 0: Reduction to $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$.** Since $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mat
hbb{C}) \to \mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ is surjective (every class in $\mathrm{P
GL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ has a representative with determinant 1), we have $\mathrm{PG
L}(2,\mathbb{C}) \cong \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$. We work with $\mathrm{PSL}(2
,\mathbb{C})$ throughout.

**Step 1: The homomorphism is either trivial or injective.** $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ is
 a compact, connected Lie group whose Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(3) \cong \mathf
rak{su}(2)$ is simple, and whose center is trivial. Therefore the only closed no
rmal subgroups of $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ are $\{I\}$ and $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ itself. The
kernel of any continuous homomorphism $\phi: \mathrm{SO}(3) \to \mathrm{PSL}(2,\
mathbb{C})$ is a closed normal subgroup, so $\phi$ is either trivial or injectiv
e.

**Step 2: The image of a nontrivial $\phi$ is a compact subgroup isomorphic to $
\mathrm{SO}(3)$.** If $\phi$ is injective, then $\phi(\mathrm{SO}(3))$ is a comp
act subgroup of $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ (compactness is preserved under con
tinuous maps), isomorphic to $\mathrm{SO}(3)$.

**Step 3: Every compact subgroup lies in a maximal compact subgroup, and all max
imal compact subgroups are conjugate.** By the Cartan–Iwasawa–Mostow theorem, ev
ery compact subgroup of a Lie group is contained in a maximal compact subgroup,
and any two maximal compact subgroups are conjugate.

The maximal compact subgroup of $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ is $\mathrm{PSU}(2)
$. This follows from the polar decomposition $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C}) \cong \m
athrm{SU}(2) \times \mathbb{R}^3$ (as a manifold), which shows $\mathrm{SU}(2)$
is the maximal compact subgroup of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$; quotienting by $
\{\pm I\}$ gives $\mathrm{PSU}(2)$ as the maximal compact subgroup of $\mathrm{P
SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$.

Therefore, there exists $S \in \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ such that $\phi(\math
rm{SO}(3)) \subset S \cdot \mathrm{PSU}(2) \cdot S^{-1}$.

**Step 4: The image equals the conjugated $\mathrm{PSU}(2)$.** Since $\phi$ is i
njective, $\phi(\mathrm{SO}(3)) \cong \mathrm{SO}(3)$. We have $\mathrm{PSU}(2)
= \mathrm{SU}(2)/\{\pm I\} \cong \mathrm{SO}(3)$, so $\dim \phi(\mathrm{SO}(3))
= \dim \mathrm{PSU}(2) = 3$. Since $\phi(\mathrm{SO}(3))$ is a closed subgroup o
f $S \cdot \mathrm{PSU}(2) \cdot S^{-1}$ of the same dimension, and $S \cdot \ma
thrm{PSU}(2) \cdot S^{-1}$ is connected, we get $\phi(\mathrm{SO}(3)) = S \cdot
\mathrm{PSU}(2) \cdot S^{-1}$.

**Step 5: Reduction to an automorphism of $\mathrm{SO}(3)$.** Define $\psi: \mat
hrm{SO}(3) \to \mathrm{PSU}(2)$ by $\psi(X) = S^{-1}\phi(X)S$. This is an isomor
phism from $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ to $\mathrm{PSU}(2) \cong \mathrm{SO}(3)$, i.e., an
automorphism of $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ (after identifying $\mathrm{PSU}(2)$ with $\mat
hrm{SO}(3)$ via the standard isomorphism $\phi_0$).

**Step 6: All automorphisms of $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ are inner.** The Lie algebra $\m
athfrak{so}(3)$ is of type $A_1$, whose Dynkin diagram is a single node. There a
re no diagram automorphisms, so $\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{so}(3)) = 1$ and $\mathr
m{Aut}(\mathfrak{so}(3)) = \mathrm{Inn}(\mathfrak{so}(3))$. Since $\mathrm{SO}(3
)$ is connected with trivial center, $\mathrm{Inn}(\mathrm{SO}(3)) \cong \mathrm
{SO}(3)/Z(\mathrm{SO}(3)) = \mathrm{SO}(3)$, and $\mathrm{Out}(\mathrm{SO}(3)) =
 1$. Hence $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{SO}(3)) \cong \mathrm{SO}(3)$, and every automo
rphism is inner.

Therefore $\psi = \mathrm{Inn}_T \circ \phi_0$ for some $T \in \mathrm{PSU}(2) \
subset \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$, where $\phi_0: \mathrm{SO}(3) \xrightarrow{\
sim} \mathrm{PSU}(2)$ is the standard identification. Concretely:
$$\psi(X) = T\, \phi_0(X)\, T^{-1}.$$

**Step 7: Conclusion.** Combining Steps 5 and 6:
$$\phi(X) = S\, \psi(X)\, S^{-1} = S\, T\, \phi_0(X)\, T^{-1} S^{-1} = (ST)\, \p
hi_0(X)\, (ST)^{-1}.$$

Since $S \in \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ and $T \in \mathrm{PSU}(2) \subset \mat
hrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$, we have $ST \in \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$. Thus every
 nontrivial homomorphism $\phi$ is conjugate to the standard embedding $\phi_0$
by an element of $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C}) \cong \mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{C})$.

**Final argument.** Given two homomorphisms $f, g: \mathrm{SO}(3) \to \mathrm{PG
L}(2,\mathbb{C})$:

- If both are trivial, take $S = I$: $g(X) = I \cdot f(X) \cdot I = f(X)$ for al
l $X$.
- If both are nontrivial, by the above, there exist $S_1, S_2 \in \mathrm{PGL}(2
,\mathbb{C})$ with $f = S_1 \phi_0 S_1^{-1}$ and $g = S_2 \phi_0 S_2^{-1}$. Then
 $g(X) = (S_2 S_1^{-1})\, f(X)\, (S_2 S_1^{-1})^{-1}$, so $S = S_2 S_1^{-1}$ wor
ks.
- If one is trivial and the other is not, no such $S$ exists (conjugation preser
ves whether the image is $\{I\}$ or not).

In all cases where both homomorphisms are of the same type (both trivial or both
 nontrivial), the desired $S$ exists. $\blacksquare$
