### Step 1: The completions are unramified extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_2$

Since $e_i = 1$, the completion $K_{\mathfrak{P}_i}$ is an **unramified** extens

$$[K_{\mathfrak{P}_i} : \mathbb{Q}_2] = e_i \cdot f_i = f_i.$$

### Step 2: Uniqueness of unramified extensions inside $\overline{\mathbb{Q}_2}$

A fundamental fact of local field theory: **inside a fixed algebraic closure $\o
verline{\mathbb{Q}_2}$, there is a unique unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_2$
 of each degree $n$.** Denote this unique subfield by $U_n$. Explicitly, $U_n =
\mathbb{Q}_2(\zeta_{2^n - 1})$, obtained by adjoining a primitive $(2^n-1)$-th r

### Step 3: The image of each completion in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}_2}$ is uniquel

both completions into $\overline{\mathbb{Q}_2}$ as $\mathbb{Q}_2$-algebras (comp

mathbb{Q}_2 \hookrightarrow K_{\mathfrak{P}_i}$).

**Claim:** Any $\mathbb{Q}_2$-embedding $\sigma_i : K_{\mathfrak{P}_i} \hookrigh
tarrow \overline{\mathbb{Q}_2}$ has image exactly $U_{f_i}$.

*Proof of claim:* The image $\sigma_i(K_{\mathfrak{P}_i})$ is a subfield of $\ov
erline{\mathbb{Q}_2}$ that is an unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_2$ of degre

and embeddings preserve the extension structure). By the uniqueness of Step 2, t

square$

This is the crucial point: **the image is independent of the choice of embedding

_i}$ into $\overline{\mathbb{Q}_2}$ (the Galois group of $U_{f_i}/\mathbb{Q}_2$
has order $f_i$), they all have the same image $U_{f_i}$.

### Step 4: The intersection of unramified extensions

The unramified extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_2$ inside $\overline{\mathbb{Q}_2}$ for
m a lattice isomorphic to the divisibility lattice of positive integers:

$$U_m \subseteq U_n \iff m \mid n.$$

This follows because $U_n$ is the splitting field of $x^{2^n - 1} - 1$ over $\ma
thbb{Q}_2$, and $2^m - 1 \mid 2^n - 1$ iff $m \mid n$.

From this, the compositum satisfies $U_m \cdot U_n = U_{\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)}
$ (the unique unramified extension of degree $\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)$). Since $
U_m \cap U_n$ is a subextension of the unramified extension $U_m/\mathbb{Q}_2$,
it is itself unramified, hence $U_m \cap U_n = U_d$ for some $d$. By the degree
formula:

$$[U_m U_n : \mathbb{Q}_2] = \frac{[U_m:\mathbb{Q}_2]\,[U_n:\mathbb{Q}_2]}{[U_m
\cap U_n : \mathbb{Q}_2]} = \frac{mn}{d}.$$

Since $U_m U_n = U_{\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)}$, we get $\operatorname{lcm}(m,n) =
 mn/d$, so $d = \gcd(m,n)$. Therefore:

$$U_m \cap U_n = U_{\gcd(m,n)}.$$
