## Proof

For any $N\ge 1$ and $\varepsilon>0$, $t\ge 0$, use the identity
$$\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon t}=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}(-\varepsilon t)^{n}+\frac{(-\vareps

$$I(\varepsilon)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}(-\varepsilon)^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n}e^{-t}\

where
$$R_{N}(\varepsilon)=(-\varepsilon)^{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{N}e^{-t}}{1+\va

### Step 2. Evaluate the coefficients

The moments are the Gamma function values
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n}e^{-t}\,dt=\Gamma(n+1)=n!.$$
Hence
$$I(\varepsilon)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}(-1)^{n}n!\,\varepsilon^{n}+R_{N}(\varepsilon).
$$

### Step 3. Bound the remainder — asymptotic character

Since $1+\varepsilon t\ge 1$ for $\varepsilon>0,\,t\ge 0$,
$$\bigl|R_{N}(\varepsilon)\bigr|\le \varepsilon^{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{N}e^{-t}\
,dt=N!\,\varepsilon^{N}.$$
Therefore
$$R_{N}(\varepsilon)=O\!\bigl(N!\,\varepsilon^{N}\bigr)\qquad(\varepsilon\to 0^{

and consequently, for every fixed $N$,
$$I(\varepsilon)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}(-1)^{n}n!\,\varepsilon^{n}+O\!\bigl(\varepsilo
n^{N}\bigr)\qquad(\varepsilon\to 0^{+}).$$

This is precisely the definition of an asymptotic expansion:
$$\boxed{\,I(\varepsilon)\sim\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}n!\,\varepsilon^{n}\quad
(\varepsilon\to 0^{+})\,}$$
i.e.
$$I(\varepsilon)\sim 1-\varepsilon+2!\,\varepsilon^{2}-3!\,\varepsilon^{3}+4!\,\
varepsilon^{4}-\cdots.$$

### Step 4. The series is divergent (factorial growth) — it is genuinely asympto

silon^{n}}=(n+1)\varepsilon,$$
