Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum number of cables (out of 10)
 that can be made "Balanced" — where the Primary/Secondary port sequence alterna
tes perfectly along each cable's 9 junctions.

**Answer: 10 (all cables can be balanced simultaneously).**

## Proof

**Setup.** We have 10 cables, every pair crossing exactly once, no triple crossi
ngs — giving C(10,2) = 45 junctions. At each junction, one cable is assigned Pri
mary (P) and the other Secondary (S). A cable is Balanced if its port sequence a
long its 9 junctions alternates: P,S,P,S,P,S,P,S,P or S,P,S,P,S,P,S,P,S.

**Key idea.** We get to choose both the cable arrangement and the port assignmen
ts. I exhibit a specific arrangement and port assignment where all 10 cables are
 balanced.

**The arrangement (lines tangent to a parabola).**

Consider the parabola y = x². For each i = 1, 2, ..., 10, define cable i as the
line tangent to the parabola at x = i:

$$L_i: y = 2ix - i^2$$

These are 10 distinct lines with distinct slopes 2, 4, ..., 20, so every pair cr
osses exactly once. The crossing of L_i and L_j (i ≠ j) occurs at:

$$2ix - i^2 = 2jx - j^2 \implies x = \frac{i+j}{2}, \quad y = ij$$

**No three lines are concurrent:** If L_i, L_j, L_k shared a point, then crossin
gs (i,j) and (i,k) would coincide, requiring (i+j)/2 = (i+k)/2, hence j = k — co
ntradiction. ✓

**Crossing order along each cable.** Along cable i, the crossing with cable j is
 at x-coordinate (i+j)/2. Since this is strictly increasing in j, cable i crosse
s the other cables in order of increasing index:

$$1, 2, \ldots, i-1, \quad i+1, \ldots, 10$$

So the position of the crossing with cable j in cable i's sequence is:
- **p_i(i,j) = j** if j < i (it's the j-th crossing),
- **p_i(i,j) = j − 1** if j > i (it's the (j−1)-th crossing).

**Port assignment.** Assign ports by parity. Define:

> Cable i is routed through **Primary** at position p iff p ≡ i (mod 2); otherwi
se **Secondary**.

Equivalently: odd-indexed cables (i odd) get P at odd positions (start with P),
and even-indexed cables (i even) get P at even positions (start with S). Since e
ach cable has 9 crossings, this produces a perfectly alternating sequence for ev
ery cable. ✓

**Verification that each junction has one P and one S.** Consider the junction b
etween cables i and j with i < j:
- Cable i is P here iff p_i(i,j) ≡ i (mod 2), i.e., (j−1) ≡ i (mod 2), i.e., **i
 + j is odd**.
- Cable j is P here iff p_j(i,j) ≡ j (mod 2), i.e., i ≡ j (mod 2), i.e., **i + j
 is even**.

These conditions are complementary: exactly one of (i+j odd) and (i+j even) hold
s. So exactly one cable is P and the other is S at every junction. ✓

**Concrete check (cables 2 and 5):**
- Cable 2 (even): crossing with 5 is at position 4 (even) → P.
- Cable 5 (odd): crossing with 2 is at position 2 (even) → S.
- One P, one S. ✓

**Concrete check (cables 3 and 6):**
- Cable 3 (odd): crossing with 6 is at position 5 (odd) → P.
- Cable 6 (even): crossing with 3 is at position 3 (odd) → S.
- One P, one S. ✓

**Each cable is balanced.** Cable i has 9 junctions at positions 1 through 9. By
 construction, position p is P iff p ≡ i (mod 2), giving the alternating pattern
 P,S,P,S,P,S,P,S,P (if i odd) or S,P,S,P,S,P,S,P,S (if i even). Both are perfect
ly alternating. ✓

**Conclusion.** In the parabola-tangent line arrangement with the parity-based p
ort assignment, all 10 cables are simultaneously Balanced. Since 10 is the total
 number of cables, this is the maximum possible.

The maximum possible number of Balanced cables is **10**.
