## Answer

**No.** The statement is false. Here is a counterexample.

---

### Counterexample

Let $\mathcal{H} = \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$ with standard orthonormal basis $\{e_1, e
_2, \dots\}$. Define
$$
p_n := \sum_{k=1}^n |e_k\rangle\langle e_k|,
$$
the projection onto $\operatorname{span}\{e_1,\dots,e_n\}$. Then $\{p_n\}$ is a
monotone increasing sequence of projections and $p_n \to I$ in the strong operat
or topology.

Set
$$
A := \mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}), \qquad B := \mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}) + \mathbb{C}
\cdot I \;=\; \{k + \lambda I : k \in \mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}),\; \lambda \in \m
athbb{C}\}.
$$

Both $A$ and $B$ are $C^*$-subalgebras of $B(\mathcal{H})$. Note that each $p_n$
 is a finite-rank projection, hence $p_n \in \mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}) \subset A
\cap B$, so $p_n A p_n$ and $p_n B p_n$ are corners (hereditary subalgebras) and
 thus $C^*$-algebras.

### Verification of the hypotheses

**Each $p_n A p_n$ is finite dimensional.** Since $p_n \in A = \mathcal{K}(\math
cal{H})$, the corner $p_n A p_n = p_n\,\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\,p_n$ consists o
f all operators supported on the $n$-dimensional space $p_n\mathcal{H}$, i.e.,
$$
p_n A p_n = \mathcal{B}(p_n \mathcal{H}) \cong M_n(\mathbb{C}),
$$
which is finite dimensional. ✓

**$p_n A p_n \simeq p_n B p_n$ for every $n$.** An arbitrary element of $B$ is $
k + \lambda I$ with $k \in \mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})$, $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$.
Compressing:
$$
p_n(k+\lambda I)p_n = \underbrace{p_n k\, p_n}_{\in\, p_n\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}
)p_n = M_n(\mathbb{C})} + \underbrace{\lambda\, p_n}_{\in\, M_n(\mathbb{C})}.
$$
Hence $p_n B p_n \subseteq M_n(\mathbb{C})$. Conversely, $M_n(\mathbb{C}) = p_n\
,\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})\,p_n \subseteq p_n B p_n$ (since $\mathcal{K}(\mathcal
{H}) \subset B$). Therefore
$$
p_n B p_n = M_n(\mathbb{C}) = p_n A p_n,
$$
so in particular $p_n A p_n \simeq p_n B p_n$ for every $n$. ✓

**$p_n \to 1$ strongly.** Standard. ✓

### $A \not\simeq B$

The algebra $A = \mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})$ is **non-unital** (the identity $I$ o
n the infinite-dimensional space $\mathcal{H}$ is not compact). The algebra $B =
 \mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}) + \mathbb{C}\cdot I$ is **unital** with unit $I$. Sinc
e *-isomorphisms of $C^*$-algebras preserve the existence of a unit, a non-unita
l $C^*$-algebra cannot be *-isomorphic to a unital one. Therefore
$$
A \not\simeq B.
$$

### Conclusion

All hypotheses are satisfied — $\{p_n\}$ monotone with $p_n \to 1$ (SOT), each $
p_n A p_n$ finite dimensional, and $p_n A p_n \simeq p_n B p_n$ for every $n$ —
yet $A \not\simeq B$. The implication **fails**.

The essential obstruction is that the abstract isomorphism type of each finite-d
imensional compression $p_n A p_n$ does not detect whether the identity operator
 belongs to the ambient algebra. The compressions $p_n A p_n$ and $p_n B p_n$ ag
ree exactly at every finite level, but the "limit" $\bigcup_n p_n A p_n$ (the fi
nite-rank operators) has two distinct $C^*$-completions inside $B(\mathcal{H})$:
 the non-unital $\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})$ and the unital $\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{
H}) + \mathbb{C}I$. Without a compatibility/intertwining condition on the isomor
phisms $p_n A p_n \xrightarrow{\sim} p_n B p_n$, these local isomorphisms cannot
 be assembled into a global one.
