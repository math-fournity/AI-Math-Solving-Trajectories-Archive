## Solution

**Step 1: Identify $f$ via the identity theorem.**

The function $f$ is holomorphic on an open ball $B(0,R)$ centered at 0. Since $\
{\gamma\}$ is compact and contains both $\gamma(0)=1$ and $\gamma(1)=-1$ (both a
t distance 1 from 0), the open ball must have radius $R > 1$. Hence $B(0,R)$ con
tains the entire closed unit disk, including the lower semicircle $\sigma = \{e^
{it} : \pi \le t \le 2\pi\}$.

On $\sigma$, we are given $f(e^{it}) = e^{2it}+1 = (e^{it})^2 + 1$, i.e., $f(z)
= z^2 + 1$ for all $z \in \sigma$.

Both $f(z)$ and $g(z) = z^2 + 1$ are holomorphic on $B(0,R)$, and they agree on
$\sigma$, which is a set with a limit point in $B(0,R)$. By the **identity theor
em**, $f(z) = z^2 + 1$ on all of $B(0,R)$.

**Consistency check:** $f'(z) = 2z$, so $f'(0) = 0$. ✓ This matches the given co
ndition.

**Step 2: Compute the integral.**

$$\int_\gamma \frac{f(z)}{z^2}\,dz = \int_\gamma \frac{z^2+1}{z^2}\,dz = \int_\g
amma \left(1 + \frac{1}{z^2}\right)dz.$$

Since $\gamma(t) \neq 0$ for all $t$, the integrand $1/z^2$ has the antiderivati
ve $-1/z$ on $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$, which is valid along $\gamma$. Likewise
, $1$ has antiderivative $z$. Applying the fundamental theorem of contour integr
ation:

$$\int_\gamma 1\,dz = \gamma(1) - \gamma(0) = (-1) - (1) = -2,$$

$$\int_\gamma \frac{1}{z^2}\,dz = \left[-\frac{1}{z}\right]_{\gamma(0)}^{\gamma(
1)} = -\frac{1}{\gamma(1)} + \frac{1}{\gamma(0)} = -\frac{1}{-1} + \frac{1}{1} =
 1 + 1 = 2.$$

**Step 3: Combine.**

$$\int_\gamma \frac{f(z)}{z^2}\,dz = -2 + 2 = \boxed{0}.$$

**Remark on independence of $\gamma$:** The result is independent of the particu
lar path $\gamma$. The key insight is that the boundary data on the lower semici
rcle, combined with holomorphicity, forces $f(z) = z^2+1$ globally (via the iden
tity theorem). The condition $f'(0)=0$ is then automatically satisfied and serve
s as a consistency check. Once $f$ is determined, $\frac{f(z)}{z^2} = 1 + \frac{
1}{z^2}$ has an antiderivative on $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$, so the integral de
pends only on the endpoints $\gamma(0)=1$ and $\gamma(1)=-1$, yielding $0$.
