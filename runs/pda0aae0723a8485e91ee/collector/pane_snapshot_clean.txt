## Solution

We solve $\dot{x} = Ax + g(t)$ with $A = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 3 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bm
atrix}$ and $g(t) = e^{-t}\begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$.

### Step 1. Homogeneous solution — eigenvalues and eigenvectors

The characteristic polynomial of $A$:
$$\det(A - \lambda I) = (-1-\lambda)(1-\lambda) - 3 = \lambda^2 - 4 = 0,$$
so $\lambda_1 = 2$, $\lambda_2 = -2$.

**For $\lambda_1 = 2$:** $(A - 2I) = \begin{bmatrix} -3 & 3 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatr
ix}$, giving eigenvector $v_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$.

**For $\lambda_2 = -2$:** $(A + 2I) = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 1 & 3 \end{bmatri
x}$, giving eigenvector $v_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$.

Hence
$$x_h(t) = c_1 e^{2t}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} + c_2 e^{-2t}\begin{bm
atrix} 3 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}.$$

### Step 2. Particular solution

Since the forcing term is $e^{-t}\begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$ and $-1$
is **not** an eigenvalue of $A$, we try $x_p(t) = e^{-t} v$ with $v$ a constant
vector.

Substituting $\dot{x}_p = -e^{-t} v$:
$$-e^{-t} v = A\, e^{-t} v + e^{-t}\begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix},$$
so
$$-(A + I)v = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad (A +
 I)v = \begin{bmatrix} -3 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix}.$$

Now $A + I = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 3 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$, so we solve
$$\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 3 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} v_1 \\ v_2 \end{bm
atrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -3 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix}.$$

- Row 1: $3v_2 = -3 \Rightarrow v_2 = -1$.
- Row 2: $v_1 + 2v_2 = -2 \Rightarrow v_1 - 2 = -2 \Rightarrow v_1 = 0$.

Thus $v = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$.

**Verification:** $A v = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 3 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bma
trix} 0 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -3 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$, and $Av +
 \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = -
v$. ✓

Therefore
$$x_p(t) = e^{-t}\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}.$$

### Step 3. General solution

Combining the homogeneous and particular solutions:

$$\boxed{\,x(t) = c_1\, e^{2t}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} + c_2\, e^{-2
t}\begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} + e^{-t}\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ -1 \end{bma
trix}\,}$$

i.e.,
$$x_1(t) = c_1 e^{2t} + 3 c_2 e^{-2t}, \qquad x_2(t) = c_1 e^{2t} - c_2 e^{-2t}
- e^{-t},$$
where $c_1, c_2$ are arbitrary constants determined by initial conditions.
