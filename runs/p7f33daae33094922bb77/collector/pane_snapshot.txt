**Claim:** Yes, $DS_1 = DS_2$.

**Proof.**

Recall the setup. $F: M \to N$ is a smooth embedding, i.e. an injective smooth i
mmersion that is a homeomorphism of $M$ onto $F(M)$ (where $F(M)$ carries the su
bspace topology from $N$).

**Step 1 — Description of $DS_1$.** The structure $DS_1$ is the smooth structure
 on $F(M)$ *transported from $M$ via $F$*: its charts are
$$\bigl(F(U_\alpha),\; \varphi_\alpha \circ F^{-1}\bigr), \qquad (U_\alpha,\varp
hi_\alpha)\in\Sigma.$$
Since $F$ is a homeomorphism $M \to F(M)$ (subspace topology), the topology indu
ced by these charts coincides with the subspace topology on $F(M) \subseteq N$.
By construction, $F: M \to (F(M), DS_1)$ is a diffeomorphism. Moreover, the tran
sported structure is **unique**: if $DS$ is any smooth structure on the topologi
cal space $F(M)$ for which $F: M \to (F(M), DS)$ is a diffeomorphism, then $DS =
 DS_1$. Indeed, the identity
$$\mathrm{id} = F \circ F^{-1}: (F(M), DS) \xrightarrow{F^{-1}} M \xrightarrow{F
} (F(M), DS_1)$$
is a composition of diffeomorphisms, hence a diffeomorphism, so $DS = DS_1$.

**Step 2 — $F: M \to (F(M), DS_2)$ is a diffeomorphism.** This is the standard t
heorem on smooth embeddings (Lee, *Introduction to Smooth Manifolds*, Theorem 4.
22 / Proposition 5.2). We recall the argument.

Since $F$ is an immersion, for every $p \in M$ there exist charts $(U,\varphi)$
on $M$ centered at $p$ and $(V,\psi)$ on $N$ centered at $F(p)$ such that the co
ordinate form of $F$ is
$$\psi \circ F \circ \varphi^{-1}(x^1,\dots,x^m) = (x^1,\dots,x^m,0,\dots,0), \q
quad m = \dim M.$$
Because $F$ is an embedding (hence a homeomorphism onto its image), shrinking $U
$ and $V$ if necessary we may arrange $F(U) = V \cap F(M)$. Then $(V \cap F(M),\
, \pi \circ \psi)$ — where $\pi$ projects onto the first $m$ coordinates — is a
slice chart for $F(M)$ in $N$, exhibiting $F(M)$ as a regular submanifold. These
 slice charts define $DS_2$.

In these charts the map $F: M \to (F(M), DS_2)$ reads
$$(\pi\circ\psi)\circ F \circ \varphi^{-1}(x^1,\dots,x^m) = (x^1,\dots,x^m),$$
the identity, and its inverse $(F(U), DS_2) \to U$ reads $\varphi \circ F^{-1} \
circ (\pi\circ\psi|_{V\cap F(M)})^{-1}$, also the identity. Hence $F: M \to (F(M
), DS_2)$ is a local diffeomorphism, and being bijective it is a global diffeomo
rphism.

**Step 3 — Conclusion by uniqueness.** Both $DS_1$ and $DS_2$ are smooth structu
res on the same topological space $F(M)$ (subspace topology), and both make $F:
M \to F(M)$ a diffeomorphism. By the uniqueness established in Step 1,
$$DS_1 = DS_2. \qquad \blacksquare$$
