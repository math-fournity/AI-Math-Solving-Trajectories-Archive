## Solution

We count 100-character sequences over the digit set $\{1,3,5,7,9\}$ such that ev
ery pair of adjacent digits has absolute difference exactly $2$.

**Graph model.** Place the five digits on a path graph $P_5$ in their natural or
der:
$$1 \;-\; 3 \;-\; 5 \;-\; 7 \;-\; 9$$
Two digits differ by exactly $2$ precisely when they are adjacent on this path.
Thus a valid 100-digit key is exactly a walk of length $99$ (100 vertices) on $P
_5$, and the total count is
$$T_{100} \;=\; \mathbf{1}^{\!\top} A^{99}\,\mathbf{1},$$
where $A$ is the adjacency matrix of $P_5$ and $\mathbf{1}=(1,1,1,1,1)^{\!\top}$
.

**Eigenstructure of $P_5$.** The eigenvalues of the path graph $P_5$ are
$$\lambda_k = 2\cos\!\tfrac{k\pi}{6},\qquad k=1,\dots,5,$$
giving $\sqrt{3},\;1,\;0,\;-1,\;-\sqrt{3}$. The eigenvectors for $k=2,4$ (eigenv
alues $1,-1$) are antisymmetric about the center, hence orthogonal to the all-on
es vector $\mathbf{1}$. Therefore $\mathbf{1}$ has nonzero projection only onto
the three symmetric eigenvectors with eigenvalues $\sqrt{3},\,0,\,-\sqrt{3}$, an
d
$$T_n \;=\; \alpha\,(\sqrt{3})^{\,n-1} \;+\; \beta\,(0)^{\,n-1} \;+\; \gamma\,(-
\sqrt{3})^{\,n-1}.$$

**Determining the coefficients.** Using the initial values (computed directly fr
om the recurrence $v_n(d)=\sum_{d'\sim d}v_{n-1}(d')$ with $v_1\equiv 1$):

| $n$ | $T_n$ |
|-----|-------|
| 1 | 5 |
| 2 | 8 |
| 3 | 14 |

From $n=3$: $\;3(\alpha+\gamma)=14\;\Rightarrow\;\alpha+\gamma=\tfrac{14}{3}$.
From $n=1$: $\;\alpha+\beta+\gamma=5\;\Rightarrow\;\beta=\tfrac{1}{3}$.
From $n=2$: $\;\sqrt{3}(\alpha-\gamma)=8\;\Rightarrow\;\alpha-\gamma=\tfrac{8\sq
rt{3}}{3}$.

Solving:
$$\alpha=\frac{7+4\sqrt{3}}{3},\qquad \gamma=\frac{7-4\sqrt{3}}{3},\qquad \beta=
\frac{1}{3}.$$

(One may verify $7\pm4\sqrt{3}=(2\pm\sqrt{3})^2$.) Hence for $n\ge 2$ (where $0^
{\,n-1}=0$):
$$T_n \;=\; \frac{1}{3}\Big[(7+4\sqrt{3})(\sqrt{3})^{\,n-1} \;+\; (7-4\sqrt{3})(
-\sqrt{3})^{\,n-1}\Big].$$

**Evaluating at $n=100$.** Since $99$ is odd, $(-\sqrt{3})^{99}=-(\sqrt{3})^{99}
$, so the two terms combine:
$$T_{100} \;=\; \frac{1}{3}(\sqrt{3})^{99}\Big[(7+4\sqrt{3})-(7-4\sqrt{3})\Big]
\;=\; \frac{1}{3}(\sqrt{3})^{99}\cdot 8\sqrt{3} \;=\; \frac{8}{3}(\sqrt{3})^{100
} \;=\; \frac{8}{3}\cdot 3^{50} \;=\; 8\cdot 3^{49}.$$

**Verification against the recurrence.** The closed form predicts, for even $n=2
k$, $T_{2k}=8\cdot 3^{k-1}$. Checking against the direct recurrence:
- $T_2 = 8\cdot 3^0 = 8$ ✓
- $T_4 = 8\cdot 3^1 = 24$ ✓
- $T_6 = 8\cdot 3^2 = 72$ ✓
- $T_8 = 8\cdot 3^3 = 216$ ✓ (computed from the recurrence: $a_8=27,\,b_8=54,\,c
_8=54$, total $=54+108+54=216$)

All agree, confirming the formula.

## Answer

The total number of unique 100-digit encryption keys satisfying the constraints
