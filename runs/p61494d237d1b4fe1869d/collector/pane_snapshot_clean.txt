## Proof

We show that the only integer solution to $77x^{12}-49y^{12}+2z^{12}=63xyz^{10}$
 is $(x,y,z)=(0,0,0)$.

### Step 1: Solutions with a zero coordinate

**If $y=0$:** The equation becomes $77x^{12}+2z^{12}=0$. Since $x^{12},z^{12}\ge
q 0$ and $77,2>0$, both terms are non-negative, so $x=z=0$.

**If $x=0$:** The equation becomes $2z^{12}=49y^{12}$. Taking $v_7$ (the 7-adic
valuation): $12\,v_7(z)=2+12\,v_7(y)$, i.e. $12(v_7(z)-v_7(y))=2$, which has no
integer solution. Hence $y=z=0$.

**If $z=0$:** The equation becomes $77x^{12}=49y^{12}$, i.e. $11x^{12}=7y^{12}$.
 Taking $v_{11}$: $1+12\,v_{11}(x)=12\,v_{11}(y)$, i.e. $12(v_{11}(y)-v_{11}(x))
=1$, impossible. Hence $x=y=0$.

So any nontrivial solution must have $x,y,z$ all nonzero.

### Step 2: 7-adic valuation argument (all coordinates nonzero)

Assume $x,y,z\neq 0$. Set $\alpha=v_7(x),\ \beta=v_7(y),\ \gamma=v_7(z)$ (non-ne
gative integers). The 7-adic valuations of the four terms are:

| Term | $v_7$ |
|---|---|
| $77x^{12}=7\cdot 11\cdot x^{12}$ | $A:=1+12\alpha$ |
| $49y^{12}=7^2\cdot y^{12}$ | $B:=2+12\beta$ |
| $2z^{12}$ | $C:=12\gamma$ |
| $63xyz^{10}=7\cdot 9\cdot xyz^{10}$ | $R:=1+\alpha+\beta+10\gamma$ |

Since $77x^{12}-49y^{12}+2z^{12}=63xyz^{10}$, we have $v_7(\text{LHS})=v_7(\text
{RHS})=R$. By the ultrametric property, $v_7(\text{LHS})\geq\min(A,B,C)$, with *
*equality if the minimum is unique**.

We analyze which of $A,B,C$ is the minimum.

**Case $A=\min(A,B,C)$.** Then $A\leq B$ gives $1+12\alpha\leq 2+12\beta$, so $\
alpha\leq\beta$. Also $A\leq C$ gives $1+12\alpha\leq 12\gamma$, so $\gamma\geq\
alpha+1$. The condition $R\geq A$ gives:
$$R-A=\beta+10\gamma-11\alpha\geq \alpha+10(\alpha+1)-11\alpha=10>0.$$
So $R\geq A+10$. Now check whether $A$ can be tied: $A=B\Rightarrow 12(\alpha-\b
eta)=1$ (impossible); $A=C\Rightarrow 12(\gamma-\alpha)=1$ (impossible). So $A$
is the **unique** minimum, giving $v_7(\text{LHS})=A<R$. Contradiction.

**Case $B=\min(A,B,C)$.** Then $B\leq A$ gives $\alpha\geq\beta+1$, and $B\leq C
$ gives $\gamma\geq\beta+1$. Then:
$$R-B=\alpha+10\gamma-11\beta-1\geq(\beta+1)+10(\beta+1)-11\beta-1=10>0.$$
So $R\geq B+10$. Ties are impossible: $B=A\Rightarrow 12(\alpha-\beta)=1$; $B=C\
Rightarrow 12(\gamma-\beta)=2$. So $B$ is the unique minimum, giving $v_7(\text{
LHS})=B<R$. Contradiction.

**Case $C=\min(A,B,C)$.** Then $C\leq A$ gives $\gamma\leq\alpha$, and $C\leq B$
 gives $\gamma\leq\beta$. Then:
$$R-C=1+\alpha+\beta-2\gamma\geq 1+\gamma+\gamma-2\gamma=1>0.$$
So $R\geq C+1$. Ties are impossible: $C=A\Rightarrow 12(\gamma-\alpha)=1$; $C=B\
Rightarrow 12(\gamma-\beta)=2$. So $C$ is the unique minimum, giving $v_7(\text{
LHS})=C<R$. Contradiction.

### Conclusion

All cases lead to a contradiction, so no solution with $x,y,z$ all nonzero exist
s. Combined with Step 1, the only integer solution is $\boxed{(0,0,0)}$.
