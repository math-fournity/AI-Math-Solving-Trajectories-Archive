## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{X_n}{Y_n}\right]\to \frac{a}{b}$.

**Assumptions (standard interpretation).** "$\{X_n\}$ is bounded" means there ex
ists a constant $M>0$ such that $|X_n|\le M$ almost surely for every $n$. "$\{Y_
n\}$ is bounded away from zero" means there exists a constant $c>0$ such that $|
Y_n|\ge c$ almost surely for every $n$.

---

**Step 1: Almost-sure limit of the ratio.**

Since $X_n\to a$ a.s. and $Y_n\to b$ a.s., and $b\neq 0$ (because $|Y_n|\ge c>0$
 forces $|b|\ge c>0$), the map $(x,y)\mapsto x/y$ is continuous at $(a,b)$. By t
he continuous mapping theorem,

$$\frac{X_n}{Y_n}\;\xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}}\;\frac{a}{b}.$$

**Step 2: Uniform domination of the ratio.**

For every $n$ and every sample point in the probability-one event on which the b
ounds hold,

$$\left|\frac{X_n}{Y_n}\right|\le \frac{|X_n|}{|Y_n|}\le \frac{M}{c}=:K.$$

Thus the family $\{X_n/Y_n\}_{n\ge 1}$ is uniformly bounded by the deterministic
 constant $K$, hence uniformly integrable.

**Step 3: Apply dominated convergence.**

The random variable $Z:=K$ (a constant) is integrable and satisfies $|X_n/Y_n|\l
e Z$ a.s. for all $n$, with $X_n/Y_n\to a/b$ a.s. By the dominated convergence t
heorem,

$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{X_n}{Y_n}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\mathbb{E}\!\left[\
frac{a}{b}\right]=\frac{a}{b}.$$

**Remark on necessity of the hypotheses.** The boundedness of $X_n$ and the lowe
r bound on $|Y_n|$ together provide the uniform bound $K$ on $|X_n/Y_n|$, which
is exactly what makes dominated convergence applicable. Without such control (e.
g., if only $X_n\to a$ and $Y_n\to b$ a.s. with $b\neq 0$ but no uniform bounds)
, the ratio need not be uniformly integrable, and convergence of expectations ca
n fail. For example, on $[0,1]$ let $Y_n\equiv 1$ and $X_n = n\cdot\mathbf{1}_{[
0,1/n]}$; then $X_n\to 0$ a.s. but $\mathbb{E}[X_n]=1\not\to 0$. So the boundedn
ess hypotheses are essential, and with them the conclusion holds.

---

Therefore, under the stated boundedness conditions,

$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{X_n}{Y_n}\right]\to \frac{a}{b}.}$$
