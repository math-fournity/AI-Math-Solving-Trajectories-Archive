## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. If $X$ and $Y$ are $\mathcal{F}$-Lévy processes (possibly on dif
ferent probability spaces) with $X_t \sim Y_t$ for all $t \geq 0$, then $X$ and
$Y$ have the same finite-dimensional distributions.

**Setup.** Fix $k \in \mathbb{N}$ and $0 \leq t_1 < t_2 < \cdots < t_k$. Set $t_
0 := 0$ and define the **increments**
$$Z_j := X_{t_j} - X_{t_{j-1}}, \qquad j = 1, \dots, k.$$
Note $Z_1 = X_{t_1} - X_0 = X_{t_1}$ since $X_0 = 0$.

**Step 1: The increments $Z_1, \dots, Z_k$ are independent.**

We prove by induction that $Z_j$ is independent of $(Z_1, \dots, Z_{j-1})$ for e
ach $j \geq 2$.

Since $X$ is $\mathcal{F}$-adapted, each $X_{t_i}$ is $\mathcal{F}_{t_i}$-measur
able, hence $Z_i = X_{t_i} - X_{t_{i-1}}$ is $\mathcal{F}_{t_{i-1}}$-measurable.
.. actually $Z_i$ is $\mathcal{F}_{t_i}$-measurable. In particular, $(Z_1, \dots
, Z_{j-1})$ is $\mathcal{F}_{t_{j-1}}$-measurable (since $t_{j-1} \geq t_i$ for
$i \leq j-1$).

By the $\mathcal{F}$-Lévy property, $Z_j = X_{t_j} - X_{t_{j-1}}$ is independent
 of $\mathcal{F}_{t_{j-1}}$. Since $(Z_1, \dots, Z_{j-1})$ is $\mathcal{F}_{t_{j
-1}}$-measurable, $Z_j$ is independent of $(Z_1, \dots, Z_{j-1})$.

By induction (a standard result: if each $Z_j$ is independent of the $\sigma$-al
gebra generated by all previous ones, then all are jointly independent), $Z_1, \
dots, Z_k$ are mutually independent.

**Step 2: The law of each $Z_j$ is determined by a one-dimensional marginal.**

By the stationary increments property, $Z_j = X_{t_j} - X_{t_{j-1}} \sim X_{t_j
- t_{j-1}}$. Denoting by $\mu_t$ the law of $X_t$ on $(E, \mathcal{B}(E))$, we h
ave
$$Z_j \sim \mu_{t_j - t_{j-1}}, \qquad j = 1, \dots, k.$$

Combining Steps 1 and 2, the joint law of $(Z_1, \dots, Z_k)$ on $E^k$ is the **
product measure**
$$\mu_{t_1} \otimes \mu_{t_2 - t_1} \otimes \cdots \otimes \mu_{t_k - t_{k-1}}.$
$

**Step 3: Recover the joint law of $(X_{t_1}, \dots, X_{t_k})$ from the incremen
ts.**

Define the continuous linear map $T: E^k \to E^k$ by
$$T(z_1, \dots, z_k) = (z_1,\; z_1 + z_2,\; \dots,\; z_1 + z_2 + \cdots + z_k).$
$
Its inverse $T^{-1}(x_1, \dots, x_k) = (x_1,\; x_2 - x_1,\; \dots,\; x_k - x_{k-
1})$ is also continuous. Hence $T$ is a Borel isomorphism of $E^k$.

Since $(X_{t_1}, \dots, X_{t_k}) = T(Z_1, \dots, Z_k)$, for any $B_1, \dots, B_k
 \in \mathcal{B}(E)$:
$$\operatorname{P}\!\left[X_{t_1} \in B_1, \dots, X_{t_k} \in B_k\right] = \bigl
(\mu_{t_1} \otimes \mu_{t_2 - t_1} \otimes \cdots \otimes \mu_{t_k - t_{k-1}}\bi
gr)\!\left(T^{-1}(B_1 \times \cdots \times B_k)\right).$$

**Step 4: The same holds for $Y$, giving identical probabilities.**

The entire argument for $X$ used only:
- $X_0 = 0$,
- independent increments (independence of $X_{t_j} - X_{t_{j-1}}$ from $\mathcal
{F}_{t_{j-1}}$),
- stationary increments ($X_{t_j} - X_{t_{j-1}} \sim X_{t_j - t_{j-1}}$),

Since $Y$ is also $\mathcal{F}$-Lévy and $Y_t \sim X_t$ for all $t$ (so $\operat
orname{Law}(Y_t) = \mu_t$), the identical computation yields
$$\operatorname{P}\!\left[Y_{t_1} \in B_1, \dots, Y_{t_k} \in B_k\right] = \bigl
(\mu_{t_1} \otimes \mu_{t_2 - t_1} \otimes \cdots \otimes \mu_{t_k - t_{k-1}}\bi
gr)\!\left(T^{-1}(B_1 \times \cdots \times B_k)\right).$$

The right-hand sides are **equal**, being expressed purely in terms of the measu
res $\mu_t$ (which are common to $X$ and $Y$) and the fixed map $T$.

$$\operatorname{P}\!\left[X_{t_1} \in B_1, \dots, X_{t_k} \in B_k\right] = \oper
