## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. For any $n \times n$ complex matrix $A$ with $A_{ii}=1$ for all
$i$, the column space of $A$ contains a vector all of whose components are nonze
ro.

**Setup.** Let $A \in \mathbb{C}^{n\times n}$ with $A_{ii}=1$. A vector $b$ lies
 in the column space of $A$ iff $b = Ax$ for some $x \in \mathbb{C}^n$. We seek
$x$ such that every component of $Ax$ is nonzero, i.e.

$$(Ax)_i = \sum_{j=1}^{n} A_{ij}\,x_j \neq 0 \quad \text{for all } i = 1,\dots,n
.$$

Since $A_{ii}=1$, the $i$-th row of $A$ is not identically zero, so the linear f
orm

$$L_i(x) := (Ax)_i = e_i^{\!\top}\! A\, x$$

is a **nonzero** linear form on $\mathbb{C}^n$.

**Key argument.** Consider the polynomial

$$P(x) := \prod_{i=1}^{n} L_i(x).$$

This is a product of $n$ nonzero linear forms, hence a nonzero polynomial of tot
al degree $n$ on $\mathbb{C}^n$. A fundamental fact: **a nonzero polynomial over
 $\mathbb{C}$ (or any infinite field) cannot vanish identically on the whole spa
ce.** (For instance, restrict to a generic line $x = x_0 + t\,v$; the univariate
 polynomial $P(x_0+tv)$ is nonzero for a suitable choice of $x_0, v$ avoiding al
l the finitely many "bad" directions, and a nonzero univariate polynomial has on
ly finitely many roots.)

Therefore there exists $x^* \in \mathbb{C}^n$ with $P(x^*) \neq 0$. By definitio
n of $P$, this means

$$L_i(x^*) = (Ax^*)_i \neq 0 \quad \text{for every } i = 1,\dots,n.$$

**Conclusion.** The vector $b := Ax^*$ lies in the column space of $A$ and has e
very component nonzero. Hence the column space necessarily contains a vector wit
h all nonzero components. $\blacksquare$
