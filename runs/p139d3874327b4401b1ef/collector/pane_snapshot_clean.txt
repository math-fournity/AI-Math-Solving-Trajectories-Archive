## Proof

**Yes, it is possible.** We construct an explicit discontinuous function between
 two spaces each carrying the uniform topology (and hence each having all pairwi
se distances at most 1).

### Setup

Recall the **uniform metric** on $\mathbb{R}^J$:
$$\bar{\rho}(x, y) = \sup_{\alpha \in J}\, \bar{d}(x_\alpha, y_\alpha), \qquad \
bar{d}(a,b) = \min\{|a-b|,\, 1\}.$$
Since $\bar{d} \le 1$, we have $\bar{\rho}(x,y) \le 1$ for all $x, y$. The topol
ogy induced by $\bar{\rho}$ is the **uniform topology**. On $\mathbb{R}$ itself
(the case $|J|=1$), $\bar{\rho}(x,y) = \min\{|x-y|,1\}$ induces the **same topol
ogy** as the usual metric $|x-y|$ (they agree on all balls of radius $< 1$), and
 also satisfies $\bar{\rho} \le 1$.

Define the two spaces:
- $X = \mathbb{R}^{\omega}$ equipped with the uniform metric $\bar{\rho}$.
- $Y = \mathbb{R}$ equipped with the uniform metric $\bar{\rho}_Y(a,b) = \min\{|
a-b|, 1\}$.

Both carry the uniform topology, and in both the distance between any two points
 is at most $1$.

### The function

Define $f : X \to Y$ by
$$f(x_1, x_2, x_3, \dots) = \begin{cases} 1, & x_1 \ge 0, \\ 0, & x_1 < 0. \end{
cases}$$

### Discontinuity

Let $a = (0, 0, 0, \dots) \in X$ and consider the sequence $b^{(n)} = (-\tfrac{1
}{n},\, 0,\, 0,\, \dots) \in X$.

**Convergence in the domain.** We have
$$\bar{\rho}(b^{(n)}, a) = \sup_{k} \bar{d}\!\big(b^{(n)}_k,\, a_k\big) = \bar{d
}\!\big(-\tfrac{1}{n},\, 0\big) = \tfrac{1}{n} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.$$
So $b^{(n)} \to a$ in the uniform topology on $X$.

**Non-convergence of images.** We have $f(a) = 1$ (since $a_1 = 0 \ge 0$) and $f
(b^{(n)}) = 0$ for every $n$ (since $b^{(n)}_1 = -1/n < 0$). Thus
$$\bar{\rho}_Y\!\big(f(b^{(n)}),\, f(a)\big) = \min\{|0 - 1|,\, 1\} = 1 \quad \t
ext{for all } n,$$
which does **not** tend to $0$. Hence $f(b^{(n)}) \not\to f(a)$ in $Y$.

### Conclusion

A function between metric spaces is continuous iff it is sequentially continuous
. We have exhibited a sequence $b^{(n)} \to a$ in $X$ whose images do not conver
ge to $f(a)$ in $Y$. Therefore $f$ is **discontinuous** at $a$.

Both $X = \mathbb{R}^{\omega}$ and $Y = \mathbb{R}$ are equipped with the unifor
m topology, and in both spaces the distance between any two points is at most $1
$. This gives an explicit construction of a discontinuous function between two s
uch spaces. $\blacksquare$
