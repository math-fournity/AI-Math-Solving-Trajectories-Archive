**Answer:** Yes, it is true. If $\sum_{i \in I} x_i$ converges to $x$ in the sen
se defined, then $\{i \in I : x_i \neq 0\}$ is at most countable.

### Proof

**Step 1. A tail-estimate lemma.**

Assume $\sum_{i \in I} x_i$ converges to $x$. We claim:

> For every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists a finite set $F_\varepsilon \subseteq
 I$ such that for every finite set $K \subseteq I \setminus F_\varepsilon$,
> $$\Bigl\|\sum_{i \in K} x_i\Bigr\| < 2\varepsilon.$$

Indeed, by convergence applied with $\varepsilon$, there is a finite $F_\varepsi
lon \subseteq I$ such that for every finite $J$ with $F_\varepsilon \subseteq J
\subseteq I$,
$$\Bigl\|\sum_{i \in J} x_i - x\Bigr\| < \varepsilon. \tag{$*$}$$

Let $K \subseteq I \setminus F_\varepsilon$ be finite. Both $F_\varepsilon$ and
$F_\varepsilon \cup K$ are finite supersets of $F_\varepsilon$, so by $(*)$:
$$\Bigl\|\sum_{i \in F_\varepsilon} x_i - x\Bigr\| < \varepsilon, \qquad \Bigl\|
\sum_{i \in F_\varepsilon \cup K} x_i - x\Bigr\| < \varepsilon.$$

Subtracting (and using that $F_\varepsilon$ and $K$ are disjoint),
$$\Bigl\|\sum_{i \in K} x_i\Bigr\| = \Bigl\|\sum_{i \in F_\varepsilon \cup K} x_
i - \sum_{i \in F_\varepsilon} x_i\Bigr\| \le \Bigl\|\sum_{i \in F_\varepsilon \
cup K} x_i - x\Bigr\| + \Bigl\|x - \sum_{i \in F_\varepsilon} x_i\Bigr\| < 2\var
epsilon.$$

**Step 2. Each "large-term" set is finite.**

For each $n \in \mathbb{N}$, define
$$S_n := \bigl\{i \in I : \|x_i\| \ge \tfrac{1}{n}\bigr\}.$$

We show $S_n$ is finite. Apply Step 1 with $\varepsilon = \frac{1}{2n}$ to obtai
n a finite $F \subseteq I$ such that every finite $K \subseteq I \setminus F$ sa
tisfies $\|\sum_{i \in K} x_i\| < \frac{1}{n}$.

Suppose, for contradiction, that $S_n$ is infinite. Since $F$ is finite, $S_n \s
etminus F$ is nonempty; pick any $i \in S_n \setminus F$ and take $K = \{i\} \su
bseteq I \setminus F$. Then
$$\|x_i\| = \Bigl\|\sum_{j \in K} x_j\Bigr\| < \frac{1}{n},$$
contradicting $i \in S_n$ (which requires $\|x_i\| \ge \frac{1}{n}$). Hence $S_n
$ is finite.

**Step 3. The nonzero set is a countable union of finite sets.**

Observe
$$\{i \in I : x_i \neq 0\} = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} S_n,$$
since $\|x_i\| > 0$ iff $\|x_i\| \ge \frac{1}{n}$ for some $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

Each $S_n$ is finite by Step 2, so the union is a countable union of finite sets
, which is at most countable.

Therefore, if $\sum_{i \in I} x_i$ converges to $x$ in the given (unordered / un
conditional) sense, then $x_i$ is nonzero for at most countably many indices $i
\in I$. $\blacksquare$
