## Proof

**Claim:** For $1 < p < 2$, the closure of $L^1(\mathbb{R}) \cap L^p(\mathbb{R})
$ in the $L^p$-norm equals $L^p(\mathbb{R})$.

**Proof.**

We show that $L^1(\mathbb{R}) \cap L^p(\mathbb{R})$ is dense in $L^p(\mathbb{R})
$.

Let $f \in L^p(\mathbb{R})$. For each $n, m \in \mathbb{N}$, define

$$f_{n,m}(x) = f(x) \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{|f(x)| \leq n\}}(x) \cdot \mathbf{1}_{[-
m,m]}(x).$$

**Step 1: $f_{n,m} \in L^1(\mathbb{R}) \cap L^p(\mathbb{R})$.**

Since $|f_{n,m}| \leq n$ and $\operatorname{supp}(f_{n,m}) \subseteq [-m,m]$, we
 have

$$\|f_{n,m}\|_1 = \int_{-m}^{m} |f_{n,m}|\,dx \leq n \cdot 2m < \infty,$$

so $f_{n,m} \in L^1(\mathbb{R})$. Also,

$$\|f_{n,m}\|_p^p = \int_{-m}^{m} |f_{n,m}|^p\,dx \leq \int_{\mathbb{R}} |f|^p\,
dx < \infty,$$

so $f_{n,m} \in L^p(\mathbb{R})$.

**Step 2: $f_{n,m} \to f$ in $L^p$ as $n, m \to \infty$.**

We compute:

$$\|f_{n,m} - f\|_p^p = \int_{\mathbb{R}} |f(x)|^p \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{|f(x)| >
n\} \cup \{|x| > m\}}(x)\,dx.$$

The integrand is dominated by $|f(x)|^p$, which is integrable since $f \in L^p$.
 Moreover, the integrand converges to $0$ pointwise as $n, m \to \infty$ (since
$|f(x)|^p \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{|f|>n\}\cup\{|x|>m\}} \to 0$ for every $x$). By th
e Dominated Convergence Theorem,

$$\lim_{n,m \to \infty} \|f_{n,m} - f\|_p^p = 0.$$

**Conclusion.**

Every $f \in L^p(\mathbb{R})$ is the $L^p$-limit of the sequence $\{f_{n,n}\}_{n
=1}^\infty \subset L^1(\mathbb{R}) \cap L^p(\mathbb{R})$. Therefore $L^1(\mathbb
{R}) \cap L^p(\mathbb{R})$ is dense in $L^p(\mathbb{R})$, i.e.,

$$\overline{L^1(\mathbb{R}) \cap L^p(\mathbb{R})}^{\,L^p} = L^p(\mathbb{R}).$$

**Remark.** The argument uses only $1 \leq p < \infty$; the restriction $1 < p <
 2$ is not essential. The key ingredients are: (i) truncation in the range to ob
tain boundedness, and (ii) truncation in the domain to obtain compact support, b
oth of which preserve $L^p$ membership while ensuring $L^1$ membership via bound
edness times finite measure.
