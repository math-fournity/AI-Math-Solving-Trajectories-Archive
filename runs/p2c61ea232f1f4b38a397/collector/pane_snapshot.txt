## Proof

**Definition.** The *Kuratowski character* of a subset $A$ of a topological spac
e $X$ is the number of distinct sets obtainable from $A$ by repeatedly applying
the closure operator $k$ and the complement operator $c$ (in any order, any fini
te number of times). Equivalently, it is the cardinality of the orbit of $A$ und
er the semigroup $\langle k, c \rangle$ generated by closure and complement.

**Kuratowski's Closure-Complement Theorem (1922).** For any subset $A$ of any to
pological space $X$, the Kuratowski character of $A$ is at most **14**.

*Proof of the theorem (sketch).* The semigroup $\langle k, c \rangle$ is subject
 to the relations:
- $c^2 = \mathrm{id}$ (complement is an involution),
- $k^2 = k$ (closure is idempotent),
- $kckckck = kck$ (the key Kuratowski relation).

Since $c^2 = \mathrm{id}$ and $k^2 = k$, every word in $k$ and $c$ reduces to an
 alternating word. The alternating words starting with $k$ are:
$$k,\; kc,\; kck,\; kckc,\; kckck,\; kckckc,$$
and the relation $kckckck = kck$ shows that no further distinct $k$-initiated wo
rds arise (the sequence stabilizes). This gives **6** such words.

The alternating words starting with $c$ are:
$$c,\; ck,\; ckc,\; ckck,\; ckckc,\; ckckck,\; ckckckc,$$
and the relation $(ck)^4 = (ck)^2$ (obtained by left-composing $c$ with $kckckck
 = kck$) shows stabilization. This gives **7** such words.

Together with the identity, the semigroup has at most $1 + 6 + 7 = 14$ elements.
 Hence the orbit of any $A$ has at most 14 elements. $\square$

**Sharpness.** The bound 14 is achievable: there exist topological spaces (e.g.,
 a suitable subset of $\mathbb{R}$ with the standard topology) and subsets $A$ w
hose Kuratowski orbit has exactly 14 distinct elements.

**Main Argument.** Since the Kuratowski character of any subset of any topologic
al space is at most 14, **no subset can have Kuratowski character 15**. The numb
er 15 exceeds the universal maximum of 14, so the premise

> "there exists a subset $A$ with Kuratowski character 15"

is **always false** in every topological space.

The statement to be evaluated is the implication:

$$\bigl(\exists A \subseteq X :\ \text{Kuratowski character of } A = 15\bigr) \;
\Longrightarrow\; \bigl(\exists B \subseteq X :\ \text{Kuratowski character of }
 B = 24\bigr).$$

Since the antecedent is always false (no subset of any topological space can hav
e Kuratowski character exceeding 14), the implication is **vacuously true**.

Indeed, the consequent is also always false—no subset can have Kuratowski charac
ter 24 either—but this is irrelevant: an implication with a false antecedent is
true regardless of the truth value of the consequent.

**Conclusion.** Yes. If there exists a subset with Kuratowski character 15, then
 there must also exist a subset with Kuratowski character 24—**vacuously**, beca
use the Kuratowski closure-complement theorem guarantees that no subset of any t
opological space can have Kuratowski character greater than 14, rendering the hy
pothesis impossible.
