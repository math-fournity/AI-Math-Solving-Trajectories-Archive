## Solution

Let $t = \sqrt{x}$, so $x = t^{2}$ and $t \to \infty$ as $x \to \infty$. Define

$$
F(t)=\left(\frac{t+c}{t-c}\right)^{t^{2}}e^{-2ct}.
$$

Taking logarithms,

$$
\ln F(t)=t^{2}\ln\!\frac{t+c}{t-c}-2ct.
$$

Using the Taylor expansion $\ln(1+u)=u-\tfrac{u^{2}}{2}+\tfrac{u^{3}}{3}-\cdots$
 for $|u|<1$ (valid for large $t$),

$$
\ln\frac{t+c}{t-c}=\ln\!\left(1+\frac{c}{t}\right)-\ln\!\left(1-\frac{c}{t}\righ
t)=2\!\left[\frac{c}{t}+\frac{c^{3}}{3t^{3}}+\frac{c^{5}}{5t^{5}}+\cdots\right].
$$

Multiplying by $t^{2}$,

$$
t^{2}\ln\frac{t+c}{t-c}=2\!\left[ct+\frac{c^{3}}{3t}+\frac{c^{5}}{5t^{3}}+\cdots
\right].
$$

Therefore

$$
\ln F(t)=2ct+\frac{2c^{3}}{3t}+\frac{2c^{5}}{5t^{3}}+\cdots-2ct=\frac{2c^{3}}{3t
}+\frac{2c^{5}}{5t^{3}}+O(t^{-5}).
$$

Substituting back $t=\sqrt{x}$,

$$
\ln F(x)=\frac{2c^{3}}{3\sqrt{x}}+\frac{2c^{5}}{5x^{3/2}}+O(x^{-5/2}).
$$

Exponentiating,

$$
F(x)=\exp\!\left(\frac{2c^{3}}{3\sqrt{x}}+O(x^{-3/2})\right)=1+\frac{2c^{3}}{3\s
qrt{x}}+\frac{2c^{6}}{9x}+O(x^{-3/2}).
$$
