Looking at this problem, I need to count zeros of $f(z) = z^3 + 2z^2 - z - 2 + e
^z$ in the left half-plane $\{z : \operatorname{Re}(z) < 0\}$.

## Setup

Factor the polynomial part:
$$z^3 + 2z^2 - z - 2 = z^2(z+2) - (z+2) = (z^2-1)(z+2) = (z-1)(z+1)(z+2).$$

So $f(z) = (z-1)(z+1)(z+2) + e^z$.

**Key observation:** In the left half-plane, $|e^z| = e^{\operatorname{Re}(z)} \
leq 1$, while $|z^3+2z^2-z-2| \to \infty$ as $|z|\to\infty$. So for large $|z|$
in the left half-plane, $f(z)\neq 0$, meaning there are only **finitely many** z
eros there. The argument principle on a large semicircular contour is valid.

## Real zeros (sanity check)

On the real line, $f(x) = (x-1)(x+1)(x+2) + e^x$:

| $x$ | $f(x)$ |
|-----|--------|
| $-3$ | $-8 + e^{-3} < 0$ |
| $-2$ | $0 + e^{-2} > 0$ |
| $-1$ | $0 + e^{-1} > 0$ |
| $0$ | $-2 + 1 = -1 < 0$ |
| $1$ | $0 + e > 0$ |

So there are real zeros in $(-3,-2)$, $(-1,0)$, and $(0,1)$. The first two lie i
n the left half-plane.

## Argument principle

Take the contour $C_R$: the imaginary axis segment from $-iR$ to $iR$, then the
left semicircle $z=Re^{i\theta}$, $\theta\in[\pi/2,\,3\pi/2]$, traversed counter
clockwise around the left half-plane.

**No zeros on the contour:** On the imaginary axis, $z=iy$:
$$f(iy) = \underbrace{(-2y^2 - 2 + \cos y)}_{u(y)} + i\underbrace{(-y^3 - y + \s
in y)}_{v(y)}.$$
Since $\cos y \leq 1$, we have $u(y) \leq -2y^2 - 1 < 0$ for all $y$, so $f(iy)\
neq 0$. On the semicircle, for large $R$: $|e^z|\leq 1 < R^3 - 2R^2 - R - 2 \leq
 |z^3+2z^2-z-2|$, so $f\neq 0$ by the reverse triangle inequality.

**Semicircle contribution:** For large $R$, $f(z)\approx z^3$ on the semicircle.
 The argument of $z^3 = R^3 e^{3i\theta}$ changes by $3\cdot(3\pi/2 - \pi/2) = 3
\pi$.

**Imaginary axis contribution:** We track $\arg(u(y)+iv(y))$ as $y$ goes from $-
R$ to $R$.

- $u(y) < 0$ always, so the curve stays in the left half $\{u<0\}$ of the $u$-$v
$ plane.
- $v(y)$ is odd. Compute $v'(y) = -3y^2 - 1 + \cos y \leq -3y^2 \leq 0$, with eq
uality only at $y=0$. So $v$ is **strictly decreasing**, with $v(0)=0$. Thus $v(
y)>0$ for $y<0$ and $v(y)<0$ for $y>0$.

The curve crosses the negative real axis **exactly once** (at $y=0$). Tracing th
e continuous argument:

- At $y=-R$ (large): $u\approx -2R^2$, $v\approx R^3>0$, so $\arg\to \pi/2$ from
 above.
- At $y=0$: $u=-1$, $v=0$, so $\arg = \pi$.
- At $y=R$ (large): $u\approx -2R^2$, $v\approx -R^3<0$, so $\arg\to 3\pi/2$ fro
m below.

The argument increases continuously from $\pi/2$ to $3\pi/2$, a change of $\pi$.

## Counting zeros

Total argument change $= \pi + 3\pi = 4\pi$.

$$\text{Number of zeros} = \frac{4\pi}{2\pi} = 2.$$

This is consistent with the two real zeros found in $(-3,-2)$ and $(-1,0)$, conf
irming there are **no non-real zeros** in the left half-plane.
