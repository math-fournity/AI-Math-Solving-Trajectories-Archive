**Answer: TRUE.** The image of a compact operator cannot contain an infinite-dim
ensional closed subspace.

**Proof.**

Let $X, Y$ be infinite-dimensional Banach spaces and $T: X \to Y$ a compact line
ar operator. Suppose, for contradiction, that $T(X)$ contains an infinite-dimens
ional closed subspace $M$ of $Y$.

**Step 1: Reduce to a surjective compact operator onto $M$.**

Set $X_M := T^{-1}(M) = \{x \in X : Tx \in M\}$. Since $T$ is continuous and $M$
 is closed in $Y$, $X_M$ is a closed subspace of $X$, hence a Banach space. Beca
use $M \subseteq T(X)$, the restriction
$$T\big|_{X_M} : X_M \longrightarrow M$$
is **surjective**: for every $y \in M$ there exists $x \in X$ with $Tx = y$, and
 such $x$ belongs to $X_M$ by definition.

Moreover, $T\big|_{X_M}$ is **compact**, being the restriction of a compact oper
ator to a closed subspace.

**Step 2: Apply the Open Mapping Theorem.**

Both $X_M$ and $M$ are Banach spaces, and $T\big|_{X_M}: X_M \to M$ is a bounded
 surjective linear operator. By the Open Mapping Theorem, it is an open map. Con
sequently, there exists $r > 0$ such that
$$r \cdot B_M \;\subseteq\; T\big(B_{X_M}\big),$$
where $B_{X_M}$ and $B_M$ denote the closed unit balls. Equivalently,
$$B_M \;\subseteq\; \tfrac{1}{r}\, T\big(B_{X_M}\big).$$

**Step 3: Use compactness to force $B_M$ to be compact.**

Since $T$ is compact, $T(B_{X_M})$ is relatively compact in $Y$. Therefore $\tfr
ac{1}{r}T(B_{X_M})$ is relatively compact, and its closure
$$K := \overline{\tfrac{1}{r}\,T(B_{X_M})}$$
is compact in $Y$. From Step 2,
$$B_M \subseteq K.$$

Now $M$ is closed in $Y$, and $B_M$ is closed in $M$ (hence closed in $Y$). Thus
 $B_M$ is a closed subset of the compact set $K$, so $B_M$ is **compact**.

**Step 4: Contradiction with infinite-dimensionality.**

By Riesz's lemma, the closed unit ball of a normed space is compact if and only
if the space is finite-dimensional. Since $M$ is infinite-dimensional, $B_M$ can
not be compact. This contradicts Step 3.

**Conclusion.**

The assumption that $T(X)$ contains an infinite-dimensional closed subspace lead
s to a contradiction. Therefore the image of a compact operator $T: X \to Y$ can
not contain any infinite-dimensional closed subspace of $Y$. $\blacksquare$
