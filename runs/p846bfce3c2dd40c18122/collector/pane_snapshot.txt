## Solution

**Answer: The universal cover of $Gr_2(4)$ is $S^2 \times S^2$.**

### Proof

**Step 1: Identify $Gr_2(4)$ as a homogeneous space.**

The Grassmann manifold of 2-planes in $\mathbb{R}^4$ is
$$Gr_2(4) = O(4)/(O(2)\times O(2)).$$
Its **oriented** version (the Grassmannian of *oriented* 2-planes) is
$$\widetilde{Gr}_2(4) = SO(4)/(SO(2)\times SO(2)) = SO(4)/T^2,$$
where $T^2 = SO(2)\times SO(2)$ is the maximal torus of $SO(4)$. The natural map
 $\widetilde{Gr}_2(4) \to Gr_2(4)$ is a 2-fold covering (each unoriented plane h
as exactly two orientations).

**Step 2: Compute $\pi_1(Gr_2(4))$.**

For $1 < k < n-1$, it is a standard fact that $\pi_1(Gr_k(\mathbb{R}^n)) \cong \
mathbb{Z}/2$. Thus $\pi_1(Gr_2(4)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$, so the universal cover i
s a double cover. It suffices to show $\widetilde{Gr}_2(4)$ is simply connected.

**Step 3: Compute $\pi_1(\widetilde{Gr}_2(4))$ via the long exact sequence.**

From the fibration $T^2 \hookrightarrow SO(4) \to SO(4)/T^2$, the long exact hom
otopy sequence gives:
$$\cdots \to \pi_1(T^2) \xrightarrow{i_*} \pi_1(SO(4)) \to \pi_1(SO(4)/T^2) \to
\pi_0(T^2) \to \cdots$$

We have $\pi_1(T^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$, $\pi_1(SO(4)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$, and
$\pi_0(T^2)=0$. The map $i_*$ is induced by the inclusion of each $SO(2)$ factor
 into $SO(4)$. Each $S^1 = SO(2)$ factor rotates a 2-plane, and the loop rotatin
g through $2\pi$ represents the generator of $\pi_1(SO(4)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$.
Hence **each generator** of $\mathbb{Z}^2$ maps surjectively onto $\mathbb{Z}/2$
, so $i_*$ is surjective. Therefore:
$$\pi_1(SO(4)/T^2) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2)/\operatorname{im}(i_*) = 0.$$

The oriented Grassmannian $\widetilde{Gr}_2(4)$ is **simply connected**, so it i
s the universal cover of $Gr_2(4)$.

**Step 4: Identify $\widetilde{Gr}_2(4) \cong S^2 \times S^2$.**

The universal cover of $SO(4)$ is $\operatorname{Spin}(4) \cong SU(2)\times SU(2
) \cong S^3 \times S^3$, with covering kernel $\{(1,1),(-1,-1)\} \cong \mathbb{Z
}/2$. The maximal torus of $\operatorname{Spin}(4)$ is $\tilde{T}^2 = S^1 \times
 S^1 \subset SU(2)\times SU(2)$, and the restriction $\tilde{T}^2 \to T^2$ is a
double cover whose kernel is $\{(1,1),(-1,-1)\}$ — the same $\mathbb{Z}/2$.

Now consider the map $\operatorname{Spin}(4)/\tilde{T}^2 \to SO(4)/T^2$. The fib
er over $gT^2$ consists of cosets $\tilde{g}\,\tilde{T}^2$ where $\tilde{g}$ is
a lift of $g$. There are two lifts $\tilde{g}$ and $\tilde{g}\cdot(-1,-1)$, but
since $(-1,-1)\in \tilde{T}^2$, these give the **same** coset. Hence the map is
a **bijection** (a homeomorphism), confirming:
$$\widetilde{Gr}_2(4) = SO(4)/T^2 \cong \operatorname{Spin}(4)/\tilde{T}^2 = (SU
(2)\times SU(2))/(S^1\times S^1).$$

Since the quotient splits over the product:
$$(SU(2)\times SU(2))/(S^1\times S^1) \cong (SU(2)/S^1)\times(SU(2)/S^1) \cong S
^2 \times S^2.$$

**Dimension check:** $\dim(S^2\times S^2) = 4 = \dim Gr_2(4)$. ✓

**Conclusion:** The universal cover of $Gr_2(4)$ is $\boxed{S^2 \times S^2}$.
