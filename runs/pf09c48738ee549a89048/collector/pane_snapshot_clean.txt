## Analysis

The condition is $n \mid 1 + \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} k^{n-1}$, i.e., $\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
k^{n-1} \equiv -1 \pmod{n}$.

For $n \geq 2$, $0^{n-1} = 0$, so this is equivalent to:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n-1} k^{n-1} \equiv -1 \pmod{n}. \tag{$\star$}$$

### The forward direction (prime $\Rightarrow$ condition) is easy

If $p$ is prime, Fermat's little theorem gives $k^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$ for e
ach $k = 1, \dots, p-1$. Hence $\sum_{k=1}^{p-1} k^{p-1} \equiv p-1 \equiv -1 \p
mod{p}$, so $(\star)$ holds. ✓

### The reverse direction (condition $\Rightarrow$ prime) is an open problem

The statement that $(\star)$ holds **only** for primes is **Giuga's conjecture**
 (1950). This conjecture remains **open** — it has neither been proven nor refut
ed.

**What is known:**

site and satisfies $(\star)$, one can show $n$ must be squarefree and for every
prime $p \mid n$, $(p-1) \mid (n-1)$ — i.e., $n$ is a Carmichael number (by Kors
elt's criterion). The argument: if $p^2 \mid n$ for some prime $p$, then working
 modulo $p^2$ and using the structure of the sum forces a contradiction with $(\
star)$; if $p \mid n$ but $(p-1) \nmid (n-1)$, then there exists a unit $a$ with
 $a^{n-1} \not\equiv 1 \pmod{p}$, and summing $a^{n-1} \cdot k^{n-1}$ over $k$ y
ields a contradiction.
