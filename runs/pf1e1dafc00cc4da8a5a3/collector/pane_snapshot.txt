**Claim.** The unique analytic function $f$ on the domain $D=\{z:|z|<1\}$ satisf
ying $f\!\left(\tfrac{1}{n}\right)=\tfrac{1}{n^{3}}$ for every positive integer
$n$ is

$$f(z)=z^{3}.$$

**Proof.**

Define $g(z)=z^{3}$. This is a polynomial, hence analytic (holomorphic) on all o
f $\mathbb{C}$, and in particular on $D$.

For every positive integer $n\ge 1$, we have $\tfrac{1}{n}\in D$ (since $0<\tfra
c{1}{n}\le 1<1$... more precisely $\tfrac{1}{n}<1$ for $n\ge 2$, and for $n=1$,
$\tfrac{1}{1}=1$ which lies on the boundary; however the condition is imposed fo

strictly inside $D$, with $n=1$ giving the boundary point $1\notin D$). The rele
vant set of interior points is

$$S=\left\{\tfrac{1}{n}:n\ge 2\right\}\subset D.$$

The set $S$ has an accumulation point: $0=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}$, and $0
\in D$.

By hypothesis, $f\!\left(\tfrac{1}{n}\right)=\tfrac{1}{n^{3}}=g\!\left(\tfrac{1}
{n}\right)$ for every $n\ge 2$. Thus $f$ and $g$ agree on the set $S\subset D$,
and $S$ has an accumulation point $0$ lying in $D$.

By the **Identity Theorem** for holomorphic functions: *if two functions analyti
c on a connected open set agree on a subset that has an accumulation point withi
n that open set, then the two functions are identical on the entire open set.*

The domain $D=\{z:|z|<1\}$ is connected (it is a disk). Both $f$ and $g$ are ana
lytic on $D$. They agree on $S$, whose accumulation point $0$ belongs to $D$. Th
erefore

$$f(z)=g(z)=z^{3}\quad\text{for all }z\in D.$$

**Verification.** $f(z)=z^{3}$ is analytic on $D$, and $f\!\left(\tfrac{1}{n}\ri
ght)=\left(\tfrac{1}{n}\right)^{3}=\tfrac{1}{n^{3}}$ for every positive integer
$n$. The boundary value at $n=1$ (i.e., $z=1$) is consistent with the formula, t

**Uniqueness** is guaranteed by the Identity Theorem: any two analytic functions

Therefore the unique analytic function satisfying the given condition is $\boxed
