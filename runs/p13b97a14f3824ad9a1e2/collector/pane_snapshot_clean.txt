# Proof: Every Hilbert space admits an operator space structure

## Answer

**Yes, it is possible.** Every Hilbert space can be endowed with an operator spa
ce structure. In fact, there are several natural and inequivalent such structure
s. Below I give an explicit construction.

---

## Preliminaries

Recall that an **operator space** is a closed subspace $E \subseteq \mathcal{B}(
\mathcal{K})$ of bounded operators on some Hilbert space $\mathcal{K}$, equipped
 with the inherited matrix norms: for $[x_{ij}] \in M_n(E)$,

$$\|[x_{ij}]\|_n \;=\; \|[x_{ij}]\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K}^{(n)})},$$

where $[x_{ij}]$ acts on $\mathcal{K} \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathcal{K}$ ($n$ cop
ies) in the natural way. By Ruan's theorem, an abstract operator space is a Bana
ch space $E$ together with a sequence of norms $\|\cdot\|_n$ on $M_n(E)$ satisfy
ing the Ruan axioms (R1) and (R2); and every such abstract structure arises as a
 closed subspace of some $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})$.

Thus, to give a Hilbert space $H$ an operator space structure, it suffices to ex
hibit a linear isometry $\iota: H \to \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})$ for some Hilbert
 space $\mathcal{K}$; the image $\iota(H)$ is then a closed subspace of $\mathca
l{B}(\mathcal{K})$ and inherits an operator space structure, which we pull back
to $H$ via $\iota$.

---

## Construction (Column Hilbert Space Structure)

Let $H$ be a Hilbert space. Set $\mathcal{K} = H \oplus \mathbb{C}$.

**Define the map** $\iota_C : H \to \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})$ as follows. For $\
xi \in H$ and $(\eta, \lambda) \in H \oplus \mathbb{C}$, set

$$\iota_C(\xi)(\eta, \lambda) \;=\; (\lambda\,\xi,\; 0).$$

**Claim 1: $\iota_C(\xi)$ is a bounded operator on $\mathcal{K}$.**

For $(\eta, \lambda) \in H \oplus \mathbb{C}$,

$$\|\iota_C(\xi)(\eta,\lambda)\| = \|(\lambda\xi, 0)\| = |\lambda|\,\|\xi\| \leq
 \|\xi\|\cdot\|(\eta,\lambda)\|,$$

since $\|(\eta,\lambda)\|^2 = \|\eta\|^2 + |\lambda|^2 \geq |\lambda|^2$. Hence
$\|\iota_C(\xi)\| \leq \|\xi\|$.

**Claim 2: $\|\iota_C(\xi)\| = \|\xi\|$ (the map is isometric).**

Take $(\eta, \lambda) = (0, 1) \in H \oplus \mathbb{C}$, which has norm $1$. The
n

$$\|\iota_C(\xi)(0,1)\| = \|(1\cdot\xi, 0)\| = \|\xi\|.$$

So $\|\iota_C(\xi)\| \geq \|\xi\|$. Combined with Claim 1, $\|\iota_C(\xi)\| = \
|\xi\|$.

**Claim 3: $\iota_C$ is linear.**

For $\xi_1, \xi_2 \in H$ and $\alpha \in \mathbb{C}$,

$$\iota_C(\alpha\xi_1 + \xi_2)(\eta,\lambda) = (\lambda(\alpha\xi_1+\xi_2), 0) =
 \alpha(\lambda\xi_1,0) + (\lambda\xi_2, 0) = \alpha\,\iota_C(\xi_1)(\eta,\lambd
a) + \iota_C(\xi_2)(\eta,\lambda).$$

So $\iota_C$ is a linear isometry from $H$ into $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})$.

**Conclusion of the construction.** Since $\iota_C$ is a linear isometry, the im
age $\iota_C(H)$ is a **closed** subspace of $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})$ (closed
because $H$ is complete and $\iota_C$ is isometric). Therefore $\iota_C(H)$ is a
n operator space, and we transport this structure back to $H$:

$$\|[\xi_{ij}]\|_n^{\mathrm{col}} \;:=\; \|[\iota_C(\xi_{ij})]\|_{\mathcal{B}(\m
athcal{K}^{(n)})}, \qquad [\xi_{ij}] \in M_n(H).$$

This defines a valid operator space structure on $H$, called the **column Hilber
t space** structure, denoted $C$.

---

## Other Structures (showing non-uniqueness)

The same Hilbert space $H$ admits other, genuinely different operator space stru
ctures:

**Row structure ($R$).** Define $\iota_R : H \to \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})$ (with
 $\mathcal{K} = H \oplus \mathbb{C}$) by

$$\iota_R(\xi)(\eta, \lambda) = (0,\; \langle \eta, \xi \rangle).$$

One checks $\|\iota_R(\xi)\| = \|\xi\|$ (the norm is achieved at $(\eta,\lambda)
 = (\xi/\|\xi\|, 0)$ when $\xi \neq 0$), so this is again a linear isometry, giv
ing the **row Hilbert space** structure.

**Operator Hilbert space ($OH$, Pisier).** Pisier constructed a canonical operat
or space structure on $H$ that is completely isometric to its operator-space dua
l. This is the unique (up to complete isometry) operator space structure on $H$
that is both self-dual and "homogeneous" in a suitable sense.

**Non-equivalence.** For $\dim H \geq 2$, the column and row structures are **no
t** completely isometric. For instance, the identity map $\mathrm{id}: C \to R$
is isometric (both norms equal $\|\xi\|$ at level $n=1$) but is **not** complete
ly isometric: at level $n=2$, the matrix norms already differ. Concretely, if $\
{e_1, e_2\}$ is an orthonormal pair in $H$, then

$$\left\|\begin{pmatrix} e_1 & e_2 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\right\|_{M_2(C)} = 1,
\qquad \left\|\begin{pmatrix} e_1 & e_2 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\right\|_{M_2(R)}
= \sqrt{2},$$

so the two structures are distinct. This shows the question is non-trivial: ther
e is no single "forced" operator space structure on a Hilbert space, yet structu
res do exist.

---

## Summary

Given any Hilbert space $H$:

1. We constructed an explicit linear isometry $\iota_C: H \hookrightarrow \mathc
al{B}(H \oplus \mathbb{C})$, exhibiting $H$ as a closed subspace of a $C^*$-alge
bra.
2. The inherited matrix norms give $H$ a valid operator space structure (the col
umn structure $C$).
3. Alternative structures ($R$, $OH$) also exist and are generally inequivalent.

Therefore, **yes, it is possible to give an operator space structure to a Hilber
t space.**
