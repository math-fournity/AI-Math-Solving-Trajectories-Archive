 determine the deflection $u(x,t)$.

## Solution

We solve the boundary-value problem by separation of variables (Fourier's method
).

**The equation:**
$$u_{tt} + a^2 u_{xxxx} + \frac{k}{\rho S}\,u = 0, \quad 0<x<l,\; t>0$$

**Boundary conditions (simply-supported beam):**
$$u(0,t)=u_{xx}(0,t)=u(l,t)=u_{xx}(l,t)=0$$

**Initial conditions:**
$$u(x,0)=f(x),\quad u_t(x,0)=F(x)$$

---

### Step 1. Separation of variables

Set $u(x,t)=X(x)\,T(t)$. Substituting and dividing by $XT$:

$$\frac{T''}{T}+a^2\frac{X''''}{X}+\frac{k}{\rho S}=0$$

Separate with constant $\mu$:

$$X''''=\mu\,X,\qquad T''+\left(a^2\mu+\frac{k}{\rho S}\right)T=0$$

### Step 2. Spatial eigenvalue problem

$$X''''=\mu\,X,\quad X(0)=X''(0)=X(l)=X''(l)=0$$

For a simply-supported beam the eigenfunctions and eigenvalues are well known. D
irect verification: try $X_n(x)=\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}$. Then

$$X_n''''=\left(\frac{n\pi}{l}\right)^4\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}=\left(\frac{n\pi}{l}
\right)^4 X_n$$

so $\mu_n=\left(\frac{n\pi}{l}\right)^4$, $n=1,2,3,\dots$ The boundary condition
s are satisfied since $\sin 0=\sin n\pi=0$ and $X_n''=-\left(\frac{n\pi}{l}\righ
t)^2\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}$ also vanishes at $x=0,l$.

The system $\{\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}\}$ is complete and orthogonal on $[0,l]$, so
these are all eigenfunctions.

### Step 3. Temporal equation

For each $n$, define

$$\omega_n^2 = a^2\left(\frac{n\pi}{l}\right)^4+\frac{k}{\rho S}$$

The time equation becomes $T_n''+\omega_n^2 T_n=0$, with general solution

$$T_n(t)=A_n\cos(\omega_n t)+B_n\sin(\omega_n t)$$

### Step 4. General solution

$$u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[A_n\cos(\omega_n t)+B_n\sin(\omega_n t)\right]
\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}$$

### Step 5. Determining coefficients from initial data

From $u(x,0)=f(x)$:

$$f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}$$

By orthogonality $\int_0^l \sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}\sin\frac{m\pi x}{l}\,dx=\frac{l}
{2}\delta_{nm}$:

$$A_n=\frac{2}{l}\int_0^l f(\xi)\sin\frac{n\pi\xi}{l}\,d\xi$$

From $u_t(x,0)=F(x)$: differentiating the series,

$$u_t(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[-A_n\omega_n\sin(\omega_n t)+B_n\omega_n\cos
(\omega_n t)\right]\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}$$

Setting $t=0$:

$$F(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}B_n\omega_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}$$

Hence

$$B_n=\frac{2}{l\,\omega_n}\int_0^l F(\xi)\sin\frac{n\pi\xi}{l}\,d\xi$$

### Final Answer

$$\boxed{u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[A_n\cos(\omega_n t)+B_n\sin(\omega_n t)
\right]\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}}$$

where

$$\omega_n=\sqrt{a^2\left(\frac{n\pi}{l}\right)^4+\frac{k}{\rho S}},$$

$$A_n=\frac{2}{l}\int_0^l f(\xi)\sin\frac{n\pi\xi}{l}\,d\xi,\qquad B_n=\frac{2}{
l\,\omega_n}\int_0^l F(\xi)\sin\frac{n\pi\xi}{l}\,d\xi.$$

**Physical interpretation.** Each mode $\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}$ oscillates harmoni
cally with frequency $\omega_n$. Compared to the free beam ($k=0$), the elastic
foundation raises every natural frequency via the additive term $\frac{k}{\rho S
}$, so $\omega_n^2 = a^2\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{l}\right)^4+\frac{k}{\rho S} > a^2\!
\left(\frac{n\pi}{l}\right)^4$. The mode shapes themselves are unchanged because
 the foundation term $\frac{k}{\rho S}u$ is proportional to $u$ and does not alt
er the spatial eigenvalue problem.
