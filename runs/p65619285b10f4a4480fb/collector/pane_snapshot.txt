# Proof

**Claim.** The statement $\mathcal{H}(U \times V) \simeq \mathcal{H}(U)\,\wideha
t{\otimes}\,\mathcal{H}(V)$ is **true** for open affine sets $U, V$ (Zariski-ope
n in some $\mathbb{C}^n$). In particular it holds for $U = V = \mathbb{C}^{\time
s}$.

---

## 1.  Open affine sets are Stein

An *open affine set* here means a Zariski-open affine variety; its analytificati
on is a Stein space. This is the classical **analytification theorem**: the anal
ytification of any affine $\mathbb{C}$-variety is a Stein space (Gunning–Rossi,
Forster). In particular every open Riemann surface is Stein, so

$$\mathbb{C}^{\times} = \mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\} = \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
[z,z^{-1}]$$

is Stein, and products of Stein spaces are Stein, hence $\mathbb{C}^{\times}\tim
es\mathbb{C}^{\times}$ is Stein.

## 2.  The tensor-product theorem for Stein spaces

Let $X,Y$ be Stein spaces and endow $\mathcal{O}(X),\mathcal{O}(Y)$ with the com
pact-open (Fréchet) topology. The canonical map

$$\kappa:\mathcal{O}(X)\otimes\mathcal{O}(Y)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}(X\times Y
),\qquad f\otimes g\longmapsto\bigl((x,y)\mapsto f(x)g(y)\bigr)$$

extends to the completed projective tensor product, and the **tensor-product the
orem for Stein spaces** (Forster, *Riemannsche Flächen*, §29; Gunning–Rossi, *An
alytic Functions*) asserts that the induced map

$$\widehat{\kappa}:\mathcal{O}(X)\,\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}\,\mathcal{O}(Y)\xrigh
tarrow{\;\;\sim\;\;}\mathcal{O}(X\times Y)$$

is an isomorphism of topological $\mathbb{C}$-algebras.

Moreover $\mathcal{O}(X)$ is a **nuclear** Fréchet space for every Stein space $
X$ (a consequence of the Schwartz kernel theorem / the structure theorem for Ste
in algebras). Nuclearity collapses all reasonable tensor topologies:

$$\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}\;=\;\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}\;=:\;\widehat{\oti
mes}.$$

So for Stein $X,Y$ one may write unambiguously

$$\mathcal{O}(X\times Y)\;\cong\;\mathcal{O}(X)\,\widehat{\otimes}\,\mathcal{O}(
Y).\tag{$\star$}$$

## 3.  Application to $U=V=\mathbb{C}^{\times}$

Since $\mathbb{C}^{\times}$ is Stein, applying $(\star)$ with $X=Y=\mathbb{C}^{\
times}$ gives

$$\boxed{\;\mathcal{H}\bigl(\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathbb{C}^{\times}\bigr)\;
\simeq\;\mathcal{H}\bigl(\mathbb{C}^{\times}\bigr)\,\widehat{\otimes}\,\mathcal{
H}\bigl(\mathbb{C}^{\times}\bigr)\;}$$

as topological $\mathbb{C}$-algebras (and in particular as vector spaces).

## 4.  Direct verification for $\mathbb{C}^{\times}$ (sanity check)

To see concretely that no pathology hides in the puncture, expand every $f\in\ma
thcal{H}(\mathbb{C}^{\times})$ as a Laurent series convergent on $\mathbb{C}\set
minus\{0\}$:

$$f(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_n z^{n},\qquad \limsup_{n\to+\infty}|a_n|^{1/n}=0,
\quad \limsup_{m\to+\infty}|a_{-m}|^{1/m}=0.$$

Setting $w=1/z$, the negative part $\sum_{m\ge1}a_{-m}w^{m}$ is entire and vanis
hes at $0$. Thus, as topological vector spaces,

$$\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C}^{\times})\;\cong\;\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C})\;\oplus\;\mat
hcal{H}_{0}(\mathbb{C})\;\cong\;\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathcal{H}(\mathbb
{C}),$$

where $\mathcal{H}_{0}(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C})$ (both nuclear Fr
échet, via $g\mapsto z\,g$). Hence

$$\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C}^{\times})\,\widehat{\otimes}\,\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C}^{\
times})\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{i,j\in\{+,-\}}\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C})\,\widehat{\otim
es}\,\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C})\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{i,j\in\{+,-\}}\mathcal{H}(\mathb
b{C}^{2}),$$

using the standard fact $\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\cong\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C})
^{\widehat{\otimes}\,n}$.

On the other side, every $F\in\mathcal{H}\bigl((\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}\bigr)$
has a bi-Laurent expansion

$$F(z_{1},z_{2})=\sum_{(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}a_{n_{1}n_{2}}\,z_{1}^{n_{
1}}z_{2}^{n_{2}},$$

convergent on $(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}$. Splitting by the signs of $n_{1},n_{2
}$ gives exactly four summands, each identified (after $z_{i}\leftrightarrow 1/z
_{i}$ on the negative parts) with $\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C}^{2})$:

$$\mathcal{H}\bigl((\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}\bigr)\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{i,j\in\{+,
-\}}\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C}^{2}).$$

The two decompositions agree, confirming the isomorphism explicitly for $\mathbb
{C}^{\times}$.

## 5.  Conclusion

The statement is **true**. The general reason is that open affine sets are Stein
, and the tensor-product theorem for Stein spaces gives $\mathcal{O}(X\times Y)\
cong\mathcal{O}(X)\,\widehat{\otimes}\,\mathcal{O}(Y)$ (with the completed tenso
r product unambiguous because Stein algebras are nuclear). The special case $U=V
=\mathbb{C}^{\times}$ is verified both by this theorem and by an explicit Lauren
t-series decomposition into four copies of $\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C}^{2})$ on each
 side.
