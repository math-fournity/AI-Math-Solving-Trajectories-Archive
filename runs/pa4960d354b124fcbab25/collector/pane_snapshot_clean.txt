## Solution

We evaluate
$$A = \lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\right)^{1/(n(n+1))}.$$

**Step 1: Reduce to a logarithmic sum.**

Let $P_n = \prod_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}$. Then $\log A = \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1
}{n(n+1)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\log\binom{n}{k}$, provided the limit exists.

**Step 2: Simplify the sum.**

Using $\log\binom{n}{k}=\log(n!)-\log(k!)-\log((n-k)!)$ and summing over $k$:
$$\sum_{k=0}^{n}\log\binom{n}{k}=(n+1)\log(n!)-2\sum_{k=0}^{n}\log(k!).$$

Rewrite $\sum_{k=0}^{n}\log(k!)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\log j=\sum_{j=1}^{n
}(n+1-j)\log j=(n+1)\log(n!)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}j\log j$.

Substituting:
$$\sum_{k=0}^{n}\log\binom{n}{k}=(n+1)\log(n!)-2\Big[(n+1)\log(n!)-\sum_{j=1}^{n
}j\log j\Big]=-(n+1)\log(n!)+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}j\log j.$$

Dividing by $n(n+1)$:
$$S_n:=\frac{1}{n(n+1)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\log\binom{n}{k}=-\frac{\log(n!)}{n}+\frac{
2\sum_{j=1}^{n}j\log j}{n(n+1)}.$$

**Step 3: Asymptotics via Stirling and Euler–Maclaurin.**

Stirling: $\log(n!)=n\log n-n+O(\log n)$, hence
$$\frac{\log(n!)}{n}=\log n-1+o(1).$$

For the weighted sum, $f(x)=x\log x$ is increasing for $x\ge 1$, and by the inte
gral comparison / Euler–Maclaurin:
$$\sum_{j=1}^{n}j\log j=\int_{1}^{n}x\log x\,dx+O(n\log n)=\frac{n^{2}}{2}\log n
-\frac{n^{2}}{4}+O(n\log n).$$

Therefore
$$\frac{2\sum_{j=1}^{n}j\log j}{n(n+1)}=\frac{n^{2}\log n-\frac{n^{2}}{2}+O(n\lo
g n)}{n^{2}+n}=\frac{\log n-\frac{1}{2}+O(\frac{\log n}{n})}{1+\frac{1}{n}}=\log
 n-\frac{1}{2}+o(1).$$

**Step 4: Combine.**

$$S_n=\big[-\log n+1+o(1)\big]+\big[\log n-\tfrac{1}{2}+o(1)\big]=\tfrac{1}{2}+o
(1).$$

The $\log n$ terms cancel exactly, leaving a finite limit:
$$\log A=\lim_{n\to\infty}S_n=\frac{1}{2}.$$

Hence
$$\boxed{A=e^{1/2}=\sqrt{e}.}$$
