# Proof that $\mathcal{S}(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ has enough injectives

## Setup and key ingredients

Let $(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ be a ringed space. We denote the category of $\mathcal{
O}_X$-modules by $\mathcal{S}(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$. We must show that every $\math
cal{O}_X$-module $\mathcal{F}$ admits a monomorphism into an injective $\mathcal
{O}_X$-module, and then deduce the existence of injective resolutions.

We use two classical facts:

**Fact 1 (Enough injectives for modules over a ring).** For any ring $A$ (in par
ticular, for the local ring $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ at any point $x \in X$), the cat
egory $\mathrm{Mod}_A$ has enough injectives: every $A$-module $M$ embeds into a
n injective $A$-module. (This follows from Baer's criterion and the existence of
 injective hulls via Zorn's lemma applied to essential extensions.)

**Fact 2 (Products of injectives are injective).** In any abelian category, a pr
oduct of injective objects is injective. *Proof:* If $\{I_\alpha\}$ are injectiv
e and $f: A \rightarrowtail B$ is a monomorphism with a map $g: A \to \prod_\alp
ha I_\alpha$, then for each $\alpha$ the composite $\pi_\alpha \circ g: A \to I_
\alpha$ extends to $h_\alpha: B \to I_\alpha$ by injectivity of $I_\alpha$. The
universal property of the product yields $h: B \to \prod_\alpha I_\alpha$ with $
\pi_\alpha \circ h = h_\alpha$, and $h \circ f = g$ since $\pi_\alpha \circ h \c
irc f = h_\alpha \circ f = \pi_\alpha \circ g$ for all $\alpha$. $\square$

## The skyscraper sheaf and its injectivity

For a point $x \in X$ and an $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$-module $M$, let $i_x: \{x\} \ho
okrightarrow X$ denote the inclusion and define the **skyscraper sheaf** $(i_x)_
* M$ by:
$$
(i_x)_* M(U) = \begin{cases} M & \text{if } x \in U, \\ 0 & \text{if } x \notin
U, \end{cases}
$$
with the obvious restriction maps. This is an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module: when $x \i
n U$, the ring $\mathcal{O}_X(U)$ acts on $M$ through the natural map $\mathcal{
O}_X(U) \to \mathcal{O}_{X,x}$.

**Lemma.** There is a natural isomorphism
$$
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_X}(\mathcal{G},\, (i_x)_* M) \;\cong\; \mathrm{Hom}_{\
mathcal{O}_{X,x}}(\mathcal{G}_x,\, M)
$$
for every $\mathcal{O}_X$-module $\mathcal{G}$.

*Proof.* A morphism $\varphi: \mathcal{G} \to (i_x)_* M$ induces on stalks at $x
$ an $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$-linear map $\varphi_x: \mathcal{G}_x \to ((i_x)_* M)_x
= M$. Conversely, given an $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$-linear map $\psi: \mathcal{G}_x \
to M$, define for each open $U$:
- If $x \in U$: the map $\mathcal{G}(U) \to M$ is $\mathcal{G}(U) \to \mathcal{G
}_x \xrightarrow{\psi} M$ (stalk map followed by $\psi$).
- If $x \notin U$: the map $\mathcal{G}(U) \to 0$ is zero.

These assemble into a morphism of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules, and the two construct
ions are mutually inverse. $\square$

**Corollary.** If $I_x$ is an injective $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$-module, then $(i_x)_
* I_x$ is an injective $\mathcal{O}_X$-module.

*Proof.* The stalk functor $(-)_x: \mathcal{S}(X, \mathcal{O}_X) \to \mathrm{Mod
}_{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}$ is exact (exactness of sheaves is detected on stalks). By
 the Lemma, $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_X}(-, (i_x)_* I_x) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\m
athcal{O}_{X,x}}((-)_x, I_x)$, which is the composition of the exact functor $(-
)_x$ with the exact functor $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}(-, I_x)$ (exact be
cause $I_x$ is injective). Hence $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_X}(-, (i_x)_* I_x)$
is exact, so $(i_x)_* I_x$ is injective. $\square$

## Main proof: enough injectives

Let $\mathcal{F}$ be an arbitrary $\mathcal{O}_X$-module.

**Step 1.** For each point $x \in X$, the stalk $\mathcal{F}_x$ is an $\mathcal{
O}_{X,x}$-module. By **Fact 1**, there exists an embedding $\mathcal{F}_x \hookr
ightarrow I_x$ into an injective $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$-module $I_x$.

**Step 2.** There is a natural monomorphism of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules
$$
\mathcal{F} \;\longrightarrow\; \prod_{x \in X} (i_x)_* \mathcal{F}_x,
$$
defined on each open $U$ by sending a section $s \in \mathcal{F}(U)$ to the fami
ly $(s_x)_{x \in U}$ of its germs. On each stalk at $y \in X$, this map is the i
dentity $\mathcal{F}_y \to \mathcal{F}_y$ (since $((i_x)_* \mathcal{F}_x)_y = \m
athcal{F}_y$ if $x = y$ and $0$ otherwise, so the product stalk is $\mathcal{F}_
y$). Being an isomorphism on every stalk, the map is a monomorphism (in fact, it
 is a monomorphism since it is injective on all stalks).

**Step 3.** The embeddings $\mathcal{F}_x \hookrightarrow I_x$ induce monomorphi
sms of skyscraper sheaves $(i_x)_* \mathcal{F}_x \hookrightarrow (i_x)_* I_x$ (c
hecked on stalks: at $x$ this is $\mathcal{F}_x \hookrightarrow I_x$, elsewhere
it is $0 \to 0$). Taking the product over all $x \in X$:
$$
\prod_{x \in X} (i_x)_* \mathcal{F}_x \;\hookrightarrow\; \prod_{x \in X} (i_x)_
* I_x.
$$

**Step 4.** By the **Corollary**, each $(i_x)_* I_x$ is an injective $\mathcal{O
}_X$-module. By **Fact 2**, the product $\mathcal{I} := \prod_{x \in X} (i_x)_*
I_x$ is injective.

**Step 5.** Composing the monomorphisms from Steps 2 and 3:
$$
\mathcal{F} \;\hookrightarrow\; \prod_{x \in X} (i_x)_* \mathcal{F}_x \;\hookrig
htarrow\; \prod_{x \in X} (i_x)_* I_x = \mathcal{I},
$$
we obtain a monomorphism of $\mathcal{F}$ into the injective $\mathcal{O}_X$-mod
ule $\mathcal{I}$.

Since $\mathcal{F}$ was arbitrary, $\mathcal{S}(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ has **enough
injectives**. $\square$

## Implication: existence of injective resolutions

The property of having enough injectives immediately yields, for every $\mathcal
{O}_X$-module $\mathcal{F}$, an **injective resolution**: a long exact sequence
$$
0 \to \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{I}^0 \to \mathcal{I}^1 \to \mathcal{I}^2 \to \cdo
ts
$$
with each $\mathcal{I}^n$ injective. The construction is inductive:

- **Degree 0:** Embed $\mathcal{F} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{I}^0$ (possible by e
nough injectives). Set $\mathcal{C}^0 = \mathrm{coker}(\mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{
I}^0)$.
- **Degree $n+1$:** Embed $\mathcal{C}^n \hookrightarrow \mathcal{I}^{n+1}$ (pos
sible by enough injectives). Set $\mathcal{C}^{n+1} = \mathrm{coker}(\mathcal{C}
^n \to \mathcal{I}^{n+1})$.

The exactness of $0 \to \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{I}^0 \to \mathcal{I}^1 \to \cdo
ts$ follows at each stage from the definition of the cokernels and the chosen em
beddings.

This is the foundational result that enables **derived functor theory** in the c
ategory of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules: for any left-exact additive functor $T: \mat
hcal{S}(X, \mathcal{O}_X) \to \mathcal{A}$ (into an abelian category $\mathcal{A
}$), the right derived functors $R^n T$ are well-defined by
$$
R^n T(\mathcal{F}) := H^n\bigl(T(\mathcal{I}^\bullet)\bigr),
$$
where $\mathcal{I}^\bullet$ is any injective resolution of $\mathcal{F}$. The re
sult is independent of the choice of injective resolution (up to canonical isomo
rphism), because injective resolutions are unique up to homotopy in the homotopy
 category of complexes. In particular, this gives rise to sheaf cohomology $H^n(
X, -) = R^n \Gamma(X, -)$, sheaf Ext, and the full apparatus of homological alge
bra on ringed spaces.
