# Solution

**Answer: n = 9.**

## Interpretation

We seek indices with $1 \le i < 2j-i \le n$ such that $A_i, A_j, A_{2j-i}$ share
 a color. The condition $i < 2j-i$ forces $j > i \ge 1$, so the triple $(i, j, 2
j-i)$ is a 3-term arithmetic progression with positive common difference $j-i$.
Thus a "good" coloring is precisely one containing a **monochromatic 3-term arit
hmetic progression**, and we seek the least $n$ forcing this — the van der Waerd
en number $W(2,3)$.

## Part 1: $n = 8$ is not forced (a non-good coloring exists)

Color $\{1,\dots,8\}$ by the pattern **R, B, B, R, R, B, B, R**:
$$c(1)=R,\ c(2)=B,\ c(3)=B,\ c(4)=R,\ c(5)=R,\ c(6)=B,\ c(7)=B,\ c(8)=R.$$

The 3-term APs in $\{1,\dots,8\}$ and their colors:

| AP | Colors | AP | Colors |
|---|---|---|---|
| (1,2,3) | RBB | (1,3,5) | RBR |
| (2,3,4) | BBR | (2,4,6) | BRB |
| (3,4,5) | BRR | (3,5,7) | BRB |
| (4,5,6) | RRB | (4,6,8) | RBR |
| (5,6,7) | RBB | (1,4,7) | RRB |
| (6,7,8) | BBR | (2,5,8) | BRR |

None is monochromatic, so $n=8$ does not force goodness.

## Part 2: $n = 9$ is forced

Suppose for contradiction that a 2-coloring of $\{1,\dots,9\}$ has **no** monoch
romatic 3-AP. Swapping colors if needed, assume $c(5)=R$.

The four APs through 5 — $(1,5,9),(2,5,8),(3,5,7),(4,5,6)$ — each contain $5=R$,
 so in each pair $\{1,9\},\{2,8\},\{3,7\},\{4,6\}$, **at least one element is $B
$**. Also, any AP through 5 has its other two entries not both $R$.

**Case I: $c(3)=R$.** Then $c(7)=B$ (pair $\{3,7\}$). From $(1,3,5)$ with $3,5$
both $R$, we get $c(1)=B$. From $(1,4,7)$ with $1,7$ both $B$, we get $c(4)=R$.
But now $(3,4,5)=R,R,R$ — monochromatic. **Contradiction.**

**Case II: $c(3)=B$.**

*Subcase IIa: $c(7)=R$.* From $(5,7,9)$ ($5,7$ both $R$): $c(9)=B$. From $(3,6,9
)$ ($3,9$ both $B$): $c(6)=R$. From $(4,5,6)$ ($5,6$ both $R$): $c(4)=B$. From $
(2,3,4)$ ($3,4$ both $B$): $c(2)=R$. From pair $\{2,8\}$: $c(8)=B$. Now $(5,6,7)
=R,R,R$ — monochromatic. **Contradiction.**

*Subcase IIb: $c(7)=B$.*

- **If $c(6)=R$:** From pair $\{4,6\}$: $c(4)=B$. From $(2,3,4)$ ($3,4$ both $B$
): $c(2)=R$. From pair $\{2,8\}$: $c(8)=B$. From $(7,8,9)$ ($7,8$ both $B$): $c(
9)=R$. From pair $\{1,9\}$: $c(1)=B$. Now $(1,4,7)=B,B,B$ — monochromatic. **Con
tradiction.**

- **If $c(6)=B$:** From $(3,6,9)$ ($3,6$ both $B$): $c(9)=R$. From pair $\{1,9\}
$: $c(1)=B$. From $(1,2,3)$ ($1,3$ both $B$): $c(2)=R$. From pair $\{2,8\}$: $c(
8)=B$. Now $(6,7,8)=B,B,B$ — monochromatic. **Contradiction.**

Every case yields a contradiction, so **every** 2-coloring of $\{1,\dots,9\}$ co
ntains a monochromatic 3-term AP.

## Conclusion

Since $n=8$ admits a coloring with no monochromatic 3-term AP, but $n=9$ does no
t, the least natural number $n$ such that all colorings of $n$ points are good i
s

$$\boxed{n = 9}.$$
