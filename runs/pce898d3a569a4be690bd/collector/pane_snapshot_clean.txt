**Claim.** Let $\{A_n\}$ be a sequence of real symmetric matrices converging (en
trywise, hence in any norm) to a real symmetric matrix $A$. For each $n$, let $\
lambda_1(n) \ge \lambda_2(n) \ge \cdots \ge \lambda_m(n)$ be the eigenvalues of
$A_n$ in decreasing order, and $\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \cdots \ge \lambda_m
$ those of $A$. Then for every $k = 1, \dots, m$,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} \lambda_k(n) = \lambda_k.$$

**Proof.** We use the **Courant–Fischer variational characterization** of the ei
genvalues of a real symmetric matrix. For an $m \times m$ real symmetric matrix
$B$ with eigenvalues $\mu_1 \ge \cdots \ge \mu_m$, the $k$-th largest eigenvalue
 is
$$
\mu_k \;=\; \max_{\substack{V \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m \\ \dim V = k}} \;\min_{\su
bstack{x \in V \\ \|x\|=1}} x^\top B\,x
\;=\; \min_{\substack{V \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m \\ \dim V = m-k+1}} \;\max_{\subs
tack{x \in V \\ \|x\|=1}} x^\top B\,x.
$$

---

**Step 1 (Weyl's inequality).** We first establish the key estimate. Let $B, C$
be $m\times m$ real symmetric matrices. Then for every $k$,
$$
|\lambda_k(B) - \lambda_k(C)| \;\le\; \|B - C\|_{\mathrm{op}},
$$
where $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ is the operator (spectral) norm.

*Proof of the estimate.* Write $B = C + (B-C)$. Using the max-min formulation,
$$
\lambda_k(B) = \max_{\dim V = k}\min_{\substack{x\in V\\\|x\|=1}} x^\top C x + x
^\top(B-C)x.
$$
Since $B-C$ is symmetric with operator norm $\|B-C\|_{\mathrm{op}}$, we have $|x
^\top(B-C)x| \le \|B-C\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ for every unit vector $x$. Hence
$$
\min_{\substack{x\in V\\\|x\|=1}} x^\top B x \;\le\; \min_{\substack{x\in V\\\|x
\|=1}} x^\top C x + \|B-C\|_{\mathrm{op}},
$$
and taking the max over all $k$-dimensional subspaces $V$ gives
$$
\lambda_k(B) \le \lambda_k(C) + \|B-C\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
$$
By symmetry (swapping $B$ and $C$),
$$
\lambda_k(C) \le \lambda_k(B) + \|B-C\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
$$
Combining,
$$
|\lambda_k(B) - \lambda_k(C)| \le \|B-C\|_{\mathrm{op}}. \qquad\square
$$

---

**Step 2 (Apply to the sequence).** Since $A_n \to A$ entrywise and all matrices
 are $m\times m$ (finite-dimensional), convergence holds in every norm, in parti
cular the operator norm:
$$
\|A_n - A\|_{\mathrm{op}} \;\longrightarrow\; 0 \quad\text{as } n\to\infty.
$$
(On a finite-dimensional space all norms are equivalent, and $\|A_n - A\|_{\math
rm{op}} \le \|A_n - A\|_F \to 0$, where $\|\cdot\|_F$ is the Frobenius norm, whi
ch is just the $\ell^2$-norm of the entries.)

Applying Weyl's inequality with $B = A_n$ and $C = A$:
$$
|\lambda_k(n) - \lambda_k| \;\le\; \|A_n - A\|_{\mathrm{op}} \;\longrightarrow\;
 0.
$$
Therefore
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty} \lambda_k(n) = \lambda_k \quad\text{for every } k = 1, \dots,
m.
$$

---

**Remark on the dimension.** The statement implicitly assumes all $A_n$ and $A$
have the same size $m\times m$ (otherwise "the $k$-th largest eigenvalue" is not
 well-defined for a fixed $k$ across the sequence). Under this assumption—which
is the standard and necessary hypothesis—the conclusion holds for every $k = 1,
\dots, m$.

The essential ingredients are: (i) symmetry, which guarantees real eigenvalues a
nd the Courant–Fischer characterization; (ii) finite-dimensionality, which makes
 entrywise convergence equivalent to operator-norm convergence; and (iii) Weyl's
 inequality, which controls the perturbation of each ordered eigenvalue by the o
perator norm of the difference. $\blacksquare$
