## Solution

We solve Laplace's equation $\nabla^2 u = 0$ in the half-space $z > 0$ with boun
dary data $u(x,y,0) = f(x,y)$.

### Step 1. Fundamental solution (Green's function)

The free-space Green's function in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is
$$
G_0(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{r}') = \frac{1}{4\pi|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'|}, \qquad
\nabla'^2 G_0 = -\delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}').
$$

To enforce the Dirichlet condition on the plane $z = 0$, we use the **method of
images**. For a source point $\mathbf{r}' = (x', y', z')$ with $z' > 0$, its ima
ge is $\mathbf{r}'^* = (x', y', -z')$. The Dirichlet Green's function for the ha
lf-space is
$$
G_D(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{r}') = \frac{1}{4\pi|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'|} - \frac{
1}{4\pi|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'^*|}.
$$

On the boundary $z = 0$ (i.e. $\mathbf{r} = (x,y,0)$), we have $|\mathbf{r} - \m
athbf{r}'| = |\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'^*|$, so $G_D\big|_{z=0} = 0$, as required
.

### Step 2. Poisson integral formula via Green's third identity

For a harmonic function $u$ in a domain $\Omega$ with boundary $\partial\Omega$,
 Green's second identity gives
$$
u(\mathbf{r}) = \iint_{\partial\Omega} \left[ u(\mathbf{r}')\,\frac{\partial G_D
}{\partial n'} - G_D\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial n'} \right] dS',
$$
where $n'$ is the outward normal to $\Omega$.

For the half-space $\Omega = \{z > 0\}$, the boundary is the plane $z' = 0$ with
 outward normal $n' = -\hat{z}$ (pointing into $z < 0$). Since $G_D = 0$ on the
boundary, the second term vanishes. We need $\partial G_D / \partial n'$ evaluat
ed at $z' = 0$.

### Step 3. Computing the normal derivative

With $\mathbf{r} = (x, y, z)$, $z > 0$, and $\mathbf{r}' = (x', y', z')$:
$$
G_D = \frac{1}{4\pi}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x-x')^2+(y-y')^2+(z-z')^2}} - \frac{1}
{\sqrt{(x-x')^2+(y-y')^2+(z+z')^2}}\right].
$$

Differentiating with respect to $z'$:
$$
\frac{\partial G_D}{\partial z'} = \frac{1}{4\pi}\left[\frac{-(z'-z)}{|\mathbf{r
}-\mathbf{r}'|^3} - \frac{-(z'+z)}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'^*|^3}\right] \cdot (-
1) \cdot (-1)
$$

More carefully:
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial z'}\frac{1}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|} = \frac{z - z'}{|
\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|^3}, \qquad
\frac{\partial}{\partial z'}\frac{1}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'^*|} = \frac{-(z+z')
}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'^*|^3}.
$$

So
$$
\frac{\partial G_D}{\partial z'} = \frac{1}{4\pi}\left[\frac{z - z'}{|\mathbf{r}
-\mathbf{r}'|^3} + \frac{z + z'}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'^*|^3}\right].
$$

Evaluating at $z' = 0$, where $|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'| = |\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}
'^*| = \sqrt{(x-x')^2+(y-y')^2+z^2}$:
$$
\left.\frac{\partial G_D}{\partial z'}\right|_{z'=0} = \frac{1}{4\pi}\cdot\frac{
2z}{\left[(x-x')^2+(y-y')^2+z^2\right]^{3/2}}.
$$

Since $n' = -\hat{z}$, we have $\partial/\partial n' = -\partial/\partial z'$, h
ence
$$
\left.\frac{\partial G_D}{\partial n'}\right|_{z'=0} = -\frac{1}{4\pi}\cdot\frac
{2z}{\left[(x-x')^2+(y-y')^2+z^2\right]^{3/2}}.
$$

### Step 4. The Poisson integral formula

Substituting into Green's identity (with $u(\mathbf{r}') = f(x', y')$ on $z' = 0
$, and $dS' = dx'\,dy'$):
$$
u(x, y, z) = \iint_{\mathbb{R}^2} f(x', y')\cdot \frac{1}{4\pi}\cdot\frac{2z}{\l
eft[(x-x')^2+(y-y')^2+z^2\right]^{3/2}}\,dx'\,dy'.
$$

(The sign works out: the boundary integral carries a minus from $\partial/\parti
al n'$ and the standard identity, yielding a positive kernel — consistent with t
he maximum principle, since $u$ is a weighted average of $f$.)

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle u(x, y, z) = \frac{z}{2\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^2} \frac{f(x',
 y')}{\left[(x-x')^2 + (y-y')^2 + z^2\right]^{3/2}}\,dx'\,dy'}
$$

This is the **Poisson integral formula for the upper half-space** in three dimen
sions.

### Step 5. Verification

**Harmonicity.** The kernel $P_z(x-x', y-y') = \dfrac{z}{2\pi[(x-x')^2+(y-y')^2+
z^2]^{3/2}}$ is harmonic in $(x,y,z)$ for $z > 0$ (it is $-\partial_{z'}G_D|_{z'
=0}$, and $G_D$ is harmonic in $\mathbf{r}$ for $\mathbf{r} \neq \mathbf{r}'$; e
quivalently, one checks directly that $\nabla^2_{x,y,z} P_z = 0$ for $z > 0$). D
ifferentiating under the integral (valid for $z > 0$ where the kernel is smooth)
, $\nabla^2 u = \iint f\, \nabla^2 P_z\, dx'dy' = 0$.

**Boundary condition.** The kernel $P_z$ is an approximate identity: $P_z \geq 0
$, $\iint P_z\,dx'dy' = 1$ (verified by the substitution $x'-x = \rho\cos\theta$
, $y'-y=\rho\sin\theta$, giving $\frac{z}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^\infty \f
rac{\rho\,d\rho\,d\theta}{(\rho^2+z^2)^{3/2}} = \frac{z}{2\pi}\cdot 2\pi\cdot \f
rac{1}{z} = 1$), and $P_z \to 0$ uniformly away from $(x',y')=(x,y)$ as $z\to 0^
+$. Therefore, at every point of continuity of $f$,
$$
\lim_{z\to 0^+} u(x,y,z) = f(x,y).
$$

**Uniqueness.** Suppose $u_1, u_2$ are two bounded harmonic functions in $z>0$ w
ith the same boundary data. Then $w = u_1 - u_2$ is harmonic, bounded, and $w\to
 0$ as $z\to 0^+$. By the maximum principle applied to $w$ on the half-ball $B_R
^+ = \{|\mathbf{r}|<R,\, z>0\}$ and letting $R\to\infty$ (using boundedness to c
ontrol the hemispherical part), $w \equiv 0$. Hence the solution is unique withi
n the class of bounded functions.

This completes the solution.
