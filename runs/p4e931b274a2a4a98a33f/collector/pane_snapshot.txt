### Step 1: Lifting the inhomogeneous boundary conditions

We seek a simple function $\ell(x,t)$ satisfying the boundary data. Since the da
ta is linear in nothing but we need $\ell(0,t)=\sin t$ and $\ell(L,t)=\cos t$, w
e choose $\ell$ linear in $x$:

$$\ell(x,t) = \left(1-\frac{x}{L}\right)\sin t + \frac{x}{L}\cos t =: a(x)\sin t
 + b(x)\cos t,$$

where $a(x)=1-x/L$, $b(x)=x/L$. Clearly $\ell(0,t)=\sin t$, $\ell(L,t)=\cos t$.

Set $u = v + \ell$. Then $v$ must satisfy **homogeneous** Dirichlet conditions $
v(0,t)=v(L,t)=0$.

### Step 2: Equation and initial data for $v$

Since $\ell$ is linear in $x$, $\ell_{xx}=0$; and since $\ell$ is a linear combi
nation of $\sin t,\cos t$, we have $\ell_{tt}=-\ell$. Substituting into $u_{tt}=
c^2u_{xx}$:

$$v_{tt} - c^2 v_{xx} = -(\ell_{tt}-c^2\ell_{xx}) = \ell = a(x)\sin t + b(x)\cos
 t. \tag{1}$$

The initial data for $v$:
- $v(x,0) = u(x,0)-\ell(x,0) = 0 - b(x) = -x/L$,
- $v_t(x,0) = u_t(x,0)-\ell_t(x,0) = 0 - a(x) = -(1-x/L)$.

### Step 3: Eigenfunction expansion

The Dirichlet eigenfunctions on $(0,L)$ are $\phi_n(x)=\sin(n\pi x/L)$ with $\la
mbda_n=(n\pi/L)^2$. Set

$$v(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty T_n(t)\sin\!\left(\tfrac{n\pi x}{L}\right), \qquad \o
mega_n := \frac{cn\pi}{L}.$$

Expand the forcing $F(x,t)=a(x)\sin t+b(x)\cos t = \sum_n (f_n\sin t+g_n\cos t)\
phi_n(x)$ and the initial data $v(x,0)=\sum A_n\phi_n$, $v_t(x,0)=\sum B_n\phi_n
$.

**Fourier sine coefficients.** With $\alpha_n=n\pi/L$:

$$f_n=\frac{2}{L}\int_0^L\!\left(1-\tfrac{x}{L}\right)\sin(\alpha_n x)\,dx = \fr
ac{2}{n\pi},$$

$$g_n=\frac{2}{L}\int_0^L\!\frac{x}{L}\sin(\alpha_n x)\,dx = \frac{2(-1)^{n+1}}{
n\pi},$$

$$A_n=\frac{2}{L}\int_0^L\!\left(-\tfrac{x}{L}\right)\sin(\alpha_n x)\,dx = \fra
c{2(-1)^n}{n\pi},$$

$$B_n=\frac{2}{L}\int_0^L\!-\left(1-\tfrac{x}{L}\right)\sin(\alpha_n x)\,dx = -\
frac{2}{n\pi}.$$

(These use $\int_0^L x\sin(\alpha_n x)\,dx = \frac{L(-1)^{n+1}}{\alpha_n}$ and $
\int_0^L(1-x/L)\sin(\alpha_n x)\,dx=\frac{1}{\alpha_n}$.)

### Step 4: ODE for each mode

Substituting into (1) gives the forced oscillator

$$T_n'' + \omega_n^2 T_n = f_n\sin t + g_n\cos t, \qquad T_n(0)=A_n,\; T_n'(0)=B
_n. \tag{2}$$

#### Generic (non-resonant) case: $\omega_n\neq 1$

A particular solution is $T_n^{(p)} = \frac{f_n\sin t+g_n\cos t}{\omega_n^2-1}$.
 The general solution is

$$T_n(t) = C_n\cos(\omega_n t)+D_n\sin(\omega_n t)+\frac{f_n\sin t+g_n\cos t}{\o
mega_n^2-1}.$$

From the initial conditions:

$$C_n = A_n - \frac{g_n}{\omega_n^2-1} = \frac{2(-1)^n\omega_n^2}{n\pi(\omega_n^
2-1)}, \qquad D_n = \frac{1}{\omega_n}\!\left(B_n-\frac{f_n}{\omega_n^2-1}\right
) = \frac{-2\omega_n}{n\pi(\omega_n^2-1)}.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{T_n(t)=\frac{2}{n\pi(\omega_n^2-1)}\Big[(-1)^n\omega_n^2\cos(\omega_n t
)-\omega_n\sin(\omega_n t)+\sin t+(-1)^{n+1}\cos t\Big].} \tag{3}$$

#### Resonant case: $\omega_{n_0}=1$ for some $n_0$ (i.e. $L=c\,n_0\pi$)

For that single mode, the forcing frequency coincides with the natural frequency
. The particular solution acquires secular terms:

$$T_{n_0}^{(p)} = -\frac{f_{n_0}}{2}\,t\cos t + \frac{g_{n_0}}{2}\,t\sin t,$$

so

$$T_{n_0}(t)=C_{n_0}\cos t+D_{n_0}\sin t -\frac{f_{n_0}}{2}\,t\cos t+\frac{g_{n_
0}}{2}\,t\sin t,$$

with $C_{n_0},D_{n_0}$ fixed by $T_{n_0}(0)=A_{n_0}$, $T_{n_0}'(0)=B_{n_0}$. All
 other modes retain the form (3).

### Step 5: Complete solution

$$\boxed{u(x,t)=\left(1-\frac{x}{L}\right)\sin t+\frac{x}{L}\cos t+\sum_{n=1}^{\
infty}T_n(t)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right),} \tag{4}$$

with $T_n$ given by (3) (and the resonant replacement above if $L=c\,n_0\pi$ for
 some integer $n_0$), and $\omega_n=cn\pi/L$.

### Verification

- **Boundary conditions:** $v(0,t)=v(L,t)=0$ (each $\phi_n$ vanishes at the endp
oints), and $\ell(0,t)=\sin t$, $\ell(L,t)=\cos t$. ✓
- **Initial displacement:** $u(x,0)=\ell(x,0)+v(x,0)=b(x)+(-b(x))=0$. ✓
- **Initial velocity:** $u_t(x,0)=\ell_t(x,0)+v_t(x,0)=a(x)+(-a(x))=0$. ✓
- **PDE:** $v_{tt}-c^2v_{xx}=\ell$ by construction of each $T_n$ satisfying (2);
 $\ell_{tt}-c^2\ell_{xx}=-\ell$; hence $u_{tt}-c^2u_{xx}=(v_{tt}-c^2v_{xx})+(\el
l_{tt}-c^2\ell_{xx})=\ell-\ell=0$. ✓

The series converges in the classical sense for $t>0$ (and in the appropriate So
bolev sense up to $t=0$) provided the data is compatible; here the Fourier coeff
icients decay as $O(1/n)$, giving a convergent sine series representing the (pie
cewise smooth) initial data.
