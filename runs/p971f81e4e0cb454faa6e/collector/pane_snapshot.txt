## Solution

We determine the rank of the Jacobian of $\tilde F: \mathbb{R}P^2 \to \mathbb{R}
^4$ induced by $F(x,y,z) = (x^2-y^2,\, xy,\, xz,\, yz)$ on $S^2$.

### The Jacobian on $\mathbb{R}^3$

$$dF = \begin{pmatrix} 2x & -2y & 0 \\ y & x & 0 \\ z & 0 & x \\ 0 & z & y \end{
pmatrix}$$

with columns $c_1 = (2x, y, z, 0)^T$, $c_2 = (-2y, x, 0, z)^T$, $c_3 = (0, 0, x,
 y)^T$.

### Restricting to $T_{\bar x}\mathbb{R}P^2$

Since $\mathbb{R}P^2 = S^2/\!\sim$ and $F(-p)=F(p)$, the map descends to $\tilde
 F$. The tangent space $T_{\bar x}\mathbb{R}P^2$ is identified with the tangent
space to $S^2$ at a representative $p=(x,y,z)$, i.e. the 2-plane $p^\perp = \{v
\in \mathbb{R}^3 : v\cdot p = 0\}$.

We must compute $\operatorname{rank}\big(dF\big|_{p^\perp}\big)$.

**Key observation.** For the radial vector $p=(x,y,z)$:
$$dF(p) = x\,c_1 + y\,c_2 + z\,c_3 = \big(2(x^2-y^2),\; 2xy,\; 2xz,\; 2yz\big) =
 2\,F(p).$$

So $dF(p) = 2F(p)$, which vanishes only when $F(p)=0$.

**Case 1: Generic points** ($F(p)\neq 0$, i.e. not the poles). The full Jacobian
 $dF:\mathbb{R}^3\to\mathbb{R}^4$ has trivial kernel. Indeed, if $\alpha c_1+\be
ta c_2+\gamma c_3=0$, the second and first rows give $y\alpha+x\beta=0$ and $x\a
lpha-y\beta=0$, whence $(x^2+y^2)\alpha=0$ and $(x^2+y^2)\beta=0$. Away from the
 poles, $x^2+y^2>0$, so $\alpha=\beta=0$, and then the third row gives $\gamma=0
$. Thus $dF$ is injective on $\mathbb{R}^3$, hence injective on the 2-plane $p^\
perp$, giving $\operatorname{rank}=2$.

**Case 2: The poles** $p=(0,0,\pm 1)$. Here $x=y=0$, $z=\pm 1$:
$$c_1=(0,0,\pm 1,0),\quad c_2=(0,0,0,\pm 1),\quad c_3=(0,0,0,0).$$
The tangent plane is $p^\perp = \{(\alpha,\beta,0)\}$. Then
$$dF(\alpha,\beta,0) = \alpha\,c_1+\beta\,c_2 = (0,0,\pm\alpha,\pm\beta),$$
which is injective in $(\alpha,\beta)$. So $\operatorname{rank}=2$.

### Conclusion

At every point of $\mathbb{R}P^2$, the Jacobian restricted to $T_{\bar x}\mathbb
{R}P^2$ has rank

$$\boxed{2}$$

This confirms $\tilde F$ is an immersion (the Veronese-type embedding of $\mathb
b{R}P^2$ into $\mathbb{R}^4$).
