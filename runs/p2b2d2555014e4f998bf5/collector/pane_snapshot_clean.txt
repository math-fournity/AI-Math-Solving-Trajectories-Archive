Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a Blaschke product with non
-negative Taylor coefficients can have a zero at $z_0$ without $\overline{z_0}$
also being a zero.

**Answer: No, it is not possible.**

**Proof.**

Let $B(z)$ be a Blaschke product (finite or infinite) with non-negative Taylor c
oefficients, so $B(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} b_n z^n$ with $b_n \geq 0$ for all $
n$.

**Key fact:** A Blaschke product is an *inner function*: $|B(e^{i\theta})| = 1$
for almost every $\theta \in [0, 2\pi)$.

Since $|B(e^{i\theta})|^2 = 1$ a.e., we expand:

$$|B(e^{i\theta})|^2 = B(e^{i\theta})\,\overline{B(e^{i\theta})} = \sum_{n,m \ge
q 0} b_n\, \overline{b_m}\, e^{i(n-m)\theta}.$$

Because all $b_n \geq 0$, we have $\overline{b_m} = b_m$, so:

$$|B(e^{i\theta})|^2 = \sum_{n,m \geq 0} b_n b_m\, e^{i(n-m)\theta}.$$

For this to equal $1$ a.e., every Fourier coefficient of order $k \neq 0$ must v
anish:

$$\sum_{n \geq 0} b_{n+k}\, b_n = 0 \quad \text{for all } k \geq 1.$$

Since every term $b_{n+k}\, b_n \geq 0$, this forces:

$$b_{n+k}\, b_n = 0 \quad \text{for all } n \geq 0,\ k \geq 1.$$

This means: **no two nonzero coefficients can exist at different indices.** Inde
ed, if $b_j > 0$ and $b_\ell > 0$ with $j < \ell$, then setting $n = j$ and $k =
 \ell - j \geq 1$ gives $b_\ell\, b_j > 0$, a contradiction.

Therefore **at most one** coefficient $b_n$ is nonzero. The constant-term (Parse
val) condition $\sum b_n^2 = 1$ (which follows from $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
|B(e^{i\theta})|^2\,d\theta = 1$) then forces exactly one $b_n = 1$ and all othe
rs to be $0$.

Hence $B(z) = z^n$ for some $n \geq 0$.

The only zero of $z^n$ is at $z_0 = 0$, and $\overline{0} = 0$ is trivially also
 a zero. Therefore there is no Blaschke product with non-negative coefficients h
aving a zero at some $z_0$ where $\overline{z_0}$ fails to be a zero. $\blacksqu
are$
