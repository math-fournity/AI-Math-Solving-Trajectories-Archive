# Proof

**Claim:** Yes. The figure-eight knot in $S^3$ is a $(1,1)$-knot whose complemen
t is hyperbolic.

---

## Step 1: The figure-eight knot is a 2-bridge knot

The figure-eight knot ($4_1$ in the Rolfsen table) is the 2-bridge knot correspo
nding to the rational number $5/2$. It has bridge number $2$ with respect to the
 standard genus-$0$ Heegaard sphere of $S^3$.

## Step 2: Every 2-bridge knot in $S^3$ is a $(1,1)$-knot

Let $K$ be a 2-bridge knot in $S^3$, in 2-bridge position with respect to the st
andard Heegaard sphere $S$. So $S^3 = B_1 \cup_S B_2$ with $B_1, B_2$ 3-balls, a
nd $K \cap B_i$ consists of two unknotted, boundary-parallel arcs for each $i$.

**Constructing the Heegaard torus.** In $B_1$, take a small tube following one b
ridge arc $\alpha$ of $K \cap B_1$, connecting two small disks on $S$ near the e
ndpoints of $\alpha$. Attaching this tube to $S$ yields a torus $T$. This torus
bounds two solid tori $V_1$ (containing the tube) and $V_2$ (the complementary s
olid torus), giving a genus-$1$ Heegaard splitting $S^3 = V_1 \cup_T V_2$.

**Verifying $(1,1)$-position.** With respect to this splitting:
- $K \cap V_1$ is the single arc $\alpha$, lying in the tube, which is boundary-
parallel in $V_1$.
- $K \cap V_2$ is the single arc $\beta$ (the remaining bridge from $B_2$), whic
h is boundary-parallel in $V_2$.
- $K \cap T = 2$ points.

Therefore $K$ is in $(1,1)$-position with respect to $T$.

*(Equivalently, this follows from the classical fact that a knot in $S^3$ is a $
(1,1)$-knot if and only if it has tunnel number $\leq 1$, and 2-bridge knots hav
e tunnel number $1$: adding one bridge as a tunnel yields a genus-$2$ handlebody
.)*

## Step 3: The figure-eight knot complement is hyperbolic

By the Geometrization Theorem for knot complements, a knot complement in $S^3$ i
s either a Seifert fibered space (when the knot is a torus knot), a toroidal man
ifold (when the knot is a satellite), or hyperbolic.

The figure-eight knot is **not a torus knot**: its Alexander polynomial $\Delta(
t) = t^2 - 3t + 1$ is not of the form $\frac{(t^{pq}-1)(t-1)}{(t^p-1)(t^q-1)}$ f
or any coprime $p, q$.

The figure-eight knot is **not a satellite knot**: by Schubert's bridge number i
nequality, a non-trivial satellite knot $K$ with companion $C$ and pattern $P$ s
atisfies $b(K) \geq b(C) + b(P) - 1 \geq 2 + 2 - 1 = 3$. Since the figure-eight
knot has bridge number $2$, it cannot be a satellite.

Therefore $S^3 \setminus K$ is hyperbolic. (This was first established directly
by Riley in 1975; the figure-eight knot complement is the simplest hyperbolic 3-
manifold, with volume $2V_3 \approx 2.02988\ldots$)

## Conclusion

The figure-eight knot is a $(1,1)$-knot in $S^3$ (by Steps 1–2) whose complement
 is hyperbolic (by Step 3). Hence the answer is **yes**.

Moreover, infinitely many such examples exist: every hyperbolic 2-bridge knot (e
.g., $5_2$, $6_1$, $6_2$, etc.) is a $(1,1)$-knot with hyperbolic complement by
the same argument.
