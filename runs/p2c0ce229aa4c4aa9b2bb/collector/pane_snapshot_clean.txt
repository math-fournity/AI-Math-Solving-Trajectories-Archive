**Answer: Yes.**

**Construction.** Let $X$ be the figure-eight space (the wedge of two circles, $
S^1 \vee S^1$), with basepoint $x_0$ the wedge point. The fundamental group is t
he free group on two generators:
$$\pi_1(X, x_0) \cong F_2 = \langle a, b \rangle,$$
which is non-abelian.

Let $f$ be the loop traversing the first circle once (representing $a \in F_2$),
 and let $g$ be the loop $b \cdot a \cdot b^{-1}$ (traverse the second circle, t
hen the first, then the second in reverse), representing $bab^{-1} \in F_2$.

**Claim 1: $f$ and $g$ are freely homotopic.**

There is a standard bijection between free homotopy classes of loops in $X$ and
conjugacy classes of $\pi_1(X, x_0)$. Concretely, a free homotopy $H: S^1 \times
 [0,1] \to X$ between two based loops drags the basepoint along a path $\gamma$
from $x_0$ to $x_0$, and the effect on the element of $\pi_1$ is conjugation by
$[\gamma]$:
$$[g] = [\gamma] \cdot [f] \cdot [\gamma]^{-1}.$$
Conversely, any conjugate $h[f]h^{-1}$ is realized by a free homotopy that slide
s the basepoint along $h$.

Since $g$ represents $bab^{-1} = b \cdot a \cdot b^{-1}$, which is the conjugate
 of $a = [f]$ by $b$, the loops $f$ and $g$ are freely homotopic (the basepoint
slides once around the second circle during the homotopy).

**Claim 2: $f$ and $g$ are not based-homotopic.**

Based homotopy classes of loops at $x_0$ are in bijection with elements of $\pi_
1(X, x_0) = F_2$. So $f$ and $g$ are based-homotopic if and only if $a = bab^{-1
}$ in $F_2$.

But $F_2$ is a free group, and in a free group the only element commuting with $
a$ (when $a$ is a generator) is a power of $a$. The equation $a = bab^{-1}$ is e
quivalent to $ab = ba$, i.e., $a$ and $b$ commute. Since $F_2$ is free on $\{a,
b\}$, the generators $a$ and $b$ do not commute (this is immediate: the reduced
words $ab$ and $ba$ are distinct reduced words). Hence $a \neq bab^{-1}$ in $F_2
$.

Therefore $f$ and $g$ are not based-homotopic.

**Conclusion.** In $X = S^1 \vee S^1$ with basepoint the wedge point, the loop $
f$ representing $a$ and the loop $g$ representing $bab^{-1}$ are freely homotopi
c (being conjugate in $\pi_1$) but not based-homotopic (since $a \neq bab^{-1}$
in the free group $F_2$). This gives the desired example.
