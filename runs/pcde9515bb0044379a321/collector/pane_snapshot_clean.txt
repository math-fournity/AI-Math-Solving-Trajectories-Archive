## Solution

**Yes**, the compact set $K$ also contains the support of the narrow limit $\mu$
.

**Proof.** Recall that the support $\operatorname{supp}(\nu)$ of a Borel measure
 $\nu$ is the smallest closed set $F$ with $\nu(X\setminus F)=0$; equivalently,
$x\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu)$ iff every open neighborhood of $x$ has strictly p
ositive $\nu$-measure.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose there exists a point
$$x\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\setminus K.$$

Since $X$ is a metric space (hence Hausdorff) and $K$ is compact, $K$ is closed.
 Therefore $x\notin K$ implies $x$ lies in the open set $X\setminus K$. Moreover
, the distance from $x$ to the compact set $K$ is strictly positive:
$$\delta:=d(x,K)=\min_{y\in K}d(x,y)>0,$$
where the minimum is attained by compactness of $K$ and continuity of $y\mapsto
d(x,y)$.

Consider the open ball $U:=B(x,\delta/2)$. By construction,
$$U\cap K=\varnothing.$$
Since $\operatorname{supp}(\mu_n)\subseteq K$ for every $n$, we have $\mu_n(X\se
tminus K)=0$, and because $U\subseteq X\setminus K$,
$$\mu_n(U)=0\quad\text{for all }n.$$

Now we apply the **portmanteau theorem** for narrow (weak) convergence: for ever
y open set $U$,
$$\mu(U)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mu_n(U).$$
Applying this to $U=B(x,\delta/2)$,
$$\mu\bigl(B(x,\delta/2)\bigr)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mu_n\bigl(B(x,\delta/2)\b
igr)=\liminf_{n\to\infty}0=0.$$

But $x\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)$ and $B(x,\delta/2)$ is an open neighborhood o
f $x$, so by definition of support,
$$\mu\bigl(B(x,\delta/2)\bigr)>0.$$

This is a contradiction. Hence no such $x$ exists, and we conclude
$$\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\subseteq K.$$

**Remark on the hypotheses.** Two ingredients are essential. First, narrow conve
rgence combined with the portmanteau lower bound for open sets is what transfers
 the vanishing of $\mu_n$ on $U$ to the vanishing of $\mu$ on $U$. Second, compa
ctness of $K$ (not merely closedness) is used to guarantee that a point $x\notin
 K$ has a neighborhood *disjoint* from $K$; this is automatic in a metric space
for any closed set, but the compactness is what ensures $K$ is closed in the fir
st place (in a general topological space compact sets need not be closed). In a
metric space every closed set already admits such a separation, so in fact the a
rgument shows the stronger statement: if the $\mu_n$ are all supported in a **cl
osed** set $F$ and $\mu_n\to\mu$ narrowly, then $\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\subset
eq F$. The compactness hypothesis in the problem is a sufficient (and natural) c
ondition ensuring $K$ is closed.
