## Solution

Let $N = 10^{10}$. We seek $v_2\!\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N}\binom{2N}{2n}5^n\right)$.

**Step 1: Closed form via the even-binomial identity.**

The even-indexed terms of $(1+x)^{2N}$ are extracted by
$$\sum_{n=0}^{N}\binom{2N}{2n}x^{2n} = \frac{(1+x)^{2N}+(1-x)^{2N}}{2}.$$
Setting $x=\sqrt{5}$:
$$S := \sum_{n=0}^{N}\binom{2N}{2n}5^n = \frac{(1+\sqrt{5})^{2N}+(1-\sqrt{5})^{2
N}}{2}.$$

**Step 2: Connect to Lucas numbers.**

Let $\phi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}$, $\psi=\frac{1-\sqrt5}{2}$. Then $1+\sqrt5=2\phi$
and $1-\sqrt5=2\psi$, so
$$S = \frac{2^{2N}(\phi^{2N}+\psi^{2N})}{2} = 2^{2N-1}\,L_{2N},$$
where $L_m = \phi^m+\psi^m$ is the $m$-th **Lucas number** ($L_0=2,\,L_1=1,\,L_{
m}=L_{m-1}+L_{m-2}$).

Thus $v_2(S) = (2N-1) + v_2(L_{2N})$.

**Step 3: 2-adic valuation of Lucas numbers.**

We establish the classical result:

$$v_2(L_n) = \begin{cases} 0 & 3\nmid n,\\ 2 & n\equiv 3\pmod{6},\\ 1 & n\equiv
0\pmod{6}. \end{cases}$$

*Proof sketch.* The identity $L_{2m}=L_m^2-2(-1)^m$ is key.

- **Base parity:** Checking $L_0=2,\,L_1=1,\,L_2=3,\,L_3=4,\,L_4=7,\,L_5=11$ sho
ws $L_n$ is even iff $3\mid n$; when $3\nmid n$, $L_n$ is odd, giving $v_2=0$.

- **Odd multiples of 3** ($n\equiv 3\pmod 6$): $L_3=4$, so $v_2=2$. If $v_2(L_m)
=2$ with $m$ odd, write $L_m=4k$, $k$ odd. Then $L_{2m}=L_m^2+2=16k^2+2=2(8k^2+1
)$, and $8k^2+1$ is odd (since $k$ odd $\Rightarrow k^2\equiv1\pmod8$), so $v_2(
L_{2m})=1$.

- **Even multiples of 3** ($n\equiv0\pmod6$): If $v_2(L_m)=1$ with $m$ even, wri
te $L_m=2k$, $k$ odd. Then $L_{2m}=L_m^2-2=4k^2-2=2(2k^2-1)$, and $2k^2-1$ is od
d, so $v_2(L_{2m})=1$. The valuation **stays** $1$ under all further doublings.

This confirms the valuation never exceeds $2$ and follows the stated pattern. $\
square$

**Step 4: Apply to $n = 2N = 2\cdot 10^{10}$.**

Factor: $2\cdot 10^{10} = 2^{11}\cdot 5^{10}$. Since neither $2$ nor $5$ is divi
sible by $3$,
$$3 \nmid 2\cdot 10^{10}.$$
Therefore $v_2(L_{2N}) = 0$, and
$$v_2(S) = (2N-1) + 0 = 2\cdot 10^{10} - 1.$$

**Verification with small cases.**

- $N=1$: $S=\binom20+\binom22\cdot5=1+5=6=2\cdot3$, $v_2=1=2(1)-1$. ✓
- $N=2$: $S=1+30+25=56=2^3\cdot7$, $v_2=3=2(2)-1$. ✓
- $N=5$: $S=62976=2^9\cdot123$, $v_2=9=2(5)-1$. ✓
- $N=3$ (where $3\mid 2N=6$): $S=576=2^6\cdot9$, $v_2=6=(2\cdot3-1)+v_2(L_6)=5+1
$. ✓ (confirms the extra factor appears exactly when $3\mid 2N$).

**Conclusion.**

The greatest power of $2$ dividing $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{10^{10}}\binom{2\cd
ot 10^{10}}{2n}5^n$ is

$$\boxed{2^{\,2\cdot 10^{10}-1}}.$$
