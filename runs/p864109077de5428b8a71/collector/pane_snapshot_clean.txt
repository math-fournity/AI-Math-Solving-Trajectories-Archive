## Karhunen-Loève Expansion of the Wiener Process

**Setup.** The Wiener process $X(t)$, $0 < t < T$, has autocorrelation $R_X(t,s)
 = \sigma^2\min(t,s)$. The K-L expansion requires solving the integral eigenvalu
e problem

$$\int_0^T R_X(t,s)\,\phi(s)\,ds = \lambda\,\phi(t), \qquad 0 < t < T.$$

---

### Step 1. Convert to a differential equation

Substituting the kernel:

$$\sigma^2\!\left[\int_0^t s\,\phi(s)\,ds + t\!\int_t^T \phi(s)\,ds\right] = \la
mbda\,\phi(t). \tag{1}$$

**Differentiate once** with respect to $t$ (Leibniz rule):

$$\sigma^2\!\left[t\,\phi(t) + \int_t^T \phi(s)\,ds - t\,\phi(t)\right] = \lambd
a\,\phi'(t),$$

which simplifies to

$$\sigma^2\!\int_t^T \phi(s)\,ds = \lambda\,\phi'(t). \tag{2}$$

**Differentiate again:**

$$-\sigma^2\,\phi(t) = \lambda\,\phi''(t) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \phi''(t) +
\omega^2\,\phi(t) = 0, \qquad \omega^2 := \frac{\sigma^2}{\lambda}. \tag{3}$$

---

### Step 2. Boundary conditions

- **At $t=0$:** Equation (1) gives $\lambda\,\phi(0) = 0$, hence (for $\lambda\n
eq 0$) $\phi(0)=0$.
- **At $t=T$:** Equation (2) gives $\lambda\,\phi'(T)=\sigma^2\!\int_T^T\phi(s)\
,ds = 0$, hence $\phi'(T)=0$.

---

### Step 3. Solve the eigenvalue problem

The general solution of (3) is $\phi(t)=A\cos(\omega t)+B\sin(\omega t)$.

- $\phi(0)=0 \Rightarrow A=0$, so $\phi(t)=B\sin(\omega t)$.
- $\phi'(T)=0 \Rightarrow B\omega\cos(\omega T)=0 \Rightarrow \cos(\omega T)=0$.

Thus

$$\omega_n T = \left(n-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\pi = \frac{(2n-1)\pi}{2}, \qquad n=1,
2,3,\dots$$

so

$$\boxed{\omega_n = \frac{(2n-1)\pi}{2T}}.$$

**Eigenvalues:**

$$\boxed{\lambda_n = \frac{\sigma^2}{\omega_n^2} = \frac{4\,\sigma^2\,T^2}{(2n-1
)^2\,\pi^2}, \qquad n=1,2,3,\dots}$$

**Normalized eigenfunctions.** Choose $B$ so that $\int_0^T \phi_n^2(t)\,dt = 1$
:

$$\int_0^T \sin^2(\omega_n t)\,dt = \frac{T}{2} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad B=\sqr
t{\frac{2}{T}}.$$

Hence

$$\boxed{\phi_n(t) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{T}}\,\sin\!\left(\frac{(2n-1)\pi\,t}{2T}\rig
ht), \qquad 0 < t < T.}$$

---

### Step 4. Expansion coefficients

The K-L coefficients are the projections

$$\boxed{X_n = \int_0^T X(t)\,\phi_n(t)\,dt.}$$

**Properties.** Since $E[X(t)]=0$, we have $E[X_n]=0$. Orthogonality of the eige
nfunctions gives

$$E[X_n X_m] = \lambda_n\,\delta_{nm},$$

i.e. the coefficients are uncorrelated, each with variance $\lambda_n$.

---

### Step 5. The K-L expansion

$$\boxed{X(t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} X_n\,\phi_n(t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} X_n\,\s
qrt{\frac{2}{T}}\,\sin\!\left(\frac{(2n-1)\pi\,t}{2T}\right), \qquad 0 < t < T,}
$$

with

$$X_n = \int_0^T X(t)\,\sqrt{\frac{2}{T}}\,\sin\!\left(\frac{(2n-1)\pi\,t}{2T}\r
ight)dt, \qquad E[X_n]=0,\quad E[X_n^2]=\frac{4\sigma^2 T^2}{(2n-1)^2\pi^2}.$$

---

### Verification (Mercer's theorem / energy check)

The total energy must satisfy

$$\int_0^T R_X(t,t)\,dt = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_n.$$

Left side: $\int_0^T \sigma^2 t\,dt = \frac{\sigma^2 T^2}{2}$.

Right side:

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{4\sigma^2 T^2}{(2n-1)^2\pi^2} = \frac{4\sigma^2 T^2}{
\pi^2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2n-1)^2} = \frac{4\sigma^2 T^2}{\pi^2}\cdot\f
rac{\pi^2}{8} = \frac{\sigma^2 T^2}{2}. \quad\checkmark$$

The identity $\sum_{n=1}^\infty (2n-1)^{-2} = \pi^2/8$ confirms consistency, val
idating the eigenvalue–eigenfunction pair.
