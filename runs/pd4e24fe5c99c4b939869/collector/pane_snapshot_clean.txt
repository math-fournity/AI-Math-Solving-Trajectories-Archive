## Proof

**Claim:** $X_n/\log(n)$ does **not** necessarily converge to $0$ almost surely.
 The given conditions are insufficient. We construct an explicit counterexample.

### Setup

Let $X \sim \mathrm{Exp}(1)$, with density $e^{-x}$ on $[0,\infty)$ and $\mathbb
{E}(X)=1<\infty$.

Let $B\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)$, and let $(U_n)_{n\ge1}$, $(V_n)_{n\ge1}$ be
independent i.i.d. $\mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)$ sequences, all mutually independent.
The common variable $B$ makes the $X_n$ **dependent**.

### Construction of $X_n$

**Conditional on $B=1$** (the "bad" regime): For each $n\ge 3$,
$$
X_n = \begin{cases}
\log n + (V_n - \tfrac12) & \text{if } U_n \le \frac1n,\\[4pt]
G_n^{-1}(V_n) & \text{if } U_n > \frac1n,
\end{cases}
$$
where $G_n$ is the CDF of $\mathrm{Exp}(1)$ truncated to $[0,n]$, i.e., with den
sity $g_n(x)=\frac{e^{-x}}{1-e^{-n}}\mathbf{1}_{[0,n]}(x)$.

When $U_n\le 1/n$, $X_n$ is uniform on $[\log n - \frac12,\, \log n+\frac12]$, w
hich lies inside $[0,n]$ for $n\ge 3$.

**Conditional on $B=0$** (the "good" regime): $X_n = G_n^{-1}(V_n)$, i.e., $X_n$
 are i.i.d. truncated exponentials on $[0,n]$.

### Verification of all hypotheses

**(i) Nonnegativity:** $X_n\in[0,n]$ in both regimes. ✓

**(ii) Density on $[0,n]$:** The unconditional density of $X_n$ is
$$
f_n(x)=\tfrac12\,f_n^{(1)}(x)+\tfrac12\,f_n^{(0)}(x),
$$
where $f_n^{(0)}=g_n$ and
$$
f_n^{(1)}(x)=\frac1n\,\mathbf{1}_{[\log n-1/2,\,\log n+1/2]}(x)+\frac{n-1}{n}\cd
ot\frac{e^{-x}}{1-e^{-n}}\,\mathbf{1}_{[0,n]}(x).
$$
Both components are densities on $[0,n]$, so $f_n$ is a density on $[0,n]$. ✓

**(iii) $\mathbb{E}(X_n)<\infty$:** $X_n\le n$, so $\mathbb{E}(X_n)\le n<\infty$
. ✓

**(iv) Weak convergence $X_n\Rightarrow X$:** For any bounded continuous $\varph
i$,
$$
\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X_n)] = \tfrac12\,\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X_n)\mid B=1]+\tfrac12\,
\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X_n)\mid B=0].
$$
In the $B=0$ regime: $\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X_n)\mid B=0]=\int_0^n \varphi(x)\frac{
e^{-x}}{1-e^{-n}}dx\to\int_0^\infty \varphi(x)e^{-x}dx=\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X)]$.

In the $B=1$ regime:
$$
\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X_n)\mid B=1]=\frac1n\int_{\log n-1/2}^{\log n+1/2}\varphi(x)
\,dx+\frac{n-1}{n}\int_0^n\varphi(x)\frac{e^{-x}}{1-e^{-n}}dx.
$$
The second term $\to\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X)]$. The first term is bounded by $\|\va
rphi\|_\infty/n\to 0$.

So both conditional expectations $\to\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X)]$, hence $\mathbb{E}[
\varphi(X_n)]\to\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X)]$. ✓

**(v) $X$ has density $e^{-x}$ on $[0,\infty)$ with $\mathbb{E}(X)=1<\infty$.**
✓

**(vi) The $X_n$ are dependent:** They share the common variable $B$, so they ar
e not independent. ✓

### Failure of almost sure convergence

Work on the event $\{B=1\}$, which has probability $1/2$.

For $n\ge 3$, on the event $\{U_n\le 1/n\}$, we have $X_n\ge \log n - \frac12 >
\frac12\log n$ (since $\log n>1$ for $n\ge 3$), hence
$$
\frac{X_n}{\log n}>\frac12.
$$

The events $\{U_n\le 1/n\}$ are conditionally independent given $B=1$, with $\ma
thbb{P}(U_n\le 1/n\mid B=1)=1/n$. Since $\sum_{n=3}^\infty \frac1n=\infty$, the
**second Borel–Cantelli lemma** gives
$$
\mathbb{P}\!\left(U_n\le \tfrac1n\ \text{i.o.}\ \Big|\ B=1\right)=1.
$$

Therefore
$$
\mathbb{P}\!\left(\frac{X_n}{\log n}>\frac12\ \text{i.o.}\ \Big|\ B=1\right)=1,
$$
and consequently
$$
\mathbb{P}\!\left(\frac{X_n}{\log n}\not\to 0\right)\ge\mathbb{P}(B=1)\cdot 1=\f
rac12>0.
$$

### Conclusion

We have constructed a sequence of dependent nonnegative random variables $(X_n)$
, each with a density on $[0,n]$ and finite expectation, converging weakly to $X
$ with a density on $[0,\infty)$ and finite expectation, yet $X_n/\log(n)$ fails
 to converge to $0$ almost surely (it exceeds $1/2$ infinitely often with probab
ility at least $1/2$).

The fundamental reason is that **weak convergence constrains only marginal distr
ibutions**, while **almost sure convergence depends on the joint distribution**
(the coupling). The hypotheses provide no control over the joint law, so no almo
st-sure conclusion can be drawn.

**$X_n/\log(n)$ does not necessarily converge to $0$ almost surely.**
