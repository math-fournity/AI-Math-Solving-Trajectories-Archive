**Theorem.** *Let $U$ be a unipotent upper triangular group over a local field $

s is relatively compact.*

**Proof.**

**Step 1: Topological properties of $U$.**

A local field $K$ of characteristic zero is either $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{C}$, o
r a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. In all cases, $K$ is locally compact, $\
sigma$-compact, and second-countable.

The group $U$ is a unipotent algebraic group over $K$. As an algebraic variety,
$U$ is isomorphic to affine space $\mathbb{A}^m_K$ (where $m = \dim U$; the coor
dinates are the entries above the diagonal). Consequently, $U(K) \cong K^m$ as a
 topological space. Therefore:

- $U$ is **locally compact** (product of locally compact spaces),
- $U$ is **$\sigma$-compact** (since $K = \bigcup_{n} C_n$ for compact $C_n$, we
 have $U = \bigcup_n C_n^m$),
- $U$ is **second-countable**, hence **metrizable** (by the Birkhoff–Kakutani th
eorem, a first-countable Hausdorff topological group is metrizable).

**Step 2: $U$ admits a proper left-invariant metric.**

We invoke the following classical result:

> **Theorem (Struble, 1974).** *A locally compact group $G$ admits a proper left
-invariant metric (i.e., a left-invariant metric compatible with the topology su
ch that every closed ball is compact) if and only if $G$ is $\sigma$-compact and
 metrizable.*

Since $U$ is locally compact, $\sigma$-compact, and metrizable, Struble's theore
m furnishes a proper left-invariant metric $d_L$ on $U$. "Proper" means: for eve
ry $R < \infty$, the closed ball $\overline{B}_{d_L}(x, R) = \{y \in U : d_L(x,y
) \leq R\}$ is compact.

*(For the archimedean case $K \in \{\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{C}\}$, one can construct
 $d_L$ explicitly: $U$ is a simply connected nilpotent Lie group, so the exponen
tial map $\exp: \mathfrak{u} \to U$ is a diffeomorphism. Choose any inner produc
t on the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{u}$ and extend it to a left-invariant Riemannian
 metric on $U$. The induced Riemannian distance $d_L$ is left-invariant. Since l
eft-invariant Riemannian metrics on Lie groups are geodesically complete, the Ho
pf–Rinow theorem guarantees that closed bounded sets are compact.)*

**Step 3: Conversion to a proper right-invariant metric.**

Define $d_R: U \times U \to [0,\infty)$ by
$$d_R(x, y) = d_L(x^{-1}, y^{-1}).$$

We verify the required properties:

1. **Metric:** $d_R$ inherits the metric axioms from $d_L$ (the map $(x,y) \maps
to (x^{-1}, y^{-1})$ is a bijection on $U \times U$).

2. **Right-translation invariance:** For any $g \in U$,
$$d_R(xg, yg) = d_L(g^{-1}x^{-1}, g^{-1}y^{-1}) = d_L(x^{-1}, y^{-1}) = d_R(x, y
),$$
where the second equality uses left-invariance of $d_L$.

3. **Compatibility with topology:** Since inversion $x \mapsto x^{-1}$ is a home
omorphism of $U$ and $d_L$ is compatible with the topology, $d_R$ is also compat
ible.

4. **Balls are relatively compact:** For any $x \in U$ and $R < \infty$, the clo
sed ball is
$$\overline{B}_{d_R}(x, R) = \{y \in U : d_L(x^{-1}, y^{-1}) \leq R\} = \{y : d_
L(e,\, xy^{-1}) \leq R\} = \overline{B}_{d_L}(e, R)^{-1} \cdot x.$$
Here we used left-invariance: $d_L(x^{-1}, y^{-1}) = d_L(e, x\,y^{-1})$. Since $
\overline{B}_{d_L}(e, R)$ is compact (by properness of $d_L$), its image under t
he inversion homeomorphism $\overline{B}_{d_L}(e, R)^{-1}$ is compact, and right
-multiplication by $x$ (a homeomorphism) preserves compactness. Thus $\overline{
B}_{d_R}(x, R)$ is compact.

In particular, every open ball $B_{d_R}(x, R) \subset \overline{B}_{d_R}(x, R)$
is relatively compact.

**Conclusion.** The metric $d_R$ is right-translation-invariant, compatible with
 the topology of $U$, and every ball of finite radius is relatively compact. $\b
lacksquare$
