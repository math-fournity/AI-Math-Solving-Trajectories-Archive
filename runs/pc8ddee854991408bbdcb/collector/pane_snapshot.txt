## Proof

We are given:
- $X: U \to \mathbb{R}^3$ a proper regular patch, $U \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ open,
- $X_i := D_i X$ (the $i$-th partial derivative of $X$),
- $u_j: (-\epsilon, \epsilon) \to \mathbb{R}^2$, $u_j(t) := t E_j$, with $E_1 =
(1,0)$, $E_2 = (0,1)$.

We must verify the chain of equalities
$$\left(X_i \circ u_j\right)'(0) \;=\; DX_i\!\left(u_j(0)\right)\,Du_j(0) \;=\;
X_{ij}(0,0).$$

---

**Step 1 — The first equality is the Chain Rule.**

The map $X_i: U \to \mathbb{R}^3$ is smooth (since $X$ is a regular patch, its p
artial derivatives of all orders exist and are continuous on $U$, and $(0,0) \in
 U$ because $U$ is open and $u_j(0) = (0,0)$). The map $u_j: (-\epsilon,\epsilon
) \to \mathbb{R}^2$ is smooth. Hence the composition
$$X_i \circ u_j : (-\epsilon, \epsilon) \to \mathbb{R}^3$$
is smooth, and by the Chain Rule its derivative at $t = 0$ is
$$\left(X_i \circ u_j\right)'(0) \;=\; DX_i\!\big(u_j(0)\big)\;\circ\; Du_j(0),$
$
where $DX_i(u_j(0)) : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ is the total derivative (Ja
cobian) of $X_i$ at $u_j(0)$ and $Du_j(0): \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^2$ is the t
otal derivative of $u_j$ at $0$. Identifying these linear maps with their repres
enting matrices gives the first equality.

---

**Step 2 — Evaluate the two factors.**

Since $u_j(t) = t E_j$:
$$u_j(0) = 0 \cdot E_j = (0,0), \qquad u_j'(t) = E_j, \quad\text{so}\quad Du_j(0
) = E_j.$$

Thus
$$DX_i\!\big(u_j(0)\big)\,Du_j(0) \;=\; DX_i(0,0)\,E_j.$$

The product $DX_i(0,0)\,E_j$ is, by definition of the total derivative as the li
near map whose action on a vector is the directional derivative, the directional
 derivative of $X_i$ at $(0,0)$ in the direction $E_j$:
$$DX_i(0,0)\,E_j \;=\; \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{X_i\big((0,0) + h E_j\big) - X_i(0,0
)}{h} \;=\; D_j X_i(0,0).$$

---

**Step 3 — Identify the result as $X_{ij}(0,0)$.**

Recall $X_i = D_i X$, so
$$D_j X_i(0,0) \;=\; D_j\!\left(D_i X\right)(0,0).$$

By the standard notation $X_{ij} := D_j D_i X$ (the second-order partial derivat
ive, differentiating first in the $i$-th, then in the $j$-th coordinate), this i
s precisely
$$D_j D_i X(0,0) \;=\; X_{ij}(0,0).$$

---

**Step 4 — Assemble the chain.**

Combining Steps 1–3:
$$\left(X_i \circ u_j\right)'(0) \;\underset{\text{Chain Rule}}{=}\; DX_i\!\big(
u_j(0)\big)\,Du_j(0) \;\underset{u_j(0)=(0,0),\, Du_j(0)=E_j}{=}\; DX_i(0,0)\,E_
j \;\underset{\text{directional derivative}}{=}\; D_j X_i(0,0) \;\underset{X_i =
 D_i X}{=}\; X_{ij}(0,0).$$

All three expressions are therefore equal, as required. $\blacksquare$
