## Analysis

For a number with digits $d_1, \ldots, d_k$ (each in $\{1,\ldots,9\}$), the trai
ling zeros of the digit product equals $\min(v_2, v_5)$ where $v_p$ is the $p$-a
dic valuation of the product.

**Digit contributions:**
- 5 contributes $v_5 = 1$ (only digit contributing to $v_5$)
- 8 contributes $v_2 = 3$ (most efficient for $v_2$)
- 2, 4, 6 contribute $v_2 = 1, 2, 1$ respectively
- 1, 3, 7, 9 contribute nothing

Let $c$ = count of 5s. Then $v_5 = c$, so we need $c \geq 24$ and $v_2 \geq 24$,
 with $\min(v_2, v_5) = 24$.

## Minimum digit count

We need at least 24 fives. For $v_2 \geq 24$, the most efficient digit is 8 ($v_
2 = 3$ each), requiring $\lceil 24/3 \rceil = 8$ eights. So the minimum is $24 +
 8 = 32$ digits, achieved only with exactly **24 fives and 8 eights** ($v_2 = v_
5 = 24$).

Any 31-digit attempt fails: 24 fives + 7 remaining digits gives $v_2 \leq 21 < 2
4$.
