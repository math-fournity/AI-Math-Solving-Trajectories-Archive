## Proof

Let $G = \langle \sigma \rangle$ where $\sigma: x \mapsto x+1$, acting on $\math
bb{K}(x)$ by $\sigma\bigl(f(x)\bigr) = f(x+1)$. Since $\operatorname{char}\mathb
b{K}=0$, the automorphism $\sigma$ has infinite order (the polynomial $x$ is not
 periodic: $\sigma^n(x)=x+n\neq x$ for $n\neq 0$), so $G\cong\mathbb{Z}$.

We claim the fixed field is
$$\mathbb{K}(x)^G = \mathbb{K}.$$

**Step 1: Reduction from rational functions to polynomials.**

Let $h(x) = \dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)} \in \mathbb{K}(x)^G$, written in lowest terms wit
h $g$ monic. The condition $\sigma(h)=h$ gives
$$\frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{f(x+1)}{g(x+1)}, \qquad\text{i.e.,}\qquad f(x)\,g(x+
1) = f(x+1)\,g(x). \tag{$\ast$}$$

Since $\gcd(f,g)=1$ in $\mathbb{K}[x]$, and the substitution $x\mapsto x+1$ is a
 ring automorphism of $\mathbb{K}[x]$, we also have $\gcd\bigl(f(x+1),g(x+1)\big
r)=1$. From $(\ast)$, $g(x)\mid f(x)\,g(x+1)$; since $\gcd(g(x),f(x))=1$, we get
 $g(x)\mid g(x+1)$. Both $g(x)$ and $g(x+1)$ are monic of the same degree, so
$$g(x+1) = g(x).$$
Substituting back into $(\ast)$: $f(x)\,g(x+1)=f(x+1)\,g(x)$ becomes $f(x)\,g(x)
=f(x+1)\,g(x)$, and since $g\neq 0$,
$$f(x+1) = f(x).$$

Thus both $f$ and $g$ are polynomials invariant under $x\mapsto x+1$.

**Step 2: A polynomial invariant under $x\mapsto x+1$ is constant.**

Let $p(x)\in\mathbb{K}[x]$ with $p(x+1)=p(x)$. Suppose for contradiction that $\
deg p = n \geq 1$, and write $p(x) = a_n x^n + \cdots$ with $a_n\neq 0$. Then
$$p(x+1) - p(x) = a_n\bigl[(x+1)^n - x^n\bigr] + \text{(lower degree terms)}.$$
The leading term of $(x+1)^n - x^n$ is $n\,x^{n-1}$. Since $\operatorname{char}\
mathbb{K}=0$ and $n\geq 1$, we have $n\neq 0$ in $\mathbb{K}$, so the coefficien
t of $x^{n-1}$ in $p(x+1)-p(x)$ is $n\,a_n \neq 0$. Hence $p(x+1)-p(x)$ is a non
zero polynomial of degree $n-1\geq 0$, contradicting $p(x+1)=p(x)$.

Therefore $\deg p = 0$, i.e., $p\in\mathbb{K}$.

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

Applying Step 2 to $f$ and $g$: both are constants in $\mathbb{K}$. Hence $h = f
/g \in \mathbb{K}$. Conversely, every element of $\mathbb{K}$ is fixed by $\sigm
a$ (which acts trivially on constants). Therefore
$$\boxed{\mathbb{K}(x)^G = \mathbb{K}.}$$
