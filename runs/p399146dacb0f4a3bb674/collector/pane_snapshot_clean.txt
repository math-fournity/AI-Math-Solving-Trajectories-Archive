**Problem.** Find a degree-8 polynomial $p(x)$ such that the hyperelliptic curve
 $X: y^2 = p(x)$ admits a fixed-point free involution.

---

## Construction

Take
$$p(x) = x^8 + 1.$$

This is a polynomial of degree $8$. Its roots are the $8$ distinct solutions of
$x^8 = -1$, namely $x = e^{i\pi(2k+1)/8}$ for $k=0,\dots,7$, all simple. Hence t
he affine curve $y^2 = x^8+1$ is nonsingular, and since $\deg p = 8 = 2g+2$ with
 $g=3$, the smooth projective model $X$ is a hyperelliptic Riemann surface of ge
nus $3$ with two points at infinity $\infty_+$ and $\infty_-$.

## The involution

Define
$$\sigma : X \to X, \qquad \sigma(x, y) = (-x,\, -y).$$

**Well-definedness.** Since $p(-x) = (-x)^8 + 1 = x^8 + 1 = p(x)$, the polynomia
l $p$ is even. Thus if $(x,y)$ satisfies $y^2 = p(x)$, then $(-y)^2 = y^2 = p(x)
 = p(-x)$, so $(-x,-y)$ also lies on $X$. Hence $\sigma$ is a well-defined autom
orphism of $X$.

**Involution.** $\sigma^2(x,y) = \sigma(-x,-y) = (x,y)$, so $\sigma^2 = \mathrm{
id}_X$.

## Fixed-point freeness

**Affine part.** A fixed point of $\sigma$ in the affine part must satisfy $(x,y
) = (-x,-y)$, i.e., $x = -x$ and $y = -y$, giving $x = 0$ and $y = 0$. But the p
oint $(0,0)$ does **not** lie on $X$, since $0^2 = 0 \neq 1 = 0^8 + 1 = p(0)$. S
o $\sigma$ has no fixed point in the affine part.

**Points at infinity.** To analyze the behavior at infinity, set $u = 1/x$ and $
v = y/x^{g+1} = y/x^4$ (a standard chart near infinity for a hyperelliptic curve
 of even degree). Then
$$v^2 = \frac{p(x)}{x^8} = \frac{x^8+1}{x^8} = 1 + u^8.$$
As $u \to 0$, we get $v \to \pm 1$, giving the two points at infinity $\infty_+
= (u,v)=(0,1)$ and $\infty_- = (u,v)=(0,-1)$.

Under $\sigma$: $x \mapsto -x$ gives $u \mapsto -u$, and $y \mapsto -y$ gives $v
 = y/x^4 \mapsto -y/(-x)^4 = -y/x^4 = -v$. So in this chart $\sigma(u,v) = (-u,
-v)$. Evaluating at the points at infinity:
$$\sigma(\infty_+) = \sigma(0, 1) = (0, -1) = \infty_-, \qquad \sigma(\infty_-)
= \sigma(0,-1) = (0, 1) = \infty_+.$$
Thus $\sigma$ **swaps** the two points at infinity; neither is fixed.

## Conclusion

The involution $\sigma : (x,y) \mapsto (-x,-y)$ on $X : y^2 = x^8 + 1$ has no fi
xed points on the entire smooth projective curve (neither in the affine part nor
 at infinity). Therefore $p(x) = x^8 + 1$ is the desired degree-$8$ polynomial.
