**Proof.** Define the partial sums $S_N = \sum_{n=1}^{N} a_n$. We will show that
 $S_N \to \infty$, proving divergence.

**Key claim.** For every integer $n \ge 1$ and every integer $k \ge 0$,
$$
a_n \;\le\; \sum_{i=2^k n}^{2^k(n+1)-1} a_i. \tag{$\ast$}
$$

**Proof of claim, by induction on $k$.**

*Base case* ($k=0$): the right-hand side is $\sum_{i=n}^{n} a_i = a_n$, so ($\as
t$) holds with equality.

*Inductive step* ($k \to k+1$): Assume ($\ast$) holds for some $k \ge 0$. Applyi
ng the hypothesis $a_i \le a_{2i}+a_{2i+1}$ to each index $i$ in the range $[2^k
 n,\; 2^k(n+1)-1]$,
$$
\sum_{i=2^k n}^{2^k(n+1)-1} a_i \;\le\; \sum_{i=2^k n}^{2^k(n+1)-1}\bigl(a_{2i}+
a_{2i+1}\bigr).
$$
As $i$ runs over $[2^k n,\, 2^k(n+1)-1]$, the index $2i$ runs over all **even**
integers in $[2^{k+1}n,\, 2^{k+1}(n+1)-2]$ and $2i+1$ runs over all **odd** inte
gers in $[2^{k+1}n+1,\, 2^{k+1}(n+1)-1]$. Together these are exactly all integer
s in $[2^{k+1}n,\, 2^{k+1}(n+1)-1]$, each appearing once. Hence
$$
\sum_{i=2^k n}^{2^k(n+1)-1}\bigl(a_{2i}+a_{2i+1}\bigr) \;=\; \sum_{j=2^{k+1}n}^{
2^{k+1}(n+1)-1} a_j.
$$
Combining with the induction hypothesis $a_n \le \sum_{i=2^k n}^{2^k(n+1)-1} a_i
$ gives
$$
a_n \;\le\; \sum_{j=2^{k+1}n}^{2^{k+1}(n+1)-1} a_j,
$$
which is ($\ast$) with $k+1$ in place of $k$. This completes the induction. $\sq
uare$

**Applying the claim with $n=1$.** For every $k \ge 0$,
$$
a_1 \;\le\; \sum_{i=2^k}^{2^{k+1}-1} a_i. \tag{$\ast\ast$}
$$

The index ranges $[2^k, 2^{k+1}-1]$ for $k=0,1,2,\dots$ partition the positive i
ntegers: $[1,1], [2,3], [4,7], [8,15], \dots$ Summing ($\ast\ast$) over $k=0,1,\
dots,m-1$,
$$
m\,a_1 \;\le\; \sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\sum_{i=2^k}^{2^{k+1}-1} a_i \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{2^
m-1} a_i \;=\; S_{2^m-1}.
$$

Since $a_1 > 0$ by hypothesis, the right-hand side $S_{2^m-1} \ge m\,a_1 \to \in
fty$ as $m \to \infty$. Thus the partial sums $\{S_N\}$ are unbounded (they exce
ed $m\,a_1$ along the subsequence $N=2^m-1$).

A series of non-negative terms converges if and only if its partial sums are bou
nded. Since $\{S_N\}$ is unbounded, $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n$ diverges. $\blacks
quare$
