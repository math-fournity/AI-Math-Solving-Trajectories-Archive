## Proof

**Convert to polar coordinates.** Let $x = r\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\theta$. Then
 $x^2+y^2 = r^2$ and $xy = \frac{r^2}{2}\sin(2\theta)$. The equation becomes:

$$r^4 - r^2\sin(2\theta) = 0 \implies r^2\bigl(r^2 - \sin(2\theta)\bigr) = 0.$$

So either $r=0$ (the origin) or $r^2 = \sin(2\theta)$.

**Determine where the curve exists.** We need $\sin(2\theta)\geq 0$, which holds
 for $\theta\in[0,\pi/2]\cup[\pi,3\pi/2]$. This gives two symmetric loops — a le
mniscate (a $45°$ rotation of Bernoulli's lemniscate $r^2=\cos(2\theta)$ with $a
=1$).

**Compute the area of one loop** ($\theta\in[0,\pi/2]$) using the polar area for
mula $A=\frac{1}{2}\int r^2\,d\theta$:

$$A_1 = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin(2\theta)\,d\theta = \frac{1}{2}\left[-\fra
c{\cos(2\theta)}{2}\right]_0^{\pi/2} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\
right) = \frac{1}{2}.$$

**By symmetry**, the second loop ($\theta\in[\pi,3\pi/2]$) contributes $A_2 = \f
rac{1}{2}$.

**Total area:**

$$A = A_1 + A_2 = \frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2} = 1.$$

This matches the known result for the lemniscate $r^2 = \cos(2\theta)$ (total ar
ea $= a^2 = 1$), since our curve is merely a rotation of it.

$$\boxed{A = 1}$$
