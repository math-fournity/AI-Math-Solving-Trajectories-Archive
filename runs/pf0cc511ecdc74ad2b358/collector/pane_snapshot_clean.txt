## Solution

We compute $\lim_{p\to 0^+}\int_{C_p}\frac{e^{3iz}}{z^2-1}\,dz$, where $C_p: z =
 1+pe^{i\theta}$ is the circle of radius $p$ centered at $z=1$, traversed counte
rclockwise (positive orientation).

**Factor the integrand.** Since $z^2-1=(z-1)(z+1)$, we write

$$\frac{e^{3iz}}{z^2-1}=\frac{f(z)}{z-1},\qquad f(z):=\frac{e^{3iz}}{z+1}.$$

**Identify the analytic part.** The function $f(z)=\frac{e^{3iz}}{z+1}$ is analy
tic in a neighborhood of $z=1$ (its only singularity is at $z=-1$, which is dist
ance $2$ away). In particular, for any $0<p<2$, $f$ is analytic on and inside $C
_p$.

**Apply Cauchy's integral formula.** Since $C_p$ is a positively oriented simple
 closed curve enclosing $z=1$, and $f$ is analytic on and inside $C_p$, Cauchy's
 integral formula gives (exactly, for every $0<p<2$):

$$\int_{C_p}\frac{f(z)}{z-1}\,dz = 2\pi i\, f(1).$$

**Evaluate at $z=1$:**

$$f(1)=\frac{e^{3i\cdot 1}}{1+1}=\frac{e^{3i}}{2}.$$

Therefore

$$\int_{C_p}\frac{e^{3iz}}{z^2-1}\,dz = 2\pi i\cdot\frac{e^{3i}}{2}=\pi i\, e^{3
i}.$$

Since this value is independent of $p$ (for all $0<p<2$), the limit as $p\to 0^+
$ is the same value:

$$\lim_{p\to 0^+}\int_{C_p}\frac{e^{3iz}}{z^2-1}\,dz = \pi i\, e^{3i}.$$
