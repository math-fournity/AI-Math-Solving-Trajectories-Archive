## Proof

**Setup.** Let $f(x) = 2x^3 - 2x$. We seek positive $a$ such that four distinct
points $(a, f(a)), (b, f(b)), (c, f(c)), (d, f(d))$ on the curve $y = f(x)$ form
 a rectangle.

**Step 1: Opposite vertices must have $x$-values summing to $0$.**

A rectangle's diagonals bisect each other, so pairing opposite vertices $(p,q)$
and $(r,s)$:
$$p + q = r + s =: \sigma, \qquad f(p) + f(q) = f(r) + f(s).$$

Compute $f(p) + f(q) = 2(p^3+q^3) - 2(p+q) = 2\sigma(p^2 - pq + q^2) - 2\sigma =
 2\sigma^3 - 6\sigma\, pq - 2\sigma.$

So the $y$-midpoint condition gives $\sigma \cdot pq = \sigma \cdot rs$. If $\si
gma \neq 0$, then $pq = rs$, and combined with $p+q = r+s = \sigma$, the pairs $
\{p,q\}$ and $\{r,s\}$ are roots of the same quadratic — contradicting distinctn
ess. Therefore **$\sigma = 0$**: each pair of opposite vertices has $x$-values s
umming to zero.

**Step 2: Reduce to finding $b$ with equal diagonals.**

Since $a > 0$, the pair containing $a$ is $(a, -a)$, so the four $x$-values are
$\{a, -a, b, -b\}$ with $b \neq 0$, $b \neq \pm a$.

Diagonal through $(a, f(a))$ and $(-a, f(-a))$: since $f(a) - f(-a) = 4a^3 - 4a
= 4a(a^2-1)$,
$$D_1^2 = (2a)^2 + (4a(a^2-1))^2 = 4a^2\bigl[1 + 4(a^2-1)^2\bigr].$$

Similarly $D_2^2 = 4b^2\bigl[1 + 4(b^2-1)^2\bigr]$. Equal diagonals requires:
$$a^2\bigl[1+4(a^2-1)^2\bigr] = b^2\bigl[1+4(b^2-1)^2\bigr].$$

**Step 3: Analyze $H(u) = 4u^3 - 8u^2 + 5u$ on $u \geq 0$.**

Setting $u = a^2$, $v = b^2$, we need $H(u) = H(v)$ with $u \neq v$, $u, v > 0$.

$$H'(u) = 12u^2 - 16u + 5 = 0 \implies u = \tfrac{1}{2},\; \tfrac{5}{6}.$$

Key values: $H(0) = 0$, $H(1/2) = 1$ (local max), $H(5/6) = 25/27$ (local min),
$H(1) = 1$, $H(u) \to \infty$ as $u \to \infty$.

$H$ increases on $(0, 1/2)$, decreases on $(1/2, 5/6)$, increases on $(5/6, \inf
ty)$.

**Step 4: Determine the valid range of $u = a^2$.**

For $H(u) = H(v)$ with $v \neq u$, $v > 0$, the horizontal line $y = H(u)$ must
hit the graph at another point:

- **$u \in (0, 1/3)$:** $H(u) \in (0, 25/27)$. Since $\min_{(1/2,\infty)} H = 25
/27 > H(u)$, no other intersection. **Invalid.**
- **$u = 1/3$:** $H(1/3) = 25/27 = H(5/6)$, and $5/6 \neq 1/3$. **Valid.** (Boun
dary.)
- **$u \in (1/3, 1)$:** $H(u) \in (25/27, 1)$, hitting both other branches. **Va
lid.**
- **$u = 1$:** $H(1) = 1 = H(1/2)$, and $1/2 \neq 1$. **Valid.** (Boundary.)
- **$u \in (1, \infty)$:** $H(u) > 1 = \max_{(0,5/6)} H$, so no other intersecti
on. **Invalid.**

The equation $H(u) = 25/27$ factors as $(u - 5/6)^2(u - 1/3) = 0$ (after scaling
), confirming $u = 1/3$ is the left boundary. The equation $H(u) = 1$ factors as
 $(u-1/2)^2(u-1) = 0$, confirming $u = 1$ is the right boundary.

**Step 5: Non-degeneracy check.**

At the boundaries, $v \neq u$ and the four $x$-values $\{a, -a, b, -b\}$ are dis
tinct (since $a^2 \neq b^2$). The four points are never collinear (collinearity
through the origin would require $f(a)/a = f(b)/b$, i.e., $a^2 = b^2$, excluded)
. So genuine rectangles exist throughout.

**Conclusion.** The valid range is $u = a^2 \in [1/3, 1]$, i.e., $a \in [1/\sqrt
{3}, 1]$, giving $L = 1/\sqrt{3}$ and $R = 1$.

$$3L^2 + R^2 = 3 \cdot \frac{1}{3} + 1 = \boxed{2}.$$
