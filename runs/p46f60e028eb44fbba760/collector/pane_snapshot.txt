## Proof

**Setup and metric considerations.** Equip $M$ with a Riemannian metric and $\ti
lde{M}$ with the lifted (pullback) metric, so that $\pi: \tilde{M} \to M$ is a l
ocal isometry. A local isometry is non-expanding on global distances:
$$d_M(\pi(a), \pi(b)) \leq d_{\tilde{M}}(a, b) \quad \text{for all } a, b \in \t
ilde{M}.$$

**Key claim:** $B_1(\tilde{p}) \subset S$.

**Proof of claim.** Let $\tilde{x} \in B_1(\tilde{p})$ be arbitrary. We show $\t
ilde{x} \in S$.

**Step 1: The projection lands in $B_1(p)$.** Set $x = \pi(\tilde{x})$ and $p =
\pi(\tilde{p})$. By the non-expanding property:
$$d_M(x, p) = d_M(\pi(\tilde{x}), \pi(\tilde{p})) \leq d_{\tilde{M}}(\tilde{x},
\tilde{p}) < 1.$$
Hence $x \in B_1(p)$, so the section $j$ is defined at $x$.

**Step 2: Use the section to get a canonical lift in $T$.** Let $t = j(x) \in T$
. By construction, $t \in B_1(\tilde{p})$ and $\pi(t) = x$.

**Step 3: Relate $\tilde{x}$ and $t$ via a deck transformation.** Both $\tilde{x
}$ and $t$ lie in the fiber $\pi^{-1}(x)$. Since $\pi: \tilde{M} \to M$ is the u
niversal cover, the deck transformation group $\mathrm{Deck}(\tilde{M}/M) \cong
\pi_1(M)$ acts **freely and transitively** on each fiber. Therefore there exists
 a unique $g \in \pi_1(M)$ such that
$$g(t) = \tilde{x}.$$

**Step 4: This $g$ qualifies for the union defining $S$.** Since $\tilde{x} = g(
t) \in g(T)$ and simultaneously $\tilde{x} \in B_1(\tilde{p})$, we have
$$\tilde{x} \in g(T) \cap B_1(\tilde{p}) \neq \emptyset.$$
Thus $g$ is among the deck transformations summed over in the definition of $S$,
 so $g(T) \subset S$.

**Step 5: Conclusion.** Since $\tilde{x} = g(t) \in g(T) \subset S$, we have $\t
ilde{x} \in S$.

Since $\tilde{x} \in B_1(\tilde{p})$ was arbitrary, we conclude
$$B_1(\tilde{p}) \subset S. \qquad \blacksquare$$

**Remark.** The argument is essentially tautological once observed: the point $\
tilde{x}$ itself serves as the witness that its own deck-transformation translat
e $g(T)$ intersects $B_1(\tilde{p})$. The only non-trivial ingredient is that th
e projection of $B_1(\tilde{p})$ lands inside $B_1(p)$ (guaranteed by $\pi$ bein
g non-expanding), ensuring the section $j$ is defined wherever needed. The measu
rability of $j$ plays no role in the set-theoretic inclusion.
