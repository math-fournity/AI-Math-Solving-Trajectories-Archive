# Proof

**Coordinate setup.** Place $A=(0,0)$ and $B=(d,0)$ where $d=AB$. Then:
- $\Gamma_1: x^2+y^2=r^2$ (center $A$, radius $r$),
- $\Gamma_2: x^2+y^2=2dx$ (center $B=(d,0)$, radius $d$, since $A\in\Gamma_2$).

The **radical axis** of $\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2$ is $2dx=r^2$, i.e. the vertical line
 $\ell:\ x=\dfrac{r^2}{2d}$. We prove $N\in\ell$.

**Parametrizing the tangent line at $S$.** Let $S=(s_1,s_2)\in\Gamma_1$, so $s_1
^2+s_2^2=r^2$. The tangent to $\Gamma_1$ at $S$ is $s_1 x+s_2 y=r^2$, which pass
es through both $P$ and $Q$. Parametrize this line as
$$X(t)=(s_1-ts_2,\ s_2+ts_1),$$
with direction $(-s_2,s_1)\perp S$. Set $P=X(t_P)$, $Q=X(t_Q)$.

**Quadratic for $t$.** Substituting $X(t)$ into $\Gamma_2$:
$$(s_1-ts_2)^2+(s_2+ts_1)^2=2d(s_1-ts_2)\implies r^2(1+t^2)=2d(s_1-ts_2),$$
$$r^2 t^2+2d\,s_2\,t+(r^2-2d\,s_1)=0.\tag{$\star$}$$

So $t_P,t_Q$ are the roots; by Vieta:
$$t_P+t_Q=-\frac{2d\,s_2}{r^2},\qquad t_P t_Q=\frac{r^2-2d\,s_1}{r^2}.$$

Solving for $s_1,s_2$:
$$s_1=\frac{r^2(1-t_P t_Q)}{2d},\qquad s_2=-\frac{r^2(t_P+t_Q)}{2d}.\tag{1}$$

The constraint $s_1^2+s_2^2=r^2$ gives
$$\frac{r^4}{4d^2}\bigl[(1-t_Pt_Q)^2+(t_P+t_Q)^2\bigr]=r^2\implies (1+t_P^2)(1+t
_Q^2)=\frac{4d^2}{r^2}.\tag{2}$$

**Key coordinates.** Using (1):

- $P_x=s_1-t_P s_2=\dfrac{r^2(1+t_P^2)}{2d}$, so $M=\bigl(\tfrac{r^2(1+t_P^2)}{2
d},\,0\bigr)$.
- $Q_x=s_1-t_Q s_2=\dfrac{r^2(1+t_Q^2)}{2d}$.
- $Q_y=s_2+t_Q s_1=-\dfrac{r^2\,t_P(1+t_Q^2)}{2d}$.

**Finding $T$.** The point $T\ne S$ is the other tangent point from $Q$ to $\Gam
ma_1$, lying on $\Gamma_1$ and on the polar of $Q$ ($Q\cdot X=r^2$). Parametrize
 the polar (which passes through $S$) as $X=S+u(-Q_y,Q_x)$. Solving $|X|^2=r^2$
yields $u=0$ (giving $S$) and $u=\tfrac{2t_Q}{1+t_Q^2}$ (giving $T$). A direct c
omputation gives
$$T_y=s_2+u\,Q_x=\frac{r^2(t_Q-t_P)}{2d}.\tag{3}$$

**Cross product.** With $a=s_1,b=s_2,t=t_Q$:
$$Q\times T=Q_x T_y-Q_y T_x=\frac{(a-tb)(b(1-t^2)+2ta)-(b+ta)(a(1-t^2)-2tb)}{1+t
^2}=\frac{(a^2+b^2)\,t(1+t^2)}{1+t^2}=r^2\,t_Q.\tag{4}$$

**Computing $N=AQ\cap MT$.** Write $N=\lambda Q$ (on line $AQ$) and $N=M+\mu(T-M
)$ (on line $MT$). Since $M_y=0$:
$$\lambda Q_y=\mu\,T_y\implies \mu=\frac{\lambda Q_y}{T_y},$$
$$\lambda Q_x=M_x+\frac{\lambda Q_y}{T_y}(T_x-M_x)\implies \lambda=\frac{M_x\,T_
y}{Q_x T_y-Q_y T_x+Q_y M_x}.$$

**Denominator.** Using (4) and the product $Q_y M_x$:
$$Q_y M_x=\left(-\frac{r^2 t_P(1+t_Q^2)}{2d}\right)\!\left(\frac{r^2(1+t_P^2)}{2
d}\right)=-\frac{r^4\,t_P(1+t_P^2)(1+t_Q^2)}{4d^2}\overset{(2)}{=}-r^2\,t_P.$$

Hence the denominator is $r^2 t_Q+Q_y M_x=r^2 t_Q-r^2 t_P=r^2(t_Q-t_P)$.

**Numerator.**
$$M_x\,T_y\,Q_x=\frac{r^2(1+t_P^2)}{2d}\cdot\frac{r^2(t_Q-t_P)}{2d}\cdot\frac{r^
2(1+t_Q^2)}{2d}=\frac{r^6(1+t_P^2)(1+t_Q^2)(t_Q-t_P)}{8d^3}.$$

**Conclusion.**
$$N_x=\lambda Q_x=\frac{M_x\,T_y\,Q_x}{r^2(t_Q-t_P)}=\frac{r^4(1+t_P^2)(1+t_Q^2)
}{8d^3}\overset{(2)}{=}\frac{r^4\cdot 4d^2/r^2}{8d^3}=\frac{r^2}{2d}.$$

This value depends only on the fixed quantities $r$ and $d=AB$, and is **indepen
dent of $P$** (i.e., of $t_P,t_Q$). Therefore $N$ always lies on the line
$$\boxed{x=\frac{r^2}{2d}},$$
which is precisely the **radical axis of $\Gamma_1$ and $\Gamma_2$** — a line in
dependent of the position of $P$ on $\Gamma_2$. $\qquad\blacksquare$
