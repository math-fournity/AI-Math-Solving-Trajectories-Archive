Looking at this problem, I need to analyze the two natural group actions on the
fiber of a universal cover and determine whether "sharply transitive + agree on
one element" guarantees equivalence in all cases.

## Proof

Let $p: \tilde{X} \to X$ be a universal cover, $x_0 \in X$, $F = p^{-1}(x_0)$, a
nd $G = \pi_1(X, x_0)$. Fix a basepoint $\tilde{x}_0 \in F$.

**The two actions.**

1. **Deck transformation action (left action):** Via the isomorphism $\phi: G \t
o \operatorname{Deck}(\tilde{X}/X)$, define $g \cdot_D \tilde{x} := \phi(g)(\til
de{x})$.

2. **Monodromy action (right action):** For $g = [\gamma] \in G$ and $\tilde{x}
\in F$, define $\tilde{x} \cdot_M g$ as the endpoint of the lift of $\gamma$ sta
rting at $\tilde{x}$.

**The isomorphism $\phi$ is a homomorphism.** By definition, $\phi(g)(\tilde{x}_
0) = \tilde{x}_0 \cdot_M g$. For $g, h \in G$:
$$\phi(gh)(\tilde{x}_0) = \tilde{x}_0 \cdot_M (gh) = (\tilde{x}_0 \cdot_M g) \cd
ot_M h = \phi(g)(\tilde{x}_0) \cdot_M h.$$
Since $\phi(g)$ is a deck transformation, it commutes with path lifting, so $\ph
i(g)(\tilde{x}_0) \cdot_M h = \phi(g)(\tilde{x}_0 \cdot_M h) = \phi(g)(\phi(h)(\
tilde{x}_0)) = (\phi(g) \circ \phi(h))(\tilde{x}_0)$. By sharp transitivity (tri
vial stabilizer), $\phi(gh) = \phi(g) \circ \phi(h)$. $\checkmark$

**Both actions are sharply transitive.** This is standard: the deck group acts f
reely and transitively on each fiber of a universal cover, and monodromy gives a
 free and transitive action of $\pi_1$ on the fiber.

**They agree on $\tilde{x}_0$.** By definition of $\phi$:
$$g \cdot_D \tilde{x}_0 = \phi(g)(\tilde{x}_0) = \tilde{x}_0 \cdot_M g. \quad \c
heckmark$$

**Key computation.** For any $\tilde{x} \in F$, by sharp transitivity of the dec
k action there exists a unique $h \in G$ with $\tilde{x} = h \cdot_D \tilde{x}_0
 = \tilde{x}_0 \cdot_M h$. Then:

$$g \cdot_D \tilde{x} = g \cdot_D (h \cdot_D \tilde{x}_0) = (gh) \cdot_D \tilde{
x}_0 = \tilde{x}_0 \cdot_M (gh),$$

$$\tilde{x} \cdot_M g = (\tilde{x}_0 \cdot_M h) \cdot_M g = \tilde{x}_0 \cdot_M
(hg).$$

These are equal for all $g, h \in G$ **if and only if** $gh = hg$ for all $g, h$
, i.e., $G$ is abelian.

**Conclusion.** The two actions are equal (and hence equivalent) **if and only i
f $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ is abelian**. For spaces with non-abelian fundamental group—e.
g., the wedge of two circles $S^1 \vee S^1$ with $\pi_1 \cong F_2$ (the free gro
up on two generators)—the two actions are **not** equivalent.

**Why the naive argument fails.** The standard argument—"two sharply transitive
actions of the same group on the same set that agree on one point must be equal"
—is valid when **both actions are on the same side** (both left or both right).
Indeed, if $\cdot_1, \cdot_2$ are both left actions, $g \cdot_1 s_0 = g \cdot_2
s_0$ for all $g$ implies: for any $s = g_s \cdot_1 s_0 = g_s \cdot_2 s_0$ and an
y $g$,
$$g \cdot_1 s = (g g_s) \cdot_1 s_0 = (g g_s) \cdot_2 s_0 = g \cdot_2 (g_s \cdot
_2 s_0) = g \cdot_2 s.$$
But here, one action is a **left** action and the other is a **right** action. T
he multiplication order is reversed: the deck action composes as $(gh) \cdot_D =
 g \cdot_D (h \cdot_D)$ while the monodromy composes as $\cdot_M (hg) = (\cdot_M
 h) \cdot_M g$. Agreement on one point forces $gh = hg$ for all $g, h$, which is
 exactly the abelian condition.

**Counterexample.** Take $X = S^1 \vee S^1$ (wedge of two circles), so $G = \pi_
1(X, x_0) \cong F_2 = \langle a, b \rangle$. Let $g = a, h = b$. Then:
- Deck: $a \cdot_D (b \cdot_D \tilde{x}_0) = (ab) \cdot_D \tilde{x}_0 = \tilde{x
}_0 \cdot_M (ab)$,
- Monodromy: $(b \cdot_D \tilde{x}_0) \cdot_M a = (\tilde{x}_0 \cdot_M b) \cdot_
M a = \tilde{x}_0 \cdot_M (ba)$.

Since $ab \neq ba$ in $F_2$, the two actions disagree at the point $b \cdot_D \t
ilde{x}_0$, even though they agree at $\tilde{x}_0$. $\square$
