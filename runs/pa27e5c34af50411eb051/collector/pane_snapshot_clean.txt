**Answer: No.** The statement is false. There exist infinite torsion abelian gro
ups with unbounded element orders that admit nontrivial homomorphisms from $(G,+
)=(\mathbb{R},+)$.

### Counterexample

Let $H = \mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty})$, the Prüfer $p$-group (for any fixed prime $p$)
, realized as the subgroup of $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ consisting of elements who
se order is a power of $p$:
$$\mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty}) = \{\,a/p^{k}+\mathbb{Z}\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} : a\in
\mathbb{Z},\ k\ge 0\,\}.$$

This group is infinite, torsion, and the orders of its elements are $\{1,p,p^{2}
,\dots\}$, which are **not uniformly bounded** by any constant. So $H$ satisfies
 the hypotheses.

### A nontrivial homomorphism $\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty})$

Recall the canonical decomposition
$$\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{\ell\ \text{prime}}\mathbb{Z}(\ell^{\
infty}),$$
and let $\pi_{p}:\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty})$ be the projecti
on onto the $p$-primary summand, a well-defined surjective group homomorphism. C
oncretely, for $a/b\in\mathbb{Q}$ in lowest terms with $b=p^{k}m$ and $\gcd(m,p)
=1$, picking $r$ with $rm\equiv 1\pmod{p^{k}}$ gives
$$\pi_{p}(a/b+\mathbb{Z}) = ar/p^{k}+\mathbb{Z}\in\mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty}).$$

Let $g:\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ be the quotient map $g(q)=q+\mathbb{Z
}$, and set $f=\pi_{p}\circ g:\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty})$. This is a gr
oup homomorphism, and it is **nontrivial** since
$$f(1/p)=\pi_{p}(1/p+\mathbb{Z})=1/p+\mathbb{Z}\neq 0.$$

Now view $\mathbb{R}$ as a $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space and choose a basis containi
ng $1$. This gives a $\mathbb{Q}$-linear (hence $\mathbb{Z}$-linear) direct-sum
decomposition $\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{Q}\cdot 1\oplus V$. Define
$$F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty}),\qquad F(q\cdot 1+v)=f(q).$$
Then $F$ is a group homomorphism, and
$$F(1/p)=f(1/p)=1/p+\mathbb{Z}\neq 0,$$
so $F$ is **nontrivial**.

### Conclusion

The group $H=\mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty})$ is an infinite torsion abelian group whose
element orders are not uniformly bounded, yet there exists a nontrivial homomorp
hism $\mathbb{R}\to H$. Therefore the claimed assertion — that only the trivial
homomorphism can exist — is **false**.

(The error in the intuition that "unbounded torsion forces triviality" is that u
nboundedness of orders does **not** imply the group is *reduced*. A divisible to
rsion group such as $\mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty})$ is a perfectly good target for maps
 out of the divisible group $\mathbb{R}$; what would force triviality is the abs
ence of any nonzero *divisible* subgroup in $H$, i.e. $H$ being reduced — a cond
ition independent of, and not implied by, having unbounded exponent.)
