## Proof

Let $D$ be a finite-dimensional central division algebra over its center $Z$, an
d set $\dim_Z D = n^2$, so $D$ has **degree** $n$.

### Step 1. The centralizer of a subfield

Let $F$ be a subfield of $D$ with $Z \subseteq F \subseteq D$. Define the centra
lizer
$$C_D(F) = \{x \in D : xf = fx \text{ for all } f \in F\}.$$

**$C_D(F)$ is a division algebra.** Since $D$ is a division algebra, any nonzero
 $x \in C_D(F)$ has an inverse $x^{-1} \in D$. For any $f \in F$, $xf = fx$ impl
ies $x^{-1}f = fx^{-1}$ (multiply on both sides by $x^{-1}$), so $x^{-1} \in C_D
(F)$. Hence $C_D(F)$ is closed under inverses and is a division subalgebra.

**The center of $C_D(F)$ is $F$.** Clearly $F \subseteq Z(C_D(F))$ since every e
lement of $F$ commutes with every element of $C_D(F)$. Conversely, if $z \in Z(C
_D(F))$, then $z$ commutes with everything that commutes with $F$, i.e., $z \in
C_D(C_D(F))$. We now apply the **double centralizer theorem**: since $F$ is a si
mple subalgebra of the central simple algebra $D$, we have $C_D(C_D(F)) = F$. Th
us $z \in F$, proving $Z(C_D(F)) = F$.

### Step 2. The degree formula $\deg(C_D(F)) = n/[F:Z]$

Consider $D \otimes_Z F$, which is a central simple algebra over $F$ of dimensio
n $n^2$ (hence of degree $n$). The space $D$ is a left $D \otimes_Z F$-module vi
a $(d \otimes f)\cdot x = dxf$.

**$D$ is a simple $D\otimes_Z F$-module.** Any submodule is a left ideal of $D$
(from the $D$-action) that is stable under right multiplication by $F$. Since $D
$ is a division algebra, its only left ideals are $0$ and $D$. Hence $D$ is simp
le.

Since $D\otimes_Z F$ is central simple over $F$, we have $D \otimes_Z F \cong M_
r(\Delta)$ for some central division algebra $\Delta$ over $F$ of degree $d$, wi
th $rd = n$. The unique simple module over $M_r(\Delta)$ is $\Delta^r$ (column v
ectors), of $F$-dimension $rd^2$. Since $D$ is simple:
$$\dim_F D = rd^2 = nd.$$
But $\dim_F D = n^2/[F:Z]$, so:
$$\frac{n^2}{[F:Z]} = nd \implies [F:Z] = \frac{n}{d}.$$

The endomorphism ring of the simple module $D$ over $D\otimes_Z F \cong M_r(\Del
ta)$ is $\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}$. On the other hand, computing directly: an $F$-li
near endomorphism $\varphi$ of $D$ commuting with the $D\otimes_Z F$-action sati
sfies $\varphi(dx f) = d\,\varphi(x)\,f$, which forces $\varphi$ to be right mul
tiplication by some element of $C_D(F)$. Thus:
$$\operatorname{End}_{D\otimes_Z F}(D) \cong C_D(F)^{\mathrm{op}} \cong \Delta^{
\mathrm{op}},$$
so $C_D(F) \cong \Delta$ and:
$$\deg(C_D(F)) = d = \frac{n}{[F:Z]}.$$

In particular, $[F:Z]$ divides $n$.

### Step 3. Maximal subfields are exactly those with $[F:Z] = n$

**($\Rightarrow$) Suppose $F$ is a maximal subfield** (no subfield of $D$ proper
ly contains $F$). We claim $C_D(F) = F$. Indeed, if there exists $x \in C_D(F) \
setminus F$, then $F[x]$ is a commutative subring of $D$ (since $x$ commutes wit
h $F$ and with itself). Being a finite-dimensional integral domain over the fiel
d $F$, it is a field — a subfield of $D$ properly containing $F$, contradicting
maximality. So $C_D(F) = F$, meaning $\deg(C_D(F)) = 1$. By the degree formula:
$$1 = \frac{n}{[F:Z]} \implies [F:Z] = n.$$

**($\Leftarrow$) Suppose $[F:Z] = n$.** Then $\deg(C_D(F)) = n/n = 1$, so $C_D(F
) = F$. If $G$ were a subfield with $F \subsetneq G \subseteq D$, then $G \subse
teq C_D(F) = F$, a contradiction. Hence $F$ is maximal.

### Step 4. Conclusion

Every maximal subfield $F$ of $D$ satisfies:
$$\dim_Z F = [F:Z] = n = \sqrt{\dim_Z D}.$$

This value depends only on $D$ (equivalently, on $\dim_Z D$), not on the choice
of maximal subfield. Therefore $\dim_Z F$ **is an invariant** of $D$, the same f
or all maximum subfields.

$\blacksquare$
