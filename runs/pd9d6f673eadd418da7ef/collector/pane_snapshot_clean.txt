## Proof

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains only the problem; no solut
ion, answer, or proof sketch is leaked. Proceeding.

---

We solve the boundary value problem by applying the Fourier transform in the $x$
-variable.

### 1. Fourier transform of the PDE

Define the Fourier transform in $x$:
$$\widehat{u}(\xi, y) \;=\; \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} u(x,y)\,e^{-i\xi x}\,dx.$$

Under this transform, $u_{xx} \mapsto -\xi^{2}\,\widehat{u}$. Applying it to $\D
elta u = 0$ gives, for each $\xi$,
$$\widehat{u}_{yy}(\xi,y) - \xi^{2}\,\widehat{u}(\xi,y) = 0.$$

This is an ODE in $y$ with general solution
$$\widehat{u}(\xi,y) = A(\xi)\,e^{|\xi|\,y} + B(\xi)\,e^{-|\xi|\,y}.$$

### 2. Use boundedness to select the decaying branch

Since $u$ is bounded on $H$, say $|u|\le M$, we have $|\widehat{u}(\xi,y)|\le 2\
pi M$ (integrability in the distributional sense; more rigorously one works with
 tempered distributions or $L^\infty$-bounded solutions whose transform is a dis
tribution). In any case the exponentially growing mode $e^{|\xi|y}$ is incompati
ble with boundedness as $y\to\infty$ for $\xi\ne 0$, so we must have $A(\xi)=0$.
 Thus
$$\widehat{u}(\xi,y) = B(\xi)\,e^{-|\xi|\,y}.$$

### 3. Impose the boundary condition

At $y=0$: $\widehat{u}(\xi,0) = \widehat{f}(\xi)$, hence $B(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi
)$, and
$$\widehat{u}(\xi,y) = \widehat{f}(\xi)\,e^{-|\xi|\,y}.$$

### 4. Invert: the Poisson kernel

By the convolution theorem,
$$u(x,y) = (f * P_y)(x), \qquad P_y(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e
^{-|\xi|\,y}\,e^{i\xi x}\,d\xi.$$

Compute $P_y$ directly:
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-|\xi|y}e^{i\xi x}\,d\xi = \int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\xi
 y}e^{i\xi x}\,d\xi + \int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\xi y}e^{-i\xi x}\,d\xi = \frac{1}{y-
ix}+\frac{1}{y+ix} = \frac{2y}{x^{2}+y^{2}}.$$

Therefore the **Poisson kernel** for the upper half-plane is
$$P_y(x) = \frac{1}{\pi}\,\frac{y}{x^{2}+y^{2}}, \qquad y>0,$$

and the solution is
$$\boxed{\,u(x,y) = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{y}{(x-t)^{2}+y^{2
}}\,f(t)\,dt\,}, \qquad y>0.$$

### 5. Verification

**(a) Harmonicity.** For $y>0$, $P_y(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{y}{x^2+y^2}$ is smoot
h and one checks directly $\Delta P_y = 0$ (it is the imaginary part of $\frac{1
}{\pi z}$, $z=x+iy$, hence harmonic). Differentiating under the integral (justif
ied for $y>0$ since $P_y$ and its derivatives decay rapidly) gives $\Delta u = \
int \Delta_x P_y(x-t)\,f(t)\,dt = 0$.

**(b) Boundedness.** Since $P_y\ge 0$ and $\int P_y(x)\,dx = 1$ (a direct residu
e/elementary computation), if $|f|\le M$ then
$$|u(x,y)| \le M\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} P_y(x-t)\,dt = M.$$

**(c) Boundary condition.** $P_y$ is an approximate identity: $\int P_y=1$, and
for any $\delta>0$, $\int_{|t|>\delta}P_y(t)\,dt \to 0$ as $y\to 0^+$ (since $P_
y(t)\le \frac{y}{\pi\delta^2}$ there). With $f$ bounded and continuous at $x$,
$$u(x,y) - f(x) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} P_y(t)\bigl[f(x-t)-f(x)\bigr]\,dt \to
0 \quad\text{as }y\to 0^+,$$
by splitting the integral into $|t|<\delta$ (use continuity) and $|t|\ge\delta$
(use the tail estimate and boundedness). Hence $u(x,0)=f(x)$.

### 6. Uniqueness

If $v$ is another bounded harmonic function on $H$ with $v(x,0)=0$, the function
 $w=v$ satisfies $|w|\le M'$ and $w\to 0$ at the boundary. By the maximum princi
ple applied on the truncated domain $H_R=\{(x,y): x^2+y^2<R^2,\, y>0\}$: on the
semicircle $x^2+y^2=R^2$ the barrier $\varphi(x,y)=\frac{2M'}{\pi}\arg(R+iz)$ (h
armonic, $=M'$ on the arc, $=0$ on $y=0$) dominates $w$, and similarly $-w$; let
ting $R\to\infty$ yields $w\equiv 0$. Thus the bounded solution is unique.

---

The unique bounded solution of the Laplace equation on the upper half-plane with
 boundary data $f$ is the **Poisson integral**

$$u(x,y) = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{y}{(x-t)^{2}+y^{2}}\,f(t)\,
dt, \qquad y>0,\quad u(x,0)=f(x).$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
