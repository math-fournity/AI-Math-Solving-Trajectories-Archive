## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. We have $|e^{At}| \leq e^{-\lambda t}$, so $M=1$ suffices and $e
^{At}$ is a semigroup of contractions.

**Step 1: The semigroup acts diagonally on each basis vector.**

Since $e_k \in D(A)$ and $Ae_k = -\lambda_k e_k$, the function $u(t) = e^{-\lamb
da_k t}e_k$ solves the abstract Cauchy problem
$$\frac{d}{dt}u(t) = -\lambda_k e^{-\lambda_k t}e_k = A\big(e^{-\lambda_k t}e_k\
big), \qquad u(0)=e_k.$$
By uniqueness of the classical solution to the abstract Cauchy problem for initi
al data in $D(A)$, we conclude
$$e^{At}e_k = e^{-\lambda_k t}e_k, \qquad \forall\, t\ge 0.$$

**Step 2: The estimate on finite linear combinations.**

Let $x = \sum_{k=1}^{N} c_k e_k$ be a finite linear combination. By linearity of
 $e^{At}$,
$$e^{At}x = \sum_{k=1}^{N} c_k\, e^{-\lambda_k t}\, e_k.$$
Since $\{e_k\}$ is orthonormal,
$$\|e^{At}x\|^2 = \sum_{k=1}^{N} |c_k|^2\, e^{-2\lambda_k t}.$$
Because $\lambda_k \geq \lambda = \inf_k \lambda_k > 0$, we have $e^{-\lambda_k
t} \leq e^{-\lambda t}$ for every $k$ and every $t\ge 0$. Hence
$$\|e^{At}x\|^2 \leq \sum_{k=1}^{N} |c_k|^2\, e^{-2\lambda t} = e^{-2\lambda t}\
|x\|^2,$$
so
$$\|e^{At}x\| \leq e^{-\lambda t}\|x\|.$$

**Step 3: Extension to all of $H$ by density and continuity.**

The finite linear combinations of $\{e_k\}$ are dense in $H$. For each fixed $t\
ge 0$, the operator $e^{At}\colon H\to H$ is bounded (as an element of a strongl
y continuous semigroup on a Hilbert space). Therefore the inequality $\|e^{At}x\
|\le e^{-\lambda t}\|x\|$, established on a dense subspace, extends by continuit
y to all $x\in H$. Consequently
$$|e^{At}| = \sup_{\|x\|=1}\|e^{At}x\| \leq e^{-\lambda t}, \qquad \forall\, t\g
e 0.$$

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

We may take $M=1$ in the Hille–Yosida estimate. Since $\lambda>0$, we have $e^{-
\lambda t}\le 1$ for all $t\ge 0$, so $|e^{At}|\le 1$; that is, $e^{At}$ is a se
migroup of contractions (indeed, an exponentially stable contraction semigroup w
ith decay rate $\lambda$).

The key reason $M=1$ works here is that $\{e_k\}$ is an **orthonormal** basis of
 eigenvectors with eigenvalues all bounded below by $\lambda>0$: the semigroup a
cts as multiplication by $e^{-\lambda_k t}\le e^{-\lambda t}$ on each coordinate
, and orthonormality ensures that the coordinatewise bound lifts to a norm bound
 without any constant $M>1$.
