## Proof

Let $k$ be the base field (assumed algebraically closed, or more generally any f
ield over which $\mu_2 = \{\pm 1\}$ is a well-defined constant group scheme). We
 work with $\mathbb{G}_m = \operatorname{Spec} k[T, T^{-1}]$, the multiplicative
 group scheme, with comultiplication $T \mapsto T \otimes T$.

**The subgroup $\mu_2$.** The constant subgroup $\{\pm 1\} = \mu_2 \hookrightarr
ow \mathbb{G}_m$ corresponds to the Hopf subalgebra quotient $k[T,T^{-1}] \to k[
\mu_2] = k \times k$ sending $T \mapsto (1,-1)$. Equivalently, $\mu_2$ acts on $
\mathbb{G}_m$ by the automorphism $T \mapsto -T$.

**The quotient.** The quotient $\mathbb{G}_m / \mu_2$ is, by definition (for a f
inite group acting freely),
$$\mathbb{G}_m / \mu_2 = \operatorname{Spec}\bigl(k[T, T^{-1}]^{\mu_2}\bigr),$$
where $\mu_2$ acts on the coordinate ring by $T \mapsto -T$.

**Computing the invariants.** A general element of $k[T, T^{-1}]$ is a finite La
urent sum $f(T) = \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} a_n T^n$. The nontrivial element of $\
mu_2$ sends $f \mapsto f(-T) = \sum_n a_n (-1)^n T^n$. Thus $f$ is invariant iff
 $a_n = (-1)^n a_n$ for all $n$, i.e., $a_n = 0$ for all odd $n$. Hence
$$k[T, T^{-1}]^{\mu_2} = \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} k \cdot T^{2n} = k[T^2, T^
{-2}].$$

**The isomorphism.** Define the ring map
$$\varphi^* : k[U, U^{-1}] \longrightarrow k[T, T^{-1}]^{\mu_2} = k[T^2, T^{-2}]
, \qquad U \longmapsto T^2.$$
This is an isomorphism of $k$-algebras (it is surjective by the computation abov
e, and injective since $U$ is transcendental). It is moreover a Hopf algebra iso
morphism: the comultiplication on $k[T^2, T^{-2}]$ sends $T^2 \mapsto T^2 \otime
s T^2$, which matches the comultiplication $U \mapsto U \otimes U$ on $k[U, U^{-
1}]$ under $\varphi^*$.

Taking $\operatorname{Spec}$, we obtain an isomorphism of algebraic groups
$$\varphi : \mathbb{G}_m / \mu_2 \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; \mathbb{G}_m.$$

**Explicit form.** The quotient map $q : \mathbb{G}_m \to \mathbb{G}_m/\mu_2$ co
rresponds to the inclusion $k[T^2, T^{-2}] \hookrightarrow k[T, T^{-1}]$. Compos
ing with $\varphi$ (which identifies $T^2 \leftrightarrow U$), the composite
$$\mathbb{G}_m \xrightarrow{q} \mathbb{G}_m/\mu_2 \xrightarrow{\varphi} \mathbb{
G}_m$$
is the **squaring map**
$$\operatorname{sq} : \mathbb{G}_m \to \mathbb{G}_m, \qquad x \longmapsto x^2,$$
since on coordinate rings it is $U \mapsto T^2$.

**Verification.** The squaring map is a group homomorphism ($(xy)^2 = x^2 y^2$)
with kernel $\ker(\operatorname{sq}) = \{x : x^2 = 1\} = \{\pm 1\} = \mu_2$. The
 induced map $\mathbb{G}_m/\mu_2 \to \mathbb{G}_m$ is precisely $\varphi$, which
 we showed is an isomorphism on coordinate rings. $\square$
