## Proof

**Setup.** Let \(\pi: M \to M/G\) be the quotient map. Since \(G\) is discrete a
nd acts freely (without fixed points) and properly discontinuously (guaranteed b
y the hypothesis that \(M/G\) is a manifold), the projection \(\pi\) is a coveri
ng map. Each fiber \(\pi^{-1}(\bar p)\) is the orbit \(G\cdot p\).

**Defining the metric.** Let \(\bar p \in M/G\) and pick any lift \(p \in M\) wi
th \(\pi(p)=\bar p\). Since \(\pi\) is a local diffeomorphism, its differential
\(d\pi_p : T_p M \to T_{\bar p}(M/G)\) is a linear isomorphism. Define an inner
product on \(T_{\bar p}(M/G)\) by
\[
\bar g_{\bar p}(X, Y) \;=\; g_p\!\big((d\pi_p)^{-1}X,\;(d\pi_p)^{-1}Y\big).
\]

**Well-definedness (independence of lift).** Let \(p' = \gamma\cdot p\) be anoth
er lift of the same \(\bar p\), for some \(\gamma \in G\). Since \(\gamma\) is a
n isometry, \(d\gamma_p : T_p M \to T_{p'}M\) preserves \(g\):
\[
g_{p'}(d\gamma_p\, v,\; d\gamma_p\, w) = g_p(v,w).
\]
Because \(\pi\circ\gamma = \pi\), we have \(d\pi_{p'}\circ d\gamma_p = d\pi_p\),
 hence \((d\pi_{p'})^{-1} = d\gamma_p \circ (d\pi_p)^{-1}\). Therefore
\[
g_{p'}\!\big((d\pi_{p'})^{-1}X,\,(d\pi_{p'})^{-1}Y\big)
= g_{p'}\!\big(d\gamma_p(d\pi_p)^{-1}X,\; d\gamma_p(d\pi_p)^{-1}Y\big)
= g_p\!\big((d\pi_p)^{-1}X,\,(d\pi_p)^{-1}Y\big),
\]
so \(\bar g_{\bar p}\) is independent of the choice of lift.

**Smoothness.** On any evenly covered neighborhood \(U\subset M/G\), choose a lo
cal section \(s: U \to M\) (so \(\pi\circ s = \mathrm{id}_U\)). Then on \(U\),
\[
\bar g = s^{*} g,
\]
the pullback of the smooth tensor \(g\) by the smooth map \(s\). Hence \(\bar g\
) is smooth on \(U\), and by covering \(M/G\) with such neighborhoods, \(\bar g\
) is a smooth \((0,2)\)-tensor field.

**Positive definiteness.** Immediate: \(d\pi_p\) is an isomorphism and \(g_p\) i
s positive definite, so \(\bar g_{\bar p}\) is positive definite.

**Key property: \(\pi\) is a local isometry.** By construction, for every \(p\in
 M\) and \(v,w\in T_pM\),
\[
\bar g_{\pi(p)}(d\pi_p\, v,\; d\pi_p\, w) = g_p(v,w),
\]
so \(\pi^*\bar g = g\); equivalently \(\pi\) is a Riemannian local isometry.

**Uniqueness.** Suppose \(\bar g'\) is another Riemannian metric on \(M/G\) maki
ng \(\pi\) a local isometry. Then for all \(p, v, w\):
\[
\bar g'_{\pi(p)}(d\pi_p\,v,\,d\pi_p\,w) = g_p(v,w) = \bar g_{\pi(p)}(d\pi_p\,v,\
,d\pi_p\,w).
\]
Since \(d\pi_p\) is surjective onto \(T_{\pi(p)}(M/G)\), this gives \(\bar g' =
\bar g\). Hence the metric is the unique one for which \(\pi\) is a Riemannian s
ubmersion (here, local isometry).

**Conclusion.** The Riemannian structure on \(M/G\) is the unique metric \(\bar
g\) such that the quotient projection \(\pi: M \to M/G\) is a local isometry. Co
ncretely, at each \(\bar p = \pi(p)\),
\[
\boxed{\;\bar g_{\bar p}(X,Y) \;=\; g_p\!\big((d\pi_p)^{-1}X,\,(d\pi_p)^{-1}Y\bi
g)\;}
\]
which is well-defined because every element of \(G\) is an isometry, smooth beca
use it is locally the pullback of \(g\) by a smooth section, and unique among me
trics making \(\pi\) a local isometry.
