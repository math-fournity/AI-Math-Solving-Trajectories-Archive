## Solution

**Setting up the substitution.** Let $f_1(x)=4x(1-x)$. Using the standard substi
tution $x=\sin^2\theta$ with $\theta\in[0,\pi/2]$ (which is a bijection from $[0
,\pi/2]$ to $[0,1]$):

$$f_1(x)=4\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta=\sin^2(2\theta).$$

**Iterates.** Since $f_{n+1}(x)=f_n(f_1(x))$, by induction $f_n(x)=f_1^{(n)}(x)$
 (the $n$-th iterate). Applying the substitution repeatedly:

$$f_n(x)=\sin^2(2^n\theta),\qquad x=\sin^2\theta,\ \theta\in[0,\pi/2].$$

The range of $f_n$ on $[0,1]$ is $[0,1]$, so the **maximum value is $1$** and th
e **minimum value is $0$**.

**Counting $a_n$ (max = 1).** We need $\sin^2(2^n\theta)=1$, i.e. $2^n\theta=\fr
ac{\pi}{2}+k\pi=\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{2}$, giving

$$\theta=\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{2^{n+1}}.$$

The constraint $\theta\in[0,\pi/2]$ requires $0\le 2k+1\le 2^n$. Since $2k+1$ is
 odd and positive, $k=0,1,\dots,2^{n-1}-1$, yielding $2^{n-1}$ values of $\theta
$. Since $\sin^2\theta$ is strictly increasing (hence injective) on $[0,\pi/2]$,
 these give $2^{n-1}$ distinct values of $x$. Thus

$$a_n=2^{n-1}.$$

**Counting $b_n$ (min = 0).** We need $\sin^2(2^n\theta)=0$, i.e. $2^n\theta=k\p
i$, giving

$$\theta=\frac{k\pi}{2^n}.$$

The constraint $\theta\in[0,\pi/2]$ requires $0\le k\le 2^{n-1}$, yielding $2^{n
-1}+1$ values of $k$. Again by injectivity of $\sin^2\theta$ on $[0,\pi/2]$, the
se give $2^{n-1}+1$ distinct values of $x$. Thus

$$b_n=2^{n-1}+1.$$

**Verification.**
- $n=1$: $f_1$ max $1$ at $x=\tfrac12$ ($a_1=1=2^0$ ✓); min $0$ at $x=0,1$ ($b_1
=2=2^0+1$ ✓).
- $n=2$: max when $f_1(x)=\tfrac12$, i.e. $x=\tfrac{1\pm 1/\sqrt2}{2}$, two poin
ts ($a_2=2=2^1$ ✓); min $0$ at $x=0,1,\tfrac12$ ($b_2=3=2^1+1$ ✓).

$$\boxed{a_n=2^{n-1},\qquad b_n=2^{n-1}+1}$$
