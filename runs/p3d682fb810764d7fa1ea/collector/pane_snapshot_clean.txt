Looking at this problem, I need to evaluate the nested sum and find the digit su
m in base 100.

## Setting up the collapse

The sum is over chains $8 \ge a_1 \ge a_2 \ge \cdots \ge a_{100} \ge 0$:
$$K = \sum \binom{8}{a_1}\binom{a_1}{a_2}\cdots\binom{a_{99}}{a_{100}}.$$

**Key identity:** $\sum_{a_{k+1}=0}^{a_k} \binom{a_k}{a_{k+1}} c^{a_{k+1}} = (1+
c)^{a_k}$.

## Collapsing from the inside out

- **Collapse $a_{100}$:** $\sum_{a_{100}=0}^{a_{99}}\binom{a_{99}}{a_{100}} = 2^
{a_{99}}$.
- **Collapse $a_{99}$:** $\sum_{a_{99}=0}^{a_{98}}\binom{a_{98}}{a_{99}}\cdot 2^
{a_{99}} = (1+2)^{a_{98}} = 3^{a_{98}}$.
- **Collapse $a_{98}$:** $\sum_{a_{98}=0}^{a_{97}}\binom{a_{97}}{a_{98}}\cdot 3^
{a_{98}} = 4^{a_{97}}$.

**Pattern:** After collapsing $a_{100-k}$, the base $k+2$ sits on $a_{99-k}$.

Collapsing all 100 sums, the last is $a_1$ (where $100-k=1$, so $k=99$), giving
base $99+2 = 101$ on $a_0 = 8$:
$$K = \sum_{a_1=0}^{8}\binom{8}{a_1}\cdot 100^{a_1} = (1+100)^8 = 101^8.$$

## Base-100 digit sum

Since $101 = 1\cdot 100 + 1$:
$$101^8 = (100+1)^8 = \sum_{k=0}^{8}\binom{8}{k}100^k.$$

The base-100 digits are $\binom{8}{0},\binom{8}{1},\ldots,\binom{8}{8} = 1, 8, 2
8, 56, 70, 56, 28, 8, 1$.

All are $< 100$, so **no carrying occurs** — these are exactly the base-100 digi
ts.

**Sum of digits:**
$$1+8+28+56+70+56+28+8+1 = \sum_{k=0}^{8}\binom{8}{k} = 2^8 = \boxed{256}.$$
