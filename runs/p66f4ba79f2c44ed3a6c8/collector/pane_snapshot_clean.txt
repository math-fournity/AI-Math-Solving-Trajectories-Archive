**Problem.** Find all Lebesgue integrable functions $f$ on $[0,1]$ such that $\i
nt_0^1 f(t)\,t^n\,dt = 0$ for every non-negative integer $n$.

---

**Claim.** The only such function is $f = 0$ almost everywhere.

---

**Proof.**

The "if" direction is immediate: if $f = 0$ a.e., then $f(t)t^n = 0$ a.e. for ev
ery $n$, so every moment vanishes. We prove the "only if" direction.

**Step 1. Vanishing against all polynomials.**
By linearity of the integral, the hypothesis $\int_0^1 f(t)\,t^n\,dt = 0$ for ev
ery $n \geq 0$ implies
$$
\int_0^1 f(t)\,p(t)\,dt = 0
$$
for every polynomial $p$.

**Step 2. Vanishing against all continuous functions.**
Consider the linear functional $L : C[0,1] \to \mathbb{R}$ defined by $L(g) = \i
nt_0^1 g(t) f(t)\,dt$. It is bounded with respect to the supremum norm, since
$$
|L(g)| \leq \|g\|_\infty \int_0^1 |f(t)|\,dt = \|g\|_\infty \,\|f\|_1.
$$
By the Weierstrass approximation theorem, polynomials are dense in $C[0,1]$ with
 respect to $\|\cdot\|_\infty$. Since $L$ vanishes on the dense subset of polyno
mials and is continuous, $L$ vanishes on all of $C[0,1]$:
$$
\int_0^1 g(t)\,f(t)\,dt = 0 \qquad \text{for every } g \in C[0,1]. \tag{$\ast$}
$$

**Step 3. Reducing to $\int|f| = 0$.**
The function $\operatorname{sgn}(f)$ (defined as $1$ where $f>0$, $-1$ where $f<
0$, and $0$ where $f=0$) is bounded and measurable, hence belongs to $L^1[0,1]$.
 Since $C[0,1]$ is dense in $L^1[0,1]$, there exists a sequence $(g_k) \subset C
[0,1]$ with $g_k \to \operatorname{sgn}(f)$ in $L^1$. Replacing $g_k$ by $\max(-
1,\min(1,g_k))$ (which remains continuous, stays in $[-1,1]$, and does not incre
ase the $L^1$-error since $\operatorname{sgn}(f) \in [-1,1]$), we may assume $|g
_k| \leq 1$ for all $k$.

Since $g_k \to \operatorname{sgn}(f)$ in $L^1$, a subsequence $(g_{k_j})$ conver
ges to $\operatorname{sgn}(f)$ almost everywhere. Now
$$
\bigl|g_{k_j}(t) - \operatorname{sgn}(f)(t)\bigr|\cdot|f(t)| \leq 2\,|f(t)| \in
L^1[0,1],
$$
and the left-hand side tends to $0$ a.e. By the Dominated Convergence Theorem,
$$
\int_0^1 g_{k_j}(t)\,f(t)\,dt \;\longrightarrow\; \int_0^1 \operatorname{sgn}(f)
(t)\,f(t)\,dt = \int_0^1 |f(t)|\,dt.
$$
But by $(\ast)$ every term on the left equals $0$. Therefore
$$
\int_0^1 |f(t)|\,dt = 0.
$$

**Step 4. Conclusion.**
A non-negative integrable function with zero integral vanishes almost everywhere
. Hence $|f| = 0$ a.e., i.e. $f = 0$ a.e.

---

Therefore the unique Lebesgue integrable function on $[0,1]$ whose moments again
st $t^n$ all vanish for $n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$ is the zero function (up to equalit
y almost everywhere).
