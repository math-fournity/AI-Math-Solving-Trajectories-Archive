## Proof

Let $d_n$ denote the number of decimal digits of $A_n$. Since $A_1=0$ and $A_2=1
$ each have one digit, and concatenation gives $d_n = d_{n-1}+d_{n-2}$, we have
$d_n = F_n$ (Fibonacci numbers with $F_1=F_2=1$).

The concatenation rule gives the recurrence
$$A_n = A_{n-1}\cdot 10^{d_{n-2}} + A_{n-2} = A_{n-1}\cdot 10^{F_{n-2}} + A_{n-2
}.$$

**Reducing modulo 11.** Since $10\equiv -1\pmod{11}$, we get $10^{F_{n-2}}\equiv
 (-1)^{F_{n-2}}\pmod{11}$. Setting $r_n = A_n \bmod 11$:
$$r_n \equiv (-1)^{F_{n-2}}\, r_{n-1} + r_{n-2} \pmod{11}.$$

**Parity of Fibonacci numbers.** The sequence $F_n \bmod 2$ is $1,1,0,1,1,0,\ldo
ts$ with period 3, so $F_k$ is even iff $k\equiv 0\pmod{3}$. Thus $(-1)^{F_{n-2}
} = +1$ when $n\equiv 2\pmod{3}$, and $=-1$ otherwise. The recurrence becomes:
$$r_n \equiv \begin{cases} r_{n-1}+r_{n-2} & \text{if } n\equiv 2\pmod{3},\\ -r_
{n-1}+r_{n-2} & \text{if } n\equiv 0,1\pmod{3}. \end{cases} \pmod{11}$$

**Computing the sequence.** With $r_1=0$, $r_2=1$:

| $n$ | $n\bmod 3$ | $r_n$ |
|-----|-----------|-------|
| 1 | 1 | 0 |
| 2 | 2 | 1 |
| 3 | 0 | $-1+0 = 10$ |
| 4 | 1 | $-10+1 = 2$ |
| 5 | 2 | $2+10 = 1$ |
| 6 | 0 | $-1+2 = 1$ |
| 7 | 1 | $-1+1 = 0$ |
| 8 | 2 | $0+1 = 1$ |

At $n=7$ we have $(r_7,r_8)=(0,1)=(r_1,r_2)$, and $7\equiv 1\pmod{3}$ matches $1
\equiv 1\pmod{3}$. Since the recurrence is fully determined by the pair $(r_{n-1
},r_n)$ together with $n\bmod 3$, the sequence is **periodic with period 6**:
$$0,\; 1,\; 10,\; 2,\; 1,\; 1,\; 0,\; 1,\; 10,\; 2,\; 1,\; 1,\;\ldots$$

**Conclusion.** The residue $r_n=0$ (i.e., $11\mid A_n$) occurs exactly when $n\
equiv 1\pmod{6}$.

$$\boxed{n\equiv 1\pmod{6}}$$

That is, $11$ divides $A_n$ if and only if $n\in\{1,7,13,19,25,\ldots\}$.
