## Proof

**Claim:** $B \otimes_A K$ has only finitely many prime ideals.

**Step 1: $B \otimes_A K$ is a finite-dimensional $K$-algebra.**

The field $K = \operatorname{Frac}(A/\mathfrak p)$ is an $A$-algebra via the com
posite $A \twoheadrightarrow A/\mathfrak p \hookrightarrow K$. Hence $B \otimes_
A K$ is naturally a $K$-algebra (hence a ring).

Since $B$ is finitely generated as an $A$-module, choose $A$-module generators $
b_1, \dots, b_n$. Every element of $B \otimes_A K$ is a finite $K$-linear combin
ation of elements $b \otimes k$; writing $b = \sum a_i b_i$ and using $b_i \otim
es k = k(b_i \otimes 1)$, we see that $b_1 \otimes 1, \dots, b_n \otimes 1$ gene
rate $B \otimes_A K$ as a $K$-vector space. Thus
$$\dim_K(B \otimes_A K) \le n < \infty.$$

**Step 2: A finite-dimensional algebra over a field is Artinian.**

Every left/right/two-sided ideal of $B \otimes_A K$ is in particular a $K$-subsp
ace. A strictly descending chain of ideals is a strictly descending chain of $K$
-subspaces, whose dimensions form a strictly decreasing sequence of non-negative
 integers bounded above by $n$. Such a sequence terminates. Hence $B \otimes_A K
$ is an Artinian ring.

**Step 3: An Artinian ring has only finitely many prime ideals.**

Recall two standard facts about Artinian rings:

- *Every prime ideal is maximal.* Indeed, if $\mathfrak q \subseteq R$ is prime,
 then $R/\mathfrak q$ is an Artinian domain. An Artinian domain is a field (for
any nonzero $x$, the descending chain $(x) \supseteq (x^2) \supseteq \cdots$ sta
bilizes, giving $x^n = x^{n+1}u$ for some $u$, and since $x \neq 0$ in a domain,
 $1 = xu$). Hence $\mathfrak q$ is maximal.

- *An Artinian ring is a finite direct product of Artinian local rings.* This is
 the structure theorem for Artinian rings: $R \cong \prod_{i=1}^{m} R_{\mathfrak
 m_i}$ where $\mathfrak m_1, \dots, \mathfrak m_m$ are the (finitely many) maxim
al ideals, each localization $R_{\mathfrak m_i}$ is local Artinian, and the prod
uct decomposition is indexed by the maximal ideals. In particular, the set of ma
ximal ideals is finite.

Combining these: every prime ideal of $B \otimes_A K$ is maximal, and there are
only finitely many maximal ideals. Therefore $B \otimes_A K$ has only finitely m
any prime ideals.

(If $B \otimes_A K = 0$, it has no prime ideals at all, which is also finite — s
o the conclusion holds trivially in that case as well.)

**Conclusion:** Yes, $B \otimes_A K$ has only finitely many prime ideals. $\blac
ksquare$
