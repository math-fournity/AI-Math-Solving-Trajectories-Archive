## Solution

### 1. Simplify the boundary condition at $r_1$

The dynamic boundary condition is
$$\pi r_1^2\, c^*\rho^*\, \frac{dU}{dt} = 2\pi r_1\,\lambda\,\frac{\partial u}{\
partial r}\bigg|_{r=r_1}, \qquad U(t)=u(r_1,t).$$

Dividing by $2\pi r_1$:
$$\frac{c^*\rho^*\,r_1}{2}\,\frac{dU}{dt} = \lambda\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial
r}\bigg|_{r=r_1}. \tag{BC₁}$$

Since $a^2 = \lambda/(c\rho)$ (thermal diffusivity of the tube material), set
$$h \;=\; \frac{c^*\rho^*}{c\rho} \;=\; \frac{c^*\rho^*\,a^2}{\lambda},$$
the ratio of volumetric heat capacities (liquid to tube).

### 2. Separation of variables

Let $u(r,t) = R(r)\,T(t)$. The PDE gives
$$\frac{T'}{a^2 T} = \frac{R'' + \frac{1}{r}R'}{R} = -\mu^2,$$
so
$$T(t) = e^{-a^2\mu^2 t}, \qquad R'' + \frac{1}{r}R' + \mu^2 R = 0 \quad\text{(B
essel equation of order 0)}.$$

### 3. Eigenfunctions from the boundary condition at $r_2$

The general solution is $R(r) = A\,J_0(\mu r) + B\,Y_0(\mu r)$. The condition $R
'(r_2)=0$ gives
$$A\,J_1(\mu r_2) + B\,Y_1(\mu r_2) = 0 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; B = -A\,\frac{J_
1(\mu r_2)}{Y_1(\mu r_2)}.$$

Define the eigenfunction (up to a multiplicative constant):
$$\boxed{R_\mu(r) = J_0(\mu r)\,Y_1(\mu r_2) - J_1(\mu r_2)\,Y_0(\mu r).}$$

Its derivative is $R_\mu'(r) = \mu\bigl[J_1(\mu r_2)\,Y_1(\mu r) - J_1(\mu r)\,Y
_1(\mu r_2)\bigr]$.

### 4. Eigenvalue equation from the dynamic condition at $r_1$

For the separated solution, $U(t) = R(r_1)\,e^{-a^2\mu^2 t}$, so $dU/dt = -a^2\m
u^2 R(r_1)\,e^{-a^2\mu^2 t}$, and $u_r|_{r_1} = R'(r_1)\,e^{-a^2\mu^2 t}$. Subst
ituting into (BC₁):

$$\frac{c^*\rho^*\,r_1}{2}\bigl(-a^2\mu^2\bigr)R(r_1) = \lambda\,R'(r_1),$$

$$\boxed{R'(r_1) + \frac{h\,r_1\,\mu^2}{2}\,R(r_1) = 0.} \tag{Eigenvalue equatio
n}$$

Explicitly, the positive eigenvalues $\mu_n$ ($n=1,2,3,\dots$) are the roots of:

$$\mu\Bigl[J_1(\mu r_2)\,Y_1(\mu r_1) - J_1(\mu r_1)\,Y_1(\mu r_2)\Bigr] + \frac
{h\,r_1\,\mu^2}{2}\Bigl[J_0(\mu r_1)\,Y_1(\mu r_2) - J_1(\mu r_2)\,Y_0(\mu r_1)\
Bigr] = 0.$$

**Zero eigenvalue.** For $\mu=0$: the ODE reduces to $(rR')'=0$, giving $R = A\l
n r + B$. The condition $R'(r_2)=0$ forces $A=0$, so $R_0 = \text{const}$, and t
he $r_1$ condition $0 + 0 = 0$ is automatically satisfied. Thus $\mu_0 = 0$ with
 $R_0(r) = 1$.

### 5. Orthogonality (modified inner product)

The eigenvalue $\mu^2$ appears in the boundary condition at $r_1$, making this a
 **non-standard (quadratic) eigenvalue problem**. For two distinct eigenfunction
s $R_n, R_m$ ($\mu_n \neq \mu_m$), the Lagrange identity for $(rR')' + \mu^2 r R
 = 0$ gives:

$$(\mu_m^2 - \mu_n^2)\int_{r_1}^{r_2} r\,R_n R_m\,dr = \Bigl[r\bigl(R_m R_n' - R
_n R_m'\bigr)\Bigr]_{r_1}^{r_2}.$$

At $r_2$: both $R_n'(r_2)=R_m'(r_2)=0$, so the contribution vanishes. At $r_1$,
using $R_k'(r_1) = -\frac{h r_1 \mu_k^2}{2}R_k(r_1)$:

$$R_m(r_1)R_n'(r_1) - R_n(r_1)R_m'(r_1) = \frac{h\,r_1}{2}(\mu_m^2-\mu_n^2)\,R_n
(r_1)\,R_m(r_1).$$

Assembling:
$$(\mu_m^2-\mu_n^2)\left[\int_{r_1}^{r_2} r\,R_n R_m\,dr + \frac{h\,r_1^2}{2}\,R
_n(r_1)\,R_m(r_1)\right] = 0.$$

Hence the eigenfunctions are orthogonal with respect to the **energy inner produ
ct** (which includes the lumped thermal mass of the liquid):

$$\boxed{\langle R_n, R_m\rangle = \int_{r_1}^{r_2} r\,R_n(r)\,R_m(r)\,dr + \fra
c{h\,r_1^2}{2}\,R_n(r_1)\,R_m(r_1) = N_n\,\delta_{nm},}$$

where the norm $N_n > 0$ (being a sum of non-negative terms with $h>0$).

### 6. Expansion coefficients from the initial condition

The initial condition $u(r,0) = f(r)$ and $U(0) = f(r_1)$ gives
$$f(r) = C_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} C_n\,R_{\mu_n}(r), \qquad r\in[r_1,r_2].$$

Taking the modified inner product with $R_{\mu_m}$ and using orthogonality:

$$\boxed{C_n = \frac{1}{N_n}\left[\int_{r_1}^{r_2} r\,f(r)\,R_{\mu_n}(r)\,dr + \
frac{h\,r_1^2}{2}\,f(r_1)\,R_{\mu_n}(r_1)\right], \qquad n = 0,1,2,\dots}$$

with $R_{\mu_0}(r)=1$ and
$$N_0 = \frac{r_2^2 - r_1^2 + h\,r_1^2}{2}.$$

For $n \geq 1$, the norm can be evaluated via the Bessel identity $\frac{d}{dr}\
!\left[\frac{r^2}{2}\!\left(R^2 + R'^2/\mu^2\right)\right] = rR^2$:

$$N_n = \frac{r_2^2}{2}\,R_{\mu_n}^2(r_2) + \frac{r_1^2(h-1)}{2}\,R_{\mu_n}^2(r_
1) - \frac{h^2 r_1^4 \mu_n^2}{8}\,R_{\mu_n}^2(r_1).$$

### 7. Complete solution

$$\boxed{u(r,t) = C_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} C_n\,R_{\mu_n}(r)\,e^{-a^2 \mu_n^2 t
},}$$

where:
- $R_{\mu_n}(r) = J_0(\mu_n r)\,Y_1(\mu_n r_2) - J_1(\mu_n r_2)\,Y_0(\mu_n r)$,
- $\mu_0 = 0$, $R_0 = 1$; $\mu_n > 0$ ($n\geq 1$) are roots of the eigenvalue eq
uation in §4,
- $h = c^*\rho^* a^2/\lambda$,
- $C_n$ and $N_n$ are given above.

**Physical interpretation.** The $C_0$ term is the steady-state temperature reac
hed as $t\to\infty$:
$$u_{\text{ss}} = C_0 = \frac{2\displaystyle\int_{r_1}^{r_2} r\,f(r)\,dr + h\,r_
1^2\,f(r_1)}{r_2^2 - r_1^2 + h\,r_1^2},$$
which is the total initial thermal energy (tube + liquid) divided by the total t
hermal mass — a weighted average consistent with energy conservation ($dE/dt = 0
$ since the outer wall is insulated). Each subsequent mode decays exponentially
with rate $a^2\mu_n^2$.
