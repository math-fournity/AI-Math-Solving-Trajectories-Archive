# Problem

In a futuristic circular city with a radius of exactly 1 kilometer, a civil engineering firm is tasked with installing $k$ power substations, labeled $S_1, S_2, \dots, S_k$. To ensure perfect symmetry and load balancing, these substations are placed at the vertices of a regular $k$-gon inscribed along the city’s circular perimeter.

The city's power grid suffers from "proximity resistance" between every pair of substations. For any two distinct substations $S_i$ and $S_j$, the resistance they generate is defined as the inverse square of the straight-line distance between them, expressed as $(S_iS_j)^{-2}$.

To calculate the total thermal load on the grid, the chief engineer needs to find the sum of these resistances across all possible ordered pairs of distinct substations.

Calculate the value of $\displaystyle \sum_{i \neq j}^{k} \frac{1}{(S_iS_j)^2}$.
