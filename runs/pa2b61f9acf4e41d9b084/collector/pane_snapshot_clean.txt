Looking at this problem, I need to count valid sequences from (0,0,0) to (n,n,n)
 in 3n steps under the given constraints.

## Analysis of the Constraints

The four conditions simplify. From conditions 1 and 2: $y+1 \geq x$ and $x \geq
y$ together force $x \in \{y, y+1\}$ at every state. Combined with conditions 3
and 4: $y \geq z \geq 0$.

**Key observation on X-Y alternation.** Let $a, b, c$ be the running counts of X
, Y, Z steps. Since $a - b \in \{0, 1\}$:
- Adding X changes $a-b$ by $+1$: valid only when $a = b$ (so $a-b = 0 \to 1$).
- Adding Y changes $a-b$ by $-1$: valid only when $a = b+1$ (so $a-b = 1 \to 0$)
.
- Adding Z leaves $a-b$ unchanged.

Starting at $a=b=0$ ($a-b=0$), the **X-Y subsequence is forced to alternate: X,
Y, X, Y, ..., X, Y** (n pairs). No other ordering of X and Y steps is possible.

## Reduction to a Ballot Problem

The Z steps can be inserted into any of the $2n+1$ gaps of the fixed X-Y alterna
ting sequence. The only remaining constraint is $y \geq z$, i.e., $b \geq c$ at
all times.

Label the gaps $0, 1, \ldots, 2n$. Gap $g$ has $b(g) = \lfloor g/2 \rfloor$ (the
 number of Y's placed before that gap). Placing the $i$-th Z requires $b \geq i$
 at that moment (since after placement $c = i$, needing $b \geq i$). So the $i$-
th Z must go in a gap $g_i \geq 2i$.

Since Z's appear in order: $g_1 \leq g_2 \leq \cdots \leq g_n$, with $2i \leq g_
i \leq 2n$.

**Bijection to lattice paths.** A non-decreasing sequence $g_1 \leq \cdots \leq
g_n$ with $g_i \geq 2i$ corresponds to a lattice path from $(0,0)$ to $(2n, n)$
using $2n$ right-steps (R) and $n$ up-steps (U), where $g_i$ is the x-coordinate
 of the $i$-th U-step. The constraint $g_i \geq 2i$ means: before the $i$-th U-s
tep, at least $2i$ R-steps have occurred, equivalently $2 \cdot (\text{#U so far
}) \leq \text{#R so far}$ at every point. This is the condition that the path ne
ver crosses above the line $y = x/2$.

## Counting via Raney's Lemma (Cycle Lemma)

Assign weight $+1$ to each R and $-2$ to each U. For paths of $2n$ R's and $n$ U
's (total weight $0$) with all partial sums $\geq 0$:

**Prepend one extra R** to get $2n+1$ R's and $n$ U's, total weight $= 1 > 0$. B
y Raney's lemma, for any fixed sequence with positive sum $s$, exactly $s$ of it
s $m$ cyclic rotations have all partial sums strictly positive. Since the sum is
 $1$ (coprime to length $3n+1$, so no sequence has nontrivial period), each rota
tion class has exactly $3n+1$ elements. Thus the count of sequences with all par
tial sums $> 0$ is:

$$\frac{1}{3n+1}\binom{3n+1}{n}$$

Removing the forced leading R (which shifts all partial sums down by 1, converti
ng $> 0$ to $\geq 0$) gives a bijection to our desired paths. Simplifying:

$$P(n) = \frac{1}{3n+1}\binom{3n+1}{n} = \frac{(3n)!}{n!\,(2n+1)!} = \frac{1}{2n
+1}\binom{3n}{n}$$

These are the **Fuss–Catalan numbers** (3-ary Catalan numbers).

## Computation

$$P(1) = \frac{1}{3}\binom{3}{1} = \frac{3}{3} = 1$$

$$P(2) = \frac{1}{5}\binom{6}{2} = \frac{15}{5} = 3$$

$$P(3) = \frac{1}{7}\binom{9}{3} = \frac{84}{7} = 12$$

$$P(4) = \frac{1}{9}\binom{12}{4} = \frac{495}{9} = 55$$
