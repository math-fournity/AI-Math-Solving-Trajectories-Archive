## Proof

Recall the definition of the boundary of an oriented $n$-simplex. For
$$[v_0\,v_1\,\dots\,v_n],$$
the boundary operator $\partial$ acts by
$$\partial[v_0\,v_1\,\dots\,v_n] \;=\; \sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\,[v_0\,\dots\,\wid
ehat{v_i}\,\dots\,v_n],$$
where $\widehat{v_i}$ means the vertex $v_i$ is deleted. The boundary operator i
s linear on chains, so for any chain $c$ and scalar $a$,
$$\partial(a\,c)=a\,\partial c.$$

---

### Boundary of $\tau=[v_0\,v_1\,v_2]$

Applying the definition with $n=2$:

$$\partial\tau \;=\; (-1)^0[v_1\,v_2]+(-1)^1[v_0\,v_2]+(-1)^2[v_0\,v_1]$$

$$\boxed{\;\partial\tau \;=\; [v_1\,v_2]-[v_0\,v_2]+[v_0\,v_1]\;}$$

---

### Boundary of $\sigma=[w_0\,w_1\,w_2\,w_3]$

Applying the definition with $n=3$:

$$\partial\sigma \;=\; (-1)^0[w_1\,w_2\,w_3]+(-1)^1[w_0\,w_2\,w_3]+(-1)^2[w_0\,w
_1\,w_3]+(-1)^3[w_0\,w_1\,w_2]$$

$$\boxed{\;\partial\sigma \;=\; [w_1\,w_2\,w_3]-[w_0\,w_2\,w_3]+[w_0\,w_1\,w_3]-
[w_0\,w_1\,w_2]\;}$$

---

### Boundary of $-\tau$

Since $-\tau=-[v_0\,v_1\,v_2]$ is the same simplex with the opposite orientation
, and $\partial$ is linear:

$$\partial(-\tau) \;=\; -\,\partial\tau \;=\; -\bigl([v_1\,v_2]-[v_0\,v_2]+[v_0\
,v_1]\bigr)$$

$$\boxed{\;\partial(-\tau) \;=\; -[v_1\,v_2]+[v_0\,v_2]-[v_0\,v_1]\;}$$

Equivalently, applying the definition directly to $-\tau$ (which reverses orient
ation, i.e. swaps two vertices, say $v_0\leftrightarrow v_1$, giving $-[v_0\,v_1
\,v_2]=[v_1\,v_0\,v_2]$):

$$\partial[v_1\,v_0\,v_2]=[v_0\,v_2]-[v_1\,v_2]+[v_1\,v_0].$$

Using $[v_1\,v_0]=-[v_0\,v_1]$, this becomes $[v_0\,v_2]-[v_1\,v_2]-[v_0\,v_1]$,
 which matches $-\partial\tau$. ✓

---

### Boundary of $-\sigma$

By linearity:

$$\partial(-\sigma) \;=\; -\,\partial\sigma \;=\; -\bigl([w_1\,w_2\,w_3]-[w_0\,w
_2\,w_3]+[w_0\,w_1\,w_3]-[w_0\,w_1\,w_2]\bigr)$$

$$\boxed{\;\partial(-\sigma) \;=\; -[w_1\,w_2\,w_3]+[w_0\,w_2\,w_3]-[w_0\,w_1\,w
_3]+[w_0\,w_1\,w_2]\;}$$

One may verify directly as well: $-\sigma=[w_1\,w_0\,w_2\,w_3]$ (swap $w_0,w_1$)
, and
$$\partial[w_1\,w_0\,w_2\,w_3]=[w_0\,w_2\,w_3]-[w_1\,w_2\,w_3]+[w_1\,w_0\,w_3]-[
w_1\,w_0\,w_2].$$
Using $[w_1\,w_0\,w_3]=-[w_0\,w_1\,w_3]$ and $[w_1\,w_0\,w_2]=-[w_0\,w_1\,w_2]$,
 this gives
$$[w_0\,w_2\,w_3]-[w_1\,w_2\,w_3]-[w_0\,w_1\,w_3]+[w_0\,w_1\,w_2],$$
matching $-\partial\sigma$. ✓

---

### Relationships

**Between $\partial\tau$ and $\partial(-\tau)$:**

$$\boxed{\;\partial(-\tau)=-\,\partial\tau\;}$$

The boundary of $-\tau$ is the negative (the orientation-reversed chain) of the
boundary of $\tau$. Every face of $\partial(-\tau)$ is the same geometric simple
x as the corresponding face of $\partial\tau$, but with the opposite orientation
.

**Between $\partial\sigma$ and $\partial(-\sigma)$:**

$$\boxed{\;\partial(-\sigma)=-\,\partial\sigma\;}$$

Likewise, the boundary of $-\sigma$ is the negative of the boundary of $\sigma$.

**General principle.** Both relationships are instances of the linearity of the
boundary homomorphism
$$\partial:C_n\to C_{n-1}.$$
For any $n$-chain $c$ and integer $a$,
$$\partial(a\,c)=a\,\partial c.$$
Taking $a=-1$ and $c=\tau$ (resp. $c=\sigma$) yields the two relations above. Ge
ometrically, reversing the orientation of an oriented simplex reverses the induc
ed orientation on every one of its boundary faces, so the entire oriented bounda
ry chain changes sign.
