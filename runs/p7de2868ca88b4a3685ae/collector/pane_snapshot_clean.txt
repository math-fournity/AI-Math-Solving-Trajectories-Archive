The statement is **TRUE** (under the natural convention that β ≠ 0, since the ra
tio is undefined at β = 0).

**Proof.**

Let $K/\mathbb{Q}$ have degree $n$, and let $\sigma_1 = \mathrm{id}, \sigma_2, \
dots, \sigma_n : K \hookrightarrow \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ be the $n$ embeddings.
 For $\beta \in \mathcal{O}_K$, $\beta \neq 0$, the norm is

$$N(\beta) = N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\beta) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} \sigma_i(\beta) = \beta \
cdot \prod_{i=2}^{n} \sigma_i(\beta),$$

so

$$\frac{N(\beta)}{\beta} = \prod_{i=2}^{n} \sigma_i(\beta).$$

I claim this product lies in $\mathcal{O}_K$. Three facts establish this:

**Step 1: $N(\beta)/\beta \in K$.** Since $\beta$ is an algebraic integer, $N(\b
eta) = \prod_i \sigma_i(\beta)$ is a symmetric function of the conjugates of $\b
eta$, hence equals (up to sign) the constant term of the minimal polynomial of $
\beta$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, so $N(\beta) \in \mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{Q}$. Thus
$N(\beta)/\beta = N(\beta) \cdot \beta^{-1}$, with $N(\beta) \in \mathbb{Q} \sub
set K$ and $\beta^{-1} \in K$ (since $K$ is a field), gives $N(\beta)/\beta \in
K$.

**Step 2: $N(\beta)/\beta$ is an algebraic integer.** Each conjugate $\sigma_i(\
beta)$ is a root of the same monic polynomial $m_\beta(x) \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ sat
isfied by $\beta$, hence each $\sigma_i(\beta)$ is an algebraic integer. The set
 of all algebraic integers (in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$) forms a ring, so the pro
duct $\prod_{i=2}^{n} \sigma_i(\beta)$ is an algebraic integer.

**Step 3: Conclusion.** $\mathcal{O}_K$ is precisely the set of algebraic intege
rs that lie in $K$. By Steps 1 and 2, $N(\beta)/\beta$ is both an algebraic inte
ger and an element of $K$, so

$$\frac{N(\beta)}{\beta} \in \mathcal{O}_K.$$

**Remark on $\beta = 0$.** The ratio $N(0)/0$ is undefined, so the statement is
understood to range over $\beta \neq 0$; under this standard convention it holds
 for every $\beta \in \mathcal{O}_K$.

**Concrete check.** For $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ (degree 2), $N(\beta)/\beta =
 \bar{\beta}$ (the conjugate), which is an algebraic integer in $K$ since conjug
ation preserves $\mathcal{O}_K$. For $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})$, $\beta = \sq
rt[3]{2}$: $N(\beta) = 2$, and $N(\beta)/\beta = 2/\sqrt[3]{2} = \sqrt[3]{4} = (
\sqrt[3]{2})^2 \in \mathcal{O}_K$. Both confirm the general argument.
