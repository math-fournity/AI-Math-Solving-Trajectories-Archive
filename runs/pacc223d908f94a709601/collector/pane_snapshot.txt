Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether integrability of a function
 on a sub-σ-algebra $\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ of a σ-finite measure sp
ace $(X, \mathcal{F}, \mu)$ implies integrability on the original σ-algebra $\ma
thcal{F}$.

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $(X, \mathcal{F}, \mu)$ be a σ-finite measure space, and let $\ma
thcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ be a sub-σ-algebra. Denote by $\mu|_{\mathcal{G}
}$ the restriction of $\mu$ to $\mathcal{G}$. Suppose $f: X \to \overline{\mathb
b{R}}$ is integrable on $(X, \mathcal{G}, \mu|_{\mathcal{G}})$. We show $f$ is i
ntegrable on $(X, \mathcal{F}, \mu)$.

**Integrability on $\mathcal{G}$ gives two things:**
1. $f$ is $\mathcal{G}$-measurable, and
2. $\int_X |f|\,d\mu|_{\mathcal{G}} < \infty$.

**Step 1: $\mathcal{G}$-measurability implies $\mathcal{F}$-measurability.**

Since $\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$, every $\mathcal{G}$-measurable functi
on is automatically $\mathcal{F}$-measurable. Indeed, for any Borel set $B \subs
eteq \mathbb{R}$, we have $f^{-1}(B) \in \mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$, so
$f^{-1}(B) \in \mathcal{F}$.

**Step 2: The integrals coincide.**

We show that for any non-negative $\mathcal{G}$-measurable function $g: X \to [0
, \infty]$,
$$\int_X g\,d\mu|_{\mathcal{G}} = \int_X g\,d\mu.$$

- **Simple functions:** Let $s = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i \mathbf{1}_{A_i}$ be a $\mat
hcal{G}$-measurable simple function, so each $A_i \in \mathcal{G}$. Then
$$\int_X s\,d\mu|_{\mathcal{G}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i\, \mu|_{\mathcal{G}}(A_i) =
 \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i\, \mu(A_i) = \int_X s\,d\mu,$$
since $\mu|_{\mathcal{G}}(A_i) = \mu(A_i)$ for $A_i \in \mathcal{G}$.

- **General non-negative functions:** Let $g \geq 0$ be $\mathcal{G}$-measurable
. Choose a sequence of $\mathcal{G}$-measurable simple functions $s_n \uparrow g
$. By the Monotone Convergence Theorem applied in both measure spaces:
$$\int_X g\,d\mu|_{\mathcal{G}} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \int_X s_n\,d\mu|_{\mathca
l{G}} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \int_X s_n\,d\mu = \int_X g\,d\mu.$$

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

Applying Step 2 to $g = |f|$ (which is $\mathcal{G}$-measurable since $f$ is):
$$\int_X |f|\,d\mu = \int_X |f|\,d\mu|_{\mathcal{G}} < \infty.$$

Together with Step 1 ($f$ is $\mathcal{F}$-measurable), this shows $f$ is integr
able on $(X, \mathcal{F}, \mu)$.

**Remark on σ-finiteness.** The σ-finiteness hypothesis is not needed for this r
esult — the implication holds for arbitrary measure spaces. The key fact is pure
ly that $\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ ensures both measurability transfer
and equality of integrals for $\mathcal{G}$-measurable functions, since the inte
gral of a $\mathcal{G}$-measurable function depends only on the values of the me
asure on $\mathcal{G}$-measurable sets.

**The statement is TRUE.**
