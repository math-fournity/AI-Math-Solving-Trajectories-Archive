## Proof

**Setup.** Let $A, B, C$ be the three stations. Place $A$ at the origin with the
 angle bisector $AL$ along the positive $x$-axis. Let $\angle BAC = 2\alpha$, so
 the bisector splits it into two angles of $\alpha$. Set $AB = u$, $AC = v$, and
 write
$$B = (u\cos\alpha,\; u\sin\alpha), \qquad C = (v\cos\alpha,\; -v\sin\alpha).$$

**Key points.** Since $L$ lies on the bisector at distance $t$ from $A$:
$$L = (t, 0).$$
The midpoint of $BC$ is
$$M = \left(\tfrac{(u+v)\cos\alpha}{2},\; \tfrac{(u-v)\sin\alpha}{2}\right).$$
The condition "$L$ is the midpoint of $M$ and $H$" gives $H = 2L - M$:
$$H = \left(2t - \tfrac{(u+v)\cos\alpha}{2},\; -\tfrac{(u-v)\sin\alpha}{2}\right
).$$

**Perpendicularity $AH \perp BC$.** The direction of $BC$ is $C - B = (-(u{-}v)\
cos\alpha,\; -(u{+}v)\sin\alpha)$. Setting $S = u+v$, $D = u-v$, the orthogonali
ty condition $H \cdot (C-B) = 0$ yields (assuming $D \neq 0$):
$$-D\cos\alpha\!\left(2t - \tfrac{S\cos\alpha}{2}\right) + \tfrac{DS\sin^2\alpha
}{2} = 0$$
$$\Longrightarrow\quad -2t\cos\alpha + \tfrac{S}{2}\left(\cos^2\alpha + \sin^2\a
lpha\right) = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{S = u+v = 4t\cos\alpha}.$$

**Angle-bisector length.** The standard formula $AL = \frac{2uv\cos\alpha}{u+v}$
 with $AL = t$ and $u+v = 4t\cos\alpha$ gives
$$t = \frac{2uv\cos\alpha}{4t\cos\alpha} = \frac{uv}{2t} \quad\Longrightarrow\qu
ad \boxed{uv = 2t^2}.$$

**Using $AH = h$.** We have $D^2 = (u-v)^2 = S^2 - 4uv = 16t^2\cos^2\alpha - 8t^
2 = 8t^2\cos 2\alpha$. Then
$$h^2 = |H|^2 = 4t^2\sin^4\alpha + \tfrac{D^2\sin^2\alpha}{4} = 4t^2\sin^4\alpha
 + 2t^2\cos 2\alpha\,\sin^2\alpha.$$
Factoring: $h^2 = 2t^2\sin^2\alpha\,(2\sin^2\alpha + \cos 2\alpha) = 2t^2\sin^2\
alpha\,(\sin^2\alpha + \cos^2\alpha)$, so
$$\boxed{h^2 = 2t^2\sin^2\alpha}.$$
(This is consistent: $\sin^2\alpha = h^2/(2t^2) \le 1$ requires $h \le t\sqrt{2}
$, and $\cos 2\alpha = (t^2-h^2)/t^2 \ge 0$ requires $t \ge h$ — both expected s
ince the altitude is the shortest distance from $A$ to line $BC$.)

**Circumradius.** With $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}\cdot BC \cdot h$ and $R = \fra
c{BC \cdot u \cdot v}{4\,\text{Area}}$:
$$R = \frac{BC \cdot uv}{4 \cdot \frac{1}{2} BC \cdot h} = \frac{uv}{2h} = \frac
{2t^2}{2h} = \boxed{\dfrac{t^2}{h}}.$$

**Verification.** Take $\alpha = 30°$: then $\sin^2\alpha = 1/4$, giving $h = t/
\sqrt{2}$; $u+v = 2\sqrt{3}\,t$, $uv = 2t^2$, $D = 2t$, so $u = t(\sqrt{3}+1)$,
$v = t(\sqrt{3}-1)$. Direct computation yields $BC = t\sqrt{6}$, $\text{Area} =
t^2\sqrt{3}/2$, and $R = t\sqrt{2} = t^2/h$. ✓ All conditions (midpoint, perpend
icularity, lengths) check out.

The radius of the circumcircle is

$$\boxed{R = \dfrac{t^2}{h}}.$$
