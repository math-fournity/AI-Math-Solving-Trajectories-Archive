## Proof

**Setup and interpretation.** Let $M, N \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ be smooth simple c
urves (smooth $1$-dimensional submanifolds, possibly with boundary, or simple cl
osed curves) of the same finite length $L := L[M] = L[N] < \infty$. A "parametri
zed curve $\gamma$ in $M$" means a piecewise-$C^1$ map $\gamma:[a,b]\to \mathbb{
R}^n$ with $\gamma([a,b])\subseteq M$, and $L[\gamma]$ is its Euclidean arc leng
th. Since $\gamma$ lies in $M$, this equals the arc length traversed along $M$.

**Answer: Yes, such an $F$ exists (provided $M$ and $N$ are of the same topologi
cal type — both simple arcs, or both simple closed curves — which is in any case
 necessary for any bijection to exist). Moreover, every such $F$ is an arc-lengt
h isometry, and is unique up to the obvious reflection/rotation.**

---

### 1. Arc-length coordinates

Since $M$ is a smooth simple curve, it admits a smooth arc-length parametrizatio
n
$$\alpha:[0,L]\to M \quad(\text{or } \alpha:S^1_L:=\mathbb{R}/L\mathbb{Z}\to M \
text{ if closed}),$$
with $|\alpha'(s)|\equiv 1$ and $\alpha$ a smooth diffeomorphism onto $M$. Likew
ise let
$$\beta:[0,L]\to N$$
be a smooth arc-length parametrization of $N$, with $|\beta'(s)|\equiv 1$.

Define the arc-length coordinate $s_M:=\alpha^{-1}:M\to[0,L]$ and $s_N:=\beta^{-
1}:N\to[0,L]$, both smooth.

### 2. Construction of $F$

Set
$$F:=\beta\circ \alpha^{-1}:M\longrightarrow N.$$
This is a composition of smooth diffeomorphisms, hence a smooth bijection (diffe
omorphism) $M\to N$.

### 3. $F$ preserves all curve lengths

Let $\gamma:[a,b]\to M$ be any piecewise-$C^1$ curve in $M$. Write $\gamma=\alph
a\circ h$ where $h=s_M\circ\gamma:[a,b]\to[0,L]$ is piecewise $C^1$. Then
$$L[\gamma]=\int_a^b|\alpha'(h(t))\,h'(t)|\,dt=\int_a^b|h'(t)|\,dt.$$
On the other hand,
$$F\circ\gamma=\beta\circ\alpha^{-1}\circ\alpha\circ h=\beta\circ h,$$
so
$$L[F\circ\gamma]=\int_a^b|\beta'(h(t))\,h'(t)|\,dt=\int_a^b|h'(t)|\,dt=L[\gamma
].$$
Thus $F$ satisfies the required condition. $\checkmark$

### 4. Necessity: every such $F$ is an arc-length isometry

Conversely, suppose $F:M\to N$ is any smooth bijection with $L[\gamma]=L[F\circ\
gamma]$ for every $\gamma$ in $M$. Define
$$g:=s_N\circ F\circ s_M^{-1}:[0,L]\to[0,L],$$
a smooth bijection (hence strictly monotone). For any piecewise-$C^1$ $h:[a,b]\t
o[0,L]$, the curve $\gamma:=\alpha\circ h$ lies in $M$, and the hypothesis gives
$$\int_a^b|h'(t)|\,dt=L[\gamma]=L[F\circ\gamma]=L[\beta\circ g\circ h]=\int_a^b|
g'(h(t))\,h'(t)|\,dt.$$
Choosing $h$ supported near any point $u\in(0,L)$ with $h'>0$ on a small interva
l forces $|g'(u)|=1$. Since $g$ is smooth and monotone, $g'\equiv +1$ or $g'\equ
iv -1$ on all of $[0,L]$. Hence
$$g(s)=s \quad\text{or}\quad g(s)=L-s$$
(for closed curves, $g(s)=s+c\pmod L$, a rotation). Therefore
$$F=\beta\circ g\circ\alpha^{-1},$$
which is exactly an arc-length isometry. In particular $F$ is determined up to t
he reflection $s\mapsto L-s$ (open case) or a rotation (closed case), and the co
nstruction in step 2 (with $g=\mathrm{id}$) always realizes one.

### 5. Topological remark

If $M$ is a simple arc ($\cong[0,1]$) and $N$ a simple closed curve ($\cong S^1$
), or vice versa, then $M$ and $N$ are not even homeomorphic, so no bijection —
let alone a smooth one — can exist. Under the standing hypothesis that $M,N$ are
 of the same topological type (the only case in which the question is non-vacuou
s), the arc-length isometry constructed above always exists.

---

**Conclusion.** Yes. Between two smooth simple curves $M,N\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ o
f equal finite length and the same topological type, there always exists a smoot
h bijection $F:M\to N$ preserving the length of every parametrized curve; explic
itly $F=\beta\circ\alpha^{-1}$ for arc-length parametrizations $\alpha,\beta$. E
very map satisfying the condition is of this form (up to reflection/rotation), i
.e. an arc-length isometry.
