## Solution

**Setup.** Let $a = BC = 7$, $b = CA = 8$, $c = AB = 9$. By the Law of Cosines:

$$\cos A = \frac{b^2+c^2-a^2}{2bc} = \frac{96}{144} = \frac{2}{3}, \quad \sin A
= \frac{\sqrt{5}}{3}$$

$$\cos B = \frac{a^2+c^2-b^2}{2ac} = \frac{66}{126} = \frac{11}{21}, \quad \sin
B = \frac{8\sqrt{5}}{21}$$

$$\cos C = \frac{a^2+b^2-c^2}{2ab} = \frac{32}{112} = \frac{2}{7}, \quad \sin C
= \frac{3\sqrt{5}}{7}$$

The area of $\triangle ABC$ is $\Delta = \frac{1}{2}bc\sin A = 12\sqrt{5}$, and
the circumradius is:

$$R = \frac{a}{2\sin A} = \frac{21}{2\sqrt{5}} = \frac{21\sqrt{5}}{10}, \qquad R
^2 = \frac{441}{20}.$$

**Key Fact 1: All three circles have radius $R$.** Since $\angle BHC = 180° - A$
, the circumradius of $\triangle BCH$ is $\frac{BC}{2\sin(\angle BHC)} = \frac{a
}{2\sin A} = R$. By symmetry, $\Gamma_A, \Gamma_B, \Gamma_C$ all have radius $R$
.

**Key Fact 2: The centers form a triangle congruent to $ABC$.** The center of $\
Gamma_A$ (circumcircle of $BCH$) is the reflection $O_A$ of the circumcenter $O$
 over line $BC$. This follows because: (i) $|O_A B| = |OB| = R$ and $|O_A C| = |
OC| = R$ (reflection preserves distances to points on the mirror line), and (ii)
 the reflection of $H$ over $BC$ lies on the circumcircle of $ABC$ (a classical
result), so $|O_A H| = |O\,H^*| = R$ where $H^*$ is that reflection. Similarly,
$O_B$ and $O_C$ are reflections of $O$ over $CA$ and $AB$.

**Claim:** The distances between the centers equal the side lengths: $|O_B O_C|
= a = 7$, $|O_A O_C| = b = 8$, $|O_A O_B| = c = 9$.

*Verification via coordinates.* Place $B = (0,0)$, $C = (7,0)$. Then $A = \left(
\frac{33}{7}, \frac{24\sqrt{5}}{7}\right)$, the orthocenter is $H = \left(\frac{
33}{7}, \frac{22\sqrt{5}}{35}\right)$, and the circumcenter is $O = \left(\frac{
7}{2}, \frac{7\sqrt{5}}{5}\right)$. Reflecting $O$ over each side:

$$O_A = \left(\tfrac{7}{2},\, -\tfrac{7\sqrt{5}}{5}\right), \quad O_B = \left(\t
frac{115}{14},\, \tfrac{71\sqrt{5}}{35}\right), \quad O_C = \left(\tfrac{17}{14}
,\, \tfrac{71\sqrt{5}}{35}\right).$$

Computing pairwise distances confirms $|O_B O_C| = 7$, $|O_A O_C| = 8$, $|O_A O_
B| = 9$. $\checkmark$

**Key Fact 3: The triple intersection has area $0$.** Each pair of circles inter
sects at $H$ and one vertex:
- $\Gamma_A \cap \Gamma_B = \{C, H\}$, $\quad \Gamma_A \cap \Gamma_C = \{B, H\}$
, $\quad \Gamma_B \cap \Gamma_C = \{A, H\}$.

Since $\Gamma_A \cap \Gamma_B \cap \Gamma_C \subseteq \{C, H\} \cap \{B, H\} = \
{H\}$, and $H$ lies on all three circles, the triple intersection is exactly $\{
H\}$, which has area $0$.

*(Geometric reason: $H$ is the circumcenter of $\triangle O_A O_B O_C$ since thi
s triangle is congruent to $ABC$ and has circumradius $R$. As $ABC$ is acute, $H
$ lies inside $\triangle O_A O_B O_C$, so the three inward-pointing half-planes
at $H$ span $360°$ and their intersection is just the point $H$.)*

**Computing the union area.** By inclusion–exclusion with triple intersection $=
 0$:

$$\text{Area}(\Gamma_A \cup \Gamma_B \cup \Gamma_C) = 3\pi R^2 - \bigl[L(7) + L(
8) + L(9)\bigr],$$

where $L(d) = 2R^2 \arccos\!\left(\frac{d}{2R}\right) - \frac{d}{2}\sqrt{4R^2 -
d^2}$ is the lens area for two circles of radius $R$ at distance $d$.

**Evaluating each lens:**

For $d = 7$: $\;\frac{d}{2R} = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{3} = \sin A$, so $\arccos\!\left(
\frac{\sqrt{5}}{3}\right) = \frac{\pi}{2} - A$, and $4R^2 - 49 = \frac{196}{5}$,
 giving $\sqrt{4R^2 - 49} = \frac{14\sqrt{5}}{5}$.

$$L(7) = \frac{441}{10}\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - A\right) - \frac{49\sqrt{5}}{5}.$$

For $d = 8$: $\;\frac{d}{2R} = \frac{8\sqrt{5}}{21} = \sin B$, so $\arccos\!\lef
t(\frac{8\sqrt{5}}{21}\right) = \frac{\pi}{2} - B$, and $\sqrt{4R^2 - 64} = \fra
c{11\sqrt{5}}{5}$.

$$L(8) = \frac{441}{10}\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - B\right) - \frac{44\sqrt{5}}{5}.$$

For $d = 9$: $\;\frac{d}{2R} = \frac{3\sqrt{5}}{7} = \sin C$, so $\arccos\!\left
(\frac{3\sqrt{5}}{7}\right) = \frac{\pi}{2} - C$, and $\sqrt{4R^2 - 81} = \frac{
6\sqrt{5}}{5}$.

$$L(9) = \frac{441}{10}\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - C\right) - \frac{27\sqrt{5}}{5}.$$

**Summing the lenses:**

$$L(7)+L(8)+L(9) = \frac{441}{10}\left(\frac{3\pi}{2} - (A+B+C)\right) - \frac{(
49+44+27)\sqrt{5}}{5}.$$

Since $A + B + C = \pi$:

$$= \frac{441}{10}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{120\sqrt{5}}{5} = \frac{441\pi}{20}
 - 24\sqrt{5}.$$

**Final computation:**

$$\text{Area} = 3\pi \cdot \frac{441}{20} - \left(\frac{441\pi}{20} - 24\sqrt{5}
\right) = \frac{1323\pi}{20} - \frac{441\pi}{20} + 24\sqrt{5} = \frac{882\pi}{20
} + 24\sqrt{5}.$$
