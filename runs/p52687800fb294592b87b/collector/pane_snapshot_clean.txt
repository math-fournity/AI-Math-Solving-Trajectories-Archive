# Universal Covering Space of a Closed Surface of Genus 2

## Answer

The universal covering space of a closed orientable surface of genus 2 is the **
hyperbolic plane** $\mathbb{H}^2$ (equivalently, the open unit disk $\mathbb{D}
= \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| < 1\}$, or the upper half-plane $\{z \in \mathbb{C} :
 \operatorname{Im}(z) > 0\}$).

## Proof

Let $\Sigma_2$ denote a closed orientable surface of genus 2.

**Step 1: Topology of $\Sigma_2$.** A closed orientable surface of genus $g$ has
 Euler characteristic
$$\chi(\Sigma_g) = 2 - 2g.$$
For $g = 2$, we obtain $\chi(\Sigma_2) = 2 - 4 = -2 < 0$.

**Step 2: The universal cover is simply connected.** By definition, the universa
l covering space $\widetilde{\Sigma_2}$ is the unique (up to homeomorphism) simp
ly connected covering space of $\Sigma_2$. Since $\Sigma_2$ is a connected, loca
lly path-connected, and semilocally simply connected space (it is a manifold), a
 universal cover exists and is unique up to covering-space isomorphism.

**Step 3: Classification of simply connected 2-manifolds.** A simply connected 2
-manifold without boundary is homeomorphic to one of: the 2-sphere $S^2$, the Eu
clidean plane $\mathbb{R}^2$, or the open disk $\mathbb{D}$ (which is homeomorph
ic to $\mathbb{R}^2$ as well). In fact, every simply connected, non-compact 2-ma
nifold is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^2$, and the only simply connected compact
2-manifold is $S^2$.

So the candidates for $\widetilde{\Sigma_2}$ are $S^2$ or $\mathbb{R}^2$ (topolo
gically).

**Step 4: Ruling out $S^2$.** If $\widetilde{\Sigma_2} \cong S^2$, then $\Sigma_
2 \cong S^2/\Gamma$ where $\Gamma \cong \pi_1(\Sigma_2)$ acts freely and properl
y discontinuously on $S^2$ by deck transformations. Since $S^2$ is compact and $
\Gamma$ acts freely, the quotient map $S^2 \to S^2/\Gamma$ is a covering map wit
h $|\Gamma|$ sheets. But $S^2$ is simply connected, so $\Gamma$ must be trivial,
 giving $\Sigma_2 \cong S^2$, which has genus 0, a contradiction. Hence $\wideti
lde{\Sigma_2}$ is non-compact, i.e., $\widetilde{\Sigma_2} \cong \mathbb{R}^2$ t
opologically.

**Step 5: The geometric (Riemannian) structure — uniformization.** While topolog
ically the universal cover is $\mathbb{R}^2$, the *natural* geometric structure
is determined by the uniformization theorem. Equip $\Sigma_2$ with any Riemannia
n metric (compatible with its orientation). The uniformization theorem states th
at every simply connected Riemann surface is conformally equivalent to exactly o
ne of: the Riemann sphere $\hat{\mathbb{C}}$, the complex plane $\mathbb{C}$, or
 the open unit disk $\mathbb{D}$.

The type is determined by the sign of the Euler characteristic:
- $\chi > 0$ (genus 0): spherical type, universal cover $\cong \hat{\mathbb{C}}
\cong S^2$.
- $\chi = 0$ (genus 1, the torus): Euclidean (parabolic) type, universal cover $
\cong \mathbb{C} \cong \mathbb{R}^2$ with the flat metric.
- $\chi < 0$ (genus $\geq 2$): hyperbolic type, universal cover $\cong \mathbb{D
}$ with the hyperbolic metric.

Since $\chi(\Sigma_2) = -2 < 0$, the universal cover is of **hyperbolic type**.

**Step 6: The deck transformation group.** The fundamental group $\pi_1(\Sigma_2
)$ is a discrete subgroup of $\operatorname{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ (a Fuchsian gro
up) acting on $\mathbb{H}^2$ by Möbius transformations:
$$z \mapsto \frac{az + b}{cz + d}, \quad \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pma
trix} \in \operatorname{SL}(2, \mathbb{R}).$$
This action is free, properly discontinuous, and by isometries of the hyperbolic
 metric $ds^2 = \frac{dx^2 + dy^2}{y^2}$. The surface is recovered as the quotie
nt:
$$\Sigma_2 \cong \mathbb{H}^2 / \pi_1(\Sigma_2).$$

A presentation of the fundamental group is:
$$\pi_1(\Sigma_2) = \langle a_1, b_1, a_2, b_2 \mid [a_1, b_1][a_2, b_2] = 1 \ra
ngle,$$
which is a torsion-free cocompact Fuchsian group (a surface group of genus 2). T
he single relation $[a_1, b_1][a_2, b_2] = 1$ encodes the gluing of the boundary
 of a regular hyperbolic octagon (the fundamental domain in $\mathbb{H}^2$) whos
e sides are paired according to the word $a_1 b_1 a_1^{-1} b_1^{-1} a_2 b_2 a_2^
{-1} b_2^{-1}$.

**Step 7: Explicit fundamental domain.** A concrete model: in the Poincaré disk
$\mathbb{D}$, choose a regular hyperbolic octagon centered at the origin with al
l interior angles equal to $\pi/4$ (so the angle sum is $2\pi$, giving a smooth
surface after gluing). Pair opposite-type sides via the generators $a_1, b_1, a_
2, b_2$. The quotient is a closed genus-2 surface with a complete hyperbolic met
ric of constant curvature $-1$, and area $4\pi = 2\pi|\chi(\Sigma_2)|$ by the Ga
uss–Bonnet theorem.

## Conclusion

The universal covering space of a closed surface of genus 2 is the **hyperbolic
plane** $\mathbb{H}^2$ (equivalently the open unit disk $\mathbb{D}$). The funda
mental group $\pi_1(\Sigma_2) = \langle a_1, b_1, a_2, b_2 \mid [a_1, b_1][a_2,
b_2] = 1\rangle$ acts on $\mathbb{H}^2$ as a torsion-free cocompact Fuchsian gro
up, and $\Sigma_2 \cong \mathbb{H}^2 / \pi_1(\Sigma_2)$.
