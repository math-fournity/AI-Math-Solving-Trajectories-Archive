## Proof

Let $T(w) = i\frac{1-w}{1+w}$ be the Cayley map, a conformal bijection $\mathbb{
D}\to\mathbb{H}$ (with $T(0)=i$). Since each $f_n:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{H}$ is co
nformal and onto, the composition
$$\psi_n := T^{-1}\circ f_n : \mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}$$
is an automorphism of $\mathbb{D}$, hence univalent (one-to-one) on $\mathbb{D}$
.

**Step 1: $f$ is constant.** Each $f_n$ is univalent on the domain $\mathbb{D}$,
 and $f_n\to f$ uniformly on compact subsets. By Hurwitz's theorem, a locally un
iform limit of univalent holomorphic functions is either univalent or constant.
Since $f$ is **not** one-to-one, $f$ must be constant, say $f\equiv c$.

**Step 2: $c\notin\mathbb{H}$.** Suppose for contradiction that $c\in\mathbb{H}$
. Then $T^{-1}$ is holomorphic in a neighborhood of $c$, so
$$\psi_n = T^{-1}\circ f_n \longrightarrow T^{-1}(c) \quad\text{uniformly on com
pact subsets of }\mathbb{D}.$$
Set $\psi^* := T^{-1}(c)\in\mathbb{D}$ (an interior point, since $c\in\mathbb{H}
$). The $\psi_n$ are automorphisms of $\mathbb{D}$, hence univalent; by Hurwitz
again, their limit $\psi^*$ is either univalent or constant.

We claim an interior constant limit is impossible. Indeed, each $\psi_n$ is onto
 $\mathbb{D}$, so $\psi_n$ has a zero in $\mathbb{D}$. If $\psi^*\equiv\text{con
st}$ with $|\psi^*|>0$, then by Hurwitz applied to $\psi_n-\psi^*$ (whose limit
$\equiv 0$... ) — more directly: applying Hurwitz to $\psi_n$ itself, a nonzero
constant limit would force $\psi_n$ to have no zeros for large $n$, contradictin
g surjectivity onto $\mathbb{D}$. If $\psi^*\equiv 0$, then $\psi_n(0)\to 0$; bu
t $|\psi_n(0)|=|a_n|$ where $\psi_n(z)=e^{i\theta_n}\frac{z-a_n}{1-\bar a_n z}$,
 so $a_n\to 0$, which makes $\psi_n\to e^{i\theta^*}z$, an automorphism — not th
e constant $0$. Contradiction. Hence $\psi^*$ is univalent, i.e. an automorphism
 of $\mathbb{D}$.

But then $f = T\circ\psi^*$ is a composition of two univalent maps, hence unival
ent — contradicting the hypothesis that $f$ is not one-to-one. Therefore $c\noti
n\mathbb{H}$.

**Step 3: Conclusion.** Since $f_n(\mathbb{D})=\mathbb{H}$ and $f_n\to c$ unifor
mly on compacts, the constant $c$ lies in $\overline{\mathbb{H}}=\mathbb{H}\cup\
mathbb{R}$. Having ruled out $c\in\mathbb{H}$, we must have $c\in\mathbb{R}$, i.
e. $\Im c = 0$. Since $f\equiv c$,

$$\boxed{\Im f(0)=0}.$$

*Remark (consistency check).* The value is attained: taking $\psi_n(z)=\frac{z-a
_n}{1-a_n z}$ with $a_n\to -1$ (so $|a_n|<1$, $a_n\to -1\in\partial\mathbb{D}$),
 we have $\psi_n\to\frac{z+1}{1+z}\equiv 1$ uniformly on compacts, and $f_n=T\ci
rc\psi_n\to i\frac{1-1}{1+1}=0$, a non-injective constant limit with $\Im f(0)=0
$. (The case $\psi_n\to -1$ is excluded since then $1+\psi_n\to 0$ and $f_n$ blo
ws up, so no finite limit exists — consistent with the hypothesis that $f_n$ con
verges.)
