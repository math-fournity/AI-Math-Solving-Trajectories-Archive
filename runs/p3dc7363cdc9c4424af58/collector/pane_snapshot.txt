## Proof

**Claim.** In a category with a final object $1$, every point $p: 1 \to A$ is a
monomorphism.

**Recall.** An object $1$ is *final* if for every object $C$ there exists a uniq
ue morphism $C \to 1$. We denote this unique morphism by $!_C : C \to 1$.

**To show:** $p: 1 \to A$ is a monomorphism, i.e., for any object $C$ and any pa
ir of morphisms $g, h: C \to 1$, if $p \circ g = p \circ h$, then $g = h$.

**Proof.** Let $C$ be an arbitrary object, and let $g, h: C \to 1$ be any two mo
rphisms. Since $1$ is final, there is **exactly one** morphism from $C$ to $1$.
Therefore both $g$ and $h$ must equal the unique morphism $!_C : C \to 1$:

$$g = !_C \quad \text{and} \quad h = !_C.$$

Hence $g = h$ holds **unconditionally**, regardless of whether $p \circ g = p \c
irc h$ or not.

In particular, the implication "$p \circ g = p \circ h \implies g = h$" is satis
fied (its conclusion is always true). Since $C$, $g$, and $h$ were arbitrary, th
is verifies the definition of monomorphism for $p$.

Therefore every point $p: 1 \to A$ is a monomorphism. $\blacksquare$
