## Proof

**Setup.** Let $F:\Omega_1\to\Omega_2$ be a smooth map between compact, oriented
 $n$-dimensional manifolds with boundary. The (topological) degree $\operatornam
e{Deg} F$ is defined via the induced map on relative homology
$$F_*:H_n(\Omega_1,\partial\Omega_1)\longrightarrow H_n(\Omega_2,\partial\Omega_
2),$$
where orientation gives canonical isomorphisms $H_n(\Omega_i,\partial\Omega_i)\c
ong\mathbb Z$; thus $F_*[\Omega_1]=(\operatorname{Deg}F)\,[\Omega_2]$.

Equivalently (de Rham / integration characterization, valid for manifolds with b
oundary via relative cohomology): for every smooth $n$-form $\omega$ on $\Omega_
2$,
$$\int_{\Omega_1}F^*\omega=(\operatorname{Deg}F)\int_{\Omega_2}\omega.$$

**Choice of the regular value.** We pick $y_0\in\operatorname{int}(\Omega_2)$ sa
tisfying

1. $y_0$ is a regular value of $F$, and
2. $y_0\notin F(\partial\Omega_1)$.

Such $y_0$ exists in abundance: by Sard's theorem the set of regular values of $
F$ has full measure in $\Omega_2$. Moreover $F(\partial\Omega_1)$ has measure ze
ro in $\Omega_2$, because $F|_{\partial\Omega_1}:\partial\Omega_1\to\Omega_2$ ha
s $\dim\partial\Omega_1=n-1<n=\dim\Omega_2$, so every point of $\partial\Omega_1
$ is critical for $F|_{\partial\Omega_1}$, and Sard gives $\operatorname{meas}(F
(\partial\Omega_1))=0$. Hence regular values of $F$ lying in $\operatorname{int}
(\Omega_2)\setminus F(\partial\Omega_1)$ exist.

Condition (2) is equivalently stated as "$y_0$ is a regular value of $F|_{\parti
al\Omega_1$" — vacuously, since the differential of an $(n-1)\to n$ map can neve
r be surjective, so regularity forces the preimage to be empty.

**Structure of the preimage.** Since $y_0$ is regular for $F$ and $y_0\notin F(\
partial\Omega_1)$, the preimage $F^{-1}(y_0)$ is a $0$-dimensional submanifold c
ontained in $\operatorname{int}(\Omega_1)$. Being a closed subset of the compact
 $\Omega_1$, it is finite:
$$F^{-1}(y_0)=\{x_1,\dots,x_k\}\subset\operatorname{int}(\Omega_1).$$
At each $x_i$, $dF_{x_i}:T_{x_i}\Omega_1\to T_{y_0}\Omega_2$ is an isomorphism;
define $\operatorname{sign}(dF_{x_i})\in\{+1,-1\}$ according to whether $dF_{x_i
}$ preserves or reverses the orientations.

**Deriving the formula.** Choose pairwise disjoint coordinate neighborhoods $U_i
\subset\operatorname{int}(\Omega_1)$ of $x_i$ small enough that $F|_{U_i}$ is a
diffeomorphism onto a neighborhood $V_i=F(U_i)$ of $y_0$ (inverse function theor
em). Let $V=\bigcap_i V_i$ be a small neighborhood of $y_0$ with $\overline V\su
bset\bigcap_i V_i$, and let $\omega$ be a smooth bump $n$-form supported in $V\s
ubset\operatorname{int}(\Omega_2)$ with $\int_{\Omega_2}\omega=1$. Then $F^*\ome
ga$ is supported in $\bigsqcup_i U_i'\subset\bigsqcup_i U_i$, where $U_i'=(F|_{U
_i})^{-1}(V)$. On each $U_i'$ the change-of-variables formula gives
$$\int_{U_i'}F^*\omega=\operatorname{sign}(dF_{x_i})\int_{V}\omega=\operatorname
{sign}(dF_{x_i})\cdot 1.$$
Summing,
$$\int_{\Omega_1}F^*\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\operatorname{sign}(dF_{x_i}).$$
By the integration characterization of degree, the left side equals $\operatorna
me{Deg}F\cdot\int_{\Omega_2}\omega=\operatorname{Deg}F$. Therefore

$$\boxed{\;\operatorname{Deg}F=\sum_{x\in F^{-1}(y_0)}\operatorname{sign}(dF_x)\
;}$$

where the sum ranges over the (finite) preimage, all of whose points lie in the
interior of $\Omega_1$.

**Independence of $y_0$.** The right-hand side is independent of the chosen admi
ssible $y_0$ (any two such are connected by a path of regular values avoiding $F
(\partial\Omega_1)$, and the signed count is locally constant, changing only whe
n crossing a critical value — at which births/deaths of opposite-sign pairs canc
el). This matches the fact that $\operatorname{Deg}F$ is a homotopy invariant de
fined homologically.

**Comparison with the closed case.** The formula is *identical* in form to the c
losed-manifold formula $\operatorname{Deg}F=\sum_{x\in F^{-1}(y_0)}\operatorname
{sign}(dF_x)$. The only modification required for manifolds with boundary is the
 **hypothesis on the regular value**: one must choose $y_0\in\operatorname{int}(
\Omega_2)$ that is a regular value of $F$ **and** avoids the boundary image $F(\
partial\Omega_1)$ (equivalently, is a regular value of $F|_{\partial\Omega_1}$).
 This forces the entire preimage into the interior, where the local sign-countin
g argument of the closed case applies verbatim. Sard's theorem guarantees such $
y_0$ exist, since $F(\partial\Omega_1)$ has measure zero in $\Omega_2$.
