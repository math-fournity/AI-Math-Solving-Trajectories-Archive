# Normal Modes of a Circular Drum Head

## Setup

We seek solutions of
$$\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left(r\frac{\partial v}{\partial r}\r
ight)+\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial^2 v}{\partial\theta^2}=\frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\par
tial^2 v}{\partial t^2},\qquad v(1,\theta,t)=0,$$
on $0\le r\le 1$, $0\le\theta<2\pi$, with $v$ finite at $r=0$ and $2\pi$-periodi
c in $\theta$.

## Separation of variables

**Step 1: Separate time.** Set $v(r,\theta,t)=u(r,\theta)\,T(t)$. Substituting a
nd dividing by $uT$:
$$\frac{1}{u}\!\left[\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left(r\frac{\parti
al u}{\partial r}\right)+\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial\theta^2}\righ
t]=\frac{1}{c^2}\frac{T''}{T}=-\lambda,$$
where $\lambda\ge 0$ (the sign is forced by the boundary condition $u(1,\theta)=
0$ and finiteness at $r=0$, which make the spatial operator self-adjoint with no
n-positive eigenvalues of $-\nabla^2$). This gives
$$T''+\lambda c^2\,T=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad T(t)=A\cos(\omega t)+B\sin(\ome
ga t),\qquad \omega=c\sqrt{\lambda},$$
and the spatial eigenvalue problem
$$\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left(r\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\r
ight)+\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial\theta^2}+\lambda\,u=0,\qquad u(1
,\theta)=0,\quad u\text{ finite at }r=0.$$

**Step 2: Separate angle.** Set $u(r,\theta)=R(r)\,\Theta(\theta)$. Then
$$\frac{r}{R}\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(r\frac{dR}{dr}\right)+\lambda r^2=-\frac{1}{\Th
eta}\frac{d^2\Theta}{d\theta^2}=m^2,$$
where $m^2\ge 0$ is a separation constant. Periodicity $\Theta(\theta+2\pi)=\The
ta(\theta)$ forces $m=0,1,2,\dots$, giving
$$\Theta_m(\theta)=C\cos(m\theta)+D\sin(m\theta)\qquad(m\ge 1),\qquad \Theta_0=\
text{const}\qquad(m=0).$$

**Step 3: Radial equation — Bessel's equation.** The radial part becomes
$$r^2 R''+rR'+(\lambda r^2-m^2)R=0.$$
Setting $x=\sqrt{\lambda}\,r$ (so $R(r)=J_m(\sqrt{\lambda}\,r)$ up to normalizat
ion), this is **Bessel's equation of order $m$**. The general solution is
$$R(r)=\alpha\,J_m(\sqrt{\lambda}\,r)+\beta\,Y_m(\sqrt{\lambda}\,r).$$
Since $Y_m$ diverges at $r=0$, finiteness requires $\beta=0$, leaving $R(r)=J_m(
\sqrt{\lambda}\,r)$.

**Step 4: Boundary condition determines eigenvalues.** The condition $u(1,\theta
)=0$ gives $R(1)=0$, i.e.
$$J_m(\sqrt{\lambda})=0.$$
Let $j_{m,n}$ denote the $n$-th positive zero of $J_m$ ($n=1,2,3,\dots$). Then
$$\sqrt{\lambda}=j_{m,n}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \lambda_{m,n}=j_{m,n}^2,\qquad
 \omega_{m,n}=c\,j_{m,n}.$$

## The normal modes

For each $m=0,1,2,\dots$ and $n=1,2,3,\dots$, the normal mode is

$$\boxed{v_{m,n}^{(\cos)}(r,\theta,t)=J_m(j_{m,n}\,r)\cos(m\theta)\cos(c\,j_{m,n
}\,t),}$$
$$\boxed{v_{m,n}^{(\sin)}(r,\theta,t)=J_m(j_{m,n}\,r)\sin(m\theta)\cos(c\,j_{m,n
}\,t),}$$

together with the same spatial profiles multiplied by $\sin(c\,j_{m,n}\,t)$ (the
 phase-shifted temporal partner). For $m=0$ the $\sin(m\theta)$ family vanishes
identically, so only the axisymmetric mode

$$v_{0,n}(r,t)=J_0(j_{0,n}\,r)\cos(c\,j_{0,n}\,t)$$

survives. Explicitly:

- **$m=0$ (axisymmetric):** $v_{0,n}=J_0(j_{0,n}r)\cos(c\,j_{0,n}t)$, one mode p
er $n$.
- **$m\ge 1$ (azimuthal variation):** two degenerate modes per $(m,n)$ pair, $\c
os(m\theta)$ and $\sin(m\theta)$, sharing the same frequency $\omega_{m,n}=c\,j_
{m,n}$.

Here $J_m$ is the Bessel function of the first kind of order $m$, and $j_{m,n}$
is its $n$-th positive zero.

## General solution

By completeness of $\{J_m(j_{m,n}r)\cos(m\theta),\,J_m(j_{m,n}r)\sin(m\theta)\}$
 in $L^2$ of the disk, the general solution is the superposition

$$v(r,\theta,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}J_0(j_{0,n}r)\bigl[A_{0,n}\cos(c\,j_{0,n}t)+\
bar A_{0,n}\sin(c\,j_{0,n}t)\bigr]$$
$$+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}J_m(j_{m,n}r)\Bigl\{\bigl[A_{m,n}\cos(m
\theta)+B_{m,n}\sin(m\theta)\bigr]\cos(c\,j_{m,n}t)$$
$$\qquad\qquad\qquad+\bigl[\bar A_{m,n}\cos(m\theta)+\bar B_{m,n}\sin(m\theta)\b
igr]\sin(c\,j_{m,n}t)\Bigr\},$$

with coefficients fixed by initial data via the orthogonality relation
$$\int_0^1 r\,J_m(j_{m,n}r)\,J_m(j_{m,n'}r)\,dr=\tfrac12\bigl[J_{m+1}(j_{m,n})\b
igr]^2\,\delta_{nn'}.$$

## Physical interpretation

Each mode $J_m(j_{m,n}r)\cos(m\theta)$ (or $\sin(m\theta)$) vibrates at a single
 frequency $\omega_{m,n}=c\,j_{m,n}$:
- $n$ counts the radial nodes (the boundary $r=1$ plus $n-1$ interior circular n
odal lines at $r=j_{m,k}/j_{m,n}$ for the lower zeros),
- $m$ counts the diametral nodal lines through the origin,
- the twofold degeneracy for $m\ge1$ reflects rotational symmetry: any rotated l
inear combination $\cos(m(\theta-\theta_0))$ is equally a mode at the same frequ
ency.

This is the classic result that the drum's spectrum is **not** harmonically rela
ted (the $j_{m,n}$ are not integer multiples of a fundamental), which is why a d
rum's pitch is less "musical" than a string's — one can hear the shape of a drum
 (almost).
