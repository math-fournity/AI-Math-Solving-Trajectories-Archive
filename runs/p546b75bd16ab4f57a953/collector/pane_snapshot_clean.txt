Looking at this problem, I need to find the largest $n$ such that the evaluation
 map $P \mapsto P(\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3})$ is injective on base-$n$ polynomials.

**Answer: $n = 9$.**

## Proof

Let $\alpha = \sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}$. Two base-$n$ polynomials $P, Q$ satisfy $P(\al
pha) = Q(\alpha)$ iff $R(\alpha) = 0$ where $R = P - Q$ has coefficients in $\{-
(n-1), \ldots, n-1\}$. So we need: **the only multiple of the minimal polynomial
 of $\alpha$ with coefficients in $\{-(n-1),\ldots,n-1\}$ is zero.**

**Minimal polynomial.** We compute $\alpha^2 = 5 + 2\sqrt{6}$, $\alpha^4 = 49 +
20\sqrt{6}$, so $\alpha^4 - 10\alpha^2 + 1 = 0$. The polynomial $m(x) = x^4 - 10
x^2 + 1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ (it has no rational roots and doesn't
factor into rational quadratics), so it is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$. T
hus $R(\alpha) = 0$ with $R \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ iff $m \mid R$. Since $m$ is moni
c, by Gauss's lemma $R = m \cdot S$ for some $S \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$.

**Reduction to a quadratic.** Since $m(x) = x^4 - 10x^2 + 1$ is even, write $S(x
) = S_e(x^2) + x\, S_o(x^2)$. Then:
$$R(x) = \bigl(x^4 - 10x^2 + 1\bigr)S_e(x^2) \;+\; x\bigl(x^4 - 10x^2 + 1\bigr)S
_o(x^2).$$
The even-indexed coefficients of $R$ are exactly the coefficients of $(t^2 - 10t
 + 1)\,S_e(t)$, and the odd-indexed coefficients are those of $(t^2 - 10t + 1)\,
S_o(t)$. If $S \neq 0$, at least one of $S_e, S_o$ is nonzero, giving a **nonzer
o multiple of $f(t) = t^2 - 10t + 1$ in $\mathbb{Z}[t]$** with height $\leq \|R\
|_\infty$. Conversely, any nonzero multiple of $f(t)$ with height $H$ yields a n
onzero multiple of $m(x)$ with height $H$ (via $t \mapsto x^2$). Therefore:

$$\min\{\|R\|_\infty : R \in \mathbb{Z}[x],\; R \neq 0,\; m \mid R\} \;=\; \min\
{\\|Q\|_\infty : Q \in \mathbb{Z}[t],\; Q \neq 0,\; f \mid Q\}.$$

**Lower bound (height $\leq 8$ is impossible).** The roots of $f(t) = t^2 - 10t
+ 1$ are $\lambda = 5 + 2\sqrt{6}$ and $\mu = 5 - 2\sqrt{6}$, with $\lambda\mu =
 1$ and $\lambda \approx 9.899$.

Suppose $Q(t) = f(t)\,T(t) \neq 0$ with $Q(t) = \sum_{k=0}^N c_k t^k$, $c_N \neq
 0$, and $|c_k| \leq 8$ for all $k$. Since $Q(\lambda) = 0$:
$$|c_N|\,\lambda^N = \left|\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} c_k\,\lambda^k\right| \leq 8\sum_{k=
0}^{N-1}\lambda^k = 8\cdot\frac{\lambda^N - 1}{\lambda - 1}.$$
Therefore:
$$|c_N| \leq \frac{8}{\lambda - 1}\left(1 - \lambda^{-N}\right) < \frac{8}{\lamb
da - 1} = \frac{8}{4 + 2\sqrt{6}} = 2\sqrt{6} - 4 \approx 0.899 < 1.$$
Since $c_N$ is a nonzero integer, $|c_N| \geq 1$, a contradiction. So **no nonze
ro multiple of $f$ has height $\leq 8$**, hence no nonzero multiple of $m$ has h
eight $\leq 8$.

**Upper bound (height 9 is achievable).** Take $S(x) = x^2 + 1$, so:
$$R(x) = m(x)(x^2+1) = (x^4 - 10x^2 + 1)(x^2+1) = x^6 - 9x^4 - 9x^2 + 1,$$
which has height $9$.

**Conclusion.** The minimum height of a nonzero multiple of $m(x)$ is exactly $9
$.

- For $n = 9$: coefficients of $P - Q$ lie in $\{-8,\ldots,8\}$, and no nonzero
multiple of $m$ has height $\leq 8$, so $P - Q = 0$, i.e., $P = Q$. The represen
tation is unique. ✓

- For $n = 10$: set $P(x) = x^6 + 1$ and $Q(x) = 9x^4 + 9x^2$. Both are base-$10
$ polynomials (coefficients in $\{0,\ldots,9\}$, positive leading coefficient),
$P \neq Q$, and $P(\alpha) - Q(\alpha) = R(\alpha) = 0$. So uniqueness fails. ✗

The largest positive integer $n$ is $\boxed{9}$.
