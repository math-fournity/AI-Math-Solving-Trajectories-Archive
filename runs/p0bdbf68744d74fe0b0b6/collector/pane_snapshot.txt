We solve the functional equation
$$f(x+h)=e^{x}f(h)+e^{h}f(x),\qquad \lim_{x\to 0}\frac{f(x)}{x}=0.$$

### Step 1. Reduce to Cauchy's equation

Define $g(x)=e^{-x}f(x)$, i.e. $f(x)=e^{x}g(x)$. Substituting into the equation:

$$e^{x+h}g(x+h)=e^{x}\cdot e^{h}g(h)+e^{h}\cdot e^{x}g(x)=e^{x+h}\bigl(g(h)+g(x)
\bigr).$$

Dividing by $e^{x+h}\neq 0$:
$$g(x+h)=g(x)+g(h).$$

So $g$ satisfies **Cauchy's functional equation**.

### Step 2. Use the limit condition

Setting $h=0$ in the original equation gives $f(x)=e^{x}f(0)+f(x)$, hence $f(0)=
0$, and likewise $g(0)=0$.

The limit condition becomes
$$\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{f(x)}{x}=\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{e^{x}g(x)}{x}=\lim_{x\to 0}\fra
c{g(x)}{x}=0.$$

In particular $g(x)=x\cdot\frac{g(x)}{x}\to 0=g(0)$ as $x\to 0$, so **$g$ is con
tinuous at $0$**.

### Step 3. Conclude $g$ is linear

A well-known result: an additive function ($g(x+h)=g(x)+g(h)$) that is continuou
s at one point is continuous everywhere and is of the form $g(x)=cx$ for some co
nstant $c$.

*Proof sketch.* Additivity gives $g(q)=q\,g(1)$ for every rational $q$. Continui
ty at $0$ then extends this to all real $x$ by approximating $x$ with rationals
$q_n\to x$: $g(x)=\lim g(q_n)=\lim q_n g(1)=x\,g(1)$.

So $g(x)=cx$ with $c=g(1)$.

### Step 4. Pin down the constant

From Step 2,
$$0=\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{g(x)}{x}=\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{cx}{x}=c.$$

Hence $c=0$, so $g\equiv 0$, and therefore
$$f(x)=e^{x}g(x)\equiv 0.$$

### Verification

$f\equiv 0$: LHS $=0$, RHS $=e^{x}\cdot 0+e^{h}\cdot 0=0$. ✓
Limit: $0/x\to 0$. ✓

### General solution

The general solution of the functional equation **subject to** $\displaystyle\li
m_{x\to 0}\frac{f(x)}{x}=0$ is uniquely

$$\boxed{f(x)\equiv 0.}$$

(Without the limit condition, the general solution would be $f(x)=e^{x}g(x)$ for
 any additive $g$; the regularity imposed by the limit forces $g\equiv 0$.)
