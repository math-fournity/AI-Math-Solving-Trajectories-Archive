## Proof

We evaluate the iterated integral
$$I(\epsilon) = \int_{\Lambda/s}^{\Lambda}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{i}{
y^{2}-x^{2}+i\epsilon}\,dy\right)dx$$
for fixed $\epsilon>0$, then take $\epsilon\to0^{+}$.

### Step 1. The inner integral in $y$

Fix $x>0$ (the outer range $[\Lambda/s,\Lambda]\subset(0,\infty)$ since $s,\Lamb
da>0$). The poles of the integrand in $y$ are the roots of $y^{2}=x^{2}-i\epsilo
n$, namely
$$y_{+}=+\sqrt{x^{2}-i\epsilon},\qquad y_{-}=-\sqrt{x^{2}-i\epsilon},$$
where we choose the branch with $\operatorname{Re}\sqrt{x^{2}-i\epsilon}>0$. Sin
ce $x^{2}-i\epsilon$ lies in the fourth quadrant, $\operatorname{Im}y_{+}<0$ (be
low the real axis) and $\operatorname{Im}y_{-}>0$ (above the real axis).

Partial fractions give
$$\frac{i}{y^{2}-x^{2}+i\epsilon}=\frac{i}{(y-y_{+})(y-y_{-})}=\frac{i}{y_{+}-y_
{-}}\left(\frac{1}{y-y_{+}}-\frac{1}{y-y_{-}}\right)=\frac{i}{2\sqrt{x^{2}-i\eps
ilon}}\left(\frac{1}{y-y_{+}}-\frac{1}{y-y_{-}}\right).$$

We use the standard identity (obtained by splitting into real/imaginary parts, o
r by a semicircular contour):
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{y-z}=i\pi\,\operatorname{sgn}(\operatorname{I
m}z),\qquad \operatorname{Im}z\neq0.$$

Since $\operatorname{Im}y_{+}<0$ and $\operatorname{Im}y_{-}>0$,
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{y-y_{+}}=-i\pi,\qquad \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{dy}{y-y_{-}}=+i\pi.$$

Therefore
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{i}{y^{2}-x^{2}+i\epsilon}\,dy=\frac{i}{2\sqrt{x^{
2}-i\epsilon}}\bigl((-i\pi)-(i\pi)\bigr)=\frac{i}{2\sqrt{x^{2}-i\epsilon}}(-2i\p
i)=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{x^{2}-i\epsilon}}.$$

### Step 2. The outer integral and the limit

Substituting back,
$$I(\epsilon)=\int_{\Lambda/s}^{\Lambda}\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{x^{2}-i\epsilon}}\,dx.$
$

The interval of integration is a compact subset of $(0,\infty)$ (both $\Lambda/s
$ and $\Lambda$ are positive), and on this interval $\sqrt{x^{2}-i\epsilon}\to x
$ **uniformly** as $\epsilon\to0^{+}$. Hence we may exchange the limit with the
integral:
$$\lim_{\epsilon\to0^{+}}I(\epsilon)=\int_{\Lambda/s}^{\Lambda}\lim_{\epsilon\to
0^{+}}\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{x^{2}-i\epsilon}}\,dx=\int_{\Lambda/s}^{\Lambda}\frac{\pi
}{x}\,dx.$$

### Step 3. Final evaluation

$$\int_{\Lambda/s}^{\Lambda}\frac{\pi}{x}\,dx=\pi\Bigl[\log x\Bigr]_{\Lambda/s}^
{\Lambda}=\pi\bigl(\log\Lambda-\log(\Lambda/s)\bigr)=\pi\log\Lambda-\pi(\log\Lam
bda-\log s)=\pi\log s.$$

Thus
$$\boxed{\lim_{\epsilon\to0^{+}}\int_{\Lambda/s}^{\Lambda}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty
}\frac{i}{y^{2}-x^{2}+i\epsilon}\,dy\,dx=\pi\log s.}$$
