# Solution

**Claim.** Let $S$ be a nodal Enriques surface, $\pi: X \to S$ its K3 cover, and
 $R \subset S$ a $(-2)$-curve. Then
$$\pi^{-1}(R) = R_1 \sqcup R_2,$$
where $R_1, R_2$ are disjoint $(-2)$-curves (smooth rational curves with self-in
tersection $-2$) on the K3 surface $X$, exchanged by the deck transformation $\s
igma$ of the cover.

---

## Proof

**Step 1. The K3 cover is étale.**

An Enriques surface has $K_S$ numerically trivial with $2K_S \sim 0$ but $K_S \n
ot\sim 0$; thus $K_S$ is a non-trivial 2-torsion line bundle. The K3 cover $\pi:
 X \to S$ is the canonical cover associated to this 2-torsion element. Since $K_
S$ is numerically trivial (in particular base-point free in the trivial sense —
it has no zeros as a section of a numerically trivial bundle), the associated do
uble cover is **étale** (unramified). The deck transformation $\sigma$ is theref
ore a **fixed-point-free** involution on the K3 surface $X$, and $S = X/\langle\
sigma\rangle$.

**Step 2. A $(-2)$-curve on $S$ is a smooth rational curve.**

By the adjunction formula on $S$:
$$2g(R) - 2 = R^2 + R \cdot K_S.$$
Since $K_S$ is numerically trivial, $R \cdot K_S = 0$, and $R^2 = -2$ by hypothe
sis. Hence $2g(R) - 2 = -2$, giving $g(R) = 0$. So $R \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ is a s
mooth rational curve.

**Step 3. The restricted cover $\pi^{-1}(R) \to R$ is an étale double cover of $
\mathbb{P}^1$.**

Since $\pi$ is étale, its restriction to the smooth curve $R \cong \mathbb{P}^1$
 is an étale double cover of $\mathbb{P}^1$ (after resolving whether it is conne
cted or split).

**Step 4. The cover splits: $\pi^{-1}(R) = R_1 \sqcup R_2$.**

Over $\mathbb{C}$, $\mathbb{P}^1$ is simply connected: $\pi_1(\mathbb{P}^1) = 0$
. Therefore every étale cover of $\mathbb{P}^1$ is trivial. In particular, the é
tale double cover $\pi^{-1}(R) \to R$ must be the **trivial** (disconnected) cov
er: two disjoint copies of $\mathbb{P}^1$. Write
$$\pi^{-1}(R) = R_1 \sqcup R_2, \qquad R_1 \cong R_2 \cong \mathbb{P}^1.$$

**Step 5. Each component is a $(-2)$-curve on $X$.**

Since $\pi$ is étale of degree 2, for any curve $C$ on $S$:
$$(\pi^* C)^2 = \deg(\pi) \cdot C^2 = 2\,C^2.$$
Here $\pi^* R = R_1 + R_2$, so
$$(R_1 + R_2)^2 = 2 \cdot (-2) = -4.$$
Since the cover is étale and $R_1, R_2$ are distinct irreducible components lyin
g over different sheets, they are disjoint: $R_1 \cdot R_2 = 0$. Therefore
$$R_1^2 + R_2^2 = -4, \qquad R_1^2 = R_2^2 = -2.$$
Combined with $R_i \cong \mathbb{P}^1$, each $R_i$ is a $(-2)$-curve on the K3 s
urface $X$.

**Step 6. The deck transformation exchanges the two components.**

The free involution $\sigma$ preserves $\pi^{-1}(R)$ and acts on it as the deck
transformation of the (trivial) double cover. Since $\sigma$ has no fixed points
, it cannot fix either component; it must exchange them:
$$\sigma(R_1) = R_2, \qquad \sigma(R_2) = R_1. \qquad \blacksquare$$

---

**Remark.** The nodal hypothesis on $S$ is not needed for this particular result
 — the conclusion holds for any Enriques surface and any $(-2)$-curve on it, sin
ce the argument uses only that the K3 cover is étale and that $\mathbb{P}^1$ is
simply connected. (The nodal structure becomes relevant when one considers the p
reimage of the **nodal curve** $N$ itself, which — unlike a $(-2)$-curve — has a
 singularity, and whose preimage is a single smooth elliptic curve rather than a
 split union.)
