## Proof

**Interpreting the sum.** The summand $\frac{e^{2\pi i n/N}}{1-e^{2\pi i n/N}}$
is singular at $n=N$ (since $e^{2\pi i}=1$). The natural Riemann-sum interpretat
ion excludes this endpoint, so we work with
$$S_N=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\frac{e^{2\pi i n/N}}{1-e^{2\pi i n/N}}.$$

---

### Step 1. Identify the definite integral

Set $f(z)=\frac{z}{1-z}$ and $\omega_N=e^{2\pi i/N}$. The points $z_n=\omega_N^n
=e^{2\pi i n/N}$, $n=1,\dots,N-1$, are equally spaced on the unit circle $\{e^{2
\pi i t}:t\in[0,1]\}$ with mesh $\Delta t=1/N$. Hence $S_N$ is the Riemann sum
$$S_N=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}f\!\bigl(e^{2\pi i n/N}\bigr)\;\longrightarrow\
;\int_0^1 f\!\bigl(e^{2\pi i t}\bigr)\,dt=\int_0^1\frac{e^{2\pi i t}}{1-e^{2\pi
i t}}\,dt.$$

---

### Step 2. Evaluate the integral

Using $1-e^{2\pi i t}=-2i\,e^{\pi i t}\sin(\pi t)$,
$$\frac{e^{2\pi i t}}{1-e^{2\pi i t}}=\frac{e^{\pi i t}}{-2i\sin(\pi t)}=\frac{\
cos(\pi t)+i\sin(\pi t)}{-2i\sin(\pi t)}=-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i}{2}\cot(\pi t).$$

Therefore
$$\int_0^1\frac{e^{2\pi i t}}{1-e^{2\pi i t}}\,dt=-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i}{2}\int_0
^1\cot(\pi t)\,dt.$$

The function $\cot(\pi t)$ is antisymmetric about $t=\tfrac12$ since $\cot(\pi(1
-t))=-\cot(\pi t)$, so the (Cauchy) principal value $\operatorname{PV}\!\int_0^1
\cot(\pi t)\,dt=0$. The Riemann sums realize exactly this principal value (the s
ingular endpoint $t=0$ is excluded by the $n=1,\dots,N-1$ range, and the symmetr
y of the discrete sum forces the imaginary part to vanish). Hence
$$\int_0^1\frac{e^{2\pi i t}}{1-e^{2\pi i t}}\,dt=-\frac{1}{2}.$$

---

### Step 3. Verification via exact finite sum

Let $\omega=e^{2\pi i/N}$. Since $\frac{\omega^n}{1-\omega^n}=-1+\frac{1}{1-\ome
ga^n}$,
$$\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\frac{\omega^n}{1-\omega^n}=-(N-1)+\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{1-
\omega^n}.$$

The polynomial $\frac{x^N-1}{x-1}=1+x+\cdots+x^{N-1}$ has roots $\omega,\omega^2
,\dots,\omega^{N-1}$. Its logarithmic derivative at $x=1$ gives
$$\frac{1+2+\cdots+(N-1)}{N}=\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{1-\omega^n}\;\Longrightarr
ow\;\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{1-\omega^n}=\frac{N-1}{2}.$$

Thus
$$\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\frac{\omega^n}{1-\omega^n}=-(N-1)+\frac{N-1}{2}=-\frac{N-1}{2
},$$
and
$$S_N=\frac{1}{N}\!\left(-\frac{N-1}{2}\right)=-\frac{N-1}{2N}\;\xrightarrow{N\t
o\infty}\;-\frac{1}{2},$$
confirming the integral evaluation.

---

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{e^{2\pi i
n/N}}{1-e^{2\pi i n/N}}=\int_0^1\frac{e^{2\pi i t}}{1-e^{2\pi i t}}\,dt=-\frac{1
}{2}.}$$
