## Proof

We evaluate $\displaystyle L = \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n!\,z^n}{n^n}$ for $z\in\m
athbb{C}$ with $|z|=e$.

**Step 1: Reduce to the modulus.** Set $a_n = \dfrac{n!\,z^n}{n^n}$. Then

$$
|a_n| = \frac{n!\,|z|^n}{n^n} = \frac{n!\,e^n}{n^n},
$$

since $|z|=e$. Thus the modulus of $a_n$ is independent of the argument of $z$.

**Step 2: Apply Stirling's formula.** Stirling's approximation gives

$$
n! \sim \sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^n \quad (n\to\infty),
$$

i.e. $\dfrac{n!}{\sqrt{2\pi n}\,(n/e)^n}\to 1$. Substituting,

$$
|a_n| = \frac{n!\,e^n}{n^n} \sim \frac{\sqrt{2\pi n}\,(n/e)^n\,e^n}{n^n} = \sqrt
{2\pi n}\cdot\frac{n^n\,e^n}{e^n\,n^n} = \sqrt{2\pi n}.
$$

Hence

$$
|a_n| \sim \sqrt{2\pi n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\infty.
$$

**Step 3: Conclude.** Since $|a_n|\to\infty$, the sequence $\{a_n\}$ has no fini
te accumulation point in $\mathbb{C}$. In particular, for every $M>0$ there exis
ts $N$ such that $|a_n|>M$ for all $n\ge N$, which means $a_n\to\infty$ in the e
xtended complex plane $\widehat{\mathbb{C}}$.

Therefore the limit does not exist as a finite complex number; rather,

$$
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n!\,z^n}{n^n} = \infty \text{ in } \widehat{\mathb
b{C}}\quad(\text{i.e., } |a_n|\to\infty),\ \text{for all } z \text{ with } |z|=e
.}
$$

The argument of $z$ only rotates each term $a_n$ by the angle $n\arg z$, but sin
ce the modulus $\sqrt{2\pi n}\to\infty$ regardless, the sequence diverges to inf
inity for every such $z$.
