## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. For any $g \in G$, the left translate $L_g f$ is right uniformly
 continuous.

**Setup.** Recall the definition: $f$ is right uniformly continuous on $G$ if fo
r every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists an $e$-neighborhood $U$ such that for all
 $x, x' \in G$,
$$x(x')^{-1} \in U \implies |f(x) - f(x')| < \varepsilon.$$

Fix $g \in G$ and $\varepsilon > 0$. We must produce an $e$-neighborhood $V$ suc
h that for all $x, x' \in G$,
$$x(x')^{-1} \in V \implies |(L_g f)(x) - (L_g f)(x')| < \varepsilon.$$

**Construction of $V$.** By the right uniform continuity of $f$ applied to $\var
epsilon$, there is an $e$-neighborhood $U$ with
$$y(y')^{-1} \in U \implies |f(y) - f(y')| < \varepsilon. \tag{$\ast$}$$

Define
$$V := g\, U\, g^{-1} = \{gug^{-1} : u \in U\}.$$

Since $G$ is a topological group, conjugation $c_g \colon G \to G$, $h \mapsto g
hg^{-1}$, is a homeomorphism fixing the identity $e$. Hence $V = c_g(U)$ is an $
e$-neighborhood (it is open, contains $e = geg^{-1}$, and is a neighborhood of $
e$).

**Verification.** Let $x, x' \in G$ with $x(x')^{-1} \in V$. Set $y := g^{-1}x$
and $y' := g^{-1}x'$. Then
$$y(y')^{-1} = (g^{-1}x)(g^{-1}x')^{-1} = g^{-1}x(x')^{-1}g.$$
Since $x(x')^{-1} \in V = gUg^{-1}$, write $x(x')^{-1} = gug^{-1}$ for some $u \
in U$. Then
$$y(y')^{-1} = g^{-1}(gug^{-1})g = u \in U.$$
Applying $(\ast)$ with this $y, y'$,
$$|f(y) - f(y')| < \varepsilon.$$
But $f(y) = f(g^{-1}x) = (L_g f)(x)$ and $f(y') = f(g^{-1}x') = (L_g f)(x')$, so
$$|(L_g f)(x) - (L_g f)(x')| < \varepsilon.$$

**Conclusion.** For every $\varepsilon > 0$ we exhibited an $e$-neighborhood $V
= gUg^{-1}$ satisfying the defining condition for $L_g f$. Therefore $L_g f$ is
right uniformly continuous. (Note: the boundedness hypothesis on $f$ was not nee
ded; the argument works for any right uniformly continuous $f$ on any topologica
l group, in particular on any locally compact group.)
