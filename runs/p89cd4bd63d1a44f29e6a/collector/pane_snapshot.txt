## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $E$ must have Lebesgue measure zero.

**Proof.**

By hypothesis, for every $x \in E$ there exists $r_x > 0$ with
$$m\bigl(B(x,r_x)\cap E\bigr)=0,$$
where $B(x,r_x)=(x-r_x,x+r_x)$ and $m$ denotes Lebesgue measure. The family
$$\mathcal{U}=\bigl\{B(x,r_x):x\in E\bigr\}$$
is an open cover of $E$ in $\mathbb{R}$.

Since $\mathbb{R}$ is second-countable (hence Lindelöf), every open cover of any
 subset of $\mathbb{R}$ admits a countable subcover. Therefore there exist point
s $x_1,x_2,\dots\in E$ such that
$$E\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}B(x_i,r_{x_i}).$$

Consequently,
$$E=E\cap\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}B(x_i,r_{x_i})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\bigl(E\cap
B(x_i,r_{x_i})\bigr).$$

By the hypothesis, each set $E\cap B(x_i,r_{x_i})$ has Lebesgue measure zero. A
countable union of measure-zero sets has measure zero (by countable subadditivit
y of Lebesgue measure). Hence
$$m(E)\le\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}m\bigl(E\cap B(x_i,r_{x_i})\bigr)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty
}0=0.$$

Therefore $m(E)=0$. $\blacksquare$
