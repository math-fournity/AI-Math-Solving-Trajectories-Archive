## Example

Let $k = \mathbb{R}$ (the field of real numbers, which is **not** algebraically
closed), and let

$$f(x) = x^{2} + 1 \;\in\; \mathbb{R}[x].$$

We work in the polynomial ring $\mathbb{R}[x]$ in one variable (the Nullstellens
atz is a statement about $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ for any $n \ge 1$, so $n=1$ is admis
sible).

---

## Proof that the Nullstellensatz fails

We must verify two things:

**(1) The ideal $(f) = (x^{2}+1)$ is proper in $\mathbb{R}[x]$.**

An ideal $(g)$ is proper iff $g$ is not a unit. The units of $\mathbb{R}[x]$ are
 exactly the nonzero constant polynomials (since $\deg(gh)=\deg g + \deg h$, a u
nit must have degree $0$). Because

$$\deg(x^{2}+1) = 2 \ge 1,$$

$x^{2}+1$ is not a unit, hence $(x^{2}+1) \subsetneq \mathbb{R}[x]$.

Equivalently, the quotient ring

$$\mathbb{R}[x]/(x^{2}+1) \;\cong\; \mathbb{C}$$

is not the zero ring, confirming that $(x^{2}+1)$ is a proper ideal.

**(2) The zero set $V(f)$ is empty.**

By definition,

$$V(f) = \{\, a \in \mathbb{R} : f(a) = 0 \,\} = \{\, a \in \mathbb{R} : a^{2}+1
 = 0 \,\}.$$

For every real number $a$, we have $a^{2} \ge 0$, and therefore

$$a^{2} + 1 \;\ge\; 1 \;>\; 0.$$

Hence $a^{2}+1 = 0$ has **no** solution in $\mathbb{R}$, i.e.

$$V(x^{2}+1) = \varnothing \;\subset\; \mathbb{R}^{1}.$$

---

## Conclusion

We have exhibited a field $k = \mathbb{R}$ that is not algebraically closed, and
 a polynomial $f = x^{2}+1 \in \mathbb{R}[x]$, such that:

- the ideal $(f)$ is **proper** in $\mathbb{R}[x]$, yet
- the affine zero set $V(f) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{1}$ is **empty**.

This is precisely the failure of the (weak) Nullstellensatz: over an algebraical
ly closed field, every proper ideal has a nonempty zero set, but here a proper i
deal has no zero at all. The obstruction is exactly the non-algebraic-closedness
 of $\mathbb{R}$ — the roots $\pm i$ of $x^{2}+1$ lie in $\mathbb{C}\setminus\ma
thbb{R}$, not in $k$ itself.
