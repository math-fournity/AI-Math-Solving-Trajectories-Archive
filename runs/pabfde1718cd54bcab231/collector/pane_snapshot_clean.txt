## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. For every finitely generated projective $A$-module $P$, there ex
ists an exact sequence
$$0 \to K \to A^{m} \to P \to 0$$
with $K$ finitely generated (in fact finitely generated projective).

---

**Construction and proof.**

Since $P$ is finitely generated, there exist elements $p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\in P$ g
enerating $P$ as an $A$-module. These define a surjective $A$-module homomorphis
m
$$\pi:A^{m}\longrightarrow P,\qquad (a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\longmapsto a_{1}p_{1}+\c
dots+a_{m}p_{m}.$$
Let $K=\ker(\pi)$. Then by definition we have the exact sequence
$$0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow A^{m}\xrightarrow{\;\pi\;}P\longrightarrow 0
. \tag{$\ast$}$$

It remains to show that $K$ is finitely generated. Since $P$ is **projective**,
the surjection $\pi$ splits: there exists an $A$-linear section $s:P\to A^{m}$ w
ith $\pi\circ s=\mathrm{id}_{P}$. The splitting lemma then yields a direct-sum d
ecomposition
$$A^{m}\;\cong\;K\oplus P,\qquad x=\bigl(x-s(\pi(x))\bigr)+s(\pi(x)).$$

Hence $K$ is a direct summand of the free module $A^{m}$. A direct summand of a
projective module is projective, so $K$ is projective. Moreover, $K$ is a direct
 summand of the finitely generated module $A^{m}$, hence $K$ is finitely generat
ed (it is a quotient of $A^{m}$ via the projection $A^{m}\to K$).

Thus the exact sequence $(\ast)$ has all the required properties: $A^{m}$ is fre
e of finite rank, $P$ is finitely generated projective, and $K=\ker(\pi)$ is fin
itely generated (indeed finitely generated projective). $\blacksquare$
