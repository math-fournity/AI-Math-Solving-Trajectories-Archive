Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every compact operator on $
\ell_2$ admits a nontrivial reducing subspace.

**Answer: No.** I will construct a compact operator on $\ell_2$ with no nontrivi
al reducing subspace.

---

## Proof

Let $\{e_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ be the standard orthonormal basis of $\ell_2$. Choose
 a sequence $(\alpha_n)$ with $\alpha_n > 0$, $\alpha_n \to 0$, and all $\alpha_
n$ distinct — for instance, $\alpha_n = 2^{-n}$.

Define $T: \ell_2 \to \ell_2$ by
$$Te_n = \alpha_n\, e_{n+1}, \quad n \ge 1,$$
extended linearly and continuously. This is a **weighted unilateral shift**.

### Step 1: $T$ is compact

Define the finite-rank truncation $T_N$ by $T_N e_n = \alpha_n e_{n+1}$ for $n \
le N$ and $T_N e_n = 0$ for $n > N$. Each $T_N$ has finite rank (its range is $\
operatorname{span}\{e_2, \dots, e_{N+1}\}$). Since
$$\|T - T_N\| = \sup_{n > N} \alpha_n = \alpha_{N+1} \to 0,$$
$T$ is the operator-norm limit of finite-rank operators, hence **compact**.

### Step 2: $T^*T$ is diagonal with distinct eigenvalues

We have $T^* e_{n+1} = \alpha_n\, e_n$ for $n \ge 1$ and $T^* e_1 = 0$. Therefor
e
$$T^*T\, e_n = T^*(\alpha_n e_{n+1}) = \alpha_n^2\, e_n.$$
So $T^*T$ is diagonal in the basis $\{e_n\}$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_n = \alph
a_n^2 = 4^{-n}$. These eigenvalues are **all distinct and positive**, each with
a **one-dimensional eigenspace** $\operatorname{span}\{e_n\}$.

### Step 3: $T$ has no nontrivial reducing subspace

Suppose $M \subseteq \ell_2$ is a closed subspace that **reduces** $T$ (i.e., $T
M \subseteq M$ and $T^*M \subseteq M$). Let $P$ be the orthogonal projection ont
o $M$. Then $P$ commutes with both $T$ and $T^*$, hence commutes with $T^*T$.

**Claim:** $P$ is diagonal in the basis $\{e_n\}$.

Since $P$ commutes with $T^*T$, for each $n$:
$$T^*T(Pe_n) = P(T^*T e_n) = P(\lambda_n e_n) = \lambda_n\, Pe_n.$$
So $Pe_n$ is an eigenvector of $T^*T$ with eigenvalue $\lambda_n$. Since the eig
enspace for $\lambda_n$ is one-dimensional ($\operatorname{span}\{e_n\}$), we ge
t $Pe_n = c_n\, e_n$ for some scalar $c_n$. As $P$ is an orthogonal projection,
$c_n \in \{0, 1\}$. Setting $S = \{n : c_n = 1\}$, we have
$$P = \sum_{n \in S} |e_n\rangle\langle e_n|.$$

**Claim:** $S$ is either $\emptyset$ or $\mathbb{N}$.

Since $P$ commutes with $T$, compare $PTe_n$ and $TPe_n$:
$$PTe_n = P(\alpha_n e_{n+1}) = \alpha_n\, \mathbf{1}_S(n+1)\, e_{n+1},$$
$$TPe_n = T(\mathbf{1}_S(n)\, e_n) = \alpha_n\, \mathbf{1}_S(n)\, e_{n+1}.$$
Equality gives $\mathbf{1}_S(n+1) = \mathbf{1}_S(n)$ for all $n \ge 1$. By induc
tion, either all indicators are $0$ ($S = \emptyset$, $M = \{0\}$) or all are $1
$ ($S = \mathbb{N}$, $M = \ell_2$).

### Conclusion

The operator $T$ is compact (Step 1) and has **no nontrivial reducing subspace**
 (Step 3): the only reducing subspaces are $\{0\}$ and $\ell_2$. Therefore, **no
t every compact operator in $\mathcal{B}(\ell_2)$ has a reducing subspace**. $\b
lacksquare$
