Looking at this problem, I need to find the area of an isosceles triangle ABC (A
B = AC) with circumradius 2, where a circle Ω of radius 1 is tangent to the circ
umcircle Γ and to sides AB, AC.

## Setup

Place the circumcircle Γ at the origin O = (0,0) with radius R = 2. Since AB = A
C, the triangle is symmetric about the y-axis. Put A = (0, 2) at the top.

Let the half-angle at A be θ, so ∠A = 2θ. By the inscribed angle theorem, B and
C are at:
- B = (−2 sin 2θ, 2 cos 2θ), C = (2 sin 2θ, 2 cos 2θ)

**Side lengths** (via law of sines with 2R = 4):
- AB = AC = 4 cos θ, BC = 4 sin 2θ

**Area** = ½ · AB · AC · sin(∠A) = ½ · (4 cos θ)² · sin 2θ = **16 sin θ cos³ θ**

## Locating Circle Ω

Since Ω is tangent to both AB and AC, its center lies on the angle bisector of ∠
A — the y-axis — at (0, y₀).

**Tangency with Γ:** Ω (radius 1) lies inside Γ (radius 2), so internal tangency
 gives |y₀| = R − r = 1, meaning y₀ = 1 or y₀ = −1.

**Tangency with AB:** The line AB has equation x sin θ − y cos θ + 2 cos θ = 0.
The distance from (0, y₀) to this line is:
$$\frac{|\cos\theta(2 - y_0)|}{1} = 1$$

**Case y₀ = 1:** cos θ · 1 = 1 → cos θ = 1 → θ = 0. Degenerate. ✗

**Case y₀ = −1:** 3 cos θ = 1 → **cos θ = 1/3**. ✓

## Validity Check

With cos θ = 1/3: cos 2θ = 2/9 − 1 = −7/9, so the base BC is at height 2(−7/9) =
 −14/9 ≈ −1.556. The center y₀ = −1 is above the base (−1 > −1.556), so it's ins
ide the triangle.

The foot of perpendicular from (0, −1) to line AB lands at parameter t = 3/4 alo

ts extension). ✓

I also verified the external-bisector case and external-tangency cases — all eit

## Computing the Area

With cos θ = 1/3:
- sin θ = √(1 − 1/9) = 2√2/3

$$\text{Area} = 16 \cdot \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{27} = \frac{32\sqrt{
2}}{81}$$

## Final Answer

The area is $\frac{a\sqrt{b}}{c} = \frac{32\sqrt{2}}{81}$, giving:
- a = 32, b = 2 (squarefree), c = 81, gcd(32, 81) = 1 ✓
