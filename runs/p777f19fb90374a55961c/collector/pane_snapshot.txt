The Poincaré–Bendixson Theorem concerns the ω-limit set of a trajectory that is
confined to a **compact, positively invariant region** of the plane (a "trapping
 region") containing only finitely many critical points. Under these hypotheses
the theorem concludes that the ω-limit set must be either a critical point, a cl
osed orbit, or a graphic (a union of trajectories and critical points).

For the system **x′ = x, y′ = y** the general solution is

$$x(t) = x_0 e^t, \qquad y(t) = y_0 e^t.$$

These solutions indeed exist for all t ∈ ℝ, and there are clearly no closed traj
ectories (every nonzero solution moves strictly outward along a ray, with |(x(t)
,y(t))| → ∞ as t → +∞ and → 0 as t → −∞).

The theorem fails to apply for the following reason.

**There is no compact, positively invariant region that contains a trajectory.**

Suppose K ⊂ ℝ² were compact and positively invariant, and suppose a trajectory γ
(t) = (x₀eᵗ, y₀eᵗ) with (x₀,y₀) ≠ (0,0) remained in K for all t ≥ 0. Since K is
compact it is bounded, so there is some M with |(x,y)| ≤ M for all (x,y) ∈ K. Bu
t |γ(t)| = |(x₀,y₀)|eᵗ → ∞ as t → +∞, contradicting γ(t) ∈ K. Hence **no nontriv
ial trajectory can be trapped in a compact set**.

The only trajectory that stays bounded forward in time is the equilibrium at the
 origin, whose ω-limit set is just {0} — a trivial case that yields no closed or
bit and to which the interesting conclusion of the theorem (existence of a perio
dic orbit) is not directed.

Thus the essential hypothesis of the Poincaré–Bendixson Theorem — the existence
of a **bounded, positively invariant (trapping) region** containing the forward
orbit — is **not satisfied** by this system. The solutions exist for all time, b
ut they escape to infinity rather than being confined, so the theorem's mechanis
m (which forces the ω-limit set to be a critical point, closed orbit, or graphic
 inside a compact set) has nothing to act on. This is precisely why the theorem
does not apply, even though solutions are global and no closed trajectories exis
t.
