**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

We show that the point set $P$ can be preprocessed in $O(n \log n)$ time into a
data structure of $O(n)$ space that answers half-plane emptiness queries ("does
the query half-plane contain at least one point of $P$?") in $O(\log n)$ time.

**Duality setup.** Use the standard point-line duality that maps a point $p = (p
_x, p_y)$ to the line $p^* : y = p_x \cdot x - p_y$. Every point of $P$ yields a
 distinct dual line (distinct $p_x$ values give distinct slopes, by the general-
position assumption).

**Classification of query half-planes.** Any closed half-plane can be written as
 $ax + by + c \ge 0$. There are three cases:

1. **$b > 0$:** rewrite as $y \ge (-a/b)\,x - c/b$, an *upper* half-plane with s
lope $m = -a/b$ and intercept $d = -c/b$.
2. **$b < 0$:** rewrite as $y \le (-a/b)\,x - c/b$, a *lower* half-plane.
3. **$b = 0$ (vertical):** rewrite as $x \ge -c/a$ or $x \le -c/a$.

This classification is $O(1)$.

**Case 1 — Upper half-plane $y \ge mx + d$.** A point $p = (p_x, p_y) \in P$ lie
s in this half-plane iff
$$p_y \ge m\,p_x + d \iff p_x \cdot m - p_y \le -d \iff p^*(m) \le -d,$$
where $p^*(m) = p_x \cdot m - p_y$ is the value of the dual line $p^*$ evaluated
 at $x = m$. Therefore the half-plane contains some point of $P$ iff
$$\min_{p \in P} p^*(m) \le -d,$$
i.e., iff the **lower envelope** of the dual lines, evaluated at $x = m$, is at
most $-d$.

The lower envelope (pointwise minimum) of $n$ lines is a piecewise-linear concav
e function determined by at most $n$ lines. It can be computed in $O(n \log n)$
time (sort by slope, then a standard convex-hull-trick / monotonic-stack sweep).
 Evaluating it at a query abscissa $m$ is done by binary search over the $O(n)$
breakpoints in $O(\log n)$ time.

**Case 2 — Lower half-plane $y \le mx + d$.** By the symmetric argument, $p$ lie
s in the half-plane iff $p^*(m) \ge -d$, so the half-plane is non-empty iff the
**upper envelope** (pointwise maximum) of the dual lines at $x = m$ is at least
$-d$. The upper envelope is computed and queried identically: $O(n \log n)$ prep
rocessing, $O(\log n)$ query.

**Case 3 — Vertical half-plane.** For $x \ge c$: non-empty iff $\max_{p \in P} p
_x \ge c$, computed in $O(1)$ from a precomputed maximum. For $x \le c$: non-emp
ty iff $\min_{p \in P} p_x \le c$, likewise $O(1)$.

**Data structure.** Store:
- the lower envelope of the $n$ dual lines (sorted breakpoint array + line segme
nts),
- the upper envelope of the $n$ dual lines,
- $\min p_x$ and $\max p_x$.

Total space $O(n)$; total preprocessing $O(n \log n)$.

**Query.** Given a query half-plane, classify it in $O(1)$, then:
- upper half-plane → evaluate lower envelope at $m$, compare to $-d$: $O(\log n)
$,
- lower half-plane → evaluate upper envelope at $m$, compare to $-d$: $O(\log n)
$,
- vertical → compare $c$ to precomputed min/max: $O(1)$.

In every case the query time is $O(\log n)$.

**Role of general position.** Distinct $x$-coordinates guarantee distinct slopes
 among the dual lines (no parallel dual lines), and "no three collinear" guarant
ees no three dual lines are concurrent, so the envelopes have no degeneracies —
each envelope segment is unambiguously defined and the binary search is clean. T
hese assumptions simplify but are not essential; degeneracies can be handled by
standard symbolic perturbation.

**Conclusion.** Yes — a data structure with $O(n)$ space, $O(n \log n)$ preproce
ssing, and $O(\log n)$ query time for half-plane emptiness on $P$ exists, obtain
ed by point-line duality and envelope queries.
