## Proof

We wish to minimize $\operatorname{tr}(AA^T)$ over all $n \times n$ non-singular
 matrices $A$ with integer entries.

**Key identity.** For any matrix $A = (a_{ij})$,
$$\operatorname{tr}(AA^T) = \sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij}^2,$$
since the $(i,i)$-entry of $AA^T$ is $\sum_{j} a_{ij}^2$. Thus $\operatorname{tr
}(AA^T)$ equals the sum of squares of all entries of $A$.

**Lower bound.** Since $A$ is non-singular, no row of $A$ can be identically zer
o (a zero row would force $\det A = 0$). Therefore every row $i$ contains at lea
st one entry with $|a_{ij}|\ge 1$. Since all entries are integers, every non-zer
o entry satisfies $a_{ij}^2 \ge 1$. Hence each row contributes at least $1$ to t
he sum $\sum_{i,j} a_{ij}^2$, giving
$$\operatorname{tr}(AA^T) = \sum_{i,j} a_{ij}^2 \ge n.$$

**Achievability.** The identity matrix $A = I_n$ has integer entries, is non-sin
gular with $\det(I_n)=1$, and
$$\operatorname{tr}(I_n I_n^T) = \operatorname{tr}(I_n) = n,$$
since its entries are $0$ and $1$, with exactly $n$ entries equal to $1$.

**Conclusion.** The lower bound $n$ is attained by the identity matrix, so the m
inimum value is

$$\boxed{n}.$$
