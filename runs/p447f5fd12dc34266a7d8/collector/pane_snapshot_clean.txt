# Proof

**Claim.** Let $M$ be a smooth $n$-dimensional manifold and $p \in M$. The tange
nt space $T_p M$ is isomorphic (as a real vector space) to $\mathbb{R}^n$. The i
somorphism, however, is not canonical: it depends on a choice of basis, e.g. one
 induced by a coordinate chart.

---

## 1. $T_p M$ is a real vector space of dimension $n$

Recall the standard construction. Let $M$ be a smooth manifold of dimension $n$,
 and $p \in M$. The tangent space $T_p M$ can be defined as the set of derivatio
ns at $p$:

$$T_p M := \{ D : C^\infty(M) \to \mathbb{R} \mid D \text{ is $\mathbb{R}$-linea
r and satisfies the Leibniz rule } D(fg) = D(f)\,g(p) + f(p)\,D(g) \}.$$

Equipped with pointwise addition and scalar multiplication,
$$(D_1 + D_2)(f) := D_1(f) + D_2(f), \qquad (\lambda D)(f) := \lambda\, D(f),$$
$T_p M$ is a real vector space. (The zero derivation is the additive identity; t
he Leibniz rule and linearity are preserved under these operations.)

To determine its dimension, choose any coordinate chart $(U, \varphi)$ about $p$
, with coordinate functions $x^1, \dots, x^n$. The coordinate vectors

$$\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right|_p, \quad i = 1, \dots, n,$$

defined by $\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right|_p (f) := \frac{\partial}{
\partial x^i}(f \circ \varphi^{-1})\big|_{\varphi(p)}$, form a basis of $T_p M$:

- **Spanning.** For any derivation $D \in T_p M$, one shows (using a Taylor expa
nsion of $f$ in local coordinates together with the fact that $D$ annihilates co
nstants) that
$$D = \sum_{i=1}^n D(x^i)\,\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right|_p.$$
So the coordinate vectors span $T_p M$.

- **Linear independence.** If $\sum_i c^i \left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\ri
ght|_p = 0$, applying this derivation to the coordinate function $x^j$ gives $c^
j = 0$ for each $j$.

Hence $\dim_\mathbb{R} T_p M = n$.

## 2. Every $n$-dimensional real vector space is isomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^n$

This is a standard theorem of linear algebra. Let $V$ be any $n$-dimensional rea
l vector space and let $\{e_1, \dots, e_n\}$ be any basis. The map

$$\Phi : V \to \mathbb{R}^n, \qquad \Phi\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^n v^i e_i\right) = (v
^1, \dots, v^n)$$

is a linear bijection (it is linear by construction, injective because bases are
 linearly independent, and surjective because bases span). Hence $V \cong \mathb
b{R}^n$ as real vector spaces.

## 3. Conclusion: $T_p M \cong \mathbb{R}^n$

Applying Section 2 to $V = T_p M$ (which has dimension $n$ by Section 1), we con
clude:

$$\boxed{T_p M \cong \mathbb{R}^n \quad \text{as real vector spaces.}}$$

Concretely, any coordinate chart $(U, \varphi)$ about $p$ produces an explicit i
somorphism by sending each derivation to its coordinate components:
$$D \;\longmapsto\; \bigl(D(x^1), \dots, D(x^n)\bigr) \in \mathbb{R}^n,$$
with inverse $(v^1,\dots,v^n) \mapsto \sum_i v^i \left.\frac{\partial}{\partial
x^i}\right|_p$.

## 4. Important caveat: the isomorphism is not canonical

The isomorphism $T_p M \cong \mathbb{R}^n$ is **not natural/canonical**. It depe
nds on the choice of basis, which in turn depends on the choice of coordinate ch
art (or more generally on any choice of frame). Different charts yield different
 isomorphisms, related by the Jacobian of the transition map. There is no basis-
independent isomorphism $T_p M \to \mathbb{R}^n$ unless extra structure is suppl
ied (such as a Riemannian metric providing an orthonormal frame, or a global tri
vialization of the tangent bundle, which exists only for parallelizable manifold
s).

This is why, although $T_p M$ is *abstractly* isomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^n$ at ev
ery point, one cannot identify $T_p M$ with $\mathbb{R}^n$ in a coordinate-free
manner. The tangent *bundle* $TM$ is generally not globally isomorphic to $M \ti
mes \mathbb{R}^n$ (i.e., not generally trivial), even though each fiber $T_p M$
is fiberwise isomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^n$.

## 5. Final answer

**Yes.** The tangent space $T_p M$ of a smooth $n$-dimensional manifold $M$ at a
ny point $p$ is always isomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^n$ as a real vector space, sinc
e it is an $n$-dimensional real vector space and every such space is isomorphic
to $\mathbb{R}^n$. The isomorphism, however, is not canonical — it requires a ch
oice of basis (e.g., from a coordinate chart), and no coordinate-independent ide
ntification exists in general.
