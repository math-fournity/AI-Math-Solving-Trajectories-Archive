## Proof

**Claim.** The statement is **true**: the quotient map $\pi: R \to M$ restricts
to an isomorphism $I_j \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} M$ for some $j$.

---

**Step 1. Each $I_j$ is a simple left $R$-module.**

$I_j$ consists of all matrices in $R = M_n(D)$ that are zero outside the $j$-th
column. As a left $R$-module, $I_j \cong D^n$ (column vectors) via the map sendi
ng a matrix in $I_j$ to its $j$-th column, with $R$ acting by left matrix multip
lication.

Since $D$ is a division ring, $M_n(D)$ acts transitively on $D^n \setminus \{0\}
$: given any two nonzero column vectors $v, w \in D^n$, there exists $A \in M_n(
D)$ with $Av = w$ (extend $v$ to a basis using the fact that $D$ is a division r
ing, then map that basis to one containing $w$). Hence $D^n$ has no nontrivial p
roper $R$-submodule: any nonzero submodule contains a nonzero vector, and by tra
nsitivity it contains every nonzero vector, hence is all of $D^n$. Therefore $I_
j$ is simple. ∎

**Step 2. $R$ is semisimple as a left module over itself.**

By hypothesis $R = \bigoplus_{j=1}^{n} I_j$, and each $I_j$ is simple by Step 1.
 Thus ${}_R R$ is a direct sum of simple modules, i.e., semisimple. ∎

**Step 3. The restriction $\pi|_{I_j}$ is either zero or an isomorphism.**

Let $\pi: R \to M$ be the quotient map, a surjective $R$-module homomorphism. Fo
r each $j$, the image $\pi(I_j)$ is an $R$-submodule of $M$. Since $M$ is simple
, $\pi(I_j) \in \{0, M\}$.

- If $\pi(I_j) = 0$, the restriction is zero.
- If $\pi(I_j) = M$, then $\pi|_{I_j}: I_j \to M$ is surjective. Its kernel is a
 submodule of the simple module $I_j$, hence is $0$ or $I_j$. It cannot be $I_j$
 (the image is $M \neq 0$), so $\ker = 0$, and $\pi|_{I_j}$ is an isomorphism. ∎

**Step 4. Some restriction is an isomorphism.**

By surjectivity of $\pi$:
$$M = \pi(R) = \pi\!\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n} I_j\right) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} \pi(I_
j).$$
If $\pi(I_j) = 0$ for every $j$, then $M = 0$, contradicting the convention that
 a simple module is nonzero. Hence $\pi(I_j) = M$ for at least one $j$, and by S
tep 3 the map $\pi|_{I_j}: I_j \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} M$ is an isomorphism. ∎

---

**Conclusion.** The quotient map $\pi: R \to M$ restricts to an isomorphism $I_j
 \cong M$ for some $j$. The statement is **true**. This is the standard conseque
nce of the semisimplicity of $R = M_n(D)$ as a left module over itself: every si
mple quotient of a semisimple module is isomorphic to one of its simple summands
.
