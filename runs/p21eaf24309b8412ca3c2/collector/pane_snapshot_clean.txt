## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{|z|=4} \tan z\, dz$ using the residue theorem.

**Poles of $\tan z$.** Since $\tan z = \sin z / \cos z$, the poles are the zeros
 of $\cos z$, which occur exactly at
$$z = \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi, \quad k \in \mathbb{Z}$$
(all zeros of cosine are real). Numerically:
- $z = \pi/2 \approx 1.571$, $|z| < 4$ ✓
- $z = -\pi/2 \approx -1.571$, $|z| < 4$ ✓
- $z = 3\pi/2 \approx 4.712$, $|z| > 4$ ✗
- $z = -3\pi/2 \approx -4.712$, $|z| > 4$ ✗

So exactly **two** simple poles lie inside $|z|=4$: $z_1 = \pi/2$ and $z_2 = -\p
i/2$.

**Residues.** At each simple pole $z_0$ (where $\cos z_0 = 0$ and $\sin z_0 \neq
 0$):
$$\operatorname{Res}(\tan z, z_0) = \frac{\sin z_0}{(\cos z)'\big|_{z_0}} = \fra
c{\sin z_0}{-\sin z_0} = -1.$$

Hence
$$\operatorname{Res}(\tan z, \pi/2) = -1, \qquad \operatorname{Res}(\tan z, -\pi
/2) = -1.$$

**Applying the residue theorem** (positively oriented contour):
$$\int_{|z|=4} \tan z\, dz = 2\pi i \sum \operatorname{Res} = 2\pi i\,(-1 - 1) =
 -4\pi i.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{|z|=4} \tan z\, dz = -4\pi i}$$
