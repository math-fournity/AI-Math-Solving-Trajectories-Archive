## Proof

Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix with $\operatorname{tr}(A) = 0$. The space of
such matrices is the traceless subspace $\mathfrak{sl}(n, \mathbb{R}) \subset \m
athbb{R}^{n \times n}$, which has dimension $n^2 - 1$ (the trace condition $\sum
_i A_{ii} = 0$ imposes one linear constraint on the $n^2$-dimensional space of a
ll matrices).

**Reformulation as linear functionals.** Each measurement $\vec{h}^T A \vec{x} =
 \vec{h}^T A \vec{x}$ is a linear functional in $A$. Via the Frobenius inner pro
duct $\langle M, A\rangle_F = \operatorname{tr}(M^T A)$, we can write:
$$\vec{h}^T A \vec{x} = \langle \vec{h}\,\vec{x}^T,\; A \rangle_F,$$
so each measurement corresponds to the rank-1 matrix $\vec{h}\,\vec{x}^T$. The m
easurement, restricted to traceless matrices, depends only on the class of $\vec
{h}\,\vec{x}^T$ in the quotient $\mathbb{R}^{n \times n} / \mathbb{R}\cdot I$ (s
ince $\langle I, A\rangle_F = \operatorname{tr}(A) = 0$ for traceless $A$).

---

### Lower Bound: $n^2 - 1$ measurements are necessary

The space $V = \{A \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n} : \operatorname{tr}(A)=0\}$ has di
mension $n^2 - 1$. Each linear measurement $\ell_k: V \to \mathbb{R}$ is a linea
r functional. If $\ell_k \neq 0$, its kernel has dimension $\dim(V) - 1$. With $
m$ measurements, the combined map $\Phi = (\ell_1, \ldots, \ell_m): V \to \mathb
b{R}^m$ has kernel of dimension at least $\dim(V) - m$. For $\Phi$ to be injecti
ve (uniquely identifying $A$), we need $\ker(\Phi) = \{0\}$, hence:
$$\dim(V) - m \leq 0 \implies m \geq n^2 - 1.$$

---

### Upper Bound: $n^2 - 1$ measurements suffice

We exhibit an explicit set of $n^2 - 1$ measurements. Let $e_1, \ldots, e_n$ be
the standard basis of $\mathbb{R}^n$. Consider the measurements:
$$A\,e_j \cdot e_i = e_i^T A\, e_j = A_{ij}, \quad \text{for all } (i,j) \in \{1
,\ldots,n\}^2 \setminus \{(n,n)\}.$$

There are exactly $n^2 - 1$ such pairs. These measurements directly reveal every
 entry of $A$ except $A_{nn}$. The trace condition then determines the remaining
 entry:
$$A_{nn} = -\sum_{i=1}^{n-1} A_{ii}.$$

Thus all $n^2$ entries of $A$ are determined, so $A$ is uniquely identified amon
g traceless matrices.

**Verification of linear independence.** The corresponding rank-1 matrices $\{E_
{ij} = e_i e_j^T : (i,j) \neq (n,n)\}$ are linearly independent modulo $I$: if $
\sum_{(i,j)\neq(n,n)} c_{ij}\, E_{ij} = \lambda I$ for some $\lambda$, then exam
ining the $(n,n)$-entry gives $0 = \lambda$, so $\lambda = 0$, and then all $c_{
ij} = 0$ by the linear independence of the $E_{ij}$ in $\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$.
 Hence the $n^2 - 1$ functionals are linearly independent on $V$, confirming inj
ectivity.

---

### Conclusion

The lower bound and upper bound coincide. The minimum number of linear measureme
nts required to uniquely identify a traceless $n \times n$ matrix is:

$$\boxed{n^2 - 1}$$
