## Solution

**Notation.** Let $s(n)$ denote the sum of the decimal digits of $n$. We are giv
en $A = s(4444^{4444})$, $B = s(A)$, and must compute $s(B)$.

**Step 1: Bound $A$.**

Since $4444 < 10000 = 10^4$,
$$4444^{4444} < (10^4)^{4444} = 10^{17776}.$$
So $4444^{4444}$ has at most $17776$ digits, giving
$$A = s(4444^{4444}) \le 17776 \cdot 9 = 159984.$$

**Step 2: Bound $B$.**

$A \le 159984$, so $A$ is at most a 6-digit number. Among all integers $\le 1599
84$, the maximum digit sum is attained at $99999$, giving $s(99999) = 45$. Hence
$$B = s(A) \le 45.$$

**Step 3: Bound $s(B)$.**

Since $B \le 45$, the maximum digit sum of an integer $\le 45$ is $s(39) = 12$.
Thus
$$s(B) \le 12.$$

**Step 4: Use the congruence $s(n) \equiv n \pmod 9$.**

The digit-sum function satisfies $s(n) \equiv n \pmod 9$, so iterating:
$$s(B) \equiv B \equiv A \equiv 4444^{4444} \pmod 9.$$

Compute $4444 \pmod 9$: $4+4+4+4 = 16$, $1+6 = 7$, so $4444 \equiv 7 \pmod 9$. T
he powers of $7$ modulo $9$ cycle with period $3$:
$$7^1 \equiv 7,\quad 7^2 \equiv 49 \equiv 4,\quad 7^3 \equiv 28 \equiv 1 \pmod 9
.$$
Since $4444 \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ (as $4444 = 3\cdot 1481 + 1$),
$$4444^{4444} \equiv 7^{4444} \equiv 7^1 \equiv 7 \pmod 9.$$

Therefore
$$s(B) \equiv 7 \pmod 9.$$

**Step 5: Conclude.**

We have $s(B) \le 12$ and $s(B) \equiv 7 \pmod 9$. The positive integers $\le 12
$ that are $\equiv 7 \pmod 9$ are exactly $\{7\}$ (the next would be $16 > 12$).
 Since $4444^{4444} > 0$, we have $A, B, s(B) \ge 1$, so $s(B) = 7$.

$$\boxed{s(B) = 7}$$
