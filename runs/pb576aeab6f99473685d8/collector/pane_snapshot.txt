## Solution

**Parametrize the curve.** The curve $C$ is the intersection of $x^2+z^2=1$ and
$y=x^2$. Using the cylinder $x^2+z^2=1$, set
$$x=\cos t,\quad z=\sin t,\quad y=\cos^2 t,\qquad t\in[0,2\pi].$$
So $\mathbf{r}(t)=(\cos t,\,\cos^2 t,\,\sin t)$.

**Compute the speed.**
$$\mathbf{r}'(t)=(-\sin t,\,-2\cos t\sin t,\,\cos t).$$
$$|\mathbf{r}'(t)|^2=\sin^2 t+4\cos^2 t\sin^2 t+\cos^2 t=1+4\cos^2 t\sin^2 t.$$
Since $x=\cos t$ and $z=\sin t$, this gives
$$|\mathbf{r}'(t)|^2=1+4x^2z^2,\qquad\text{i.e.}\quad |\mathbf{r}'(t)|=\sqrt{1+4
x^2z^2}.$$

**The integrand equals the speed.** The line integral becomes
$$\int_C\sqrt{1+4x^2z^2}\,ds=\int_0^{2\pi}\sqrt{1+4x^2z^2}\;|\mathbf{r}'(t)|\,dt
=\int_0^{2\pi}\bigl(1+4\cos^2 t\sin^2 t\bigr)\,dt.$$

**Evaluate.** Using $4\cos^2 t\sin^2 t=\sin^2(2t)=\dfrac{1-\cos 4t}{2}$:
$$\int_0^{2\pi}\!\left(1+\frac{1-\cos 4t}{2}\right)dt=\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{3-\cos
4t}{2}\,dt=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl[3t-\frac{\sin 4t}{4}\Bigr]_0^{2\pi}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot
 6\pi=3\pi.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_C\sqrt{1+4x^2z^2}\,ds=3\pi.}$$
