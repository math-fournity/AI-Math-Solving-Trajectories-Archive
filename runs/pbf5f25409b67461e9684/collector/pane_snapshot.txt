# Proof

**Theorem (Lebesgue's criterion).** Let $S \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ be Jordan measu
rable and let $f: S \to \mathbb{R}$ be bounded. Then $f$ is Riemann integrable o
ver $S$ if and only if the set $D$ of its discontinuities has Lebesgue measure z
ero in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

---

## Setup and definitions

Since $S$ is Jordan measurable, it is bounded, so there exists a closed box $B \
supset S$. Define the **zero extension**
$$
\tilde f(x) = \begin{cases} f(x), & x \in S,\\ 0, & x \notin S.\end{cases}
$$
The Riemann integral of $f$ over $S$ is, by definition,
$$
\int_S f \;:=\; \int_B \tilde f,
$$
and $f$ is Riemann integrable over $S$ iff $\tilde f$ is Riemann integrable over
 $B$.

Let $D_f \subset S$ be the set of discontinuities of $f$ (relative to $S$), and
let $D_{\tilde f} \subset B$ be the set of discontinuities of $\tilde f$ (relati
ve to $B$).

**Key claim.** $D_{\tilde f} \subseteq D_f \cup \partial S$.

*Proof of claim.* Suppose $x \in B$ is a discontinuity of $\tilde f$.

- If $x \in \operatorname{int}(S)$: near $x$, $\tilde f = f$, so $\tilde f$ is d
iscontinuous at $x$ iff $f$ is discontinuous at $x$, i.e., $x \in D_f$.
- If $x \in \operatorname{int}(\mathbb{R}^n \setminus S)$: near $x$, $\tilde f \
equiv 0$, so $\tilde f$ is continuous at $x$. Contradiction.
- If $x \in \partial S$: then $x \in \partial S$.

So $D_{\tilde f} \subseteq D_f \cup \partial S$. $\square$

Since $S$ is Jordan measurable, $\partial S$ has Lebesgue measure zero (this is
a standard characterization: $S$ is Jordan measurable iff $\partial S$ has measu
re zero). Therefore
$$
m(D_{\tilde f}) \le m(D_f) + m(\partial S) = m(D_f) + 0 = m(D_f),
$$
and conversely $D_f \subseteq D_{\tilde f}$ (since on $\operatorname{int}(S)$ th
ey agree, and $D_f \subset \overline S$), so in fact
$$
m(D_{\tilde f}) = m(D_f) \quad\text{— both are zero or both nonzero, as } m(\par
tial S)=0.
$$
More precisely: $D_{\tilde f} \subseteq D_f \cup \partial S$ and $D_f \subseteq
D_{\tilde f} \cup \partial S$, so $D_{\tilde f}$ has measure zero $\iff$ $D_f$ h
as measure zero, using $m(\partial S)=0$.

Thus it suffices to prove the classical Lebesgue criterion for $\tilde f$ on the
 box $B$.

---

## Classical Lebesgue criterion (box case)

**Lemma.** Let $g: B \to \mathbb{R}$ be bounded, $B$ a closed box. Then $g$ is R
iemann integrable over $B$ iff the set $D_g$ of its discontinuities has Lebesgue
 measure zero.

### Proof of Lemma

**Oscillation.** For $x \in B$, define the oscillation
$$
\omega_g(x) = \inf_{\delta > 0} \sup\{|g(y)-g(z)| : y,z \in B,\; \|y-x\|,\|z-x\|
 < \delta\}.
$$
Then $g$ is continuous at $x$ iff $\omega_g(x) = 0$. So
$$
D_g = \{x \in B : \omega_g(x) > 0\} = \bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty} \{x \in B : \omega_
g(x) \ge 1/k\}.$$
Denote $D_g^{(k)} = \{x : \omega_g(x) \ge 1/k\}$; these are closed sets (oscilla
tion is upper semicontinuous).

#### ($\Rightarrow$) Integrable $\Rightarrow$ $m(D_g) = 0$.

Let $\varepsilon > 0$. Since $g$ is Riemann integrable, there exists a partition
 $P$ of $B$ into sub-boxes $\{B_i\}$ such that
$$
U(P,g) - L(P,g) = \sum_i \big(\sup_{B_i} g - \inf_{B_i} g\big) \operatorname{vol
}(B_i) < \varepsilon.
$$
Let $I$ be the set of indices $i$ with $\operatorname{int}(B_i) \cap D_g^{(k)} \
ne \emptyset$. For such $i$, $\sup_{B_i} g - \inf_{B_i} g \ge 1/k$ (since some p
oint in $B_i$ has oscillation $\ge 1/k$, and the sup–inf over $B_i$ dominates th
e local oscillation). Hence
$$
\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i \in I} \operatorname{vol}(B_i) \le \sum_{i \in I} \big(\sup_{
B_i} g - \inf_{B_i} g\big)\operatorname{vol}(B_i) \le U(P,g)-L(P,g) < \varepsilo
n.
$$
So $\sum_{i\in I}\operatorname{vol}(B_i) < k\varepsilon$. The set $D_g^{(k)}$ is
 covered by the (finitely many) boxes $\{B_i\}_{i\in I}$ together with the bound
aries of all $B_i$ (which have total measure zero). Therefore $D_g^{(k)}$ can be
 covered by boxes of total volume $< k\varepsilon$. Since $\varepsilon$ is arbit
rary, $m^*(D_g^{(k)}) = 0$, so $m(D_g^{(k)})=0$. Then
$$
m(D_g) \le \sum_{k=1}^\infty m(D_g^{(k)}) = 0.
$$

#### ($\Leftarrow$) $m(D_g) = 0$ $\Rightarrow$ integrable.

Let $M = \sup_B g - \inf_B g$ (finite, since $g$ bounded). Let $\varepsilon > 0$
. For each $k$, $D_g^{(k)} \subseteq D_g$ has measure zero, so it can be covered
 by countably many open boxes of total volume $< \varepsilon/(2M)$. Since $D_g^{
(k)}$ is closed and bounded (compact), finitely many of them suffice; their unio
n $U$ is open with $\operatorname{vol}(U) < \varepsilon/(2M)$.

The set $K = B \setminus U$ is compact, and $\omega_g(x) < 1/k$ for all $x \in K
$. By compactness and the definition of oscillation, there exists $\delta > 0$ s
uch that for every $x \in K$ and every sub-box $B' \ni x$ with diameter $<\delta
$, $\sup_{B'} g - \inf_{B'} g < 1/k$. (Each $x \in K$ has a neighborhood where t
he oscillation is $< 1/k$; extract a finite subcover and take a Lebesgue number.
)

Now take a partition $P$ of $B$ into sub-boxes of diameter $<\delta$. Split the
sub-boxes into:
- **Type A:** $B_i \subseteq U$ (interior meets $U$). Their total volume $\le \o
peratorname{vol}(U) < \varepsilon/(2M)$.
- **Type B:** $B_i \cap K \ne \emptyset$ (so $B_i \not\subseteq U$, meaning $B_i
$ meets $K$). For these, $\sup_{B_i} g - \inf_{B_i} g < 1/k$.

Then
$$
U(P,g) - L(P,g) = \sum_{\text{A}}(\cdots)\operatorname{vol}(B_i) + \sum_{\text{B
}}(\cdots)\operatorname{vol}(B_i)
\le M \cdot \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} + \frac{1}{k}\operatorname{vol}(B).
$$
Choose $k$ large enough that $\operatorname{vol}(B)/k < \varepsilon/2$. Then $U(
P,g)-L(P,g) < \varepsilon/2 + \varepsilon/2 = \varepsilon$.

Since $\varepsilon > 0$ was arbitrary, $g$ is Riemann integrable. $\square$ (Lem
ma)

---

## Completing the theorem

Apply the Lemma to $g = \tilde f$ on $B$:

$$
f \text{ Riemann integrable over } S \iff \tilde f \text{ Riemann integrable ove
r } B \iff m(D_{\tilde f}) = 0.
$$

By the Key Claim and $m(\partial S) = 0$ (Jordan measurability of $S$):
$$
m(D_{\tilde f}) = 0 \iff m(D_f) = 0.
$$

Combining:
$$
f \text{ Riemann integrable over } S \iff m(D_f) = 0,
$$
i.e., $f$ is Riemann integrable over $S$ if and only if its discontinuity set $D
$ has Lebesgue measure zero. $\blacksquare$
