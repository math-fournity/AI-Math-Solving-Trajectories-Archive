## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. If $M$ is a projective left $R[G]$-module, then $M$ is projectiv
e as a left $R[H]$-module. (The hypothesis $\operatorname{char} R = 0$ is not ne
eded; the result holds for any ring $R$.)

---

**Key observation.** The group ring $R[G]$ is free as a left $R[H]$-module, of r
ank $[G:H]$.

*Proof of the observation.* The left action of $R[H]$ on $R[G]$ is by left multi
plication. The left $H$-orbits on $G$ (under $h\cdot g = hg$) are exactly the **
right cosets** $Hg$. Choosing one representative $g_i$ per right coset, $G = \bi
gsqcup_{i=1}^{[G:H]} Hg_i$, and correspondingly

$$R[G] \;=\; \bigoplus_{i=1}^{[G:H]} R[H]\,g_i \quad\text{(as left $R[H]$-module
s)}.$$

Each summand $R[H]\,g_i$ is a free left $R[H]$-module of rank $1$ with basis $\{
g_i\}$, since the map $R[H]\to R[H]g_i$, $x\mapsto xg_i$, is an $R[H]$-module is
omorphism (it is surjective by definition and injective because the $g$'s form a
n $R$-basis of $R[G]$, so $xg_i=0$ forces $x=0$). Hence

$$R[G]\;\cong\;R[H]^{[G:H]}\quad\text{as left $R[H]$-modules}.\qquad\blacksquare
$$

---

**Main argument.** Since $M$ is projective over $R[G]$, there exists an $R[G]$-m
odule $N$ and a free $R[G]$-module $F=(R[G])^{(I)}$ (for some index set $I$) suc
h that

$$M\oplus N \;=\; F \qquad\text{in $R[G]$-Mod}.$$

Apply the restriction functor $\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}:\,R[G]\text{-Mod}\to R
[H]\text{-Mod}$ (forget the $G$-action, keep the $H$-action). This is an exact f
unctor, so the splitting is preserved:

$$\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}M\;\oplus\;\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}N \;=\; \operat
orname{Res}^{G}_{H}F \qquad\text{in $R[H]$-Mod}.$$

It remains to note that $\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}F$ is free over $R[H]$. Indee
d, using the observation,

$$\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}F \;=\; \operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}\bigl((R[G])^{(I)}
\bigr) \;\cong\; \bigl(\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}R[G]\bigr)^{(I)} \;\cong\; \big
l(R[H]^{[G:H]}\bigr)^{(I)} \;\cong\; R[H]^{([G:H]\cdot|I|)},$$

which is a free $R[H]$-module. Therefore $\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}M$ is a dire
ct summand of a free $R[H]$-module, which is precisely the definition of a proje
ctive $R[H]$-module.

---

**Remark on the hypotheses.** Only two ingredients were used: (i) $H\le G$ with
finite index $[G:H]$ (so that the coset decomposition is finite and $R[G]$ is fr
ee, not merely projective, over $R[H]$), and (ii) the exactness of restriction.
Neither requires $\operatorname{char}R=0$; the conclusion is valid for **every**
 base ring $R$ and every subgroup $H$ of finite index in $G$. The characteristic
-$0$ assumption is relevant for the *converse* direction (relative projectivity
/ the Higman criterion, where one needs $|G|$ or $|H|$ to be invertible in $R$),
 but not for the direction asked here.
