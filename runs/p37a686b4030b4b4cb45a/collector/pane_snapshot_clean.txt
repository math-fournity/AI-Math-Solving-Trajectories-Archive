## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. For any matrix norm $\|\cdot\|$ on $\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}$, any
 $A\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}$, and any $\epsilon>0$, there exists $\gamma\in(0,1
)$ such that $\rho_\epsilon(A)\ge\gamma^{(1+\ln n)/n}\|A^n\|^{1/n}$ for all $n\i
n\mathbb{N}$.

The desired inequality is equivalent to $\gamma\le\bigl(\rho_\epsilon(A)/\|A^n\|
^{1/n}\bigr)^{n/(1+\ln n)}$ for all $n$, so it suffices to show $\inf_{n\ge1}\bi
gl(\rho_\epsilon(A)/\|A^n\|^{1/n}\bigr)^{n/(1+\ln n)}>0$, then pick $\gamma$ bel
ow this infimum.

---

**Case 1: $A=0$.** Then $\|A^n\|=0$ for all $n\ge1$; the inequality $0\ge0$ hold
s for any $\gamma\in(0,1)$. ∎

**Case 2: $A$ nilpotent, $A\ne0$ (so $\rho(A)=0$, $A^d=0$).** For $n\ge d$, $\|A
^n\|=0$ and the inequality is trivial. For $1\le n\le d-1$ with $\|A^n\|>0$: sin
ce $\epsilon>0$, the $\epsilon$-pseudospectrum $\Lambda_\epsilon(A)$ is an open
set containing $\Lambda(A)=\{0\}$, hence $\rho_\epsilon(A)>0$. Each ratio $r_n:=
\rho_\epsilon(A)/\|A^n\|^{1/n}$ is a fixed positive real, so $r_n^{n/(1+\ln n)}>
0$. The infimum over finitely many positive values is positive; choose $\gamma\i
n(0,\min(\inf,1))$. ∎

**Case 3: $\rho(A)>0$.** Let $k$ be the maximum Jordan-block size among eigenval
ues of modulus $\rho(A)$. By the Jordan decomposition $A=PJP^{-1}$, each entry o
f $J^n$ is $O(n^{k-1}\rho(A)^n)$. Since all norms on $\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}$ ar
e equivalent (covering both submultiplicative and non-submultiplicative norms),
there exists $C>0$ (depending on the norm and $A$) such that

$$\|A^n\|\le C\,n^{k-1}\,\rho(A)^n,\qquad n\ge1.$$

Since $\rho_\epsilon(A)\ge\rho(A)>0$ and $\|A^n\|>0$ (as $A^n$ has eigenvalue $\
lambda^n$ with $|\lambda|=\rho(A)>0$), we obtain for every $n\ge1$:

$$\frac{\rho_\epsilon(A)}{\|A^n\|^{1/n}}\;\ge\;\frac{\rho(A)}{C^{1/n}\,n^{(k-1)/
n}\,\rho(A)}\;=\;\frac{1}{C^{1/n}\,n^{(k-1)/n}}.$$

Raising to the power $n/(1+\ln n)$:

$$\left(\frac{\rho_\epsilon(A)}{\|A^n\|^{1/n}}\right)^{\!n/(1+\ln n)}\;\ge\;C^{-
1/(1+\ln n)}\cdot n^{-(k-1)/(1+\ln n)}.$$

**Lower bound on each factor, uniform in $n\ge1$:**

- **Second factor:** $n^{-(k-1)/(1+\ln n)}=e^{-(k-1)\ln n/(1+\ln n)}$. Since $0\
le\frac{\ln n}{1+\ln n}<1$, the exponent satisfies $-(k-1)\frac{\ln n}{1+\ln n}\
ge -(k-1)$, giving $n^{-(k-1)/(1+\ln n)}\ge e^{-(k-1)}$.

- **First factor:** If $C\ge1$, then $\frac{1}{1+\ln n}\le1$ gives $C^{-1/(1+\ln
 n)}\ge C^{-1}$. If $0<C<1$, then $C^{-1/(1+\ln n)}\ge1$. In either case, $C^{-1
/(1+\ln n)}\ge\min(C^{-1},1)$.

Combining:

$$\left(\frac{\rho_\epsilon(A)}{\|A^n\|^{1/n}}\right)^{\!n/(1+\ln n)}\;\ge\;\und
erbrace{\min(C^{-1},1)\cdot e^{-(k-1)}}_{=:\,\delta\;>\;0}\qquad\text{for all }n
\ge1.$$

Choose any $\gamma\in\bigl(0,\,\min(\delta,1)\bigr)$, which is nonempty since $\
delta>0$. Then $\gamma\le\bigl(\rho_\epsilon(A)/\|A^n\|^{1/n}\bigr)^{n/(1+\ln n)
}$ for all $n$, i.e.,

$$\rho_\epsilon(A)\;\ge\;\gamma^{(1+\ln n)/n}\,\|A^n\|^{1/n}\qquad\forall\,n\in\
mathbb{N}.\qquad\blacksquare$$

---

**Summary.** The key ingredients are: (1) the Jordan-form bound $\|A^n\|\le C\,n
^{k-1}\rho(A)^n$ valid for *any* matrix norm via norm equivalence; (2) the fact
that $\rho_\epsilon(A)\ge\rho(A)$; and (3) the observation that the correction f
actor $\gamma^{(1+\ln n)/n}\to1$ as $n\to\infty$ slowly enough that the polynomi
al growth $n^{k-1}$ in the Jordan bound is absorbed, leaving a uniform positive
lower bound $\delta=\min(C^{-1},1)\,e^{-(k-1)}>0$ from which a valid $\gamma\in(
0,1)$ is selected.
