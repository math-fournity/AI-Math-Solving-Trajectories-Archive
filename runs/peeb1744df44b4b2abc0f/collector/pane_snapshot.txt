# Solution

**Answer: No.**

## Proof

We exhibit a non-elementary hyperbolic group $\Gamma$ and a torsion element $a \
in \Gamma$ that does **not** fix every point of the Gromov boundary $\partial\Ga
mma$.

### The example: a right-angled pentagon reflection group

Let $P \subset \mathbb{H}^2$ be a right-angled hyperbolic pentagon (a compact ge
odesic pentagon with all five interior angles equal to $\pi/2$); such a pentagon
 exists in $\mathbb{H}^2$. Let $\Gamma$ be the group generated by reflections in
 the five sides of $P$.

**$\Gamma$ is word-hyperbolic.** The pentagon $P$ is a compact convex fundamenta
l domain for the action of $\Gamma$ on $\mathbb{H}^2$ (by the standard Coxeter r
eflection tiling). Hence $\Gamma$ acts geometrically (properly discontinuously a
nd cocompactly by isometries) on $\mathbb{H}^2$. By the Švarc–Milnor lemma, $\Ga
mma$ is finitely generated and quasi-isometric to $\mathbb{H}^2$, which is $\del
ta$-hyperbolic. Therefore $\Gamma$ is word-hyperbolic, with Gromov boundary $\pa
rtial\Gamma \cong S^1_\infty$.

**$\Gamma$ is non-elementary.** Since $\Gamma$ acts cocompactly on $\mathbb{H}^2
$, its limit set is the entire boundary $S^1_\infty$, which is infinite. Hence $
\Gamma$ is not virtually cyclic, i.e., $\Gamma$ is non-elementary.

**A torsion element that moves boundary points.** Let $r \in \Gamma$ be the refl
ection in one side $s$ of $P$. Then $r$ has order $2$, so $r$ is torsion. As an
isometry of $\mathbb{H}^2$, $r$ fixes the geodesic $s$ pointwise. On the boundar
y $S^1_\infty = \partial\Gamma$, the reflection $r$ fixes exactly the two ideal
endpoints $p, q$ of the geodesic $s$ (the two points where $s$ meets the circle
at infinity). Every other point $x \in S^1_\infty \setminus \{p,q\}$ is sent by
$r$ to its mirror image across $s$, which is a *different* point of $S^1_\infty$
.

Therefore $r$ does **not** fix every element of $\partial\Gamma$.

### Conclusion

We have produced a non-elementary hyperbolic group $\Gamma$ (the right-angled pe
ntagon reflection group) and a torsion element $r \in \Gamma$ (a side reflection
 of order $2$) that fixes only two points of $\partial\Gamma \cong S^1_\infty$ a
nd moves all the rest. Hence a torsion element of a non-elementary hyperbolic gr
oup need **not** fix every element of the Gromov boundary.

**The answer is no.**
