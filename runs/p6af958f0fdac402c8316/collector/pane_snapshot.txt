## Proof

Let $k \in \{\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{C}\}$ and let $X$ be a smooth variety over $k$
of dimension $n$. We consider $X(k)$, the set of $k$-rational points, equipped w
ith the analytic (Euclidean) topology. We show $X(k)$ is a $k$-manifold of dimen
sion $n$ (in the real case, $X(\mathbb{R})$ may be empty, which is the empty man
ifold).

### Covering by affine opens

Since $X$ is a separated scheme of finite type over $k$, we cover it by finitely
 many affine open subschemes
$$U_\alpha = \operatorname{Spec} k[x_1,\dots,x_m]/(f_1,\dots,f_r).$$
Each $U_\alpha(k)$ embeds into $k^m$ as the zero locus $V(f_1,\dots,f_r)$. The a
nalytic topology on $X(k)$ is generated by the subsets $U_\alpha(k) \subset k^m$
 with the subspace topology from $k^m \cong \mathbb{R}^m$ or $\mathbb{C}^m$.

### The Jacobian criterion for smoothness

Since $X$ is smooth of dimension $n$ over $k$, each $U_\alpha$ is smooth of rela
tive dimension $n$ over $k$. By the **Jacobian criterion for smoothness** (see,
e.g., Hartshorne, *Algebraic Geometry*, or Liu, *Algebraic Geometry*), smoothnes
s of $U_\alpha$ over $k$ is equivalent to:

> At every point $p \in U_\alpha$ (over $\bar{k}$), the Jacobian matrix
> $$J(p) = \left(\frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_j}\Big|_p\right)_{1 \le i \le r,
\; 1 \le j \le m}$$
> has rank $m - n$ over the residue field $\kappa(p)$.

### Applying the implicit function theorem at $k$-rational points

**Complex case ($k = \mathbb{C}$).** Take any point $p \in U_\alpha(\mathbb{C})
\subset \mathbb{C}^m$. The residue field $\kappa(p) = \mathbb{C}$. The Jacobian
criterion gives $\operatorname{rank} J(p) = m - n$. After reordering coordinates
, some $(m-n)\times(m-n)$ minor of $J(p)$ is nonzero. By the **holomorphic impli
cit function theorem**, there is an open neighborhood $W \subset \mathbb{C}^m$ o
f $p$ such that $U_\alpha(\mathbb{C}) \cap W$ is the graph of a holomorphic func
tion of $n$ variables — hence a complex submanifold of $\mathbb{C}^m$ of dimensi
on $n$, locally biholomorphic to $\mathbb{C}^n$. These local charts on each $U_\
alpha(\mathbb{C})$ are compatible on overlaps (transition functions are holomorp
hic, being restrictions of regular/algebraic functions), endowing $X(\mathbb{C})
$ with the structure of a **complex manifold of dimension $n$**.

**Real case ($k = \mathbb{R}$).** Take any point $p \in U_\alpha(\mathbb{R}) \su
bset \mathbb{R}^m$ (if one exists). The residue field $\kappa(p) = \mathbb{R}$.
The Jacobian criterion gives $\operatorname{rank} J(p) = m - n$ over $\mathbb{R}
$. Some $(m-n)\times(m-n)$ minor of $J(p)$ (with real entries) is nonzero. By th
e **real analytic implicit function theorem**, there is an open neighborhood $W
\subset \mathbb{R}^m$ of $p$ such that $U_\alpha(\mathbb{R}) \cap W$ is the grap
h of a real-analytic function of $n$ variables — a real-analytic submanifold of
$\mathbb{R}^m$ of dimension $n$, locally diffeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^n$. These
local charts patch on overlaps (transition functions are real-analytic, being re
strictions of regular functions with real coefficients), endowing $X(\mathbb{R})
$ with the structure of a **real-analytic manifold of dimension $n$**. If $X(\ma
thbb{R}) = \varnothing$, it is the empty manifold.

### Why smoothness is the right (and necessary) condition

The rank condition on the Jacobian at *every* point — including all $k$-rational
 points — is exactly what the implicit function theorem needs. A merely **regula
r** but non-smooth variety over a non-perfect field could fail this (geometric s
ingularities over $\bar{k}$), but $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$ are **perfect fi
elds**, so for finite-type schemes regularity $\iff$ smoothness, and there is no
 gap. Conversely, dropping smoothness breaks the conclusion: e.g., the nodal cur
ve $y^2 = x^3 + x^2$ over $\mathbb{R}$ is singular at the origin, and its real l
ocus has a node (self-intersection), which is **not** a manifold.

### Conclusion

For every smooth variety $X$ over $\mathbb{C}$, the set $X(\mathbb{C})$ with the
 analytic topology is a complex manifold of dimension $\dim X$. For every smooth
 variety $X$ over $\mathbb{R}$, the set $X(\mathbb{R})$ with the analytic topolo
gy is a real-analytic manifold of dimension $\dim X$ (possibly empty). In both c
ases, the smoothness hypothesis guarantees, via the Jacobian criterion and the i
mplicit function theorem, that the $k$-rational points form a manifold. $\blacks
quare$
