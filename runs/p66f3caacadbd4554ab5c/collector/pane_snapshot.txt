## Proof

**Claim.** The family of all closed subspaces of a separable Banach space $X$ ha
s cardinality at most the continuum $\mathfrak{c} = 2^{\aleph_0}$. Moreover, if
$\dim X \geq 2$, this cardinality equals $\mathfrak{c}$ exactly; in particular i
t is **not** countably infinite.

---

### Upper bound: at most $\mathfrak{c}$

Since $X$ is a separable metric space, $|X| \leq \mathfrak{c}$ (every point is t
he limit of a sequence from the countable dense set, giving an injection $X \hoo
krightarrow D^{\mathbb{N}}$ where $D$ is countable).

Every closed subset $F \subseteq X$ is itself a separable metric space (subspace
 of a separable metric space), so $F$ admits a countable dense subset $E_F \subs
eteq F$, and $F = \overline{E_F}$. Thus the map

$$F \longmapsto \text{(some countable dense subset of } F\text{)}$$

injects the family of closed subsets into the family of countable subsets of $X$
 (after choosing, say, the lexicographically least countable dense subset, or mo
re cleanly: $F$ is uniquely determined by the collection of all countable subset
s whose closure is $F$, and the map $F \mapsto \overline{E}$ from countable subs
ets $E$ to closed sets is surjective). The number of countable subsets of $X$ is

$$|X|^{\aleph_0} \leq \mathfrak{c}^{\aleph_0} = (2^{\aleph_0})^{\aleph_0} = 2^{\
aleph_0 \cdot \aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_0} = \mathfrak{c}.$$

Hence the family of all closed subsets of $X$ has cardinality $\leq \mathfrak{c}
$, and therefore the family of closed **linear** subspaces (a subfamily) also ha
s cardinality $\leq \mathfrak{c}$.

### Lower bound: exactly $\mathfrak{c}$ when $\dim X \geq 2$

**Case 1: $X$ is infinite-dimensional.**

By Mazur's theorem, every infinite-dimensional Banach space contains a **basic s
equence** $(x_n)_{n=1}^{\infty}$: a sequence that is a Schauder basis for its cl
osed linear span $Z = \overline{\operatorname{span}}\{x_n\}$.

For each subset $S \subseteq \mathbb{N}$, define

$$Y_S := \overline{\operatorname{span}}\{x_n : n \in S\} \subseteq Z \subseteq X
.$$

Each $Y_S$ is a closed subspace of $X$. We claim the map $S \mapsto Y_S$ is inje
ctive.

Suppose $S_1 \neq S_2$; say $n_0 \in S_1 \setminus S_2$. Since $(x_n)$ is a Scha
uder basis for $Z$, every $z \in Z$ has a unique expansion $z = \sum_{n} a_n x_n
$. For $z \in Y_{S_2} = \overline{\operatorname{span}}\{x_n : n \in S_2\}$, the
coefficient $a_{n_0}$ of $x_{n_0}$ is $0$ (because $n_0 \notin S_2$ and finite l
inear combinations of $\{x_n : n \in S_2\}$ never involve $x_{n_0}$; this passes
 to the closure by continuity of the coordinate functional $a_{n_0}(\cdot)$, whi
ch is bounded since $(x_n)$ is a Schauder basis). But $x_{n_0} \in Y_{S_1}$ and
$x_{n_0}$ has coefficient $a_{n_0} = 1 \neq 0$. Therefore $x_{n_0} \in Y_{S_1} \
setminus Y_{S_2}$, so $Y_{S_1} \neq Y_{S_2}$.

This gives an injection $2^{\mathbb{N}} \hookrightarrow \{\text{closed subspaces
 of } X\}$, yielding at least $|\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})| = 2^{\aleph_0} = \mathf
rak{c}$ closed subspaces.

**Case 2: $2 \leq \dim X = n < \infty$.**

Then $X \cong \mathbb{R}^n$. The Grassmannian $\mathrm{Gr}(1, n)$ of lines throu
gh the origin in $\mathbb{R}^n$ is parametrized by $\mathbb{R}P^{n-1}$, which ha
s cardinality $\mathfrak{c}$. Each line is a closed subspace. So there are at le
ast $\mathfrak{c}$ closed subspaces.

---

### Conclusion

Combining the upper and lower bounds:

$$|\{\text{closed subspaces of } X\}| = \mathfrak{c} \quad \text{whenever } \dim
 X \geq 2.$$

In all cases (including the trivial cases $\dim X \in \{0, 1\}$, where the count
 is $1$ or $2$ respectively), the cardinality is **at most the continuum**. It i
s **not** countably infinite in general—for every separable Banach space of dime
nsion at least $2$, the cardinality is exactly the continuum $\mathfrak{c} = 2^{
\aleph_0}$.
