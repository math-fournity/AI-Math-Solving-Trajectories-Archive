**Claim:** $S(n) = \dfrac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^n}$.

*Proof of claim.* Consider the ordinary generating function $G(x)=\sum_{n\ge 0}S
(n)\,x^{2n}$. Substituting and swapping the (absolutely convergent for small $|x
|$) sums:

$$G(x)=\sum_{k\ge 0}\binom{2k}{k}\left(\frac{-1}{2}\right)^k\sum_{n\ge 0}\binom{
2n}{k}x^{2n}.$$

The even-indexed part of $\sum_{m\ge 0}\binom{m}{k}x^m = \frac{x^k}{(1-x)^{k+1}}
$ is extracted via $\frac{f(x)+f(-x)}{2}$:

$$\sum_{n\ge 0}\binom{2n}{k}x^{2n}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{x^k}{(1-x)^{k+1}}+\fra
c{(-x)^k}{(1+x)^{k+1}}\right].$$

Hence

$$G(x)=\frac{1}{2(1-x)}\sum_{k\ge 0}\binom{2k}{k}\!\left(\frac{-x}{2(1-x)}\right
)^k+\frac{1}{2(1+x)}\sum_{k\ge 0}\binom{2k}{k}\!\left(\frac{x}{2(1+x)}\right)^k.
$$

Using $\sum_{k\ge 0}\binom{2k}{k}t^k=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-4t}}$:

- First sum: $t=\frac{-x}{2(1-x)}$, giving $\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\frac{2x}{1-x}}}=\s

- Second sum: $t=\frac{x}{2(1+x)}$, giving $\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\frac{2x}{1+x}}}=\s

Therefore

$$G(x)=\frac{1}{2(1-x)}\sqrt{\frac{1-x}{1+x}}+\frac{1}{2(1+x)}\sqrt{\frac{1+x}{1
-x}}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{(1-x)(1+x)}}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{(1+x)(1-x)}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-
x^2}}.$$

By the generalized binomial theorem, $\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}=\sum_{n\ge 0}\frac{
\binom{2n}{n}}{4^n}x^{2n}$. Comparing coefficients:

$$S(n)=\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^n}.\qquad\blacksquare$$
