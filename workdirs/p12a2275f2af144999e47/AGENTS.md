# Problem

In a vast, uncharted territory, a cartographer is planning a complex grid of infrastructure. The land is initially a single, continuous expanse. To organize the territory, the cartographer decides to deploy $n$ distinct pairs of parallel high-speed rail tracks across the entire landscape.

Each pair consists of two lines that never meet each other. However, to maximize the fragmentation of the land into the highest possible number of unique development zones, the cartographer ensures that every line is positioned such that it is not parallel to any line from a different pair. Furthermore, the tracks are laid out so that no more than two lines ever intersect at the exact same geographic point.

Given these strategic placements of $n$ pairs of parallel tracks, what is the maximum number of separate development regions into which the territory can be divided?
