## Solution

We are given positive integers $a, b, c$ satisfying:

1. $abc + 9 = ab + bc + ca$
2. $a + b + c = 10$

**Key identity.** Consider the product $(a-1)(b-1)(c-1)$:

$$(a-1)(b-1)(c-1) = abc - ab - bc - ca + (a+b+c) - 1.$$

Rearranging:

$$abc - ab - bc - ca = (a-1)(b-1)(c-1) - (a+b+c) + 1.$$

From constraint 1: $abc - ab - bc - ca = -9$. Substituting constraint 2 ($a+b+c=
10$):

$$-9 = (a-1)(b-1)(c-1) - 10 + 1$$

$$(a-1)(b-1)(c-1) = 0.$$

Therefore **at least one of $a, b, c$ equals 1**.

**Verification that the first constraint is then automatic.** Suppose $a = 1$. T
hen $b + c = 9$, and the first constraint becomes:

$$bc + 9 = b + bc + c = (b+c) + bc = 9 + bc,$$

which holds identically. So the first constraint is automatically satisfied when
ever one dimension equals 1 (given the perimeter constraint). The problem reduce
s to:

> Count ordered triples $(a,b,c)$ of positive integers with $a+b+c=10$ and at le
ast one of them equal to $1$.

**Counting.** The total number of ordered triples of positive integers summing t
o 10 is $\binom{10-1}{3-1} = \binom{9}{2} = 36$.

The number of triples with **none** equal to 1 (i.e., $a,b,c \geq 2$): setting $
a'=a-1, b'=b-1, c'=c-1 \geq 1$ with $a'+b'+c' = 7$, this is $\binom{6}{2} = 15$.

By inclusion-exclusion, the number with at least one equal to 1:

$$36 - 15 = 21.$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{21}$
