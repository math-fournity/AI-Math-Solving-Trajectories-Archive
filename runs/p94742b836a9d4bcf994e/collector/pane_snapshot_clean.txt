## Proof

We seek a holomorphic function $f$ that maps the annulus $A = \{z : a < |z| < b\
}$ bijectively onto itself, with $f(\{|z|=a\}) = \{|z|=b\}$ and $f(\{|z|=b\}) =
\{|z|=a\}$.

**Claim.** The general form is
$$f(z) = e^{i\theta}\,\frac{ab}{z}, \qquad \theta \in \mathbb{R}.$$

---

### Step 1. Reduction to a standard annulus

Set $r = b/a > 1$ and define $\varphi(z) = z/a$, which maps $A$ conformally onto
 the standard annulus $A_r = \{w : 1 < |w| < r\}$. Then $g = \varphi \circ f \ci
rc \varphi^{-1}$ is a conformal automorphism of $A_r$ that swaps the boundary co
mponents $|w|=1$ and $|w|=r$. It suffices to classify such $g$.

### Step 2. Classification of automorphisms of $A_r$

Let $g$ be a conformal automorphism of $A_r$. Since $g$ is holomorphic and injec
tive on $A_r$ with $g(A_r)=A_r$, it extends continuously to the boundary $\parti
al A_r$ (by the Schwarz reflection / Osgood–Carathéodory theorem, since $A_r$ is
 a finitely connected domain with analytic boundary), and maps each boundary com
ponent to a boundary component.

**Case (i): $g$ preserves each component.** Then $|g|=1$ on $|w|=1$ and $|g|=r$
on $|w|=r$. The function $h(w) = g(w)/w$ satisfies $|h|=1$ on $|w|=1$ and $|h|=1
$ on $|w|=r$. By the maximum principle applied to $h$ and $1/h$ on $A_r$, $h$ is
 constant of modulus $1$, so $g(w) = e^{i\alpha} w$.

**Case (ii): $g$ swaps the components.** Then $|g|=r$ on $|w|=1$ and $|g|=1$ on
$|w|=r$. Consider $k(w) = g(w)\,w/r$. On $|w|=1$: $|k| = |g|\cdot 1/r = r/r = 1$
. On $|w|=r$: $|k| = |g|\cdot r/r = 1\cdot 1 = 1$. So $|k|=1$ on both boundary c
omponents. By the maximum principle applied to $k$ and $1/k$ on $A_r$, $k$ is co
nstant of modulus $1$. Hence
$$g(w) = e^{i\theta}\,\frac{r}{w}.$$

### Step 3. Verify the swapping map

For $g(w) = e^{i\theta} r/w$: it is holomorphic on $A_r$ (since $w\neq 0$ there)
, and $g(g(w)) = e^{i\theta}r/(e^{i\theta}r/w) = w$, so it is an involution, hen
ce bijective. On $|w|=1$, $|g|=r$; on $|w|=r$, $|g|=1$. It swaps the boundary co
mponents as required.

### Step 4. Convert back to $A$

From $g = \varphi \circ f \circ \varphi^{-1}$, i.e. $g(w) = \frac{1}{a}f(aw)$, w
e get
$$f(z) = a\, g(z/a) = a\cdot e^{i\theta}\,\frac{r}{z/a} = a\cdot e^{i\theta}\,\f
rac{b/a}{z/a} = e^{i\theta}\,\frac{ab}{z}.$$

### Step 5. Direct verification

For $f(z) = e^{i\theta}\,ab/z$:
- Holomorphic on $A$ since $z \neq 0$ in $A$.
- $f(f(z)) = e^{i\theta}ab/(e^{i\theta}ab/z) = z$, so $f$ is an involution and h
ence a bijection $A \to A$.
- $|z|=a \Rightarrow |f(z)| = ab/a = b$; $\;|z|=b \Rightarrow |f(z)| = ab/b = a$
. The inner and outer boundaries are swapped.

Therefore the holomorphic functions mapping the annulus $a<|z|<b$ onto itself an
d permuting the two boundary components are exactly
$$\boxed{\,f(z) = e^{i\theta}\,\dfrac{ab}{z},\quad \theta\in\mathbb{R}\,}.$$
