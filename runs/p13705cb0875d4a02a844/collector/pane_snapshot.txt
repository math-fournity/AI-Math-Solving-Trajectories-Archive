## Proof

Set $c = -(s^2+1)$, so $f(X) = X^4 - c \in K[X]$.

### Step 1. Irreducibility of $f$ over $K$

We use the binomial irreducibility criterion: for $\operatorname{char} K \neq 2$
, $X^4 - c$ is irreducible over $K$ iff $c \notin K^{*2}$ and $c \notin -4K^{*4}
$.

**$c \notin K^{*2}$:** Suppose $-(s^2+1) = (p(s)/q(s))^2$ with $p,q \in \mathbb{
Q}[s]$ coprime. Then $p(s)^2 = -(s^2+1)\,q(s)^2$. Comparing leading coefficients
: if $\deg p = m$, $\deg q = n$, then $2m = 2+2n$ so $m = n+1$, and $\operatorna
me{lc}(p)^2 = -\operatorname{lc}(q)^2$, giving $(\operatorname{lc}(p)/\operatorn
ame{lc}(q))^2 = -1$, impossible in $\mathbb{Q}$.

**$c \notin -4K^{*4}$:** $c = -4d^4$ would give $s^2+1 = 4d^4 = (2d^2)^2$, makin
g $s^2+1$ a square in $K$. But $(as+b)^2 = a^2s^2+2abs+b^2 = s^2+1$ forces $a^2=
1$, $2ab=0 \Rightarrow b=0$, then $b^2=0\neq 1$. So $s^2+1 \notin K^{*2}$, hence
 $c \notin -4K^{*4}$.

Therefore $f$ is irreducible over $K$, and $[K(\alpha):K]=4$ where $\alpha^4 = c
 = -(s^2+1)$.

### Step 2. The splitting field

The roots of $f$ are $\alpha,\, i\alpha,\, -\alpha,\, -i\alpha$ where $i=\sqrt{-
1}$. The splitting field is
$$L = K(\alpha, i) = \mathbb{Q}(s,\alpha,i).$$

### Step 3. $i \notin K(\alpha)$, so $[L:K]=8$

View $K(\alpha) = \mathbb{Q}(s,\alpha)$ as an extension of $F := \mathbb{Q}(\alp
ha)$. Since $\alpha$ is transcendental over $\mathbb{Q}$ (because $\alpha^4 = -(
s^2+1)$ is transcendental), $F \cong \mathbb{Q}(t)$. The relation $\alpha^4 = -(
s^2+1)$ gives $s^2 = -(1+\alpha^4)$, so
$$K(\alpha) = F\!\left(\sqrt{d}\right), \quad d = -(1+\alpha^4) \in F.$$

By Kummer's criterion, $\sqrt{-1} \in F(\sqrt{d})$ iff $-1 \in F^{*2}$ or $-1 \e
quiv d \pmod{F^{*2}}$ (i.e. $-1/d \in F^{*2}$, i.e. $1+\alpha^4 \in F^{*2}$).

- **$-1 \notin F^{*2}$:** $F \cong \mathbb{Q}(t) \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}(t)$,
 and $-1$ is not a square in $\mathbb{R}(t)$.
- **$1+\alpha^4 \notin F^{*2}$:** In $\mathbb{Q}(t)$, if $(at^2+bt+c)^2 = 1+t^4$

^2 = 0 \neq 1$. Contradiction.

Hence $i \notin K(\alpha)$, so $[L:K(\alpha)] = 2$ and
$$[L:K] = [L:K(\alpha)]\cdot[K(\alpha):K] = 2 \cdot 4 = 8.$$

### Step 4. The Galois group is $D_4$

Since $f$ is irreducible, $G = \operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ acts transitively on the
 4 roots, so $G$ is a transitive subgroup of $S_4$ with $|G| = [L:K] = 8$. The o
nly transitive subgroup of $S_4$ of order 8 is a Sylow $2$-subgroup of $S_4$, wh
ich is isomorphic to the dihedral group $D_4$ of order 8.

### Step 5. Confirming the structure $G \cong \mathbb{Z}/4 \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/2$

$$\operatorname{Gal}(L/K(i)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}, \quad \sigma: \alpha
\mapsto i\alpha.$$

The quotient $G / \operatorname{Gal}(L/K(i)) \cong \operatorname{Gal}(K(i)/K) \c

$$\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}(\alpha) = \tau\sigma(\alpha) = \tau(i\alpha) = (-i)\alpha

So $\tau\sigma\tau^{-1} = \sigma^{-1}$: the quotient acts on $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathb

$$G \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \cong D_4.$$
