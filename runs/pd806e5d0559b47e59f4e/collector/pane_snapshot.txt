Looking at this problem, I need to find $h(t)$ given the PDE and boundary condit
ions.

**Problem:** $u_{xt}(x,t) + u(x,t)\,u_{xx}(x,t) = h(t)$, with $u(\pi,t) = u_x(0,
t) = u_x(\pi,t) = 0$, $x \in [0,\pi]$, $t > 0$.

**Key observation:** $h(t)$ is independent of $x$, so evaluating the PDE at any
fixed $x$ yields the same $h(t)$. The boundary condition at $x = \pi$ is especia
lly powerful because **two** conditions are imposed there simultaneously.

---

**Proof.**

The boundary conditions hold for all $t > 0$:

$$u(\pi, t) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad u_x(\pi, t) = 0.$$

**Step 1: Differentiate the Neumann condition at $x = \pi$ with respect to $t$.*
*

Since $u_x(\pi, t) = 0$ for all $t > 0$, differentiating in $t$ gives

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\, u_x(\pi, t) = u_{xt}(\pi, t) = 0 \quad \text{for
 all } t > 0.$$

(Regularity is assumed, so $u_{xt}$ exists and the interchange of derivatives is
 valid.)

**Step 2: Evaluate the PDE at $x = \pi$.**

Setting $x = \pi$ in the PDE:

$$u_{xt}(\pi, t) + u(\pi, t)\, u_{xx}(\pi, t) = h(t).$$

Substituting the boundary values:
- $u_{xt}(\pi, t) = 0$ (from Step 1),
- $u(\pi, t) = 0$ (Dirichlet condition),

we obtain

$$0 + 0 \cdot u_{xx}(\pi, t) = h(t),$$

hence

$$\boxed{h(t) = 0.}$$

---

**Consistency check via integration over $[0, \pi]$.**

Integrating the PDE over $x \in [0, \pi]$:

$$\int_0^\pi u_{xt}\,dx + \int_0^\pi u\, u_{xx}\,dx = \pi\, h(t).$$

**First term:**
$$\int_0^\pi u_{xt}\,dx = \frac{d}{dt}\int_0^\pi u_x\,dx = \frac{d}{dt}\bigl[u(\
pi,t) - u(0,t)\bigr] = \frac{d}{dt}\bigl[0 - u(0,t)\bigr] = -u_t(0,t).$$

**Second term** (integration by parts):
$$\int_0^\pi u\, u_{xx}\,dx = \Bigl[u\, u_x\Bigr]_0^\pi - \int_0^\pi u_x^2\,dx.$
$

At the endpoints: $u(\pi,t)\,u_x(\pi,t) = 0 \cdot 0 = 0$ and $u(0,t)\,u_x(0,t) =
 u(0,t) \cdot 0 = 0$. Thus

$$\int_0^\pi u\, u_{xx}\,dx = -\int_0^\pi u_x^2\,dx.$$

Combining:

$$-u_t(0,t) - \int_0^\pi u_x^2\,dx = \pi\, h(t).$$

With $h(t) = 0$, this yields the consistency relation

$$u_t(0,t) = -\int_0^\pi u_x^2(x,t)\,dx \leq 0,$$

which is a nontrivial constraint on the solution $u$ but is fully compatible wit
h the PDE and boundary data — it does not produce any contradiction. This confir
ms that $h(t) = 0$ is the unique value consistent with all boundary conditions.

---

**Conclusion.** The only function $h(t)$ consistent with the PDE and the boundar
y conditions $u(\pi,t) = u_x(0,t) = u_x(\pi,t) = 0$ is

$$h(t) = 0.$$
