## Proof

**Claim:** The real line $\mathbb{R}$ is a one-dimensional manifold that is sepa
rable, connected, and has no minimum or maximum.

**Verification of each property:**

**1. One-dimensional manifold.** A topological space $M$ is a 1-dimensional mani
fold if it is Hausdorff, second-countable, and every point has a neighborhood ho
meomorphic to an open subset of $\mathbb{R}$. The space $\mathbb{R}$ itself sati
sfies all of these:
- $\mathbb{R}$ is Hausdorff (any two distinct points can be separated by disjoin
t open intervals).
- $\mathbb{R}$ is second-countable (the collection of open intervals with ration
al endpoints is a countable basis).
- Every point $x \in \mathbb{R}$ has a neighborhood (e.g., $\mathbb{R}$ itself,
or $(x-1, x+1)$) that is homeomorphic to an open subset of $\mathbb{R}$ via the
identity map.

Hence $\mathbb{R}$ is a 1-dimensional manifold.

**2. Separable.** A space is separable if it contains a countable dense subset.
The rational numbers $\mathbb{Q}$ are countable, and $\mathbb{Q}$ is dense in $\
mathbb{R}$ (between any two real numbers there exists a rational). Therefore $\m
athbb{R}$ is separable.

**3. Connected.** The space $\mathbb{R}$ is connected. To see this: suppose $\ma
thbb{R} = U \cup V$ where $U, V$ are disjoint nonempty open sets. Pick $a \in U$
 and $b \in V$ with $a < b$ (WLOG). The set $S = \{x \in [a,b] : x \in U\}$ is n
onempty (contains $a$) and bounded above by $b$. Let $c = \sup S$. Then $c \in [
a,b]$. If $c \in U$, since $U$ is open, some interval $(c, c+\varepsilon) \subse
t U$, contradicting $c$ being an upper bound of $S$. If $c \in V$, since $V$ is
open, some interval $(c-\varepsilon, c+\varepsilon) \subset V$, but then no poin
t of $S$ lies in $(c-\varepsilon, c]$, contradicting $c = \sup S$. Either way we
 get a contradiction, so $\mathbb{R}$ is connected.

**4. No minimum or maximum.** The real line $\mathbb{R}$ has no smallest element
 (for any $x \in \mathbb{R}$, the element $x - 1$ is smaller) and no largest ele
ment (for any $x \in \mathbb{R}$, the element $x + 1$ is larger). Thus $\mathbb{
R}$ has no minimum and no maximum.

**Conclusion.** The real line $\mathbb{R}$ is a one-dimensional manifold that is
 separable, connected, and has no minimum or maximum. $\blacksquare$
