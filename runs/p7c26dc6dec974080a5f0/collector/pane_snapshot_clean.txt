## Box Dimension of the Randomly Thinned Square Fractal

**Setup.** Let $S_0$ be the unit square $[0,1]^2$. At step $n\geq 1$, every squa
re composing $S_{n-1}$ is subdivided into $3\times 3 = 9$ congruent sub-squares
of side $3^{-n}$, and exactly one of the nine is removed. The resulting set is $
S_n$. The limiting set is
$$S = \bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty} S_n.$$

**Counting boxes at the natural scales.** Regardless of *which* sub-square is re
moved at each step (the choice is random but always removes exactly one out of n
ine), the cardinality of the covering is deterministic:

- $S_0$: $1 = 8^0$ square of side $3^0 = 1$.
- $S_1$: $8 = 8^1$ squares of side $3^{-1}$.
- $S_2$: each of the $8$ squares spawns $9$ sub-squares and loses $1$, giving $8
\cdot 8 = 8^2$ squares of side $3^{-2}$.
- $S_n$: $8^n$ squares of side $3^{-n}$, by induction.

**The limit set is nonempty inside every surviving square.** Each square $Q$ app
earing in $S_n$ is a compact set. Inside $Q$, the construction produces $8^k$ ne
sted sub-squares of $S_{n+k}$ for every $k\geq 0$, and these form a decreasing s
equence of nonempty compact sets. By Cantor's intersection theorem, $S\cap Q\neq
\varnothing$. Hence $S$ meets every one of the $8^n$ squares of $S_n$.

**Exact box count at scale $3^{-n}$.** Let $N(\varepsilon)$ denote the minimum n
umber of axis-aligned boxes of side $\varepsilon$ required to cover $S$.

- *Upper bound:* $S\subseteq S_n$, and $S_n$ is the union of $8^n$ squares of si
de $3^{-n}$, so $N(3^{-n})\le 8^n$.
- *Lower bound:* $S$ intersects each of the $8^n$ squares of $S_n$. These square
s are separated by gaps of width at least $3^{-n}$ (the removed sub-squares crea
te gaps), so no box of side $3^{-n}$ can cover points in two distinct surviving
squares. Therefore $N(3^{-n})\ge 8^n$.

Combining, $N(3^{-n}) = 8^n$ exactly.

**Computing the dimension.** The (upper and lower) box dimension is
$$
\overline{\dim}_B(S) = \limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\log N(\varepsilon)}{\log
(1/\varepsilon)},\qquad \underline{\dim}_B(S) = \liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{
\log N(\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}.
$$

Along the sequence $\varepsilon_n = 3^{-n}$,
$$
\frac{\log N(3^{-n})}{\log(1/3^{-n})} = \frac{\log 8^n}{\log 3^n} = \frac{n\log
8}{n\log 3} = \frac{\log 8}{\log 3}.
$$

It remains to confirm the limit exists (not just along the subsequence). For $\v
arepsilon\in(3^{-(n+1)},\,3^{-n}]$, monotonicity of $N$ gives $8^n \le N(\vareps
ilon)\le 8^{n+1}$, so
$$
\frac{\log 8^n}{\log 3^{n+1}} \le \frac{\log N(\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)
} \le \frac{\log 8^{n+1}}{\log 3^n},
$$
i.e.
$$
\frac{n\log 8}{(n+1)\log 3} \le \frac{\log N(\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}
\le \frac{(n+1)\log 8}{n\log 3}.
$$
Both bounds tend to $\log 8/\log 3$ as $n\to\infty$ ($\varepsilon\to 0$). By the
 squeeze theorem the full limit exists and equals $\log 8/\log 3$.

**Conclusion.** The randomness affects *which* sub-square is deleted at each ste
p but never *how many* — always one of nine, leaving eight. Therefore the box co
unt at every scale $3^{-n}$ is the deterministic value $8^n$, and the box dimens
ion is

$$
\boxed{\dim_B(S) = \dfrac{\log 8}{\log 3} = \dfrac{3\log 2}{\log 3} \approx 1.89
28.}
$$

This coincides with the box dimension of the (deterministic) Sierpiński carpet,
since the random construction preserves the same branching factor $8$ and scale
factor $3$ at every level.
