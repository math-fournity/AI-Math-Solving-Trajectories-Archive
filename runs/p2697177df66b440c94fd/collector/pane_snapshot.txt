## Proof

Let $f$ denote the principal branch of the square root. Its domain (the set on w
hich it is defined, which is also its pre-image since $f$ is injective there) is

$$D = \mathbb{C} \setminus (-\infty, 0],$$

the complex plane with the non-positive real axis removed. On this domain, every
 $z$ has a unique argument $\arg(z) \in (-\pi, \pi)$, and

$$f(z) = \sqrt{z} = \sqrt{|z|}\, e^{i\arg(z)/2}.$$

**Image of $f$.** For $z \in D$, we have $|z| > 0$ and $\arg(z) \in (-\pi,\pi)$,
 so $|f(z)| = \sqrt{|z|} \in (0,\infty)$ and $\arg(f(z)) = \tfrac{1}{2}\arg(z) \
in (-\pi/2, \pi/2)$. The condition $\arg(w) \in (-\pi/2, \pi/2)$ is equivalent t
o $\Re(w) > 0$. Hence

$$f(D) = \{w \in \mathbb{C} : \Re(w) > 0\},$$

the **open right half-plane**.

Conversely, every $w$ with $\Re(w) > 0$ satisfies $\arg(w) \in (-\pi/2,\pi/2)$,
so $z = w^2$ has $\arg(z) = 2\arg(w) \in (-\pi,\pi)$, hence $z \in D$ and $f(z)
= w$. This confirms $f(D) = \{w : \Re(w) > 0\}$.

**Comparison.** The domain is $D = \mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]$, which contai
ns, for instance, the point $-1 + i$ (its real part is negative, but it is not o
n the non-positive real axis, so it belongs to $D$). However, $-1 + i \notin \{w
 : \Re(w) > 0\}$ since $\Re(-1+i) = -1 < 0$.

On the other hand, every point of the image satisfies $\Re(w) > 0$, which automa
tically places it off the non-positive real axis, so $f(D) \subseteq D$. The inc
lusion is strict:

$$f(D) = \{w : \Re(w) > 0\} \subsetneq \mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0] = D.$$

Therefore, the image of the principal branch of the square root is **not** the s
ame as its pre-image (domain). The image is the open right half-plane, which is
a proper subset of the slit plane $\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]$ that serves a
s the domain.
