# Problem

A specialized logistics firm manages a collection $A$ of distinct high-security identification codes, each represented by a unique natural number. To stress-test their security protocol, the firm investigates "balanced splits" of these codes.

A balanced split occurs when the firm divides the entire collection $A$ into two separate, non-empty vaults, $A_1$ and $A_2$, such that every code belongs to exactly one vault. The split is considered "demonic" if the following mathematical condition is met: the least common multiple of all identification codes stored in Vault $A_1$ is exactly equal to the greatest common divisor of all identification codes stored in Vault $A_2$.

The firm’s lead cryptographer discovers that for their specific collection $A$, there are exactly 2016 unique ways to perform such a demonic split. 

What is the minimum number of identification codes that must be in collection $A$ for this exact number of demonic partitions to exist?
