## Solution

**Step 1: Factor the polynomial.**

$$A^{10} - 3A^9 + 2A^8 = A^8(A^2 - 3A + 2) = A^8(A-1)(A-2)$$

So the minimal polynomial $m_A(x)$ divides $x^8(x-1)(x-2)$. The eigenvalues of $
A$ lie in $\{0, 1, 2\}$.

**Step 2: Determine Jordan block constraints.**

For a $3\times 3$ matrix, the minimal polynomial has degree $\leq 3$. The factor
 $x^8$ permits a largest Jordan block for eigenvalue $0$ of size $\leq 8$ — auto
matically satisfied since the matrix is only $3\times 3$. The factors $(x-1)$ an
d $(x-2)$ appear only to the first power, so eigenvalues $1$ and $2$ admit **onl
y $1\times 1$ Jordan blocks** (they are semisimple).

**Step 3: Count distinct Jordan forms.**

Two matrices are similar iff they share the same Jordan normal form. Let $m_0, m
_1, m_2$ be the algebraic multiplicities of $0, 1, 2$, with $m_0+m_1+m_2=3$. For
 eigenvalue $0$, the number of possible Jordan structures equals $p(m_0)$, the n
umber of partitions of $m_0$ (since the block-size cap of $8$ is never binding f
or size $\leq 3$). For eigenvalues $1$ and $2$, each contributes exactly one str
ucture (all $1\times 1$ blocks).

For each fixed $m_0$, the number of non-negative pairs $(m_1,m_2)$ with $m_1+m_2
 = 3-m_0$ is $4-m_0$.

| $m_0$ | $p(m_0)$ | pairs $(m_1,m_2)$ | contribution |
|-------|----------|--------------------|--------------|
| 0 | 1 | 4 | 4 |
| 1 | 1 | 3 | 3 |
| 2 | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| 3 | 3 | 1 | 3 |

Here $p(0)=1,\; p(1)=1,\; p(2)=2\;\{2,\;1{+}1\},\; p(3)=3\;\{3,\;2{+}1,\;1{+}1{+
}1\}$.

**Total:** $4 + 3 + 4 + 3 = \boxed{14}$

**Step 4: Verify achievability.** Each counted Jordan form is a valid $3\times 3
$ matrix over $\mathbb{C}$ whose minimal polynomial divides $x^8(x-1)(x-2)$, and
 distinct Jordan forms give non-similar matrices. Hence we can select one repres
entative from each of the 14 similarity classes, achieving $|\mathcal{S}| = 14$.
