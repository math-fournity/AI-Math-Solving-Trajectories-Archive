## Proof

Let $A = 2^{1982} - 1982^{1982}$.

**Step 1: $A$ is an integer.**

Both $2^{1982}$ and $1982^{1982}$ are powers of integers, hence integers. The di
fference of two integers is an integer, so $A \in \mathbb{Z}$.

**Step 2: Determine the largest integer strictly less than $A$.**

For any integer $A$, the set of integers satisfying $n < A$ is
$$\{\ldots,\; A-3,\; A-2,\; A-1\},$$
which has a maximum element, namely $A - 1$. (The next integer $A$ itself fails
the strict inequality, and every integer below $A-1$ is smaller.)

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

Therefore the largest integer $n$ such that $n < 2^{1982} - 1982^{1982}$ is

$$\boxed{n = 2^{1982} - 1982^{1982} - 1}.$$

*Remark on sign (sanity check).* Since $1982 > 2$, we have $1982^{1982} \gg 2^{1
982}$, so $A$ is a large negative integer. The largest integer strictly below it
 is simply one less, confirming $n = A - 1$. No approximation or estimation is n
eeded because both quantities are exact integers — the strict inequality shifts
the answer by exactly $-1$ from the integer value $A$.
