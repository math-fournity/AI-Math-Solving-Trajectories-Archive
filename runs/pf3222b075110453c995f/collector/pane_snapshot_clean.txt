**Answer: Yes.** The Hermitian logarithm $P^* D' P$ (where $A = P^* D P$ is a un
itary diagonalization with $D$ positive diagonal, and $D' = \log D$) is the **on
ly** Hermitian logarithm of a positive definite matrix $A$.

## Proof

Let $A$ be positive definite, and let $X$ be any Hermitian matrix satisfying $e^
X = A$. We show $X$ is forced to be the spectral logarithm of $A$.

**Step 1: $X$ has real eigenvalues, and they are real logarithms of the eigenval
ues of $A$.**

Since $X$ is Hermitian, it is unitarily diagonalizable with **real** eigenvalues
: $X = Q \Lambda Q^*$, $\Lambda = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_
n)$, $\lambda_i \in \mathbb{R}$. Then
$$A = e^X = Q\, e^\Lambda\, Q^*,$$
so $e^{\lambda_i}$ are the eigenvalues of $A$. Since $A$ is positive definite, i
ts eigenvalues $\mu_1, \dots, \mu_n$ are strictly positive. Hence each $e^{\lamb
da_i} = \mu_{\sigma(i)}$ for some permutation $\sigma$, and because $\lambda_i$
is **real**,
$$\lambda_i = \log \mu_{\sigma(i)} \quad (\text{the unique real logarithm}).$$

**Step 2: $X$ is constant on each eigenspace of $A$, equal to $(\log \mu) I$.**

Let $E_\mu = \ker(A - \mu I)$ be the eigenspace of $A$ for eigenvalue $\mu > 0$.
 We claim $X|_{E_\mu} = (\log \mu)\, I_{E_\mu}$.

Indeed, for $v \in E_\mu$, $A v = \mu v$, i.e. $e^X v = \mu v$. Restrict $X$ to
the invariant subspace $E_\mu$ (invariance follows because $X$ commutes with $A
= e^X$). The restricted operator $X|_{E_\mu}$ is Hermitian, so diagonalize it on
 $E_\mu$:
$$X|_{E_\mu} = R\, \operatorname{diag}(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_k)\, R^*, \qquad
\alpha_j \in \mathbb{R}.$$
Then $e^{X|_{E_\mu}} = \mu I_{E_\mu}$ gives $e^{\alpha_j} = \mu$ for every $j$,
hence $\alpha_j = \log \mu$ for every $j$. Therefore
$$X|_{E_\mu} = (\log \mu)\, I_{E_\mu}.$$

**Step 3: $X$ equals the spectral logarithm.**

Decompose $\mathbb{C}^n = \bigoplus_\mu E_\mu$ (orthogonal sum, since $A$ is nor
mal). By Step 2,
$$X = \bigoplus_\mu (\log \mu)\, I_{E_\mu}.$$
This is precisely the operator that acts as $\log \mu$ on each eigenspace $E_\mu
$ of $A$ — i.e. the spectral logarithm $\log A$. In the notation of the problem,
 if $A = P^* D P$ with $D = \operatorname{diag}(\mu_1, \dots, \mu_n)$, then
$$X = P^*\, \operatorname{diag}(\log \mu_1, \dots, \log \mu_n)\, P = P^* D' P.$$

**Step 4: Uniqueness.**

The argument made no choice: every Hermitian $X$ with $e^X = A$ was forced, on e
ach eigenspace of $A$, to be the scalar $(\log \mu) I$. There is no freedom — no
t even when $A$ has repeated eigenvalues, since within a repeated eigenspace $X$
 is still pinned to the scalar $(\log\mu)I$. Hence the Hermitian logarithm is un
ique.

**Remark on why non-Hermitian logarithms exist but Hermitian ones do not multipl
y.** A general (non-Hermitian) logarithm of $A$ is obtained by replacing $\log \
mu_i$ with $\log \mu_i + 2\pi i k_i$ ($k_i \in \mathbb{Z}$) in the spectral pict
ure, or by conjugating with non-unitary matrices when eigenvalues repeat. These
produce non-real eigenvalues or non-Hermitian structure. The Hermitian hypothesi
s forces all $k_i = 0$ (real eigenvalues) and forces the scalar action on each e
igenspace, eliminating every degree of freedom.

$\blacksquare$
