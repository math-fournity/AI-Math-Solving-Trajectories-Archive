## Proof

ntries $a_{ij}(x)$ are $C^\infty$ functions (of a real variable $x$, on some ope

**Step 1: $A$ is everywhere invertible.**

Positive definiteness means that for every $x\in\Omega$ and every nonzero $z\in\
mathbb{C}^N$,
$$z^{*}\,A(x)\,z>0.$$
In particular every eigenvalue of $A(x)$ is strictly positive, so $\det A(x)\neq
 0$ for all $x$. Hence $A^{-1}(x)$ exists pointwise on all of $\Omega$.

igma(i)}(x)$$

Likewise, each cofactor $C_{ij}(x)$ is (up to sign) the determinant of an $(N-1)

C^\infty$.

**Step 3: The reciprocal of the determinant is $C^\infty$.**

From Step 1, $\det A(x)>0$ for all $x\in\Omega$ (positive definiteness gives a p
ositive real determinant). The map $t\mapsto 1/t$ is $C^\infty$ on $(0,\infty)$,

$$\frac{1}{\det A(x)}\in C^\infty(\Omega).$$

**Step 4: The inverse is $C^\infty$.**

The classical adjugate formula gives
$$A^{-1}(x)=\frac{1}{\det A(x)}\,\operatorname{adj}(A)(x),$$

$$\bigl(A^{-1}\bigr)_{ij}(x)=\frac{C_{ji}(x)}{\det A(x)}.$$

The numerator $C_{ji}(x)$ is $C^\infty$ (Step 2) and the denominator $\det A(x)$

**Conclusion.** Yes — the inverse matrix $A^{-1}$ is also composed of $C^\infty$
 functions. $\blacksquare$
