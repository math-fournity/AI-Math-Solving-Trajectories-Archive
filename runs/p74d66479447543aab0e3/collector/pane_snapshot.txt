## Proof

We claim **yes**, $\nu \ll \lambda$, and we exhibit the Radon–Nikodym derivative
 explicitly.

**Setup.** For a Borel set $A \subseteq [0,\infty)$,
$$
\nu(A) = \lambda\!\left(f^{-1}[A]\right), \qquad f(x) = x^2.
$$
Since $f^{-1}[A] = \{\sqrt{a} : a \in A\} \cup \{-\sqrt{a} : a \in A\}$ and the
two halves are reflections of each other (Lebesgue measure is reflection-invaria
nt),
$$
\nu(A) = 2\,\lambda\!\left(\sqrt{A}\right), \qquad \sqrt{A} := \{\sqrt{a} : a \i
n A,\ a \ge 0\}.
$$

**Change of variables.** Writing the definition as an integral and splitting $\m
athbb{R}$ into $[0,\infty)$ and $(-\infty,0]$,
$$
\nu(A) = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbf{1}_A(x^2)\,dx = 2\int_{0}^{\infty} \mathbf{1}
_A(x^2)\,dx.
$$
Apply the $C^1$-diffeomorphism $t = x^2$ on $(0,\infty)$, i.e. $x = \sqrt{t}$, $
dx = \dfrac{dt}{2\sqrt{t}}$:
$$
\nu(A) = 2\int_{0}^{\infty} \mathbf{1}_A(t)\,\frac{dt}{2\sqrt{t}} = \int_{0}^{\i
nfty} \mathbf{1}_A(t)\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}.
$$
The substitution is legitimate because $t \mapsto 1/\sqrt{t}$ is locally integra
ble on $[0,\infty)$:
$$
\int_{0}^{\varepsilon} t^{-1/2}\,dt = 2\sqrt{\varepsilon} < \infty \quad \text{f
or every } \varepsilon > 0.
$$
The endpoint $t = 0$ contributes nothing to the integral (it is a single point,
$\lambda$-null), so the value $+\infty$ of $1/\sqrt{t}$ there is harmless.

**Density.** Define
$$
g(t) := \frac{\mathbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}(t)}{\sqrt{t}} \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\math
bb{R}).
$$
Combining $\nu((-\infty,0)) = 0$ with the integral formula above, for every Bore
l $B \subseteq \mathbb{R}$,
$$
\nu(B) = \int_{B} g(t)\,d\lambda(t).
$$
Hence $\nu = g\,\lambda$, i.e. $\nu$ has Radon–Nikodym derivative $g = \dfrac{d\
nu}{d\lambda}$ with respect to Lebesgue measure.

**Absolute continuity.** If $\lambda(B) = 0$, then
$$
\nu(B) = \int_B g\,d\lambda = 0
$$
(the integral of any measurable function over a null set is $0$). Therefore $\nu
 \ll \lambda$.

**Conclusion.** The measure $\nu$ is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebes
gue measure, with density
$$
\frac{d\nu}{d\lambda}(t) = \frac{\mathbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}(t)}{\sqrt{t}}.
$$
