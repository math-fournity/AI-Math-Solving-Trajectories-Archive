$ that is clean or admits an embedding of $G$.

**Answer: Yes.** For every directed multigraph $G$ of arbitrary cardinality, suc
h a space exists.

## Proof

**Construction.** Let $G = (V, E)$ be a directed multigraph, where $V$ is a set
of vertices and $E$ is a multiset of directed edges, each edge $e \in E$ having
a source $s(e) \in V$ and target $t(e) \in V$. We construct a topological space
$T$ — the **geometric realization** of $G$ — as a 1-dimensional CW complex:

1. **0-skeleton:** Place one 0-cell (point) for each vertex $v \in V$. Equip $V$
 with the discrete topology.

2. **1-cells:** For each edge $e \in E$, take a distinct copy $I_e$ of the close
d interval $[0,1]$. Attach $I_e$ to the 0-skeleton via the characteristic map $\
varphi_e : \partial I_e = \{0,1\} \to V$ defined by $\varphi_e(0) = s(e)$ and $\
varphi_e(1) = t(e)$.

3. **Topology:** $T$ is the quotient space of the disjoint union $V \sqcup \bigs
qcup_{e \in E} I_e$ under the identification $0 \sim s(e)$, $1 \sim t(e)$ for ea
ch $e \in E$, equipped with the quotient topology (equivalently, the weak topolo
gy with respect to the closed cells).

**This construction is valid for arbitrary cardinality.** The disjoint union of
$\lambda$ copies of $[0,1]$ for any cardinal $\lambda = |E|$ is well-defined, an
d the quotient of any topological space by any equivalence relation is well-defi
ned. No restriction on $|V|$ or $|E|$ is required — the construction applies to
finite, countable, uncountable, and arbitrarily large cardinals alike.

**Embedding of $G$ into $T$.** Define $\iota : G \to T$ by:
- Each vertex $v \in V$ maps to the corresponding 0-cell in $T$.
- Each edge $e \in E$ maps to the interior of the corresponding 1-cell $I_e$ in
$T$, extended to the closed arc by including endpoints.

This is a topological embedding:
- **Injectivity on vertices:** Distinct vertices map to distinct 0-cells.
- **Injectivity on edges:** Distinct edges map to distinct 1-cells. Even when mu
ltiple edges share the same source and target (multigraph), each edge $e$ has it
s own copy $I_e$, so the arcs are distinct and share only their endpoints.
- **Homeomorphism on each piece:** The restriction of $\iota$ to each edge (view
ed as a copy of $[0,1]$) is a homeomorphism onto its image (the corresponding cl
osed 1-cell). The restriction to each vertex is trivially a homeomorphism onto a
 point.
- **Continuity:** $\iota$ is continuous by the universal property of the quotien
t topology.

**Cleanliness.** The space $T$ is clean: distinct edges (1-cells) intersect only
 at their endpoints (0-cells), which are exactly the vertices of $G$. No two edg
es cross in their interiors. This is the defining property of a clean drawing, a
nd it holds by construction — the 1-cells are attached only at the 0-skeleton.

**Direction preservation.** The direction of each edge $e$ is encoded by the ori
entation of the arc $I_e$: the arc is oriented from $s(e)$ (the $0$-endpoint) to
 $t(e)$ (the $1$-endpoint). Thus the embedding respects the directed structure o
f $G$.

**Comparison with the known results.** The problem notes that certain graphs (e.
g., $V = 2^{\mathbb{R}}$ with edges defined by finite symmetric difference) cann
ot be drawn in any $\mathbb{R}^n$. This is a constraint on *Euclidean* spaces, n
ot on topological spaces in general. Our construction does not embed $G$ into a
pre-existing space like $\mathbb{R}^n$; rather, it *builds a space tailored to $
G$*. The geometric realization $T$ is a 1-dimensional CW complex whose topology
is determined by $G$ itself, so no ambient-space obstruction arises. The cardina
lity of $T$ is $|V| + |E| \cdot \mathfrak{c}$, which is well-defined for any car
dinals $|V|, |E|$.

**Conclusion.** For every directed multigraph $G$ of arbitrary cardinality, the
geometric realization $T$ — constructed as a 1-dimensional CW complex with one 0
-cell per vertex and one 1-cell per edge — is a $G$-spacious topological space t
hat is clean and admits an embedding of $G$. The construction requires no assump
tion on the cardinality of $G$ and no restriction on the ambient space. $\blacks
quare$
