## Solution

We evaluate $I=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x\sin(\pi x)}{x^2+2x+5}\,dx$ by cont
our integration.

**Step 1: Complexify.** Since $\sin(\pi x)=\operatorname{Im}(e^{i\pi x})$ and th
e denominator is real on $\mathbb{R}$,

$$I=\operatorname{Im}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x\,e^{i\pi x}}{x^2+2x+5}\,dx
.$$

Set $f(z)=\dfrac{z\,e^{i\pi z}}{z^2+2z+5}$.

**Step 2: Poles.** Factor the denominator:
$$z^2+2z+5=(z+1)^2+4=(z-(-1+2i))(z-(-1-2i)).$$
Simple poles at $z_0=-1+2i$ (upper half-plane) and $z_1=-1-2i$ (lower half-plane
).

**Step 3: Contour.** Since $\pi>0$, $|e^{i\pi z}|=e^{-\pi\,\operatorname{Im}z}$
decays in the upper half-plane. Close the contour over the upper semicircle $C_R
$. By Jordan's lemma the arc contribution $\to 0$ as $R\to\infty$ (integrand $\s
im R/R^2=1/R$, exponentially damped). Only $z_0$ lies inside.

**Step 4: Residue at $z_0=-1+2i$.**
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_0}f(z)=\frac{z_0\,e^{i\pi z_0}}{z_0-z_1}=\frac{(-1+2i)
\,e^{i\pi(-1+2i)}}{4i}.$$
Compute the exponential:
$$e^{i\pi(-1+2i)}=e^{-i\pi-2\pi}=e^{-i\pi}e^{-2\pi}=(-1)e^{-2\pi}=-e^{-2\pi}.$$
Hence
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_0}f(z)=\frac{(-1+2i)(-e^{-2\pi})}{4i}=\frac{(1-2i)e^{-
2\pi}}{4i}.$$
Using $\dfrac{1}{i}=-i$:
$$\frac{1-2i}{4i}=\frac{1}{4i}-\frac{2i}{4i}=-\frac{i}{4}-\frac{1}{2}=-\frac{1}{
2}-\frac{i}{4}.$$
So
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_0}f(z)=e^{-2\pi}\!\left(-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{i}{4}\right
).$$

**Step 5: Apply the residue theorem.**
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x\,e^{i\pi x}}{x^2+2x+5}\,dx=2\pi i\cdot e^{-2\pi
}\!\left(-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{i}{4}\right)=2\pi e^{-2\pi}\!\left(-\frac{i}{2}+\fra
c{1}{4}\right)=\pi e^{-2\pi}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}-i\right).$$

**Step 6: Extract the imaginary part.**
$$I=\operatorname{Im}\!\left[\pi e^{-2\pi}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}-i\right)\right]=-\
pi e^{-2\pi}.$$

$$\boxed{I=-\pi e^{-2\pi}}$$
