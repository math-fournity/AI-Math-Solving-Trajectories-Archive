## Proof

**Setup.** The paraboloid $K$ is the surface of revolution obtained by rotating
$y = \frac{3}{4} - x^2$ about the $y$-axis, giving
$$K:\quad y = \tfrac{3}{4} - r^2, \qquad r^2 = x^2 + z^2.$$
The plane $H$ passes through the origin and is tilted $45^\circ$ from the $y$-ax
is. Since $K$ is rotationally symmetric about the $y$-axis, the enclosed volume
is the same for any such plane; we may take
$$H:\quad y = x.$$

**Intersection curve.** Setting $y = x$ in $y = \frac{3}{4} - x^2 - z^2$:
$$x = \tfrac{3}{4} - x^2 - z^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; x^2 + x + z^2 = \tfrac{3}{4}
\;\Longrightarrow\; \bigl(x+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)^2 + z^2 = 1.$$
This is a circle of radius $1$ (lying in the plane $y=x$), so the enclosed solid
 projects onto the disk
$$D = \bigl\{(x,z): \bigl(x+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)^2 + z^2 \le 1\bigr\}$$
in the $xz$-plane. The height of the solid above a point $(x,z)\in D$ is
$$\Bigl(\tfrac{3}{4}-x^2-z^2\Bigr) - x = 1 - \bigl(x+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)^2 - z^2.
$$

**Cross-sections (single-integral method).** Slice the solid by planes $x = \tex
t{const}$. At fixed $x$, set $b = 1 - \bigl(x+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)^2$; the cross-s
ection in the $yz$-plane is the region between the horizontal line $y = x$ and t
he parabola $y = \bigl(\frac{3}{4}-x^2\bigr) - z^2$, with $z\in[-\sqrt{b},\,\sqr
t{b}]$. Its area is
$$A(x) = \int_{-\sqrt{b}}^{\sqrt{b}} \bigl(b - z^2\bigr)\,dz = \Bigl[bz - \tfrac
{z^3}{3}\Bigr]_{-\sqrt{b}}^{\sqrt{b}} = \tfrac{4}{3}\,b^{3/2} = \tfrac{4}{3}\Big
l[1-\bigl(x+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)^2\Bigr]^{3/2}.$$
The variable $x$ ranges over $\bigl[-\tfrac{3}{2},\,\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr]$ (where $
b\ge 0$). Hence
$$V = \int_{-3/2}^{1/2} A(x)\,dx = \tfrac{4}{3}\int_{-3/2}^{1/2}\Bigl[1-\bigl(x+
\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)^2\Bigr]^{3/2}dx.$$

**Evaluation.** Substitute $u = x + \tfrac{1}{2}$, then $u = \sin\theta$:
$$V = \tfrac{4}{3}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-u^2)^{3/2}\,du = \tfrac{4}{3}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\p
i/2}\cos^4\theta\,d\theta = \tfrac{8}{3}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^4\theta\,d\theta.$$
By Wallis's formula, $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^4\theta\,d\theta = \frac
{3}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2} = \frac{3\pi}{16}$. Therefore
$$V = \tfrac{8}{3}\cdot\frac{3\pi}{16} = \frac{\pi}{2}.$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{\dfrac{\pi}{2}}$.
