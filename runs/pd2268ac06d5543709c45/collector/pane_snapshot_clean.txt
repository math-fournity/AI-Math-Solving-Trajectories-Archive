## Part (a): Solution via Duhamel's Principle

We solve the inhomogeneous wave equation on the whole line with zero initial dat
a:

$$u_{tt} = c^2 u_{xx} + Q(x,t), \qquad u(x,0)=0,\quad u_t(x,0)=0.$$

**Duhamel's principle.** For each fixed $\tau \ge 0$, let $v(x,t;\tau)$ solve th
e *homogeneous* wave equation with initial data at time $t=\tau$:

$$v_{tt} = c^2 v_{xx}, \qquad v(x,\tau;\tau)=0,\quad v_t(x,\tau;\tau)=Q(x,\tau).
$$

Then

$$u(x,t) = \int_0^t v(x,t;\tau)\,d\tau.$$

**Verification.** Differentiating,
- $u_t(x,t) = v(x,t;t) + \int_0^t v_t(x,t;\tau)\,d\tau = 0 + \int_0^t v_t\,d\tau
$ (since $v(x,t;t)=0$).
- $u_{tt} = v_t(x,t;t) + \int_0^t v_{tt}\,d\tau = Q(x,t) + \int_0^t c^2 v_{xx}\,
d\tau = Q(x,t) + c^2 u_{xx}$.

The initial conditions $u(x,0)=0$, $u_t(x,0)=0$ are immediate. ✓

**Evaluating $v$.** Shifting time so $s = t-\tau$, the homogeneous solution with
 data $v(x,0)=0$, $v_t(x,0)=Q(x,\tau)$ is given by d'Alembert's formula:

$$v(x,t;\tau) = \frac{1}{2c}\int_{x-c(t-\tau)}^{x+c(t-\tau)} Q(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi.$
$

**Final solution:**

$$\boxed{\displaystyle u(x,t) = \frac{1}{2c}\int_0^t\!\int_{x-c(t-\tau)}^{x+c(t-
\tau)} Q(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau.}$$

Equivalently, writing $r = t-\tau$ and noting the integration region is the back
ward cone $|\xi - x| \le c(t-\tau)$:

$$u(x,t) = \frac{1}{2c}\iint_{\{(\xi,\tau):\,0\le \tau \le t,\; |\xi-x|\le c(t-\
tau)\}} Q(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau.$$

## Part (b): Source locations influencing $u(x_1,t_1)$

From the formula, $Q(\xi,\tau)$ enters the integrand for $u(x_1,t_1)$ **if and o
nly if**

$$0 \le \tau \le t_1 \quad\text{and}\quad |\xi - x_1| \le c\,(t_1 - \tau).$$

This is the **backward (past) light cone** of the space-time point $(x_1, t_1)$:

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \mathcal{C}^-(x_1,t_1) = \bigl\{(\xi,\tau) : 0 \le \tau \
le t_1,\;\; x_1 - c(t_1-\tau) \le \xi \le x_1 + c(t_1-\tau)\bigr\}.}$$

**Physical interpretation.** A disturbance (source) emitted at position $\xi$ an
d time $\tau$ propagates at speed $c$ in both directions along the $x$-axis. It
can reach $x_1$ by time $t_1$ precisely when the travel distance $|\xi - x_1|$ d
oes not exceed the available propagation distance $c(t_1-\tau)$, i.e., when it l
ies within the characteristic cone emanating backward from $(x_1,t_1)$. Sources
outside this cone (either too far away, or occurring too late with $\tau > t_1$,
 or before $t=0$) have **no influence** on $u(x_1,t_1)$.

Geometrically, in the $(x,t)$-plane the cone is the isosceles triangle (for $c>0
$) with apex at $(x_1,t_1)$, base on the $x$-axis ($\tau=0$) spanning $[x_1 - ct
_1,\, x_1 + ct_1]$, and sides of slope $\pm 1/c$ (the characteristics $x \pm ct
= \text{const}$).
