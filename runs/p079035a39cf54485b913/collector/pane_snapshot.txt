## Proof

We evaluate the integral
$$I = \int_0^1 \frac{\arctan x}{x}\,dx.$$

**Power series expansion.** For $|x|\le 1$, the arctangent has the convergent se
ries
$$\arctan x = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n\, x^{2n+1}}{2n+1}.$$

Dividing by $x$ (valid for $x\neq 0$, and the integrand has a removable singular
ity at $x=0$ with limit $1$):
$$\frac{\arctan x}{x} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n\, x^{2n}}{2n+1}.$$

**Termwise integration.** The series converges uniformly on $[0,1]$ (by the Weie
rstrass $M$-test with $M_n = \tfrac{1}{2n+1}$, and $\sum \tfrac{1}{2n+1}$ is dom
inated; more precisely the alternating series converges uniformly on $[0,1]$). H
ence we may integrate term by term:
$$I = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{2n+1}\int_0^1 x^{2n}\,dx = \sum_{n=0}^{\
infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{2n+1}\cdot\frac{1}{2n+1} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n
}{(2n+1)^2}.$$

**Identifying Catalan's constant.** Catalan's constant is *defined* by
$$K = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{(2n+1)^2} = 1 - \frac{1}{9} + \frac{1}{2
5} - \frac{1}{49} + \cdots.$$

Therefore
$$I = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{(2n+1)^2} = K.$$

**Numerical confirmation.** Summing the first several terms:
- $n=0$: $+1.000000$
- $n=1$: $-0.111111$ → $0.888889$
- $n=2$: $+0.040000$ → $0.928889$
- $n=3$: $-0.020408$ → $0.908481$
- $n=4$: $+0.012346$ → $0.920827$
- $n=5$: $-0.008264$ → $0.912563$
- $n=6$: $+0.005917$ → $0.918480$
- $n=7$: $-0.004444$ → $0.914036$

The partial sums oscillate and converge toward $K \approx 0.9159655941\ldots$, w
hich is the known value of Catalan's constant.

**Conclusion.** The integral equals Catalan's constant exactly:
$$\boxed{\int_0^1 \frac{\arctan x}{x}\,dx = K \approx 0.9159655941\ldots}$$

The two quantities are not merely numerically close — they are *identical*, sinc
e both are given by the same series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^
n}{(2n+1)^2}$.
