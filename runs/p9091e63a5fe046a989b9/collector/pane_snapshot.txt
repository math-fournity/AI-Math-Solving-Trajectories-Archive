## Solution

We evaluate $\int_C \frac{z}{1-\cos z}\,dz$ where $C$ is the unit circle $|z|=1$
 traversed counterclockwise.

**Step 1: Locate the singularities.**

The integrand $f(z) = \dfrac{z}{1-\cos z}$ has singularities where $1 - \cos z =
 0$, i.e., $\cos z = 1$, which occurs at $z = 2k\pi$ for $k \in \mathbb{Z}$.

Inside the unit disk $|z| < 1$:
- $k = 0 \Rightarrow z = 0$: inside.
- $k = \pm 1 \Rightarrow z = \pm 2\pi \approx \pm 6.283$: outside ($|z| > 1$).

So the only singularity enclosed by $C$ is $z = 0$.

**Step 2: Compute the residue at $z = 0$.**

Expand $\cos z$ around $z = 0$:

$$\cos z = 1 - \frac{z^2}{2!} + \frac{z^4}{4!} - \frac{z^6}{6!} + \cdots$$

$$1 - \cos z = \frac{z^2}{2} - \frac{z^4}{24} + \frac{z^6}{720} - \cdots = \frac
{z^2}{2}\left(1 - \frac{z^2}{12} + \frac{z^4}{360} - \cdots\right)$$

Therefore:

$$\frac{z}{1-\cos z} = \frac{z}{\dfrac{z^2}{2}\left(1 - \dfrac{z^2}{12} + \cdots
\right)} = \frac{2}{z}\cdot\frac{1}{1 - \dfrac{z^2}{12} + \cdots}$$

Using the geometric series $\dfrac{1}{1-u} = 1 + u + u^2 + \cdots$ with $u = \df
rac{z^2}{12} - \cdots$:

$$\frac{z}{1-\cos z} = \frac{2}{z}\left(1 + \frac{z^2}{12} + \cdots\right) = \fr
ac{2}{z} + \frac{z}{6} + \cdots$$

The Laurent expansion has a simple pole at $z=0$ with residue (coefficient of $1
/z$):

$$\operatorname{Res}(f, 0) = 2.$$

**Step 3: Apply the Residue Theorem.**

$$\int_C \frac{z}{1-\cos z}\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}(f, 0) = 2\pi i
 \cdot 2 = 4\pi i.$$

$$\boxed{\int_C \frac{z}{1-\cos z}\,dz = 4\pi i}$$
