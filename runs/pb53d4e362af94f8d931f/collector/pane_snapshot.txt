## Proof

**Step 1: Characterize the "closer to $x_1$" region.**

The set of points closer to $x_1$ than to $x_2$ is:
$$H_1 = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : \|x - x_1\| \leq \|x - x_2\|\}$$

Expanding both sides:
$$\|x\|^2 - 2\,x \cdot x_1 + \|x_1\|^2 \leq \|x\|^2 - 2\,x \cdot x_2 + \|x_2\|^2
$$

This simplifies to:
$$x \cdot (x_2 - x_1) \leq \frac{\|x_2\|^2 - \|x_1\|^2}{2}$$

The boundary is the **perpendicular bisector hyperplane** of $x_1$ and $x_2$, wh
ich passes through the midpoint $\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}$ with normal direction $x_2 -
 x_1$.

**Step 2: Key observation — the bisector passes through the origin.**

When $\|x_1\| = \|x_2\|$, the right-hand side becomes $0$, so the bisector is:
$$\{x : x \cdot (x_2 - x_1) = 0\}$$

This is a hyperplane **through the origin**. The two half-spaces $H_1$ and $H_2$
 are then:
$$H_1 = \{x : x \cdot (x_2 - x_1) \leq 0\}, \quad H_2 = \{x : x \cdot (x_2 - x_1
) \geq 0\}$$

**Step 3: Apply spherical symmetry.**

A spherically symmetric distribution has density $f(x) = g(\|x\|)$, making it in
variant under all orthogonal transformations $T \in O(n)$.

Consider the **reflection** through the bisector hyperplane (which passes throug
h the origin with unit normal $\hat{u} = \frac{x_2 - x_1}{\|x_2 - x_1\|}$):
$$R(x) = x - 2(x \cdot \hat{u})\,\hat{u}$$

This reflection is an orthogonal transformation ($R \in O(n)$), so by spherical
symmetry:
$$P(X \in H_1) = P(R(X) \in H_1) = P(X \in R^{-1}(H_1)) = P(X \in H_2)$$

since $R$ maps $H_1$ to $H_2$ and vice versa.

**Step 4: Conclude.**

Since $H_1 \cup H_2 = \mathbb{R}^n$ (up to the measure-zero bisector hyperplane)
 and $P(X \in H_1) = P(X \in H_2)$:

$$P(X \in H_1) = P(X \in H_2) = \frac{1}{2}$$

**Why the answer is distribution-free:** The key is that the bisector passes thr
ough the origin (the center of symmetry). For any spherically symmetric distribu
tion — whether Gaussian, uniform on a sphere, or any other — the reflection symm
etry about any hyperplane through the origin guarantees equal probability in bot
h half-spaces. The specific form of $g(\|x\|)$ is irrelevant.

$$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}}$$
