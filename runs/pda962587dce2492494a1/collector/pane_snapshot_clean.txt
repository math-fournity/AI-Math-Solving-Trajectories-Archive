# Solution

**Claim:** Every real symmetric $n \times n$ matrix can be written as $M = \sum_
{i=1}^{k} j_i v_i v_i^T$ with scalars $j_i$ and vectors $v_i \in \mathbb{R}^n$,
and the minimum value of $k$ (valid for *all* symmetric matrices) is $\boxed{n}$
.

## Proof

### Upper bound: $k = n$ suffices

Let $M$ be any real symmetric $n \times n$ matrix. By the **Spectral Theorem**,
$M$ is orthogonally diagonalizable: there exists an orthogonal matrix $Q = [q_1
\mid \cdots \mid q_n]$ and real eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$ such t
hat

$$M = Q \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n) Q^T = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \l
ambda_i\, q_i q_i^T.$$

Setting $j_i = \lambda_i$ and $v_i = q_i$ gives exactly the required form with $
k = n$. Hence every symmetric matrix admits such a representation with $n$ terms
.

### Lower bound: $k = n$ is necessary

We exhibit a single symmetric matrix that forces $k \ge n$: the identity matrix
$I_n$.

For any scalar $j_i$ and vector $v_i$:
- If $j_i = 0$ or $v_i = 0$, the term $j_i v_i v_i^T$ is the zero matrix, of ran
k $0$.
- Otherwise, $v_i v_i^T$ is the outer product of a nonzero vector with itself, h
ence has rank $1$; multiplying by the nonzero scalar $j_i$ preserves rank $1$.

So every summand $j_i v_i v_i^T$ has rank $\le 1$. By subadditivity of rank,

$$\operatorname{rank}\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k} j_i v_i v_i^T\right) \le \sum_{i=1}^
{k} \operatorname{rank}(j_i v_i v_i^T) \le k.$$

The identity matrix satisfies $\operatorname{rank}(I_n) = n$. Therefore any repr
esentation $I_n = \sum_{i=1}^{k} j_i v_i v_i^T$ must obey $k \ge n$.

### Conclusion

The upper and lower bounds match: $k = n$ is both sufficient (by the Spectral Th
eorem) and necessary (by the rank argument applied to $I_n$). Thus the minimum n
umber of vectors required is

$$k_{\min} = n.$$
