**Answer: Yes, $P$ must be zero everywhere.**

**Proof.**

Suppose for contradiction that $P \neq 0$ vanishes on $\mathcal{S}$.

**Case $d = 0$:** $P$ is a nonzero constant, so $Z(P) = \emptyset$. But $\mathca
l{S} \neq \emptyset$ (any point in $\mathbb{R}^2$ is within distance 1 of some p
oint of $\mathcal{S}$), and $P$ vanishing on $\mathcal{S}$ requires $\mathcal{S}
 \subseteq Z(P) = \emptyset$, contradiction.

**Case $d \geq 1$:** Write $P = P_d + P_{d-1} + \cdots + P_0$ where $P_k$ is hom
ogeneous of degree $k$ and $P_d \neq 0$.

Since $\mathcal{S} \subseteq Z(P)$ and $\mathcal{S}$ is a 1-net, the superset $Z
(P)$ is also a 1-net: every point of $\mathbb{R}^2$ lies within distance 1 of $\
mathcal{S} \subseteq Z(P)$.

**Finding a direction where $Z(P)$ escapes to infinity.** The leading form $P_d$
 is a nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree $d$. It cannot vanish identically
 on $S^1$: if $P_d|_{S^1} \equiv 0$, then by homogeneity $P_d$ vanishes on all o
f $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$, hence $P_d \equiv 0$, contradicting $P_d \neq
0$.

The function $f(\theta) := P_d(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ is a trigonometric polyn
omial of degree $d$ (each monomial $\cos^j\theta\,\sin^{d-j}\theta$ is a linear
combination of $e^{im\theta}$ for $|m| \leq d$). Since $f \not\equiv 0$, it has
at most $2d$ zeros on $[0, 2\pi)$. Choose $\theta_0$ with $f(\theta_0) \neq 0$.

**$Z(P)$ is excluded from a sector at infinity.** By continuity, there exist $\e
psilon > 0$ and $c_0 > 0$ such that $|P_d(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)| \geq c_0$ for
 $|\theta - \theta_0| \leq \epsilon$. Set $M = \max_{k < d}\,\max_\theta |P_k(\c
os\theta,\sin\theta)|$. For $r \geq 1$:

$$|P(r\cos\theta, r\sin\theta)| \geq r^d c_0 - \sum_{k=0}^{d-1} r^k M \geq r^{d-
1}(r\,c_0 - dM).$$

Choose $R = \max(1,\, 2dM/c_0)$. Then for all $r \geq R$ and $|\theta - \theta_0
| \leq \epsilon$:

$$P(r\cos\theta,\, r\sin\theta) \neq 0.$$

So $Z(P) \cap \{(r,\theta) : r \geq R,\; |\theta - \theta_0| \leq \epsilon\} = \
emptyset$.

**Exhibiting a point far from $Z(P)$.** Let $p = (r\cos\theta_0,\, r\sin\theta_0
)$ with $r$ large. Since $Z(P)$ avoids the sector $\{r \geq R,\; |\theta-\theta_
0| \leq \epsilon\}$, it is contained in:

$$A = \{q : \|q\| \leq R\} \quad\cup\quad B = \{q : \|q\| > R,\; |\arg q - \thet
a_0| \geq \epsilon\}.$$

*Distance from $p$ to $A$:* at least $r - R$.

*Distance from $p$ to $B$:* any $q = (\rho\cos\phi, \rho\sin\phi) \in B$ satisfi
es $\rho > R$ and $|\phi - \theta_0| \geq \epsilon$, so

$$\|p - q\|^2 = r^2 + \rho^2 - 2r\rho\cos(\phi - \theta_0) \geq r^2 + \rho^2 - 2
r\rho\cos\epsilon = (r - \rho\cos\epsilon)^2 + \rho^2\sin^2\epsilon.$$

Minimizing over $\rho > R$ (with $|\phi - \theta_0| = \epsilon$): the minimizer
is $\rho = r\cos\epsilon$ (valid when $r\cos\epsilon > R$), giving

$$\operatorname{dist}(p, B) = r\cos\epsilon\sin\epsilon = \tfrac{r}{2}\sin(2\eps
ilon).$$

Both $r - R$ and $\frac{r}{2}\sin(2\epsilon)$ grow linearly in $r$. For $r$ suff
iciently large (namely $r > \max\!\big(R+1,\; R/\!\cos\epsilon,\; 2/\!\sin(2\eps
ilon)\big)$):

$$\operatorname{dist}(p,\, Z(P)) \geq \min\!\big(r - R,\; \tfrac{r}{2}\sin(2\eps
ilon)\big) > 1.$$

This contradicts $Z(P)$ being a 1-net. Therefore $P = 0$. $\blacksquare$
