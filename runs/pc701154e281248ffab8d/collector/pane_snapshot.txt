**Problem.** Find a formula for the area of a hyperbolic $n$-gon in terms of its
 interior angles $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_n$.

**Answer.** The area is

$$\boxed{\text{Area} = (n-2)\pi - \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i.}$$

---

**Proof.**

We use two ingredients:

1. **Gauss–Bonnet for a hyperbolic triangle.** For a geodesic triangle $T$ in th
e hyperbolic plane with interior angles $A, B, C$,

$$\text{Area}(T) = \pi - (A+B+C).$$

This follows from the Gauss–Bonnet theorem: with Gaussian curvature $K=-1$,

$$\iint_T K\,dA + \sum_{\text{vertices}}(\pi - \text{interior angle}) = 2\pi,$$

so $-\text{Area}(T) + 3\pi - (A+B+C) = 2\pi$, giving $\text{Area}(T)=\pi-(A+B+C)
$.

2. **Triangulation.** Any simple geodesic $n$-gon $P$ with vertices $V_1,\dots,V
_n$ can be triangulated into $n-2$ geodesic triangles by drawing the $n-3$ diago
nals from $V_1$ to $V_3, V_4, \dots, V_{n-1}$. The resulting triangles are

$$T_2=(V_1,V_2,V_3),\quad T_3=(V_1,V_3,V_4),\quad \dots,\quad T_{n-1}=(V_1,V_{n-
1},V_n),$$

for a total of $n-2$ triangles whose union is $P$ and whose interiors are disjoi
nt.

**Angle accounting.** We sum the interior angles of all $n-2$ triangles and comp
are with the polygon's interior angles $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n$.

- **At $V_1$:** the $n-2$ triangles all share $V_1$, and their angles at $V_1$ p
artition the full interior angle $\alpha_1$ of the polygon. Their sum is $\alpha
_1$.
- **At $V_2$:** only $T_2$ touches $V_2$, and its angle there is the entire inte
rior angle $\alpha_2$.
- **At $V_n$:** only $T_{n-1}$ touches $V_n$, and its angle there is the entire
interior angle $\alpha_n$.
- **At $V_k$ for $3 \le k \le n-1$:** exactly two triangles, $T_{k-1}=(V_1,V_{k-
1},V_k)$ and $T_k=(V_1,V_k,V_{k+1})$, meet at $V_k$. Their two angles at $V_k$ p
artition the polygon's interior angle $\alpha_k$, so they sum to $\alpha_k$.

Therefore the total angle sum over all triangles is

$$\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\bigl(\text{angle sum of }T_j\bigr) \;=\; \alpha_1 + \alpha_2
+ \cdots + \alpha_n \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_i.$$

**Summing the areas.** By additivity of area over the disjoint triangulation and
 the triangle formula,

$$\text{Area}(P) \;=\; \sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\text{Area}(T_j) \;=\; \sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\B
igl[\pi - \bigl(\text{angle sum of }T_j\bigr)\Bigr].$$

The first sum contributes $(n-2)\pi$ (one $\pi$ per triangle, $n-2$ triangles).
The second sum is the total angle sum computed above, $\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_i$.
Hence

$$\text{Area}(P) \;=\; (n-2)\pi - \sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_i.$$

**Consistency checks.**

- *Triangle ($n=3$):* $\text{Area}=\pi-(\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3)$, recovering
 the classical hyperbolic triangle area formula.
- *Ideal $n$-gon ($\alpha_i=0$ for all $i$):* $\text{Area}=(n-2)\pi$, the known
area of an ideal $n$-gon.
- *Euclidean limit:* in the Euclidean plane $\sum\alpha_i=(n-2)\pi$, so the form
ula gives $0$, consistent with the fact that Euclidean polygons have no curvatur
e contribution to area.

This completes the proof. $\blacksquare$
