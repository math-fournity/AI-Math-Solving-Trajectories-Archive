Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there exists a Banach space
 of doubly-indexed sequences whose unit ball is uniformly decaying at infinity.

## Analysis

The condition requires: for every ε > 0, there exists k such that all sequences
in the unit ball have entries ≤ ε outside the window [−k, k]. Equivalently, the
coordinate projection norms ‖π_n‖ → 0 as |n| → ∞.

**Key obstacle in standard spaces:** In c₀ or ℓᵖ, the standard basis vector eₙ h
as norm 1 and πₙ(eₙ) = 1, so ‖πₙ‖ = 1 for all n — this fails.

**Key idea:** Use a *weighted* space where basis vectors at distant indices have
 large norms, forcing the unit ball to be small at those coordinates.

## Proof

**Yes**, such a Banach space exists.

**Construction.** Define

$$C = \left\{v = (v_n)_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}} : \sum_{n
\in \mathbb{Z}} (1+|n|)^2\, v_n^2 < \infty\right\}$$

with the norm

$$\|v\| = \left(\sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} (1+|n|)^2\, v_n^2\right)^{1/2}.$$

**Step 1: C is a Banach space.**

Define the map $T: C \to \ell^2(\mathbb{Z})$ by $(Tv)_n = (1+|n|)\,v_n$. This ma
p is:
- **Linear:** clear from the definition.
- **Bijective:** the inverse is $(T^{-1}w)_n = w_n/(1+|n|)$, which maps $\ell^2(
\mathbb{Z})$ onto $C$ since $\sum (1+|n|)^2 (w_n/(1+|n|))^2 = \sum w_n^2 < \inft
y$.
- **Isometric:** $\|Tv\|_{\ell^2}^2 = \sum_n (1+|n|)^2 v_n^2 = \|v\|^2$.

Since $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z})$ is a complete Banach (Hilbert) space and $T$ is an is
ometric isomorphism, $C$ is also a complete Banach space. Clearly $C \subset \ma
thbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}}$.

**Step 2: The decay condition holds.**

Let $v \in C$ with $\|v\| \leq 1$. For any $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, since every term
in the sum is non-negative:

$$(1+|n|)^2\, v_n^2 \;\leq\; \sum_{m \in \mathbb{Z}} (1+|m|)^2\, v_m^2 \;=\; \|v
\|^2 \;\leq\; 1.$$

Therefore:

$$|v_n| \;\leq\; \frac{1}{1+|n|}.$$

**Step 3: Verifying the uniform condition.**

Given any $\varepsilon > 0$, choose $k \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $\frac{1}{1+k}
< \varepsilon$ (possible since $\frac{1}{1+k} \to 0$).

Then for every $v \in C$ with $\|v\| \leq 1$ and every $n$ with $|n| \geq k$:

$$|v_n| \;\leq\; \frac{1}{1+|n|} \;\leq\; \frac{1}{1+k} \;<\; \varepsilon.$$

This is exactly the required condition: the single choice of $k$ works uniformly
 for all $v$ in the unit ball.

**Remark on sharpness.** The bound is tight: the sequence $v^{(n)}$ with $v^{(n)
}_n = \frac{1}{1+|n|}$ and all other entries zero satisfies $\|v^{(n)}\| = 1$ an
d $|v^{(n)}_n| = \frac{1}{1+|n|}$, confirming $\|\pi_n\| = \frac{1}{1+|n|} \to 0
$.

$\blacksquare$
