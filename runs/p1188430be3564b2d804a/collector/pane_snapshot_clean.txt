**Answer: No.** Here is the proof.

## Proof

### Structure of the algebra and the endomorphism

A unital finite-dimensional C*-algebra has the form $A = \bigoplus_{i=1}^{k} M_{
n_i}(\mathbb{C})$. Let $e_i$ denote the unit (central projection) of the $i$-th
summand, so $\sum_i e_i = 1_A$.

Let $\phi: A \to A$ be a unital *-endomorphism. For each pair $(i,j)$, let $\phi
_{ij}: M_{n_i} \to M_{n_j}$ be the $(i,j)$-component of $\phi$. Each $\phi_{ij}$
 is a *-homomorphism, hence is either **zero** or unitarily equivalent to the am
plification map $a \mapsto a \otimes I_{n_j/n_i}$ (which requires $n_i \mid n_j$
). This follows from the fact that $M_{n_i}$ is simple with a unique irreducible
 representation (the standard one on $\mathbb{C}^{n_i}$), so every *-representat
ion on $\mathbb{C}^{n_j}$ is a direct sum of $n_j/n_i$ copies of it.

**Key constraint from unitality.** Since $\phi(1_A) = 1_A$, we have $\sum_i \phi
(e_i) = \sum_j f_j$ (where $f_j$ is the unit of the $j$-th codomain summand). Th
e $j$-th component gives $\sum_i \phi_{ij}(e_i) = I_{n_j}$. When $\phi_{ij} \neq
 0$, it is unital as a map $M_{n_i} \to M_{n_j}$, so $\phi_{ij}(e_i) = I_{n_j}$.
 Since each summand is either $0$ or $I_{n_j}$ and they sum to $I_{n_j}$, **exac
tly one** $i$ satisfies $\phi_{ij} \neq 0$ for each $j$.

Define $\sigma: \{1,\dots,k\} \to \{1,\dots,k\}$ by letting $\sigma(j)$ be the u
nique $i$ with $\phi_{ij} \neq 0$. Then:
$$\phi(v)_j = U_j\bigl(v_{\sigma(j)} \otimes I_{m_j}\bigr)U_j^*, \qquad m_j := n
_j / n_{\sigma(j)},$$
for some unitary $U_j \in M_{n_j}$.

### Decomposition into cycle and transient parts

Since $\sigma$ is a self-map of a finite set, every orbit under $\sigma$ eventua
lly enters a cycle. Partition the index set into:
- **Cycle blocks**: indices belonging to a cycle of $\sigma$.
- **Transient blocks**: indices in tails leading into cycles.

Let $A_{\text{cyc}}$ and $A_{\text{trans}}$ be the corresponding direct summands
 of $A$.

**Claim 1.** $\phi(A_{\text{cyc}}) \subseteq A_{\text{cyc}}$ and $\phi(A_{\text{
trans}}) \subseteq A_{\text{trans}}$.

*Proof.* If $j$ is a cycle block, then $\sigma(j)$ is also a cycle block (cycles
 are closed under $\sigma$), so $\phi(v)_j$ depends only on $v_{\sigma(j)} \in A
_{\text{cyc}}$. If $v \in A_{\text{trans}}$, then $v_{\sigma(j)} = 0$ for cycle
blocks $j$, so $\phi(v)_j = 0$ for all cycle blocks. Hence $\phi(A_{\text{trans}
}) \subseteq A_{\text{trans}}$ and $\phi(A_{\text{cyc}}) \subseteq A_{\text{cyc}
}$. $\square$

**Claim 2.** $\phi|_{A_{\text{trans}}}$ is nilpotent, so all its eigenvalues are
 $0$.

*Proof.* For a transient block $j$, $\phi(v)_j$ depends on $v_{\sigma(j)}$, whic
h is strictly closer to a cycle. After $r$ iterations (where $r$ is the maximum
tail length), $\sigma^r(j)$ is a cycle block for every transient $j$. Since $A_{
\text{trans}}$ has no component at cycle blocks, $\phi^r|_{A_{\text{trans}}} = 0
$. $\square$

### Eigenvalues from the cycle part

Consider a cycle of $\sigma$ of length $r$: $j_0, j_1, \dots, j_{r-1}$ with $\si
gma(j_{i+1 \bmod r}) = j_i$. The amplification factors satisfy:
$$\prod_{i=0}^{r-1} m_{j_{i+1 \bmod r}} = \prod_{i=0}^{r-1} \frac{n_{j_{i+1 \bmo
d r}}}{n_{j_i}} = 1 \quad \text{(telescoping)}.$$

Let $\rho_i: M_{n_{j_i}} \to M_{n_{j_{i+1 \bmod r}}}$ be the amplification $a \m
apsto U_{j_{i+1}}(a \otimes I_{m_{j_{i+1}}})U_{j_{i+1}}^*$.

Suppose $\phi(v) = \lambda v$ with $\lambda \neq 0$. Restricting to the cycle, t
he eigenvalue equations give:
$$v_{j_{i+1}} = \lambda^{-1}\,\rho_i(v_{j_i}).$$

Composing around the full cycle:
$$\lambda^r \, v_{j_0} = (\rho_{r-1} \circ \cdots \circ \rho_0)(v_{j_0}).$$

The composition $\Psi := \rho_{r-1} \circ \cdots \circ \rho_0 : M_{n_{j_0}} \to
M_{n_{j_0}}$ is a unital *-homomorphism (composition of unital *-homomorphisms).
 Since $M_{n_{j_0}}$ is **simple**, $\Psi$ is injective; since domain and codoma
in have the same finite dimension ($n_{j_0}^2$), $\Psi$ is bijective, hence a *-
automorphism. By the **Skolem–Noether theorem**, every *-automorphism of $M_n$ i
s inner: $\Psi(a) = W a W^*$ for some unitary $W \in M_{n_{j_0}}$.

Therefore:
$$\lambda^r \, v_{j_0} = W\, v_{j_0}\, W^*.$$

This means $\lambda^r$ is an eigenvalue of the conjugation map $\operatorname{Ad
}_W: a \mapsto Waw^*$. If $W$ has eigenvalues $\mu_1, \dots, \mu_{n_{j_0}}$ (all
 with $|\mu_s| = 1$ since $W$ is unitary), then the eigenvalues of $\operatornam
e{Ad}_W$ are $\mu_s / \mu_t$ for $s,t = 1, \dots, n_{j_0}$, all of which satisfy
:
$$\left|\frac{\mu_s}{\mu_t}\right| = \frac{|\mu_s|}{|\mu_t|} = 1.$$

Hence $|\lambda^r| = 1$, which gives $|\lambda| = 1$.

### Conclusion

- Every non-zero eigenvalue of $\phi$ arises from the cycle part $A_{\text{cyc}}
$.
- Every such eigenvalue satisfies $|\lambda| = 1$.
- The transient part $A_{\text{trans}}$ contributes only the eigenvalue $0$.

Therefore, **a unital *-endomorphism of a unital finite-dimensional C*-algebra c
annot have a non-zero eigenvalue with absolute value greater than 1**. $\blacksq
