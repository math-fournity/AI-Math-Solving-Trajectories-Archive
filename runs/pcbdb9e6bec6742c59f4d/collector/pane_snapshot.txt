# Solution

## Part (a)

We solve Laplace's equation $u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0$ in the left half-plane $\Omega=\{(
x,y):x<0,\;-\infty<y<\infty\}$ with boundary data $u(0,y)=g(y)$.

**Fourier transform in $y$.** Let $\widehat{u}(x,k)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u(x,y
)e^{-iky}\,dy$. Transforming the PDE in $y$ gives
$$\frac{d^2\widehat{u}}{dx^2}-k^{2}\widehat{u}=0,\qquad x<0,$$
with $\widehat{u}(0,k)=\widehat{g}(k)$ and the boundedness condition $\widehat{u
}(x,k)\to 0$ as $x\to-\infty$. The bounded solution is
$$\widehat{u}(x,k)=\widehat{g}(k)\,e^{|k|\,x},\qquad x<0$$
(notice $x<0$ so $e^{|k|x}\to 0$ as $x\to-\infty$).

**Inverting.** Using $e^{|k|x}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{-x}{x^{2}+s^{2}}\,e^
{iks}\,\frac{ds}{\pi}$ (the Fourier representation of the Poisson kernel, valid
for $x<0$ since $-x>0$), and the convolution theorem,
$$u(x,y)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}P(x,y-t)\,g(t)\,dt,\qquad P(x,s)=\frac{1}{\pi}\,
\frac{-x}{x^{2}+s^{2}}.$$

**Verification.** The kernel $P(x,s)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{-x}{x^{2}+s^{2}}$ (with
$-x>0$) is harmonic in $(x,s)$ for $x<0$:
$$P_{xx}+P_{ss}=\frac{1}{\pi}\left[\frac{2x^{2}-2s^{2}}{(x^{2}+s^{2})^{2}}\cdot(
-x)\Big/\cdots\right]=0,$$
a standard computation. Hence $u$ is harmonic. As $x\to 0^{-}$, $P$ is an approx
imate identity: $\int P\,ds=1$ and $P$ concentrates at $s=0$, so $u(x,y)\to g(y)
$ at every continuity point of $g$. Finally $u\to 0$ as $x\to-\infty$.

$$\boxed{\;u(x,y)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{-x}{x^{2}+(y-t)^{2}}
\,g(t)\,dt,\qquad x<0.\;}$$

## Part (b)

Here $g(t)=\mathbf{1}_{[-1,1]}(t)$. Substituting,
$$u(x,y)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{-x}{x^{2}+(y-t)^{2}}\,dt.$$

Set $a=-x>0$ and substitute $s=\dfrac{t-y}{a}$, $dt=a\,ds$:
$$u(x,y)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\frac{-1-y}{a}}^{\frac{1-y}{a}}\frac{a}{a^{2}+a^{2}s
^{2}}\,a\,ds=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\frac{-1-y}{a}}^{\frac{1-y}{a}}\frac{ds}{1+s^{2}
}=\frac{1}{\pi}\Big[\arctan s\Big]_{\frac{-1-y}{a}}^{\frac{1-y}{a}}.$$

Therefore
$$u(x,y)=\frac{1}{\pi}\left[\arctan\!\left(\frac{1-y}{-x}\right)+\arctan\!\left(
\frac{1+y}{-x}\right)\right],\qquad x<0.$$

**Sanity checks.**
- On the boundary $x\to 0^{-}$ with $|y|<1$: both arguments $\to+\infty$, so eac
h arctan $\to\pi/2$, giving $u\to\frac{1}{\pi}(\pi/2+\pi/2)=1$. ✓
- With $|y|>1$: one argument $\to+\infty$, the other $\to-\infty$, giving $\frac
{1}{\pi}(\pi/2-\pi/2)=0$. ✓
- At $y=\pm1$: $u\to\frac{1}{2}$, the expected value at a jump discontinuity. ✓
- As $x\to-\infty$: arguments $\to 0$, so $u\to 0$. ✓

$$\boxed{\;u(x,y)=\frac{1}{\pi}\left[\arctan\!\left(\frac{1-y}{-x}\right)+\arcta
n\!\left(\frac{1+y}{-x}\right)\right],\qquad x<0.\;}$$
