**Key equivalence.** An embedding $\Sigma_n \hookrightarrow O(N)$ is precisely a
 faithful real representation $\rho: \Sigma_n \to O(N)$ of dimension $N$. Thus t

nimal dimension $d(n)$ of a faithful real representation of $\Sigma_n$: we need
$N \geq d(n)$, so the answer is $n = \max\{m : d(m) \leq N\}$.

**Claim:** For all $n \geq 2$, $d(n) = n - 1$.

**Upper bound: $d(n) \leq n-1$.** The permutation representation of $\Sigma_n$ o
n $\mathbb{R}^n$ (permuting coordinates) is faithful and orthogonal. It decompos
es as $\mathbf{1} \oplus V_{\text{std}}$, where $\mathbf{1}$ is the trivial repr
esentation (spanned by $(1,\ldots,1)$) and $V_{\text{std}} = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^
n : \sum x_i = 0\}$ is the **standard representation** of dimension $n-1$. For $
n \geq 3$, $V_{\text{std}}$ is faithful: its kernel consists of permutations fix
ing every vector in the hyperplane $\sum x_i = 0$, which forces the permutation
to be the identity (since the differences $e_i - e_j$ span this hyperplane and a
 permutation fixing all of them must fix every index). For $n = 2$, the sign rep
resentation (dimension $1 = n-1$) is faithful. So $d(n) \leq n-1$.

**Lower bound: $d(n) \geq n-1$.** We show no faithful representation of dimensio
n $< n-1$ exists. Any real representation decomposes as a direct sum of irreduci
bles, and its kernel is the intersection of the kernels of its irreducible summa
nds. We use the classification of irreducible representations of $\Sigma_n$ (par
ametrized by partitions of $n$) and the key fact:

> **Fact:** For $n \geq 5$, the only irreducible representations of $\Sigma_n$ o
f dimension $< n-1$ are the trivial representation $\mathbf{1}$ (dimension 1, ke
rnel $= \Sigma_n$) and the sign representation $\text{sgn}$ (dimension 1, kernel
 $= A_n$).

This is a classical result from the representation theory of symmetric groups: t
he standard representation $(n-1,1)$ is the unique smallest non-linear irreducib
le, and all other non-trivial irreducibles have dimension $\geq n-1$ (in fact $\
geq \binom{n}{2} - 1$ for the next ones when $n$ is large).

Any representation of dimension $< n-1$ for $n \geq 5$ can only involve $\mathbf
{1}$ and $\text{sgn}$. Its kernel is:
- $\Sigma_n$ if only $\mathbf{1}$ appears,
- $A_n$ if only $\text{sgn}$ appears (or $\mathbf{1} \oplus \text{sgn}$),
- $A_n$ in any combination.

Since $A_n \neq \{e\}$ for $n \geq 3$, no such representation is faithful. Hence
 $d(n) \geq n-1$.

**Small cases ($n = 2, 3, 4$):**

- **$n = 2$:** The only irreducible of dimension $< 1$ is trivial (dimension 0,
not useful). The sign representation has dimension 1 and is faithful. So $d(2) =
 1 = n-1$. ✓

- **$n = 3$:** Irreducibles of dimension $< 2$: only $\mathbf{1}$ and $\text{sgn
}$ (both dimension 1). Any combination has kernel containing $A_3 \cong \mathbb{
Z}/3 \neq \{e\}$. So $d(3) = 2 = n-1$. ✓

- **$n = 4$:** Irreducibles of dimension $< 3$: $\mathbf{1}$ (dim 1, kernel $\Si
gma_4$), $\text{sgn}$ (dim 1, kernel $A_4$), and $(2,2)$ (dim 2). The representa
tion $(2,2)$ has character $\chi(e)=2$, $\chi(\text{double transposition})=2$, s

ations of these three irreducibles: every combination has kernel $\supseteq V_4$

**Conclusion.** For all $n \geq 2$, $d(n) = n-1$. Therefore $\Sigma_n$ embeds in
 $O(N)$ if and only if $N \geq n - 1$, i.e., $n \leq N + 1$.

The largest integer $n$ such that $\Sigma_n \hookrightarrow O(N)$ is:
