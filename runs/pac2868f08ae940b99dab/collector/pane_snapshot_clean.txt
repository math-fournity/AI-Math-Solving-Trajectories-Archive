Claim: Yes, there exists an affine scheme with exactly two points and dimension
1.

Construction. Let $k$ be a field and let $R = k[[t]]$ be the ring of formal
power series over $k$. Set $X = \operatorname{Spec}(R)$.

$R$ is a DVR. Every nonzero element $f \in k[[t]]$ can be written uniquely as $f
= t^n u$ where $n \geq 0$ and $u \in k[[t]]^{\times}$ is a unit (with $u$ having
nonzero constant term). This makes $R$ a discrete valuation ring with
uniformizer $t$, maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m} = (t)$, and residue field $R/
\mathfrak{m} \cong k$.

$R$ has exactly two prime ideals. The zero ideal $(0)$ is prime since $R$ is an
integral domain. Now let $\mathfrak{p}$ be any nonzero prime ideal. Pick $0 \neq
f \in \mathfrak{p}$ and write $f = t^n u$ with $u$ a unit. Since $\mathfrak{p}$
is a proper ideal, $u \notin \mathfrak{p}$, and since $\mathfrak{p}$ is prime
and $t^n = f \cdot u^{-1} \cdot u \in \mathfrak{p}$... more directly: $f = t^n u
\in \mathfrak{p}$ and $u$ is a unit, so $t^n \in \mathfrak{p}$. Since $
\mathfrak{p}$ is prime, $t \in \mathfrak{p}$, hence $(t) \subseteq
\mathfrak{p}$. But $(t)$ is maximal (as $R/(t) \cong k$ is a field), so $
\mathfrak{p} = (t)$.

Therefore the only prime ideals of $R$ are $(0)$ and $(t)$, giving $
\operatorname{Spec}(R)$ exactly two points.

The dimension is 1. The unique chain of distinct prime ideals is $$(0)
\subsetneq (t),$$ which has length 1. There is no longer chain (there are only
two primes total), and the chain is nontrivial (the inclusion is strict). Hence
$\dim R = 1$.

spectrum of a commutative ring) with exactly two points and Krull dimension 1. $
