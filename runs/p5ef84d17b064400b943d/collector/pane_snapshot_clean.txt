Looking at this problem, I need to determine the minimum $k$ such that every par
tition of $\mathbb{R}^2$ into connected sets is $k$-colorable, where two parts a
re adjacent if their closures intersect.

**Answer: $k = \infty$.**

## Proof

**Claim:** For every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, there exists a partition of $\mathbb{R}
^2$ into connected sets whose adjacency graph has chromatic number at least $n$.
 Consequently, no finite $k$ suffices.

### Construction (Generalized Lakes of Wada)

Fix $n \geq 5$. We construct $n$ pairwise disjoint connected open sets $U_1, \do
ts, U_n$ inside the closed unit disk $\overline{D} \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ that **
share a common boundary**.

**Procedure:** Enumerate a countable dense subset $\{q_1, q_2, \dots\}$ of $\ove
rline{D}$. We iteratively dig canals. At stage $m$, we extend the canal of lake
$i = ((m-1) \bmod n)+1$ by carving a thin open strip that:
- remains within $\overline{D}$,
- does not touch any existing canal of another lake,
- reaches within distance $1/m$ of the point $q_m$.

After countably many stages:
- Each $U_i$ is **connected** (indeed path-connected): it is a single canal exte
nded countably many times, each extension connected to the previous.
- Each $U_i$ is **open** (a union of open strips).
- The $U_i$ are **pairwise disjoint** by construction.
- Every point of $\overline{D}$ lies in $\overline{U_i}$ for every $i$, since th
e canals get arbitrarily close to every point of the dense set $\{q_m\}$, hence
to every point of $\overline{D}$ by density.

Let $B = \overline{D} \setminus \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} U_i$. Then:
$$\partial U_1 = \partial U_2 = \cdots = \partial U_n = B.$$

### Forming a Partition of $\mathbb{R}^2$

Partition $\mathbb{R}^2$ into:
- the $n$ connected open sets $U_1, \dots, U_n$,
- the connected components of $B$ (each is a connected set),
- the exterior $E = \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \overline{D}$, which is connected.

This is a partition of $\mathbb{R}^2$ into connected sets.

### The Adjacency Graph Contains $K_n$

For any $i \neq j$:
$$\overline{U_i} \cap \overline{U_j} \supseteq B \neq \emptyset.$$

Therefore every pair of distinct lakes is adjacent. The adjacency graph contains
 the complete graph $K_n$ as a subgraph, so its chromatic number is at least $\c
hi(K_n) = n$.

Since $n$ is arbitrary, for every finite $k$ there exists a partition requiring
more than $k$ colors. Hence:
$$\boxed{k = \infty}.$$

### Applicability of the Four Color Theorem

The Four Color Theorem is **not applicable** in this context. Here is why its co
nditions fail:

1. **The FCT requires a planar graph.** It states that every planar graph is 4-c
olorable and applies to dual graphs of planar maps — cell decompositions of the
sphere (or plane) into finitely many simply connected regions (faces homeomorphi
c to disks).

2. **The adjacency graph here need not be planar.** The construction above produ
ces an adjacency graph containing $K_n$ for any $n$. Since $K_n$ is non-planar f
or $n \geq 5$, the adjacency graph is non-planar, and the FCT cannot be invoked.

3. **The regions are not "tame."** In a planar map to which the FCT applies, eac
h face is simply connected and only finitely many faces meet at any point. The L
akes of Wada regions have fractal-like common boundaries; infinitely many region
s accumulate at every boundary point, and the regions are not simply connected.
The resulting structure is not a finite cell decomposition.

4. **When the FCT *would* apply:** If we restricted to partitions where each par
t is a connected open set whose closure is homeomorphic to a closed disk, only f
initely many parts meet at any point, and the partition forms a finite cell comp
lex, then the adjacency graph would be planar and the FCT would give $k = 4$. Bu
t for arbitrary topological partitions into connected sets — with no restriction
 on the topology of the parts — the adjacency graph can be any finite graph (or
at least contain $K_n$ for all $n$), and no finite bound exists.
