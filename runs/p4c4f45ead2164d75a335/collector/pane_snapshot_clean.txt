## Proof

A three-digit number is **colorful** if its three digits are all distinct. We se
ek the minimum number of colorful numbers that *guarantees* at least one is divi
sible by 3.

By the pigeonhole principle, this equals
$$\bigl(\text{maximum number of colorful numbers not divisible by }3\bigr) + 1.$
$

---

### Total colorful numbers

The hundreds digit $h\in\{1,\dots,9\}$ (9 choices), the tens digit $t\in\{0,\dot
s,9\}\setminus\{h\}$ (9 choices), and the units digit $u\in\{0,\dots,9\}\setminu
s\{h,t\}$ (8 choices). Hence
$$\text{Total} = 9\cdot 9\cdot 8 = 648.$$

---

### Colorful numbers divisible by 3

A number is divisible by 3 iff its digit sum $h+t+u\equiv 0\pmod 3$. Partition d
igits by residue mod 3:

| Residue | Digits | Count |
|---|---|---|
| $0$ | $\{0,3,6,9\}$ | 4 |
| $1$ | $\{1,4,7\}$ | 3 |
| $2$ | $\{2,5,8\}$ | 3 |

For the hundreds digit, $0$ is excluded, so residue-0 contributes only $\{3,6,9\
}$ (3 choices); residues 1 and 2 still contribute 3 each.

We count ordered residue triples $(r_h,r_t,r_u)$ with $r_h+r_t+r_u\equiv 0\pmod
3$.

**Same-residue cases** (all three digits from one residue class, automatically d
istinct within the class):
- $(0,0,0)$: $h\in\{3,6,9\}$ (3), then $t,u$ from the 3 remaining residue-0 digi
ts: $3\cdot 3\cdot 2 = 18$.
- $(1,1,1)$: $3\cdot 2\cdot 1 = 6$.
- $(2,2,2)$: $3\cdot 2\cdot 1 = 6$.

Subtotal: $18+6+6 = 30$.

**All-distinct-residue cases** (permutations of $(0,1,2)$; digits are automatica
lly distinct since residues differ):
- $(0,1,2)$ and $(0,2,1)$: $h$ from residue 0 (3 choices), $t,u$ from residue cl
asses of size 3 each: $3\cdot3\cdot3 = 27$ each → $54$.
- $(1,0,2),(1,2,0),(2,0,1),(2,1,0)$: $h$ from residue 1 or 2 (3 choices), and on
e of $t,u$ comes from residue 0 (4 choices), the other from size 3: $3\cdot4\cdo
t3 = 36$ each → $144$.

Subtotal: $54+144 = 198$.

**Total divisible by 3:** $30 + 198 = 228$.

*Verification.* Summing per hundreds digit $d=1,\dots,9$: the counts are $26,26,
24,26,26,24,26,26,24$, giving $26\cdot6+24\cdot3 = 156+72 = 228$. ✓

---

### Colorful numbers *not* divisible by 3

$$648 - 228 = 420.$$

---

### Conclusion

We can select all $420$ colorful numbers that are not divisible by $3$; this set
 contains no multiple of $3$. Therefore $420$ colorful numbers do **not** suffic
e. But any collection of $421$ colorful numbers must include at least one from t
he $228$ divisible by $3$, since at most $420$ can avoid them.

$$\boxed{421}$$
