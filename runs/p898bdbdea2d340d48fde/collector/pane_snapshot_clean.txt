## Proof

Let \(B_n = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : \|x\| \le 1\}\) be the unit ball, and let \(X
, Y\) be independent random vectors uniformly distributed in \(B_n\). We compute
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty} \mathbb{E}\big[\|X - Y\|\big].
\]

**Decomposition into radius and direction.** Write \(X = R_1 U\) and \(Y = R_2 V
\), where:
- \(U, V\) are independent and uniformly distributed on the unit sphere \(S^{n-1
}\);
- \(R_1, R_2\) are independent radii with density \(f_R(r) = n r^{n-1}\) on \([0
,1]\);
- all four random quantities are mutually independent.

This is valid because, for a uniform point in the ball, the direction is uniform
 on the sphere and independent of the radius, with \(R \stackrel{d}{=} U_0^{1/n}
\) for \(U_0 \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)\).

**Step 1: The radii concentrate at 1.** Since \(R_i = U_i^{1/n}\) with \(U_i \si
m \mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)\), for any \(\varepsilon > 0\),
\[
\mathbb{P}(|R_i - 1| > \varepsilon) = \mathbb{P}(R_i < 1 - \varepsilon) = (1-\va
repsilon)^n \to 0.
\]
Hence \(R_i \to 1\) in probability.

**Step 2: The inner product of two independent uniform unit vectors concentrates
 at 0.** By rotational symmetry, fix \(U = e_1\); then \(U \cdot V = V_1\), the
first coordinate of a uniform unit vector. A standard representation gives
\[
V_1 \stackrel{d}{=} \frac{Z_1}{\sqrt{Z_1^2 + \cdots + Z_n^2}}, \qquad Z_1, \dots
, Z_n \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} N(0,1).
\]
By the law of large numbers, \(Z_1^2 + \cdots + Z_n^2 \to n\) a.s., while \(Z_1
= O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)\). Therefore
\[
U \cdot V = V_1 \to 0 \quad \text{in probability}.
\]

**Step 3: \(\|X - Y\|^2 \to 2\) in probability.** We have
\[
\|X - Y\|^2 = R_1^2 + R_2^2 - 2 R_1 R_2 (U \cdot V).
\]
By Steps 1 and 2, \(R_1^2 \to 1\), \(R_2^2 \to 1\), and \(R_1 R_2 (U\cdot V) \to
 0\), all in probability (using the fact that \(R_1, R_2\) are bounded, so produ
cts of convergent-in-probability sequences converge in probability). Hence
\[
\|X - Y\|^2 \to 2 \quad \text{in probability},
\]
and by the continuous mapping theorem,
\[
\|X - Y\| \to \sqrt{2} \quad \text{in probability}.
\]

**Step 4: Convergence of the expectation.** Since \(X, Y \in B_n\), we have \(\|
X - Y\| \le 2\) almost surely. The random variables \(\|X - Y\|\) are uniformly
bounded, so convergence in probability implies convergence in \(L^1\) (by the bo
unded convergence theorem / a direct dominated-convergence argument with dominat
ing constant \(2\)):
\[
\mathbb{E}\big[\|X - Y\|\big] \to \sqrt{2}.
\]

**Conclusion.** The limit is
\[
\boxed{\sqrt{2}}.
\]
