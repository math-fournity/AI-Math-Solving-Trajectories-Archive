## Proof

We want a constant $M$ such that $\|f\|_{L^2} \le M\,\|f\|_H$ for all $f \in L^2
(-1,1)$, where

$$\|f\|_H^2 = \int_{-1}^{1} |f(x)|^2 \frac{1}{1+x^2}\,dx, \qquad \|f\|_{L^2}^2 =
 \int_{-1}^{1} |f(x)|^2\,dx.$$

**Step 1: Pointwise comparison of the weights.**

On $[-1,1]$ we have $0 \le x^2 \le 1$, hence

$$1 \le 1+x^2 \le 2.$$

Dividing by $1+x^2 > 0$:

$$\frac{1}{1+x^2} \ge \frac{1}{2}, \qquad \text{i.e.} \quad 1 \le 2\cdot\frac{1}
{1+x^2} \quad \text{for all } x\in[-1,1].$$

**Step 2: The upper bound $\|f\|_{L^2}^2 \le 2\,\|f\|_H^2$.**

For any $f\in L^2(-1,1)$,

$$\|f\|_{L^2}^2 = \int_{-1}^{1}|f(x)|^2\cdot 1\,dx \le \int_{-1}^{1}|f(x)|^2\cdo
t 2\cdot\frac{1}{1+x^2}\,dx = 2\,\|f\|_H^2.$$

Taking square roots:

$$\|f\|_{L^2} \le \sqrt{2}\,\|f\|_H.$$

So $M = \sqrt{2}$ works.

**Step 3: Sharpness — no smaller constant suffices.**

We show the ratio $\|f\|_{L^2}^2/\|f\|_H^2$ can be made arbitrarily close to $2$
, so $M$ cannot be taken smaller than $\sqrt{2}$.

For $\varepsilon > 0$, let $f_\varepsilon(x) = \mathbf{1}_{[1-\varepsilon,\,1]}(
x)$ (the indicator of the interval $[1-\varepsilon, 1]$). Then

$$\frac{\|f_\varepsilon\|_{L^2}^2}{\|f_\varepsilon\|_H^2} = \frac{\int_{1-\varep
silon}^{1} 1\,dx}{\int_{1-\varepsilon}^{1} \frac{1}{1+x^2}\,dx} = \frac{\varepsi
lon}{\displaystyle\int_{1-\varepsilon}^{1}\frac{dx}{1+x^2}}.$$

By the mean value theorem for integrals (the integrand $1/(1+x^2)$ is continuous
), there exists $c_\varepsilon \in [1-\varepsilon, 1]$ with

$$\int_{1-\varepsilon}^{1}\frac{dx}{1+x^2} = \frac{\varepsilon}{1+c_\varepsilon^
2}.$$

As $\varepsilon\to 0^+$, we have $c_\varepsilon \to 1$, so $1+c_\varepsilon^2 \t
o 2$. Therefore

$$\frac{\|f_\varepsilon\|_{L^2}^2}{\|f_\varepsilon\|_H^2} = 1+c_\varepsilon^2 \;
\longrightarrow\; 2 \quad \text{as } \varepsilon\to 0^+.$$

Hence $\sup_{f\ne 0} \frac{\|f\|_{L^2}}{\|f\|_H} = \sqrt{2}$, and the supremum i
s not attained (it would require $f$ supported only at $x=\pm 1$, which is a nul
l set).

**Conclusion.**

The optimal (smallest) constant is

$$\boxed{M = \sqrt{2}}.$$
