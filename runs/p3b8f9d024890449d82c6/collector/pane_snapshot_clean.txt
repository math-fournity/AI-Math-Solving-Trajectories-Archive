# Power Iteration Convergence for a Normal Matrix

## Setup

Let $A$ be normal ($A^*A = AA^*$), hence unitarily diagonalizable: $A = U\Lambda
 U^*$ with $U = [u_1,\dots,u_n]$ unitary and $\Lambda = \mathrm{diag}(\lambda_1,
\dots,\lambda_n)$. Assume a **strictly dominant** eigenvalue
$$|\lambda_1| > |\lambda_2| \ge \cdots \ge |\lambda_n|,$$
and define the **spectral gap ratio**
$$\theta \;=\; \left|\frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1}\right| \;<\; 1.$$

Power iteration with a start $x_0$ (normalized, $\|x_0\|=1$) produces
$$x_{k+1} = \frac{A x_k}{\|A x_k\|}, \qquad r_k = x_k^* A x_k \;\;(\text{Rayleig
h quotient}).$$

Expand $x_0 = \sum_i c_i u_i$ with $c_1\neq 0$. Then
$$A^k x_0 = c_1 \lambda_1^k\!\left(u_1 + \sum_{i\ge 2}\alpha_i\!\left(\tfrac{\la
mbda_i}{\lambda_1}\right)^{\!k} u_i\right), \qquad \alpha_i := \frac{c_i}{c_1}.$
$

Define the **error vector**
$$\delta_k := \sum_{i\ge 2}\alpha_i\!\left(\tfrac{\lambda_i}{\lambda_1}\right)^{
\!k} u_i, \qquad \|\delta_k\|^2 = \sum_{i\ge 2}|\alpha_i|^2\!\left|\tfrac{\lambd
a_i}{\lambda_1}\right|^{2k}.$$

Since $A$ is normal, the eigenvectors are orthonormal, so $\|\delta_k\|$ is exac
tly the norm of the off-eigenvector part. The normalized iterate is
$$x_k = \frac{u_1 + \delta_k}{\|u_1+\delta_k\|}, \qquad \|u_1+\delta_k\|^2 = 1+\
|\delta_k\|^2.$$

---

## 1. Eigenvector angle — linear rate $\theta$

Let $\phi_k$ be the angle between $x_k$ and $u_1$. Since $u_1\perp\delta_k$,
$$\sin\phi_k = \frac{\|\delta_k\|}{\|u_1+\delta_k\|} = \frac{\|\delta_k\|}{\sqrt
{1+\|\delta_k\|^2}}.$$

For large $k$, $\|\delta_k\|\to 0$, so $\sin\phi_k \sim \|\delta_k\|$, and the d
ominant term of $\|\delta_k\|$ is the $i=2$ contribution:
$$\boxed{\;\sin\phi_k \;\sim\; |\alpha_2|\,\theta^{\,k}\;} \qquad (k\to\infty).$
$

**Qualitatively:** the angle decays **linearly** with asymptotic rate $\theta$ p
er iteration. On a semilog plot ($\log\sin\phi_k$ vs $k$) this is a straight lin
e of slope $\log\theta$. An initial transient (a plateau or steeper drop) occurs
 while the $i\ge 3$ components are still comparable to the $i=2$ component; once
 $|\lambda_2/\lambda_1|^k$ dominates, the line settles to its asymptotic slope.

---

## 2. Eigenvalue (Rayleigh quotient) error — linear rate $\theta^2$

Using orthonormality of the $u_i$,
$$r_k = \frac{(u_1+\delta_k)^* A (u_1+\delta_k)}{\|u_1+\delta_k\|^2}
     = \frac{\lambda_1 + \sum_{i\ge 2}|\alpha_i|^2\!\left|\tfrac{\lambda_i}{\lam
bda_1}\right|^{2k}\!\lambda_i}{1+\sum_{i\ge 2}|\alpha_i|^2\!\left|\tfrac{\lambda
_i}{\lambda_1}\right|^{2k}}.$$

Writing $S_k := \sum_{i\ge 2}|\alpha_i|^2\!\left|\tfrac{\lambda_i}{\lambda_1}\ri
ght|^{2k}$,
$$r_k - \lambda_1 = \frac{\sum_{i\ge 2}|\alpha_i|^2\!\left|\tfrac{\lambda_i}{\la
mbda_1}\right|^{2k}(\lambda_i-\lambda_1)}{1+S_k} \;\sim\; |\alpha_2|^2(\lambda_2
-\lambda_1)\,\theta^{\,2k}.$$

Hence
$$\boxed{\;|r_k-\lambda_1| \;\sim\; |\alpha_2|^2\,|\lambda_2-\lambda_1|\,\theta^
{\,2k}\;}.$$

**Qualitatively:** the eigenvalue error decays linearly with rate $\theta^2$ — *
*twice as fast** (in exponent) as the eigenvector angle. The cross-terms vanish
because of normality (orthonormal eigenvectors); the leading error is *quadratic
* in the eigenvector error. On the semilog plot this is a line of slope $2\log\t
heta$, twice as steep as the angle line. This is the classical "Rayleigh quotien
t doubles the convergence rate" phenomenon.

---

## 3. Backward error — linear rate $\theta$

The (relative) backward error of the pair $(r_k,x_k)$ is
$$\eta_k := \frac{\|A x_k - r_k x_k\|}{\|A\|\,\|x_k\|} = \frac{\|A x_k - r_k x_k
\|}{\|A\|}.$$

Compute the residual in the eigenbasis:
$$A x_k - r_k x_k = \frac{(\lambda_1-r_k)u_1 + \sum_{i\ge 2}\alpha_i\!\left(\tfr
ac{\lambda_i}{\lambda_1}\right)^{\!k}(\lambda_i-r_k)\,u_i}{\|u_1+\delta_k\|}.$$

Now $\lambda_1-r_k = O(\theta^{2k})$ (from §2) while $\lambda_i - r_k = (\lambda
_i-\lambda_1)+O(\theta^{2k}) = O(1)$. Therefore the $u_1$-component is $O(\theta
^{2k})$ and is negligible compared with the $i\ge 2$ terms, which are $O(\theta^
k)$. The dominant term is $i=2$:
$$\boxed{\;\eta_k \;\sim\; \frac{|\alpha_2|\,|\lambda_2-\lambda_1|}{\|A\|}\,\the
ta^{\,k}\;}.$$

**Qualitatively:** the backward error decays at the **same linear rate $\theta$*
* as the eigenvector angle — the two lines on the semilog plot are **parallel**.
 The residual is governed by how well $x_k$ aligns with $u_1$, not by how well $
r_k$ approximates $\lambda_1$; the eigenvalue is already far more accurate than
the vector at every step.

---

## 4. Iterations to backward stability vs $\theta$

Backward stability is reached when $\eta_k \lesssim \mathbf{u}\,\|A\|$ (machine
epsilon $\mathbf u$). Solving $|\alpha_2|\,|\lambda_2-\lambda_1|\,\theta^k/\|A\|
 \approx \mathbf u$:
$$k_\star \;\approx\; \frac{\log\!\big(|\alpha_2|\,|\lambda_2-\lambda_1|/(\|A\|\
,\mathbf u)\big)}{\log(1/\theta)} \;\approx\; \frac{\log(1/\mathbf u)}{\log(1/\t
heta)} \quad(\text{up to a modest additive constant}).$$

For double precision, $\mathbf u\approx 2.2\times 10^{-16}$, so $\log(1/\mathbf
u)\approx 36.0$:

| $\theta$ | $\log(1/\theta)$ | $k_\star\approx 36/\log(1/\theta)$ |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.693 | 52 |
| 0.9 | 0.105 | 343 |
| 0.99 | 0.0100 | 3600 |
| 0.999 | 0.00100 | 36 000 |

**Key relations:**
- $k_\star\propto 1/\log(1/\theta)$: as $\theta\to 1$ (degenerate spectrum), ite
rations blow up — the method becomes impractical.
- The eigenvalue error reaches the noise floor at roughly $k_\star/2$, since its
 rate is $\theta^2$:
$$k_{\text{eig}} \approx \frac{\log(1/\mathbf u)}{2\log(1/\theta)} = \frac{k_\st
ar}{2}.$$

---

## 5. The convergence plot — qualitative summary

On a semilog plot of (error) vs iteration $k$, three regimes appear:

1. **Transient** (small $k$): all three curves may bend, reflecting the relative
 weights $\alpha_i$ of non-dominant components; the curve is not yet a straight
line.

2. **Asymptotic linear regime**: three straight lines —
   - eigenvector angle $\sin\phi_k$: slope $\log\theta$,
   - backward error $\eta_k$: slope $\log\theta$ (**parallel** to the angle),
   - eigenvalue error $|r_k-\lambda_1|$: slope $2\log\theta$ (**twice as steep**
).

3. **Noise floor**: each curve flattens at the level set by roundoff ($\approx\m
athbf u\|A\|$ for the residual and angle; $\approx\mathbf u\,|\lambda_1|$ for th
e eigenvalue). The eigenvalue curve hits the floor first (at $\approx k_\star/2$
), then the angle and backward-error curves hit it together (at $\approx k_\star
$). Past the floor, roundoff noise causes irregular jitter rather than further d
ecrease.

The single parameter $\theta=|\lambda_2/\lambda_1|$ controls everything: it sets
 the common slope of the angle/backward-error lines, the doubled slope of the ei
genvalue line, and — through $k_\star\approx\log(1/\mathbf u)/\log(1/\theta)$ —
the iteration count at which backward stability is achieved.
