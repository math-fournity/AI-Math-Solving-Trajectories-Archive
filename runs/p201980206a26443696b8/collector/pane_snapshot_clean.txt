## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. For any Lebesgue measurable $A \subset \mathbb{R}$ with $m(A) <
\infty$ and any $\epsilon > 0$, there exist an open set $G$ and a closed set $F$
 with $F \subset A \subset G$ and $m(G \setminus F) < \epsilon$.

The proof proceeds in three steps.

---

### Step 1 — Outer approximation by an open set

Since $A$ is Lebesgue measurable, by the definition of Lebesgue (outer) measure
as an infimum over countable open-interval covers,

$$m(A) = \inf\left\{\sum_{n} |I_n| : A \subset \bigcup_n I_n,\; I_n \text{ open
intervals}\right\}.$$

Because $m(A) < \infty$, for $\epsilon/2 > 0$ there exist open intervals $\{I_n\
}$ with $A \subset \bigcup_n I_n =: G$ and

$$m(G) \le \sum_n |I_n| < m(A) + \epsilon/2.$$

The set $G$ is open, and $G \setminus A$ is measurable (difference of measurable
 sets) with finite measure, so by additivity of $m$ on measurable sets,

$$m(G \setminus A) = m(G) - m(A) < \epsilon/2. \tag{1}$$

---

### Step 2 — Inner approximation by a closed set

First, since $m(A) < \infty$ and $A \cap [-n, n] \nearrow A$, continuity from be
low gives $m(A \cap [-n,n]) \to m(A)$, i.e. $m(A \setminus [-n,n]) \to 0$. Choos
e $N$ large enough that

$$m\bigl(A \setminus [-N, N]\bigr) < \epsilon/2. \tag{2}$$

The set $B := [-N, N] \setminus A$ is measurable and bounded, hence $m(B) \le 2N
 < \infty$. Applying Step 1 to $B$ with tolerance $\epsilon/2$, there exists an
open set $U \supset B$ with

$$m(U \setminus B) < \epsilon/2. \tag{3}$$

Define $F := [-N, N] \setminus U = [-N, N] \cap U^c$. This is closed (intersecti
on of two closed sets). Since $B = [-N,N]\setminus A \subset U$, we have

$$F = [-N,N] \setminus U \subset [-N,N] \setminus B = [-N,N] \cap A \subset A,$$

so $F \subset A$ as required.

Now compute $m(A \setminus F)$. Decompose:

$$A \setminus F = \bigl(A \setminus [-N,N]\bigr) \;\cup\; \bigl(A \cap [-N,N] \s
etminus F\bigr),$$

a disjoint union (the first piece lies outside $[-N,N]$, the second inside). For
 the second piece, using $F = [-N,N] \cap U^c$,

$$A \cap [-N,N] \setminus F = A \cap [-N,N] \cap U \subset U \cap A \subset U \s
etminus B,$$

where the last inclusion holds because $U \setminus B = U \cap ([-N,N]^c \cup A)
 \supset U \cap A$. Therefore, by (3),

$$m\bigl(A \cap [-N,N] \setminus F\bigr) \le m(U \setminus B) < \epsilon/2.$$

Combining with (2),

$$m(A \setminus F) = m\bigl(A \setminus [-N,N]\bigr) + m\bigl(A \cap [-N,N] \set
minus F\bigr) < \epsilon/2 + \epsilon/2 = \epsilon. \tag{4}$$

---

### Step 3 — Combining both approximations

From Step 1, take open $G \supset A$ with $m(G \setminus A) < \epsilon/2$ (equat
ion (1)).
From Step 2, take closed $F \subset A$ with $m(A \setminus F) < \epsilon/2$ (equ
ation (4)).

Then

$$G \setminus F = (G \setminus A) \;\cup\; (A \setminus F),$$

a disjoint union, so by subadditivity (in fact additivity, since both are measur
able and disjoint),

$$m(G \setminus F) = m(G \setminus A) + m(A \setminus F) < \epsilon/2 + \epsilon
/2 = \epsilon.$$

We have $F \subset A \subset G$ with $F$ closed, $G$ open, and $m(G \setminus F)
 < \epsilon$. $\blacksquare$
