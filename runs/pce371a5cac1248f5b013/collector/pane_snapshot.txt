## Solution

**Step 1: Extract $f(g(x))$ from the integral condition.**

Given $\displaystyle\int_0^a f(g(x))\,dx = 1 - \frac{e^{-2a}}{2}$ for all real $
a$, differentiate both sides with respect to $a$:

$$f(g(a)) = -\frac{d}{da}\!\left(\frac{e^{-2a}}{2}\right) = e^{-2a}.$$

So for all real $x$:
$$f(g(x)) = e^{-2x}. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2: Differentiate (1) to get a chain-rule identity.**

$$f'(g(x))\,g'(x) = -2e^{-2x} = -2\,f(g(x)). \tag{2}$$

**Step 3: Simplify the functional-differential equation.**

The given equation is
$$x\,g(f(x))\,f'(g(x))\,g'(x) = f(g(x))\,g'(f(x))\,f'(x).$$

Substitute (2) into the left-hand side:

$$\text{LHS} = x\,g(f(x))\cdot\bigl(-2\,f(g(x))\bigr).$$

Since $f(g(x)) = e^{-2x} > 0$, divide both sides by $f(g(x))$:

$$-2x\,g(f(x)) = g'(f(x))\,f'(x).$$

The right-hand side is exactly $\frac{d}{dx}\bigl[g(f(x))\bigr]$ by the chain ru
le. Setting $u(x) = g(f(x))$:

$$u'(x) = -2x\,u(x). \tag{3}$$

**Step 4: Solve the ODE.**

Equation (3) is separable: $\frac{u'}{u} = -2x$, giving $\ln u = -x^2 + C_0$, he
nce

$$u(x) = g(f(x)) = C\,e^{-x^2}.$$

Since $g$ is positive everywhere and $f$ is nonnegative, $g(f(x)) > 0$, confirmi
ng $C > 0$.

**Step 5: Apply the initial condition.**

Given $g(f(0)) = 1$:

$$C\,e^{0} = 1 \implies C = 1.$$

Therefore
$$g(f(x)) = e^{-x^2}.$$

**Step 6: Compute the answer.**

$$g(f(4)) = e^{-4^2} = e^{-16}.$$
