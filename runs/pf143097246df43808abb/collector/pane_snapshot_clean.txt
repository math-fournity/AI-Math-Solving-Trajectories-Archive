## Solution

We solve the nonhomogeneous heat equation by eigenfunction expansion (separation
 of variables with a time-dependent forcing).

### Step 1. Homogeneous eigenvalue problem

The associated homogeneous problem $u_t - a^2 u_{xx} = 0$ with Neumann condition
s $u_x(0,t)=u_x(\pi,t)=0$ has eigenfunctions obtained from $X''+\lambda X=0$, $X
'(0)=X'(\pi)=0$:

$$X_n(x)=\cos(nx),\qquad \lambda_n=n^2,\qquad n=0,1,2,\dots$$

These form a complete orthogonal system on $(0,\pi)$ with
$$\int_0^\pi \cos(nx)\cos(mx)\,dx=\begin{cases}\pi,&n=m=0\\ \pi/2,&n=m\ge 1\\ 0,
&n\ne m.\end{cases}$$

### Step 2. Expand the solution and the forcing

Write
$$u(x,t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}T_n(t)\cos(nx).$$

The forcing $A\cos(\omega t)$ is independent of $x$, so it projects only onto th
e $n=0$ mode:
$$A\cos(\omega t)=A\cos(\omega t)\cdot 1 = A\cos(\omega t)\,X_0(x).$$

Substituting into the PDE $u_t-a^2 u_{xx}=A\cos(\omega t)$ and using $X_n''=-n^2
 X_n$:

- **$n=0$ mode:** $X_0''=0$, so
  $$T_0'(t)=A\cos(\omega t).$$

- **$n\ge 1$ modes:** the forcing has no projection, so
  $$T_n'(t)+a^2 n^2 T_n(t)=0.$$

### Step 3. Solve the ODEs

For $n\ge 1$:
$$T_n(t)=T_n(0)\,e^{-a^2 n^2 t}.$$

For $n=0$:
$$T_0(t)=T_0(0)+\int_0^t A\cos(\omega s)\,ds=T_0(0)+\frac{A}{\omega}\sin(\omega
t).$$

(The case $\omega=0$ is the limit $\frac{A}{\omega}\sin(\omega t)\to At$; we ass
ume $\omega\ne 0$ as written.)

### Step 4. Determine the constants from the initial condition

At $t=0$:
$$u(x,0)=f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}T_n(0)\cos(nx).$$

By orthogonality:
$$T_0(0)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi f(s)\,ds,\qquad T_n(0)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi
f(s)\cos(ns)\,ds,\quad n\ge 1.$$

### Step 5. Assemble the solution

$$\boxed{\;u(x,t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi f(s)\,ds+\frac{A}{\omega}\sin(\omega t
)+\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_0^\pi f(s)\cos(ns)\,ds\right)e^{-a^
2 n^2 t}\cos(nx).\;}$$

### Verification

**PDE.** The $n=0$ part contributes $u_t=A\cos(\omega t)$, $u_{xx}=0$, giving $u
_t-a^2u_{xx}=A\cos(\omega t)$. For $n\ge 1$, $u_t=-a^2n^2 T_n(0)e^{-a^2n^2t}\cos
(nx)$ and $-a^2u_{xx}=a^2n^2 T_n(0)e^{-a^2n^2t}\cos(nx)$, which cancel. Hence th
e PDE is satisfied. ✓

**Boundary conditions.** $u_x(x,t)=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\,T_n(t)\sin(nx)$. At $x
=0$: $\sin(0)=0$; at $x=\pi$: $\sin(n\pi)=0$. So $u_x(0,t)=u_x(\pi,t)=0$. ✓

**Initial condition.** At $t=0$: $\sin(\omega\cdot0)=0$, $e^{0}=1$, so
$$u(x,0)=T_0(0)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}T_n(0)\cos(nx)=f(x)$$
by the Fourier cosine expansion of $f$. ✓

The three defining conditions are all satisfied, so this is the (unique) solutio
n.
