## Solution

**Problem.** Compute $E\!\left[\int_0^\infty (W_t - t)^+\,dt\right]$, the expect
ed area between the Wiener process $W_t$ and the line $y=t$ where $W_t$ lies abo
ve the line.

**Why the integral is finite.** Since $W_t/t \to 0$ a.s., we have $W_t - t \to -
\infty$ a.s., so the process $W_t - t$ (Brownian motion with drift $-1$) eventua
lly becomes and remains negative. Thus $\int_0^\infty (W_t-t)^+\,dt < \infty$ a.
s.

**Applying Fubini.** Since $(W_t - t)^+ \geq 0$, Tonelli's theorem gives:
$$E\!\left[\int_0^\infty (W_t - t)^+\,dt\right] = \int_0^\infty E\!\left[(W_t -
t)^+\right]dt.$$

**Computing $E[(W_t - t)^+]$.** Since $W_t \sim N(0,t)$, we have $W_t - t \sim N
(-t,\, t)$. For $X \sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)$:
$$E[X^+] = \sigma\,\phi\!\left(\frac{\mu}{\sigma}\right) + \mu\,\Phi\!\left(\fra
c{\mu}{\sigma}\right),$$
where $\phi, \Phi$ are the standard normal density and CDF. With $\mu = -t$, $\s
igma = \sqrt{t}$, and $\mu/\sigma = -\sqrt{t}$:
$$E[(W_t - t)^+] = \sqrt{t}\,\phi(\sqrt{t}) - t\,\Phi(-\sqrt{t}).$$

**Integrating over $t$.** Substitute $u = \sqrt{t}$, so $t = u^2$, $dt = 2u\,du$
:
$$\int_0^\infty E[(W_t-t)^+]\,dt = 2\int_0^\infty \left[u^2\phi(u) - u^3\Phi(-u)
\right]du = I_A - I_B,$$
where $I_A = 2\int_0^\infty u^2\phi(u)\,du$ and $I_B = 2\int_0^\infty u^3\Phi(-u
)\,du$.

**Evaluating $I_A$:** By symmetry of $\phi$ and $E[Z^2]=1$ for $Z\sim N(0,1)$:
$$I_A = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} u^2\phi(u)\,du = 1.$$

**Evaluating $I_B$:** Integrate by parts with $f = \Phi(-u)$, $g' = u^3$ (so $f'
 = -\phi(u)$, $g = u^4/4$):
$$\int_0^\infty u^3\Phi(-u)\,du = \underbrace{\left[\frac{u^4}{4}\Phi(-u)\right]
_0^\infty}_{=\,0} + \frac{1}{4}\int_0^\infty u^4\phi(u)\,du.$$
Using $\int_0^\infty u^4\phi(u)\,du = \frac{1}{2}E[Z^4] = \frac{1}{2}\cdot 3 = \
frac{3}{2}$:
$$\int_0^\infty u^3\Phi(-u)\,du = \frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{3}{2} = \frac{3}{8}, \qq
uad I_B = 2\cdot\frac{3}{8} = \frac{3}{4}.$$

**Final result:**
$$E\!\left[\int_0^\infty (W_t - t)^+\,dt\right] = I_A - I_B = 1 - \frac{3}{4} =
\boxed{\dfrac{1}{4}}.$$
