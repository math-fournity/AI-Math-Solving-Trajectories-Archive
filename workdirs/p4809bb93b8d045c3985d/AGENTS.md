# Problem

In a remote 4-kilometer by 4-kilometer forest, a forest ranger has divided the terrain into 16 identical 1-square-kilometer zones, arranged in a perfect $4 \times 4$ square grid. The ranger’s station is located in the clearing at the center of the zone in the extreme southwest corner of the grid. 

The ranger must conduct a survey by visiting the center of every single one of the 16 zones exactly once before returning to the station to file a report. Due to the dense thickets, the ranger can only travel directly between the centers of zones that share a boundary (moving north, south, east, or west); diagonal travel between zones is impossible.

How many different paths can the ranger take to complete this survey and return to the starting station?
