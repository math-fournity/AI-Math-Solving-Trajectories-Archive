Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum $|A|$ such that $m/\log n =
\Theta(n^2/|A|)$ w.h.p., where $m$ is the number of balls thrown until $\log n$
land in $A$.

## Setup

Let $|A| = a$ with $1 \leq a \leq n^2-1$ (both $A,B$ non-empty). Each ball lands
 in $A$ with probability $p = a/n^2$. The total throws $m$ until $k = \log n$ su
ccesses is **negative binomial**: $m = G_1 + \cdots + G_k$ where $G_i$ are i.i.d
. Geometric($p$).

**Expectation:** $\mathbb{E}[m] = k/p = \frac{n^2 \log n}{a}$, so $\frac{\mathbb
{E}[m]}{\log n} = \frac{n^2}{a}$.

## Concentration via Binomial CDF Identity

The key identity: $P(m > t) = P(\mathrm{Bin}(t,p) < k)$ for integer $t$.

**Upper tail** (for fixed $\delta > 0$):
$$P\!\left(m > (1{+}\delta)\tfrac{k}{p}\right) = P\!\left(\mathrm{Bin}\!\left((1
{+}\delta)\tfrac{k}{p},\, p\right) < k\right).$$
Here $\mu = (1{+}\delta)k$ and $k = \frac{\mu}{1+\delta} = (1-\epsilon)\mu$ with
 $\epsilon = \frac{\delta}{1+\delta}$. By the Chernoff lower-tail bound:
$$\leq \exp\!\left(-\frac{\epsilon^2 \mu}{2}\right) = \exp\!\left(-\frac{\delta^
2 k}{2(1+\delta)}\right) = n^{-\delta^2/(2(1+\delta))}.$$

**Lower tail** (for fixed $\delta \in (0,1)$):
$$P\!\left(m < (1{-}\delta)\tfrac{k}{p}\right) = P\!\left(\mathrm{Bin}\!\left((1
{-}\delta)\tfrac{k}{p},\, p\right) \geq k\right).$$
Here $\mu = (1-\delta)k$ and $k = \frac{\mu}{1-\delta} = (1+\epsilon)\mu$ with $
\epsilon = \frac{\delta}{1-\delta}$. By the Chernoff upper-tail bound:
$$\leq \exp\!\left(-\frac{\epsilon^2 \mu}{2+\epsilon}\right) = \exp\!\left(-\fra
c{\delta^2 k}{(1-\delta)(2-\delta)}\right) = n^{-\delta^2/((1-\delta)(2-\delta))
}.$$

## Conclusion of Concentration

Both exponents are positive for $\delta \in (0,1)$, so for any fixed $\delta$:
$$P\!\left(\left|\frac{m}{\log n} - \frac{n^2}{a}\right| > \delta\,\frac{n^2}{a}
\right) \leq n^{-c_1(\delta)} + n^{-c_2(\delta)} = O\!\left(n^{-c(\delta)}\right
), \quad c(\delta)>0.$$

Therefore $\frac{m}{\log n} = (1+o(1))\,\frac{n^2}{a}$ **w.h.p.** (in the strong
 sense, $1 - n^{-\Omega(1)}$).

In particular, for sufficiently large $n$, w.h.p.:
$$\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{n^2}{a} \;\leq\; \frac{m}{\log n} \;\leq\; 2\cdot\frac{n
^2}{a},$$
which is exactly $\frac{m}{\log n} = \Theta\!\left(\frac{n^2}{a}\right)$ w.h.p.
with absolute constants.

## Why This Holds for All $a \geq 1$

The concentration depends only on $k = \log n \to \infty$. The coefficient of va
riation of $m$ is $\sqrt{(1-p)/k} \leq 1/\sqrt{\log n} \to 0$ **regardless of $p
$** (hence regardless of $a$). The Chernoff bounds yield polynomial decay $n^{-c
(\delta)}$ for every $a \geq 1$, since the exponent involves $k = \log n$ (givin
g the $n^{-c}$ factor) but **not** $p$ or $a$ in a way that could vanish.

**Verification at $a=1$:** $p = 1/n^2$, $\mathbb{E}[m] = n^2 \log n$, concentrat
ion gives $m = (1+o(1))\,n^2\log n$ w.h.p., so $m/\log n = (1+o(1))\,n^2 = \Thet
a(n^2/1)$. ✓

## Answer

Since both $A$ and $B$ must be non-empty, and the $\Theta$-relation holds w.h.p.
 for every $a \geq 1$, the **minimum cardinality** of $A$ is:

$$\boxed{|A| = 1}$$
