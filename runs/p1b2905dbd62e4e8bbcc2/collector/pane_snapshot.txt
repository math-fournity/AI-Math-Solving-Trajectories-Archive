Proof

Setup and Key Observation

The pullback is defined as $f^*(-) = f^{-1}(-) \otimes_{f^{-1}\mathcal{O}_Y}
\mathcal{O}X$. The inverse image functor $f^{-1}$ is exact (exactness is checked
on stalks, and $(f^{-1}\mathcal{G})x = \mathcal{G}{f(x)}$). So the question
reduces to: **is $-\otimes{f^{-1}\mathcal{O}_Y} \mathcal{O}_X$ exact?**

Tensor product is always right exact. It is left exact (hence exact) if and only
if $\mathcal{O}_X$ is flat as an $f^{-1}\mathcal{O}_Y$-module, which is
equivalent to $f$ being a flat morphism.

A morphism of smooth projective schemes need not be flat. The failure of left
exactness is measured by $\operatorname{Tor}_1^{f^{-1}\mathcal{O}_Y}(-,
\mathcal{O}_X)$.

Counterexample

$f: X \hookrightarrow Y$ the closed immersion of the point $p = [0:1]$. Both $X$
and $Y$ are smooth projective schemes over $k$.

of the skyscraper sheaf):

$$0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}(-1) \xrightarrow{;\cdot s;} \mathcal{O}

This is an exact sequence with $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ and $\mathcal{O}$ locally free
(of rank 1), and $\mathcal{O}_p$ the skyscraper sheaf at $p$ (not locally free).

$$f^\mathcal{O}(-1) \xrightarrow{;0;} f^\mathcal{O}$$

$$0 \longrightarrow k \xrightarrow{;0;} k \longrightarrow k \longrightarrow 0$$

When Is the Pullback Exact?

The pullback $f^*$ is exact (preserves exactness of all exact sequences,
including those of locally free sheaves) if and only if $f$ is flat.

Proof of the flat case. If $f$ is flat, then $\mathcal{O}_X$ is flat as an
$f^{-1}\mathcal{O}_Y$-module. Since $f^{-1}$ is exact and tensor product with a
flat module is exact, the composition $f^* = f^{-1}(-)\otimes \mathcal{O}_X$ is
exact. Therefore, any exact sequence — including the given long exact sequence
of locally free sheaves — remains exact after pullback. $\square$

Proof of the converse. If $f^*$ is exact on all exact sequences, then in
particular $\operatorname{Tor}_1^{f^{-1}\mathcal{O}_Y}(\mathcal{G}, \mathcal{O}
_X) = 0$ for all $\mathcal{O}_Y$-modules $\mathcal{G}$, which means $\mathcal{O}
_X$ is flat over $f^{-1}\mathcal{O}_Y$, i.e., $f$ is flat. $\square$

Remark on the Special Case Where $\mathcal{F}$ Is Also Locally Free

If every sheaf in the sequence — including $\mathcal{F}$ — is locally free, then
the answer becomes yes (without any flatness hypothesis on $f$). The reason:
decompose the long exact sequence into short exact sequences $0 \to \mathcal{K}i
\to \mathcal{F}i \to \mathcal{K}{i-1} \to 0$. Starting from the right ($
\mathcal{K}{-1} = \mathcal{F}$ locally free), each short exact sequence has a
locally free right term, hence is locally split (a locally free sheaf is locally
projective, so the identity lifts). Local splitting is preserved by any additive
functor, including $f^*$, so each pulled-back short exact sequence remains
exact, and the full pulled-back long exact sequence is exact.

However, in the general case where $\mathcal{F}_n, \ldots, \mathcal{F}_0$ are
locally free but $\mathcal{F}$ is merely the cokernel (the typical setting of a
locally free resolution), the intermediate kernels $\mathcal{K}_i$ need not be
locally free, the short exact sequences need not split, and the pullback can
fail to be exact — as the counterexample above demonstrates.

Conclusion

The pulled-back sequence $$0 \longrightarrow f^\mathcal{F}_n \longrightarrow
\cdots \longrightarrow f^\mathcal{F}_0 \longrightarrow f^\mathcal{F}
\longrightarrow 0$$ is not exact in general. The pullback functor $f^$ is right
exact (so the sequence is exact from $f^*\mathcal{F}_0$ onward), but left
exactness fails unless $f$ is flat. The counterexample of a closed point in $
\mathbb{P}^1$ with the Koszul resolution of a skyscraper sheaf provides an
explicit failure of exactness.

PROOF COMPLETE
