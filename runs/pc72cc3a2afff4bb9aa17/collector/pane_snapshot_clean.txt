**Theorem.** Let $k$ be an arbitrary field, and let $\varphi: A \to B$ be a homo
morphism of abelian varieties over $k$. Then the scheme-theoretic image $\varphi
(A)$ is an abelian subvariety of $B$ defined over $k$.

**Proof.**

Let $Z$ denote the scheme-theoretic image of $\varphi$, i.e., the closed subsche
me of $B$ defined by the ideal sheaf $\mathcal{I} = \ker(\mathcal{O}_B \to \varp
hi_*\mathcal{O}_A)$. We verify that $Z$ is an abelian subvariety of $B$ defined
over $k$.

**Step 1: $Z$ is defined over $k$.**

Since $\varphi$ is a $k$-morphism, the sheaf $\mathcal{I} = \ker(\mathcal{O}_B \
to \varphi_*\mathcal{O}_A)$ is a quasi-coherent sheaf of ideals on $B$ defined o
ver $k$. Hence $Z$ is a closed subscheme of $B$ defined over $k$.

**Step 2: $Z$ is a closed subgroup scheme of $B$.**

Since $\varphi$ is a homomorphism, we have $m_B \circ (\varphi \times \varphi) =
 \varphi \circ m_A$, $i_B \circ \varphi = \varphi \circ i_A$, and $\varphi(0_A)
= 0_B$. The scheme-theoretic image of $A \times A \to B \times B$ is $Z \times Z
$ (the scheme-theoretic image commutes with products for morphisms of finite typ
e between Noetherian schemes, since $A$ is of finite type and geometrically inte
gral). Therefore the group operations on $B$ restrict to $Z$: the multiplication
 $m_B|_{Z \times Z}$ factors through $Z$, the inverse $i_B|_Z$ factors through $
Z$, and $0_B \in Z$. Thus $Z$ is a closed subgroup scheme of $B$.

**Step 3: $Z$ is reduced.**

The scheme-theoretic image of a morphism from a reduced scheme is reduced. Indee
d, $\mathcal{O}_Z = \mathcal{O}_B/\mathcal{I} = \operatorname{im}(\mathcal{O}_B
\to \varphi_*\mathcal{O}_A)$, which embeds into $\varphi_*\mathcal{O}_A$. Since
$A$ is reduced (abelian varieties are smooth, hence reduced), $\varphi_*\mathcal
{O}_A$ is a sheaf of reduced rings, so any subring is reduced. Hence $\mathcal{O
}_Z$ is reduced.

**Step 4: $Z$ is smooth.**

The morphism $\varphi$ factors as $A \xrightarrow{\pi} Z \hookrightarrow B$, whe
re $\pi: A \to Z$ is a surjective homomorphism of group schemes of finite type o
ver $k$. By a theorem of Grothendieck (SGA 3, Exp. VI$_{\text{B}}$), a surjectiv
e homomorphism of group schemes of finite type over a field is faithfully flat.
Hence $\pi$ is fppf. Since $A$ is smooth (abelian varieties are smooth) and smoo
thness descends under fppf morphisms, $Z$ is smooth.

**Step 5: $Z$ is geometrically connected.**

$Z$ is connected as the image of the connected scheme $A$. Since $Z$ is a smooth
 connected group scheme over $k$, it has a $k$-rational point (the identity $0_B
$). A smooth connected scheme over $k$ with a $k$-rational point is geometricall
y connected: smoothness implies geometric reducedness, and for a geometrically r
educed connected scheme, the geometrically connected components are defined over
 a separable extension of $k$; the presence of a $k$-rational point forces a sin
gle geometric component.

**Step 6: $Z$ is proper.**

$Z$ is a closed subscheme of $B$, and $B$ is proper (abelian varieties are proje
ctive, hence proper). Therefore $Z$ is proper over $k$.

**Step 7: Conclusion.**

$Z$ is a smooth, proper, geometrically connected group scheme over $k$ with a $k
$-rational identity element. This is precisely the definition of an abelian vari
ety over $k$. Since $Z$ is a closed subgroup scheme of the abelian variety $B$,
it is an abelian subvariety of $B$. By Step 1, it is defined over $k$.

Therefore, $\varphi(A)$ has the structure of an abelian subvariety of $B$ define
d over $k$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The key subtlety over an arbitrary (possibly imperfect) field is Ste
p 4: a reduced closed subgroup of a smooth group scheme need not be smooth in ge
neral, but here the smoothness of $Z$ is forced by fppf descent from the smooth
source $A$ via the surjective homomorphism $A \to Z$. This argument works unifor
mly over any base field, perfect or imperfect.
