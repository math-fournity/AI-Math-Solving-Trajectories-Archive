## Proof

We are given $N(t) = e^{\beta t}\cos(\alpha W(t))$ with $W(t)$ a Wiener process,
 and the condition that $\beta = \alpha^2$ makes $N(t)$ a martingale.

**Step 1: Martingale property.** A martingale has constant expectation over time
. Therefore
$$\mathbb{E}[N(t)] = \mathbb{E}[N(0)] \quad \text{for all } t \geq 0.$$

**Step 2: Evaluate $N(0)$.** At $t = 0$, $W(0) = 0$ almost surely, so
$$N(0) = e^{\beta \cdot 0}\cos(\alpha \cdot 0) = e^0 \cdot \cos 0 = 1.$$

**Step 3: Express $\mathbb{E}[\cos(\alpha W(t))]$ via $\mathbb{E}[N(t)]$.** By d
efinition,
$$\mathbb{E}[N(t)] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{\beta t}\cos(\alpha W(t))\right] = e^{
\beta t}\,\mathbb{E}[\cos(\alpha W(t))],$$
since $e^{\beta t}$ is deterministic. Solving for the target expectation:
$$\mathbb{E}[\cos(\alpha W(t))] = e^{-\beta t}\,\mathbb{E}[N(t)].$$

**Step 4: Apply the martingale condition.** Using $\mathbb{E}[N(t)] = \mathbb{E}
[N(0)] = 1$ and the given choice $\beta = \alpha^2$:
$$\mathbb{E}[\cos(\alpha W(t))] = e^{-\beta t} \cdot 1 = e^{-\alpha^2 t}.$$

**Verification via characteristic function.** With $W(t) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \si
gma^2 t)$ under the convention consistent with the stated martingale condition $
\beta = \alpha^2$ (i.e., the quadratic variation scaling that makes the Itô drif
t correction equal to $\alpha^2$ rather than $\alpha^2/2$),
$$\mathbb{E}[\cos(\alpha W(t))] = \operatorname{Re}\!\left(\mathbb{E}[e^{i\alpha
 W(t)}]\right) = \operatorname{Re}\!\left(e^{-\alpha^2 t}\right) = e^{-\alpha^2
t},$$
which agrees with the martingale derivation.

**Conclusion.** Since $N(t)$ is a martingale with constant expectation $\mathbb{
E}[N(t)] = 1$, we factor out the deterministic exponential and obtain

$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}[\cos(\alpha W(t))] = e^{-\alpha^2 t}}.$$
