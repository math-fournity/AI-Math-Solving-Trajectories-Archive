## Solution

**Setup.** Place the cable on the $x$-axis with $B$ (Beta) at the origin:
$$A = (-4,\,0),\quad B = (0,\,0),\quad G = (2,\,0),\quad D = (8,\,0),$$
and let $T = (x,\,y)$ with $y > 0$.

**Angle formula.** For a segment from $P=(p,0)$ to $Q=(q,0)$, the angle $\theta$
 it subtends at $T$ satisfies
$$\tan\theta = \frac{|y(q-p)|}{(p-x)(q-x)+y^{2}}.$$

**Equal angles $\angle ATB = \angle BTG$.** Using $AB$ ($p=-4,q=0$) and $BG$ ($p
=0,q=2$):
$$\frac{4y}{x^{2}+4x+y^{2}} = \frac{2y}{x^{2}-2x+y^{2}}.$$
Cancelling $2y\neq 0$ and cross-multiplying:
$$2(x^{2}-2x+y^{2}) = x^{2}+4x+y^{2} \;\Longrightarrow\; x^{2}-8x+y^{2}=0 \;\Lon
grightarrow\; (x-4)^{2}+y^{2}=16.$$
So $T$ lies on the circle centered at $(4,0)$, radius $4$ (passing through $B$ a
nd $D$).

**Equal angles $\angle BTG = \angle GTD$.** Using $BG$ ($p=0,q=2$) and $GD$ ($p=
2,q=8$):
$$\frac{2y}{x^{2}-2x+y^{2}} = \frac{6y}{x^{2}-10x+16+y^{2}}.$$
Cancelling $2y$ and cross-multiplying:
$$x^{2}-10x+16+y^{2} = 3(x^{2}-2x+y^{2}) \;\Longrightarrow\; x^{2}+2x+y^{2}=8 \;
\Longrightarrow\; (x+1)^{2}+y^{2}=9.$$
So $T$ lies on the circle centered at $(-1,0)$, radius $3$ (passing through $A$
and $G$).

**Finding $T$.** Subtract the two circle equations:
$$(x-4)^{2}-(x+1)^{2} = 16-9 \;\Longrightarrow\; -10x+15 = 7 \;\Longrightarrow\;
 x = \tfrac{4}{5}.$$
Then $y^{2} = 16 - (-\tfrac{16}{5})^{2} = \tfrac{400-256}{25} = \tfrac{144}{25}$
, so $y = \tfrac{12}{5}$.

**Computing $\alpha$.** Using segment $BG$:
$$\tan\alpha = \frac{2y}{x^{2}-2x+y^{2}} = \frac{2\cdot\frac{12}{5}}{\frac{16}{2
5}-\frac{8}{5}+\frac{144}{25}} = \frac{\frac{24}{5}}{\frac{16-40+144}{25}} = \fr
ac{\frac{24}{5}}{\frac{120}{25}} = \frac{\frac{24}{5}}{\frac{24}{5}} = 1.$$

**Verification** with the other two segments:
- $AB$: $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{4\cdot\frac{12}{5}}{\frac{16}{25}+\frac{16}{5}+\fra
c{144}{25}} = \dfrac{\frac{48}{5}}{\frac{240}{25}} = \dfrac{48/5}{48/5} = 1.$ ✓
- $GD$: $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{6\cdot\frac{12}{5}}{\frac{16}{25}-\frac{40}{5}+\fra
c{400}{25}+\frac{144}{25}} = \dfrac{\frac{72}{5}}{\frac{360}{25}} = \dfrac{72/5}
{72/5} = 1.$ ✓

Since $0 < \alpha < \tfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\tan\alpha = 1$,

$$\boxed{\alpha = 45^{\circ}}.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
