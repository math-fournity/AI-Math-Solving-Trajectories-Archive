## Proof

**Claim.** Let $\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}$, identified with $[-\pi,
\pi)$. Define
$$
f(x) = \mathbf{1}_{(0,\pi)}(x) = \begin{cases} 1, & x \in (0,\pi),\\ 0, & x \in
[-\pi,0). \end{cases}
$$
Then $f \in L^\infty(\mathbb{T})$ (with $\|f\|_\infty = 1$), but the Fejér sums
$\sigma_N(f)$ do **not** converge to $f$ in $L^\infty(\mathbb{T})$.

---

**Proof.** Each Fejér sum
$$
\sigma_N(f)(x) = \sum_{k=-N}^{N}\left(1-\frac{|k|}{N+1}\right)\hat f(k)\,e^{ikx}
 = (f * F_N)(x)
$$
is a trigonometric polynomial, hence a **continuous** function on $\mathbb{T}$.

Suppose, for contradiction, that $\|\sigma_N(f) - f\|_{L^\infty} \to 0$. Then th
ere exists $N_0$ such that for all $N \ge N_0$,
$$
\|\sigma_N(f) - f\|_\infty < \tfrac{1}{4}.
$$
This means $|\sigma_N(f)(x) - f(x)| < \tfrac{1}{4}$ for **a.e.** $x \in \mathbb{
T}$.

**Step 1: $\sigma_N(f) \ge \tfrac{3}{4}$ on $(0,\pi)$.**
On $(0,\pi)$ we have $f = 1$ a.e., so $\sigma_N(f)(x) > \tfrac{3}{4}$ for a.e. $
x \in (0,\pi)$. Since $\sigma_N(f)$ is continuous, the set $U = \{x : \sigma_N(f
)(x) > \tfrac{3}{4}\}$ is open. If there existed $x_0 \in (0,\pi)$ with $\sigma_
N(f)(x_0) \le \tfrac{3}{4}$, then by continuity there would be an open interval
around $x_0$ on which $\sigma_N(f) \le \tfrac{3}{4}$, and this interval has posi
tive measure—contradicting the fact that $\sigma_N(f) > \tfrac{3}{4}$ a.e. on $(
0,\pi)$. Hence $\sigma_N(f) \ge \tfrac{3}{4}$ on all of $(0,\pi)$.

**Step 2: $\sigma_N(f) \le \tfrac{1}{4}$ on $(-\pi,0)$.**
By the identical argument applied to the set where $f = 0$ (namely $(-\pi,0)$, w
hich has positive measure), continuity forces $\sigma_N(f) \le \tfrac{1}{4}$ on
all of $(-\pi,0)$.

**Step 3: Contradiction at $x = 0$.**
Since $\sigma_N(f)$ is continuous on $\mathbb{T}$, taking the limit $x \to 0^+$
(with $x \in (0,\pi)$) gives
$$
\sigma_N(f)(0) = \lim_{x\to 0^+} \sigma_N(f)(x) \ge \tfrac{3}{4}.
$$
Taking the limit $x \to 0^-$ (with $x \in (-\pi,0)$) gives
$$
\sigma_N(f)(0) = \lim_{x\to 0^-} \sigma_N(f)(x) \le \tfrac{1}{4}.
$$
This is a contradiction, since $\tfrac{3}{4} > \tfrac{1}{4}$.

Therefore $\|\sigma_N(f) - f\|_\infty \not\to 0$; in fact $\|\sigma_N(f) - f\|_\
infty \ge \tfrac{1}{4}$ for all $N$ (the essential supremum cannot drop below $\
tfrac{1}{4}$, since the jump discontinuity forces a defect of at least $\tfrac{1
}{2}$ in the pointwise sup, and even the essential sup is bounded below by a pos
itive constant).

**Remark on the general principle.** Every $\sigma_N(f)$ is continuous, and $L^\
infty$-convergence of continuous functions to $f$ would make $f$ equal a.e. to a
 uniform limit of continuous functions—hence a.e. equal to a continuous function
. Any $L^\infty$ function that is **not** a.e. equal to a continuous function (s
uch as any function with a genuine jump discontinuity, like the indicator above)
 therefore serves as a counterexample. $\blacksquare$
