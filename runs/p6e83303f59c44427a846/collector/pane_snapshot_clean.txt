## Proof

We characterize all $A \in M_n(\mathbb{C})$ with $\sin(A)=0$.

**Step 1: Reduction to Jordan form.** Write $A = PJP^{-1}$ where $J = \bigoplus_
i J_{k_i}(\lambda_i)$ is the Jordan normal form, with $J_{k_i}(\lambda_i) = \lam
bda_i I + N_i$ and $N_i$ the nilpotent block ($N_i^{k_i}=0$, $N_i^{k_i-1}\neq 0$
). Since $\sin$ is an entire function, $\sin(A) = P\,\sin(J)\,P^{-1}$, and
$$\sin(J) = \bigoplus_i \sin\!\big(J_{k_i}(\lambda_i)\big).$$
Thus $\sin(A)=0 \iff \sin(J_{k_i}(\lambda_i))=0$ for every $i$.

**Step 2: Compute $\sin$ on a Jordan block.** For a block $J_k(\lambda) = \lambd
a I + N$ with $N^k=0$, the functional calculus gives
$$\sin(J_k(\lambda)) = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \frac{\sin^{(j)}(\lambda)}{j!}\,N^{j} =
\sin(\lambda)\,I + \cos(\lambda)\,N - \frac{\sin(\lambda)}{2!}\,N^2 - \frac{\cos
(\lambda)}{3!}\,N^3 + \cdots.$$
For this to be the zero matrix, every coefficient of $N^j$ (for $j=0,\dots,k-1$)
 must vanish, because $\{I, N, \dots, N^{k-1}\}$ are linearly independent (they
have distinct nonzero superdiagonal structures).

**Step 3: The zeros of $\sin$ are simple.** The zeros of $\sin(z)$ in $\mathbb{C
}$ are exactly $z = m\pi$, $m\in\mathbb{Z}$. At each such zero,
$$\sin'(m\pi) = \cos(m\pi) = (-1)^m \neq 0.$$
So every zero of $\sin$ is **simple**: $\sin(\lambda)=0 \Rightarrow \sin'(\lambd
a)=\cos(\lambda)\neq 0$.

**Step 4: Jordan blocks must be $1\times 1$.** For $\sin(J_k(\lambda))=0$ we nee
d $\sin(\lambda)=0$, hence $\lambda=m\pi$ and $\cos(\lambda)=(-1)^m\neq 0$. But
the coefficient of $N$ in $\sin(J_k(\lambda))$ is $\cos(\lambda)\neq 0$. Since $
N\neq 0$ whenever $k\ge 2$, the term $\cos(\lambda)N$ is nonzero, contradicting
$\sin(J_k(\lambda))=0$. Therefore $k=1$ for every block: **all Jordan blocks are
 $1\times 1$**, i.e., $A$ is diagonalizable, and every eigenvalue satisfies $\si
n(\lambda)=0$, i.e., $\lambda\in\pi\mathbb{Z}$.

**Step 5: Converse.** If $A$ is diagonalizable with eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\dots
,\lambda_n \in \pi\mathbb{Z}$, write $A = P\,\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lamb
da_n)\,P^{-1}$. Then
$$\sin(A) = P\,\mathrm{diag}(\sin\lambda_1,\dots,\sin\lambda_n)\,P^{-1} = P\,\ma
thrm{diag}(\sin(m_1\pi),\dots,\sin(m_n\pi))\,P^{-1} = 0,$$
since $\sin(m\pi)=0$ for all $m\in\mathbb{Z}$.

**Conclusion.** The matrices $A\in M_n(\mathbb{C})$ satisfying $\sin(A)=0$ are e
xactly the **diagonalizable** matrices whose eigenvalues all belong to $\pi\math
bb{Z}=\{m\pi : m\in\mathbb{Z}\}$. Equivalently,
$$\boxed{A = P\,\mathrm{diag}(m_1\pi,\dots,m_n\pi)\,P^{-1},\quad P\in GL_n(\math
bb{C}),\ m_i\in\mathbb{Z}.}$$
