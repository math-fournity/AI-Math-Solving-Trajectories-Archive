**Proof.**

Let $(X, \mu)$ be the measure space and $H$ a Hilbert space with inner product $
\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_H$ and induced norm $\|\cdot\|_H$. Recall that $L^1
(X, H)$ denotes the Bochner space of (equivalence classes of) Bochner-measurable
 functions $f : X \to H$ satisfying
$$\|f\|_{L^1(X,H)} := \int_X \|f(\omega)\|_H \, d\mu(\omega) < \infty.$$

Assume $x_n \to x$ in $L^1(X, H)$, i.e.,
$$\|x_n - x\|_{L^1(X,H)} = \int_X \|x_n(\omega) - x(\omega)\|_H \, d\mu(\omega)
\longrightarrow 0.$$

Fix an arbitrary $h \in H$. We must show that the scalar-valued functions $\omeg
a \mapsto \langle x_n(\omega), h \rangle_H$ converge to $\omega \mapsto \langle
x(\omega), h \rangle_H$ in $L^1(X, \mathbb{R})$, i.e.,
$$\int_X \bigl|\langle x_n(\omega), h\rangle_H - \langle x(\omega), h\rangle_H\b
igr| \, d\mu(\omega) \longrightarrow 0.$$

**Step 1 (Pointwise identity).** By linearity of the inner product in the first
argument,
$$\langle x_n(\omega), h\rangle_H - \langle x(\omega), h\rangle_H = \langle x_n(
\omega) - x(\omega),\; h\rangle_H.$$

**Step 2 (Cauchy–Schwarz in $H$).** For every $\omega \in X$,
$$\bigl|\langle x_n(\omega) - x(\omega), h\rangle_H\bigr| \le \|x_n(\omega) - x(
\omega)\|_H \cdot \|h\|_H.$$

**Step 3 (Integrate).** Integrating over $X$ and using that $\|h\|_H$ is a const
ant independent of $\omega$,
$$\int_X \bigl|\langle x_n(\omega) - x(\omega), h\rangle_H\bigr| \, d\mu(\omega)
 \le \|h\|_H \int_X \|x_n(\omega) - x(\omega)\|_H \, d\mu(\omega) = \|h\|_H \cdo
t \|x_n - x\|_{L^1(X,H)}.$$

**Step 4 (Take the limit).** Since $\|x_n - x\|_{L^1(X,H)} \to 0$ by hypothesis
and $\|h\|_H$ is a fixed finite constant,
$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \int_X \bigl|\langle x_n(\omega), h\rangle_H - \langle x(\
omega), h\rangle_H\bigr| \, d\mu(\omega) \le \lim_{n \to \infty} \|h\|_H \cdot \
|x_n - x\|_{L^1(X,H)} = 0.$$

The left-hand side is a nonnegative quantity bounded above by a sequence tending
 to $0$, hence it tends to $0$ as well. Therefore
$$\langle x_n, h\rangle_H \to \langle x, h\rangle_H \quad \text{in } L^1(X, \mat
hbb{R}).$$

**Remark on measurability.** The map $\omega \mapsto \langle x_n(\omega), h\rang
le_H$ is measurable (hence in $L^1(X,\mathbb{R})$) because it is the composition
 of the Bochner-measurable function $x_n$ with the continuous (hence Borel-measu
rable) linear functional $v \mapsto \langle v, h\rangle_H$. The same holds for $
x$. Thus the $L^1(X,\mathbb{R})$-norms above are well defined, and the estimate
is valid.

Since $h \in H$ was arbitrary, the statement holds for every fixed $h \in H$. $\
blacksquare$
