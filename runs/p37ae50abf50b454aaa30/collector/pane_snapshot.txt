## Proof

**Claim:** For every $m \in \mathbb{N}$, the set $\{\sin n^m \mid n \in \mathbb{
N}\}$ is dense in $[-1,1]$.

The proof proceeds in three steps.

### Step 1: Reduction to a distribution problem mod $2\pi$

The function $\sin: \mathbb{R} \to [-1,1]$ is continuous, surjective, and $2\pi$
-periodic. It descends to a continuous surjection $\sin: \mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{
Z} \to [-1,1]$. A basic topological fact is:

> *If $f: X \to Y$ is a continuous surjection between topological spaces and $D
\subseteq X$ is dense, then $f(D)$ is dense in $Y$.*

Applying this with $X = [0, 2\pi)$, $Y = [-1,1]$, $f = \sin$, and $D = \{n^m \bm
od 2\pi : n \in \mathbb{N}\}$, it suffices to show:

$$\{n^m \bmod 2\pi : n \in \mathbb{N}\} \text{ is dense in } [0, 2\pi) \quad \te
xt{for every } m \in \mathbb{N}. \tag{$\star$}$$

### Step 2: Irrationality of $\frac{1}{2\pi}$

The number $\pi$ is irrational (Lambert, 1761). Therefore $\frac{1}{2\pi}$ is ir
rational.

### Step 3: Application of Weyl's Equidistribution Theorem

We use the following classical result:

> **Weyl's Equidistribution Theorem (1916).** Let $P(x) = a_k x^k + a_{k-1}x^{k-
1} + \cdots + a_1 x + a_0$ be a polynomial with real coefficients. The sequence
$\{P(n)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ is *uniformly distributed* modulo $1$ if and only if a
t least one of the non-constant coefficients $a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_k$ is irration
al.

Fix $m \in \mathbb{N}$ and consider the polynomial

$$P(x) = \frac{x^m}{2\pi}.$$

This polynomial has a single non-constant coefficient $a_m = \frac{1}{2\pi}$, wh
ich is irrational by Step 2, and all other coefficients equal to $0$. By Weyl's
theorem, the sequence

$$\left\{\frac{n^m}{2\pi}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$$

is uniformly distributed modulo $1$. Uniform distribution modulo $1$ implies, in
 particular, that the fractional parts $\left\{\frac{n^m}{2\pi} \bmod 1\right\}$
 are **dense** in $[0,1)$.

Multiplying by $2\pi$, this is equivalent to saying that $\{n^m \bmod 2\pi : n \
in \mathbb{N}\}$ is dense in $[0, 2\pi)$. This establishes $(\star)$.

### Conclusion

Since $\{n^m \bmod 2\pi\}$ is dense in $[0, 2\pi)$ for every $m \in \mathbb{N}$
(Step 3), and $\sin: [0,2\pi) \to [-1,1]$ is a continuous surjection (Step 1), t
he continuous image

$$\{\sin n^m : n \in \mathbb{N}\}$$

is dense in $[-1,1]$ for every natural number $m$.

**Remark.** The argument works for every $m$ because the key ingredient—Weyl's t
heorem—applies to any polynomial with an irrational non-constant coefficient, an
d the single coefficient $\frac{1}{2\pi}$ is irrational regardless of the degree
 $m$. The case $m = 1$ is the classical Kronecker/Weyl result on irrational rota
tions; the cases $m \geq 2$ rely on the full power of Weyl's polynomial equidist
ribution theorem.
