**Claim:** Yes, every holomorphic homeomorphism $f:U\to U$ of the unit disc exte
nds to a continuous map $\overline{U}\to\overline{U}$.

**Proof.**

**Step 1. A holomorphic homeomorphism is a biholomorphism.**

Let $f:U\to U$ be holomorphic and a homeomorphism (in particular, bijective). We
 show $f$ is biholomorphic. Suppose for contradiction that $f'(z_0)=0$ for some
$z_0\in U$. Then $z_0$ is a critical point, and by the local normal form of holo
morphic maps, there exist $n\geq 2$, a neighborhood $V$ of $z_0$, and a neighbor
hood $W$ of $f(z_0)$ such that every point of $W\setminus\{f(z_0)\}$ has exactly
 $n$ preimages in $V$. This contradicts the injectivity of $f$. Hence $f'(z)\neq
 0$ for all $z\in U$, so $f$ is locally biholomorphic and its set-theoretic inve
rse $f^{-1}$ is holomorphic. Thus $f\in\operatorname{Aut}(U)$.

**Step 2. Classification of disc automorphisms.**

By the classical Schwarz lemma argument, every automorphism of $U$ is a Möbius t
ransformation of the form
$$
f(z)=e^{i\theta}\,\frac{z-a}{1-\overline{a}\,z},\qquad |a|<1,\ \theta\in\mathbb{
R}.
$$
(Recall: if $g\in\operatorname{Aut}(U)$ with $g(a)=0$, then $\varphi_a(z)=\frac{
z-a}{1-\overline a\,z}$ also sends $a$ to $0$, so $g\circ\varphi_a^{-1}$ fixes $
0$ and is an automorphism, hence a rotation by Schwarz; composing back gives the
 displayed form.)

**Step 3. Continuous extension to $\overline U$.**

The map $f(z)=e^{i\theta}\frac{z-a}{1-\overline a\,z}$ is a rational function wh
ose only pole is at $z=1/\overline a$. Since $|a|<1$, we have $|1/\overline a|>1
$, so the pole lies strictly outside $\overline U$. Consequently $f$ is continuo
us (indeed holomorphic in a neighborhood of $\overline U$) on the closed disc $\
overline U$.

It remains to check $f(\overline U)\subseteq\overline U$. For $|z|=1$ (i.e., $z\
bar z=1$, so $\bar z=1/z$),
$$
\left|\frac{z-a}{1-\overline a\,z}\right|^2=\frac{(z-a)(\bar z-\bar a)}{(1-\bar
a z)(1-a\bar z)}=\frac{1-a\bar z-\bar a z+|a|^2}{1-a\bar z-\bar a z+|a|^2}=1.
$$
So $f$ maps the unit circle to the unit circle. By the maximum principle (or dir
ectly, since $|f|<1$ on $U$ and $|f|=1$ on $\partial U$), $f(\overline U)=\overl
ine U$.

**Conclusion.** Every holomorphic homeomorphism $f:U\to U$ is a disc automorphis
m, hence a Möbius map of the above form, which is continuous on $\overline U$ an
d sends $\overline U$ onto $\overline U$. Therefore $f$ **must** extend to a con
tinuous map $f:\overline U\to\overline U$. $\blacksquare$
