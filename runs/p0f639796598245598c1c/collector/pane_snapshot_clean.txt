## Solution

**Setup.** Let the bank balance be modeled by the drifted Brownian motion

$$X(t) = x_0 + \mu\, t + \sigma\, W(t), \qquad \mu > 0,\;\sigma > 0,\;x_0 > 0,$$

where $W(t)$ is standard Brownian motion. Define the first passage time

$$\tau = \inf\{t \ge 0 : X(t) \le 0\}.$$

(By continuity of paths, hitting $0$ and going negative coincide a.s., so this i
s also the first time the balance becomes negative.)

---

**Step 1: The probability of ever going negative is strictly less than 1.**

Consider the process $M(t) = \exp\!\bigl(-\tfrac{2\mu}{\sigma^2} X(t)\bigr)$. Wr
iting it out:

$$M(t) = \exp\!\Bigl(-\tfrac{2\mu x_0}{\sigma^2} - \tfrac{2\mu^2}{\sigma^2}\,t -
 \tfrac{2\mu}{\sigma}\,W(t)\Bigr).$$

This is the Doléans-Dade exponential martingale with parameter $\theta = -2\mu/\
sigma$, so $M(t)$ is a non-negative martingale with $E[M(t)] = M(0) = \exp(-2\mu
 x_0/\sigma^2)$.

Applying the optional stopping theorem at the bounded stopping time $\tau \wedge
 t$:

$$E\bigl[M(\tau \wedge t)\bigr] = \exp\!\Bigl(-\tfrac{2\mu x_0}{\sigma^2}\Bigr).
 \tag{1}$$

Now analyze the limit $t \to \infty$. By the strong law of large numbers for Bro
wnian motion, $W(t)/t \to 0$ a.s., hence

$$\frac{X(t)}{t} = \frac{x_0}{t} + \mu + \sigma\frac{W(t)}{t} \;\longrightarrow\
; \mu > 0 \quad\text{a.s.},$$

so $X(t) \to +\infty$ a.s. On the event $\{\tau < \infty\}$, continuity gives $X
(\tau)=0$, so $M(\tau)=1$. On the event $\{\tau = \infty\}$, $X(t)\to+\infty$, s
o $M(t)\to 0$.

By dominated convergence ($0 \le M \le 1$ on $\{\tau<\infty\}$ and $M\to 0$ on $
\{\tau=\infty\}$, with $M(\tau\wedge t) \le \exp(-2\mu x_0/\sigma^2) \cdot$ ...
actually $M$ is bounded by $1$ when $X\ge 0$, which holds on $\{\tau = \infty\}$
; on $\{\tau < \infty\}$, $M(\tau\wedge t) \le 1$ for $t$ large enough since $X(
\tau)=0$), taking $t\to\infty$ in (1):

$$\boxed{P(\tau < \infty) = \exp\!\Bigl(-\tfrac{2\mu x_0}{\sigma^2}\Bigr) < 1.}$
$

---

**Step 2: The expected time is infinite.**

Since $\mu > 0$, we have $P(\tau = \infty) = 1 - \exp(-2\mu x_0/\sigma^2) > 0$.
The random variable $\tau$ takes the value $+\infty$ with positive probability,
so

$$\boxed{E[\tau] = +\infty.}$$

This is the main result: **with positive drift, there is a positive probability
the balance never becomes negative, so the unconditional expected waiting time i
s infinite.**

---

**Step 3 (supplementary): Conditional expectation given ruin occurs.**

For completeness, we compute $E[\tau \mid \tau < \infty]$. The first-passage-tim
e density (defective for $\mu>0$) is the inverse Gaussian-type density:

$$f_\tau(t) = \frac{x_0}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi\, t^3}}\,\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{(x_0+\mu\,
 t)^2}{2\sigma^2 t}\Bigr), \qquad t > 0.$$

Expanding the exponent:

$$f_\tau(t) = e^{-2\mu x_0/\sigma^2}\cdot \underbrace{\frac{x_0/\mu}{\sqrt{2\pi\
, t^3/\lambda}}\,\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{\lambda(t - x_0/\mu)^2}{2(x_0/\mu)^2\, t}\Bi
gr)}_{\text{IG}(x_0/\mu,\; x_0^2/\sigma^2) \text{ density}},$$

where $\lambda = x_0^2/\sigma^2$. (One verifies the matching by expanding the IG
 exponent $\frac{\lambda(t-\mu_{\rm IG})^2}{2\mu_{\rm IG}^2 t} = \frac{\lambda}{
2t} - \frac{\lambda}{\mu_{\rm IG}} + \frac{\lambda t}{2\mu_{\rm IG}^2}$ with $\m
u_{\rm IG}=x_0/\mu$, $\lambda = x_0^2/\sigma^2$, and noting the prefactor $e^{-\
mu x_0/\sigma^2}\cdot e^{\lambda/\mu_{\rm IG}} = e^{-\mu x_0/\sigma^2}\cdot e^{\
mu x_0/\sigma^2} = 1$ accounts for one factor, while the remaining $e^{-\mu x_0/
\sigma^2}$ combines with the $e^{-\mu x_0/\sigma^2}$ from the cross term to give
 $e^{-2\mu x_0/\sigma^2}$.)

Since the IG$(\mu_{\rm IG}, \lambda)$ distribution has mean $\mu_{\rm IG} = x_0/
\mu$:

$$E[\tau\,\mathbf{1}_{\tau<\infty}] = \int_0^\infty t\, f_\tau(t)\,dt = e^{-2\mu
 x_0/\sigma^2}\cdot\frac{x_0}{\mu}.$$

Dividing by $P(\tau<\infty) = e^{-2\mu x_0/\sigma^2}$:

$$\boxed{E[\tau \mid \tau < \infty] = \frac{x_0}{\mu}.}$$

This is intuitive: conditioned on ruin occurring, the expected time is just the
initial balance divided by the drift rate — the time it would take the determini
stic drift alone to deplete the balance.

---

**Summary.**

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| $P(\tau < \infty)$ (ruin probability) | $\exp\!\bigl(-2\mu x_0/\sigma^2\bigr)$
 |
| $E[\tau]$ (unconditional expected time) | $+\infty$ |
| $E[\tau \mid \tau < \infty]$ (conditional expected time) | $x_0/\mu$ |

The answer to the question as posed — the (unconditional) expected time until th
e balance first becomes negative — is **$+\infty$**, because the positive drift
gives a positive probability $1 - e^{-2\mu x_0/\sigma^2}$ that the balance never
 becomes negative at all.
