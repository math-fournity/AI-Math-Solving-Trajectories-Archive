## Proof

**Setup.** After the first two shots, Shanille has 1 hit and 1 miss. For each su
bsequent shot $n \geq 3$, the probability of a hit equals the current proportion
 of hits among all previous shots. Let $H_n$ denote the number of hits after $n$
 shots, so $H_2 = 1$.

**Connection to the Pólya urn.** This process is exactly a Pólya urn model. Cons
ider an urn initially containing 1 red ball (representing a hit) and 1 black bal
l (representing a miss). At each step, draw a ball, return it, and add another b
all of the same color. The probability of drawing red at each step equals the cu
rrent proportion of red balls—precisely the rule governing Shanille's shots. Sho
ts $3, 4, \dots, 100$ correspond to 98 draws from this urn, and the number of hi
ts among these 98 shots equals the number of red balls drawn.

**Pólya urn distribution.** For a Pólya urn starting with $r$ red and $b$ black
balls, after $n$ draws the number $K$ of red balls drawn satisfies

$$P(K = k) = \binom{n}{k}\frac{(r)_k\,(b)_{n-k}}{(r+b)_n},$$

where $(x)_m = x(x+1)\cdots(x+m-1)$ is the rising factorial.

**Specialize to $r = b = 1$.** With $r = b = 1$:

$$P(K = k) = \binom{n}{k}\frac{k!\,(n-k)!}{(n+1)!} = \frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}\cdot\fr
ac{k!(n-k)!}{(n+1)!} = \frac{n!}{(n+1)!} = \frac{1}{n+1}.$$

This holds for every $k \in \{0, 1, \dots, n\}$, so $K$ is **uniform** on $\{0,
1, \dots, n\}$.

**Apply to our problem.** Here $n = 98$ (shots 3 through 100). The total number
of hits after 100 shots is $1 + K$, where $K$ is uniform on $\{0, 1, \dots, 98\}
$. We want exactly 50 hits total, i.e., $K = 49$.

$$P(\text{exactly 50 hits in 100 shots}) = P(K = 49) = \frac{1}{98 + 1} = \boxed
{\dfrac{1}{99}}.$$
