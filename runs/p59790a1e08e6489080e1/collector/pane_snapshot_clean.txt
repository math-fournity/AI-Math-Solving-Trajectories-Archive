## Proof

**Problem.** Determine the value of $-1+2+3+4-5-6-7-8-9+\cdots+10000$, where the
 signs change after each perfect square.

**Step 1: Identify the block structure.**

The perfect squares are $1, 4, 9, 16, \ldots, 10000 = 100^2$. The sign is consta
nt on each block between consecutive perfect squares and flips at each square. R
eading off the given pattern:

| Block $k$ | Range | Sign | # of terms |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $\{1\}$ | $-$ | 1 |
| 2 | $\{2,3,4\}$ | $+$ | 3 |
| 3 | $\{5,6,7,8,9\}$ | $-$ | 5 |
| 4 | $\{10,\ldots,16\}$ | $+$ | 7 |

In general, **block $k$** consists of the integers from $(k-1)^2+1$ to $k^2$ (wh
ich gives $k^2-(k-1)^2 = 2k-1$ terms), and carries sign $(-1)^k$.

**Step 2: Sum of the integers in block $k$.**

The integers in block $k$ run from $(k-1)^2+1$ to $k^2$. There are $2k-1$ terms,
 with average

$$\frac{(k-1)^2+1+k^2}{2} = \frac{2k^2-2k+2}{2} = k^2-k+1.$$

So the unsigned block sum is

$$(2k-1)(k^2-k+1) = 2k^3-3k^2+3k-1.$$

A key identity: setting $n = k-1$, the sum of integers from $n^2+1$ to $(n+1)^2$
 equals

$$(2n+1)(n^2+n+1) = 2n^3+3n^2+3n+1 = (n+1)^3 + n^3.$$

**Step 3: Closed form by induction.**

Let $S_n$ denote the partial sum through block $n$, i.e., through $n^2$. We clai
m

$$S_n = (-1)^n\, n^3.$$

*Base case.* $S_1 = -1 = (-1)^1\cdot 1^3$. ✓

*Inductive step.* Suppose $S_n = (-1)^n n^3$. Block $n+1$ (integers $n^2+1$ thro
ugh $(n+1)^2$) has sign $(-1)^{n+1}$ and unsigned sum $(n+1)^3+n^3$ (by Step 2).
 Thus

$$S_{n+1} = (-1)^n n^3 + (-1)^{n+1}\bigl[(n+1)^3+n^3\bigr] = (-1)^n\bigl[n^3-(n+
1)^3-n^3\bigr] = (-1)^{n+1}(n+1)^3. \quad\checkmark$$

**Step 4: Evaluate at $n=100$.**

Since $10000 = 100^2$, the full sum is $S_{100}$:

$$S_{100} = (-1)^{100}\cdot 100^3 = 1\cdot 1{,}000{,}000 = \boxed{1{,}000{,}000}
.$$
