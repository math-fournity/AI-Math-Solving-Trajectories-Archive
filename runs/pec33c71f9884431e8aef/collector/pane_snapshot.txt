## Proof

**Answer: Yes.**

**Claim.** Let $X, Y$ be Banach spaces and $T: X \to Y$ a surjective continuous
linear operator. For every sequence $\{y_n\}$ in $Y$ with $y_n \to y \in Y$, the
re exists a sequence $\{x_n\}$ in $X$ with $x_n \to x \in X$ such that $T(x_n) =
 y_n$ for all $n$ and $T(x) = y$.

**Proof.**

Since $T: X \to Y$ is a surjective continuous linear map between Banach spaces,
by the **Open Mapping Theorem**, $T$ is an open map. Consequently, $T(B_X(0,1))$
 is an open neighborhood of $0$ in $Y$, so there exists $r > 0$ such that
$$B_Y(0, r) \subseteq T\bigl(B_X(0,1)\bigr).$$

This yields the following standard lifting estimate: for every $y \in Y$, there
exists $x \in X$ with
$$T(x) = y \quad \text{and} \quad \|x\| \le \frac{1}{r}\|y\|. \tag{$\ast$}$$

Indeed, if $y = 0$ take $x = 0$. If $y \neq 0$, set $\tilde{y} = \frac{r}{2\|y\|
} y \in B_Y(0,r)$. Then $\tilde{y} = T(\tilde{x})$ for some $\tilde{x} \in B_X(0
,1)$, so $y = T\!\left(\frac{2\|y\|}{r}\tilde{x}\right)$ and $x = \frac{2\|y\|}{
r}\tilde{x}$ satisfies $\|x\| \le \frac{2}{r}\|y\|$. (A sharper constant $1/r$ i
s obtainable by a scaling/Baire-category refinement, but any fixed constant $C =
 1/r$ suffices for the argument below.)

Now let $y_n \to y$ in $Y$.

**Step 1 (lift the limit).** Since $T$ is surjective, pick any $x \in X$ with $T
(x) = y$.

**Step 2 (lift the errors).** Set $z_n := y_n - y$. Then $z_n \to 0$ in $Y$. By
$(\ast)$, for each $n$ there exists $u_n \in X$ with
$$T(u_n) = z_n \quad \text{and} \quad \|u_n\| \le C\|z_n\|,$$
where $C = 1/r$ is the constant from $(\ast)$. Since $z_n \to 0$, we have $\|u_n
\| \le C\|z_n\| \to 0$, hence
$$u_n \to 0 \quad \text{in } X.$$

**Step 3 (combine).** Define $x_n := x + u_n$. Then:
- $T(x_n) = T(x) + T(u_n) = y + z_n = y + (y_n - y) = y_n$ for every $n$,
- $x_n \to x + 0 = x$ in $X$, since $u_n \to 0$,
- $T(x) = y$ by construction.

Thus $\{x_n\}$ is the desired convergent lift of $\{y_n\}$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The key ingredient is the Open Mapping Theorem, which requires both
$X$ and $Y$ to be complete (Banach) and $T$ to be surjective. Without completene
ss of $X$ or without surjectivity, the conclusion can fail: the bounded right-in
verse provided by $(\ast)$ is what guarantees that small errors in $Y$ lift to s
mall errors in $X$, preserving convergence.
