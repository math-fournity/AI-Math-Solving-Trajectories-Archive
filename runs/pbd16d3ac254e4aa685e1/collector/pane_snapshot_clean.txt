# Cycle Index of Rotation Groups and Wreath Products

## (a) Cycle Index of the Rotation Group of a Regular $n$-gon

The rotation group of a regular $n$-gon is the cyclic group $C_n$ of order $n$,
consisting of rotations by angles $\frac{2\pi k}{n}$ for $k = 0, 1, \ldots, n-1$
. Label the vertices $0, 1, \ldots, n-1$; rotation by $k$ positions sends $i \ma
psto i + k \pmod{n}$.

**Cycle structure of rotation by $k$.** The cycle containing vertex $0$ has leng
th $\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}$ (the smallest $m$ with $mk \equiv 0 \pmod{n}$). Since a
ll cycles have the same length, the number of cycles is $\gcd(n,k)$. Thus rotati
on by $k$ contributes the monomial $x_{n/\gcd(n,k)}^{\,\gcd(n,k)}$.

**Grouping by $d = \gcd(n,k)$.** For each divisor $d$ of $n$, the number of $k \
in \{0,\ldots,n-1\}$ with $\gcd(n,k) = d$ is $\varphi(n/d)$ (Euler's totient). S
etting $e = n/d$, the cycle index is:

$$\boxed{Z(C_n) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{d \mid n} \varphi(d)\, x_d^{\,n/d}.}$$

*Verification ($n=4$, square):* $Z(C_4) = \tfrac{1}{4}\bigl[\varphi(1)\,x_1^4 +
\varphi(2)\,x_2^2 + \varphi(4)\,x_4\bigr] = \tfrac{1}{4}(x_1^4 + x_2^2 + 2x_4)$,
 matching the identity ($x_1^4$), $180°$ ($x_2^2$), and two $90°/270°$ rotations
 ($2x_4$). ✓

## (b) Cycle Index of the Group Preserving a Partition into $k$ Classes of Size
$n$

Let the $nk$ elements be partitioned as $P = \{C_1, \ldots, C_k\}$ with $|C_i| =
 n$. A permutation $\sigma$ of the $nk$ elements *preserves* $P$ iff $\sigma(C_i
) = C_{\pi(i)}$ for some $\pi \in S_k$, and within each class $\sigma$ acts as s
ome $\sigma_i \in S_n$. This group is the **wreath product** $S_n \wr S_k = S_n^
k \rtimes S_k$ of order $(n!)^k \cdot k!$.

**Action.** An element $(\sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_k;\, \pi)$ sends $(i, a) \maps
to (\pi(i),\, \sigma_i(a))$, where $i$ indexes the class and $a \in \{1,\ldots,n
\}$ indexes the element within the class.

**Cycle analysis.** Consider a cycle of $\pi$ of length $j$, say $(i_0\; i_1\; \
cdots\; i_{j-1})$. The $jn$ elements in classes $i_0, \ldots, i_{j-1}$ are permu
ted among themselves. Tracking an element $(i_0, a)$ around the full $\pi$-cycle
:

$$(i_0, a) \to (i_1, \sigma_{i_0}(a)) \to \cdots \to (i_0,\; \sigma_{i_{j-1}} \c
irc \cdots \circ \sigma_{i_0}(a)) = (i_0, \tau(a)),$$

where $\tau = \sigma_{i_{j-1}} \circ \cdots \circ \sigma_{i_0} \in S_n$. If $a$
lies in an $\ell$-cycle of $\tau$, then $(i_0, a)$ lies in a cycle of length $j\
ell$ in the full permutation (it takes $j$ steps to return to class $i_0$, and $
\ell$ such rounds to return to the starting element). Each $\ell$-cycle of $\tau
$ yields exactly **one** cycle of length $j\ell$. The contribution of this $\pi$
-cycle to the monomial is therefore $\prod_\ell x_{j\ell}^{c_\ell(\tau)}$, where
 $c_\ell(\tau)$ is the number of $\ell$-cycles of $\tau$.

**Averaging over the $\sigma_i$.** As $\sigma_{i_0}, \ldots, \sigma_{i_{j-1}}$ r
ange independently over $S_n$, the composite $\tau$ ranges uniformly over $S_n$
(fixing any $j-1$ of the $\sigma$'s, the last determines $\tau$ arbitrarily; eac
h $\tau$ is hit exactly $(n!)^{j-1}$ times). Hence the average contribution of o
ne $j$-cycle of $\pi$ is:

$$\frac{1}{(n!)^j}\sum_{\sigma_{i_0},\ldots,\sigma_{i_{j-1}}} \prod_\ell x_{j\el
l}^{c_\ell(\tau)} = \frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\tau \in S_n} \prod_\ell x_{j\ell}^{c_\ell
(\tau)} = Z\!\bigl(S_n;\; x_j,\, x_{2j},\, x_{3j},\, \ldots\bigr),$$

where $Z(S_n) = \frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \prod_i x_i^{c_i(\sigma)}$ is
the cycle index of $S_n$, with each variable $x_i$ replaced by $x_{ji}$.

**Combining all $\pi$-cycles.** Distinct cycles of $\pi$ act on disjoint sets of
 classes, so their contributions are independent. Averaging over all $\pi \in S_
k$ and all $\sigma_i \in S_n$:

$$\boxed{Z(S_n \wr S_k) = \frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\pi \in S_k} \prod_{j=1}^{k} \bigl[Z
(S_n;\; x_j,\, x_{2j},\, x_{3j},\, \ldots)\bigr]^{c_j(\pi)},}$$

where $c_j(\pi)$ is the number of $j$-cycles in $\pi$. Equivalently, using the c
ycle index $Z(S_k) = \frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\pi \in S_k} \prod_j x_j^{c_j(\pi)}$, thi
s is the **composition** $Z(S_k) \circ Z(S_n)$ obtained by substituting $x_j \ma
psto Z(S_n; x_j, x_{2j}, \ldots)$.

## Generalization: Cycle Index of an Arbitrary Wreath Product

Both results generalize to the **wreath product $H \wr G$** of two *arbitrary* p
ermutation groups: $H$ acting on $n$ points and $G$ acting on $k$ points, with $
H \wr G$ acting on $nk$ points (the imprimitive action on $k$ blocks of size $n$
).

The same cycle analysis applies verbatim: each $j$-cycle of $g \in G$ contribute
s, on average over the $H$-components, the term $Z(H;\, x_j, x_{2j}, \ldots)$. T
herefore:

$$\boxed{Z(H \wr G) = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g \in G} \prod_{j \geq 1} \bigl[Z(H;\;
x_j,\, x_{2j},\, x_{3j},\, \ldots)\bigr]^{c_j(g)} = Z(G) \circ Z(H).}$$

**Special cases recovering (a) and (b):**

- **(b)** is recovered by setting $H = S_n$, $G = S_k$: the full symmetric group
 on each block and on the set of blocks.
- **(a)** is the cycle index of $C_n$ itself, which serves as a *building block*
: taking $H = C_n$ (rotations within each block) and $G = C_k$ (cyclically permu
ting the blocks) yields the cycle index of $C_n \wr C_k$, the group of rotations
 of a "necklace" of $k$ regular $n$-gons. More generally, $H$ and $G$ can be any
 permutation groups—cyclic, dihedral, symmetric, alternating, or others—giving a
 unified formula for the cycle index of every imprimitive permutation group buil
t as a wreath product.

The composition formula $Z(H \wr G) = Z(G) \circ Z(H)$ is the master result: it
expresses the cycle index of any wreath product purely in terms of the cycle ind
ices of its factors, and both (a) and (b) are instances of this single principle
.
