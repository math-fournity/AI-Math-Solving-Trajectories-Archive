## Solution

The numbers $a_k$ are the **unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind**, denot
ed $\left[{n \atop k}\right]$, which count permutations of $\{1,2,\dots,n\}$ wit
h exactly $k$ disjoint cycles.

### Key identity (rising factorial)

The unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind satisfy the generating function
identity
$$\sum_{k=0}^{n} \left[{n \atop k}\right] x^k \;=\; x(x+1)(x+2)\cdots(x+n-1) \;=
\; x^{\overline{n}},$$
the rising factorial.

**Proof of the identity.** Let $P_n(x) = \prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(x+i)$. We show $\prod
_{i=0}^{n-1}(x+i) = \sum_k \left[{n \atop k}\right]x^k$ by induction on $n$. The
 recurrence for the unsigned Stirling numbers is
$$\left[{n \atop k}\right] = \left[{n-1 \atop k-1}\right] + (n-1)\left[{n-1 \ato
p k}\right],$$
which corresponds exactly to the product rule $P_n(x) = P_{n-1}(x)\cdot(x+n-1)$:
 the term $\left[{n-1 \atop k-1}\right]$ comes from multiplying $x^{k-1}$ by $x$
, and $(n-1)\left[{n-1 \atop k}\right]$ comes from multiplying $x^k$ by $(n-1)$.
 The base case $P_1(x)=x=\left[{1\atop 1}\right]x$ holds. $\square$

### Evaluating the sum

The desired quantity is
$$S = \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k\, n^k = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \left[{n \atop k}\right] n^k.$$

Since $\left[{n \atop 0}\right]=0$ for $n\ge 1$, we may include the $k=0$ term h
armlessly:
$$S = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \left[{n \atop k}\right] n^k = P_n(n) = \prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(n
+i).$$

This product has $n$ consecutive factors starting from $n$:
$$S = n\cdot(n+1)\cdot(n+2)\cdots(2n-1) = \frac{(2n-1)!}{(n-1)!}.$$

### Verification ($n=3$)

For $n=3$: $a_1=2$ (the two 3-cycles), $a_2=3$ (given in the problem), $a_3=1$ (
the identity). Then
$$S = 2\cdot 3 + 3\cdot 9 + 1\cdot 27 = 6+27+27 = 60,$$
and
$$\frac{(2\cdot 3-1)!}{(3-1)!} = \frac{5!}{2!}=\frac{120}{2}=60. \;\checkmark$$

### Answer

$$\boxed{\,a_n n^n + a_{n-1} n^{n-1} + \dots + a_1 n \;=\; \dfrac{(2n-1)!}{(n-1)
!}\,}$$
