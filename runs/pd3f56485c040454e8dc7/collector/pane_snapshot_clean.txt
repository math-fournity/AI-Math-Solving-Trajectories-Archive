Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum number of universal vertices
 (master hubs) in a graph on 100 vertices where every 4-subset contains a vertex
 adjacent to the other three.

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $G$ be a graph on 100 vertices. The condition states: for every 4
-subset $S$, some vertex in $S$ is adjacent to the other three vertices of $S$.
A *master hub* is a vertex of degree 99 (universal). Let $k$ be the number of ma
ster hubs. We seek the minimum $k$.

**Step 1: Reduce to the non-master subgraph.** Remove the $k$ master hubs, leavi
ng an induced subgraph $H$ on $n = 100 - k$ vertices. Since every 4-subset of $G
$ satisfies the condition, and any 4-subset of $H$ is also a 4-subset of $G$ con
taining no master hubs, the condition must be satisfied within $H$ itself: every
 4-subset of $H$ has a vertex adjacent to the other three.

**Step 2: Complement formulation.** Let $\bar{H}$ be the complement of $H$. The
condition on $H$ becomes: every 4-vertex induced subgraph of $\bar{H}$ has an **
isolated vertex** (a vertex with no neighbors within the 4-set). Equivalently, $
\bar{H}$ contains no 4-vertex induced subgraph with minimum degree $\geq 1$.

**Step 3: Bound the non-isolated vertices of $\bar{H}$.** Let $S$ be the set of
non-isolated vertices in $\bar{H}$ (vertices with degree $\geq 1$). Key observat
ion: non-isolated vertices only connect to non-isolated vertices (if $v$ has an
edge to $u$, then $u$ has an edge to $v$, so $u$ is non-isolated). Thus all edge
s of $\bar{H}$ lie within $S$.

**Claim:** $|S| \leq 3$.

*Proof of claim.* Suppose $|S| \geq 4$.

- **No two disjoint edges:** If $\bar{H}[S]$ contained two disjoint edges $\{a,b
\}$ and $\{c,d\}$, then the 4-set $\{a,b,c,d\}$ would have every vertex with deg
ree $\geq 1$ (each is incident to at least one of the two edges, plus possibly c
ross-edges). No isolated vertex—contradiction. So $\bar{H}[S]$ has matching numb
er $\leq 1$.

- **Structure of matching number $\leq 1$:** A graph where all edges pairwise in
tersect is either a **star** (all edges share a common center) or a **triangle**
 $K_3$ (and any additional edge beyond a triangle creates a disjoint pair, as sh
own by a simple case analysis).

- **Star case:** If $\bar{H}[S]$ is a star with center $c$ and $m \geq 3$ leaves
, take the 4-set $\{c, x, y, z\}$ for three leaves $x,y,z$. In the induced subgr
aph, $c$ has degree 3 and each leaf has degree 1—no isolated vertex (this is $K_
{1,3}$). Contradiction. So $m \leq 2$, giving $|S| \leq 3$.

- **Triangle case:** $K_3$ has exactly 3 vertices, so $|S| = 3$.

In all cases, $|S| \leq 3$. $\square$

**Step 4: Universal vertices of $H$ are master hubs of $G$.** The isolated verti
ces of $\bar{H}$ are exactly the universal vertices of $H$ (adjacent to all othe
rs in $H$). There are $n - |S| \geq n - 3$ of them. Any such vertex $v$ is adjac
ent to all $n-1$ vertices in $H$ and also to all $k$ master hubs (since each mas
ter hub is universal in $G$, hence adjacent to $v$, and $G$ is undirected). So $
v$ has degree 99 in $G$, making $v$ a master hub.

But $H$ was defined as the subgraph on **non-master** vertices. So $H$ contains
no master hubs, meaning $H$ has no universal vertices, i.e., $n - |S| = 0$, givi
ng $n = |S| \leq 3$.

**Step 5: Determine $k$.** Since $n = 100 - k \leq 3$, we get $k \geq 97$.

**Step 6: $k = 97$ is achievable.** Take 97 master hubs (universal vertices) and
 3 non-master vertices with arbitrary edges among them. Every 4-subset of the 10
0 vertices must include at least one master hub (since only 3 vertices are non-m
aster). That master hub is adjacent to all other 99 vertices, hence to the other
 3 in the quartet. The condition is satisfied. ✓

**Step 7: $k = 96$ is impossible.** With $k = 96$, we have $n = 4$ non-master ve
rtices forming $H$. The single 4-subset (all of $H$) must contain a vertex adjac
ent to the other 3, i.e., a universal vertex of $H$. By Step 4, this vertex is a
 master hub of $G$—contradicting that it is a non-master vertex.

## Conclusion

The minimum possible number of master hubs is $\boxed{97}$.
