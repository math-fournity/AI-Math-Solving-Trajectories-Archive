## Proof that ${}_R R$ is injective for $R = M_n(D)$

**Claim.** The matrix ring $R = M_n(D)$ over a division ring $D$, considered as
a left module over itself, is an injective module.

**Step 1: ${}_R R$ is a semisimple module.**

Let $e_i = e_{ii}$ denote the $i$-th diagonal matrix unit. The left ideal $Re_i$
 consists of all matrices in $M_n(D)$ whose only nonzero column is the $i$-th co
lumn. As a left $R$-module,

$$Re_i \cong D^n$$

via the map sending a matrix in $Re_i$ to its $i$-th column, with $R$ acting by
left multiplication on column vectors. This module is **simple**: given any nonz
ero $v \in D^n$ and any $w \in D^n$, since $D$ is a division ring there exists a
 matrix $A \in M_n(D)$ with $Av = w$ (extend $v$ to a basis using Gaussian elimi
nation over $D$, then send each basis vector appropriately). Hence every nonzero
 element generates all of $D^n$.

Since $1 = e_1 + e_2 + \cdots + e_n$ with the $e_i$ orthogonal idempotents,

$$R = Re_1 \oplus Re_2 \oplus \cdots \oplus Re_n,$$

a direct sum of $n$ simple left $R$-modules. Therefore ${}_R R$ is **semisimple*
*.

**Step 2: Every submodule of a semisimple module is a direct summand.**

This is the defining characterization of semisimple modules. Concretely, if $M =
 \bigoplus_{k} S_k$ with each $S_k$ simple and $N \leq M$, choose a maximal subs
et $K$ of indices such that $N + \bigoplus_{k \in K} S_k$ is an internal direct
sum. For any index $\ell \notin K$, the sum $N \oplus \bigoplus_{k \in K} S_k +
S_\ell$ is not direct, so $S_\ell \cap (N \oplus \bigoplus_{k \in K} S_k) \neq 0
$; by simplicity of $S_\ell$ this forces $S_\ell \subseteq N \oplus \bigoplus_{k
 \in K} S_k$. Hence

$$M = N \oplus \bigoplus_{k \in K} S_k.$$

Applying this to ${}_R R$: **every left ideal $I$ of $R$ is a direct summand**,
i.e., $R = I \oplus J$ for some left ideal $J$.

**Step 3: Baer's criterion gives injectivity.**

By **Baer's criterion**, ${}_R R$ is injective if and only if for every left ide
al $I \leq R$ and every $R$-homomorphism $f: I \to R$, there exists an $R$-homom
orphism $\tilde{f}: R \to R$ extending $f$.

Given such $f: I \to R$, use Step 2 to write $R = I \oplus J$ for some left idea
l $J$. Define

$$\tilde{f}: R \to R, \qquad \tilde{f}(i + j) = f(i) \quad (i \in I,\; j \in J).
$$

This is well-defined because the sum is direct. It is an $R$-homomorphism: for $
r \in R$,

$$\tilde{f}(r \cdot (i+j)) = \tilde{f}(ri + rj) = f(ri) = r\cdot f(i) = r \cdot
\tilde{f}(i+j),$$

using that $ri \in I$, $rj \in J$ (both are left ideals), and $f$ is $R$-linear.
 Clearly $\tilde{f}|_I = f$.

Since every such $f$ extends, Baer's criterion yields that ${}_R R$ is **injecti
ve**.

**Conclusion.** The matrix ring $R = M_n(D)$ is self-injective: as a left module
 over itself, it is an injective module. $\blacksquare$
