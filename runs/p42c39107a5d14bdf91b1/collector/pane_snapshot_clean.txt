## Green's Function for the 1D Wave Equation

**Problem.** Find $G(x,t;\xi,\tau)$ satisfying
$$G_{tt} - c^{2}G_{xx} = \delta(x-\xi)\,\delta(t-\tau),\qquad -\infty<x<\infty,$
$
with the causality (retarded) condition $G=0$ for $t<\tau$.

**Answer.**
$$\boxed{\,G(x,t;\xi,\tau) = \frac{1}{2c}\,H\!\bigl(t-\tau - \tfrac{|x-\xi|}{c}\
bigr)\,}$$
where $H$ is the Heaviside step function. Equivalently,
$$G = \begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{2c}, & |x-\xi|\le c(t-\tau),\ t\ge\tau,\\[4pt] 0, &
 \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$

---

### Derivation

**1. Reduction by translation invariance.** The operator and domain are invarian
t under $(x,t)\mapsto(x+\xi,t+\tau)$, so it suffices to solve the case $\xi=0,\
\tau=0$ and then replace $x\to x-\xi,\ t\to t-\tau$. We seek $G(x,t)$ with
$$G_{tt}-c^{2}G_{xx}=\delta(x)\delta(t),\qquad G\big|_{t<0}=0.$$

**2. Fourier transform in $x$.** Write $G(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\in
fty}\widehat{G}(k,t)\,e^{ikx}\,dk$. The PDE becomes an ODE in $t$ for each waven
umber $k$:
$$\widehat{G}_{tt}+c^{2}k^{2}\,\widehat{G}=\delta(t),\qquad \widehat{G}(k,t<0)=0
.$$

**3. Solve the ODE.** For $t>0$ the equation is homogeneous: $\widehat{G}_{tt}+c
^{2}k^{2}\widehat{G}=0$, with general solution $\widehat{G}=A\cos(ckt)+B\sin(ckt
)$. Causality gives $\widehat{G}(0^{-})=0$, hence $\widehat{G}(0^{+})=0$ (contin
uity, since a $\delta$ in the second derivative produces a jump only in the *fir
st* derivative), so $A=0$. Integrating across $t=0$,
$$\widehat{G}_{t}(0^{+})-\widehat{G}_{t}(0^{-})=\int_{0^{-}}^{0^{+}}\delta(t)\,d
t=1,$$
and $\widehat{G}_{t}(0^{-})=0$, so $B\,ck=1$, i.e. $B=1/(ck)$. Thus
$$\widehat{G}(k,t)=\frac{\sin(ckt)}{ck},\qquad t>0.$$

**4. Invert the transform.**
$$G(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(ckt)}{ck}\,e^{ikx}\,dk
=\frac{1}{4\pi ic}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{ik(x+ct)}-e^{ik(x-ct)}}{k}\,dk
,\qquad t>0.$$
Using the distributional identity $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{
ika}}{k}\,dk=i\pi\,\operatorname{sgn}(a)$,
$$G(x,t)=\frac{1}{4\pi ic}\,i\pi\bigl[\operatorname{sgn}(x+ct)-\operatorname{sgn
}(x-ct)\bigr]=\frac{1}{4c}\bigl[\operatorname{sgn}(x+ct)-\operatorname{sgn}(x-ct
)\bigr].$$

For $t>0$ (so $ct>0$): the difference $\operatorname{sgn}(x+ct)-\operatorname{sg
n}(x-ct)$ equals $2$ when $-ct<x<ct$ and $0$ when $|x|>ct$. Therefore
$$G(x,t)=\frac{1}{2c}\,\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|<ct\}}=\frac{1}{2c}\,H\!\bigl(t-\tfrac{|
x|}{c}\bigr),\qquad t>0.$$
Since $H(t-|x|/c)=0$ automatically when $t<0$, the single formula $G=\frac{1}{2c
}H(t-|x|/c)$ encodes the causality condition as well. Restoring $\xi,\tau$:
$$G(x,t;\xi,\tau)=\frac{1}{2c}\,H\!\bigl(t-\tau-\tfrac{|x-\xi|}{c}\bigr).$$

---

### Verification (distributional)

Set $\xi=\tau=0$ and $u=\frac{1}{2c}H(t-|x|/c)$. We show $u_{tt}-c^{2}u_{xx}=\de
lta(x)\delta(t)$.

**Time derivatives.** $\partial_{t}H(t-|x|/c)=\delta(t-|x|/c)$, so $u_{tt}=\frac
{1}{2c}\delta'(t-|x|/c)$.

**Space derivatives.** With $r=|x|/c$ and $\partial_{x}r=\operatorname{sgn}(x)/c
$,
$$u_{x}=\frac{1}{2c}\bigl(-\tfrac{\operatorname{sgn}(x)}{c}\bigr)\delta(t-r),$$
$$u_{xx}=-\frac{1}{2c^{2}}\Bigl[\,2\delta(x)\,\delta(t-r)+\operatorname{sgn}(x)\
,\delta'(t-r)\bigl(-\tfrac{\operatorname{sgn}(x)}{c}\bigr)\Bigr].$$
Using $\operatorname{sgn}'(x)=2\delta(x)$ and $\operatorname{sgn}^{2}(x)=1$, and
 noting $r\big|_{x=0}=0$ so $\delta(t-r)\big|_{x=0}=\delta(t)$:
$$u_{xx}=-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\delta(x)\delta(t)+\frac{1}{2c^{3}}\delta'(t-|x|/c).$$

**Combine.**
$$u_{tt}-c^{2}u_{xx}=\frac{1}{2c}\delta'(t-|x|/c)-c^{2}\!\left[-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\
delta(x)\delta(t)+\frac{1}{2c^{3}}\delta'(t-|x|/c)\right]$$
$$=\frac{1}{2c}\delta'(t-|x|/c)+\delta(x)\delta(t)-\frac{1}{2c}\delta'(t-|x|/c)=
\delta(x)\delta(t).\quad\checkmark$$

The two $\delta'$ terms cancel exactly, leaving the required point source. Causa
lity holds because $H(t-\tau-|x-\xi|/c)=0$ whenever $t<\tau$. $\blacksquare$
