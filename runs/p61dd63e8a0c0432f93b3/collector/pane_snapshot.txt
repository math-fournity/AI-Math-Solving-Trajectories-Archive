## Proof

Let $\mathfrak{g} = T_I G$ denote the Lie algebra of $G$ (the tangent space at t
he identity). The exponential map
$$
\exp \colon \mathfrak{g} \to G
$$
is smooth and satisfies $\exp(0) = I$.

**Key fact:** The differential of $\exp$ at $0$ is the identity:
$$
d(\exp)_0 = \operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{g}}.
$$

*Proof of the key fact.* For any $X \in \mathfrak{g}$, the curve $\gamma(t) = \e
xp(tX)$ is the one-parameter subgroup of $G$ with $\gamma(0) = I$ and $\gamma'(0
) = X$. Under the canonical identification $T_0\mathfrak{g} \cong \mathfrak{g}$,
 the differential $d(\exp)_0$ sends $X$ to $\gamma'(0) = X$. Hence $d(\exp)_0 =
\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{g}}$. $\square$

Since $d(\exp)_0 = \operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{g}}$ is invertible, the **Invers
e Function Theorem** applies: there exist an open neighborhood $V$ of $0$ in $\m
athfrak{g}$ and an open neighborhood $U$ of $I$ in $G$ such that
$$
\exp\big|_V \colon V \to U
$$
is a diffeomorphism. In particular, $\exp\big|_V \colon V \to U$ is a **bijectio
n**.

Therefore, **yes**, such a neighborhood $U$ of the identity exists: $\exp$ maps
a suitable neighborhood of $0$ in $\mathfrak{g}$ bijectively (indeed diffeomorph
ically) onto a neighborhood $U$ of $I$ in $G$.

**Remark on the hypotheses.** The connectedness of $G$ is not needed for this *l
ocal* result — the Inverse Function Theorem is purely local and holds for any ma
trix Lie group (connected or not). Connectedness becomes relevant for *global* s
tatements (e.g., that $G$ is generated by any neighborhood of $I$, or that $\exp
$ surjects onto $G$ for connected compact or nilpotent groups), but the local bi
jectivity near the identity relies only on the invertibility of $d(\exp)_0$.
