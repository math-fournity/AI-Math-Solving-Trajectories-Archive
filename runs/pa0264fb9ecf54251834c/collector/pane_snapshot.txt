# Solution

**The statement is FALSE.**

## Counterexample: $\mathbb{Z}$

The group $\mathbb{Z}$ is residually finite but not profinite.

### $\mathbb{Z}$ is residually finite

For any nonzero $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, choose a prime $p$ not dividing $n$. The quo
tient homomorphism $\varphi_p : \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ sends $n$
 to $n \bmod p \neq 0$, so $n$ survives in the finite quotient $\mathbb{Z}/p\mat
hbb{Z}$. Since this holds for every non-identity element, $\mathbb{Z}$ is residu
ally finite.

### $\mathbb{Z}$ is not profinite

**Lemma.** *Every profinite group is either finite or uncountable.*

*Proof of Lemma.* A profinite group $G$ is a closed subgroup of a product $\prod
_{i \in I} F_i$ of finite discrete groups. By Tychonoff's theorem this product i
s compact, and it is Hausdorff; hence $G$ is a compact Hausdorff topological gro
up.

We show every compact Hausdorff topological group is either finite or uncountabl
e. Suppose for contradiction that $G$ is countably infinite. Since $G$ is compac
t Hausdorff, it is a Baire space: it cannot be written as a countable union of n
owhere-dense closed sets. But
$$G = \bigcup_{g \in G} \{g\},$$
and each singleton $\{g\}$ is closed (Hausdorff). This is a countable union (sin
ce $G$ is countable), so by the Baire property some singleton $\{g_0\}$ has none
mpty interior, i.e., is open.

By homogeneity — left translation $L_h : x \mapsto hx$ is a homeomorphism for ev
ery $h \in G$ — every singleton $\{h\} = L_h L_{g_0}^{-1}(\{g_0\})$ is open. Thu
s $G$ is discrete. But an infinite discrete space is not compact, contradicting
the compactness of $G$. $\square$

Since $\mathbb{Z}$ is countably infinite, it cannot be isomorphic (even as an ab
stract group) to any profinite group: profinite groups are either finite or unco
untable, and $\mathbb{Z}$ is neither.

### Conclusion

$\mathbb{Z}$ is residually finite but not profinite, so the statement **"Every r
esidually finite group is profinite"** is **false**.

**Remark.** The correct relationship is: a group is **residually finite** if and
 only if it embeds as a dense subgroup of its **profinite completion** $\hat{G}
= \varprojlim G/N$ (over all finite-index normal subgroups $N \trianglelefteq G$
). The group itself is profinite precisely when this embedding is an isomorphism
, i.e., when $G \cong \hat{G}$. For $\mathbb{Z}$, the profinite completion is $\
hat{\mathbb{Z}} \cong \prod_p \mathbb{Z}_p$, which is uncountable, so $\mathbb{Z
} \not\cong \hat{\mathbb{Z}}$.
