## Analysis

The Hadwiger-Nelson problem asks for the chromatic number of the plane — the min
imum number of colors needed to color every point in ℝ² such that no two points
at unit distance share the same color. This is denoted χ(ℝ²).

**Current state of knowledge:**

- **Lower bound: χ(ℝ²) ≥ 5.** Aubrey de Grey (2018) constructed a unit-distance
graph on 1581 vertices that requires 5 colors, disproving the long-standing conj
ecture that χ(ℝ²) = 4. This was subsequently refined by others (e.g., Parts, Jaa
nfelt) to smaller graphs (down to ~509 vertices).

- **Upper bound: χ(ℝ²) ≤ 7.** The classical hexagonal tiling argument (Hadwiger,
 1945; also attributed to others) gives a 7-coloring of the plane avoiding monoc
hromatic unit-distance pairs, by tiling the plane with small hexagons and using
a 7-color periodic pattern with hexagons of diameter slightly less than 1 and sp
acing arranged so that same-colored hexagons are separated by more than unit dis
tance.

- **The exact value (5, 6, or 7) is unknown.** This is one of the most famous op
en problems in combinatorial geometry / geometric graph theory. Despite decades
of effort and the 2018 breakthrough on the lower bound, no one has determined wh
ether χ(ℝ²) is 5, 6, or 7.

## Conclusion

The Hadwiger-Nelson problem is a **genuinely open problem** in mathematics. The
chromatic number of the plane is known only to lie in the set {5, 6, 7}, and det
ermining its exact value remains an unsolved problem. No proof of the exact valu
e exists in the mathematical literature.
