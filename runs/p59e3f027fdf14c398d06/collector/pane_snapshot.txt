The Second Incompleteness Theorem (Gödel, 1931) states the following about Peano
 Arithmetic (PA) and its consistency statement $\operatorname{Con}_{\mathbf{PA}}
$:

## Statement

If Peano Arithmetic is consistent, then PA cannot prove its own consistency. For
mally:

$$\text{If } \mathbf{PA} \text{ is consistent, then } \mathbf{PA} \nvdash \opera
torname{Con}_{\mathbf{PA}}.$$

More precisely, the theorem asserts:

$$\mathbf{PA} \vdash \operatorname{Con}_{\mathbf{PA}} \to \operatorname{G},$$

where $\operatorname{G}$ is a Gödel sentence for PA (a sentence such that $\math
bf{PA} \vdash \operatorname{G} \leftrightarrow \neg\operatorname{Prov}_{\mathbf{
PA}}(\ulcorner \operatorname{G} \urcorner)$, i.e., G says "I am not provable in
PA"). Since $\operatorname{G}$ is unprovable in PA when PA is consistent (by the
 First Incompleteness Theorem), it follows that $\operatorname{Con}_{\mathbf{PA}
}$ is also unprovable in PA.

## What $\operatorname{Con}_{\mathbf{PA}}$ Is

$\operatorname{Con}_{\mathbf{PA}}$ is a specific arithmetical sentence expressin
g the consistency of PA. It is typically formalized as:

$$\operatorname{Con}_{\mathbf{PA}} \;:=\; \neg \operatorname{Prov}_{\mathbf{PA}}
(\ulcorner 0 = 1 \urcorner),$$

i.e., "there is no PA-proof of the contradiction $0 = 1$." Here $\operatorname{P
rov}_{\mathbf{PA}}(x)$ is the standard arithmetized provability predicate (a $\S
igma_1$ formula) representing "x is the Gödel number of a PA-proof."

## Key Consequences

1. **No self-justification.** PA, even if perfectly consistent, lacks the deduct
ive strength to certify its own consistency. Any consistency proof of PA must us
e methods that go beyond what PA itself can formalize (e.g., transfinite inducti
on up to $\varepsilon_0$, as in Gentzen's consistency proof).

2. **Strength of the metatheory matters.** The implication $\mathbf{PA} \vdash \
operatorname{Con}_{\mathbf{PA}} \to \operatorname{G}$ is provable *inside* PA (t
his is the "formalized" part of the theorem). What is *not* provable in PA is $\
operatorname{Con}_{\mathbf{PA}}$ itself. The unprovability of $\operatorname{Con
}_{\mathbf{PA}}$ is established in the metatheory under the assumption that PA i
s consistent (in fact, $\omega$-consistency or even 1-consistency suffices for t
he standard formulation).

3. **Generalization.** The theorem applies to any effectively axiomatized, suffi
ciently expressive consistent theory $T$ extending PA (more generally, any consi
stent, recursively axiomatizable theory capable of representing enough arithmeti
c): $T \nvdash \operatorname{Con}_T$.

4. **Relationship to the First Theorem.** The Second Theorem is a sharpening of
the First. The First Theorem gives an unprovable true sentence G (assuming consi
stency). The Second Theorem identifies *why* PA cannot prove G: because doing so
 would require PA to first prove $\operatorname{Con}_{\mathbf{PA}}$, which it ca
nnot. The derivability conditions (Hilbert–Bernays–Löb conditions) ensure that P
A can reason about its own proof predicate well enough to internalize the First
Theorem's argument, yielding $\mathbf{PA} \vdash \operatorname{Con}_{\mathbf{PA}
} \to \operatorname{G}$.

## Precise Formulation via Derivability Conditions

Let $\Box_{\mathbf{PA}} \varphi$ abbreviate $\operatorname{Prov}_{\mathbf{PA}}(\
ulcorner \varphi \urcorner)$. The provability predicate satisfies the Hilbert–Be
rnays–Löb conditions inside PA:

- **D1:** If $\mathbf{PA} \vdash \varphi$, then $\mathbf{PA} \vdash \Box_{\mathb
f{PA}} \varphi$.
- **D2:** $\mathbf{PA} \vdash \Box_{\mathbf{PA}}(\varphi \to \psi) \to (\Box_{\m
athbf{PA}} \varphi \to \Box_{\mathbf{PA}} \psi)$.
- **D3:** $\mathbf{PA} \vdash \Box_{\mathbf{PA}} \varphi \to \Box_{\mathbf{PA}}
\Box_{\mathbf{PA}} \varphi$.

Using these, the diagonal lemma produces G with $\mathbf{PA} \vdash \operatornam
e{G} \leftrightarrow \neg \Box_{\mathbf{PA}} \operatorname{G}$. One then shows i
nside PA:

$$\mathbf{PA} \vdash \operatorname{Con}_{\mathbf{PA}} \to \operatorname{G}.$$

Since $\mathbf{PA} \nvdash \operatorname{G}$ (First Theorem, assuming consistenc
y), it follows that $\mathbf{PA} \nvdash \operatorname{Con}_{\mathbf{PA}}$.

## Summary

The Second Incompleteness Theorem says: **PA, if consistent, cannot prove $\oper
atorname{Con}_{\mathbf{PA}}$** — the arithmetized statement of its own consisten
cy. PA proves the implication $\operatorname{Con}_{\mathbf{PA}} \to \operatornam
e{G}$, but since G is unprovable in PA (assuming PA is consistent), $\operatorna
me{Con}_{\mathbf{PA}}$ must also be unprovable. Thus no sufficiently strong, con
sistent, effectively axiomatized theory can establish its own consistency.
