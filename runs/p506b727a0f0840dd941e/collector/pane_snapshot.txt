Let $\{q_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ be an enumeration of the rational numbers $\mathbb{
Q}$.

**Construction.** For each pair $(n, k)$ with $n, k \geq 1$, define
$$C_{n,k} \;=\; q_n \;+\; \frac{1}{k \cdot 2^{n+k}}\, C \;=\; \left\{\, q_n + \f
rac{c}{k \cdot 2^{n+k}} \;:\; c \in C \,\right\}.$$
This is a scaled (and translated) copy of the Cantor set, contained in the inter
val
$$\left[\, q_n,\; q_n + \tfrac{1}{k \cdot 2^{n+k}} \,\right] \;\subset\; \left(q
_n - \tfrac{1}{k},\; q_n + \tfrac{1}{k}\right).$$

Now set
$$N \;=\; \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\;\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\; C_{n,k}.$$

**$N$ has Lebesgue measure zero.** Each $C_{n,k}$ is a scaled copy of $C$ by the
 factor $\frac{1}{k \cdot 2^{n+k}}$, so
$$m(C_{n,k}) \;=\; \frac{1}{k \cdot 2^{n+k}}\cdot m(C) \;=\; 0.$$
Thus $N$ is a countable union of measure-zero sets, and by countable subadditivi
ty,
$$m(N) \;\leq\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} m(C_{n,k}) \;=\; 0.$$

**Every point of $\mathbb{R}$ is a condensation point of $N$.** Let $x \in \math
bb{R}$ be arbitrary, and let $U$ be any open neighborhood of $x$. Then $U$ conta
ins some open interval $(x - \delta,\, x + \delta)$ with $\delta > 0$.

Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}$, there exists a rational $q_n \in (x
 - \delta/2,\, x + \delta/2)$. Choose $k$ large enough that $\frac{1}{k} < \frac
{\delta}{2}$. Then for every $y \in C_{n,k}$,
$$|y - x| \;\leq\; |y - q_n| + |q_n - x| \;<\; \frac{1}{k} + \frac{\delta}{2} \;
<\; \frac{\delta}{2} + \frac{\delta}{2} \;=\; \delta,$$
so $C_{n,k} \subset (x - \delta,\, x + \delta) \subset U$.

Since $C_{n,k}$ is a scaled copy of the Cantor set, it is uncountable. Therefore
$$N \cap U \;\supseteq\; C_{n,k}$$
is uncountable.

Since $U$ was an arbitrary neighborhood of the arbitrary point $x$, every point
of $\mathbb{R}$ is a condensation point of $N$.

**Conclusion.** The set $N$ constructed above has Lebesgue measure zero, yet eve
