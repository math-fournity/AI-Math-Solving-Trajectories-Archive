## Proof

Let $s=x+y+z$, $p=xy+yz+zx$, $q=xyz$.

**Step 1: Simplify using symmetric functions.**

The key identity is
$$
(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)=sp-q.
$$
(Expanding both sides confirms this.) Hence the numerator becomes
$$
(x+y)^2(y+z)^2(z+x)^2-64x^2y^2z^2=(sp-q)^2-64q^2=(sp-9q)(sp+7q).
$$
The denominator is $q\bigl(s^3-27q\bigr)$. So
$$
F=\frac{(sp-9q)(sp+7q)}{q\,(s^3-27q)}.
$$

**Step 2: Homogeneity and normalization.**

$F$ is homogeneous of degree $0$ (numerator and denominator both degree $6$), so
 we may set $s=x+y+z=1$. Then
$$
F(p,q)=\frac{(p-9q)(p+7q)}{q\,(1-27q)},\qquad p=xy+yz+zx,\;q=xyz.
$$
The feasible set $\mathcal R=\{(p,q):x,y,z>0,\;x+y+z=1\}$ is a bounded $2$-dimen
sional region. Its boundary consists of:
- $q=0$ (one variable $=0$, i.e. the simplex boundary), and
- the discriminant curve where two variables are equal (where the map $(x,y,z)\m
apsto(p,q)$ drops rank).

**Step 3: No interior critical point.**

By AM–GM, $(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)\ge 8xyz$, i.e. $p-q\ge 8q$, so $p\ge 9q$, with equali
ty iff $x=y=z$ (excluded). Thus throughout the **interior** of $\mathcal R$ we h
ave $p>9q>0$, in particular $p>q$. Also $q<1/27$ (strict, since not all equal),
so $1-27q>0$.

Compute
$$
\frac{\partial F}{\partial p}=\frac{2(p-q)}{q(1-27q)}.
$$
Since $p>q$ and $q>0$ and $1-27q>0$ in the interior, $\partial F/\partial p\neq
0$ everywhere in the interior. Hence $F$ has **no interior critical point**, and
 its minimum over the compact closure is attained on the boundary.

**Step 4: Boundary $q=0$.** As any variable $\to 0$, $q\to 0$ while the numerato

**Step 5: Boundary — two variables equal.** By symmetry it suffices to take $x=y

So
$$
sp-9q=2+5t+2t^2-9t=2(t-1)^2,\qquad sp+7q=2+12t+2t^2=2(t^2+6t+1),

$$
s^3-27q=(2+t)^3-27t=t^3+6t^2-15t+8=(t-1)^2(t+8).
$$
Therefore, for $t\neq 1$,
$$
F(t)=\frac{2(t-1)^2\cdot 2(t^2+6t+1)}{t\cdot(t-1)^2(t+8)}=\frac{4(t^2+6t+1)}{t^2

$$
