## Proof

Recall that a matrix $M \in GL_2(\mathbb{R})$ is **symplectic** (with respect to
 the standard symplectic form encoded by $J$) precisely when it satisfies the de
fining identity

$$
M^{T} J M = J. \tag{1}
$$

Since $M(t)$ is a path of symplectic matrices, (1) holds for every $t \in (-\eps
ilon,\epsilon)$:

$$
M(t)^{T} J\, M(t) = J, \qquad \forall\, t \in (-\epsilon,\epsilon). \tag{2}
$$

Both sides of (2) are smooth matrix-valued functions of $t$ (the path $M$ is smo
oth and matrix multiplication/transposition are smooth operations). Hence we may
 differentiate (2) with respect to $t$. Using the product rule on the left-hand
side (noting that $J$ is constant),

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\Big[M(t)^{T} J\, M(t)\Big] \;=\; M'(t)^{T} J\, M(t) \;+\; M(t)^{T}
J\, M'(t).
$$

The right-hand side of (2) is the constant matrix $J$, whose derivative is the z
ero matrix. Therefore

$$
M'(t)^{T} J\, M(t) \;+\; M(t)^{T} J\, M'(t) \;=\; 0 \qquad \forall\, t \in (-\ep
silon,\epsilon). \tag{3}
$$

Now evaluate (3) at $t = 0$. By hypothesis $M(0) = I$ (the $2\times 2$ identity)
, and $m := M'(0)$. Substituting,

$$
m^{T} J\, I \;+\; I\, J\, m \;=\; 0.
$$

Since $I$ is the identity, $J\, I = J$ and $I\, J = J$, so this simplifies to

$$
m^{T} J \;+\; J\, m \;=\; 0.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\boxed{\,J\,m + m^{T} J = 0\,}.
$$

This is precisely the desired identity. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The identity $Jm + m^{T}J = 0$ says that $m$ lies in the Lie algebra
 $\mathfrak{sp}(2,\mathbb{R})$ of the symplectic group $\mathrm{Sp}(2,\mathbb{R}
)$. The argument above is the standard proof that the Lie algebra of a matrix Li
e group is obtained by differentiating the defining group identity at the identi
ty element.
