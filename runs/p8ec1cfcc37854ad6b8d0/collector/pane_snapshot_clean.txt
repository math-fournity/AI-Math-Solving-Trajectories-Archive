## Solution

**Step 1: Evaluate the inner limit.**

For fixed $n$ and $x$, set $a = \sin(n!\pi x)$ and consider
$$\lim_{t \to 0} \frac{a^2}{a^2 + t^2}.$$

- If $a \neq 0$: the expression $\to \frac{a^2}{a^2} = 1$.
- If $a = 0$: the expression equals $\frac{0}{t^2} = 0$ for every $t \neq 0$, so
 the limit is $0$.

Note $\sin(n!\pi x) = 0 \iff n!\,x \in \mathbb{Z}$. Hence the inner limit is the
 indicator
$$g_n(x) = \begin{cases} 1, & n!\,x \notin \mathbb{Z},\\ 0, & n!\,x \in \mathbb{
Z}. \end{cases}$$

**Step 2: Evaluate the outer limit $f(x) = \lim_{n\to\infty} g_n(x)$.**

*Case 1: $x \in \mathbb{Q}$.* Write $x = p/q$ in lowest terms. For every $n \ge
q$ we have $q \mid n!$, so $n!\,x = n!\,p/q \in \mathbb{Z}$, giving $g_n(x) = 0$
. Thus $f(x) = \lim_{n\to\infty} g_n(x) = 0$.

*Case 2: $x \notin \mathbb{Q}$.* Then $n!\,x \notin \mathbb{Z}$ for every $n$ (a
n integer multiple of a nonzero integer is rational, but $x$ is irrational), so
$g_n(x) = 1$ for all $n$. Thus $f(x) = 1$.

Therefore
$$f(x) = \begin{cases} 0, & x \in \mathbb{Q},\\ 1, & x \notin \mathbb{Q}. \end{c
ases}$$

This is the Dirichlet function (the characteristic function of the irrationals).

**Step 3: Determine the discontinuities.**

Both $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}$ are dense in $\mathbb{R}$.
 At any point $x_0 \in \mathbb{R}$ and for any $\delta > 0$, the interval $(x_0-
\delta, x_0+\delta)$ contains:
- a rational $r$ with $f(r) = 0$, and
- an irrational $s$ with $f(s) = 1$.

So for any candidate limit value $L$, taking $\varepsilon = \tfrac{1}{2}$ shows
no limit can exist: values of $f$ near $x_0$ are simultaneously within $\varepsi
lon$ of $0$ and within $\varepsilon$ of $1$, which are incompatible. Hence $f$ i
s discontinuous at every point of $\mathbb{R}$.

**Conclusion.** The set of discontinuities of $f$ is the entire real line:
$$\boxed{\mathbb{R}}.$$
