## Solution

We work in $A_5$, which has order $60$. We pick two elements $a,b\in A_5$ indepe
ndently and uniformly at random (ordered pair, with replacement) and ask for the
 probability that $\langle a,b\rangle$ is cyclic.

### Element structure of $A_5$

The conjugacy classes and element orders of $A_5$ are:

| Type | Order | Count |
|------|-------|-------|
| identity | 1 | 1 |
| double transpositions $(ij)(kl)$ | 2 | 15 |
| 3-cycles | 3 | 20 |
| 5-cycles | 5 | 24 |

Check: $1+15+20+24=60$. Since $A_5$ is simple, the only element orders are $1,2,
3,5$; in particular there are **no** elements of order $4,6,10,12,15,20,30,60$.
Hence every cyclic subgroup of $A_5$ has order $1$, $2$, $3$, or $5$.

### Cyclic subgroups of $A_5$

- **Order 1:** the trivial subgroup $\{e\}$ — $1$ subgroup.
- **Order 2:** each of the $15$ involutions generates a distinct subgroup $\{e,x
\}$ — $15$ subgroups.
- **Order 3:** each subgroup of order $3$ contains $2$ of the $20$ three-cycles,
 giving $20/2=10$ subgroups.
- **Order 5:** each subgroup of order $5$ contains $4$ of the $24$ five-cycles,
giving $24/4=6$ subgroups.

### Counting pairs that generate a cyclic group

A pair $(a,b)$ generates a cyclic group iff $\langle a,b\rangle$ equals some cyc
lic subgroup $C$. Distinct cyclic subgroups give disjoint sets of generating pai
rs (since $\langle a,b\rangle$ is uniquely determined), so we may sum over cycli
c subgroups $C$ the number of pairs $(a,b)\in C^2$ with $\langle a,b\rangle=C$.

For a cyclic group $C$ of order $n$ (with $n\in\{2,3,5\}$ prime, or $n=1$), the
proper subgroups are only the trivial one (since $2,3,5$ are prime). So $\langle
 a,b\rangle=C$ iff $(a,b)\neq(e,e)$, giving $n^2-1$ generating pairs.

- Order 1: $1$ pair: $(e,e)$. Total: $1\cdot 1 = 1$.
- Order 2: $2^2-1=3$ pairs each. Total: $15\cdot 3 = 45$.
- Order 3: $3^2-1=8$ pairs each. Total: $10\cdot 8 = 80$.
- Order 5: $5^2-1=24$ pairs each. Total: $6\cdot 24 = 144$.

Grand total of pairs generating a cyclic subgroup:
$$1+45+80+144 = 270.$$

### The probability

The total number of ordered pairs is $60^2=3600$, so
$$\Pr\bigl(\langle a,b\rangle\text{ is cyclic}\bigr)=\frac{270}{3600}=\frac{27}{
360}=\boxed{\dfrac{3}{40}}.$$

### Verification via commuting pairs

As a cross-check: $\langle a,b\rangle$ cyclic $\Rightarrow$ $a,b$ commute, and t
he only non-cyclic abelian subgroups of $A_5$ are the Klein four-groups $V_4$. T
here are $5$ such $V_4$'s (one for each choice of $4$ of the $5$ points), each c
ontributing $6$ commuting pairs that generate it (the $6$ ordered pairs of disti
nct non-identity elements), i.e. $5\cdot 6=30$ pairs. The number of commuting pa
irs in $A_5$ equals $|A_5|\cdot k(A_5)=60\cdot 5=300$ (five conjugacy classes).
Indeed $270+30=300$, confirming the count.
