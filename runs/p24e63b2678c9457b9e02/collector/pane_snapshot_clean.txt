## Proof

**Setup and notation.** Let $s = \frac{a+b+c}{2}$ be the semi-perimeter, where $
AB = a$, $BC = b$, $CA = c$. Note that side $AB = a$ is opposite vertex $C$. Let
 $r$ be the inradius.

**Step 1: $CPIQ$ is cyclic with diameter $CI$.**

Since $P$ is the projection of $C$ onto line $AI$, we have $\angle CPI = 90°$. S
ince $Q$ is the projection of $C$ onto line $BI$, we have $\angle CQI = 90°$. Th
us $P$ and $Q$ both lie on the circle with diameter $CI$ (the locus of points su
btending a right angle over $CI$). So $CPIQ$ is a cyclic quadrilateral inscribed
 in a circle with diameter $CI$ and circumradius $R = \frac{CI}{2}$.

**Step 2: Compute $\angle PCQ$.**

Since $CP \perp AI$ and $CQ \perp BI$, the angle $\angle PCQ$ is the supplement
of the angle between lines $AI$ and $BI$:

$$\angle PCQ = 180° - \angle AIB.$$

Using the well-known incenter angle formula $\angle AIB = 90° + \frac{C}{2}$:

$$\angle PCQ = 180° - \left(90° + \frac{C}{2}\right) = 90° - \frac{C}{2}.$$

**Step 3: Compute $PQ$ via the circumcircle.**

Since $PQ$ is a chord of the circumcircle (with diameter $CI$), by the inscribed
 angle theorem:

$$PQ = 2R \sin(\angle PCQ) = CI \sin\!\left(90° - \frac{C}{2}\right) = CI \cos\f
rac{C}{2}.$$

**Step 4: Express $CI$ in terms of $r$ and $C$.**

In the right triangle formed by $C$, $I$, and the foot of the perpendicular from
 $I$ to side $BC$, the angle at $C$ is $\frac{C}{2}$ (since $CI$ bisects $\angle
 C$) and the opposite side is $r$:

$$\sin\frac{C}{2} = \frac{r}{CI} \implies CI = \frac{r}{\sin(C/2)}.$$

**Step 5: Express $r$ using the tangent length from $C$.**

The tangent length from vertex $C$ to the incircle equals $s - a$ (where $a = AB
$ is the side opposite $C$). In the right triangle formed by $C$, the point of t
angency on $BC$, and $I$:

$$\tan\frac{C}{2} = \frac{r}{s - a} \implies r = (s - a)\tan\frac{C}{2}.$$

**Step 6: Combine.**

$$PQ = CI \cos\frac{C}{2} = \frac{r}{\sin(C/2)} \cdot \cos\frac{C}{2} = r \cot\f
rac{C}{2} = (s - a)\tan\frac{C}{2} \cdot \cot\frac{C}{2} = s - a.$$

Substituting $s = \frac{a+b+c}{2}$:

$$\boxed{PQ = \frac{b + c - a}{2}}.$$
