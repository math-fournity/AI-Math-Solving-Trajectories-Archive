# Green's Function for the General Linear ODE of Order *n*

## Setup and normalization

Consider
$$\mathcal{L}y(x) \;=\; a_n(x)\,y^{(n)}(x)+a_{n-1}(x)\,y^{(n-1)}(x)+\cdots+a_1(x
)\,y'(x)+a_0(x)\,y(x) \;=\; f(x),\qquad a\le x\le b,$$
with $a_n(x)\neq 0$ on $[a,b]$, subject to $n$ homogeneous linear boundary condi
tions $B_j[y]=0,\;j=1,\dots,n$ (each $B_j$ a linear functional acting on the $x$
-variable, e.g. evaluations of $y$ and its derivatives at $a$ or $b$, or linear
combinations thereof).

The **Green's function** $G(x,z)$ is defined as the solution of
$$\mathcal{L}_x\,G(x,z)\;=\;\delta(x-z),\qquad B_j\bigl[G(\cdot,z)\bigr]=0\;(j=1
,\dots,n),$$
so that the solution of the original problem is recovered by superposition:
$$\boxed{\;y(x)=\int_a^b G(x,z)\,f(z)\,dz.\;}$$

---

## Fundamental solution set

Let $\{y_1,\dots,y_n\}$ be a fundamental system of the homogeneous equation $\ma
thcal{L}y=0$, with Wronskian
$$W(z)=W(y_1,\dots,y_n)(z)=\det\!\bigl[y_k^{(m-1)}(z)\bigr]_{m,k=1}^{n}\neq 0.$$
By Abel's identity, $W(z)=W(a)\exp\!\bigl(-\int_a^z a_{n-1}/a_n\bigr)$, so $W$ n
ever vanishes on $[a,b]$.

For $x\neq z$, $G$ satisfies the homogeneous equation, hence it must be a linear
 combination of the $y_k$ separately on each side of $z$:
$$G(x,z)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n} c_k(z)\,y_k(x), & a\le x\le z,
\\[6pt] \displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n} d_k(z)\,y_k(x), & z\le x\le b.\end{cases}$$

The $2n$ unknown coefficient functions $\{c_k(z),d_k(z)\}$ are fixed by $2n$ con
ditions: $n$ boundary conditions plus $n$ matching/jump conditions at $x=z$.

---

## Matching and jump conditions at $x=z$

Integrate $\mathcal{L}_x G=\delta(x-z)$ over $(z-\varepsilon,z+\varepsilon)$ and
 let $\varepsilon\to 0$. Every term with a derivative of order $\le n-1$ integra
tes to zero (it is bounded), and only the leading term survives:
$$a_n(z)\Bigl[G^{(n-1)}(z^+,z)-G^{(n-1)}(z^-,z)\Bigr]=1.$$
For $G$ to be a distributional solution with $\delta$ (and no $\delta',\dots,\de
lta^{(n-1)}$) on the right, $G$ and its derivatives up to order $n-2$ must be **
continuous** at $x=z$. Thus the $n$ conditions are:

$$\boxed{\;G^{(m)}(z^+,z)-G^{(m)}(z^-,z)=0,\quad m=0,1,\dots,n-2,\qquad G^{(n-1)
}(z^+,z)-G^{(n-1)}(z^-,z)=\frac{1}{a_n(z)}.\;}$$

Writing $e_k(z):=d_k(z)-c_k(z)$, these become the linear system
$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} e_k(z)\,y_k^{(m)}(z)=\frac{\delta_{m,n-1}}{a_n(z)},\qquad m=0,\
dots,n-1.$$

The coefficient matrix is the Wronskian matrix with determinant $W(z)\neq 0$. By
 **Cramer's rule**:
$$\boxed{\;e_k(z)=d_k(z)-c_k(z)=\frac{1}{a_n(z)\,W(z)}\,C_{n-1,k}(z),\;}$$
where $C_{n-1,k}(z)=(-1)^{n-1+k}M_{n-1,k}(z)$ is the cofactor of the entry $y_k^
{(n-1)}(z)$ in the Wronskian matrix (equivalently, $(-1)^{n-k}$ times the $(n-1)
\times(n-1)$ minor obtained by deleting row $n$ and column $k$). The vector $(e_
1,\dots,e_n)$ is therefore the **unique** vector satisfying
$$\sum_k e_k\,y_k^{(m)}(z)=\delta_{m,n-1}/a_n(z),$$
i.e. it is the row $(0,\dots,0,1/a_n)$ of the inverse Wronskian matrix.

---

## Imposing the boundary conditions

It remains to determine $c_k(z)$ (then $d_k=c_k+e_k$). The $n$ boundary conditio
ns $B_j[G(\cdot,z)]=0$ are linear in the $c_k$ and $d_k$; substituting $d_k=c_k+
e_k$ yields a linear system
$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} A_{jk}(z)\,c_k(z)=r_j(z),\qquad j=1,\dots,n,$$
where $A_{jk}(z)=B_j[\tilde y_k]$ with $\tilde y_k(x)=y_k(x)\mathbf{1}_{x\le z}+
(y_k(x)+\text{correction})\mathbf{1}_{x>z}$ encodes how each boundary functional
 acts on the two pieces, and $r_j(z)=-\sum_k e_k(z)\,B_j[y_k\cdot\mathbf{1}_{x>z
}]$ is the known inhomogeneity from the jump. Provided the **uniqueness conditio
n**
$$\Delta:=\det\bigl[B_j[y_k]\bigr]_{j,k=1}^{n}\neq 0$$
holds (i.e. the homogeneous boundary-value problem has only the trivial solution
), this system is non-singular and gives, again by Cramer's rule,
$$c_k(z)=\frac{\det A^{(k)}(z)}{\det A(z)},\qquad d_k(z)=c_k(z)+e_k(z),$$
with $A^{(k)}$ the matrix $A$ with its $k$-th column replaced by $(r_1,\dots,r_n
)^{\!\top}$.

---

## Closed-form expression

Collecting the pieces, the Green's function is

$$\boxed{\;G(x,z)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n} c_k(z)\,y_k(x), & a\l
e x\le z,\\[8pt]\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Bigl(c_k(z)+\dfrac{C_{n-1,k}(z)}{a_n
(z)\,W(z)}\Bigr)\,y_k(x), & z\le x\le b,\end{cases}\;}$$

where:
- $\{y_1,\dots,y_n\}$ is any fundamental system of $\mathcal{L}y=0$;
- $W(z)=W(y_1,\dots,y_n)(z)$ is their Wronskian;
- $C_{n-1,k}(z)$ is the cofactor of $y_k^{(n-1)}(z)$ in the Wronskian matrix (en
coding the unit jump);
- $c_k(z)$ are determined by the $n$ boundary conditions $B_j[G]=0$ together wit
h the jump relation, via the non-singular system above.

---

## Verification

1. **Homogeneous equation off the diagonal.** For $x<z$ and $x>z$, $G$ is a line
ar combination of $y_k$, each satisfying $\mathcal{L}y_k=0$; hence $\mathcal{L}_
x G=0$ for $x\neq z$. ✓

2. **Distributional identity $\mathcal{L}_x G=\delta(x-z)$.** Since $G,G',\dots,
G^{(n-2)}$ are continuous at $x=z$, their distributional derivatives contain no
$\delta$-terms. The $(n-1)$-st derivative has a jump of $1/a_n(z)$, so $G^{(n)}$
 contains $(1/a_n(z))\,\delta(x-z)$ in the distributional sense, and $a_n(x)G^{(
n)}$ contributes $\delta(x-z)$. All lower-order terms $a_m G^{(m)}$ ($m\le n-1$)
 are locally integrable and contribute no singular part. Thus $\mathcal{L}_x G=\
delta(x-z)$. ✓

3. **Boundary conditions.** By construction $B_j[G(\cdot,z)]=0$ for all $j$ and
all $z\in[a,b]$. ✓

4. **Recovery of the solution.** With $y(x)=\int_a^b G(x,z)f(z)\,dz$, linearity
and the distributional identity give
$$\mathcal{L}y(x)=\int_a^b \mathcal{L}_x G(x,z)\,f(z)\,dz=\int_a^b \delta(x-z)f(
z)\,dz=f(x),$$
and $B_j[y]=\int_a^b B_j[G(\cdot,z)]f(z)\,dz=0$. Hence $y$ solves the original b
oundary-value problem. ✓

5. **Symmetry (self-adjoint case).** When $\mathcal{L}$ is formally self-adjoint
 (i.e. $\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}^*$) and the boundary conditions are self-adjoint
, the construction yields $G(x,z)=G(z,x)$, consistent with the general theory of
 self-adjoint Green's functions. ✓

---

## Summary

The Green's function for the general $n$-th order linear ODE $\mathcal{L}y=f$ on
 $[a,b]$ with homogeneous boundary conditions $B_j[y]=0$ is the unique kernel $G
(x,z)$ that (i) satisfies $\mathcal{L}_x G=0$ for $x\neq z$, (ii) is $C^{n-2}$ a
t $x=z$ with the unit jump $[G^{(n-1)}]_{z^-}^{z^+}=1/a_n(z)$, and (iii) satisfi
es $B_j[G]=0$. It is given explicitly by the two-piece formula above in terms of
 any fundamental system $\{y_k\}$, their Wronskian, and the boundary-condition m
atrix. The solution of the original inhomogeneous problem is then $y(x)=\int_a^b
 G(x,z)\,f(z)\,dz$.
