## Solution

**Answer: No.** It is not necessarily true that $0$ is an eigenvalue of $K$. A c
ompact operator on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space can have a finite spect
rum without $0$ being an eigenvalue.

### Background facts

Two standard facts about compact operators $K$ on an infinite-dimensional Hilber
t space $H$:

1. **$0\in\sigma(K)$ always.** If $0\notin\sigma(K)$, then $K$ is invertible wit
h bounded inverse $K^{-1}$, so $I = K\,K^{-1}$ would be compact (a compact opera
tor composed with a bounded operator is compact). But the identity on an infinit
e-dimensional space is not compact — contradiction.

2. **Every nonzero $\lambda\in\sigma(K)$ is an eigenvalue** (Riesz–Schauder theo
ry): the nonzero spectrum consists entirely of eigenvalues of finite multiplicit
y, with no accumulation point except possibly $0$.

These facts guarantee $0\in\sigma(K)$, but they do **not** guarantee that $0$ is
 an *eigenvalue* (i.e. that $\ker K\neq\{0\}$). The point $0$ may lie in the con
tinuous or residual part of the spectrum. The question is whether finiteness of
$\sigma(K)$ rules this out. It does not.

### Counterexample: the Volterra operator

Let $H = L^{2}[0,1]$ and define the **Volterra operator**
$$
(Vf)(x) = \int_{0}^{x} f(t)\,dt, \qquad f\in L^{2}[0,1].
$$

**$V$ is compact.** It is an integral operator with kernel $k(x,t)=\mathbf{1}_{\
{t\le x\}}\in L^{2}([0,1]^{2})$, hence it is Hilbert–Schmidt, and therefore comp
act.

**$\sigma(V)=\{0\}$, which is finite.** The Volterra operator is *quasinilpotent
*: its spectral radius is
$$
r(V)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|V^{n}\|^{1/n}=0.
$$
Indeed, one checks by induction that $|V^{n}f(x)|\le \dfrac{\|f\|_{\infty}\,x^{n
}}{n!}$, giving $\|V^{n}\|\le \dfrac{1}{n!}$ (on $L^{2}$ a similar bound $\|V^{n
}\|\le \tfrac{1}{(n-1)!}$ holds), so $\|V^{n}\|^{1/n}\to 0$. Since the spectrum
of a bounded operator on a complex Banach space is nonempty and contained in the
 closed disk of radius $r(V)$, we get
$$
\sigma(V)=\{0\}.
$$

**$0$ is not an eigenvalue of $V$.** Suppose $Vf=0$, i.e. $\int_{0}^{x}f(t)\,dt=
0$ for (a.e.) every $x\in[0,1]$. The function $F(x)=\int_{0}^{x}f(t)\,dt$ is abs
olutely continuous with $F'=f$ a.e.; since $F\equiv 0$, we get $f=0$ a.e. Hence
$$
\ker V = \{0\},
$$
so $0\notin\sigma_{p}(V)$ — it is not an eigenvalue. (In fact $0$ belongs to the
 residual spectrum: the range of $V$ is not dense.)

### Conclusion

The Volterra operator $V$ on $L^{2}[0,1]$ is compact, its spectrum $\sigma(V)=\{
0\}$ is finite, yet $0$ is **not** an eigenvalue. Therefore the answer to the qu
estion is **no**: finiteness of $\sigma(K)$ does not force $0$ to be an eigenval
ue of a compact operator on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space.
