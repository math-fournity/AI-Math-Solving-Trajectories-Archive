## Solution

**Substitution.** Let $x = 2n\pi + t$, so $t \in [0, 2\pi]$ and $\cos(2n\pi+t)=\
cos t$. Setting $f(s)=s\ln s$, the integral becomes

$$J_n=\int_{0}^{2\pi} f(2n\pi+t)\cos t\,dt.$$

**First integration by parts.** Take $u=f(2n\pi+t)$, $dv=\cos t\,dt$, so $du=(\l
n(2n\pi+t)+1)\,dt$, $v=\sin t$:

$$J_n=\bigl[f(2n\pi+t)\sin t\bigr]_{0}^{2\pi}-\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bigl(\ln(2n\pi+t)+
1\bigr)\sin t\,dt.$$

The boundary term vanishes since $\sin 0=\sin 2\pi=0$, giving

$$J_n=-\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bigl(\ln(2n\pi+t)+1\bigr)\sin t\,dt.$$

**Second integration by parts.** Let $g(t)=\ln(2n\pi+t)+1$, $dv=\sin t\,dt$, $v=
-\cos t$:

$$\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(t)\sin t\,dt=\bigl[-g(t)\cos t\bigr]_{0}^{2\pi}+\int_{0}^{2\p
i}\frac{\cos t}{2n\pi+t}\,dt.$$

The boundary term evaluates to

$$-\bigl(\ln(2(n+1)\pi)+1\bigr)+\bigl(\ln(2n\pi)+1\bigr)=\ln\frac{n}{n+1}.$$

Hence

$$J_n=-\ln\frac{n}{n+1}-\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\cos t}{2n\pi+t}\,dt=\ln\frac{n+1}{
n}-\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\cos t}{2n\pi+t}\,dt.$$

**Taking the limit.** Clearly $\ln\frac{n+1}{n}\to 0$. For the remaining integra
l,

$$\left|\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\cos t}{2n\pi+t}\,dt\right|\le\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{
dt}{2n\pi+t}=\ln\frac{2(n+1)\pi}{2n\pi}=\ln\frac{n+1}{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]
{}0.$$

Therefore

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}J_n=0-0=0.$$

$$\boxed{\lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} \int_{2n\pi}^{2(n+1)\pi} x\ln(x)\cos x\,\ma
thrm{d}x = 0}$$
