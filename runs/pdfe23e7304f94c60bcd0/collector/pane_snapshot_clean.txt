## Proof

Let $G = \operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q}) = \{\sigma_1 = \mathrm{id}, \sigma_2,
\dots, \sigma_m\}$, where $m = [E:\mathbb{Q}] = |G|$ (the extension is Galois, h
ence normal and separable).

### The construction

For a rational parameter $t \in \mathbb{Q}$, define
$$
\alpha_t \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{n} t^{\,i-1}\, x_i \;=\; x_1 + t\,x_2 + t^2 x_3 + \cd
ots + t^{n-1} x_n \;\in\; E.
$$

We will show that $\alpha_t$ is a primitive element (i.e. $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_t)=
E$) for **all but finitely many** values of $t\in\mathbb{Q}$. Hence any rational
 $t$ avoiding an explicitly computable finite exceptional set yields a primitive
 element $\alpha_t$.

### Identifying the bad values of $t$

For each ordered pair $(j,k)$ with $1\le j<k\le m$, consider
$$
F_{jk}(t) \;:=\; \sigma_j(\alpha_t)-\sigma_k(\alpha_t) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{n} t^{i
-1}\bigl(\sigma_j(x_i)-\sigma_k(x_i)\bigr).
$$

This is a polynomial in $t$ with coefficients in $E$.

**Claim.** $F_{jk}$ is not the zero polynomial.

*Proof of claim.* Since $\sigma_j\neq\sigma_k$ are distinct automorphisms of $E$
, they differ on some element of $E$. Since $\{x_1,\dots,x_n\}$ is a $\mathbb{Q}
$-basis of $E$, every element of $E$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combination of the
 $x_i$; if $\sigma_j$ and $\sigma_k$ agreed on every $x_i$, they would agree on
all of $E$, contradicting $\sigma_j\neq\sigma_k$. Hence $\sigma_j(x_i)\neq\sigma
_k(x_i)$ for at least one $i$, so $F_{jk}$ has a nonzero coefficient and is not
identically zero. $\square$

Being a nonzero polynomial, $F_{jk}$ has at most $\deg F_{jk}\le n-1$ roots in $
E$ (hence in $\mathbb{Q}$).

Define the **bad set**
$$
B \;=\; \bigcup_{1\le j<k\le m}\{\, t\in\mathbb{Q} : F_{jk}(t)=0\,\}.
$$
This is a finite union of finite sets, so $B$ is **finite**.

### Good values give primitive elements

Take any $t\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus B$. Then for all $j\neq k$,
$$
\sigma_j(\alpha_t)\neq\sigma_k(\alpha_t).
$$
So the $m$ elements $\sigma_1(\alpha_t),\dots,\sigma_m(\alpha_t)$ are **pairwise
 distinct**. These are precisely the conjugates of $\alpha_t$ over $\mathbb{Q}$
(since $E/\mathbb{Q}$ is normal, every $\mathbb{Q}$-embedding of $\mathbb{Q}(\al
pha_t)$ into $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ extends to an automorphism of $E$, i.e. to
some $\sigma_j$). Therefore the minimal polynomial of $\alpha_t$ over $\mathbb{Q
}$ has at least $m$ distinct roots, so
$$
[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_t):\mathbb{Q}] \;\ge\; m \;=\; [E:\mathbb{Q}].
$$
On the other hand $\alpha_t\in E$ gives $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_t)\subseteq E$, so $[
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_t):\mathbb{Q}]\le [E:\mathbb{Q}]=m$. Combining,
$$
[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_t):\mathbb{Q}] \;=\; [E:\mathbb{Q}],
$$
and since $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_t)\subseteq E$ with equal finite dimension, we conc
lude
$$
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_t) \;=\; E.
$$

### Constructive procedure (summary)

1. **Compute** the Galois group $G=\{\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_m\}$ of $E/\mathbb{Q}
$ (each $\sigma_j$ is determined by its action on the basis, i.e. by the matrice
s expressing $\sigma_j(x_i)$ in the basis $\{x_i\}$).
2. **Form** the polynomials $F_{jk}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t^{i-1}(\sigma_j(x_i)-\sigm
a_k(x_i))$ for each pair $j<k$.
3. **Collect** all rational roots of all $F_{jk}$ into the finite bad set $B$.
4. **Pick** any $t\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus B$ (e.g. the smallest positive integer
not in $B$, or simply $t$ larger than the maximum absolute value of all rational
 roots found).
5. **Output** $\alpha = \alpha_t = x_1 + t x_2 + t^2 x_3 + \cdots + t^{n-1}x_n$.

By the argument above, $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)=E$, so $\alpha$ is a primitive elemen
t.

### Remark (why separability matters)

The hypothesis that $E/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois is used in two places: (a) separabi
lity guarantees that the minimal polynomial of $\alpha_t$ has distinct roots, so
 counting distinct conjugates genuinely lower-bounds the degree; and (b) normali
ty guarantees that all conjugates of $\alpha_t$ are realized among $\sigma_1(\al
pha_t),\dots,\sigma_m(\alpha_t)$. Over $\mathbb{Q}$ every finite extension is se
parable (characteristic $0$), so in fact the argument works for any finite exten
sion of $\mathbb{Q}$; the Galois hypothesis ensures $|G|=[E:\mathbb{Q}]$ so the
count of conjugates is exactly $[E:\mathbb{Q}]$, making the degree comparison ti
ght.
