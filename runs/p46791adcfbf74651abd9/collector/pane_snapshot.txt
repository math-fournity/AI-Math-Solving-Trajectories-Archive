\alpha\in K$ with
$$K = \mathbb{Q}_p(\alpha).$$

Let $f(x)\in\mathbb{Q}_p[x]$ be the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\mathbb
{Q}_p$. Because $\mathbb{Q}_p$ has characteristic $0$, $f$ is separable. Let $n=
\deg f$, and let
$$\alpha=\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\dots,\alpha_n$$
be the $n$ distinct roots of $f$ in a fixed algebraic closure $\overline{\mathbb
{Q}}_p$.

Define
$$K' := \mathbb{Q}_p(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\dots,\alpha_n),$$
the field obtained by adjoining all roots of $f$ to $\mathbb{Q}_p$. This is prec
isely the splitting field of $f$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$.

We verify the two required properties:

1. **$K'/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is Galois.** A splitting field of a separable polynomial
over a field of characteristic $0$ is a finite Galois extension. Concretely: $K'
/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is finite (generated over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ by finitely many algebra
ic elements), it is normal (it is the splitting field of $f$, so every $\mathbb{
Q}_p$-embedding of $K'$ into $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ permutes the roots $\alph
a_i$ and hence maps $K'$ to itself), and it is separable (characteristic $0$). H
ence $K'/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is Galois.

2. **$K'$ is a finite extension of $K$.** Since $\alpha=\alpha_1\in K'$, we have
 $K=\mathbb{Q}_p(\alpha)\subseteq K'$. Thus $K'$ is an extension of $K$. Being f
inite over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, it is finite over the intermediate field $K$ as well
(by the tower formula $[K':\mathbb{Q}_p]=[K':K]\,[K:\mathbb{Q}_p]$).

Therefore $K'$ is a finite extension of $K$ that is Galois over $\mathbb{Q}_p$.
