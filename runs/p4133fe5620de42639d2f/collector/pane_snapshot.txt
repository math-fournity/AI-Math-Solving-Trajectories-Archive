## Proof

We are given that $g$ is holomorphic on the open unit disk $\mathbb{D} = \{z : |
z| < 1\}$, continuous on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$, with $|g(z)| \geq 1$ for $|z|
\leq 1$, $|g(z)| = 1$ for $|z| = 1$, and $g(-1) = 1$.

**Step 1: $g$ has no zeros in $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$.**

Since $|g(z)| \geq 1$ for all $|z| \leq 1$, in particular $|g(z)| \geq 1 > 0$, s
o $g(z) \neq 0$ throughout the closed disk.

**Step 2: Consider $h = 1/g$.**

Because $g$ is holomorphic on $\mathbb{D}$ and never vanishes there, the functio
n
$$h(z) = \frac{1}{g(z)}$$
is holomorphic on $\mathbb{D}$ and continuous on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$.

**Step 3: Apply the Maximum Modulus Principle to $h$.**

From the hypotheses:
- For $|z| \leq 1$: $|h(z)| = 1/|g(z)| \leq 1$.
- For $|z| = 1$: $|h(z)| = 1/|g(z)| = 1$.

Thus $h$ attains its maximum modulus $1$ on the boundary $|z| = 1$. In particula
r, $|h(-1)| = 1/|g(-1)| = 1/1 = 1$, so $h$ attains its maximum modulus at the in
terior point $z = -1$.

By the **Maximum Modulus Principle**, a holomorphic function that attains its ma
ximum modulus at an interior point must be constant. Therefore $h$ is constant o
n $\mathbb{D}$, and by continuity, constant on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$.

**Step 4: Determine the constant.**

Since $h(-1) = 1/g(-1) = 1$, we have $h \equiv 1$, hence
$$g(z) = \frac{1}{h(z)} = 1 \quad \text{for all } z \in \overline{\mathbb{D}}.$$

**Conclusion.** The unique function satisfying all the given conditions is the c
onstant function

$$\boxed{g(z) \equiv 1.}$$
