## Proof

**Claim.** The one-point compactification of $S^n \times \mathbb{R}$ is $S^{n+1}
$ with two distinct points identified, i.e., the quotient space $S^{n+1}/\{N \si
m S\}$.

---

**Step 1: $S^n \times \mathbb{R} \cong S^{n+1} \setminus \{N, S\}$.**

Recall that $S^{n+1}$ is homeomorphic to the (unreduced) suspension of $S^n$:
$$S^{n+1} \cong \Sigma S^n = \bigl(S^n \times [-1,1]\bigr)\big/\bigl(S^n \times
\{-1\} \sim \text{pt},\; S^n \times \{1\} \sim \text{pt}\bigr).$$
Under this identification, the two suspension (cone) points correspond to the no
rth pole $N$ and south pole $S$ of $S^{n+1}$. Removing them yields
$$S^{n+1} \setminus \{N, S\} \cong S^n \times (-1,1) \cong S^n \times \mathbb{R}
,$$
where the last homeomorphism uses $(-1,1) \cong \mathbb{R}$. Hence $S^n \times \
mathbb{R} \cong S^{n+1}\setminus\{N,S\}$.

---

**Step 2: The one-point compactification of $S^{n+1}\setminus\{N,S\}$ is $S^{n+1
}/\{N\sim S\}$.**

Set $X = S^{n+1}\setminus\{N,S\}$ and let $q: S^{n+1} \to Y := S^{n+1}/\{N\sim S
\}$ be the quotient map, with $p := q(N) = q(S)$ the identified point. We verify
 that $Y$ satisfies the defining properties of the one-point compactification of
 $X$:

- **$Y \setminus \{p\} \cong X$:** The map $q$ is injective on $X$ (since only $
N$ and $S$ are identified). Since $X$ is an open subspace of $S^{n+1}$ and $q$ i
s a quotient map, the restriction $q|_X : X \to q(X) = Y \setminus \{p\}$ is a h
omeomorphism.

- **$Y$ is compact:** $Y$ is the continuous image of the compact space $S^{n+1}$
.

- **$Y$ is Hausdorff:** $S^{n+1}$ is compact Hausdorff, and we are identifying t
he closed subset $\{N, S\}$ to a single point. The quotient of a compact Hausdor
ff space by collapsing a closed set to a point is Hausdorff.

- **$X$ is dense in $Y$:** Every open neighborhood of $p$ in $Y$ is the image un
der $q$ of a saturated open set $U \subseteq S^{n+1}$ containing both $N$ and $S
$. Since $U$ is open and contains $N$ (and $S$), it must contain points of $X =
S^{n+1}\setminus\{N,S\}$. Hence every neighborhood of $p$ meets $X$, so $\overli
ne{X} = Y$.

- **$Y \setminus X = \{p\}$ is a single point:** Immediate from the construction
.

By the uniqueness of the one-point compactification of a locally compact Hausdor
ff space, $Y$ is the one-point compactification of $X$.

---

**Conclusion.** Since $S^n \times \mathbb{R} \cong S^{n+1}\setminus\{N,S\}$ and
the one-point compactification of $S^{n+1}\setminus\{N,S\}$ is $S^{n+1}/\{N\sim
S\}$, the one-point compactification of $S^n \times \mathbb{R}$ is

$$\boxed{S^{n+1}\big/\{N \sim S\}},$$

i.e., **$S^{n+1}$ with two distinct points identified**.

*Remark.* For $n=0$ this gives $S^1$ with two points identified, which is the fi
gure-eight $S^1 \vee S^1$—consistent with the one-point compactification of $S^0
 \times \mathbb{R}$ (two copies of $\mathbb{R}$ sharing a single point at infini
ty).
