## Proof

Let \(W(t) \) be a standard Brownian motion with \(W(0)=0\), and let
\[
\tau(a) = \inf\{ t \ge 0 : W(t) \ge a\}.
\]

We treat the case \(a > 0\) (the case \(a=0\) gives \(\tau(0)=0\) trivially, and
 \(a<0\) reduces to \(a>0\) by symmetry \(W \mapsto -W\)). The claim is
\[
\boxed{E[\tau(a)] = +\infty.}
\]

The proof has two parts: (1) derive the Laplace transform of \(\tau(a)\) rigorou
sly using the exponential martingale and optional stopping, and (2) read off the
 mean from the Laplace transform and show it diverges.

---

### Step 1. The exponential martingale and optional stopping

For any \(\lambda \in \mathbb{R}\), the process
\[
M(t) = \exp\!\bigl(\lambda W(t) - \tfrac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\,t\bigr)
\]
is a martingale with \(E[M(t)] = 1\) for all \(t \ge 0\). (This follows from the
 independence and \(N(0,t)\) distribution of increments: \(E[\exp(\lambda W(t))]
 = \exp(\lambda^{2}t/2)\).)

The stopping time \(\tau = \tau(a)\) is almost surely finite: standard Brownian
motion is recurrent, so it visits every level \(a\) with probability \(1\). Thus
 \(\tau \wedge t \to \tau\) and \(W(\tau \wedge t) \to W(\tau) = a\) almost sure
ly as \(t \to \infty\).

Since \(\tau \wedge t\) is a bounded stopping time, the optional stopping theore
m gives, for every \(t \ge 0\),
\[
E\!\Bigl[\exp\!\bigl(\lambda W(\tau \wedge t) - \tfrac{\lambda^{2}}{2}(\tau \wed
ge t)\bigr)\Bigr] = 1. \tag{1}
\]

---

### Step 2. Passing to the limit \(t \to \infty\) (take \(\lambda > 0\))

Fix \(\lambda > 0\). We show the integrand in (1) is dominated by an integrable
constant, allowing dominated convergence.

- **On the event \(\{\tau \le t\}\):** we have \(\tau \wedge t = \tau\) and \(W(
\tau \wedge t) = a\), so the integrand equals \(\exp(\lambda a - \lambda^{2}\tau
/2) \le \exp(\lambda a)\) since \(\lambda^{2}\tau/2 \ge 0\).

- **On the event \(\{\tau > t\}\):** the path has not yet reached level \(a\), s
o \(W(t) < a\) (the running maximum is below \(a\)). Hence \(\lambda W(t) < \lam
bda a\), and
  \[
  \exp\!\bigl(\lambda W(t) - \tfrac{\lambda^{2}}{2}t\bigr) < \exp\!\bigl(\lambda
 a - \tfrac{\lambda^{2}}{2}t\bigr) \le \exp(\lambda a).
  \]

Combining both cases, the integrand in (1) is bounded by the constant \(\exp(\la
mbda a) < \infty\) for all \(t\). By dominated convergence, taking \(t \to \inft
y\) in (1):
\[
E\!\Bigl[\exp\!\bigl(\lambda a - \tfrac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\tau\bigr)\Bigr] = 1,
\]
which rearranges to the **Laplace transform of \(\tau\)**:
\[
E\!\bigl[e^{-s\tau}\bigr] = e^{-a\sqrt{2s}}, \qquad s = \tfrac{\lambda^{2}}{2} >
 0. \tag{2}
\]

---

### Step 3. The mean diverges

For a nonnegative random variable \(X\), the identity
\[
E[X] = \lim_{s \downarrow 0} \frac{1 - E[e^{-sX}]}{s} \in [0, +\infty]
\]
holds (monotone convergence applied to \((1-e^{-sX})/s \uparrow X\)). Applying t
his to \(X = \tau\) and using (2):
\[
E[\tau] = \lim_{s \downarrow 0} \frac{1 - e^{-a\sqrt{2s}}}{s}.
\]

As \(s \downarrow 0\), we have \(a\sqrt{2s} \downarrow 0\), and using \(1 - e^{-
x} \sim x\) as \(x \to 0\),
\[
\frac{1 - e^{-a\sqrt{2s}}}{s} \;\sim\; \frac{a\sqrt{2s}}{s} \;=\; \frac{a\sqrt{2
}}{\sqrt{s}} \;\xrightarrow{s \downarrow 0}\; +\infty.
\]

Therefore
\[
E[\tau(a)] = +\infty. \tag{3}
\]

---

### Step 4. Consistency check via the quadratic martingale

As an independent cross-check, \(W(t)^{2} - t\) is a martingale. Optional stoppi
ng at \(\tau \wedge n\) (bounded) gives
\[
E[\tau \wedge n] = E\!\bigl[W(\tau \wedge n)^{2}\bigr].
\]
By monotone convergence, \(E[\tau \wedge n] \uparrow E[\tau]\). On \(\{\tau \le
n\}\), \(W(\tau \wedge n)^{2} = a^{2}\); on \(\{\tau > n\}\), the running maximu
m is below \(a\) but \(W(n)^{2}\) still contributes. A direct lower bound: since
 \(W(\tau \wedge n)^{2} \ge a^{2}\,\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau \le n\}}\) and \(P(\tau \l
e n) \to 1\),
\[
E[\tau \wedge n] \ge a^{2}\,P(\tau \le n) \to a^{2},
\]
which only yields \(E[\tau] \ge a^{2}\) — a finite lower bound, consistent with
but weaker than (3). The Laplace-transform argument above is the one that pins d
own the value \(+\infty\) exactly.

---

### Conclusion

The first hitting time \(\tau(a)\) of level \(a > 0\) by standard Brownian motio
n is almost surely finite, yet its expectation is infinite:

\[
\boxed{E[\tau(a)] = +\infty \quad (a > 0).}
\]

For \(a = 0\), \(\tau(0) = 0\) so \(E[\tau(0)] = 0\); for \(a < 0\), by the symm
etry \(W \mapsto -W\) the result matches the \(|a| > 0\) case, so \(E[\tau(a)] =
 +\infty\) as well.
