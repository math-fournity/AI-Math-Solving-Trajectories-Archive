## Solution

**Contour analysis.** The contour is $\gamma(t) = t + i\sqrt{\pi^2 - t^2}$ for $
-\pi \le t \le \pi$. Writing $z = x + iy$, we have $x = t$ and $y = \sqrt{\pi^2
- t^2}$, so
$$x^2 + y^2 = t^2 + (\pi^2 - t^2) = \pi^2.$$
Thus $C$ is the upper semicircle of radius $\pi$ (since $y \ge 0$), traversed fr
om $\gamma(-\pi) = -\pi$ to $\gamma(\pi) = \pi$, passing through $\gamma(0) = i\
pi$.

**Analyticity.** The integrand $f(z) = \cos(z/2)$ is entire — it is analytic on
all of $\mathbb{C}$. Therefore, by the fundamental theorem of contour integratio
n, the integral depends only on the endpoints of the contour, and we may use any
 antiderivative.

**Antiderivative.** An antiderivative of $\cos(z/2)$ is $F(z) = 2\sin(z/2)$, sin
ce $F'(z) = 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}\cos(z/2) = \cos(z/2)$.

**Evaluation.**
$$\int_C \cos\!\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)\,dz = F(\pi) - F(-\pi) = 2\sin\!\left(\f
rac{\pi}{2}\right) - 2\sin\!\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}\right) = 2(1) - 2(-1) = 2 + 2 =
 4.$$

$$\boxed{\int_C \cos\!\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)\,dz = 4}$$
