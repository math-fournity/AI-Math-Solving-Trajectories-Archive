Theorem. Let $U$ and $V$ be vector spaces (over the same field) with $\dim U =
\dim V = n < \infty$, and let $h: U \to V$ be a linear map. Then $h$ is
injective if and only if $h$ is surjective.

Proof.

We use the rank–nullity theorem throughout. For a linear map $h: U \to V$,

$$\dim U = \dim \ker h + \dim \operatorname{im} h.$$

Since $\dim U = n$, this gives

$$\dim \operatorname{im} h = n - \dim \ker h. \tag{$\star$}$$

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

($\Rightarrow$) Injective $\Longrightarrow$ surjective.

Suppose $h$ is injective. Then $\ker h = {0}$, so $\dim \ker h = 0$. By
$(\star)$,

$$\dim \operatorname{im} h = n - 0 = n.$$

Since $\operatorname{im} h \subseteq V$ and $\dim V = n = \dim \operatorname{im}
h$, the image is a subspace of $V$ of the same (finite) dimension as $V$.
Therefore $\operatorname{im} h = V$, i.e., $h$ is surjective.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

($\Leftarrow$) Surjective $\Longrightarrow$ injective.

Suppose $h$ is surjective. Then $\operatorname{im} h = V$, so $\dim
\operatorname{im} h = \dim V = n$. By $(\star)$,

$$n = n - \dim \ker h \implies \dim \ker h = 0.$$

Hence $\ker h = {0}$, which is precisely the condition that $h$ is injective.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Remark on the role of equal dimension. The equality $\dim U = \dim V$ is
essential in both directions:

  • For ($\Rightarrow$): injectivity gives $\dim \operatorname{im} h = \dim U =
  n$; to conclude $\operatorname{im} h = V$ we need $\dim V = n$ as well, so
  that the image (a subspace of $V$) has full dimension in $V$.
  • For ($\Leftarrow$): surjectivity gives $\dim \operatorname{im} h = \dim V =
  n$; rank–nullity then yields $\dim \ker h = \dim U - n$, which equals $0$ only
  when $\dim U = n$.

Thus both implications rely on $\dim U = \dim V = n$, completing the
equivalence. $\blacksquare$
