## Solution

**Step 1: Substitution to simplify the integral.**

Let $x = t/n$, so $dx = dt/n$. When $x = 0$, $t = 0$; when $x = 1/n$, $t = 1$.

$$\int_0^{1/n} x^{x+k-1}\,dx = \int_0^1 \left(\frac{t}{n}\right)^{t/n+k-1}\frac{
dt}{n} = \int_0^1 \frac{t^{t/n+k-1}}{n^{t/n+k}}\,dt$$

**Step 2: Multiply by $n^k$ and simplify.**

$$n^k\int_0^{1/n} x^{x+k-1}\,dx = \int_0^1 \frac{t^{t/n+k-1}}{n^{t/n}}\,dt = \in
t_0^1 t^{k-1}\cdot\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)^{t/n}dt$$

**Step 3: Take the limit $n\to\infty$ via dominated convergence.**

For fixed $t\in(0,1]$, as $n\to\infty$ we have $t/n\to 0$, so

$$\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)^{t/n} = \exp\!\left(\frac{t}{n}\ln\frac{t}{n}\right)\
to \exp(0)=1$$

since $\frac{t}{n}\ln\frac{t}{n}\to 0$.

**Domination check:** For $n\ge 1$ and $t\in(0,1]$, we have $0 < t/n \le 1$, so
the base $\frac{t}{n}\in(0,1]$ and the exponent $\frac{t}{n}>0$, giving $\left(\
frac{t}{n}\right)^{t/n}\le 1$. Hence

$$t^{k-1}\cdot\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)^{t/n}\le t^{k-1},$$

and $t^{k-1}$ is integrable on $[0,1]$ for $k\in\mathbb{N}$. By the Dominated Co
nvergence Theorem,

$$f(k) = \lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1 t^{k-1}\cdot\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)^{t/n}dt
= \int_0^1 t^{k-1}\cdot 1\,dt = \frac{1}{k}.$$

**Step 4: Evaluate at $k=5$.**

$$f(5) = \frac{1}{5},\qquad \frac{1}{f(5)} = 5,\qquad \left[\frac{1}{f(5)}\right
] = \lceil 5\rceil = 5.$$
