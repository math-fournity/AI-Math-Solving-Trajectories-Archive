## Proof

$Y(t)$ is a Brownian motion with variance parameter $\sigma^2$, so $Y(t) \sim N(
0, \sigma^2 t)$ and increments are independent with $Y(t)-Y(s) \sim N(0, \sigma^
2(t-s))$ for $s < t$.

The inside racer wins iff $Y(1) > 0$.

**Condition on the midpoint.** By the independent increments property, $Y(1) - Y
(1/2)$ is independent of $Y(1/2)$, and
$$Y(1) - Y(1/2) \sim N\!\left(0,\; \sigma^2\!\left(1 - \tfrac{1}{2}\right)\right
) = N\!\left(0,\; \tfrac{\sigma^2}{2}\right).$$

We are given $Y(1/2) = \sigma$. Therefore
$$Y(1) = Y(1/2) + \bigl(Y(1)-Y(1/2)\bigr) = \sigma + Z, \qquad Z \sim N\!\left(0
,\tfrac{\sigma^2}{2}\right).$$

**Compute the winning probability.**
$$P\bigl(Y(1) > 0\bigr) = P(\sigma + Z > 0) = P\!\left(Z > -\sigma\right) = P\!\
left(\frac{Z}{\sigma/\sqrt{2}} > \frac{-\sigma}{\sigma/\sqrt{2}}\right) = P\!\le
ft(W > -\sqrt{2}\right),$$
where $W \sim N(0,1)$. By symmetry of the standard normal,
$$P(W > -\sqrt{2}) = \Phi(\sqrt{2}).$$

Thus the probability that the inside racer wins is

$$\boxed{\Phi(\sqrt{2})}.$$

Numerically, $\Phi(\sqrt{2}) = \Phi(1.4142\ldots) \approx 0.9214$.
