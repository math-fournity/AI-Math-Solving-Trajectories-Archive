# Proof

**Answer: Yes.** The convolution of two finite complex measures with compact sup
port, both singular with respect to Lebesgue measure, can have a nonzero absolut
ely continuous component. We construct an explicit example.

---

## Construction

Let $(A_n)_{n \geq 1}$ and $(B_n)_{n \geq 1}$ be two independent families of i.i
.d. Bernoulli$(1/2)$ random variables (each taking values $0$ and $1$ with proba
bility $1/2$). Define two probability measures $\mu$ and $\nu$ on $\mathbb{R}$ a
s the distributions of:

$$X = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2A_n}{4^n}, \qquad Y = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac
{B_n}{4^n}.$$

That is, $\mu = \text{Law}(X)$ and $\nu = \text{Law}(Y)$.

---

## Step 1: $\mu$ and $\nu$ have compact support

Since $A_n \in \{0,1\}$ and $B_n \in \{0,1\}$:

$$0 \leq X \leq \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2}{4^n} = \frac{2/4}{1-1/4} = \frac{2}
{3}, \qquad 0 \leq Y \leq \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{4^n} = \frac{1/4}{1-1/4}
= \frac{1}{3}.$$

So $\text{supp}(\mu) \subseteq [0, 2/3]$ and $\text{supp}(\nu) \subseteq [0, 1/3
]$, both compact. $\checkmark$

---

## Step 2: $\mu$ and $\nu$ are singular with respect to Lebesgue measure

**Singularity of $\mu$:** The support of $\mu$ is the self-similar set

$$E_\mu = \left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2a_n}{4^n} : a_n \in \{0,1\}\right\}.
$$

This is a Cantor-type set constructed by starting with $[0, 2/3]$ and, at each s
tage $n$, retaining $2^n$ closed intervals each of length $4^{-n} \cdot 2$ (the
scaling factor is $1/4$ with $2$ copies). The total length at stage $n$ is $2^n
\cdot 2 \cdot 4^{-n} = 2 \cdot (2/4)^n = 2 \cdot 2^{-n} \to 0$.

More directly: $E_\mu$ is the attractor of the IFS $\{f_0(x) = x/4,\; f_1(x) = x
/4 + 1/2\}$ restricted to $[0, 2/3]$. At stage $n$, the approximation consists o
f $2^n$ intervals of length $(1/4)^n \cdot (2/3)$. The total length is $2^n \cdo
t (2/3) \cdot 4^{-n} = (2/3)(1/2)^n \to 0$.

Therefore $E_\mu$ has Lebesgue measure $0$. Since $\mu$ is supported on $E_\mu$,
 we have $\mu \perp \lambda$. $\checkmark$

**Singularity of $\nu$:** Similarly, the support is

$$E_\nu = \left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{b_n}{4^n} : b_n \in \{0,1\}\right\},$
$

which at stage $n$ consists of $2^n$ intervals of length $4^{-n}$, giving total
length $(2/4)^n = 2^{-n} \to 0$. So $E_\nu$ has Lebesgue measure $0$, and $\nu \
perp \lambda$. $\checkmark$

---

## Step 3: $\mu * \nu$ is absolutely continuous and nonzero

The convolution $\mu * \nu$ is the distribution of $Z = X + Y$:

$$Z = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2A_n + B_n}{4^n}.$$

Set $C_n = 2A_n + B_n$. Since $A_n, B_n$ are independent Bernoulli$(1/2)$:

| $C_n$ value | $(A_n, B_n)$ | Probability |
|---|---|---|
| $0$ | $(0,0)$ | $1/4$ |
| $1$ | $(0,1)$ | $1/4$ |
| $2$ | $(1,0)$ | $1/4$ |
| $3$ | $(1,1)$ | $1/4$ |

So $(C_n)_{n \geq 1}$ are i.i.d. **uniform** on $\{0, 1, 2, 3\}$.

**Key observation — no carries:** Since each $C_n \in \{0,1,2,3\}$ and the base
is $4$, every $C_n$ is a valid base-$4$ digit (i.e., $C_n \leq 3 = 4-1$). Theref
ore the representation

$$Z = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{C_n}{4^n}$$

is a genuine base-$4$ expansion with no carrying required. The random variables
$C_n$ are i.i.d. uniform on the full digit set $\{0,1,2,3\}$.

**The pushforward is Lebesgue measure:** The map $\varphi: \{0,1,2,3\}^{\mathbb{
N}} \to [0,1]$ defined by $\varphi((c_n)) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty c_n \cdot 4^{-n}$
is the base-$4$ expansion map. When the input digits are i.i.d. uniform on $\{0,
1,2,3\}$, the pushforward measure is Lebesgue measure on $[0,1]$.

*Proof of this standard fact:* For any base-$4$ cylinder interval $I = [k/4^N, (
k+1)/4^N)$ (where $k \in \{0, 1, \ldots, 4^N - 1\}$), the event $Z \in I$ is det
ermined by the first $N$ digits $(C_1, \ldots, C_N)$, and there is exactly one d
igit sequence mapping to each such cylinder. Since each digit is uniform on $\{0
,1,2,3\}$:

$$P(Z \in I) = \frac{1}{4^N} = \lambda(I).$$

The collection of base-$4$ cylinder intervals generates the Borel $\sigma$-algeb
ra on $[0,1]$, so by the uniqueness theorem for measures (Dynkin's $\pi$-$\lambd
a$ theorem), $\mu * \nu = \lambda|_{[0,1]}$.

(The countable set of numbers with two base-$4$ expansions — those of the form $
k/4^N$ — has Lebesgue measure $0$ and does not affect the equality of measures.)

---

## Conclusion

We have constructed two probability measures $\mu$ and $\nu$ on $\mathbb{R}$ suc
h that:

1. **Both are finite** (probability measures, total mass $1$). $\checkmark$
2. **Both have compact support** ($\text{supp}(\mu) \subseteq [0, 2/3]$, $\text{
supp}(\nu) \subseteq [0, 1/3]$). $\checkmark$
3. **Both are singular with respect to Lebesgue measure** (each is supported on
a Cantor-type set of Lebesgue measure $0$). $\checkmark$
4. **Their convolution $\mu * \nu = \lambda|_{[0,1]}$ is purely absolutely conti
nuous and nonzero.** $\checkmark$

The essential mechanism is that $\mu$ uses base-$4$ digits $\{0, 2\}$ and $\nu$
uses base-$4$ digits $\{0, 1\}$; their sum yields digits $\{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ — the
**full** digit set — uniformly distributed, with no carries (since $3 < 4$). Eac
h measure individually lives on a null set (too few digits), but together they f
ill the entire interval.

Therefore, **the convolution of two finite complex measures with compact support
, both singular with respect to Lebesgue measure, can indeed have a nonzero abso
lutely continuous component.**
