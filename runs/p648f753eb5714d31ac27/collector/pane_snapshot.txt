Looking at this problem, I need to analyze the developing map construction for $
(G,X)$-manifolds and determine whether it locally agrees with charts composed wi
th the covering map.

## Answer

**Yes.** The developing map locally commutes with the (restricted) covering and
local charts: for every point $\widetilde{x}\in\widetilde{M}$, there exists a su
fficiently small neighborhood $\widetilde{U}\ni\widetilde{x}$ and a chart $(U,\v
arphi_U)$ of the $(G,X)$-structure on $M$ such that
$$dev\big|_{\widetilde{U}} \;=\; \varphi_U\circ\,\mathfrak{p}\big|_{\widetilde{U
}}.$$

### Justification

**Construction of the developing map.** Choose a base point $\widetilde{x}_0\in\
widetilde{M}$ and a chart $(U_0,\varphi_0)$ of $M$ with $\mathfrak{p}(\widetilde
{x}_0)\in U_0$. Let $\widetilde{U}_0$ be the component of $\mathfrak{p}^{-1}(U_0
)$ containing $\widetilde{x}_0$. Define $dev$ on $\widetilde{U}_0$ by
$$dev\big|_{\widetilde{U}_0} = \varphi_0\circ\,\mathfrak{p}\big|_{\widetilde{U}_
0}.$$

To extend $dev$ to all of $\widetilde{M}$, we analytically continue along paths.
 Let $\gamma:[0,1]\to\widetilde{M}$ be a path starting at $\widetilde{x}_0$. Cov
er $\gamma$ by finitely many lifted chart domains $\widetilde{U}_0,\widetilde{U}
_1,\dots,\widetilde{U}_n$, where each $\widetilde{U}_i$ is a component of $\math
frak{p}^{-1}(U_i)$ for some chart $(U_i,\varphi_i)$ of $M$, with consecutive dom
ains overlapping. On each overlap $\widetilde{U}_i\cap\widetilde{U}_{i+1}$, the
transition map $\varphi_{i+1}\circ\varphi_i^{-1}$ is the restriction of some $g_
i\in G$.

**Inductive continuation.** On $\widetilde{U}_0$, $dev=\varphi_0\circ\mathfrak{p
}$. On the overlap $\widetilde{U}_0\cap\widetilde{U}_1$, we have $\varphi_0 = g_
0\circ\varphi_1$ (where $g_0=\varphi_0\circ\varphi_1^{-1}\in G$), so $dev = g_0\
circ\varphi_1\circ\mathfrak{p}$. We extend $dev$ to $\widetilde{U}_1$ by this fo
rmula. Continuing inductively, on $\widetilde{U}_k$:
$$dev\big|_{\widetilde{U}_k} = h_k\circ\varphi_k\circ\,\mathfrak{p}\big|_{\widet
ilde{U}_k}, \qquad h_k := g_0\circ g_1\circ\cdots\circ g_{k-1}\in G.$$

**Well-definedness.** Since $\widetilde{M}$ is simply connected, any two paths f
rom $\widetilde{x}_0$ to a point $\widetilde{x}$ are homotopic (relative endpoin
ts). Analytic continuation along homotopic paths yields the same germ: this foll
ows from the monodromy argument for $(G,X)$-structures—$G$ acts by diffeomorphis
ms of $X$, the local continuation is unique on each chart domain, and homotopic
paths can be deformed through a sequence of chart domains producing identical co
mpositions of transition elements. Hence $dev:\widetilde{M}\to X$ is a well-defi
ned local diffeomorphism.

**Local commutation.** On each lifted chart domain $\widetilde{U}_k$, the develo
ping map takes the form $dev|_{\widetilde{U}_k}=h_k\circ\varphi_k\circ\mathfrak{
p}|_{\widetilde{U}_k}$ with $h_k\in G$. Now observe that $h_k\circ\varphi_k$ is
itself a chart of the $(G,X)$-structure on $M$: for any other chart $\varphi_j$,
 the transition map is
$$(h_k\circ\varphi_k)\circ\varphi_j^{-1} = h_k\circ(\varphi_k\circ\varphi_j^{-1}
)\in G,$$
since $G$ is a group and $\varphi_k\circ\varphi_j^{-1}\in G$. Thus $h_k\circ\var
phi_k$ belongs to the maximal $(G,X)$-atlas.

Setting $\varphi_{U_k}:=h_k\circ\varphi_k$, we obtain
$$dev\big|_{\widetilde{U}_k} = \varphi_{U_k}\circ\,\mathfrak{p}\big|_{\widetilde
{U}_k}$$
for a chart $\varphi_{U_k}$ of the $(G,X)$-structure. Since every point of $\wid
etilde{M}$ lies in some lifted chart domain, the desired local commutation holds
 at every point. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The chart $\varphi_{U_k}=h_k\circ\varphi_k$ is generally a $G$-trans
late of one of the original charts, not necessarily an original chart itself. Th
e element $h_k$ is the **holonomy** along the path from $\widetilde{x}_0$ to $\w
idetilde{U}_k$. When the holonomy is nontrivial, $dev$ does not agree with the *
original* chart $\varphi_k\circ\mathfrak{p}$, but it does agree with the modifie
d chart $(h_k\circ\varphi_k)\circ\mathfrak{p}$, which is a valid chart of the $(
G,X)$-structure. This is precisely why the answer to "for **some** $\varphi_U$"
is affirmative.
