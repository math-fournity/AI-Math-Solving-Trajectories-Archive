# Asymptotic Formula for the Partition Function \(p(n)\)

## The Formula

The partition function \(p(n)\), which counts the number of ways to write \(n\)
as a sum of positive integers (disregarding order), satisfies the **Hardy–Ramanu
jan asymptotic formula**:

\[
p(n) \sim \frac{1}{4n\sqrt{3}} \exp\!\left(\pi\sqrt{\frac{2n}{3}}\right), \qquad
 n \to \infty.
\]

More precisely, this means

\[
\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{p(n)}{\dfrac{1}{4n\sqrt{3}}\exp\!\left(\pi\sqrt{2n/3}\ri
ght)} = 1.
\]

A sharper version, due to **Rademacher** (1937), gives a convergent series (not
merely asymptotic) whose first term is exactly the expression above, with correc
tion terms of lower exponential order. The Hardy–Ramanujan formula is thus the l
eading term of Rademacher's exact expansion.

## Significance

1. **Birth of the circle method.** Hardy and Ramanujan (1918) introduced the *ci
rcle method* to derive this formula — a technique that became one of the most po
werful tools in analytic number theory, later refined by Littlewood and used ext
ensively (e.g., in Waring's problem and modular forms).

2. **First deep asymptotic for an arithmetic counting function.** Before this, n
o arithmetic function of comparable combinatorial complexity had been given an a
symptotic with such precision. It demonstrated that partition counting, though d
efined purely combinatorially, is governed by deep analytic (modular) structure.

3. **Connection to modular forms.** The generating function \(\sum_{n\ge 0} p(n)
 q^n = \prod_{m\ge 1}(1-q^m)^{-1} = q^{-1/24}/\eta(\tau)\) (with \(q=e^{2\pi i\t
au}\)) is essentially the reciprocal of the Dedekind eta function. The asymptoti
c emerges from the modular transformation law of \(\eta\), revealing that the gr
owth of \(p(n)\) is dictated by the principal cusp of the modular group. This li
nked combinatorics, modular forms, and analytic number theory in a single result
.

4. **Practical computability.** The formula (and Rademacher's convergent refinem
ent) allows accurate numerical computation of \(p(n)\) even for very large \(n\)
 without enumerating partitions — a striking example of asymptotic analysis enab
ling exact computation.

5. **Historical milestone.** It is often cited as one of the landmark achievemen
ts of early 20th-century mathematics, showcasing the power of importing analytic
 and modular-form techniques into combinatorial problems.
