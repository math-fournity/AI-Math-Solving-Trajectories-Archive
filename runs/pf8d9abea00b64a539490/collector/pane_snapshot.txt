$$\angle D = 360^\circ-60^\circ-90^\circ-120^\circ = 90^\circ.$$

$$B=(0,0),\quad A=(a,0),\quad C=(0,c), \qquad a,c>0.$$

**Finding $D$ from the angles at $A$ and $C$.** The polygon $A\to B\to C\to D$ i

edge.

- At $A$: side $AB$ has direction $(-1,0)$. Rotating counterclockwise by $60^\ci

$$\vec{AD}\text{-dir} = (-\cos 60^\circ,\;\sin 60^\circ)=\Bigl(-\tfrac12,\;\tfra
c{\sqrt3}{2}\Bigr).$$
So $D = A + t\bigl(-\tfrac12,\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\bigr)=\bigl(a-\tfrac t2,\;\tfrac{
t\sqrt3}{2}\bigr)$ for some $t>0$.

irc$ gives the direction of $CD$:
$$\vec{CD}\text{-dir} = \Bigl(\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2},\;\tfrac12\Bigr).$$
So $D = C + s\bigl(\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2},\tfrac12\bigr)=\bigl(\tfrac{s\sqrt3}{2},\;c
+\tfrac s2\bigr)$ for some $s>0$.

Equating the two expressions for $D=(d_x,d_y)$:
$$d_x = a-\tfrac t2 = \tfrac{s\sqrt3}{2},\qquad d_y = \tfrac{t\sqrt3}{2}=c+\tfra
c s2.$$
Solving: $t=\tfrac{2d_y}{\sqrt3}$, $s=\tfrac{2d_x}{\sqrt3}$, and
$$a = d_x+\frac{d_y}{\sqrt3},\qquad c = d_y-\frac{d_x}{\sqrt3}. \tag{$\star$}$$

$$\frac{d_x}{3a}+\frac{d_y}{3c}=1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad c\,d_x + a\,d_y = 3a

Since $\tfrac1{\sqrt3}-\sqrt3\neq 0$, we get $d_y^2=d_x^2$, i.e. $d_y=\pm d_x$.

The case $d_y=-d_x$ forces $c=d_y-\tfrac{d_x}{\sqrt3}<0$, which is impossible. H

$$a+c=\Bigl(d_x+\frac{d_x}{\sqrt3}\Bigr)+\Bigl(d_x-\frac{d_x}{\sqrt3}\Bigr)=2d_x

$$[\,ABCD\,]=\frac{d_x}{2}\cdot 2d_x=d_x^2=\frac{9}{2}.$$
