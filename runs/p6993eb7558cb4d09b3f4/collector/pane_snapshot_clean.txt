## Analysis

**Erdős Problem 421** asks: Does there exist a strictly increasing sequence $1 \
le d_1 < d_2 < \cdots$ with natural density 1 such that all contiguous products
$\prod_{u \le i \le v} d_i$ are distinct?

**Why the "obvious" candidates fail:**

- **Full sequence** $d_i = i$: Collision immediately — $P(1,3) = 1\cdot2\cdot3 =
 6 = P(2,3) = 2\cdot3$ (the factor $d_1=1$ is harmless multiplicatively).

- **Skip 1**, i.e., $d_i = i+1$: Products become $(v+1)!/u!$. Collision: $P(1,2)
=2\cdot3=6=P(5,5)=6$. More generally, products of consecutive integers can coinc
ide: $a(a+1)\cdots b = c(c+1)\cdots d$ for distinct intervals $[a,b]\neq[c,d]$ (
e.g., $2\cdot3=6$, a length-2 interval matching a length-1 interval).

**The core difficulty:**

The question reduces to whether one can remove a **density-0** subset $R\subset\
mathbb{N}$ such that, ordering the complement as $d_1<d_2<\cdots$, all contiguou
s products $d_u\cdots d_v$ are distinct.

A greedy construction (add $n$ to the sequence only if no collision is created)
is plausible heuristically — the number of "accidental" multiplicative coinciden
ces among products of consecutive integers up to $N$ appears to grow slowly (sub
