## Proof

**Preliminary observation.** For $f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^{p_n}$ to converg
e for $x \in (0,1)$, we need $x^{p_n} \to 0$, hence $p_n \to +\infty$. The natur
al interpretation ensuring this is that $p_n$ is **non-decreasing** ($p_0 \leq p
_1 \leq \cdots$), with $p_n \to +\infty$. (A literally non-increasing integer se
quence cannot tend to $+\infty$, making the series divergent; we proceed with th
e mathematically consistent interpretation.)

---

### Step 1: Reformulation via substitution

Set $x = e^{-t}$ for $t > 0$. As $x \to 1^-$, we have $t \to 0^+$. Since $1 - e^
{-t} \sim t$ as $t \to 0$, the condition becomes:

$$\lim_{t \to 0^+} t \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} e^{-t p_n} = 0. \tag{$\star$}$$

---

### Step 2: Lower bound via the counting function

Define $A(s) = |\{n \geq 0 : p_n \leq s\}|$ for $s \geq 0$. Since $p_n \to +\inf
ty$, this is finite for each $s$.

For any $t > 0$, every term with $p_n \leq 1/t$ satisfies $e^{-tp_n} \geq e^{-1}
$. Therefore:

$$t \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} e^{-tp_n} \;\geq\; t \cdot A(1/t) \cdot e^{-1}.$$

By condition $(\star)$, the left side $\to 0$, so $t \cdot A(1/t) \to 0$. Substi
tuting $s = 1/t$ (so $t \to 0^+$ corresponds to $s \to \infty$):

$$\frac{A(s)}{s} \to 0 \quad \text{as } s \to \infty. \tag{$\star\star$}$$

---

### Step 3: From the counting function to growth of $p_n$

Condition $(\star\star)$ means: for every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists $S_\va
repsilon > 0$ such that for all $s \geq S_\varepsilon$:

$$A(s) < \varepsilon\, s.$$

Since $p_n$ is non-decreasing, $A(s) < \varepsilon s$ means: for all $n \geq \lc
eil \varepsilon s \rceil$, we have $p_n > s$.

**Fix any $M > 0$.** Set $\varepsilon = 1/M$. For all $s \geq S_{1/M}$, we have
$A(s) < s/M$, which implies:

$$\text{for all } n \geq \lceil s/M \rceil, \quad p_n > s.$$

Choose $s = Mn$ for any integer $n$ large enough that $Mn \geq S_{1/M}$. Then $\
lceil s/M \rceil = n$, giving:

$$p_n > Mn \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \frac{p_n}{n} > M$$

for all $n \geq N_M := \lceil S_{1/M}/M \rceil$.

Since $M > 0$ was **arbitrary**, we conclude:

$$\frac{p_n}{n} \to +\infty \quad \text{as } n \to \infty.$$

---

### Step 4: Conclusion

Setting $M = 2023$, there exists a finite integer $N$ such that for all $n \geq
N$:

$$\frac{p_n}{n} \geq 2023.$$

The smallest such $N$ is $N = \min\{n_0 \geq 1 : \forall\, n \geq n_0,\; p_n/n \
geq 2023\}$, which is well-defined and finite by the argument above. $\square$

Mn} \leq t\, N_M + \frac{1}{M}.$$

As $t \to 0^+$, this is at most $1/M$. Since $M$ is arbitrary, $t\sum e^{-tp_n}
\to 0$, recovering $(\star)$.
