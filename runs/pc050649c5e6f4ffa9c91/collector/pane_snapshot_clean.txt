## Proof

We seek $L = \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{\Gamma(t)}{\Gamma(\alpha t)}$.

**Key fact (Laurent expansion of $\Gamma$ at $0$):** The Gamma function has a si
mple pole at $t=0$ with residue $1$. Its Laurent expansion is

$$\Gamma(t) = \frac{1}{t} - \gamma + O(t), \qquad t \to 0,$$

where $\gamma$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant.

**Substitution.** Replacing $t$ by $\alpha t$ (assuming $\alpha \neq 0$; the cas
e $\alpha = 0$ is degenerate since $\Gamma(0)$ is undefined):

$$\Gamma(\alpha t) = \frac{1}{\alpha t} - \gamma + O(t), \qquad t \to 0.$$

**Forming the ratio.**

$$\frac{\Gamma(t)}{\Gamma(\alpha t)} = \frac{\dfrac{1}{t} - \gamma + O(t)}{\dfra
c{1}{\alpha t} - \gamma + O(t)}.$$

Divide numerator and denominator by $\frac{1}{t}$:

$$\frac{\Gamma(t)}{\Gamma(\alpha t)} = \frac{1 - \gamma\, t + O(t^2)}{\dfrac{1}{
\alpha} - \gamma\, t + O(t^2)}.$$

**Taking the limit.** As $t \to 0$, the $O(t)$ and $O(t^2)$ terms vanish, giving

$$L = \frac{1 - 0}{\dfrac{1}{\alpha} - 0} = \frac{1}{1/\alpha} = \alpha.$$

**Verification via the reflection-free argument.** Equivalently, since $\Gamma(t
) \sim \frac{1}{t}$ and $\Gamma(\alpha t) \sim \frac{1}{\alpha t}$ as $t \to 0$
(both dominated by their leading pole term), the ratio of the leading terms is

$$\frac{1/t}{1/(\alpha t)} = \alpha,$$

and the sub-leading constant terms ($-\gamma$) cancel in their relative contribu
tion as $t \to 0$, confirming the limit is unaffected by them.

**Conclusion:**

$$\boxed{\lim_{t \to 0} \frac{\Gamma(t)}{\Gamma(\alpha t)} = \alpha.}$$
