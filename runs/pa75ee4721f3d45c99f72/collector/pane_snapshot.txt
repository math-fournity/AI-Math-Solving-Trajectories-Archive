## Proof

Let $A \in \mathcal{R}_n$ with $|A| > 1$. For any $x \in A \setminus \{0\}$, we
have $x^{2^n+1} = 1$. This gives $x \cdot x^{2^n} = 1$ and $x^{2^n} \cdot x = 1$
, so every nonzero element is a unit (with two-sided inverse $x^{2^n}$). Thus $A
$ is a division ring. The trivial ring $\{0\}$ satisfies the condition vacuously
 since $A \setminus \{0\} = \emptyset$.

**Step 2: Apply Wedderburn's Little Theorem.**

By Wedderburn's little theorem, every finite division ring is commutative, hence
 a field. So every ring in $\mathcal{R}_n$ is either the trivial ring $\{0\}$ or
 a finite field $\mathbb{F}_q$ for some prime power $q$.

**Step 3: Determine which fields satisfy the condition.**

For $\mathbb{F}_q$, the condition $x^{2^n+1} = 1$ for all $x \neq 0$ means every
 element of $\mathbb{F}_q^*$ satisfies $x^{2^n+1} = 1$. Since $\mathbb{F}_q^*$ i
s cyclic of order $q - 1$, this is equivalent to:
$$q - 1 \mid 2^n + 1.$$

**Step 4: Use $n = 1$ (by the given independence).**

Since $\mathcal{R}_n$ is independent of $n$ for $n \geq 1$, we may take $n = 1$,
 giving $2^1 + 1 = 3$. The condition becomes:
$$q - 1 \mid 3.$$

So $q - 1 \in \{1, 3\}$, giving $q \in \{2, 4\}$.

- $q = 2 = 2^1$ is a prime power. ✓
- $q = 4 = 2^2$ is a prime power. ✓

**Step 5: Verify these fields.**

- **$\mathbb{F}_2$:** $\mathbb{F}_2^* = \{1\}$, and $1^3 = 1$. ✓

$ for all $x \in \mathbb{F}_4^*$. ✓

No other prime powers satisfy $q - 1 \mid 3$: $q = 3$ gives $q-1 = 2 \nmid 3$; $
q = 5$ gives $q - 1 = 4 \nmid 3$; $q = 8$ gives $q - 1 = 7 \nmid 3$; etc.

The distinct (up to isomorphism) rings in $\mathcal{R}_n$ are:

| Ring | Order |
