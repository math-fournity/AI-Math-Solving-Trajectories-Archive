$$\sum_{x \in \mathbb{Z}^n} e^{-2x^{T}x}= \left(\sum_{y=-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-2y
^{2}}\right)^{n}.$$

$$x^{T}x = x_1^2 + x_2^2 + \cdots + x_n^2.$$

Hence, by the additive property of the exponential,

$$e^{-2x^{T}x} = e^{-2(x_1^2+\cdots+x_n^2)} = \prod_{i=1}^{n} e^{-2x_i^2}.$$

**Step 2. Absolute convergence (justifies interchange of sum and product).** The
 scalar series

$$\sum_{y=-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-2y^2}$$

converges absolutely, since $e^{-2y^2} \le e^{-2|y|}$ for $|y|\ge 1$ and $\sum_{
y} e^{-2|y|} < \infty$. Therefore the $n$-fold product series

$$\sum_{x \in \mathbb{Z}^n} \prod_{i=1}^{n} e^{-2x_i^2}$$

is absolutely convergent. By Tonelli's theorem (applied to the counting measure
on $\mathbb{Z}^n = \mathbb{Z} \times \cdots \times \mathbb{Z}$), the multidimens
ional sum factors into iterated one-dimensional sums:

$$\sum_{x \in \mathbb{Z}^n} \prod_{i=1}^{n} e^{-2x_i^2}
= \sum_{x_1 \in \mathbb{Z}} \sum_{x_2 \in \mathbb{Z}} \cdots \sum_{x_n \in \math
bb{Z}} \prod_{i=1}^{n} e^{-2x_i^2}.$$

**Step 3. Factor the iterated sum.** Since the summand is a product of factors e
ach depending on a single coordinate, each inner sum treats the other coordinate
s' factors as constants and pulls them out. Iterating from the innermost sum out
ward:

$$\sum_{x_1 \in \mathbb{Z}} \cdots \sum_{x_n \in \mathbb{Z}} \prod_{i=1}^{n} e^{
-2x_i^2}
= \prod_{i=1}^{n} \left(\sum_{x_i \in \mathbb{Z}} e^{-2x_i^2}\right).$$

**Step 4. Identify the common factor.** Each one-dimensional sum is identical:

$$\sum_{x_i \in \mathbb{Z}} e^{-2x_i^2} = \sum_{y=-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-2y^2},$$

since the dummy index of summation may be renamed. Therefore

$$\prod_{i=1}^{n} \left(\sum_{x_i \in \mathbb{Z}} e^{-2x_i^2}\right) = \left(\su
m_{y=-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-2y^2}\right)^{n}.$$

$$\sum_{x \in \mathbb{Z}^n} e^{-2x^{T}x} = \left(\sum_{y=-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-2
y^{2}}\right)^{n}.$$
