**Pole structure of $f$.** Since $x^2 + x = x(x-1)$ in characteristic $2$, the f
unction $f(x) = \frac{\alpha}{x(x-1)}$ has:
- a **simple pole** at $x = 0$ (order $m=1$),
- a **simple pole** at $x = 1$ (order $m=1$),
- **no pole at infinity**: as $x \to \infty$, $f(x) \sim \alpha/x^2 \to 0$.

**Ramification (Artin–Schreier theory).** For an Artin–Schreier extension $y^p -
 y = f(x)$ in characteristic $p$, a place $P$ of $k(x)$ is ramified if and only
if $f$ has a pole at $P$ of order $m$ with $\gcd(m,p)=1$. At each such place, th

$$d_P = (p-1)(m+1).$$

Both poles $x=0$ and $x=1$ have order $m=1$ with $\gcd(1,2)=1$, so both are rami
fied, each with different exponent
$$d = (2-1)(1+1) = 2.$$

The place at infinity is **unramified**: $f$ has no pole there, and the residual
 equation $y^2+y=0$ (i.e., $y(y+1)=0$) has two solutions $y=0,1$ in $k$, so the
place at infinity **splits** into two unramified places.

$$2g(C) - 2 = 2\bigl(2\cdot 0 - 2\bigr) + \sum_{P} d_P = 2(-2) + 2 + 2 = -4 + 4
