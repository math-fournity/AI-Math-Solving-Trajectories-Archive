## Proof

**Answer: Yes, $\dim(G/H) = g - h$ always.**

**Setup.** Let $\mathfrak{g} = T_eG$ be the Lie algebra of $G$ and $\mathfrak{h}
 = T_eH$ the Lie algebra of $H$. Since $H$ is a Lie subgroup, $\mathfrak{h}$ is
a Lie subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}$; since $H$ is normal, $\mathfrak{h}$ is in fa
ct an ideal of $\mathfrak{g}$. For $G/H$ to carry the structure of a Lie group (
the standard hypothesis, requiring $H$ to be closed), the quotient map
$$\pi: G \to G/H, \qquad g \mapsto gH$$
is a smooth submersion (this is the standard quotient-manifold theorem for Lie g
roups: the quotient of a Lie group by a closed subgroup admits a unique smooth s
tructure making the projection a submersion).

**Key step: the differential at the identity.** Consider the differential of $\p
i$ at the identity:
$$d\pi_e : \mathfrak{g} = T_eG \longrightarrow T_{eH}(G/H).$$

- **Surjectivity.** Since $\pi$ is a submersion, $d\pi_e$ is surjective.

- **Kernel.** The fiber of $\pi$ over the coset $eH$ is exactly $H = \pi^{-1}(eH
)$. The tangent space to a fiber at a point equals the kernel of the differentia
l of the projection at that point, so
$$\ker(d\pi_e) = T_eH = \mathfrak{h}.$$

**Dimension count.** Applying the rank–nullity theorem to the linear map $d\pi_e
$:
$$\dim \mathfrak{g} = \dim \ker(d\pi_e) + \dim \operatorname{im}(d\pi_e) = \dim
\mathfrak{h} + \dim T_{eH}(G/H).$$

Since $d\pi_e$ is surjective, $\operatorname{im}(d\pi_e) = T_{eH}(G/H)$, and so
$$\dim T_{eH}(G/H) = \dim \mathfrak{g} - \dim \mathfrak{h} = g - h.$$

**Conclusion.** The dimension of a smooth manifold equals the dimension of its t
angent space at any point. Since $G/H$ is a smooth manifold (a Lie group, as $H$
 is closed and normal), its dimension is
$$\dim(G/H) = \dim T_{eH}(G/H) = g - h.$$

Hence the dimension of the quotient group $G/H$ is always $g - h$. $\blacksquare
