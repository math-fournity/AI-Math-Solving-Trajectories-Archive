## Proof

**Setup and existence of $V$.** Since $X$ is locally compact Hausdorff, for each
 $x \in K$ there exists an open neighborhood $W_x$ of $x$ with $\overline{W_x}$
compact and $\overline{W_x} \subset U$ (by local compactness and Hausdorffness—f
irst find a neighborhood with compact closure, then shrink it inside $U$). The c
ollection $\{W_x\}_{x\in K}$ covers the compact set $K$; extract a finite subcov
er $W_{x_1},\dots,W_{x_n}$. Set $V = \bigcup_{i=1}^n W_{x_i}$. Then $V$ is open,
 $K \subset V$, $\overline{V} = \bigcup_{i=1}^n \overline{W_{x_i}}$ is compact (
finite union of compact sets), and $\overline{V} \subset U$. Thus $V$ satisfies
$K \subset V \subset \overline{V} \subset U$ with $\overline{V}$ compact. ∎ (exi
stence of $V$)

**Construction of $f$ on $\overline{V}$.** The space $\overline{V}$ is compact H
ausdorff, hence normal. We identify two disjoint closed subsets of $\overline{V}
$:

- $K$ is compact in the Hausdorff space $X$, hence closed in $X$, hence closed i
n $\overline{V}$.
- $\overline{V}\setminus V$ is closed in $\overline{V}$, since $V$ is open in $X
$ and thus $V\cap\overline{V}=V$ is open in $\overline{V}$.
- $K \cap (\overline{V}\setminus V) = \emptyset$ since $K\subset V$.

By **Urysohn's Lemma** applied to the normal space $\overline{V}$, there exists
a continuous function
$$f:\overline{V}\to[0,1]$$
such that $f|_K \equiv 1$ and $f|_{\overline{V}\setminus V}\equiv 0$.

**Extension to $X$.** Define $\tilde{f}:X\to\mathbb{R}$ by
$$\tilde{f}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x)&x\in\overline{V},\\0&x\in X\setminus\overline{V
}.\end{cases}$$

**Claim: $\tilde{f}$ is continuous on $X$.** We apply the **pasting lemma** (clo
sed version): if $A,B$ are closed in $X$ with $A\cup B=X$, and $g:A\to\mathbb{R}
$, $h:B\to\mathbb{R}$ are continuous with $g=h$ on $A\cap B$, then the combined
function is continuous.

Set $A=\overline{V}$ and $B=X\setminus V$.

- $A=\overline{V}$ is closed (compact in Hausdorff $X$).
- $B=X\setminus V$ is closed ($V$ is open).
- $A\cup B = \overline{V}\cup(X\setminus V)$. Since $V\subset\overline{V}$, we h
ave $X\setminus V\supset X\setminus\overline{V}$, so $\overline{V}\cup(X\setminu
s V)\supset\overline{V}\cup(X\setminus\overline{V})=X$. Hence $A\cup B=X$.
- $A\cap B = \overline{V}\cap(X\setminus V)=\overline{V}\setminus V$.

On $A=\overline{V}$: $\tilde{f}|_A = f$, which is continuous by construction, an
d $f=0$ on $A\cap B=\overline{V}\setminus V$.

On $B=X\setminus V$: For $x\in X\setminus\overline{V}\subset X\setminus V$, $\ti
lde{f}(x)=0$. For $x\in\overline{V}\setminus V\subset X\setminus V$, $\tilde{f}(
x)=f(x)=0$. So $\tilde{f}|_B\equiv 0$, which is continuous.

On $A\cap B=\overline{V}\setminus V$: both restrictions give $0$, so they agree.

By the pasting lemma, $\tilde{f}$ is continuous on $X$. ∎

**Verification of the required properties.**

1. **Compact support:** $\operatorname{supp}(\tilde{f})\subset\overline{V}$, whi
ch is compact.
2. **$1_K\le\tilde{f}$:** On $K$, $\tilde{f}=f=1$, so $1_K\le\tilde{f}$.
3. **$\tilde{f}\le 1_U$:** Since $V\subset U$, we have $X\setminus U\subset X\se
tminus V$. On $X\setminus V$, $\tilde{f}=0$. On $V\subset U$, $\tilde{f}=f\le 1$
. So $\tilde{f}\le 1_U$ on all of $X$.

**Answer to the question:** **Yes**, the extended function $f$ is continuous on
$X$. The continuity follows from the pasting lemma: the two closed sets $\overli
ne{V}$ and $X\setminus V$ cover $X$, the function is continuous on each (Urysohn
's function on $\overline{V}$, identically zero on $X\setminus V$), and the two
definitions agree on the intersection $\overline{V}\setminus V$ (where both give
 $0$).

### PROOF COMPLETE
