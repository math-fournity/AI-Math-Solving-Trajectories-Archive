## Solution

**The necessarily true statement is C: The graph of $T^{-1}$ is closed.**

### Proof that C holds

Recall
$$\operatorname{Graph}(T)=\{(x,Tx)\in X\times Y : x\in X\}\subseteq X\times Y,$$
which is closed in $X\times Y$ by hypothesis. The graph of the inverse is
$$\operatorname{Graph}(T^{-1})=\{(y,T^{-1}y)\in Y\times X : y\in Y\}=\{(Tx,x):x\
in X\}\subseteq Y\times X.$$

Consider the *swap map*
$$S:X\times Y\to Y\times X,\qquad S(x,y)=(y,x).$$
With the natural product norms $\|(x,y)\|=\|x\|_X+\|y\|_Y$ and $\|(y,x)\|=\|y\|_
Y+\|x\|_X$, the map $S$ is an **isometric isomorphism** (hence a homeomorphism)
between $X\times Y$ and $Y\times X$. Moreover,
$$S\bigl(\operatorname{Graph}(T)\bigr)=\{(Tx,x):x\in X\}=\operatorname{Graph}(T^
{-1}).$$

The image of a closed set under a homeomorphism is closed. Since $\operatorname{
Graph}(T)$ is closed in $X\times Y$, its image $\operatorname{Graph}(T^{-1})$ is
 closed in $Y\times X$. $\square$

### Why the other options are not necessarily true

**A is false.** $\operatorname{Graph}(T)=X\times Y$ would require that for every
 $x\in X$ and every $y\in Y$, $y=Tx$. Fixing $x$, this forces $Y=\{Tx\}$, a sing
leton. For any nontrivial $Y$ (e.g. $Y=\mathbb{R}$, $X=\mathbb{R}$, $T=\mathrm{i
d}$), the graph is a proper subset of $X\times Y$, so A fails in general.

**D is false.** The Closed Graph Theorem requires the *domain* to be complete (B
anach). Here $X,Y$ are merely normed. Concrete counterexample: let $X=(C[0,1],\|
\cdot\|_1)$ and $Y=(C[0,1],\|\cdot\|_\infty)$, and let $T:X\to Y$ be the identit
y $T(f)=f$. Then $T$ is bijective and linear. Its graph is closed: if $(f_n,f_n)
\to(f,g)$ in $X\times Y$, then $\|f_n-f\|_1\to0$ and $\|f_n-g\|_\infty\to0$; uni
form convergence implies $L^1$ convergence, so $f_n\to g$ in $L^1$, and together
 with $f_n\to f$ in $L^1$ gives $f=g$ (both continuous). Yet $T$ is **not contin
uous**, since $\|f\|_\infty\le C\|f\|_1$ fails (take a spike of height $n$ on an
 interval of width $1/n^2$: $\|f_n\|_\infty=n$ while $\|f_n\|_1=1/n$).

**B is false.** Take the reverse of the previous example: $X=(C[0,1],\|\cdot\|_\
infty)$, $Y=(C[0,1],\|\cdot\|_1)$, $T=\mathrm{id}$. The same argument shows $\op
eratorname{Graph}(T)$ is closed and $T$ is bijective. Here $T$ is continuous ($\
|f\|_1\le\|f\|_\infty$), but $T^{-1}:Y\to X$ is **not continuous** (same spike e
xample: $\|f_n\|_1=1/n\to0$ while $\|f_n\|_\infty=n\to\infty$). Hence $T^{-1}$ n
eed not be continuous.

### Conclusion

Only statement **C** is necessarily true: the graph of $T^{-1}$ is the image of
the closed graph of $T$ under the isometric swap homeomorphism $X\times Y\cong Y
\times X$, and is therefore closed.
