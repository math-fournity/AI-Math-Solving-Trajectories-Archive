## Answer

**No.** A point $x^\star$ satisfying the KKT conditions need not be a local opti
mum when the constraints are non-convex, even though the objective is strictly c
onvex. The KKT conditions are *first-order necessary* conditions; they become *s
ufficient* for (global) optimality only when the entire problem is convex (conve
x objective **and** convex feasible set). Strict convexity of $f$ alone does not
 compensate for a non-convex feasible set, because the geometry of the feasible
set can turn a KKT stationary point into a saddle point.

Below is an explicit counterexample.

---

### Counterexample

Let $x = (x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ and consider

$$
f(x,y) = x^2 + \Big(y - \tfrac12\Big)^2, \qquad g(x,y) = 1 - x^2 - y^2 \le 0.
$$

- **Objective:** $f$ is strictly convex (Hessian $\nabla^2 f = 2I \succ 0$) and
$C^\infty$.
- **Constraint:** $g$ is concave (Hessian $\nabla^2 g = -2I \prec 0$), hence **n
on-convex**, and $C^\infty$.
- **Feasible set:** $\{(x,y) : x^2 + y^2 \ge 1\}$ — the *exterior* of the unit d
isk, which is **non-convex**.

Consider the candidate point $x^\star = (0, -1)$.

#### 1. KKT conditions hold at $(0,-1)$

The gradients are
$$
\nabla f(0,-1) = (0,\, -3), \qquad \nabla g(0,-1) = (0,\, 2).
$$

- **Primal feasibility:** $g(0,-1) = 1 - 0 - 1 = 0 \le 0$. ✓ (constraint active)
- **Dual feasibility:** choose $\lambda = \tfrac32 \ge 0$. ✓
- **Complementary slackness:** $\lambda \cdot g(0,-1) = \tfrac32 \cdot 0 = 0$. ✓
- **Stationarity:** $\nabla f + \lambda \nabla g = (0,-3) + \tfrac32(0,2) = (0,0
)$. ✓

All KKT conditions are satisfied. (LICQ also holds since $\nabla g(0,-1) \ne 0$,
 so KKT is even *necessary* here.)

#### 2. $(0,-1)$ is **not** a local minimum

Restrict $f$ to the boundary of the feasible set, the unit circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1
$:
$$
f\big|_{\partial} = x^2 + (y-\tfrac12)^2 = 1 - y + \tfrac14 = \tfrac54 - y.
$$
This is maximized at $y = -1$, i.e., exactly at $(0,-1)$. Hence every nearby bou
ndary point has *strictly smaller* $f$. Concretely, for small $\varepsilon > 0$
the feasible point $\big(\varepsilon,\, -\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^2}\big)$ lies on th
e unit circle and
$$
f = \varepsilon^2 + \big(-\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^2} - \tfrac12\big)^2 = 2.25 - 0.5\
,\varepsilon^2 + O(\varepsilon^4) \;<\; 2.25 = f(0,-1).
$$
So every neighborhood of $(0,-1)$ contains feasible points with lower objective
value. Therefore $(0,-1)$ is **not a local minimum** (it is in fact a saddle: a
local *maximum* along the boundary, while $f$ increases moving radially outward
into the exterior).

#### 3. Why this happens — failure of the second-order sufficient condition

The Lagrangian is $L = f + \tfrac32\, g$, with Hessian
$$
\nabla^2 L = \nabla^2 f + \tfrac32 \nabla^2 g = 2I + \tfrac32(-2I) = -I \prec 0.
$$
The critical cone at $(0,-1)$ (active constraint with $\lambda > 0$) is $\{d : \
nabla g^T d = 0\} = \{(t,0) : t \in \mathbb{R}\}$, and on it $d^T (\nabla^2 L)\,
 d = -t^2 < 0$. The second-order sufficient condition for a local *minimum* requ
ires *non-negative* curvature on the critical cone; here it is strictly negative
, so the condition fails — consistent with $(0,-1)$ being a saddle rather than a
 local optimum.

---

### Conclusion

The KKT conditions are only first-order necessary conditions. With a non-convex
feasible set (here produced by a non-convex constraint), a KKT point can be a sa
ddle point or even a local maximum of $f$ restricted to the feasible set. Strict
 convexity of the objective does **not** by itself restore sufficiency, because
sufficiency relies on convexity of the *feasible set*, not just of $f$. To guara
ntee that a KKT point is a local optimum one must additionally verify a **second
-order sufficient condition** (positive curvature of the Lagrangian Hessian on t
he critical cone). Therefore we **cannot** claim that $x^\star$ is even a local
optimum solely from the KKT conditions.
