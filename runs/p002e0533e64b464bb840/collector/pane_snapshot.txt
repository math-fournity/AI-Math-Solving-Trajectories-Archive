## Proof

**Answer: Yes, such a function exists.**

**Key observations before construction:**

1. If $f''(0)$ exists, then $f'$ is continuous at $0$. (Proof: $f''(0)=\lim_{h\t
o 0}\frac{f'(h)-f'(0)}{h}$ exists and is finite, so $\frac{f'(h)-f'(0)}{h}$ is b
ounded near $0$, giving $|f'(h)-f'(0)|\leq M|h|\to 0$.) Thus the discontinuities
 of $f'$ must occur at points $x_n\neq 0$ with $x_n\to 0$.

2. By Darboux's theorem, $f'$ (being a derivative) has the intermediate value pr
operty, so any discontinuity of $f'$ must be oscillatory, not a jump.

**Construction.** Set $\delta=\tfrac12$. For $n\geq 3$, let $x_n=\tfrac1n$ and $
r_n=\tfrac{1}{2n(n+1)}$. The intervals $I_n=(x_n-r_n,\,x_n+r_n)$ are pairwise di
sjoint (since $r_n+r_{n+1}=\tfrac{1}{n(n+2)}<\tfrac{1}{n(n+1)}=|x_n-x_{n+1}|$) a
nd contained in $(0,\tfrac12)$.

Let $\theta:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]$ be a $C^1$ function with $\theta(t)=1$ for $|t|\
leq\tfrac12$ and $\theta(t)=0$ for $|t|\geq 1$. Set $\eta_n(x)=\theta\!\bigl(\tf
rac{x-x_n}{r_n}\bigr)$, so $\eta_n=1$ on $[x_n-\tfrac{r_n}{2},\,x_n+\tfrac{r_n}{
2}]$, $\eta_n=0$ outside $I_n$, and $|\eta_n'|\leq \tfrac{M_0}{r_n}$ where $M_0=
\|\theta'\|_\infty$.

Define:
$$\phi_n(x)=\begin{cases}\eta_n(x)\,(x-x_n)^2\sin\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{x-x_n}\bigr),
& x\neq x_n,\\[4pt] 0,& x=x_n.\end{cases}$$

$$f(x)=\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^2}\,\phi_n(x),\qquad x\in\bigl(-\tfrac12,\t
frac12\bigr).$$

---

**Condition 1: $f$ is differentiable on $(-\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$.**

Since the $I_n$ are pairwise disjoint, at most one $\phi_n(x)$ is nonzero at any
 $x$. Each $\phi_n$ is differentiable everywhere: at $x_n$, $\bigl|\frac{\phi_n(
x)-\phi_n(x_n)}{x-x_n}\bigr|=|\eta_n(x)(x-x_n)\sin(\tfrac{1}{x-x_n})|\to 0$, so
$\phi_n'(x_n)=0$; at other points, $\phi_n$ is a product of differentiable funct
ions. At boundary points of $I_n$, both $\eta_n$ and $\eta_n'$ vanish, so $\phi_
n$ and $\phi_n'$ tend to $0$ from inside, matching $\phi_n\equiv 0$ outside. Thu
s $f$ is differentiable everywhere, with $f'(x)=\frac{1}{n^2}\phi_n'(x)$ if $x\i
n I_n$ (for some $n$), and $f'(x)=0$ otherwise.

---

**Condition 2: $f''(0)$ exists.**

All $I_n\subset(0,\tfrac12)$, so $f\equiv 0$ on $(-\tfrac12,0]$, giving $f'(0)=0
$. We show $f''(0)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f'(h)}{h}=0$.

- **$h\leq 0$ or $h>0$, $h\notin\bigcup I_n$:** $f'(h)=0$, so $\frac{f'(h)}{h}=0
$.

- **$h\in I_n$ for some $n\geq 3$:** We bound $|\phi_n'(h)|$. For $h\in I_n$,
$$|\phi_n'(h)|\leq|\eta_n'(h)|\cdot r_n^2+|\eta_n(h)|\cdot(2r_n+1)\leq M_0\,r_n+
(2r_n+1)\leq M_0+3=:C,$$
using $|\eta_n'|\leq M_0/r_n$, $|h-x_n|\leq r_n$, $|\eta_n|\leq 1$, and $r_n<1$.

Also $|h|\geq x_n-r_n=\tfrac1n-\tfrac{1}{2n(n+1)}=\tfrac{2n+1}{2n(n+1)}\geq\tfra
c{1}{2n}$.

Therefore:
$$\left|\frac{f'(h)}{h}\right|=\frac{|\phi_n'(h)|}{n^2\,|h|}\leq\frac{C/n^2}{1/(
2n)}=\frac{2C}{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.$$

Since $h\in I_n$ with $h\to 0^+$ forces $n\to\infty$, we get $\frac{f'(h)}{h}\to
 0$ from the right. Combined with the left side, $f''(0)=0$. $\checkmark$

---

**Condition 3: $f'$ is discontinuous at each $x_n$, and $x_n\to 0$.**

On $(x_n-\tfrac{r_n}{2},\,x_n+\tfrac{r_n}{2})$, we have $\eta_n\equiv 1$, so:
$$\phi_n'(x)=2(x-x_n)\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{x-x_n}\Bigr)-\cos\!\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{x-
x_n}\Bigr).$$

The term $\cos\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{x-x_n}\bigr)$ oscillates as $x\to x_n$ (taking v
alues $\pm1$ along sequences), while $2(x-x_n)\sin(\tfrac{1}{x-x_n})\to 0$. Thus
 $\phi_n'$ is discontinuous at $x_n$.

By disjointness of the $I_n$, all other $\phi_m$ ($m\neq n$) vanish identically
near $x_n$. So $f'(x)=\frac{1}{n^2}\phi_n'(x)$ near $x_n$, and $f'$ is discontin
uous at $x_n$.

The sequence $x_n=\tfrac1n\to 0$ with each $x_n\in(0,\tfrac12)\subset(-\delta,\d
elta)$ satisfies condition 3. $\checkmark$

---

All three conditions are satisfied. Therefore, **such a function exists**.
