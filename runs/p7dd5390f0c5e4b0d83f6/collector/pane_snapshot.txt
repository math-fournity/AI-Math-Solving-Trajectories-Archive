## Proof

We compute $I = \int_{S^{n-1}} x_1 x_2 \, dS$, where $S^{n-1} = \{x \in \mathbb{
R}^n : \|x\| = 1\}$ and $n \geq 2$ (so that both coordinates $x_1, x_2$ are pres
ent).

**Symmetry argument.** Consider the reflection map
$$\sigma : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n, \qquad \sigma(x_1, x_2, x_3, \dots, x_
n) = (-x_1, x_2, x_3, \dots, x_n).$$

This is a linear isometry of $\mathbb{R}^n$ (it is orthogonal with $\det = -1$).
 Since $\|\sigma(x)\| = \|x\|$, it restricts to a diffeomorphism $\sigma : S^{n-
1} \to S^{n-1}$. Being the restriction of an orthogonal linear map, $\sigma$ pre
serves the Riemannian metric on $S^{n-1}$, and hence preserves the surface measu
re: $\sigma^*(dS) = dS$.

Under this map, the integrand transforms as
$$x_1 x_2 \circ \sigma = (-x_1) x_2 = -x_1 x_2.$$

Therefore, by the change of variables formula,
$$I = \int_{S^{n-1}} x_1 x_2 \, dS = \int_{S^{n-1}} (x_1 x_2 \circ \sigma)\, \si
gma^*(dS) = \int_{S^{n-1}} (-x_1 x_2)\, dS = -I.$$

Thus $I = -I$, which gives $2I = 0$, and so

$$\boxed{\int_{S^{n-1}} x_1 x_2 \, dS = 0.}$$
