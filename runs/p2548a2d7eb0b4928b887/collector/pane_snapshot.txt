## Proof

Let $\xi_n = e^{2\pi i/n}$ be a primitive $n$-th root of unity, and let $K = \ma
thbb{Q}[\xi_n]$.

**Step 1: Identify the Galois group with $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}$.**

The minimal polynomial of $\xi_n$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is the $n$-th cyclotomic pol
ynomial $\Phi_n(x)$, which has degree $\varphi(n)$. Hence $[K:\mathbb{Q}] = \var
phi(n)$, and since $K$ is the splitting field of $x^n - 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, th
e extension $K/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois with $|\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})| =
\varphi(n)$.

Any $\mathbb{Q}$-automorphism $\sigma$ of $K$ is determined by $\sigma(\xi_n)$,
which must be a root of $\Phi_n$, i.e., a primitive $n$-th root of unity. The pr
imitive $n$-th roots are exactly $\{\xi_n^{\ell} : \gcd(\ell, n) = 1\}$. Thus ev
ery automorphism is of the form $\sigma_{\ell}$ for a unique $\ell \in (\mathbb{
Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}$, where
$$\sigma_{\ell}(\xi_n) = \xi_n^{\ell}.$$

Moreover, $\sigma_{\ell} \circ \sigma_{m}(\xi_n) = \sigma_{\ell}(\xi_n^{m}) = (\
xi_n^{\ell})^{m} = \xi_n^{\ell m} = \sigma_{\ell m}(\xi_n)$, so
$$\sigma_{\ell} \circ \sigma_{m} = \sigma_{\ell m}.$$
Therefore the map $\sigma_{\ell} \mapsto \ell \bmod n$ is a group isomorphism
$$\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}[\xi_n]/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
)^{\times}.$$

**Step 2: When the group is cyclic, identify a generator.**

By Gauss's theorem on primitive roots, $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}$ is cy
clic if and only if
$$n \in \{1,\, 2,\, 4,\, p^{k},\, 2p^{k} : p \text{ an odd prime},\; k \geq 1\}.
$$

The problem assumes this cyclic case. Let $g$ be a **primitive root modulo $n$**
, i.e., a generator of the cyclic group $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}$, so
that the residue class $\bar{g}$ has order $\varphi(n)$.

**Step 3: Verify $\sigma_g$ generates the Galois group.**

Under the isomorphism $\sigma_{\ell} \leftrightarrow \ell$, the order of $\sigma
_g$ in $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ equals the multiplicative order of $g$
 modulo $n$, which is $\varphi(n) = |\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})|$. Hence $
\sigma_g$ has order equal to the full group order, so
$$\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}[\xi_n]/\mathbb{Q}) = \langle \sigma_g \rangle =
\{\sigma_g, \sigma_g^2, \dots, \sigma_g^{\varphi(n)} = \mathrm{id}\}.$$

Concretely, $\sigma_g : \xi_n \mapsto \xi_n^{g}$, and its powers are $\sigma_g^j
 : \xi_n \mapsto \xi_n^{g^j}$, which range over all primitive $n$-th roots as $j
 = 1, \dots, \varphi(n)$ since $g$ is a primitive root mod $n$.

**Conclusion.**

A generator of $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}[\xi_n]/\mathbb{Q})$ is
$$\boxed{\sigma_g : \xi_n \mapsto \xi_n^{g},}$$
where $g$ is any primitive root modulo $n$ (a generator of $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb
{Z})^{\times}$). Such a $g$ exists precisely in the cyclic case $n \in \{1, 2, 4
, p^k, 2p^k\}$ with $p$ odd prime.
