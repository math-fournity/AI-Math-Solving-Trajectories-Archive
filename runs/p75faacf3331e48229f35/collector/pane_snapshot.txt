$$\mathbb{Q}_p^\star \cong \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}_p^\star$$

(where $\mathbb{Z}$ is additive and $\mathbb{Z}_p^\star$ is multiplicative).

For **$p$ odd**: $\mathbb{Z}_p^\star \cong \mu_{p-1} \times (1 + p\mathbb{Z}_p)$

For **$p = 2$**: $\mathbb{Z}_2^\star \cong \{\pm 1\} \times (1+4\mathbb{Z}_2)$,

### Step 2: Homomorphisms from the finite part

Any group homomorphism from a **finite** group to $(\mathbb{R}, +)$ is trivial,
since $\mathbb{R}$ is torsion-free. Hence every homomorphism $\mathbb{Z}_p^\star

### Step 3: Non-trivial homomorphisms $\mathbb{Z}_p \to \mathbb{R}$ exist

$$\mathbb{Z}_p \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q} \cong \mathbb{Q}_p$$

Now $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space. Its cardinality is $2^{\aleph

$$\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}_p = 2^{\aleph_0}.$$

Likewise $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{R} = 2^{\aleph_0}$. By the Axiom of Choice,
there exists a **$\mathbb{Q}$-linear isomorphism** $\Phi: \mathbb{Q}_p \xrightar

Restricting $\Phi$ to $\mathbb{Z}_p \subset \mathbb{Q}_p$ yields a group homomor

$$\varphi := \Phi\big|_{\mathbb{Z}_p} : (\mathbb{Z}_p, +) \to (\mathbb{R}, +).$$

This restriction is **non-trivial**: since $\Phi$ is injective and $1 \in \mathb
b{Z}_p$ is nonzero, $\varphi(1) = \Phi(1) \neq 0$.

### Step 4: Construction of $f$

Define a homomorphism $\psi: \mathbb{Z}_p^\star \to \mathbb{R}$ as follows:

Since $\varphi$ is non-trivial and $\log_p$ is an isomorphism, $\psi$ is a **non

Now define $f: \mathbb{Q}_p^\star \to \mathbb{R}$ using the decomposition $\math

$$f(x) = \psi\!\left(\frac{x}{p^{v_p(x)}}\right).$$

**$f$ is a group homomorphism:** For $x = p^a u$, $y = p^b v$,

$$f(xy) = f(p^{a+b} uv) = \psi(uv) = \psi(u) + \psi(v) = f(x) + f(y).$$

The function $\log|\cdot|_p$ satisfies $\log|p|_p = -\log p \neq 0$ and $\log|u|

Our $f$ has the **opposite** pattern:

- $f(p) = \psi(1) = 0$ (zero on $p$),

Therefore $f$ is not a scalar multiple of $\log|\cdot|_p$, and $f$ is not identi
