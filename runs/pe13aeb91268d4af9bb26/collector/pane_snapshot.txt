## Proof

**Claim.** Any two finite fields having the same number $p^n$ of elements are is
omorphic, regardless of the particular irreducible polynomials (and regardless o
f whether the field is built in one step over $\mathbb{F}_p$ or in several steps
 over intermediate subfields) used in their construction.

Let $E$ and $F$ be two finite fields, each of order $p^n$ (where $p$ is prime an
d $n\geq 1$). We show $E\cong F$.

### Step 1. Both fields have characteristic $p$ and contain $\mathbb{F}_p$.

A finite field has prime characteristic, say $\ell$. Its prime subfield is $\mat
hbb{F}_\ell$, so its order is a power $\ell^k$ of $\ell$. Since $|E|=|F|=p^n$ wi
th $p$ prime, both have characteristic $p$, and the prime subfield of each is (i
somorphic to) $\mathbb{F}_p$. We may thus regard $E$ and $F$ as extensions of a
common copy of $\mathbb{F}_p$.

### Step 2. Every element of a field of order $p^n$ is a root of $x^{p^n}-x$.

The multiplicative group $E^\times = E\setminus\{0\}$ has order $p^n-1$. By Lagr
ange, every $a\in E^\times$ satisfies $a^{p^n-1}=1$, hence $a^{p^n}=a$. The elem
ent $0$ also satisfies $0^{p^n}=0$. Therefore every element of $E$ is a root of
the polynomial
$$\Phi(x)=x^{p^n}-x\in\mathbb{F}_p[x].$$
The same holds for $F$. Since $\Phi$ has degree $p^n$ and $E$ (resp. $F$) suppli
es $p^n$ distinct roots (the derivative $\Phi'(x)=-1$ is nonzero, so $\Phi$ is s
eparable), $E$ and $F$ each consist *exactly* of the full set of roots of $\Phi$
.

### Step 3. Both fields are splitting fields of $\Phi$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$.

The polynomial $\Phi=x^{p^n}-x$ splits completely over $E$ (and over $F$), and i
ts roots generate $E$ (resp. $F$) as a field over $\mathbb{F}_p$ — indeed $E$ *i
s* the set of roots together with the field operations. Hence $E$ is a splitting
 field of $\Phi$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$, and likewise $F$ is a splitting field of $
\Phi$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$.

### Step 4. Splitting fields are unique up to isomorphism.

By the uniqueness theorem for splitting fields: any two splitting fields of the
same polynomial over the same base field are isomorphic via an isomorphism fixin
g the base field. Therefore there exists a field isomorphism
$$\varphi:E\stackrel{\sim}{\longrightarrow}F$$
with $\varphi|_{\mathbb{F}_p}=\mathrm{id}$.

### Step 5. The construction details are irrelevant.

The two constructions described in the problem are:

- **Construction A:** $E_1=\mathbb{F}_p[x]/(f(x))$, where $f$ is irreducible of
degree $n$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$. This is a field of $p^n$ elements.
- **Construction B:** $E_2=\mathbb{F}_{p^m}[y]/(g(y))$, where $g$ is irreducible
 of degree $t$ over $\mathbb{F}_{p^m}$, with $n=mt$ and $m,t>1$. This is a field
 of $(p^m)^t=p^{mt}=p^n$ elements.

Both $E_1$ and $E_2$ are finite fields of order $p^n$. By Steps 1–4, any two fin
ite fields of order $p^n$ are isomorphic. Hence $E_1\cong E_2$.

The point is that the *specific* irreducible polynomial used, and the *number of
 steps* (one extension of degree $n$, or two successive extensions of degrees $m
$ and $t$ with $mt=n$), affect only the *presentation* of the field — i.e., how
its elements are named and how multiplication is computed — but not the isomorph
ism type. The isomorphism type of a finite field is determined entirely by its o
rder $p^n$.

### Conclusion.

Yes, the two fields are isomorphic. More generally:

> **Theorem.** For every prime power $q=p^n$ there exists a finite field of orde
r $q$, and it is unique up to isomorphism. Any two finite fields of the same ord
er are isomorphic.

The proof above establishes uniqueness: every field of order $p^n$ is the splitt
ing field of $x^{p^n}-x$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$, and splitting fields are unique up
 to (base-field-fixing) isomorphism. The different irreducible polynomials and t

g t$ with $mt=n$) merely give different concrete realizations of the same abstra
