**Answer: Yes.** In a simply connected domain, every closed curve is homologous
to zero.

---

### Proof

Let $D$ be a simply connected domain. By definition, $D$ is path-connected and i
ts fundamental group is trivial: $\pi_1(D) = 0$. Equivalently, every closed curv
e (loop) $\gamma$ in $D$ is **null-homotopic** — it can be continuously deformed
 to a point within $D$.

We must show every closed curve $\gamma$ in $D$ is **null-homologous**, i.e., $\
gamma$ represents the zero class in the first singular homology group $H_1(D;\ma
thbb{Z})$.

**Key implication: null-homotopic $\Rightarrow$ null-homologous.**

Let $\gamma: S^1 \to D$ be a closed curve. Since $D$ is simply connected, $\gamm
a$ is null-homotopic: there exists a continuous map
$$H: S^1 \times [0,1] \to D$$
with $H(\cdot, 0) = \gamma$ and $H(\cdot, 1) = p$ for some constant point $p \in
 D$.

The cylinder $S^1 \times [0,1]$ is homeomorphic to the closed annulus, and colla
psing the top circle $S^1 \times \{1\}$ to a point yields a disk $D^2$. The homo
topy $H$ factors through this quotient, giving a continuous map
$$\tilde{H}: D^2 \to D$$
whose restriction to $\partial D^2 \cong S^1$ is exactly $\gamma$.

In singular homology, $\tilde{H}$ is a singular $2$-chain (a single singular $2$
-simplex after suitable triangulation of $D^2$), and its boundary is precisely t
he singular $1$-chain represented by $\gamma$:
$$\partial \tilde{H} = \gamma.$$

Therefore $\gamma$ is a boundary, hence $[\gamma] = 0 \in H_1(D;\mathbb{Z})$. Si
nce $\gamma$ was arbitrary, **every closed curve in $D$ is homologous to zero.**
 $\blacksquare$

---

**Alternative via the Hurewicz theorem.** For any path-connected space $X$, the
first homology group is the abelianization of the fundamental group:
$$H_1(X;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \pi_1(X)^{\mathrm{ab}}.$$
Since $D$ is simply connected, $\pi_1(D) = 0$, so
$$H_1(D;\mathbb{Z}) \cong 0.$$
Thus every $1$-cycle — in particular every closed curve — is homologous to zero.

---

**Remark on the converse.** The converse is false in general: a domain in which
every closed curve is null-homologous need not be simply connected (e.g., a toru
s with a disk removed has $H_1 \neq 0$... actually, a better example: the "doubl
y punctured plane" type spaces can have $\pi_1$ non-abelian but $H_1 = 0$ is imp
ossible there; a cleaner example is that null-homology is weaker than null-homot
opy since $H_1$ only sees the abelianization of $\pi_1$). The implication proved
 above is strictly one-directional: simple connectivity forces null-homology, bu
t null-homology of all loops does not force simple connectivity.
