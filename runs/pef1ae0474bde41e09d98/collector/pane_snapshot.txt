## Proof

Let $f$ be an entire function, and consider $g(z) = f(1/z)$, which is holomorphi
c on $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$.

**Necessity.** Suppose $0$ is a removable singularity of $g$. By the Riemann rem
ovable singularity theorem, $g$ is bounded in some punctured neighborhood of $0$
; i.e., there exist $M>0$ and $\varepsilon>0$ such that
$$|g(z)| = |f(1/z)| \le M \quad \text{for all } 0<|z|<\varepsilon.$$
Setting $w = 1/z$, this becomes
$$|f(w)| \le M \quad \text{for all } |w| > 1/\varepsilon.$$
On the other hand, since $f$ is entire, it is continuous on the closed disk $\ov
erline{D(0, 1/\varepsilon)}$, which is compact, so $f$ is bounded there: $|f(w)|
 \le M'$ for $|w|\le 1/\varepsilon$. Combining both bounds,
$$|f(w)| \le \max(M, M') \quad \text{for all } w\in\mathbb{C}.$$
Thus $f$ is a bounded entire function. By Liouville's theorem, $f$ is constant.

**Sufficiency.** Conversely, if $f$ is constant, say $f(z)\equiv c$, then $g(z)
= f(1/z) = c$ for all $z\ne 0$, and this extends holomorphically to $0$ by setti
ng $g(0)=c$. Hence $0$ is a removable singularity of $g$.

**Conclusion.** The entire functions $f$ for which $0$ is a removable singularit
y of $f(1/z)$ are exactly the constant functions.
