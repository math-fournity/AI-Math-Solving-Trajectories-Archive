## Proof

**Setup.** Let $u = tx$, so $x = u/t$. The equation $x^2 + \tfrac{1}{2} = \cos(t
x)$ becomes
$$\frac{u^2}{t^2} + \frac{1}{2} = \cos(u). \tag{1}$$
The least positive root $x(t)$ corresponds to the least positive $u = u(t)$ sati
sfying (1).

**Existence and uniqueness of the least positive root.** Define
$$g_t(u) = \cos(u) - \frac{1}{2} - \frac{u^2}{t^2}.$$
We have $g_t(0) = 1 - \tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{1}{2} > 0$ and $g_t(\pi/3) = \tfrac{
1}{2} - \tfrac{1}{2} - \tfrac{\pi^2}{9t^2} = -\tfrac{\pi^2}{9t^2} < 0$. By the I
ntermediate Value Theorem, there exists a root in $(0, \pi/3)$.

On $(0, \pi/3)$, $\cos(u)$ is strictly decreasing (since $\sin(u) > 0$) while $\
tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{u^2}{t^2}$ is strictly increasing. Hence they cross **exact
ly once** in $(0, \pi/3)$, giving a unique root $u(t) \in (0, \pi/3)$.

This is the least positive root: for $u \in (0, \pi/3)$, $\cos(u) > 1/2$, so equ
ation (1) can be satisfied; for $u \in [\pi/3, 5\pi/3]$, $\cos(u) \le 1/2 \le 1/
2 + u^2/t^2$, so no root exists there. Thus the first positive root lies in $(0,
 \pi/3)$.

**Convergence.** Since $0 < u(t) < \pi/3$ for all $t$, the family $\{u(t)\}$ is
bounded. Let $\{t_n\} \to \infty$ be any subsequence with $u(t_n) \to u^*$. Then
 $\tfrac{u(t_n)^2}{t_n^2} \to 0$, and taking the limit in (1):
$$\frac{1}{2} = \cos(u^*).$$
Since $u^* \in [0, \pi/3]$ and $\cos(u) = 1/2$ on $[0, \pi/3]$ only at $u = \pi/
3$, we conclude $u^* = \pi/3$.

Every convergent subsequence of $u(t)$ tends to $\pi/3$, so $u(t) \to \pi/3$ as
$t \to \infty$.

**Conclusion.** Since $tx(t) = u(t) \to \pi/3$,

$$\lim_{t \to \infty} t\,x(t) = \frac{\pi}{3}.$$
