No. Here is a justification by counterexample.

**Claim.** The uniform limit of real-analytic functions on an open interval need
 not be real-analytic.

**Construction.** Consider the open interval $(-1,1)$ and the function
$$f(x)=|x|.$$
This function is continuous on $(-1,1)$ but is **not** real-analytic there: at t
he origin it is not differentiable (the left derivative is $-1$ and the right de
rivative is $+1$), whereas every real-analytic function is infinitely differenti
able at every point of its domain. Hence $f$ cannot be analytic on $(-1,1)$.

It remains to exhibit $f$ as a uniform limit of analytic functions on $(-1,1)$.

By the **Weierstrass Approximation Theorem**, every continuous function on a clo
sed bounded interval $[\alpha,\beta]$ is the uniform limit of polynomials on $[\
alpha,\beta]$. Applying this to $|x|$ on $[-1+\varepsilon,\,1-\varepsilon]$ for
each $\varepsilon>0$ is not quite enough; we need a single sequence converging u
niformly on the whole open interval. We argue as follows.

The function $|x|$ is continuous on $[-1,1]$, so by Weierstrass there exist poly
nomials $P_n$ with
$$\sup_{x\in[-1,1]}|P_n(x)-|x||\;\to\;0.$$
In particular,
$$\sup_{x\in(-1,1)}|P_n(x)-|x||\;\to\;0,$$
so $P_n\to |x|$ uniformly on the open interval $(-1,1)$.

Each $P_n$ is a polynomial, hence real-analytic (indeed entire) on $(-1,1)$. Yet
 the limit $f(x)=|x|$ is not analytic on $(-1,1)$, since it is not differentiabl
e at $0$.

**Conclusion.** Uniform convergence of analytic functions on an open interval do
es **not** preserve analyticity. The answer to the question is **no**.
