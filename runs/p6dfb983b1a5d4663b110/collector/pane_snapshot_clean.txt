## Solution

**Definition (x-visibility).** Two lattice points $P, Q \in \mathbb{Z}^d$ are *x
-visible* if the open line segment $\overline{PQ}$ contains at most $x$ lattice
points. If $v = Q - P$ and $g = \gcd(v_1, \dots, v_d)$, the lattice points on th
e open segment are $P + \frac{k}{g}v$ for $k=1,\dots,g-1$, so there are exactly
$g-1$ of them. Thus $P,Q$ are $x$-visible $\iff g - 1 \le x \iff \gcd(Q-P) \le x
+1$.

**Claim.** $g(x,d) = (x+2)^d$ for all integers $x \ge 0$ and $d \ge 1$.

---

### Lower Bound: $g(x,d) \ge (x+2)^d$

Consider the grid $S = \{0, 1, \dots, x+1\}^d \subset \mathbb{Z}^d$, which conta
ins $(x+2)^d$ points.

For any two distinct points $P, Q \in S$, the difference $v = Q - P$ satisfies $
|v_i| \le x+1$ for every coordinate $i$. Since $v \ne 0$, some coordinate $v_j$
is nonzero, and:

$$\gcd(v_1, \dots, v_d) \le |v_j| \le x+1.$$

Therefore every pair in $S$ is $x$-visible, giving $g(x,d) \ge (x+2)^d$.

---

### Upper Bound: $g(x,d) \le (x+2)^d$

Let $S \subset \mathbb{Z}^d$ be any set of mutually $x$-visible points. We show
that no two points of $S$ can share the same residue class modulo $m := x + 2$.

**Suppose for contradiction** that $P, Q \in S$ are distinct with $P \equiv Q \p
mod{m}$. Then every coordinate of $v = Q - P$ is divisible by $m = x+2$, so:

$$\gcd(v) \ge x + 2 > x + 1.$$

This means $\gcd(Q - P) \ge x+2$, so the open segment $\overline{PQ}$ contains a
t least $x+1$ lattice points—exceeding the allowed $x$. Thus $P$ and $Q$ are **n
ot** $x$-visible, contradicting the mutual $x$-visibility of $S$.

Therefore all points of $S$ occupy **distinct** residue classes modulo $x+2$. Si
nce $\mathbb{Z}^d / (x+2)\mathbb{Z}^d$ has exactly $(x+2)^d$ residue classes:

$$|S| \le (x+2)^d.$$

---

### Conclusion

Combining both bounds:

$$\boxed{g(x,d) = (x+2)^d.}$$
