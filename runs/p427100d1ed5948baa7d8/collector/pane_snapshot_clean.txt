## Proof

We determine the cardinality of the set of subrings of $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Upper bound.** Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is countable, its power set $\mathcal{P}(\ma
thbb{Q})$ has cardinality $2^{\aleph_0}$. The collection of subrings of $\mathbb
{Q}$ is a subset of $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Q})$, so it has cardinality $\leq 2^{\a
leph_0}$.

**Lower bound.** We exhibit $2^{\aleph_0}$ distinct subrings. For each set of pr
imes $S$, define
$$R_S = \mathbb{Z}\!\left[\tfrac{1}{p} : p \in S\right] = \left\{\frac{a}{b} \in
 \mathbb{Q} : a \in \mathbb{Z},\; b \text{ is a product of primes from } S\right
\}$$
(with $R_\varnothing = \mathbb{Z}$). Each $R_S$ is a subring of $\mathbb{Q}$ con
taining $1$. If $S \neq S'$, say $p \in S \setminus S'$, then $1/p \in R_S$ but
$1/p \notin R_{S'}$ (every element of $R_{S'}$ in lowest terms has denominator d
ivisible only by primes in $S'$). Hence the $R_S$ are pairwise distinct. Since t
here are countably many primes, the number of subsets $S$ is $2^{\aleph_0}$, giv
ing $2^{\aleph_0}$ distinct subrings.

**Characterization (completeness).** Every subring $R$ of $\mathbb{Q}$ containin
g $1$ equals some $R_S$. Clearly $\mathbb{Z} \subseteq R$. Let $S = \{p \text{ p
rime} : 1/p \in R\}$; then $R_S \subseteq R$. Conversely, if $a/b \in R$ (in low
est terms), pick $x, y \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $ax + by = 1$. Then $x \cdot (a/b) +
 y = (ax+by)/b = 1/b \in R$, and for each prime $p_i \mid b$, since $b/p_i \in \
mathbb{Z} \subseteq R$, we get $(1/b)\cdot(b/p_i) = 1/p_i \in R$, so $p_i \in S$
. Thus $a/b \in R_S$, giving $R \subseteq R_S$, hence $R = R_S$.

This establishes a bijection between subsets of the (countable) set of primes an
d unital subrings of $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Conclusion.** Combining the upper and lower bounds, the set of subrings of $\m
athbb{Q}$ has cardinality exactly $\boxed{2^{\aleph_0}}$ (the cardinality of the
 continuum).
