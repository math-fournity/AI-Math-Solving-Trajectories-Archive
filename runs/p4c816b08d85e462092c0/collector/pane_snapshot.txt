## Solution

We seek $x$ with both real and imaginary parts satisfying
$$\arccos\!\left(\frac{x}{2}+\frac{1}{2x}\right)=-x^{\pi}.$$

**Key substitution.** Try $x = e^{i\theta}$ with $\theta \in [0,\pi]$ (so that $
x$ lies on the unit circle and has both real and imaginary parts for $\theta\in(
0,\pi/2)\cup(\pi/2,\pi)$).

Then
$$\frac{x}{2}+\frac{1}{2x}=\frac{e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}}{2}=\cos\theta.$$

Since $\theta\in[0,\pi]$, the principal branch gives $\arccos(\cos\theta)=\theta
$.

Also $x^{\pi}=(e^{i\theta})^{\pi}=e^{i\pi\theta}$ (using the principal logarithm
 $\log(e^{i\theta})=i\theta$, valid for $\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]$).

The equation becomes
$$\theta = -e^{i\pi\theta}.$$

**Solving $\theta = -e^{i\pi\theta}$.** Since $\theta$ is real, the right side $
-e^{i\pi\theta}=-\cos(\pi\theta)-i\sin(\pi\theta)$ must also be real, forcing $\
sin(\pi\theta)=0$, i.e. $\theta\in\mathbb{Z}$.

Within $[0,\pi]$, the candidates are $\theta\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ (since $\pi\approx 3
.14159$).

- $\theta=0$: $-e^{0}=-1\neq 0$. ✗
- $\theta=1$: $-e^{i\pi}=-(-1)=1=\theta$. ✓
- $\theta=2$: $-e^{i2\pi}=-1\neq 2$. ✗
- $\theta=3$: $-e^{i3\pi}=-(-1)=1\neq 3$. ✗

So $\theta=1$ is the unique solution.

**Verification.** With $x = e^{i}=\cos 1+i\sin 1$:

1. $\dfrac{x}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2x}=\dfrac{e^{i}+e^{-i}}{2}=\cos 1$. ✓
2. $\arccos(\cos 1)=1$ (since $1\in[0,\pi]$). ✓
3. $x^{\pi}=e^{i\pi}=\cos\pi+i\sin\pi=-1$, so $-x^{\pi}=1$. ✓
4. $\arccos\!\left(\frac{x}{2}+\frac{1}{2x}\right)=1=-x^{\pi}$. ✓
5. $x=\cos 1+i\sin 1$ has $\operatorname{Re}x=\cos 1\approx 0.5403$ and $\operat
orname{Im}x=\sin 1\approx 0.8415$, both nonzero. ✓

**The exact value is**

$$\boxed{x=e^{i}=\cos 1+i\sin 1}.$$
