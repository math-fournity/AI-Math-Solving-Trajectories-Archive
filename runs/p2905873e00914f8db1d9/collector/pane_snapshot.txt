## Proof

Place the circumcircle $\omega$ as the unit circle in the complex plane with cen
ter $O$ at the origin. Let $a, b, c$ be the complex coordinates of $A, B, C$ wit
h $|a|=|b|=|c|=1$.

**Key points.**
- $D = -a$ (the second intersection of line $AO$ with $\omega$, i.e. the antipod
e of $A$).
- $H = a+b+c$ (orthocenter on the unit circumcircle).
- $M = (b+c)/2$ (midpoint of $BC$).
- $T = \dfrac{2bc}{b+c}$ (intersection of tangents at $B$ and $C$, derived below
).

**Tangents at $B$ and $C$.** The tangent at $p$ on the unit circle is $z\bar p +
 \bar z p = 2$. Solving for $p=b$ and $p=c$:
$$z/b + z/c = 2 \implies z = \frac{2bc}{b+c} =: T.$$

**Feet $X, Y$ of perpendiculars from $H$.** The chord through points $p,q$ on th
e unit circle has equation $z + pq\,\bar z = p+q$. For line $BD$ (through $b$ an
d $-a$):
$$z - ab\,\bar z = b - a.$$
The foot $X$ of the perpendicular from $h$ to this line satisfies (i) $x - ab\,\
bar x = b-a$ (on the line) and (ii) $x - h = -ab(\bar x - \bar h)$ (perpendicula
rity). Adding:
$$2x = h + ab\,\bar h + b - a.$$
With $h = a+b+c$ and $ab\,\bar h = ab(\tfrac1a+\tfrac1b+\tfrac1c) = b + a + \tfr
ac{ab}{c}$:
$$x = \frac{a + 3b + c + ab/c}{2}.$$
By symmetry ($b \leftrightarrow c$):
$$y = \frac{a + b + 3c + ac/b}{2}.$$

**Circumcenter $J$ of $\triangle MXY$.** We claim
$$J = \frac{H+T}{2} = \frac{1}{2}\!\left(a+b+c + \frac{2bc}{b+c}\right).$$
Set $S = ab + ac + 2bc = a(b+c)+2bc$. Then:
- $j - m = \dfrac{S}{2(b+c)}$,
- $j - x = -\dfrac{b}{c}\,(j - m)$, so $|j-x| = |j-m|$ since $|b/c|=1$,
- $j - y = -\dfrac{c}{b}\,(j - m)$, so $|j-y| = |j-m|$ since $|c/b|=1$.

Hence $J=(H+T)/2$ is equidistant from $M, X, Y$, confirming it is the circumcent
er of $\triangle MXY$.

**Concurrency and ratio.** Since $J = (H+T)/2$, the point $J$ is the **midpoint*
* of segment $HT$. Therefore $H, J, T$ are collinear, so line $HJ$ passes throug
h $T$ — the intersection of the tangents to $\omega$ at $B$ and $C$. The three l
ines (line $HJ$, tangent at $B$, tangent at $C$) are concurrent at $T$.

Since $J$ is the midpoint of $HT$:

$$\frac{HT}{HJ} = \boxed{2}.$$
