## Proof

**Setup.** Since $\angle BDA = 90^\circ$ and $BD = \sqrt{57}$, point $D$ lies on
 line $AC$ (the only side consistent with the isosceles condition $AB = AC$; pla
cing $D$ on line $BC$ would force $D$ to be the midpoint of $BC$, giving $BD = D
C$, contradicting $BD = \sqrt{57}$ and $DC \in \mathbb{Z}$).

Let $AD = m$ and $DC = n$, both positive integers. Since $\angle BDA = 90^\circ$
, triangle $ABD$ is right-angled at $D$, so by the Pythagorean theorem:

$$AB^2 = AD^2 + BD^2 = m^2 + 57.$$

Since $AB = AC$ and $D$ lies on segment $AC$, we have $AC = m + n$, so:

$$(m+n)^2 = m^2 + 57.$$

Expanding:

$$m^2 + 2mn + n^2 = m^2 + 57 \implies n(2m + n) = 57.$$

**Factoring.** Since $m, n$ are positive integers, both $n$ and $2m+n$ are posit
ive integers with $n < 2m+n$. The factorizations of $57$ are:

$$57 = 1 \times 57 = 3 \times 19.$$

| $n$ | $2m+n$ | $m$ | $AC = m+n$ |
|-----|--------|-----|-------------|
| $1$ | $57$   | $28$ | $29$ |
| $3$ | $19$   | $8$  | $11$ |

(The factorizations $19 \times 3$ and $57 \times 1$ give $n > 2m+n$, forcing $m
< 0$, which is invalid.)

**Verification of $AC = 11$.** With $m = 8$, $n = 3$:
- $AD = 8$, $DC = 3$ (both integers). ✓
- $AB^2 = 64 + 57 = 121$, so $AB = 11 = AC$. ✓
- $BC^2 = BD^2 + DC^2 = 57 + 9 = 66$, so $BC = \sqrt{66}$.
- Triangle inequality: $11 + 11 > \sqrt{66}$ and $11 + \sqrt{66} > 11$. ✓

**Minimality.** The only valid solutions are $AC = 29$ and $AC = 11$, so the min
imum is $AC = 11$.

$$\boxed{AC = 11}$$
