## Evaluation of $\displaystyle\mathrm{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(cx
)-\sin(dx)}{x^2}\,dx$

**Setup.** Consider the complex function
$$
f(z)=\frac{e^{icz}-e^{idz}}{z^2},
$$
which is meromorphic on $\mathbb{C}$ with a singularity at $z=0$. Since $c,d>0$,
 both $e^{icz}$ and $e^{idz}$ decay exponentially in the **upper half-plane** ($
\operatorname{Im}z>0$), so we close our contour there.

**Laurent expansion at the origin.** Expanding:
$$
e^{icz}-e^{idz}=\bigl(1+icz-\tfrac{c^{2}z^{2}}{2}+\cdots\bigr)-\bigl(1+idz-\tfra
c{d^{2}z^{2}}{2}+\cdots\bigr)=i(c-d)z-\tfrac{c^{2}-d^{2}}{2}z^{2}+\cdots
$$
Hence
$$
f(z)=\frac{i(c-d)}{z}-\frac{c^{2}-d^{2}}{2}+\cdots,
$$
so $z=0$ is a **simple pole** with residue
$$
\operatorname{Res}(f,0)=i(c-d).
$$

**The indented contour.** Let $\Gamma$ be the positively-oriented boundary of th
e upper half-disk of radius $R$ with a small semicircular indentation of radius
$\varepsilon$ around the origin:
- $\gamma_1$: the real segment $[-R,-\varepsilon]$,
- $C_\varepsilon$: the small semicircle $z=\varepsilon e^{i\theta}$, $\theta\in[
\pi,0]$ (clockwise, in the UHP, from $-\varepsilon$ to $+\varepsilon$),
- $\gamma_2$: the real segment $[\varepsilon,R]$,
- $C_R$: the large semicircle $z=Re^{i\theta}$, $\theta\in[0,\pi]$ (counterclock
wise).

The pole at $z=0$ lies **outside** $\Gamma$ (excluded by the indentation), so by
 **Cauchy's theorem**:
$$
\int_{\gamma_1}f(z)\,dz+\int_{C_\varepsilon}f(z)\,dz+\int_{\gamma_2}f(z)\,dz+\in
t_{C_R}f(z)\,dz=0. \tag{1}
$$

**Large arc vanishes.** On $C_R$, $|e^{icz}|=e^{-cR\sin\theta}\le 1$ and $|e^{id
z}|\le 1$ for $\theta\in[0,\pi]$, and $|f(z)|\le \frac{2}{R^2}$. By Jordan's lem
ma (or direct estimate), $\int_{C_R}f(z)\,dz\to 0$ as $R\to\infty$.

**Small arc contribution.** Near $z=0$, $f(z)=\frac{i(c-d)}{z}+O(1)$. Parametriz
e $C_\varepsilon$: $z=\varepsilon e^{i\theta}$, $\theta:\pi\to 0$ (clockwise), $
dz=i\varepsilon e^{i\theta}d\theta$:
$$
\int_{C_\varepsilon}f(z)\,dz=\int_{\pi}^{0}\frac{i(c-d)}{\varepsilon e^{i\theta}
}\,i\varepsilon e^{i\theta}\,d\theta+O(\varepsilon)=-(c-d)\int_{\pi}^{0}d\theta+
O(\varepsilon)=\pi(c-d)+O(\varepsilon).
$$
Equivalently, since $C_\varepsilon$ is a **clockwise** semicircle, $\int_{C_\var
epsilon}f\,dz\to -i\pi\operatorname{Res}(f,0)=-i\pi\cdot i(c-d)=\pi(c-d)$.

**Take the limits** $R\to\infty$, $\varepsilon\to 0$ in (1):
$$
\operatorname{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{icx}-e^{idx}}{x^{2}}\,dx\;+\;
\pi(c-d)\;=\;0,
$$
so
$$
\operatorname{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{icx}-e^{idx}}{x^{2}}\,dx=\pi(
d-c). \tag{2}
$$

**Extract the imaginary part.** Writing $e^{ikx}=\cos(kx)+i\sin(kx)$,
$$
\frac{e^{icx}-e^{idx}}{x^{2}}=\underbrace{\frac{\cos(cx)-\cos(dx)}{x^{2}}}_{\tex
t{real, even}}\;+\;i\underbrace{\frac{\sin(cx)-\sin(dx)}{x^{2}}}_{\text{imaginar
y, odd}}.
$$
The right-hand side of (2), $\pi(d-c)$, is **purely real**. Equating imaginary p
arts of (2):
$$
\operatorname{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(cx)-\sin(dx)}{x^{2}}\,dx=0.
$$

**Consistency check.** The integrand $\frac{\sin(cx)-\sin(dx)}{x^{2}}$ is an **o
dd** function of $x$ (since $\sin$ is odd and $x^{2}$ is even), so its symmetric
 principal value vanishes — exactly as the contour calculation confirms. (Equati
ng real parts also recovers the classical result $\operatorname{PV}\!\int_{-\inf
ty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos(cx)-\cos(dx)}{x^{2}}\,dx=\pi(d-c)$, validating the method
.)

**Remark on convergence.** Near $x=0$, $\sin(cx)-\sin(dx)\sim(c-d)x$, so the int
egrand behaves like $(c-d)/x$; the ordinary improper integral diverges for $c\ne
q d$. The indented contour naturally produces the **Cauchy principal value**, wh
ich is the meaningful interpretation here.
