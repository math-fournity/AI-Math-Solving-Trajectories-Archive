**No**, such an $r > 0$ does not exist.

**Proof.** Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists $r > 0$ such that for e
very holomorphic $f: \mathbb{D} \to \mathbb{C}$ with $f(0) = 0$, we have $D_r(0)
 \subseteq f(\mathbb{D})$.

For any $\varepsilon > 0$, consider the function
$$f_\varepsilon(z) = \varepsilon z.$$
This is holomorphic on $\mathbb{D}$, and $f_\varepsilon(0) = 0$. Its image is
$$f_\varepsilon(\mathbb{D}) = D_\varepsilon(0).$$

Choose $\varepsilon < r$ (e.g., $\varepsilon = r/2$). Then $f_\varepsilon(\mathb
b{D}) = D_\varepsilon(0)$, which does **not** contain $D_r(0)$, since the point
$r \cdot \tfrac{3}{4} \in D_r(0)$ satisfies $|r \cdot \tfrac{3}{4}| = \tfrac{3r}
{4} > \tfrac{r}{2} = \varepsilon$, so it lies outside $D_\varepsilon(0)$.

This contradicts the assumed property. Hence no such $r > 0$ exists. $\blacksqua
