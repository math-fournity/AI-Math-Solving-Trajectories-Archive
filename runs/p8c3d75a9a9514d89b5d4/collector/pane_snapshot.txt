# Derivation of the Vacuum Einstein Field Equations from the Hilbert Action

## Answer-Leak Check

termediate steps are revealed. No leak detected. Proceeding.

## Setup

The Hilbert action (with the conventional prefactor $\frac{1}{2\kappa}$ omitted,
 since it is irrelevant to the vacuum equations) is

$$S_H[g] \;=\; \int \sqrt{-g}\; R\; d^n x,$$

where $g = \det(g_{\mu\nu})$, $R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}$ is the Ricci scalar, an
d the integration is over a compact region with the boundary condition that the
metric variation $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ and its first derivatives vanish on the bou
ndary.

The equations of motion are obtained from the stationarity condition

$$\delta S_H \;=\; \int \delta\!\left(\sqrt{-g}\,R\right) d^n x \;=\; 0 \qquad \
text{for arbitrary } \delta g^{\mu\nu}.$$

We split the variation into two pieces:

$$\delta\!\left(\sqrt{-g}\,R\right) \;=\; \delta\!\left(\sqrt{-g}\right) R \;+\;
 \sqrt{-g}\;\delta R.$$

---

## Step 1 — Variation of the volume element $\sqrt{-g}$

Using $\delta g = g\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\delta g_{\mu\nu}$ and the identity (obtained b
y varying $g_{\mu\alpha}g^{\alpha\nu}=\delta_\mu^{\nu}$)

$$\delta g_{\mu\nu} = -\,g_{\mu\alpha}\,g_{\nu\beta}\,\delta g^{\alpha\beta}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
g^{\mu\nu}\,\delta g_{\mu\nu} = -\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu},$$

we get

$$\delta g = -\,g\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}.$$

Since $-g = (\sqrt{-g})^2$,

$$\delta\!\left(\sqrt{-g}\right) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{-g}}\,(-\delta g)
= \frac{1}{2\sqrt{-g}}\,g\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}
= -\,\frac{1}{2}\,\sqrt{-g}\;g_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}.$$

Hence

$$\delta\!\left(\sqrt{-g}\right) R \;=\; -\,\frac{1}{2}\,\sqrt{-g}\;g_{\mu\nu}\,
R\;\delta g^{\mu\nu}.$$

---

## Step 2 — Variation of the Ricci scalar $R$

Write $R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}$, so

$$\delta R \;=\; R_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu} \;+\; g^{\mu\nu}\,\delta R_{\mu\n
u}.$$

The second term is handled by the **Palatini identity**. The variation of the Ch
ristoffel symbol,

$$\delta\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\;\mu\nu}
= \tfrac{1}{2}g^{\lambda\rho}\!\left(\nabla_\mu\,\delta g_{\nu\rho}
+ \nabla_\nu\,\delta g_{\mu\rho}
- \nabla_\rho\,\delta g_{\mu\nu}\right),$$

is a genuine tensor (the difference of two connections). A direct computation of
 $\delta R_{\mu\nu}=\delta(\partial_\lambda\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}-\dots)$, usin
g that $\delta\Gamma$ is a tensor so that ordinary derivatives may be replaced b
y covariant ones, gives

$$\delta R_{\mu\nu}
= \nabla_\lambda\!\left(\delta\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\;\mu\nu}\right)
- \nabla_\nu\!\left(\delta\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\;\mu\lambda}\right).$$

Contracting with $g^{\mu\nu}$ and using metric compatibility $\nabla_\rho g^{\mu
\nu}=0$ to pull $g^{\mu\nu}$ inside the covariant derivatives:

$$g^{\mu\nu}\,\delta R_{\mu\nu}
= \nabla_\lambda\!\left(g^{\mu\nu}\,\delta\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\;\mu\nu}\right)
- \nabla_\nu\!\left(g^{\mu\nu}\,\delta\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\;\mu\lambda}\right).$$

Relabeling the dummy index $\nu\to\lambda$ in the second term,

$$g^{\mu\nu}\,\delta R_{\mu\nu}
= \nabla_\lambda\underbrace{\left(g^{\mu\nu}\,\delta\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\;\mu\nu}
- g^{\mu\lambda}\,\delta\Gamma^{\rho}_{\;\mu\rho}\right)}_{\displaystyle V^{\lam
bda}}
= \nabla_\lambda V^{\lambda}.$$

This is a **total covariant divergence**. By the covariant Gauss theorem,

$$\int \sqrt{-g}\;\nabla_\lambda V^{\lambda}\,d^n x
= \int \partial_\lambda\!\left(\sqrt{-g}\,V^{\lambda}\right) d^n x
= \oint_{\partial\Omega} V^{\lambda}\,d\Sigma_\lambda,$$

which reduces to a boundary term. With the boundary condition $\delta g^{\mu\nu}
\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0$ (and hence $\delta\Gamma\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0$), th
is boundary term vanishes. Therefore

$$\delta R \;=\; R_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu} \qquad\text{(up to a discarded bo
undary term)}.$$

---

## Step 3 — Assemble the variation

Combining Steps 1 and 2,

$$\delta\!\left(\sqrt{-g}\,R\right)
= -\,\frac{1}{2}\,\sqrt{-g}\;g_{\mu\nu}\,R\;\delta g^{\mu\nu}
\;+\; \sqrt{-g}\;R_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}
= \sqrt{-g}\left(R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac{1}{2}\,R\,g_{\mu\nu}\right)\delta g^{\mu\nu
}.$$

Thus

$$\delta S_H
= \int \sqrt{-g}\left(R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac{1}{2}\,R\,g_{\mu\nu}\right)\delta g^{\
mu\nu}\,d^n x.$$

---

## Step 4 — Stationarity gives the field equations

Since $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ is arbitrary (and $\sqrt{-g}>0$), the fundamental lemm
a of the calculus of variations yields

$$\boxed{\,R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac{1}{2}\,R\,g_{\mu\nu} \;=\; 0\,}.$$

This is the **vacuum Einstein field equation** $G_{\mu\nu}=0$, where $G_{\mu\nu}
=R_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac{1}{2}R\,g_{\mu\nu}$ is the Einstein tensor.

---

## Consistency check (trace)

Taking the trace with $g^{\mu\nu}$ in $n$ dimensions:

$$g^{\mu\nu}G_{\mu\nu} = R - \tfrac{n}{2}R = \left(1-\tfrac{n}{2}\right)R = 0.$$

For $n\neq 2$ this forces $R=0$, and substituting back gives $R_{\mu\nu}=0$ (vac
uum is Ricci-flat). The $n=2$ case is special: the Einstein tensor vanishes iden
tically ($G_{\mu\nu}\equiv 0$ in 2D), so the Hilbert action is topological there
 — consistent with the fact that the variation above produces $0=0$ when $n=2$.
For the physically relevant case $n=4$, the derivation yields the standard vacuu
m equations $R_{\mu\nu}=0$ as a consequence.

### Summary of the logical chain

1. Vary the action: $\delta S_H = \int\left[\delta(\sqrt{-g})\,R + \sqrt{-g}\,\d
elta R\right]d^nx$.
2. $\delta(\sqrt{-g}) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ —
 pure metric-variation identity.
3. $\delta R = R_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ — the Palatini piece $g^{\mu\nu}\d
elta R_{\mu\nu}$ is a total divergence $\nabla_\lambda V^\lambda$ and is discard

4. Collecting: $\delta S_H = \int\sqrt{-g}\left(R_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac{1}{2}Rg_{\mu\n
u}\right)\delta g^{\mu\nu}d^nx = 0$.
5. Arbitrariness of $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ $\Rightarrow$ $R_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac{1}{2}Rg
_{\mu\nu}=0$. $\blacksquare$
