## Solution

We evaluate
$$I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x^n \, e^{-\left(ax^2 + \frac{b}{x^2}\right)}\,dx,
$$
with $a, b > 0$ and $n$ an odd positive integer.

**Step 1. Convergence.** The integrand is defined for all $x \neq 0$. Near $x =
0$, the factor $e^{-b/x^2}$ decays faster than any power of $x$, so the integran
d (and its absolute value) is integrable there. As $|x| \to \infty$, the factor
$e^{-ax^2}$ decays faster than any polynomial grows, so the integrand is integra
ble at infinity. Hence the integral converges absolutely, and we may freely spli
t or change variables.

**Step 2. Parity.** The exponential factor is even in $x$:
$$e^{-\left(a(-x)^2 + \frac{b}{(-x)^2}\right)} = e^{-\left(ax^2 + \frac{b}{x^2}\
right)}.$$
Since $n$ is odd, $x^n$ is odd: $(-x)^n = -x^n$. Therefore the full integrand
$$f(x) = x^n \, e^{-\left(ax^2 + \frac{b}{x^2}\right)}$$
satisfies $f(-x) = -f(x)$; it is an **odd** function.

**Step 3. Symmetric domain.** The domain $(-\infty, \infty)$ is symmetric about
$0$. For any absolutely integrable odd function on a symmetric domain,
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx = \int_{-\infty}^{0} f(x)\,dx + \int_{0}^{\in
fty} f(x)\,dx = -\int_{0}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx + \int_{0}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx = 0.$$

(Formally: substitute $x \mapsto -u$ in the negative-half integral, using $f(-u)
 = -f(u)$.)

**Conclusion.** Since the integrand is odd and absolutely integrable on $\mathbb
{R}$,

$$\boxed{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x^n \, e^{-\left(ax^2 + \frac{b}{x^2}\right)}\,
dx = 0}$$

for every $a, b > 0$ and every odd positive integer $n$.
