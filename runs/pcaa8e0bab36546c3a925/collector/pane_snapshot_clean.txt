## Proof

Assume for contradiction that $n a_n \not\to 0$. Then there exist $\epsilon > 0$
 and a subsequence $\{n_j\}$ with $n_j \to \infty$ such that
$$
n_j\, a_{n_j} \ge \epsilon \quad\text{for all } j,
$$
i.e. $a_{n_j} \ge \dfrac{\epsilon}{n_j}$.

**Key observation.** The hypothesis $0 \le a_k \le 100\,a_n$ for $n \le k \le 2n
$ holds for *every* admissible pair $(n,k)$. Fix an index $k$ and let $n$ range
over integers with $\lceil k/2\rceil \le n \le k$. For every such $n$ we have $n
 \le k \le 2n$, so applying the hypothesis with this $n$ and this $k$ gives
$$
a_k \le 100\,a_n, \qquad\text{hence}\qquad a_n \ge \frac{a_k}{100}.
$$

**Applying the observation.** Set $k = n_j$. For every integer $n$ with $\lceil
n_j/2\rceil \le n \le n_j$ we obtain
$$
a_n \ge \frac{a_{n_j}}{100} \ge \frac{\epsilon}{100\,n_j}.
$$
The number of integers in the interval $[\lceil n_j/2\rceil,\, n_j]$ is $n_j - \
lceil n_j/2\rceil + 1 \ge n_j/2$ (for $n_j \ge 2$). Therefore
$$
\sum_{n=\lceil n_j/2\rceil}^{n_j} a_n \;\ge\; \frac{n_j}{2}\cdot\frac{\epsilon}{
100\,n_j} \;=\; \frac{\epsilon}{200}.
\tag{$*$}
$$

**Contradiction with convergence.** Since $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n$ converges, i
ts tail tends to zero: for every $\delta > 0$ there exists $N$ such that $\sum_{
n=N}^{\infty} a_n < \delta$. Take $\delta = \epsilon/200$. Because $n_j \to \inf
ty$, for all sufficiently large $j$ we have $\lceil n_j/2\rceil \ge N$, and cons
equently
$$
\sum_{n=\lceil n_j/2\rceil}^{n_j} a_n \;\le\; \sum_{n=N}^{\infty} a_n \;<\; \fra
c{\epsilon}{200}.
$$
This contradicts $(*)$.

The contradiction shows that no such subsequence $\{n_j\}$ exists; hence
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty} n\,a_n = 0.
$$
