# Galois Group of $x^6 - 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$

## Setup

Let $\alpha = \sqrt[6]{2}$ and $\omega = e^{2\pi i/6} = e^{\pi i/3}$, a primitiv
e 6th root of unity. The roots of $x^6 - 2$ are
$$\alpha,\ \alpha\omega,\ \alpha\omega^2,\ \alpha\omega^3,\ \alpha\omega^4,\ \al
pha\omega^5.$$
The splitting field is $K = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha, \omega)$.

## Irreducibility and degrees

**$x^6 - 2$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$** by Eisenstein's criterion at $p =
 2$, so $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) : \mathbb{Q}] = 6$.

**$\omega = e^{\pi i/3} = \tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{\sqrt{-3}}{2}$**, so $\mathbb{Q}
(\omega) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$ and $[\mathbb{Q}(\omega) : \mathbb{Q}] = 2$ (t
he 6th cyclotomic polynomial is $\Phi_6(x) = x^2 - x + 1$).

## The intersection $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) \cap \mathbb{Q}(\omega)$

Since $\alpha = \sqrt[6]{2}$ is real, $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) \subset \mathbb{R}$. O
n the other hand $\mathbb{Q}(\omega) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}) \not\subset \mathbb
{R}$. The intersection is a subfield of $\mathbb{Q}(\omega)$, hence either $\mat
hbb{Q}$ or $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$; but it is also a subfield of $\mathbb{Q}(\al
pha) \subset \mathbb{R}$, so it cannot be $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$. Therefore
$$\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) \cap \mathbb{Q}(\omega) = \mathbb{Q}.$$

## Order of the Galois group

By the tower law and the trivial intersection,
$$ : \mathbb{Q}] = [\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}]\cdot[\mathbb{Q}(\omega):\ma
thbb{Q}] = 6 \cdot 2 = 12.$$
So $|\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})| = 12$.

## Structure of the Galois group

Any $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ is determined by its action on
 $\alpha$ and $\omega$:
- $\sigma(\alpha)^6 = \sigma(2) = 2$, so $\sigma(\alpha) = \alpha\omega^j$ for s
ome $j \in \{0,1,2,3,4,5\}$.
- $\sigma(\omega)$ must be a primitive 6th root of unity, so $\sigma(\omega) = \
omega^k$ for $k \in \{1, 5\}$ (i.e. $\omega$ or $\omega^{-1}$).

This gives $6 \times 2 = 12$ possibilities, all realized since $|G| = 12$. Defin
e:
- $\sigma$: $\sigma(\alpha) = \alpha\omega$, $\sigma(\omega) = \omega$ — this ha
s **order 6**.
- $\tau$: $\tau(\alpha) = \alpha$, $\tau(\omega) = \omega^{-1}$ — this has **ord
er 2** (complex conjugation restricted to $K$).

**Relation.** Compute $\tau\sigma\tau$:
- On $\omega$: $\tau(\omega) = \omega^{-1}$, $\sigma(\omega^{-1}) = \omega^{-1}$
 (since $\sigma$ fixes $\omega$), $\tau(\omega^{-1}) = \omega$. So $\tau\sigma\t
au(\omega) = \omega = \sigma^{-1}(\omega)$. ✓
- On $\alpha$: $\tau(\alpha) = \alpha$, $\sigma(\alpha) = \alpha\omega$, $\tau(\
alpha\omega) = \alpha\cdot\omega^{-1} = \sigma^{-1}(\alpha)$. ✓

Hence $\tau\sigma\tau = \sigma^{-1}$, the defining relation of the dihedral grou
p.

## Conclusion

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(x^6 - 2\,/\,\mathbb{Q}) \;\cong\; D_6 \;=\; \langle
\sigma, \tau \mid \sigma^6 = \tau^2 = 1,\ \tau\sigma\tau = \sigma^{-1}\rangle,}$
$

the **dihedral group of order 12** (symmetries of a regular hexagon). It is gene
rated by a rotation $\sigma$ of order 6 (cycling the roots) and a reflection $\t
au$ of order 2 (complex conjugation), with the dihedral relation $\tau\sigma\tau
 = \sigma^{-1}$.
