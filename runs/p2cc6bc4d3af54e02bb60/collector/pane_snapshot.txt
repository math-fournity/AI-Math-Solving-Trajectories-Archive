## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. If $R_1 \subset R_2$ are real closed fields (with $R_2$ an exten
sion of $R_1$), then every element of $R_2$ that is algebraic over $R_1$ already
 lies in $R_1$. Equivalently, $R_1$ is **algebraically closed in $R_2$**.

We use three standard facts about real closed fields:

1. **Unique, definable ordering.** A real closed field $R$ carries a unique orde
ring, characterized by $x \ge 0 \iff x$ is a square in $R$. Consequently any fie
ld embedding $R_1 \hookrightarrow R_2$ between real closed fields is automatical
ly **order-preserving**: an element is negative in $R_1$ iff its image is negati
ve in $R_2$.

2. **Shape of irreducibles.** Over a real closed field $R$, every irreducible po
lynomial is either linear, or quadratic of the form $x^2+bx+c$ with discriminant
 $\Delta = b^2-4c < 0$ (so it has no root in $R$). In particular every odd-degre
e polynomial has a root in $R$.

3. **Formal reality.** A real closed field is formally real: a negative element
is **not** a square. (Equivalently $-1$ is not a sum of squares, so sums of squa
res are $\ge 0$.)

---

Let $a \in R_2$ be algebraic over $R_1$, and let $p(x) \in R_1[x]$ be its (monic
, irreducible) minimal polynomial over $R_1$.

By Fact 2 applied to the real closed field $R_1$, the irreducible polynomial $p$
 has degree $1$ or $2$.

**Case $\deg p = 1$.** Then $p(x) = x - r$ for some $r \in R_1$, so $a = r \in R
_1$. Done.

**Case $\deg p = 2$.** Write $p(x) = x^2 + bx + c$ with $b, c \in R_1$. Since $p
$ is irreducible over $R_1$, Fact 2 gives
$$\Delta := b^2 - 4c < 0 \quad \text{in } R_1.$$
By Fact 1 the inclusion $R_1 \hookrightarrow R_2$ is order-preserving, so $\Delt
a < 0$ **in $R_2$** as well.

The roots of $p$ in any extension field are $\tfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{\Delta}}{2}$. B
ut $\Delta < 0$ in $R_2$, and by Fact 3 (formal reality of the real closed field
 $R_2$) a negative element of $R_2$ is not a square. Hence $\sqrt{\Delta} \notin
 R_2$, so $p$ has **no root in $R_2$**.

This contradicts the assumption that $a \in R_2$ is a root of $p$. Therefore Cas
e $\deg p = 2$ cannot occur.

---

The only remaining possibility is $\deg p = 1$, whence $a \in R_1$.

Since $a \in R_2$ algebraic over $R_1$ was arbitrary, $R_1$ contains **all** alg
ebraic elements of $R_2$ (over $R_1$); i.e., $R_1$ is algebraically closed in $R
_2$. $\blacksquare$
