## Solution

We are asked to find the sum of
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n, \qquad a_n = \left(2\pi n + \frac{1}{n}\right)\left|\
sin\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\right|.$$

**Step 1. Remove the absolute value.** For every $n \ge 1$, we have $0 < \frac{1
}{n} \le 1 < \pi$, so $\sin\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right) > 0$. Hence
$$\left|\sin\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\right| = \sin\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right),$
$
and
$$a_n = \left(2\pi n + \frac{1}{n}\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right) = 2\pi n
\sin\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right) + \frac{\sin(1/n)}{n}.$$

**Step 2. Compute the limit of the general term.** Using the standard limit $\li
m_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1$ with $x = 1/n \to 0$:
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} 2\pi n\sin\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right) = 2\pi\lim_{n\to\infty}
\frac{\sin(1/n)}{1/n} = 2\pi\cdot 1 = 2\pi.$$
Also,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sin(1/n)}{n} = \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\cdot\frac{
\sin(1/n)}{1/n}\cdot\frac{1}{n}\cdot n = 0.$$
Therefore
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} a_n = 2\pi + 0 = 2\pi \neq 0.$$

**Step 3. Apply the $n$-th term (divergence) test.** A necessary condition for t
he series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n$ to converge is $\lim_{n\to\infty} a_n = 0$.
Since
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} a_n = 2\pi \neq 0,$$
the series **diverges**.

**Step 4. Confirm the sign of divergence.** Every term satisfies $a_n > 0$ (both
 factors are positive for $n\ge 1$). Moreover, since $a_n \to 2\pi > 0$, there e
xists $N$ such that $a_n \ge \pi$ for all $n \ge N$. Consequently the partial su
ms grow without bound:
$$\sum_{n=1}^{N} a_n \;\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}\; +\infty.$$

## Conclusion

The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(2\pi n+\frac{1}{n}\right)\left
|\sin\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\right|$ **diverges to $+\infty$**; it has no fin
ite sum.
