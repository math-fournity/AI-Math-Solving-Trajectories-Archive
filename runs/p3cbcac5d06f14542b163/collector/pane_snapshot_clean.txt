Looking at this problem, I need to determine when the integral
$$\int_{e_k}^{\infty} \frac{dx}{x \cdot \ln(x) \cdot \ln^2(x) \cdots \ln^{k-1}(x
) \cdot (\ln^k(x))^s}$$
converges, where $\ln^j$ denotes the $j$-fold iterated logarithm and $e_k$ is th
e $k$-th iterated exponential (so that all iterated logs are $\geq 1$ on the dom
ain).

**Key substitution.** Set $t = \ln(x)$, so $dt = dx/x$. Under this substitution:
- $\ln(x) \mapsto t$
- $\ln^2(x) = \ln(\ln(x)) \mapsto \ln(t)$
- $\ln^j(x) \mapsto \ln^{j-1}(t)$
- $\ln^k(x) \mapsto \ln^{k-1}(t)$
- Lower limit: $x = e_k \Rightarrow t = \ln(e_k) = e_{k-1}$; upper limit: $x \to
 \infty \Rightarrow t \to \infty$.

The integral transforms to:
$$\int_{e_{k-1}}^{\infty} \frac{dt}{t \cdot \ln(t) \cdot \ln^2(t) \cdots \ln^{k-
2}(t) \cdot (\ln^{k-1}(t))^s}$$

This is **exactly the same form with $k$ replaced by $k-1$**. By induction, repe
ating this substitution $k-1$ times reduces the integral to:
$$\int_{e}^{\infty} \frac{du}{u \cdot (\ln u)^s}$$

**Final substitution.** Set $v = \ln(u)$, $dv = du/u$:
- $u = e \Rightarrow v = 1$; $u \to \infty \Rightarrow v \to \infty$.

$$\int_1^{\infty} \frac{dv}{v^s}$$

This is the classical $p$-integral, which **converges if and only if $s > 1$**.

**Verification of base case ($k=1$):** The integral is $\int_e^\infty \frac{dx}{
x(\ln x)^s}$. With $u=\ln x$ this becomes $\int_1^\infty u^{-s}\,du$, converging
 iff $s>1$. ✓

**Verification of $k=2$:** $\int_{e^e}^\infty \frac{dx}{x\ln x\cdot(\ln\ln x)^s}
$. Setting $t=\ln x$ gives $\int_e^\infty \frac{dt}{t(\ln t)^s}$, which by the $
k=1$ case converges iff $s>1$. ✓

The induction step is valid for all $k \geq 1$, and each step preserves the conv
ergence condition.

**Conclusion.** The integral converges if and only if $\boxed{s > 1}$.
