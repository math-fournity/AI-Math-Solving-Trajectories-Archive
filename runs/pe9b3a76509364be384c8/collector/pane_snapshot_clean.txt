Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether pointwise convergence $f_n(
x) \to 0$ for all $x \in X$ (which is weak* convergence $\sigma(X^*, X)$) implie
s weak convergence $f_n \to 0$ in $X^*$ (which is $\sigma(X^*, X^{**})$).

**Answer: No, this does not follow in general.** It holds if and only if $X$ is
reflexive.

---

## Proof

**Definitions.** The condition $f_n(x) \to 0$ for every $x \in X$ is precisely c
onvergence to $0$ in the weak* topology $\sigma(X^*, X)$ on $X^*$, since $X$ emb
eds canonically into $X^{**}$ via $x \mapsto \hat{x}$ where $\hat{x}(\varphi) =
\varphi(x)$.

The statement "$f_n \to 0$ weakly in $X^*$" means $\Phi(f_n) \to 0$ for every $\
Phi \in X^{**}$, i.e., convergence in the weak topology $\sigma(X^*, X^{**})$.

Since $X \hookrightarrow X^{**}$ canonically, every weak*-continuous functional
on $X^*$ is also weakly continuous. Hence $\sigma(X^*, X^{**})$ is finer than $\
sigma(X^*, X)$: weak convergence always implies weak* convergence. The question
is whether the converse holds.

---

### Counterexample: $X = c_0$

Let $X = c_0$, the Banach space of sequences converging to $0$, equipped with th
e sup-norm. Then:
$$X^* = \ell^1, \qquad X^{**} = \ell^\infty.$$

Define $f_n = e_n \in \ell^1 = (c_0)^*$, the $n$-th standard unit vector, acting
 on $x = (x_1, x_2, \ldots) \in c_0$ by:
$$f_n(x) = x_n.$$

**Weak* convergence to 0.** For every $x \in c_0$, we have $x_n \to 0$ by defini
tion of $c_0$. Thus $f_n(x) \to 0$ for all $x \in X$, i.e., $f_n \to 0$ in $\sig
ma(X^*, X)$.

**Failure of weak convergence to 0.** The weak topology on $X^* = \ell^1$ is $\s
igma(\ell^1, \ell^\infty)$. We must test convergence against every $\Phi \in \el
l^\infty$. For $\Phi = (\varphi_1, \varphi_2, \ldots) \in \ell^\infty$:
$$\Phi(f_n) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \varphi_k \, (f_n)_k = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \va
rphi_k \, \delta_{nk} = \varphi_n.$$

Take $\Phi = (1, 1, 1, \ldots) \in \ell^\infty$. Then:
$$\Phi(f_n) = 1 \quad \text{for all } n,$$
which does **not** converge to $0$.

Therefore $f_n \not\to 0$ weakly in $X^* = \ell^1$, even though $f_n \to 0$ weak
*.

---

### The reflexive case

If $X$ is reflexive, the canonical embedding $J: X \to X^{**}$ is surjective, so
 $X^{**} = J(X)$. Every $\Phi \in X^{**}$ has the form $\Phi = \hat{x}$ for some
 $x \in X$, and thus:
$$\Phi(f_n) = \hat{x}(f_n) = f_n(x) \to 0.$$

Hence weak* convergence coincides with weak convergence, and the implication hol
ds.

---

### Conclusion

The implication "$f_n(x) \to 0$ for all $x \in X$ $\Rightarrow$ $f_n \to 0$ weak
ly in $X^*$" is **false in general**. The counterexample $X = c_0$, $f_n = e_n \
in \ell^1$ shows a sequence converging weak* to $0$ but not weakly to $0$. The i
mplication holds precisely when $X$ is reflexive, since then $\sigma(X^*, X) = \
sigma(X^*, X^{**})$.
