Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum number of positive integers
where any two distinct $a, b$ satisfy $|a-b| \geq \frac{ab}{100}$.

**Reformulation.** For $a < b$, the condition $b - a \geq \frac{ab}{100}$ is equ
ivalent to $\frac{1}{a} - \frac{1}{b} \geq \frac{1}{100}$.

So I need the longest sequence of positive integers $a_1 < a_2 < \cdots < a_n$ w
ith $\frac{1}{a_i} - \frac{1}{a_{i+1}} \geq \frac{1}{100}$ for all consecutive p
airs (consecutive pairs are the tightest constraint since reciprocals are decrea
sing).

**Greedy is optimal.** Starting from $a_1 = 1$ (maximizing the budget $1/a_1 - 1
/a_n < 1$), at each step I choose the smallest valid integer $a_{i+1}$. This is
optimal because choosing a smaller $a_{i+1}$ gives a larger $1/a_{i+1}$, leaving
 more room for future elements. Any chain extending from a larger choice can als
o extend from the greedy choice.

**The threshold** at each step: $a_{i+1} \geq \frac{100 \cdot a_i}{100 - a_i}$ (
requires $a_i < 100$).

**Verification of all consecutive pairs** ($100(b-a) \geq ab$):

- $1\to2$: $100 \geq 2$ ✓; $2\to3$: $100 \geq 6$ ✓; $\ldots$; $9\to10$: $100 \ge
q 90$ ✓
- $10\to12$: $200 \geq 120$ ✓; $12\to14$: $200 \geq 168$ ✓
- $14\to17$: $300 \geq 238$ ✓; $17\to21$: $400 \geq 357$ ✓
- $21\to27$: $600 \geq 567$ ✓; $27\to37$: $1000 \geq 999$ ✓
- $37\to59$: $2200 \geq 2183$ ✓; $59\to144$: $8500 \geq 8496$ ✓

All non-consecutive pairs automatically satisfy the constraint since the recipro
cal gap telescopes to a sum of consecutive gaps, each $\geq 1/100$.

**No insertion possible:** Between each consecutive pair, no integer $m$ can be
inserted (e.g., $1/10 - 1/11 = 1/110 < 1/100$; $1/27 - 1/28 = 1/756 < 1/100$; al
l intermediate gaps are far below $1/100$).

**Termination:** At $a_{18} = 144 \geq 100$, the threshold becomes negative — no
 further element can be added.

**Optimality proof:** By the greedy dominance argument: if two chains agree thro
ugh position $i$, and one picks $a_{i+1} = x$ (greedy minimum) while the other p
icks $a'_{i+1} = y > x$, then $1/x > 1/y$ implies $1/x - 1/100 > 1/y - 1/100$, s
o every valid continuation from $y$ is also valid from $x$. By induction, the gr
eedy chain is at least as long as any chain. Starting from $a_1 = 1$ maximizes t
he total budget $1/a_1 - 1/a_n < 1$.

**The maximum number of positive integers is $\boxed{18}$.**
