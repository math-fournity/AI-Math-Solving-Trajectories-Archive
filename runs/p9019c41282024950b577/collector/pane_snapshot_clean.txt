Looking at this problem, I need to determine the homotopy type of a Möbius strip
 with a point removed. The answer depends on whether the point is in the interio
r or on the boundary, so I'll address both cases.

**Proof.**

Let $M$ be the Möbius strip. Recall that $M$ deformation retracts onto its centr
al circle, so $M \simeq S^1$ and $\chi(M) = 0$.

---

**Case 1: $p$ is an interior point.**

Let $D \subset \operatorname{int}(M)$ be a closed disk neighborhood of $p$. The
punctured disk $D \setminus \{p\}$ deformation retracts onto $\partial D$. This
retraction extends to all of $M \setminus \{p\}$ by fixing $M \setminus \operato
rname{int}(D)$ pointwise, giving:

$$M \setminus \{p\} \simeq M \setminus \operatorname{int}(D).$$

Now $M \setminus \operatorname{int}(D)$ is a compact, connected surface with bou
ndary. Its boundary has **two** components: $\partial M$ (one circle) and $\part
ial D$ (another circle). It is non-orientable, since $M$ is non-orientable and r
emoving a disk preserves this.

**Euler characteristic.** Using the decomposition $M = \bigl(M \setminus \operat
orname{int}(D)\bigr) \cup D$ with intersection $\partial D \cong S^1$:

$$\chi(M) = \chi\bigl(M \setminus \operatorname{int}(D)\bigr) + \chi(D) - \chi(\
partial D)$$
$$0 = \chi\bigl(M \setminus \operatorname{int}(D)\bigr) + 1 - 0$$

so $\chi\bigl(M \setminus \operatorname{int}(D)\bigr) = -1$.

**Deformation retraction to a graph.** Every compact connected surface with non-
empty boundary deformation retracts onto a 1-dimensional spine (a connected grap
h $\Gamma$). For a connected graph, $\chi(\Gamma) = 1 - \beta_1(\Gamma)$. Thus:

$$-1 = 1 - \beta_1 \implies \beta_1 = 2.$$

A connected graph with first Betti number $2$ is homotopy equivalent to $S^1 \ve
e S^1$ (the wedge of two circles, i.e., the figure-eight). Therefore:

$$\boxed{M \setminus \{p\} \simeq S^1 \vee S^1 \quad \text{(interior point).}}$$

---

**Case 2: $p$ is a boundary point.**

A neighborhood of $p$ in $M$ is a half-disk $H$ (with its flat side on $\partial
 M$). The punctured half-disk $H \setminus \{p\}$ deformation retracts onto $\pa
rtial H \setminus \{p\}$, which is an open arc (the semicircular part plus the t
wo remaining segments of the diameter form a circle minus one point — an open in
terval), hence contractible. This retraction extends to $M \setminus \{p\}$ by f
ixing $M \setminus H$, giving:

$$M \setminus \{p\} \simeq M \setminus \operatorname{int}(H).$$

Now, $M \setminus \operatorname{int}(H)$ is homeomorphic to $M$: removing a half
-disk from the boundary of a surface with boundary does not change the homeomorp
hism type (one can "push" the boundary inward within a collar neighborhood to ab
sorb the notch). Since $M \simeq S^1$:

$$\boxed{M \setminus \{p\} \simeq S^1 \quad \text{(boundary point).}}$$

---

**Remark on well-definedness.** All interior points of $M$ are equivalent under
homeomorphisms of $M$ (the homeomorphism group of a connected manifold acts tran
sitively on its interior), and likewise all boundary points are equivalent (the
boundary is a single circle). So the answer in each case is independent of the s
pecific point chosen.

**Conclusion.** The homotopy type of $M \setminus \{p\}$ is:
- $S^1 \vee S^1$ (the figure-eight) if $p$ is an **interior** point,
- $S^1$ (the circle) if $p$ is a **boundary** point.
