# Transformation Properties of the Levi-Civita Symbol ε^{ijk}

## 1. Setup and Definitions

The Levi-Civita symbol in three dimensions is defined by

$$\epsilon^{ijk} = \begin{cases} +1 & \text{if } (i,j,k) \text{ is an even permu
tation of } (1,2,3), \\ -1 & \text{if } (i,j,k) \text{ is an odd permutation of
} (1,2,3), \\ 0 & \text{otherwise (any repeated index).} \end{cases}$$

Consider a general (invertible) coordinate transformation $x'^i = a^i{}_j\, x^j$
 with Jacobian matrix $A = (a^i{}_j)$ and determinant $\det A$.

## 2. The Key Identity

A genuine (contravariant) rank-3 tensor $T^{ijk}$ would transform as

$$T'^{ijk} = a^i{}_p\, a^j{}_q\, a^k{}_r\, T^{pqr}.$$

Let us compute what the right-hand side gives when $T^{pqr} = \epsilon^{pqr}$:

$$\tilde\epsilon^{ijk} := a^i{}_p\, a^j{}_q\, a^k{}_r\, \epsilon^{pqr}.$$

**Claim:** $\tilde\epsilon^{ijk} = (\det A)\,\epsilon^{ijk}$.

*Proof of claim.* The expression $a^i{}_p\, a^j{}_q\, a^k{}_r\, \epsilon^{pqr}$
is precisely the determinant formula written in index form: it is the determinan
t of the matrix whose rows are the $i$-th, $j$-th, $k$-th rows of $A$, i.e.

$$a^i{}_p\, a^j{}_q\, a^k{}_r\, \epsilon^{pqr} = \det\!\begin{pmatrix} a^i{}_1 &
 a^i{}_2 & a^i{}_3 \\ a^j{}_1 & a^j{}_2 & a^j{}_3 \\ a^k{}_1 & a^k{}_2 & a^k{}_3
 \end{pmatrix}.$$

- If $(i,j,k)$ is an even permutation of $(1,2,3)$, this determinant equals $\de
t A$ (row permutation is even).
- If $(i,j,k)$ is an odd permutation, the row swap introduces a sign, giving $-\
det A$.
- If any two of $i,j,k$ coincide, two rows are equal, so the determinant is $0$.

These are exactly the defining properties of $(\det A)\,\epsilon^{ijk}$. ∎

## 3. Distinguishing Proper and Improper Transformations

Recall $\det A$ can be positive or negative:

- **Proper transformation** ($\det A = +1$): rotations (and their compositions).
 Here
  $$\tilde\epsilon^{ijk} = (+1)\,\epsilon^{ijk} = \epsilon^{ijk}.$$
  The symbol is **invariant**, behaving exactly like a true tensor.

- **Improper transformation** ($\det A = -1$): reflections, inversion ($x \mapst
o -x$), or rotoreflections. Here
  $$\tilde\epsilon^{ijk} = (-1)\,\epsilon^{ijk} = -\epsilon^{ijk}.$$
  The symbol **changes sign**, which a genuine tensor never does under an invert
ible linear map.

So the Levi-Civita symbol transforms like a tensor **only** under proper (orient
ation-preserving) transformations, and picks up an extra sign under improper (or
ientation-reversing) ones.

## 4. Why It Is a Pseudotensor

A **pseudotensor** (or *axial tensor*) is an object that transforms as an ordina
ry tensor under proper transformations but acquires an additional factor of $\de
t A / |\det A| = \operatorname{sgn}(\det A)$ under general transformations. Equi
valently,

$$\epsilon'^{ijk} = \frac{\det A}{|\det A|}\, a^i{}_p\, a^j{}_q\, a^k{}_r\, \eps
ilon^{pqr} \cdot \frac{1}{\det A}\cdot \det A \;=\; \operatorname{sgn}(\det A)\,
 a^i{}_p\, a^j{}_q\, a^k{}_r\, \epsilon^{pqr}\,/\,(\text{tensor part}).$$

More cleanly: the Levi-Civita symbol is a **rank-3 pseudotensor** because its tr
ansformation law is

$$\boxed{\;\epsilon'^{ijk} = \operatorname{sgn}(\det A)\; a^i{}_p\, a^j{}_q\, a^
k{}_r\, \epsilon^{pqr}\;}$$

Wait — let me be precise. From §2 we have $a^i{}_p a^j{}_q a^k{}_r \epsilon^{pqr
} = (\det A)\epsilon^{ijk}$. The pseudotensor transformation law is the one that
 makes the **numerical values** of $\epsilon$ stay fixed (always $+1,-1,0$) in e
very coordinate system. That requires

$$\epsilon'^{ijk} = \frac{1}{\det A}\, a^i{}_p\, a^j{}_q\, a^k{}_r\, \epsilon^{p
qr} = \frac{\det A}{\det A}\,\epsilon^{ijk} = \epsilon^{ijk}.$$

But this introduces the factor $1/\det A$, which is **not** the tensor transform
ation law. The presence of this $1/\det A$ (or equivalently $\det A$ when compar
ing with the pure tensor rule) is exactly what makes $\epsilon$ a **tensor densi
ty** rather than a true tensor.

## 5. Pseudotensor vs. Tensor Density — Reconciling the Two Viewpoints

These two names describe the *same* object from two complementary perspectives:

### Tensor density viewpoint
A **tensor density of weight $w$** transforms with an extra factor $(\det A)^w$
compared to a true tensor. Comparing

$$\underbrace{a^i{}_p\, a^j{}_q\, a^k{}_r\, \epsilon^{pqr}}_{\text{true-tensor r
ule applied to }\epsilon} = (\det A)\,\epsilon^{ijk},$$

we see $\epsilon^{ijk}$ transforms as a **rank-3 tensor density of weight $+1$**
 (the extra factor is $(\det A)^{+1}$). If one insists on keeping the *same nume
rical components* in every frame (the convention $\epsilon'^{ijk}\equiv\epsilon^
{ijk}$ numerically), one divides by $\det A$, i.e. uses weight $-1$; conventions
 differ by sign of $w$, but the essential point is the presence of a $\det A$ fa
ctor.

### Pseudotensor viewpoint
If we restrict to **unimodular** transformations ($|\det A|=1$, which covers all
 rotations and reflections in $O(3)$), then $\det A = \pm 1$ and the density fac
tor reduces to $\operatorname{sgn}(\det A)$. The transformation law becomes

$$\epsilon'^{ijk} = \operatorname{sgn}(\det A)\;\epsilon^{ijk}\quad(\text{under
} O(3)),$$

i.e. invariant under $SO(3)$ (proper rotations, $\det A=+1$) and sign-flipping u
nder improper transformations ($\det A=-1$). This is precisely the definition of
 a **pseudotensor** (axial tensor).

### Synthesis
- "Pseudotensor" emphasizes behavior under the **orthogonal group** $O(3)$: the
sign flip under orientation reversal.
- "Tensor density" emphasizes behavior under **general linear** transformations
$GL(3,\mathbb{R})$: the $\det A$ weighting factor.

The Levi-Civita symbol is **both**: it is a rank-3 tensor density (of weight $\p
m1$ depending on convention) that, restricted to orthogonal transformations, man
ifests as a pseudotensor.

## 6. Physical Consequence — Cross Products and Axial Vectors

The cross product can be written $\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{A}\times\mathbf{B}$ with
$C^i = \epsilon^{ijk} A_j B_k$. Since $\epsilon$ contributes a factor $\operator
name{sgn}(\det A)$ under improper transformations while $A_j, B_k$ transform as
ordinary (polar) vectors, the cross product $\mathbf{C}$ transforms as a **pseud
ovector** (axial vector): it is invariant under rotations but flips sign under r
eflections. This is why angular momentum $\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{p}$
, the magnetic field $\mathbf{B}$, and magnetic moment $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ are ax
ial vectors — their pseudotensor nature is inherited directly from $\epsilon^{ij
k}$.

## 7. Summary

| Transformation type | $\det A$ | $\epsilon'^{ijk}$ vs. $\epsilon^{ijk}$ | Beha
vior |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proper rotation ($SO(3)$) | $+1$ | $= \epsilon^{ijk}$ | True tensor |
| Improper (reflection/inversion) | $-1$ | $= -\epsilon^{ijk}$ | Sign flip → pse
udotensor |
| General $GL(3)$ | arbitrary | $\propto (\det A)\,\epsilon^{ijk}$ | Tensor dens
ity of weight $1$ |

The Levi-Civita symbol $\epsilon^{ijk}$ is **not a true tensor** because under a
 general coordinate transformation it acquires a factor of $\det A$ beyond the o
rdinary tensor transformation rule:

$$a^i{}_p\, a^j{}_q\, a^k{}_r\, \epsilon^{pqr} = (\det A)\,\epsilon^{ijk}.$$

- Under **proper** transformations ($\det A = +1$) it behaves as an ordinary ten
sor.
- Under **improper** transformations ($\det A = -1$) it picks up a minus sign, t
he hallmark of a **pseudotensor**.
- Under general invertible transformations the $\det A$ factor makes it a **tens
or density** (of weight $+1$ in the convention where the symbol's components are
 carried by the tensor rule, or weight $-1$ if one normalizes to keep numerical
values fixed).

These two characterizations — pseudotensor (orthogonal-group viewpoint) and tens
or density (general-linear-group viewpoint) — are consistent descriptions of the
 same underlying object, the totally antisymmetric Levi-Civita symbol.
