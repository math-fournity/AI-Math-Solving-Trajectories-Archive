**Problem.** Determine whether the Hilbert transform of Schwarz (Schwartz) funct
ions converges to zero as $|x|\to\infty$.

**Answer: Yes.** For every $f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})$ (the Schwartz class), i
ts Hilbert transform $Hf(x)\to 0$ as $|x|\to\infty$.

---

**Proof.**

Recall the Hilbert transform is the Fourier multiplier operator with symbol $m(\
xi)=-i\,\operatorname{sgn}(\xi)$:

$$
\widehat{Hf}(\xi) = -i\,\operatorname{sgn}(\xi)\,\widehat{f}(\xi), \qquad \xi\in
\mathbb{R}.
$$

Equivalently, by Fourier inversion (using the convention $\check{g}(x)=\frac{1}{
2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}} g(\xi)\,e^{ix\xi}\,d\xi$),

$$
Hf(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}} \bigl(-i\,\operatorname{sgn}(\xi)\,\wide
hat{f}(\xi)\bigr)\,e^{ix\xi}\,d\xi.
$$

Set $g(\xi):=-i\,\operatorname{sgn}(\xi)\,\widehat{f}(\xi)$.

**Claim.** $g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$.

Indeed, $f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})$ implies $\widehat{f}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb
{R})$, hence $\widehat{f}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$. Since $|\operatorname{sgn}(\xi)
|\le 1$ for all $\xi$,

$$
|g(\xi)| = |\operatorname{sgn}(\xi)|\,|\widehat{f}(\xi)| \le |\widehat{f}(\xi)|,
$$

so $g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$ by comparison.

**Applying the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma.** The Riemann–Lebesgue lemma states that
for any $g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$, the (inverse) Fourier transform

$$
\check{g}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}} g(\xi)\,e^{ix\xi}\,d\xi
$$

satisfies $\check{g}(x)\to 0$ as $|x|\to\infty$.

Since $Hf(x)=\check{g}(x)$ with $g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$, we conclude

$$
\boxed{Hf(x)\to 0 \quad\text{as}\quad |x|\to\infty.}
$$

---

**Remark on the rate of decay.** Although $Hf(x)\to 0$, in general $Hf$ is **not
** Schwartz. The multiplier $-i\,\operatorname{sgn}(\xi)$ has a jump discontinui
ty at $\xi=0$, so $g(\xi)=-i\,\operatorname{sgn}(\xi)\,\widehat{f}(\xi)$ is not
smooth at the origin (unless $\widehat{f}(0)=\int f=0$). Consequently $Hf$ typic
ally decays only like $O(1/|x|)$ rather than rapidly. Specifically, integrating
by parts on each half-line $\xi>0$ and $\xi<0$ yields, for $|x|$ large,

$$
Hf(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{-i\,\widehat{f}(0)\,e^{ix\cdot 0^{+}} + i\,\wid
ehat{f}(0)\,e^{ix\cdot 0^{-}}}{ix} + O(|x|^{-2}) = \frac{\widehat{f}(0)}{\pi x}
+ O(|x|^{-2}),
$$

confirming the $1/x$ leading behavior (which vanishes when $\widehat{f}(0)=\int
f = 0$, in which case the decay is faster). In all cases the limit is $0$. $\bla
cksquare$
