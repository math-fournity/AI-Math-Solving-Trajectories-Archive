Looking at this problem, I need to find all squarefree integers $d$ such that $\
mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ is a degree-2 subextension of the splitting field $K = \mat
hbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ of $x^n - 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

## Proof

**Step 1: Identify the splitting field and its Galois group.**

The splitting field of $x^n - 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is the cyclotomic field $K =
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$, where $\zeta_n$ is a primitive $n$-th root of unity. Its G
alois group is
$$\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*.$$

**Step 2: Relate degree-2 subextensions to quadratic characters.**

By the Galois correspondence, degree-2 subextensions of $K/\mathbb{Q}$ correspon
d to index-2 subgroups of $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*$, equivalently to nontrivi
al group homomorphisms $\chi: (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^* \to \{\pm 1\}$, i.e., n
ontrivial quadratic Dirichlet characters modulo $n$. Each such character $\chi$
determines a unique quadratic field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d_\chi})$ where $d_\chi$ i
s squarefree, via the Kronecker–Weber theorem and the conductor-discriminant for
mula.

**Step 3: Factor the group and its quadratic characters.**

Write $n = 2^a \prod_{i=1}^{s} p_i^{e_i}$ with distinct odd primes $p_i$. By the
 Chinese Remainder Theorem,
$$(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^* \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2^a\mathbb{Z})^* \times \prod_{i=
1}^{s} (\mathbb{Z}/p_i^{e_i}\mathbb{Z})^*.$$

Every quadratic character mod $n$ factors as a product of quadratic characters m
odulo each prime-power factor.

**Step 4: Quadratic characters modulo each prime power.**

*Odd prime $p_i^{e_i}$:* The group $(\mathbb{Z}/p_i^{e_i}\mathbb{Z})^*$ is cycli
c of even order $p_i^{e_i-1}(p_i-1)$, so it has a **unique** index-2 subgroup, h
ence a unique quadratic character: the Legendre symbol $\left(\frac{\cdot}{p_i}\
right)$. This character corresponds to the quadratic field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p_i
^*})$ where
$$p_i^* = (-1)^{(p_i-1)/2}\, p_i,$$
i.e., $p_i^* = p_i$ if $p_i \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$, and $p_i^* = -p_i$ if $p_i \equi
v 3 \pmod{4}$. (This is because the conductor of $\left(\frac{\cdot}{p_i}\right)
$ is $p_i$, and the associated quadratic field has discriminant $p_i^*$.)

*Power of 2, $2^a$:*
- $a \leq 1$: $(\mathbb{Z}/2^a\mathbb{Z})^*$ is trivial, so there are **no** qua
dratic characters.
- $a = 2$: $(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$, giving **one** quadr
atic character, corresponding to $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})$.
- $a \geq 3$: $(\mathbb{Z}/2^a\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}
/2^{a-2}$, giving **three** nontrivial quadratic characters, corresponding to $\
mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$, and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$.

**Step 5: Enumerate all quadratic subfields.**

A quadratic character mod $n$ is a product of a choice of quadratic character (o
r trivial) at each prime-power factor. The corresponding quadratic field is the
compositum of the chosen quadratic fields, which equals $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ w
here $d$ is the squarefree part of the product of the chosen generators.

Define the set of "2-adic generators" $E$ depending on $a = v_2(n)$:
$$E = \begin{cases} \{1\} & \text{if } a \leq 1, \\ \{1,\, -1\} & \text{if } a =
 2, \\ \{1,\, -1,\, 2,\, -2\} & \text{if } a \geq 3. \end{cases}$$

(When $a \geq 3$, the three nontrivial characters give $-1$, $2$, $-2$; includin
g the trivial character gives $|E| = 4 = 2^2$ choices. When $a = 2$, we get $-1$
 plus trivial, so $|E| = 2$. When $a \leq 1$, only trivial, $|E| = 1$.)

**Step 6: State the result.**

The squarefree integers $d$ (with $d \neq 1$) corresponding to the degree-2 sube
xtensions of $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}$ are precisely:

$$\boxed{d \;=\; \epsilon \cdot \prod_{p \in S} p^*, \qquad p^* = (-1)^{(p-1)/2}
\,p,}$$

where:
- $S$ ranges over all subsets of the set of **odd prime divisors** of $n$,
- $\epsilon$ ranges over $E$ as defined above,
- and we exclude $d = 1$ (which corresponds to $S = \emptyset$, $\epsilon = 1$,
giving $\mathbb{Q}$ itself, not a degree-2 extension).

The total number of such squarefree integers (i.e., the number of degree-2 subex
tensions) is $2^r - 1$, where $r = s + \delta$ is the 2-rank of $(\mathbb{Z}/n\m
athbb{Z})^*$: here $s$ is the number of distinct odd prime divisors of $n$, and
$\delta = 0$ if $v_2(n) \leq 1$, $\delta = 1$ if $v_2(n) = 2$, $\delta = 2$ if $
v_2(n) \geq 3$.

**Step 7: Verification via the conductor criterion.**

By the Kronecker–Weber theorem, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}) \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\zeta
_n)$ if and only if the conductor of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ divides $n$. The con
ductor (equal to $|\Delta|$, the absolute value of the discriminant) is:
- $|d|$ if $d \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$,
- $4|d|$ if $d \equiv 2 \pmod{4}$ (so $d = 2d'$, $d'$ odd; conductor $= 8|d'|$),
- $4|d|$ if $d \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$.

One checks that the set of $d$ produced in Step 6 is exactly the set of squarefr
ee $d \neq 1$ whose conductor divides $n$, confirming the result. $\blacksquare$
