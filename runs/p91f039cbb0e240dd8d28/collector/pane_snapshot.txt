## Proof

Let $g(z) = \dfrac{1}{z^2+1}$. The zeros of $z^2+1$ are $z = \pm i$, both satisf
ying $|\pm i| = 1$, which lies on the **inner boundary** of the annulus $A = \{1
 < |z| < 2\}$, not in $A$ itself. Hence $g$ is analytic on $A$.

**Criterion.** A function $g$ analytic on a domain $\Omega$ admits an analytic a
ntiderivative on $\Omega$ if and only if $\displaystyle\int_\gamma g(z)\,dz = 0$
 for every closed curve $\gamma$ in $\Omega$.

**Reduction to a single generator.** The annulus $A$ deformation-retracts onto t
he circle $|z|=r$ for any fixed $r \in (1,2)$, so $\pi_1(A) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, g
enerated by
$$\gamma(t) = r e^{it}, \quad 0 \le t \le 2\pi.$$
Every closed curve in $A$ is homotopic to $\gamma^n$ for some $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
. Since $g$ is analytic on $A$, the integral $\int_\gamma g\,dz$ depends only on
 the homotopy class of $\gamma$, so $\int_{\gamma^n} g\,dz = n\int_\gamma g\,dz$
. Thus it suffices to show $\int_\gamma g\,dz = 0$.

**Residue computation.** For $r \in (1,2)$, the circle $|z|=r$ encloses both pol
es $z = i$ and $z = -i$ (since $|\pm i| = 1 < r$). By partial fractions:
$$\frac{1}{z^2+1} = \frac{1}{(z-i)(z+i)} = \frac{1}{2i}\cdot\frac{1}{z-i} - \fra
c{1}{2i}\cdot\frac{1}{z+i}.$$

The residues of $g$ inside $|z| < r$ are:
- $\operatorname{Res}(g, i) = \dfrac{1}{2i}$,
- $\operatorname{Res}(g, -i) = -\dfrac{1}{2i}$.

Their sum is $\dfrac{1}{2i} - \dfrac{1}{2i} = 0$.

By the residue theorem:
$$\int_\gamma g(z)\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot \sum \operatorname{Res} = 2\pi i \cdot 0 =
 0.$$

**Conclusion.** Since the integral over the generator $\gamma$ vanishes, $\int_\
gamma g\,dz = 0$ for **every** closed curve $\gamma$ in $A$. Therefore $g$ admit
s an analytic antiderivative $f$ on $A$, i.e., there exists an analytic function
 $f$ on the annulus $A$ with
$$f'(z) = \frac{1}{z^2+1}.$$

**The answer is yes: such an analytic function $f$ exists.** $\blacksquare$
