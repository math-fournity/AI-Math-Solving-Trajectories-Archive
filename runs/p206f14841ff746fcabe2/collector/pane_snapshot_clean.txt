## Proof

Since $A$ is real symmetric positive semi-definite, it admits an orthogonal diag
onalization
$$A = U\,\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n)\,U^{\top}, \qquad \lambd
a_i\ge 0.$$
Then
$$(A+I)^{-1}=U\,\operatorname{diag}\!\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{\lambda_1+1},\dots,\tfrac{1
}{\lambda_n+1}\Bigr)\,U^{\top},$$
and consequently
$$\bigl((A+I)^{-1}\bigr)^{2}=U\,\operatorname{diag}\!\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{(\lambda_1+
1)^2},\dots,\tfrac{1}{(\lambda_n+1)^2}\Bigr)\,U^{\top}.$$
Therefore
$$\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\bigl((A+I)^{-1}\bigr)^{2}\bigr)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}
{(\lambda_i+1)^2}.$$

The hypotheses translate to
$$\lambda_i\ge 0\quad\forall i,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_i=\operatorname{Tr}(
A)\le n.$$

Define $f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}$ by $f(x)=\frac{1}{(x+1)^2}$. Then
$$f'(x)=-\frac{2}{(x+1)^3}<0,\qquad f''(x)=\frac{6}{(x+1)^4}>0,$$
so $f$ is **strictly decreasing** and **strictly convex** on $[0,\infty)$.

**Step 1 — the sum is minimized when $\sum\lambda_i=n$.** Since $f$ is decreasin
g, increasing any $\lambda_i$ (while keeping it nonnegative) only decreases $\su
m f(\lambda_i)$. Hence the minimum of $\sum f(\lambda_i)$ subject to $\sum\lambd
a_i\le n$, $\lambda_i\ge 0$ is attained on the boundary $\sum\lambda_i=n$.

**Step 2 — on $\sum\lambda_i=n$, the minimum is at $\lambda_1=\cdots=\lambda_n=1
$.** With the sum fixed at $n$, Jensen's inequality for the convex function $f$
gives
$$\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(\lambda_i)\;\ge\; f\!\Bigl(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n
}\lambda_i\Bigr)=f(1)=\frac{1}{4}.$$
Thus
$$\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(\lambda_i+1)^2}\;\ge\;\frac{n}{4},$$
with equality if and only if $\lambda_1=\cdots=\lambda_n=1$, i.e., $A=I$.

**Attainment.** For $A=I$ we have $\operatorname{Tr}(A)=n\le n$ and
$$\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\bigl((I+I)^{-1}\bigr)^{2}\bigr)=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl
(\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}I\bigr)^2\bigr)=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{4}I\bigr)=\f
rac{n}{4}.$$

**Conclusion.** The sharp lower bound is
$$\boxed{\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\bigl((A+I)^{-1}\bigr)^{2}\bigr)\;\ge\;\frac{n}{
4}},$$
with equality at $A=I$.
