**Answer: No.**

**Counterexample.** Let $X = \mathbb{A}^2_k = \operatorname{Spec} k[x,y]$ for a
field $k$, and let $U = X \setminus \{(0,0)\}$ be the punctured affine plane. Th
en $X$ is integral (since $k[x,y]$ is a domain), $U$ is a proper open subscheme
(the origin is removed), yet the restriction map
$$\mathcal{O}_X(X) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \mathcal{O}_X(U)$$
is an isomorphism. Hence the implication fails.

**Proof that $\mathcal{O}_X(U) \cong \mathcal{O}_X(X) = k[x,y]$.**

Since $X$ is integral with generic point $\eta$, every nonempty open subset $V$
has $\mathcal{O}_X(V) = \bigcap_{\mathfrak{p} \in V} \mathcal{O}_{X,\mathfrak{p}
}$ inside the function field $K(X) = k(x,y)$. The open set $U$ is covered by the
 two principal opens $D(x)$ and $D(y)$, so by the sheaf property
$$\mathcal{O}_X(U) = \mathcal{O}_X(D(x)) \cap \mathcal{O}_X(D(y)) \quad\text{ins
ide } k(x,y),$$
i.e.
$$\mathcal{O}_X(U) = k[x,y,1/x] \cap k[x,y,1/y].$$

We claim this intersection equals $k[x,y]$.

- $(\supseteq)$ is clear since $k[x,y] \subseteq k[x,y,1/x]$ and $k[x,y] \subset
eq k[x,y,1/y]$.
- $(\subseteq)$: Take $h \in k[x,y,1/x] \cap k[x,y,1/y]$. Then $h = f/x^n = g/y^
m$ for some $f, g \in k[x,y]$ and $n, m \ge 0$. Clearing denominators gives $y^m
 f = x^n g$ in the UFD $k[x,y]$. Since $x$ and $y$ are distinct irreducible elem
ents, $\gcd(x^n, y^m) = 1$, so $x^n \mid f$; write $f = x^n h$ with $h \in k[x,y
]$. Then $h = f/x^n = g/y^m$, and the equality $y^m h = g$ shows $h \in k[x,y]$.
 Hence $h \in k[x,y]$.

Therefore $\mathcal{O}_X(U) = k[x,y] = \mathcal{O}_X(X)$, and the restriction ma
p (which is the inclusion of $\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in X}\mathcal{O}_{X,\mathfra
k{p}}$ into $\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in U}\mathcal{O}_{X,\mathfrak{p}}$) is the id
entity on $k[x,y]$, hence an isomorphism.

Yet $U \neq X$, since the closed point $(x,y) \in X$ corresponding to the origin
 is not in $U$.

**Remark.** The underlying reason is that the removed point has codimension $2$
in $X$. For a normal (in particular regular) integral scheme, regular functions
extend across closed subsets of codimension $\ge 2$ (algebraic Hartogs phenomeno
n), so removing such a subset leaves $\mathcal{O}_X(U)$ unchanged. The implicati
on $\mathcal{O}_X(X)\cong\mathcal{O}_X(U) \Rightarrow U=X$ does hold when $X$ is
, for instance, a Dedekind scheme (dimension $1$), since then every proper close
d point has codimension $1$ and its removal genuinely enlarges the ring of secti
ons. But in general it fails, as shown above.
