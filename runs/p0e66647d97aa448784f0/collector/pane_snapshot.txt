## Cycle Index of the Edge-Permutation Group of the Tetrahedral Rotations

**Setup.** Label the tetrahedron's vertices $1,2,3,4$, so the 6 edges are
$$12,\;13,\;14,\;23,\;24,\;34.$$
The rotational symmetry group of the regular tetrahedron is isomorphic to $A_4$
and has $|G|=12$ elements, falling into three conjugacy classes:

| Class | Size | Description |
|---|---|---|
| $e$ | 1 | identity |
| order-3 | 8 | $\pm120°$ about a vertex–opposite-face axis |
| order-2 | 3 | $180°$ about an axis through midpoints of opposite edges |

We compute the cycle structure induced on the 6 edges for each class.

---

**Identity.** Every edge is fixed:
$$\text{cycle type } x_1^6. \qquad (1\text{ element})$$

---

**Order-3 rotations (8 elements).** Consider the $120°$ rotation fixing vertex $
1$ and cycling $2\to3\to4\to2$, i.e. the permutation $(2\,3\,4)$ on vertices. Tr
acking the edges:
$$12\to13\to14\to12, \qquad 23\to34\to24\to23.$$
So the edge permutation is $(12\;13\;14)(23\;34\;24)$: two 3-cycles, cycle type
$x_3^2$.

By symmetry every order-3 rotation (about any of the 4 vertex axes, in either di
rection) gives the same type:
$$\text{cycle type } x_3^2. \qquad (8\text{ elements})$$

---

**Order-2 rotations (3 elements).** Take the $180°$ rotation about the axis thro
ugh the midpoints of opposite edges $12$ and $34$. On vertices it swaps $1\leftr
ightarrow2$ and $3\leftrightarrow4$. Tracking edges:
$$12\to12,\quad 34\to34,\quad 13\to24\to13,\quad 14\to23\to14.$$
So the edge permutation is $(12)(34)(13\;24)(14\;23)$: two fixed edges and two t
ranspositions, cycle type $x_1^2 x_2^2$.

The same holds for the other two axes (through midpoints of $\{13,24\}$ and of $
\{14,23\}$):
$$\text{cycle type } x_1^2 x_2^2. \qquad (3\text{ elements})$$

---

**Assembling the cycle index.** Averaging over the group ($|G|=12$):

$$\boxed{\,Z(G;\,x_1,\dots,x_6)=\frac{1}{12}\Big(x_1^{6}+8\,x_3^{2}+3\,x_1^{2}\,
x_2^{2}\Big)\,}$$

**Verification.** The coefficient sum is $\frac{1}{12}(1+8+3)=1$ ✓, and substitu
ting $x_k=2$ for all $k$ (2-colorings) gives $\frac{1}{12}(64+8\cdot4+3\cdot16)=
\frac{1}{12}(64+32+48)=\frac{144}{12}=12$, which equals the number of orbits of
edge-2-colorings under tetrahedral rotations — consistent with Burnside's lemma.
