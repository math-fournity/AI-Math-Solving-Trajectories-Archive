## Proof

**Setup.** Write points of $X$ in polar form $x = r\,u$ with $r\in[1,2]$ and $u\
in S^2$. This gives a homeomorphism
$$X \cong [1,2]\times S^2,\qquad x\mapsto (|x|,\, x/|x|).$$
Under this identification, $S_1 = \{1\}\times S^2$ and $S_2 = \{2\}\times S^2$,
and the equivalence relation becomes
$$(1,u)\sim(2,u)\quad\text{for all }u\in S^2.$$

**The quotient is a product.** The equivalence relation is product-type: $(t,u)\
sim(t',u')$ iff ($t=t'$ and $u=u'$) or ($\{t,t'\}=\{1,2\}$ and $u=u'$). Equivale
ntly it is generated by the relation on the first factor $[1,2]$ that identifies
 $1\sim 2$, with the second factor $S^2$ left untouched. For compact Hausdorff s
paces, the quotient of a product by a product-type equivalence relation is the p
roduct of the quotients, so
$$X/\sim \;\cong\; \bigl([1,2]/(1\sim 2)\bigr)\times S^2 \;\cong\; S^1\times S^2
.$$

**Fundamental group.** The space $S^1\times S^2$ is path-connected, so the funda
mental group is independent of base point up to isomorphism. By the product theo
rem for fundamental groups,
$$\pi_1(S^1\times S^2)\cong \pi_1(S^1)\times\pi_1(S^2).$$
We have $\pi_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb{Z}$ and $\pi_1(S^2)\cong 0$ (since $S^2$ is simp
ly connected). Therefore
$$\pi_1(X/\sim,\,x_0)\cong \mathbb{Z}\times 0 \cong \mathbb{Z}.$$

The generator is the loop obtained by fixing any $u\in S^2$ and traversing the r
adial segment $[1,2]\cdot u$ from $1\cdot u$ to $2\cdot u\sim 1\cdot u$, i.e. on
ce around the $S^1$ factor.

$$\boxed{\pi_1(X/\sim,\,x_0)\cong\mathbb{Z}.}$$
