# Proof

**Claim:** Yes, there exists a representation of $A_4$ in $\operatorname{GL}_3(\
mathbb{R})$ that preserves the unit 3-ball.

## Key observation

A matrix $M \in \operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb{R})$ preserves the unit ball $B^3 =
\{x \in \mathbb{R}^3 : \|x\| \le 1\}$ if and only if $M \in O(3)$, the orthogona
l group.

*Proof of observation:* If $M$ preserves $B^3$, then $M$ maps the boundary $\par
tial B^3 = S^2$ to itself, so $\|Mx\| = 1$ whenever $\|x\| = 1$. By linearity an
d scaling, $\|Mx\| = \|x\|$ for all $x$, so $M^T M = I$, i.e., $M \in O(3)$. The
 converse is immediate.

Thus the question reduces to: **Does there exist a homomorphism $\rho: A_4 \to O
(3)$?**

## Construction via the regular tetrahedron

Place a regular tetrahedron centered at the origin with vertices at
$$v_1 = (1,1,1),\quad v_2 = (1,-1,-1),\quad v_3 = (-1,1,-1),\quad v_4 = (-1,-1,1
).$$

Each $v_i$ has norm $\sqrt{3}$, and the pairwise dot products are $v_i \cdot v_j
 = -1$ for $i \neq j$, confirming all edges have equal length. The centroid is t
he origin.

Any symmetry of the tetrahedron (as a set of four vertices) permutes $\{v_1, v_2
, v_3, v_4\}$, giving a homomorphism from the symmetry group to $S_4$. The **rot
ational** symmetries (orientation-preserving isometries fixing the origin) form
a subgroup that maps to $A_4 \subset S_4$. This map is an isomorphism:

- **Surjectivity:** Every element of $A_4$ is realized. The identity gives $I$.
The eight 3-cycles correspond to $120°$ rotations about axes through a vertex an
d the opposite face center (4 axes × 2 directions = 8 elements). The three doubl
e transpositions $(12)(34)$, $(13)(24)$, $(14)(23)$ correspond to $180°$ rotatio
ns about axes through the midpoints of opposite edges (3 elements). This account
s for all $1 + 8 + 3 = 12$ elements of $A_4$.

- **Injectivity:** A rotation fixing all four vertices is the identity.

Each such rotation is an orthogonal transformation (it preserves the Euclidean i
nner product, since it's a rigid motion fixing the origin), so each lies in $SO(
3) \subset O(3)$.

## Conclusion

The rotational symmetry group of the regular tetrahedron yields a faithful repre
sentation
$$\rho: A_4 \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \operatorname{Rot}(\text{tetrahedron}) \hookr
ightarrow SO(3) \subset O(3) \subset \operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb{R}).$$

Since every $\rho(g) \in O(3)$, each $\rho(g)$ preserves $\|x\|$ and hence prese
rves the unit 3-ball $B^3$.

Therefore, **yes**, such a representation exists. $\blacksquare$
