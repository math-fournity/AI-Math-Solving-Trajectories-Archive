Proof: $H^1(\operatorname{Gr}(k,n),,\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Gr}(k,n)}) = 0$

Claim. For the Grassmannian $X = \operatorname{Gr}(k,n)$ over $\mathbb{C}$
(parametrizing $k$-dimensional subspaces of $\mathbb{C}^n$), the first
cohomology of the structure sheaf vanishes: $$H^1!\bigl(X,,\mathcal{O}
_X\bigr)=0.$$

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 1. Schubert cell decomposition

The Grassmannian $X = \operatorname{Gr}(k,n)$ admits a Schubert cell
decomposition. The cells are indexed by partitions $\lambda$ fitting inside a $k
\times (n{-}k)$ rectangle. For each such $\lambda$, the corresponding Schubert
cell $C_\lambda$ is isomorphic to an affine space $$C_\lambda ;\cong; \mathbb{A}
^{|\lambda|},$$ where $|\lambda|$ is the number of boxes of $\lambda$. The
closures $\overline{C_\lambda}$ (Schubert varieties) give $X$ the structure of a
CW complex (in the complex-analytic topology) whose cells have real dimension
$2|\lambda|$ — that is, only even real dimensions.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 2. Topological cohomology vanishes in odd degree

A CW complex with cells only in even dimensions has trivial cellular coboundary
maps (there are no cells in adjacent dimensions to support nonzero
coboundaries). Consequently the cellular cohomology satisfies $
$H^i_{\mathrm{top}}!\bigl(X^{\mathrm{an}},,\mathbb{C}\bigr) = 0 \quad\text{for
all odd } i.$$ In particular, $$H^1_{\mathrm{top}}!\bigl(X^{\mathrm{an}},,
\mathbb{C}\bigr) = 0.$$

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 3. Hodge decomposition

Since $X$ is a smooth projective variety, it carries a Hodge decomposition: $
$H^1_{\mathrm{top}}!\bigl(X^{\mathrm{an}},,\mathbb{C}\bigr) ;=; H^{1,0}(X) ;
\oplus; H^{0,1}(X).$$

By Dolbeault's theorem (and Serre's GAGA, identifying algebraic and analytic
coherent cohomology for projective varieties), $$H^{0,1}(X) ;\cong; H^1!
\bigl(X,,\mathcal{O}_X\bigr).$$

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 4. Conclusion

Since $H^1_{\mathrm{top}}(X^{\mathrm{an}},,\mathbb{C}) = 0$ from Step 2, both
summands in the Hodge decomposition vanish: $$H^{1,0}(X) = 0
\quad\text{and}\quad H^{0,1}(X) = 0.$$ Therefore $$\boxed{H^1!
\bigl(\operatorname{Gr}(k,n),,\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Gr}(k,n)}\bigr) = 0.}$$
