### Setup

For $\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})$, the positive roots are $\Phi^+ = \{e_i - e_j
: 1 \le i < j \le n\}$, and the half-sum of positive roots is
$$\delta = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{n+1-2k}{2}\, e_k.$$

The **Freudenthal multiplicity formula** is:
$$\text{mult}(\mu)\bigl[\|\lambda+\delta\|^2 - \|\mu+\delta\|^2\bigr] = 2\sum_{\
alpha\in\Phi^+}\sum_{k\ge 1}(\mu+k\alpha,\,\alpha)\;\text{mult}(\mu+k\alpha),$$
with $\text{mult}(\lambda)=1$ and multiplicities computed recursively from the h
ighest weight down.

The highest weight $\lambda = 2e_1+e_2 = \omega_1+\omega_2$ corresponds to the Y
oung diagram of shape $(2,1)$. The weights of this representation are $e_a+e_b+e
_c$ arising from semistandard Young tableaux (SSYT) of shape $(2,1)$: entries $a
\le b$ in row 1, entry $c$ in row 2, with $a<c$ (column strictly increasing).

### Identifying weights higher than $\mu = e_1+e_2+e_3$

A weight $\mu'$ is higher than $\mu$ iff $\mu'-\mu$ is a non-negative integer co
mbination of positive roots, equivalently the partial sums of the coefficient ve
ctor of $\mu'-\mu$ are all $\ge 0$. Checking all SSYT weights with entries in $\
{1,2,3\}$ (entries $\ge 4$ are excluded by the partial-sum condition), the weigh
ts strictly higher than $\mu$ are:

| Weight | Multiset | SSYT count (mult) |
|---|---|---|
| $\lambda = 2e_1+e_2$ | $\{1,1,2\}$ | 1 (highest weight) |
| $2e_1+e_3$ | $\{1,1,3\}$ | 1 |
| $e_1+2e_2$ | $\{1,2,2\}$ | 1 |

### Recursive computation of higher-weight multiplicities

**mult$(2e_1+e_3)$:** The only weight above it is $\lambda$. We have $2e_1+e_3 +
 (e_2-e_3) = \lambda$, so $\alpha=e_2-e_3$, $k=1$:
$$(\lambda,\, e_2-e_3)=1, \quad \text{sum}=2\cdot 1\cdot 1=2.$$
$$\|\lambda+\delta\|^2-\|2e_1+e_3+\delta\|^2 = 2(\delta,\, e_2-e_3)=2\cdot 1=2.$
$
$$\text{mult}(2e_1+e_3)=2/2=1.\quad\checkmark$$

**mult$(e_1+2e_2)$:** The only weight above it is $\lambda$. We have $e_1+2e_2+(
e_1-e_2)=\lambda$, so $\alpha=e_1-e_2$, $k=1$:
$$(\lambda,\,e_1-e_2)=1,\quad \text{sum}=2\cdot 1\cdot 1=2.$$
$$\|\lambda+\delta\|^2-\|e_1+2e_2+\delta\|^2=2(\delta,\,e_1-e_2)=2\cdot 1=2.$$
$$\text{mult}(e_1+2e_2)=2/2=1.\quad\checkmark$$

### Applying Freudenthal to $\mu = e_1+e_2+e_3$

We enumerate all $(\alpha, k)$ with $\alpha\in\Phi^+$, $k\ge 1$, such that $\mu+
k\alpha$ is a weight. Setting $\mu+k(e_i-e_j) = e_1+e_2+e_3+k\,e_i - k\,e_j$, al
l coefficients must be non-negative. The only valid cases ($k=1$) are:

| $\alpha$ | $k$ | $\mu+k\alpha$ | $(\mu+k\alpha,\,\alpha)$ | mult |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $e_1-e_2$ | 1 | $2e_1+e_3$ | $2$ | $1$ |
| $e_1-e_3$ | 1 | $2e_1+e_2=\lambda$ | $2$ | $1$ |
| $e_2-e_3$ | 1 | $e_1+2e_2$ | $2$ | $1$ |

(All other positive roots or $k\ge 2$ produce negative coefficients, hence non-w
eights.)

**Right-hand side:**
$$2\sum(\mu+k\alpha,\alpha)\,\text{mult}(\mu+k\alpha) = 2(2\cdot1 + 2\cdot1 + 2\
cdot1) = 12.$$

**Denominator:** With $a_k = (n+1-2k)/2$,
$$\|\lambda+\delta\|^2 - \|\mu+\delta\|^2 = (2+a_1)^2-(1+a_1)^2 + 0 + a_3^2-(1+a
_3)^2$$
$$= (3+2a_1) + (-1-2a_3) = 2+2(a_1-a_3) = 2+2\!\left[\tfrac{n-1}{2}-\tfrac{n-5}{
2}\right] = 2+4 = 6.$$

### Result

$$\text{mult}(\mu)\cdot 6 = 12 \implies \boxed{\text{mult}(\mu) = 2}.$$

**Verification:** The SSYT of shape $(2,1)$ with weight $e_1+e_2+e_3$ are exactl
y two:
- $\begin{smallmatrix}1&3\\2\end{smallmatrix}$ (entries $a{=}1,b{=}3,c{=}2$: $1\
le3$, $1<2$ ✓)
- $\begin{smallmatrix}1&2\\3\end{smallmatrix}$ (entries $a{=}1,b{=}2,c{=}3$: $1\
le2$, $1<3$ ✓)

This confirms the Freudenthal computation: **the multiplicity is $2$**.
