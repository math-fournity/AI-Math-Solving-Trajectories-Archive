## Proof

**Yes, such a function exists.** Consider

$$f(z) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n^2}, \qquad |z|<1.$$

**Step 1: $f$ is holomorphic on the open unit disk $D=\{ |z|<1\}$.**

This is a power series with radius of convergence $R=1$, since $\limsup_{n\to\in
fty} |1/n^2|^{1/n}=1$. Hence $f$ is holomorphic on $D$.

**Step 2: $f$ extends continuously to $S^1$.**

For $|z|\le 1$ we have $|z^n/n^2|\le 1/n^2$, and $\sum 1/n^2<\infty$. By the Wei
erstrass $M$-test, the series converges uniformly on the closed disk $\overline{
D}$. Each partial sum is a polynomial (hence continuous), so the uniform limit $
f$ is continuous on $\overline{D}$, and in particular continuous on $S^1$.

**Step 3: $f$ is not holomorphic on $S^1$.**

"Holomorphic on $S^1$" means holomorphic on some open neighborhood of $S^1$. It
suffices to show that $f$ does not extend holomorphically to any open neighborho
od of the point $z=1\in S^1$.

Suppose for contradiction that $f$ extends holomorphically to an open set $U$ wi
th $1\in U$. Then $f'$ is holomorphic on $U\cup D$. On $D$,

$$f'(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n-1}}{n}=-\frac{1}{z}\operatorname{Log}(1-z)
, \qquad |z|<1.$$

Taking $z\in(0,1)$ real and letting $z\to 1^{-}$,

$$f'(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n-1}}{n}\;\xrightarrow[z\to 1^{-}]{}\;\sum_{
n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}=+\infty.$$

But if $f'$ were holomorphic on an open neighborhood $U$ of $1$, then $f'$ would
 be bounded near $1$. This contradicts $f'(z)\to+\infty$ as $z\to 1^{-}$.

Therefore $f$ cannot be extended holomorphically past $z=1$, and consequently $f
$ is not holomorphic on $S^1$.

**Conclusion.** The function $f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^n/n^2$ is holomorphic on
 the open unit disk, continuous on $S^1$, yet not holomorphic on $S^1$. Hence su
ch a function exists.
