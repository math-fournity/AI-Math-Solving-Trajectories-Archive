Looking at this problem, I need to find $k(100, m)$ for each $m$ from 1 to 100,
where $k(100, m)$ is the minimum size of a subset of $\mathbb{Z}/100\mathbb{Z}$
that guarantees two elements $a_p, a_s$ satisfy $a_s - a_p \equiv m \pmod{100}$.

## Setting Up the Graph Model

Choosing $k$ distinct codes from 100 states = choosing a $k$-element subset $S \
subseteq \mathbb{Z}/100\mathbb{Z}$.

The trigger condition $m + a_p - a_s \equiv 0 \pmod{100}$ is equivalent to $a_s
- a_p \equiv m \pmod{100}$.

So $k(100, m) = \alpha_m + 1$, where $\alpha_m$ is the maximum size of a subset
$S \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/100\mathbb{Z}$ with no two distinct elements differing b
y $m \pmod{100}$.

## Computing the Independence Number

Define the graph $G_m$ on $\mathbb{Z}/100\mathbb{Z}$ where $x \sim y$ iff $x - y
 \equiv \pm m \pmod{100}$. Then $\alpha_m$ is the independence number of $G_m$.

Let $d = \gcd(m, 100)$. The map $x \mapsto x + m$ partitions $\mathbb{Z}/100\mat
hbb{Z}$ into $d$ orbits (cycles), each of length $L = 100/d$.

**Key facts:**
- The graph $G_m$ decomposes into $d$ disjoint cycles of length $L = 100/d$.
- The independence number of a cycle $C_L$ is $\lfloor L/2 \rfloor$.

Therefore:
$$\alpha_m = d \cdot \left\lfloor \frac{L}{2} \right\rfloor = d \cdot \left\lflo
or \frac{100}{2d} \right\rfloor = d \cdot \left\lfloor \frac{50}{d} \right\rfloo
r$$

$$k(100, m) = d \cdot \left\lfloor \frac{50}{d} \right\rfloor + 1$$
