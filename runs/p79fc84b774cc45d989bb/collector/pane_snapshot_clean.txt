Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every simple composition fa
ctor of a finite module must appear as an actual submodule.

**Answer: No, this is not true in general.**

**Counterexample.**

Let $k$ be a field and let $R = kQ$ be the path algebra of the quiver $Q: 1 \xri
ghtarrow{\alpha} 2$. An $R$-module is a representation $(V_1, V_2, f)$ where $V_
1, V_2$ are $k$-vector spaces and $f: V_1 \to V_2$ is a linear map (the arrow $\
alpha$ acts as $f$).

**Simple modules.** There are two simple $R$-modules (up to isomorphism):
- $S_1 = (k,\, 0,\, 0)$ — $k$ at vertex 1, $0$ at vertex 2.
- $S_2 = (0,\, k,\, 0)$ — $0$ at vertex 1, $k$ at vertex 2.

These are non-isomorphic since they are supported at different vertices.

**The module $M = P_1$.** Take $M = P_1 := e_1 R$, the indecomposable projective
 at vertex 1. Concretely, $P_1 = (k,\, k,\, \mathrm{id})$ — the representation w
ith $k$ at each vertex and the identity map between them.

**Jordan–Hölder filtration of $P_1$.** The submodules of $P_1 = (k, k, \mathrm{i
d})$ are subrepresentations $(W_1, W_2)$ with $f(W_1) \subseteq W_2$:
- $(0,0)$: trivial. ✓
- $(0, k)$: $f(0)=0 \subseteq k$. ✓ This is $S_2$.
- $(k, 0)$: $f(k) = k \not\subseteq 0$. ✗ **Not a submodule.**
- $(k, k)$: the whole module. ✓

So the only proper nonzero submodule is $(0,k) = S_2$, giving the composition se
ries:
$$0 = M_0 \subset M_1 = S_2 = (0,k) \subset M_2 = P_1 = (k,k,\mathrm{id}).$$

**Composition factors:**
- $M_1/M_0 = S_2/0 \cong S_2$.
- $M_2/M_1 = P_1/S_2 = (k,k,\mathrm{id})/(0,k) \cong (k, 0, 0) = S_1$.

**The factor $S_1$ does not embed in $M = P_1$.** A submodule $N \subseteq P_1$
isomorphic to $S_1 = (k,0,0)$ would have to be a 1-dimensional subrepresentation
. The only 1-dimensional subrepresentation of $P_1$ is $S_2 = (0,k)$, which is n
ot isomorphic to $S_1$ (they are distinct simples). The other candidate $(k,0)$
is not a submodule at all, since the identity map sends $k$ to $k \not\subseteq
0$.

Therefore, for $i = 2$, the composition factor $M_2/M_1 \cong S_1$ is not isomor
phic to any submodule of $M$.

**Conclusion.** The statement is **false** in general. A simple composition fact
or of a module need not be realized as a submodule of that module. The projectiv
e $P_1$ over the path algebra $k(1\to 2)$ provides an explicit counterexample: i
ts top composition factor $S_1$ does not embed in $P_1$.
