## Claim: $M \cap R \neq 0$ need **not** hold. We give a counterexample.

### Setup

Let $p$ be a rational prime and set
$$R = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)} = \left\{\tfrac{a}{b}\in\mathbb{Q} : \gcd(b,p)=1\right\}.
$$
This is the localization of $\mathbb{Z}$ at the prime ideal $(p)$, hence a **PID
** (localization of a PID is a PID). It is a DVR with unique maximal ideal $pR$,
 so it is **not a field**.

Take $n=1$ and define
$$M = (pX_1 - 1) \subset R[X_1].$$

### $M$ is maximal

There is a standard isomorphism: for any commutative ring $A$ and non-zerodiviso
r $a\in A$,
$$A[T]/(aT-1)\;\cong\; A[1/a], \qquad T\mapsto 1/a.$$
Applying this with $A=R$, $a=p$, $T=X_1$:
$$R[X_1]/(pX_1-1)\;\cong\; R[1/p] = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[1/p] = \mathbb{Q}.$$
The last equality holds because inverting $p$ in $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ makes every
nonzero integer invertible (every prime $q\neq p$ was already a unit, and now $p
$ is too), yielding all of $\mathbb{Q}$.

Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is a field, $M=(pX_1-1)$ is a **maximal ideal** of $R[X_1]$.

### $M \cap R = 0$

Suppose $r\in R\cap M$, so $r = (pX_1-1)\cdot g(X_1)$ for some $g(X_1)=\sum_{i=0
}^{m}b_i X_1^i\in R[X_1]$. Expanding:
$$r = \sum_{i=0}^{m} p\,b_i\, X_1^{i+1} - \sum_{i=0}^{m} b_i\, X_1^i.$$
Since $r\in R$ is a constant, every coefficient of $X_1^k$ for $k\geq 1$ vanishe
s. Setting $b_{m+1}:=0$, the coefficient of $X_1^{i+1}$ gives:
$$p\,b_i - b_{i+1} = 0 \quad\text{for } 0\leq i\leq m.$$
From $i=m$: $p\,b_m = b_{m+1}=0$. Since $R$ is an integral domain and $p\neq 0$,
 this forces $b_m=0$. Then from $i=m-1$: $p\,b_{m-1}=b_m=0$, so $b_{m-1}=0$. By
descending induction, $b_i=0$ for all $i$. Therefore
$$r = -b_0 = 0.$$
Hence $M\cap R = 0$.

### Conclusion

We have exhibited a PID $R=\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ (not a field) and a maximal ideal $
M=(pX_1-1)$ of $R[X_1]$ such that $M\cap R = 0$. Therefore the statement "$M\cap
 R\neq 0$" is **false in general**.

**Remark.** The obstruction is that a DVR is *not* a Jacobson ring: the zero ide
al is not an intersection of maximal ideals (there is only one maximal ideal $pR
$). For Jacobson PIDs such as $\mathbb{Z}$ or $k[t]$, the generalized Nullstelle
nsatz forces every maximal ideal of $R[X_1,\dots,X_n]$ to contract to a maximal
(hence nonzero) ideal of $R$. The counterexample exploits precisely this failure
.
