## Solution

**Setup.** Let the line through $M(3,0)$ have slope $k$: $y = k(x-3)$. It meets
the ellipse $\frac{x^2}{4}+y^2=1$ at $P=(x_1,y_1)$ and $Q=(x_2,y_2)$, with $y_i
= k(x_i-3)$. Substituting into the ellipse:

$$(1+4k^2)x^2 - 24k^2 x + 36k^2 - 4 = 0,$$

so $x_1+x_2 = \frac{24k^2}{1+4k^2}$ and $x_1 x_2 = \frac{36k^2-4}{1+4k^2}$.

**Finding $R=(X,Y)$.** Line $N_1P$ (through $(-1,0)$): $Y(x_1+1) = k(x_1-3)(X+1)
$. Line $N_2Q$ (through $(1,0)$): $Y(x_2-1) = k(x_2-3)(X-1)$.

Dividing and cross-multiplying, then solving:

$$X = \frac{2x_1 + x_2 - x_1 x_2}{x_1 - 2x_2 + 3}, \qquad Y = \frac{k(x_1-3)(X+1
)}{x_1+1}.$$

**Parametric simplification.** Set $P=(2\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)$, $Q=(2\cos\beta,
\sin\beta)$, with $u=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}$, $v=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}$. Collin
earity with $M(3,0)$ gives:

$$2\cos v = 3\cos u. \tag{$\star$}$$

Since $k = \frac{\sin\alpha}{2\cos\alpha - 3}$, using $(\star)$ one obtains the
clean relation:

$$k = -\frac{\cos u}{2\sin u} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\cot u.$$

Introduce $w = \sqrt{1-5k^2}$ (valid for $|k|<1/\sqrt{5}$, the range where the s
ecant intersects the ellipse). From $(\star)$ and $k=-\frac{1}{2}\cot u$:

$$\sin u = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+4k^2}},\quad \sin v = \frac{w}{\sqrt{1+4k^2}},\quad
\cos u = \frac{-2k}{\sqrt{1+4k^2}}.$$

Substituting these into the formulas for $X$ and $Y$ and simplifying (the key fa
ctorization is $21-10w-16w^2 = (7-8w)(2w+3)$, which cancels in $Y$):

$$X = \frac{2(2-w)}{3(1-2w)}, \qquad Y = \frac{-5k}{3(1-2w)}.$$

**Verifying the conic.** Compute:

$$X^2 - \tfrac{12}{5}Y^2 = \frac{4(2-w)^2}{9(1-2w)^2} - \frac{20k^2}{3(1-2w)^2}
= \frac{4(2-w)^2 - 60k^2}{9(1-2w)^2}.$$

Since $k^2 = \frac{1-w^2}{5}$, we get $60k^2 = 12(1-w^2)$, so the numerator beco
mes:

$$4(4-4w+w^2) - 12 + 12w^2 = 4 - 16w + 16w^2 = 4(1-2w)^2.$$

Therefore:

$$\boxed{X^2 - \tfrac{12}{5}Y^2 = \frac{4}{9}}.$$

This holds for **every** admissible $k$ (both choices of sign for $w$ give the s
ame identity), so the locus of $R$ is the hyperbola

$$\frac{x^2}{4/9} - \frac{y^2}{5/27} = 1.$$

**Eccentricity.** Here $a^2 = \frac{4}{9}$, $b^2 = \frac{5}{27}$, and

$$c^2 = a^2 + b^2 = \frac{4}{9} + \frac{5}{27} = \frac{12+5}{27} = \frac{17}{27}
.$$

Thus

$$e = \frac{c}{a} = \frac{\sqrt{17/27}}{\sqrt{4/9}} = \sqrt{\frac{17}{27}\cdot\f
rac{9}{4}} = \sqrt{\frac{17}{12}} = \frac{\sqrt{51}}{6}.$$

The eccentricity of the conic is $\dfrac{\sqrt{51}}{6}$.
