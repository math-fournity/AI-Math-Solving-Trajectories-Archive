**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof:**

Let $P$ be an $N \times N$ transition matrix that is irreducible, aperiodic (one
-periodic), and diagonalizable, with unique stationary distribution $\pi$ and st
ationary matrix $\Pi = \mathbf{1}\pi^T$.

**Step 1: Algebraic identity $(P-\Pi)^n = P^n - \Pi$.**

Since $P$ is stochastic: $P\mathbf{1} = \mathbf{1}$ and $\pi^T P = \pi^T$. These
 give:
- $P\Pi = P\mathbf{1}\pi^T = \mathbf{1}\pi^T = \Pi$
- $\Pi P = \mathbf{1}\pi^T P = \mathbf{1}\pi^T = \Pi$
- $\Pi^2 = \mathbf{1}\pi^T\mathbf{1}\pi^T = \mathbf{1}\pi^T = \Pi$

Therefore $(P-\Pi)^2 = P^2 - P\Pi - \Pi P + \Pi^2 = P^2 - \Pi$, and by induction
:

$$(P - \Pi)^n = P^n - \Pi \quad \text{for all } n \geq 1.$$

**Step 2: Spectral radius of $P - \Pi$.**

Since $P$ is diagonalizable, $P = V\Lambda V^{-1}$ with $\Lambda = \mathrm{diag}
(1, \lambda_2, \dots, \lambda_N)$. The matrix $P - \Pi$ shares the same eigenvec
tors, with eigenvalues $0, \lambda_2, \dots, \lambda_N$ (the Perron eigenvalue $
1$ is replaced by $0$). By the Perron–Frobenius theorem (irreducibility + aperio
dicity), $|\lambda_k| < 1$ for all $k \geq 2$. The spectral radius is:

$$\rho(P - \Pi) = \max_{k \geq 2} |\lambda_k|,$$

which is the second-largest eigenvalue modulus (SLEM) of $P$.

**Step 3: Total variation as a matrix norm.**

Define $\|A\|_{TV} = \max_{1 \le i \le N} \frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^N |A_{ij}|$. Thi
s is a norm on the finite-dimensional space of $N \times N$ matrices. The worst-
case total variation distance from stationarity is:

$$d(n) = \max_i \|P^n(i,\cdot) - \pi\|_{TV} = \|P^n - \Pi\|_{TV} = \|(P-\Pi)^n\|
_{TV}.$$

**Step 4: Gelfand's spectral radius formula.**

Gelfand's formula states $\rho(A) = \lim_{n\to\infty} \|A^n\|^{1/n}$ for any mat
rix $A$ and **any** norm $\|\cdot\|$ on a finite-dimensional space. The result e
xtends beyond submultiplicative norms because all norms on a finite-dimensional
space are equivalent: if $\|\cdot\|_*$ is submultiplicative and $\|\cdot\|$ is a
ny norm, then $c\|A\|_* \le \|A\| \le C\|A\|$ for constants $c, C > 0$, so $c^{1
/n}\|A^n\|_*^{1/n} \le \|A^n\|^{1/n} \le C^{1/n}\|A^n\|_*^{1/n}$, and $c^{1/n},
C^{1/n} \to 1$.

Applying this with $A = P - \Pi$ and $\|\cdot\| = \|\cdot\|_{TV}$:

$$\rho(P - \Pi) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \|(P-\Pi)^n\|_{TV}^{1/n} = \lim_{n \to \in
fty} d(n)^{1/n}.$$

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} \log d(n) = \log\!\left(\max_{k \geq 2} |\lamb
da_k|\right).$$

The exponential rate of convergence in total variation norm is exactly $\max_{k
\ge 2}|\lambda_k|$, the second-largest eigenvalue modulus (SLEM) of $P$. The dia
