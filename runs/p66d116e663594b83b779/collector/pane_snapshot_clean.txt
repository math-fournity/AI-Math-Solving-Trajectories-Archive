## Solution

A graph on $n$ vertices has at most $\binom{n}{2}$ edges. For $n = 25$:

$$\binom{25}{2} = \frac{25 \cdot 24}{2} = 300.$$

The problem asks for graphs with **303 edges** on 25 vertices. Since $303 > 300$
, no such (simple) graph can exist.

The hint to consider complements confirms this directly: a graph $G$ on 25 verti
ces with 303 edges would have a complement $\overline{G}$ with

$$\binom{25}{2} - 303 = 300 - 303 = -3$$

edges, which is impossible. Therefore no graph on 25 vertices can have 303 edges
.

Since there are no graphs with 25 vertices and 303 edges, there are no non-isomo
rphic such graphs.

**The number of non-isomorphic graphs with 25 vertices and 303 edges is $\boxed{
0}$.**
