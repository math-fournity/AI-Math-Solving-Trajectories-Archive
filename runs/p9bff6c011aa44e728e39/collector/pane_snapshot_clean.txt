## Solution

**Setup and conventions.** Let $G = \mathbb{Z} = \langle t \rangle$ be the infin
ite cyclic group, and let $M = \mathbb{Z}_2 G = \mathbb{Z}_2[\mathbb{Z}] = \math
bb{Z}_2[t, t^{-1}]$ be the group ring over $\mathbb{Z}_2 = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z
}$. We take the natural $G$-module structure on $M$ given by left multiplication
: $t$ acts on $\mathbb{Z}_2[t,t^{-1}]$ by $t \cdot f(t) = t\,f(t)$.

**Step 1: A free resolution of $\mathbb{Z}$ over $\mathbb{Z}[G]$.** For the infi
nite cyclic group $G = \langle t \rangle$, the group ring is $\mathbb{Z}[G] = \m
athbb{Z}[t, t^{-1}]$, and there is a free resolution of the trivial module $\mat
hbb{Z}$ of length 1:

$$0 \longrightarrow \mathbb{Z}[G] \xrightarrow{\;\cdot(t-1)\;} \mathbb{Z}[G] \xr
ightarrow{\;\varepsilon\;} \mathbb{Z} \longrightarrow 0,$$

where $\varepsilon$ is the augmentation map ($\varepsilon(t) = 1$) and the conne
cting map is multiplication by $t - 1$. This is exact: $\ker\varepsilon$ is the
augmentation ideal, which is generated by $t - 1$ in $\mathbb{Z}[t, t^{-1}]$, an
d multiplication by $t - 1$ is injective on the domain $\mathbb{Z}[t, t^{-1}]$.

**Step 2: Apply $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(-, M)$.** Since $\operatorna
me{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(\mathbb{Z}[G], M) \cong M$ (via $f \mapsto f(1)$), the c
ochain complex becomes

$$0 \longrightarrow M \xrightarrow{\;d^1\;} M \longrightarrow 0,$$

where the coboundary map $d^1: M \to M$ is precomposition with $\cdot(t-1)$. Con
cretely, for $m \in M$ identified with the homomorphism $f$ satisfying $f(1) = m
$:

$$d^1(m) = f\big((t-1)\cdot 1\big) = f(t) - f(1) = t\cdot m - m = (t-1)\cdot m.$
$

So $d^1$ is the map $m \mapsto (t-1)m$, i.e., multiplication by $t - 1$ in the r
ing $M = \mathbb{Z}_2[t, t^{-1}]$.

**Step 3: Compute $H^1$.** By definition,

$$H^1(G, M) = \operatorname{coker}(d^1) = M \big/ (t-1)M.$$

Here $(t-1)M$ is the (left) ideal generated by $t - 1$ in $\mathbb{Z}_2[t, t^{-1
}]$. Since $\operatorname{char}(\mathbb{Z}_2) = 2$, we have $t - 1 = t + 1$, but
 this does not change the ideal.

**Step 4: Identify the quotient.** Consider the augmentation map restricted to $
M$:

$$\varepsilon: \mathbb{Z}_2[t, t^{-1}] \longrightarrow \mathbb{Z}_2, \qquad \sum
_i a_i t^i \longmapsto \sum_i a_i.$$

This is a surjective ring homomorphism (it sends $t \mapsto 1$, hence $t^{-1} \m
apsto 1$). Its kernel is the augmentation ideal $I = \{\sum a_i t^i : \sum a_i =
 0 \text{ in } \mathbb{Z}_2\}$.

**Claim:** $I = (t-1)\mathbb{Z}_2[t, t^{-1}]$.

*Proof of claim.* The inclusion $(t-1) \subseteq I$ is clear since $\varepsilon(
t-1) = 1 - 1 = 0$. For the reverse, any element of $I$ is a finite sum $\sum a_i
 t^i$ with $\sum a_i = 0$ in $\mathbb{Z}_2$. Each such element is a $\mathbb{Z}_
2$-linear combination of terms $t^i - 1$ (since $\sum a_i t^i = \sum a_i(t^i - 1
)$ when $\sum a_i = 0$). For $i > 0$:

$$t^i - 1 = (t-1)(t^{i-1} + t^{i-2} + \cdots + t + 1),$$

and for $i < 0$, writing $i = -j$ with $j > 0$:

$$t^{-j} - 1 = t^{-j}(1 - t^j) = -t^{-j}(t^j - 1) = t^{-j}(t-1)(t^{j-1} + \cdots
 + 1) \in (t-1),$$

(using $-1 = 1$ in $\mathbb{Z}_2$). Hence $I \subseteq (t-1)$, proving the claim
. $\square$

By the First Isomorphism Theorem:

$$\mathbb{Z}_2[t, t^{-1}] \big/ (t-1)\mathbb{Z}_2[t, t^{-1}] \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z
}_2.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{H^1\!\big(\mathbb{Z},\; \mathbb{Z}_2[\mathbb{Z}]\big) \;\cong\; \mathbb
{Z}_2 \;=\; \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.}$$

**Remark.** The key points are: (1) for $G = \mathbb{Z}$, the cohomology is comp
uted from the length-1 resolution, giving $H^1(G,M) = M/(t-1)M$; (2) with $M = \
mathbb{Z}_2[G]$ and the left-regular action, $(t-1)M$ is exactly the augmentatio
n ideal; (3) the quotient by the augmentation ideal is $\mathbb{Z}_2$ via the au
gmentation map. The fact that $\mathbb{Z}_2[G]$ is *not* coinduced/injective as
a $G$-module (unlike over $\mathbb{Z}$, where $\mathbb{Z}[G]$ would give $H^1 =
0$) is what makes the answer nontrivial—the characteristic-2 structure prevents
the usual cancellation.
