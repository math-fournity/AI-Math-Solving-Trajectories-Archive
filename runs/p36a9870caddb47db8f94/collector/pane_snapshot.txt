**Solution.**

**Setup and graph formulation.** Let $M$ be a set of real numbers satisfying con
ditions (i) and (ii). From (ii), every $x \in M$ is irrational (if $x \in \mathb
b{Q}$ then $x^2 \in \mathbb{Q}$) and $x \neq 0$.

Define a graph $G$ on vertex set $M$ where distinct $x, y$ are adjacent ($x \sim
 y$) iff $x + y \in \mathbb{Q}$. By condition (i), the remaining pairs (non-edge
s of $G$) satisfy $xy \in \mathbb{Q}$; let $G'$ be the complementary graph (edge
 set partitioning $K_{|M|}$ with $G$). So:
- $x \sim y$ in $G$ $\iff$ $x+y \in \mathbb{Q}$ and $xy \notin \mathbb{Q}$,
- $x \sim y$ in $G'$ $\iff$ $xy \in \mathbb{Q}$ and $x+y \notin \mathbb{Q}$.

**Both $G$ and $G'$ are triangle-free.** If $x \sim y$ and $x \sim z$ in $G$, th
en $x+y, x+z \in \mathbb{Q}$, so $y - z \in \mathbb{Q}$. If also $y \sim z$ in $
G$, then $y + z \in \mathbb{Q}$, giving $2y \in \mathbb{Q}$, hence $y \in \mathb
b{Q}$ — contradicting (ii). So $G$ is triangle-free. Likewise, if $x \sim y$ and
 $x \sim z$ in $G'$, then $xy, xz \in \mathbb{Q}$ (with $x \neq 0$), so $y/z \in
 \mathbb{Q}$, i.e. $y = qz$ for some $q \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$. If also $y \sim
z$ in $G'$, then $yz = qz^2 \in \mathbb{Q}$, forcing $z^2 \in \mathbb{Q}$ — cont
radicting (ii). So $G'$ is triangle-free.

**Key structural property.** If $x \sim y$ and $x \sim z$ in $G$, then $y - z \i
n \mathbb{Q}$, so $y + z = 2y - (y-z) \notin \mathbb{Q}$ (else $y \in \mathbb{Q}
$). Thus $y \not\sim z$ in $G$, meaning $y \sim z$ in $G'$. So **$N_G(x)$ is a c
lique in $G'$**. Symmetrically, **$N_{G'}(x)$ is a clique in $G$**.

**Bounding $|M|$.** For any $x \in M$, the neighborhood $N_G(x)$ is a clique in
the triangle-free graph $G'$, so $|N_G(x)| \leq 2$. Likewise $|N_{G'}(x)| \leq 2
$. Since every other vertex is adjacent to $x$ in exactly one of $G, G'$:
$$|M| - 1 = |N_G(x)| + |N_{G'}(x)| \leq 4, \quad \text{so } |M| \leq 5.$$

**Ruling out $|M| = 5$.** If $|M| = 5$, then $|N_G(x)| = |N_{G'}(x)| = 2$ for ev
ery $x$, so both $G$ and $G'$ are 2-regular on 5 vertices, i.e. both are 5-cycle
s. Label the vertices so $G$ has edges $x_1x_2, x_2x_3, x_3x_4, x_4x_5, x_5x_1$.
 Then:
$$x_1+x_2,\; x_2+x_3,\; x_3+x_4,\; x_4+x_5,\; x_5+x_1 \in \mathbb{Q}.$$
From $x_1+x_2 \in \mathbb{Q}$ and $x_2+x_3 \in \mathbb{Q}$: $x_1 - x_3 \in \math
bb{Q}$. From $x_3+x_4 \in \mathbb{Q}$ and $x_4+x_5 \in \mathbb{Q}$: $x_3 - x_5 \
in \mathbb{Q}$. Hence $x_1 - x_5 \in \mathbb{Q}$. But $x_5 + x_1 \in \mathbb{Q}$
, so $2x_1 \in \mathbb{Q}$, giving $x_1 \in \mathbb{Q}$ — contradicting (ii). Th
us $|M| \leq 4$.

**Construction achieving $|M| = 4$.** Let $d = 2$ and set
$$x_1 = 1+\sqrt{2},\quad x_2 = \sqrt{2}-1,\quad x_3 = -2-\sqrt{2},\quad x_4 = 2-
\sqrt{2}.$$

*Condition (ii):* $x_1^2 = 3+2\sqrt{2}$, $x_2^2 = 3-2\sqrt{2}$, $x_3^2 = 6+4\sqr
t{2}$, $x_4^2 = 6-4\sqrt{2}$ — all irrational. ✓
