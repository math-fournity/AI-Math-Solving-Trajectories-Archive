Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest positive integer $n$ with $
s(n) = s(n+864) = 20$.

**Key identity.** When adding two numbers in base 10, the digit sum satisfies:
$$s(a+b) = s(a) + s(b) - 9c$$
where $c$ is the total number of carries produced during the addition.

**Applying to our problem.** Since $s(864) = 8+6+4 = 18$, the condition $s(n+864
) = s(n) = 20$ gives:
$$20 = 20 + 18 - 9c \implies 9c = 18 \implies c = 2.$$

So I need **exactly 2 carries** when adding $864$ to $n$, and $s(n) = 20$.

**Carry structure.** Write $n = \ldots d_3 d_2 d_1 d_0$. Adding $864$:
- **Ones:** $d_0 + 4 \ge 10$ gives carry $c_1$ (requires $d_0 \ge 6$).
- **Tens:** $d_1 + 6 + c_1 \ge 10$ gives carry $c_2$.
- **Hundreds:** $d_2 + 8 + c_2 \ge 10$ gives carry $c_3$.
- **Thousands+:** $d_3 + c_3 \ge 10$ gives carry $c_4$, etc.

Carries propagate right-to-left; a carry at position $k+1$ requires a carry at p
osition $k$ to propagate beyond the hundreds place. The valid patterns with exac
tly 2 carries are:

| Pattern | Carries at | Constraints |
|---------|-----------|-------------|
| $(1,1,0)$ | ones, tens | $d_0\ge6,\ d_1\ge3,\ d_2=0$ |
| $(1,0,1)$ | ones, hundreds | $d_0\ge6,\ d_1\le2,\ d_2\ge2$ |
| $(0,1,1)$ | tens, hundreds | $d_0\le5,\ d_1\ge4,\ d_2\ge1$ |
| $(0,0,1,1)$ | hundreds, thousands | $d_0\le5,\ d_1\le3,\ d_2\ge2,\ d_3=9$ |

**No 2-digit solution.** A 2-digit number has maximum digit sum $9+9=18 < 20$, s
o $n$ needs at least 3 digits.

**Case $(1,1,0)$:** $d_2=0$ forces $n$ to be 2-digit (if $d_3=0$), impossible. W
ith $d_3\ge1$: $s(n)=d_3+d_1+d_0=20$, $d_1\ge3$, $d_0\ge6$. Smallest: $d_3=2$, $
d_1=9$, $d_0=9$ gives $n=2099$.

**Case $(1,0,1)$:** 3-digit: $d_2\ge2$, $d_1\le2$, $d_0\ge6$, $d_2+d_1+d_0=20$.
Since $\max(d_1+d_0)=2+9=11$, we need $d_2\ge9$. Then $d_2=9$, $d_1+d_0=11$, $d_
1=2$, $d_0=9$ gives $n=929$.

**Case $(0,1,1)$:** 3-digit: $d_2\ge1$, $d_1\ge4$, $d_0\le5$, $d_2+d_1+d_0=20$.
Since $\max(d_1+d_0)=9+5=14$, we need $d_2\ge6$. Then $d_2=6$, $d_1+d_0=14$, giv
ing $d_1=9$, $d_0=5$, so $n=695$.

**Case $(0,0,1,1)$:** Requires $d_3=9$ (4-digit), giving $n\ge9335$ — much large
r.

**The minimum across all cases is $n = 695$.**

**Verification.**
- $s(695) = 6 + 9 + 5 = 20$. ✓

- Carries: $5+4=9$ (no carry), $9+6=15$ (carry 1), $6+8+1=15$ (carry 1). Exactly
