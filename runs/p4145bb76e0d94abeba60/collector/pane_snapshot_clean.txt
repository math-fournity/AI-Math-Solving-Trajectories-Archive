## Proof

**Convention.** We use the standard convention that topological groups are Hausd
orff (so compact groups are Hausdorff). Under this convention, "nontrivial topol
ogy" is automatically satisfied for any infinite compact group (Hausdorff rules
out the indiscrete topology for nontrivial groups; compact + discrete implies fi
nite, ruling out the discrete topology for infinite groups). We prove the answer
 is **Yes**.

Let $G$ be a compact, infinite, non-abelian (Hausdorff) topological group. The t
rivial subgroup $\{e\}$ and $G$ itself are always closed. We show there exists a
 third distinct closed subgroup.

---

### Case 1: $Z(G) \neq \{e\}$.

The center $Z(G)$ is closed: it equals $\bigcap_{x\in G}\{g\in G : gxg^{-1}x^{-1
}=e\}$, an intersection of closed sets (each is the preimage of the closed singl
eton $\{e\}$ under a continuous commutator map). Since $G$ is non-abelian, $Z(G)
\neq G$. By assumption $Z(G)\neq\{e\}$. Thus $\{e\},\; Z(G),\; G$ are three dist
inct closed subgroups. $\checkmark$

---

### Case 2: $Z(G) = \{e\}$.

Let $G_0$ denote the connected component of the identity — a closed normal subgr
oup.

**Subcase 2a: $\{e\} \subsetneq G_0 \subsetneq G$.**
Then $\{e\},\; G_0,\; G$ are three distinct closed subgroups. $\checkmark$

**Subcase 2b: $G_0 = G$ (i.e., $G$ is connected).**

By the structure theorem for compact connected groups, $G/Z(G) \cong \prod_{i\in
 I} S_i$ where each $S_i$ is a compact, connected, centerless, simple Lie group.
 Since $Z(G)=\{e\}$, we have $G \cong \prod_{i\in I} S_i$.

Since $G$ is non-abelian, $|I|\geq 1$ (a product over an empty index set is triv
ial, hence abelian).

- **If $|I|\geq 2$:** For any $j\in I$, the kernel of the projection $\pi_j : G
\to S_j$ is $\prod_{i\neq j} S_i$, which is a nontrivial (since $|I|\geq 2$ and
each $S_i$ is nontrivial) proper closed normal subgroup. Thus $\{e\},\; \ker\pi_
j,\; G$ are three distinct closed subgroups. $\checkmark$

- **If $|I|=1$:** Then $G\cong S_1$, a compact, connected, centerless, simple Li
e group. Such a group has dimension $\geq 3$ (the smallest example is $\mathrm{S
O}(3)$, dimension 3). It possesses a **maximal torus** $T$ — a connected, closed
, abelian subgroup of dimension equal to the rank of $S_1$, which is $\geq 1$. S
ince $\dim T \geq 1$, we have $T\neq\{e\}$; since $T$ is abelian and $G$ is non-
abelian, $T\neq G$. Thus $\{e\},\; T,\; G$ are three distinct closed subgroups.
$\checkmark$

**Subcase 2c: $G_0 = \{e\}$ (i.e., $G$ is totally disconnected).**

A compact Hausdorff totally disconnected group is **profinite**: $G \cong \varpr
ojlim G_i$ for an inverse system of finite groups $G_i$. The open normal subgrou
ps $N_i = \ker(G \to G_i)$ form a base of neighborhoods of $e$.

Since $G$ is infinite, not all $G_i$ are trivial (an inverse limit of trivial gr
oups is trivial). So some $G_j$ is nontrivial, meaning $|G_j|\geq 2$. The corres
ponding kernel $N_j$ is:
- **Open** (hence **closed**), as the kernel of a continuous map to a discrete (
finite) group.
- **Proper**: $N_j \neq G$ since $G_j$ is nontrivial.
- **Nontrivial**: $[G : N_j] = |G_j| < \infty$, and $G$ is infinite, so $N_j$ is
 infinite, hence $N_j \neq \{e\}$.

Thus $\{e\},\; N_j,\; G$ are three distinct closed subgroups. $\checkmark$

---

### Conclusion

In every case, $G$ possesses at least three distinct closed subgroups. Therefore
:

**Yes, every compact infinite non-abelian topological group with a nontrivial to
pology has at least three distinct closed subgroups.** $\blacksquare$
