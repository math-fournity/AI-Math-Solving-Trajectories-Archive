## Proof

**Claim:** For every $c \in \mathbb{C}$, the Julia set $J(f_c)$ of $f_c(z) = z^2
 + c$ has cardinality $2^{\aleph_0}$. In particular, the cardinality does **not*
* depend on the parameter.

The proof rests on three classical facts.

---

### Fact 1: $J(f_c)$ is nonempty.

For any polynomial of degree $d \geq 2$, the Julia set is nonempty. To see this,
 note that $f_c$ has fixed points (solutions of $z^2 + c = z$, i.e., $z = \frac{
1 \pm \sqrt{1 - 4c}}{2}$), so the filled Julia set $K(f_c) = \{z : \{f_c^n(z)\}_
{n \geq 0} \text{ is bounded}\}$ is nonempty. Since $J(f_c) = \partial K(f_c)$ a
nd $K(f_c)$ is a nonempty compact proper subset of $\mathbb{C}$ (points with $|z
|$ large escape to $\infty$), its boundary is nonempty.

More directly, by the Fatou–Julia theorem, $J(f_c)$ is the closure of the set of
 repelling periodic points. Every polynomial of degree $\geq 2$ has repelling pe
riodic points (for instance, $f_c^n$ has $d^n$ fixed points counted with multipl
icity, and at most finitely many can be non-repelling), so $J(f_c) \neq \emptyse
t$.

---

### Fact 2: $J(f_c)$ is perfect (has no isolated points).

For a polynomial $f$ of degree $d \geq 2$, the only **exceptional point** (a poi
nt with finite backward orbit $\bigcup_{n \geq 0} f^{-n}(z)$) is $\infty$. Since
 $\infty$ is a superattracting fixed point, it belongs to the Fatou set, not the
 Julia set.

For any non-exceptional point $z_0 \in J(f)$, the **backward orbit** $\bigcup_{n
 \geq 1} f^{-n}(z_0)$ is dense in $J(f)$ (this is a fundamental theorem of Fatou
 and Julia). Each point in $f^{-n}(z_0)$ for $n \geq 1$ is distinct from $z_0$ (
since $z_0$ is not exceptional, its backward orbit is infinite and accumulates o
n $J(f)$). Therefore $z_0$ is a limit point of $J(f) \setminus \{z_0\}$, so $z_0
$ is not isolated. Since $z_0 \in J(f)$ was arbitrary and $J(f)$ contains no exc
eptional points, $J(f)$ is perfect.

---

### Fact 3: Every nonempty perfect subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$ has cardinality $2^{
\aleph_0}$.

Let $P \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ be nonempty and perfect. We construct a **Cantor
scheme**: a family of closed balls $\{B_s\}_{s \in \{0,1\}^{<\omega}}$ indexed b
y finite binary strings, satisfying:

- $B_\emptyset$ contains a point of $P$.
- For each finite string $s$, the balls $B_{s0}$ and $B_{s1}$ are disjoint, cont
ained in $B_s$, and each contains a point of $P$.
- $\operatorname{diam}(B_s) \to 0$ as $|s| \to \infty$.

This construction is possible because every point of $P$ is a limit point, so wi
thin any ball intersecting $P$, we can find two distinct points of $P$ and place
 disjoint sub-balls around them.

For each infinite binary sequence $\alpha \in \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$, the nested
balls $B_{\alpha|_n}$ have diameters tending to $0$, so by the Cantor intersecti
on theorem, $\bigcap_{n} B_{\alpha|_n}$ is a single point $p_\alpha \in P$ (usin
g that $P$ is closed, being a compact Julia set, or more generally that $P$ is c
losed in $\mathbb{R}^n$). If $\alpha \neq \beta$, then $p_\alpha \neq p_\beta$ b
ecause the balls diverge at the first bit where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ differ and
remain disjoint thereafter.

Thus the map $\alpha \mapsto p_\alpha$ is an injection $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}} \ho
okrightarrow P$, giving $|P| \geq 2^{\aleph_0}$. Since $P \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n
$ and $|\mathbb{R}^n| = 2^{\aleph_0}$, we conclude $|P| = 2^{\aleph_0}$.

---

### Conclusion

For every $c \in \mathbb{C}$:
- $J(f_c)$ is a nonempty perfect subset of $\mathbb{C} \cong \mathbb{R}^2$ (Fact
s 1 and 2).
- Therefore $|J(f_c)| = 2^{\aleph_0}$ (Fact 3).

The cardinality of the Julia set is **always** $2^{\aleph_0}$, independent of th
e parameter $c$. It is never countable, and it does not depend on the parameter.

**Remark.** This result holds not just for the quadratic family but for the Juli
a set of *any* polynomial (or more generally, any rational map) of degree $\geq
2$: the Julia set is always a nonempty perfect set, hence always of cardinality
$2^{\aleph_0}$. The topological complexity of the Julia set (e.g., connected vs.
 totally disconnected, fractal dimension) varies dramatically with the parameter
, but its **cardinality** is an invariant: always the continuum.
