## Proof

**Answer: Yes.**

Let $p$ be any prime, and define
$$H = \mathbb{Z}\!\left[\tfrac{1}{p}\right] = \left\{\frac{a}{p^{k}} : a\in\math
bb{Z},\ k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}.$$

**$H$ is a subgroup of $(\mathbb{Q},+)$.** The sum of $a/p^{k}$ and $b/p^{m}$ is
 $(a\,p^{m}+b\,p^{k})/p^{k+m}\in H$, and the negative of $a/p^{k}$ is $-a/p^{k}\
in H$. So $H\leqslant(\mathbb{Q},+)$.

**$H$ is proper.** Pick a prime $q\neq p$. Then $1/q\notin H$: if $1/q=a/p^{k}$,
 then $p^{k}=aq$, which is impossible since $q\nmid p^{k}$. Hence $H\subsetneq\m
athbb{Q}$.

**$H$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}$.** Let $x\in\mathbb{R}$ and $\varepsilon>0$. Choo
se $k$ large enough that $p^{-k}<\varepsilon$. The set $\frac{1}{p^{k}}\mathbb{Z
}=\{a/p^{k}:a\in\mathbb{Z}\}$ is an arithmetic progression with common differenc
e $1/p^{k}$, so it meets every interval of length $1/p^{k}$. In particular, ther
e exists $a\in\mathbb{Z}$ with
$$\left|\frac{a}{p^{k}}-x\right|\leqslant\frac{1}{2p^{k}}<\varepsilon.$$
Since $a/p^{k}\in H$, every neighborhood of $x$ contains a point of $H$. As $x$
was arbitrary, $\overline{H}=\mathbb{R}$.

Thus $H=\mathbb{Z}[1/p]$ is a proper subgroup of $(\mathbb{Q},+)$ that is dense
in $\mathbb{R}$. $\blacksquare$
