## Proof

Let $K \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ be a convex body with surface area $S = S(\partial
K)$, and let $A(u)$ denote the area of the orthogonal projection of $K$ onto a p
lane with unit normal $u$, where $u$ ranges uniformly over the unit sphere $S^2$
.

**Step 1: Express $A(u)$ as a surface integral.**

Assume first that $\partial K$ is smooth with outward unit normal $n(x)$ and are
a element $dA$. The orthogonal projection onto a plane perpendicular to $u$ coll
apses each surface element $dA$ by the factor $|n(x)\cdot u|$ (the absolute valu
e accounts for both the front- and back-facing portions of the surface, which pr
oject onto the same region). Hence

$$A(u) = \frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial K} |n(x)\cdot u|\, dA(x).$$

The factor $1/2$ appears because integrating $|n\cdot u|$ over the *entire* clos
ed surface counts both the front cap ($n\cdot u > 0$) and the back cap ($n\cdot
u < 0$), each of which alone already gives the full projection area.

**Step 2: Average over all directions $u$.**

The average projection area is

$$\overline{A} = \frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{S^2} A(u)\, d\sigma(u)
= \frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{S^2} \frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial K}|n(x)\cdot u|\, dA(x)\, d
\sigma(u).$$

Swapping the order of integration (Fubini, since everything is bounded and non-n
egative):

$$\overline{A} = \frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\partial K}\left[\int_{S^2}|n(x)\cdot u|\,
d\sigma(u)\right] dA(x).$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the inner integral.**

For any fixed unit vector $n$, by rotational symmetry of the sphere,

$$\int_{S^2}|n\cdot u|\, d\sigma(u) = \int_{S^2}|u_3|\, d\sigma(u).$$

Using spherical coordinates with $u_3 = \cos\theta$,

$$\int_{S^2}|u_3|\, d\sigma = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{\pi} |\cos\theta|\sin\the
ta\, d\theta\, d\varphi = 2\pi\cdot 2\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos\theta\sin\theta\, d\thet
a = 2\pi\cdot 2\cdot \tfrac{1}{2} = 2\pi.$$

**Step 4: Conclude.**

Substituting back,

$$\overline{A} = \frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\partial K} 2\pi\, dA(x) = \frac{2\pi}{8\pi
}\, S(\partial K) = \frac{1}{4}\, S.$$

**Step 5: Extension to general convex bodies.**

The formula extends from smooth bodies to arbitrary convex bodies by approximati
on: any convex body is the limit (in Hausdorff distance) of smooth strictly conv
ex bodies, and both the surface area and the average projection area are continu
ous under such limits. Thus the identity $\overline{A} = S/4$ holds for every co
nvex body.

Therefore the average area of the orthogonal projection of a convex body onto a
plane, expressed as a fraction of the surface area, is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{4}}.$$
