Looking at this problem, I need to maximize $M = 4(a^3+b^3+c^3) - (a^4+b^4+c^4)$
 subject to $a^2+b^2+c^2 \le 8$.

**Setting up.** Define $f(x) = 4x^3 - x^4 = x^3(4-x)$, so $M = f(a)+f(b)+f(c)$.

**Analysis of $f$.** We have $f'(x) = 12x^2 - 4x^3 = 4x^2(3-x)$, so $f$ is incre
asing on $(0,3)$ and decreasing on $(3,\infty)$. Also $f(x) < 0$ for $x < 0$ (si
nce $x^3 < 0$ and $4-x > 0$) and for $x > 4$.

**All variables non-negative.** If any $x_i < 0$, then $f(x_i) < 0$ while $x_i^2
$ still consumes budget. Replacing $x_i$ with $|x_i|$ keeps the constraint satis
fied and increases $M$ (since $f(|x_i|) \ge 0 > f(x_i)$). So WLOG $a, b, c \ge 0
$.

**Constraint is active.** Since $\max_i x_i \le \sqrt{8} \approx 2.828 < 3$, eve
ry variable lies in $[0,3)$ where $f$ is strictly increasing (for $x > 0$). If t
he constraint were slack, we could increase some variable and raise $M$. So $a^2
+b^2+c^2 = 8$.

**Lagrange multiplier analysis.** At an optimum with $\sum x_i^2 = 8$, for each
$x_i > 0$:
$$f'(x_i) = 2\lambda x_i \implies 4x_i^2(3-x_i) = 2\lambda x_i \implies g(x_i) :
= 6x_i - 2x_i^2 = \lambda.$$

Since $g(x) = -2(x-\tfrac{3}{2})^2 + \tfrac{9}{2}$ is a downward parabola, the e
quation $g(x) = \lambda$ has **at most two solutions**. Thus the nonzero variabl
es take at most two distinct values $p, q$ with either $p = q$ or $p + q = 3$ (f
rom $g(p)=g(q) \Rightarrow (p-q)(3-p-q)=0$).

**Enumerating candidates.**

*Case 1: All three equal.* $a=b=c=\sqrt{8/3} \approx 1.633$. Then $M = 3f(\sqrt{
8/3}) \approx 3 \times 10.31 \approx 30.93$.

*Case 2: Two equal to $p$, one equal to $q=3-p$, all nonzero.* Constraint: $2p^2
+(3-p)^2=8 \Rightarrow 3p^2-6p+1=0$, giving $p = 1+\sqrt{6}/3 \approx 1.817$, $q
 \approx 1.183$. Then $M = 2f(p)+f(q) \approx 30.85$.

*Case 3: One equal to $p$, two equal to $q=3-p$, all nonzero.* Constraint: $p^2+
2(3-p)^2=8 \Rightarrow 3p^2-12p+10=0$, giving $p \approx 2.817$, $q \approx 0.18
3$. Then $M \approx 26.52$.

*Case 4: One variable is 0, other two equal.* $a=b=2, c=0$: $M = 2f(2) = 2(4\cdo
t 8 - 16) = 2 \times 16 = \mathbf{32}$.

*Case 5: One variable is 0, other two with $p+q=3$.* $p^2+q^2=8, p+q=3 \Rightarr
ow 2p^2-6p+1=0$, giving $p \approx 2.823, q \approx 0.177$. Then $M \approx 26.5
2$.

*Case 6: Two variables are 0.* $a = 2\sqrt{2}, b=c=0$: $M = f(2\sqrt{2}) = 64(\s
qrt{2}-1) \approx 26.51$.

**The maximum among all candidates is $M = 32$ at $(a,b,c) = (2,2,0)$ (and permu
tations).**

**Verification that $(2,2,0)$ is a local maximum.**

- *Perturbation along the sphere keeping $c=0$:* Set $a=2+\epsilon, b=2-\epsilon
$ (satisfies $a^2+b^2 = 8+2\epsilon^2 \approx 8$ to leading order; more precisel
y $b = 2-\epsilon+O(\epsilon^2)$). Using $f(2)=16$, $f'(2)=16$, $f''(2)=0$, $f''
'(2)=-24$, $f^{(4)}(2)=-24$:
$$f(2+\epsilon)+f(2-\epsilon) = 32 - 2\epsilon^4 + O(\epsilon^5) < 32.$$

- *Perturbation activating $c$:* Set $c=\gamma$, $b = 2 - \gamma^2/4 + O(\gamma^
4)$ (to maintain $a^2+b^2+c^2=8$ with $a=2$). Then:
$$M = f(2) + f(2-\gamma^2/4) + f(\gamma) \approx 32 - 4\gamma^2 + 4\gamma^3 = 32
 - 4\gamma^2(1-\gamma) < 32$$
for small $\gamma > 0$.

Both independent directions decrease $M$, confirming a local maximum.

**Conclusion.** The maximum value of the Net Kinetic Output is:

$$\boxed{M = 32}$$

achieved at $(a, b, c) = (2, 2, 0)$ and all permutations, where $a^2 + b^2 + c^2
 = 8 \le 8$.
