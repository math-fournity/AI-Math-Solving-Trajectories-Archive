## Solution

We evaluate the surface integral
$$I = \iint_S x\,dy\wedge dz + (x+y)\,dz\wedge dx + (x^2+2z)\,dx\wedge dy$$
over the closed surface $S$ of the solid bounded by $S_1: 2x^2+y^2=4z$ (elliptic
 paraboloid) and $S_2: x^2+2z=2$ (parabolic cylinder), using **direct integratio
n**.

### Step 1: Geometry and intersection curve

Setting $z = (2-x^2)/2$ from $S_2$ into $S_1$:
$$2x^2 + y^2 = 4\cdot\frac{2-x^2}{2} = 4 - 2x^2 \implies 4x^2 + y^2 = 4.$$
The projection of the solid onto the $xy$-plane is the ellipse $D: 4x^2+y^2\le 4
$, i.e. $\frac{x^2}{1}+\frac{y^2}{4}\le 1$ (semi-axes $a=1, b=2$).

The solid occupies $\frac{2x^2+y^2}{4}\le z\le 1-\frac{x^2}{2}$ over $D$. At the
 origin $S_1$ gives $z=0$, $S_2$ gives $z=1$, so $S_2$ is the **upper** surface
and $S_1$ the **lower** surface. Outward normals: $S_2$ points in $+z$, $S_1$ po
ints in $-z$.

### Step 2: Surface element formulas

For $z=f(x,y)$ with normal in $+z$:
$$dy\wedge dz = -f_x\,dx\wedge dy,\quad dz\wedge dx = -f_y\,dx\wedge dy,\quad dx
\wedge dy = dx\wedge dy.$$
The integrand becomes $[-P f_x - Q f_y + R]\,dx\,dy$. For outward normal in $-z$
, we negate.

### Step 3: Integral over $S_2$ (upper, $z = 1-x^2/2$, outward $+z$)

$f_x = -x,\; f_y = 0$. On $S_2$: $R = x^2+2z = x^2+2(1-x^2/2)=2$.

$$I_{S_2}=\iint_D\bigl[x\cdot(-(-x))+(x+y)\cdot 0+2\bigr]dx\,dy=\iint_D(x^2+2)\,
dx\,dy.$$

### Step 4: Integral over $S_1$ (lower, $z=(2x^2+y^2)/4$, outward $-z$)

$f_x = x,\; f_y = y/2$. On $S_1$: $R = x^2+2z = x^2+\frac{2x^2+y^2}{2}=2x^2+\fra
c{y^2}{2}$.

With $+z$ normal the integrand would be:
$$x(-x)+(x+y)\!\left(-\tfrac{y}{2}\right)+2x^2+\tfrac{y^2}{2}=-x^2-\tfrac{xy}{2}
-\tfrac{y^2}{2}+2x^2+\tfrac{y^2}{2}=x^2-\tfrac{xy}{2}.$$
Since outward normal is $-z$, we negate:
$$I_{S_1}=-\iint_D\!\left(x^2-\tfrac{xy}{2}\right)dx\,dy.$$

### Step 5: Combine

$$I = I_{S_2}+I_{S_1}=\iint_D\!\left(x^2+2\right)dx\,dy-\iint_D\!\left(x^2-\tfra
c{xy}{2}\right)dx\,dy=\iint_D\!\left(2+\tfrac{xy}{2}\right)dx\,dy.$$

The ellipse $D$ is symmetric about both axes; $xy/2$ is odd in $x$ (and $y$), so
 its integral vanishes:

$$I = \iint_D 2\,dx\,dy = 2\cdot\operatorname{Area}(D)=2\cdot\pi(1)(2)=2\cdot 2\
pi = \boxed{4\pi}.$$

### Verification (divergence theorem)

$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F}=\partial_x x+\partial_y(x+y)+\partial_z(x^2+2z)=1+1+2=4$.
 The volume:
$$V=\iint_D\!\left[\Bigl(1-\tfrac{x^2}{2}\Bigr)-\tfrac{2x^2+y^2}{4}\right]dx\,dy
=\iint_D\!\left(1-x^2-\tfrac{y^2}{4}\right)dx\,dy.$$
Substituting $u=x,\,v=y/2$ ($dx\,dy=2\,du\,dv$, unit disk):
$$V=2\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^1(1-r^2)r\,dr\,d\theta=2\cdot 2\pi\cdot\tfrac14=\pi
.$$
Thus $I=4V=4\pi$, confirming the direct computation.
