## Solution

**Step 1: Rewrite the equation in standard form.**

Expanding $(\sin x\, y)' = \cos x\, y + \sin x\, y'$, the equation becomes

$$y'' = \bigl(i\sin x + |y|^2\bigr)\,y' + i(\cos x + \omega)\,y.$$

**Step 2: Verify the trivial solution.**

Setting $y \equiv 0$: we get $y'=0$, $y''=0$, $|y|^2=0$, and every term on both
sides vanishes. The boundary conditions $y(\pi)=0$, $y'(\pi)=0$ are satisfied. S
o $y\equiv 0$ is a solution.

**Step 3: Prove uniqueness via the Picard–Lindelöf theorem.**

Write $y = u + iv$ with $u,v$ real, so $|y|^2 = u^2+v^2$. The equation splits in
to a real system:

$$u'' = -\sin x\, v' + (u^2+v^2)\,u' - (\cos x+\omega)\,v,$$
$$v'' = \sin x\, u' + (u^2+v^2)\,v' + (\cos x+\omega)\,u.$$

Setting $\mathbf{w}=(u,v)$ and $\mathbf{p}=(u',v')$, this is a first-order syste
m in $(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})$:

$$\mathbf{w}' = \mathbf{p}, \qquad \mathbf{p}' = G(x,\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p}),$$

where $G$ is built from polynomial terms in $(u,v,u',v')$ with smooth (in fact a
nalytic) coefficients in $x$. Hence $G$ is **smooth** and therefore **locally Li
pschitz** in $(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{p})$ on all of $\mathbb{R}^4$.

The initial data at $x_0=\pi$ is

$$u(\pi)=v(\pi)=0, \qquad u'(\pi)=v'(\pi)=0.$$

By the Picard–Lindelöf theorem, there exists a **unique** solution to this initi
al-value problem in a neighborhood of $x=\pi$. Since $y\equiv 0$ (i.e., $u\equiv
 v\equiv 0$) satisfies both the equation and the initial data, it is the unique
solution near $\pi$.

**Step 4: Global uniqueness.**

The right-hand side $G$ is smooth (hence locally Lipschitz) for all $(x,u,v,u',v
')\in\mathbb{R}^5$. Therefore the unique local solution extends uniquely to a ma
ximal interval of existence. Since the zero solution is global and satisfies the
 equation everywhere, the maximal solution coinciding with it near $\pi$ is $y\e
quiv 0$ on all of $\mathbb{R}$.

**Conclusion.** The unique solution satisfying $y(\pi)=0$ and $y'(\pi)=0$ is

$$\boxed{y(x) = 0.}$$
