## Solution

We want $y^{(100)}(1)$ for $y = \ln(2x - x^2)$.

**Step 1: Decompose the logarithm.**

$$y = \ln(2x - x^2) = \ln\bigl(x(2-x)\bigr) = \ln x + \ln(2-x), \qquad 0 < x < 2
.$$

At $x = 1$ this is well-defined, so we may differentiate each term separately.

**Step 2: $n$-th derivative of $\ln x$.**

$$\frac{d^n}{dx^n}\ln x = \frac{(-1)^{n-1}(n-1)!}{x^n}.$$

**Step 3: $n$-th derivative of $\ln(2-x)$.**

Let $f(x) = \ln(2-x)$. Computing the first few:

- $f'(x) = -(2-x)^{-1}$
- $f''(x) = -(2-x)^{-2}$
- $f'''(x) = -2(2-x)^{-3}$

The pattern (verified by induction using $\frac{d}{dx}(2-x)^{-k} = k(2-x)^{-k-1}
$) is

$$f^{(n)}(x) = -(n-1)!\,(2-x)^{-n}, \qquad n \ge 1.$$

**Step 4: Combine and evaluate at $x=1$.**

$$y^{(n)}(x) = \frac{(-1)^{n-1}(n-1)!}{x^n} - (n-1)!\,(2-x)^{-n}.$$

At $x = 1$:

$$y^{(n)}(1) = (-1)^{n-1}(n-1)! - (n-1)! = (n-1)!\bigl[(-1)^{n-1} - 1\bigr].$$

**Step 5: Plug in $n = 100$.**

$$(-1)^{99} - 1 = -1 - 1 = -2,$$

so

$$y^{(100)}(1) = 99! \cdot (-2) = -2 \times 99!.$$

**Answer: (b) $\;-2 \times 99!$**
