## Proof

Let $C \subset \mathbb{P}^2_{\mathbb{C}}$ be a projective curve and $p \in C$ a
point of multiplicity $m$. Choose affine coordinates $(x,y)$ centered at $p$ (so
 $p$ is the origin). In the local ring $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^2,p} \cong \math
bb{C}[x,y]_{(x,y)}$, the curve $C$ is defined by a single equation $f = 0$, wher
e $f \in \mathbb{C}[x,y]_{(x,y)}$ has order exactly $m$ at the origin (this is t
he definition of multiplicity $m$). Hence

$$\mathcal{O}_p(C) \cong \mathbb{C}[[x,y]]/(f), \qquad \mathfrak{m} = (x,y)\math
cal{O}_p(C),$$

where $f = f_m + f_{m+1} + \cdots$ with $f_m \neq 0$ a homogeneous polynomial of
 degree $m$ (the **tangent cone** equation) and each $f_i$ homogeneous of degree
 $i$.

**Associated graded ring.** The associated graded ring of $\mathcal{O}_p(C)$ wit
h respect to $\mathfrak{m}$ is

$$\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}} \mathcal{O}_p(C) = \bigoplus_{k \ge 0} \mathf
rak{m}^k/\mathfrak{m}^{k+1} \cong \mathbb{C}[x,y]/(\operatorname{in}(f)) = \math
bb{C}[x,y]/(f_m),$$

where $\operatorname{in}(f) = f_m$ is the initial (leading) form of $f$. This is
 a standard result: the associated graded ring of $\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]/(f)$ is $\m
athbb{C}[x,y]/(\operatorname{in} f)$. Under this isomorphism, the graded piece $
\mathfrak{m}^k/\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}$ corresponds to the degree-$k$ component of $\
mathbb{C}[x,y]/(f_m)$.

**Degree-$k$ component for $k < m$.** The ideal $(f_m) \subset \mathbb{C}[x,y]$
is generated by a homogeneous element of degree $m$. Therefore its degree-$k$ pa
rt $(f_m)_k$ consists of elements $g \cdot f_m$ with $\deg g = k - m$. For $k <
m$, we have $k - m < 0$, so there are no such $g$; hence

$$(f_m)_k = \{0\} \qquad \text{for } k < m.$$

Thus the degree-$k$ component of the quotient is unchanged by the relation:

$$\big(\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(f_m)\big)_k = \mathbb{C}[x,y]_k / (f_m)_k = \mathbb{C}[x
,y]_k \qquad (k < m).$$

The space of homogeneous polynomials of degree $k$ in two variables has dimensio
n

$$\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \mathbb{C}[x,y]_k = k + 1$$

(a basis is $\{x^k, x^{k-1}y, \dots, xy^{k-1}, y^k\}$, i.e. $k+1$ monomials).

**Conclusion.** For every $k < m$,

$$\boxed{\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \mathfrak{m}^k/\mathfrak{m}^{k+1} = k + 1.}$$
