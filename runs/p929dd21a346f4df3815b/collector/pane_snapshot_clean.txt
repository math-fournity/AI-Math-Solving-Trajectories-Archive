## Proof

We wish to find the norm of the linear functional $f : \ell_1 \to \mathbb{R}$ (o
r $\mathbb{C}$) defined by
$$
f(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x_n - x_{n+1}}{3^n}, \qquad x = (x_n) \in \ell_
1.
$$

**Step 1. Rewrite $f$ in the standard dual form.**

Splitting the sum,
$$
f(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x_n}{3^n} - \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x_{n+1}}{
3^n}.
$$
Reindexing the second sum with $m = n+1$,
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x_{n+1}}{3^n} = \sum_{m=2}^{\infty} \frac{x_m}{3^{m-1}
}.
$$
Therefore, separating the $n=1$ term of the first sum,
$$
f(x) = \frac{x_1}{3} + \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} x_n\!\left(\frac{1}{3^n} - \frac{1}{3
^{n-1}}\right)
= \frac{x_1}{3} + \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} x_n \cdot \frac{1-3}{3^n}
= \frac{x_1}{3} - 2\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{x_n}{3^n}.
$$
Thus $f(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n x_n$ where
$$
a_1 = \frac{1}{3}, \qquad a_n = -\frac{2}{3^n} \quad (n \ge 2).
$$

**Step 2. Identify the representing sequence and apply the dual of $\ell_1$.**

It is standard that $(\ell_1)^* \cong \ell_\infty$: every bounded linear functio
nal on $\ell_1$ has the form $f(x) = \sum_{n} a_n x_n$ for a unique $(a_n) \in \
ell_\infty$, and
$$
\|f\| = \|(a_n)\|_\infty = \sup_{n \ge 1} |a_n|.
$$

**Step 3. Compute $\sup_n |a_n|$.**

We have
$$
|a_1| = \frac{1}{3}, \qquad |a_n| = \frac{2}{3^n} \quad (n \ge 2).
$$
The sequence $(2/3^n)_{n \ge 2}$ is strictly decreasing, with maximum at $n=2$:
$$
|a_2| = \frac{2}{9} < \frac{1}{3} = |a_1|.
$$
Hence
$$
\sup_{n \ge 1} |a_n| = |a_1| = \frac{1}{3}.
$$

**Step 4. The bound is attained.**

Take $x = e_1 = (1, 0, 0, \dots) \in \ell_1$, with $\|e_1\|_1 = 1$. Then
$$
f(e_1) = a_1 \cdot 1 = \frac{1}{3}.
$$
So $|f(e_1)| = \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{3}\|e_1\|_1$, confirming $\|f\| \ge \frac{
1}{3}$.

Combining with the upper bound $\|f\| \le \sup_n |a_n| = \frac{1}{3}$ from Step
2, we conclude
$$
\|f\| = \frac{1}{3}.
$$
