## Proof

**Yes**, the non-flat locus can be non-empty and have codimension at least 2 in
$X$.

### Construction

Let $Y = \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{C}[x,y,z,w]/(xw - yz)$, the affine cone ove
r the smooth quadric surface $Q \cong \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ in $\mat
hbb{P}^3$. Define the **small resolution** $X$ as the closed subvariety of $\mat
hbb{A}^4 \times \mathbb{P}^1$:

$$X = \{((x,y,z,w),[s:t]) \in \mathbb{A}^4 \times \mathbb{P}^1 : xt = ys,\; zt =
 ws\},$$

with $f: X \to Y$ the natural projection induced by $((x,y,z,w),[s:t]) \mapsto (
x,y,z,w)$.

### Verification of hypotheses

**(1) $Y$ is integral, separated, finite type over $\mathbb{C}$, and normal.** $
Y$ is a closed subvariety of $\mathbb{A}^4$, hence separated and finite type ove
r $\mathbb{C}$. The polynomial $xw - yz$ is irreducible, so $Y$ is integral. For
 normality: $Y$ is a hypersurface, hence Cohen–Macaulay (satisfies $S_2$). The J
acobian matrix is $(w, -z, -y, x)$, which vanishes only at the origin, so the si
ngular locus is $\{0\}$, which has codimension $3$ in the $3$-fold $Y$. By Serre
's criterion ($R_1 + S_2$), $Y$ is normal.

**(2) $X$ is integral, separated, finite type over $\mathbb{C}$.** $X$ is a clos
ed subvariety of $\mathbb{A}^4 \times \mathbb{P}^1$, hence separated and finite
type over $\mathbb{C}$. On the chart $\{s \neq 0\}$ (set $s=1$): the equations g
ive $y = xt$, $w = zt$, so this chart is $\mathbb{A}^3$ with coordinates $(x, z,
 t)$. On the chart $\{t \neq 0\}$ (set $t=1$): $y = xs$, $w = zs$, so this chart
 is $\mathbb{A}^3$ with coordinates $(x, z, s)$. Both charts are smooth, so $X$
is smooth, hence integral.

**(3) $f$ is surjective.** The projection $\mathbb{A}^4 \times \mathbb{P}^1 \to
\mathbb{A}^4$ is proper (since $\mathbb{P}^1$ is projective), so $f: X \to Y$ is
 proper. We show $f$ is birational: for any point $(x_0, y_0, z_0, w_0) \in Y \s
etminus \{0\}$, the point $[s:t] \in \mathbb{P}^1$ is uniquely determined:
- If $(x_0, y_0) \neq (0,0)$: then $[s:t] = [x_0 : y_0]$ (from $x_0 t = y_0 s$),
 and $z_0 t = w_0 s$ follows from $x_0 w_0 = y_0 z_0$.
- If $(x_0, y_0) = (0,0)$: then $(z_0, w_0) \neq (0,0)$, and $[s:t] = [z_0 : w_0
]$.

The inverse $Y \setminus \{0\} \to X$ is given by $(x,y,z,w) \mapsto ((x,y,z,w),
 [x:y])$ (resp. $[z:w]$), which is a regular morphism. So $f$ is an isomorphism
over $Y \setminus \{0\}$, hence birational. Being proper and birational with $Y$
 integral, $f$ is surjective.

### The non-flat locus

Since $f$ is an isomorphism over $Y \setminus \{0\}$, $f$ is flat over $Y \setmi
nus \{0\}$. The fiber over the origin is:

$$f^{-1}(0) = \{((0,0,0,0), [s:t]) : [s:t] \in \mathbb{P}^1\} \cong \mathbb{P}^1
,$$

which is $1$-dimensional. The generic fiber is a single point ($0$-dimensional),
 so $f$ is birational with generic fiber dimension $0$.

For a flat morphism $f: X \to Y$ at a point $x$, the fiber dimension formula giv
es:
$$\dim_x(X_{f(x)}) = \dim_x(X) - \dim_{f(x)}(Y).$$

Here $\dim(X) = \dim(Y) = 3$ (both are irreducible of dimension $3$). At any clo
sed point $x \in f^{-1}(0) \cong \mathbb{P}^1$, we have $\dim_x(X) = 3$ and $\di
m_0(Y) = 3$, so flatness would require $\dim_x(X_0) = 0$. But $X_0 \cong \mathbb
{P}^1$, so $\dim_x(X_0) = 1$ at every closed point $x$. This is a contradiction,
 so $f$ is **not flat** at any point of $f^{-1}(0)$.

Therefore, the **non-flat locus** of $f$ is exactly $f^{-1}(0) \cong \mathbb{P}^
1$, which is:
- **non-empty** (it is $\mathbb{P}^1$), and
- has **codimension** $3 - 1 = 2$ in $X$.

### Conclusion

The non-flat locus of $f$ is non-empty and has codimension exactly $2$ in $X$. T
his provides an explicit example answering the question in the affirmative.
