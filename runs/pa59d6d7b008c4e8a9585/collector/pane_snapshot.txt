## Proof

We consider the **Koszul complex** associated to $v$: for each degree $k$, the m
ap
$$f_k : \Lambda^k V \to \Lambda^{k+1} V, \qquad f_k(\omega) = v \wedge \omega,$$
gives a chain
$$\cdots \longrightarrow \Lambda^{k-1} V \xrightarrow{\;f_{k-1}\;} \Lambda^k V \
xrightarrow{\;f_k\;} \Lambda^{k+1} V \longrightarrow \cdots$$

This is indeed a complex since $f_{k+1} \circ f_k(\omega) = v \wedge (v \wedge \
omega) = (v \wedge v) \wedge \omega = 0$.

**Claim.** The complex is exact if and only if $v \neq 0$.

---

### Necessity ($v = 0 \Rightarrow$ not exact)

If $v = 0$, then every $f_k = 0$. Hence $\operatorname{im}(f_{k-1}) = 0$ while $
\ker(f_k) = \Lambda^k V$. These are equal only when $\Lambda^k V = 0$, which fai
ls in general (e.g. $k = 0$ gives $\Lambda^0 V = \mathbb{F} \neq 0$). So the com
plex is not exact.

---

### Sufficiency ($v \neq 0 \Rightarrow$ exact)

Assume $v \neq 0$. Extend $v$ to a basis $\{v, e_2, \dots, e_n\}$ of $V$ and set
 $W = \operatorname{span}(e_2, \dots, e_n)$, so $V = \mathbb{F}v \oplus W$. By t
he direct-sum decomposition of the exterior algebra,

$$\Lambda^k V \;=\; \Lambda^k W \;\oplus\; \bigl(v \wedge \Lambda^{k-1} W\bigr).
$$

Concretely, every $\omega \in \Lambda^k V$ decomposes **uniquely** as
$$\omega = \alpha + v \wedge \beta, \qquad \alpha \in \Lambda^k W,\ \ \beta \in
\Lambda^{k-1} W.$$

**Image.** Applying $f_k$:
$$f_k(\omega) = v \wedge \alpha + v \wedge v \wedge \beta = v \wedge \alpha,$$
since $v \wedge v = 0$. Therefore
$$\operatorname{im}(f_{k-1}) = v \wedge \Lambda^{k-1} W.$$

**Kernel.** Now $\omega = \alpha + v \wedge \beta \in \ker(f_k)$ iff $v \wedge \
alpha = 0$. Because $v$ is linearly independent of $W$, the wedge $v \wedge \alp
ha$ (a sum of basis elements each containing the factor $v$) vanishes only when
$\alpha = 0$. (Equivalently, $\Lambda^\bullet V = \Lambda^\bullet W \oplus v \we
dge \Lambda^{\bullet-1} W$ is a direct sum, so $v \wedge \alpha \in v \wedge \La
mbda^k W$ is zero iff $\alpha = 0$.) Thus
$$\ker(f_k) = \{\,v \wedge \beta : \beta \in \Lambda^{k-1} W\,\} = v \wedge \Lam
bda^{k-1} W.$$

**Conclusion.** We have shown
$$\ker(f_k) = v \wedge \Lambda^{k-1} W = \operatorname{im}(f_{k-1})$$
for every $k$. Hence the Koszul complex is exact.
