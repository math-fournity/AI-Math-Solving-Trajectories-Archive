# Proof

Let $P = A_1A_2\cdots A_n$ be a convex polygon with $n\ge 5$, no three diagonals
 concurrent in the interior.

## Step 1: Reduction to distinct cells

Draw all diagonals of $P$. They partition the interior of $P$ into open convex r
egions called **cells**. Two interior points (off all diagonals) lie in the same
 cell if and only if the open segment between them crosses no diagonal — because
 cells are the connected components of $\operatorname{int}(P)\setminus\{\text{al
l diagonals}\}$, and by convexity of $P$ the segment stays inside $P$.

Hence condition (3) — every segment joining two chosen points meets some diagona
l — is **equivalent** to requiring all chosen points to lie in **pairwise distin
ct cells**.

## Step 2: One intersection point per quadrilateral

For each quadrilateral $Q_{ijkl}=A_iA_jA_kA_l$ ($i<j<k<l$), its diagonals $A_iA_
k$ and $A_jA_l$ cross at a unique interior point $X_{ijkl}$. No-concurrency give
s exactly two diagonals through each $X_{ijkl}$, so exactly **4 cells** are adja
cent to $X_{ijkl}$. Different quadrilaterals yield different intersection points
 (a crossing pair of diagonals determines the quadrilateral uniquely), giving $\
binom n4$ distinct points.

## Step 3: The assignment

At $X_{ijkl}$ the two diagonals create 4 quadrants, labeled by the pair of verti
ces they point toward: $(A_i,A_j)$, $(A_j,A_k)$, $(A_k,A_l)$, $(A_l,A_i)$. Each
quadrant contains exactly one cell adjacent to $X_{ijkl}$.

**Choice.** Assign to $Q_{ijkl}$ the cell in the **"towards $A_i,A_j$"** quadran
t at $X_{ijkl}$.

## Step 4: The cell lies inside $Q_{ijkl}$

The "towards $A_i,A_j$" quadrant is contained in triangle $X_{ijkl}A_iA_j$. Sinc
e $Q_{ijkl}$ is convex and $X_{ijkl}\in\operatorname{int}(Q_{ijkl})$, this trian
gle lies inside $Q_{ijkl}$. The adjacent cell in this quadrant is contained in t
he triangle, hence inside $Q_{ijkl}$. ✓

## Step 5: Injectivity — all assigned cells are distinct

Two distinct intersection points $X_1,X_2$ can share an adjacent cell only if th
ey are **consecutive** on some diagonal $d$ (no other intersection point between
 them on $d$); then they share exactly the two cells flanking the segment $X_1X_
2\subset d$.

**Key observation.** Let $d=A_pA_q$ with $p<q$. For any intersection point $X_{i
jkl}$ lying on $d$:

- If $d=A_iA_k$: then $\{p,q\}=\{i,k\}$ and $p=i$ (since $i<k$), so the chosen q
uadrant extends toward $A_i=A_p$.
- If $d=A_jA_l$: then $\{p,q\}=\{j,l\}$ and $p=j$ (since $j<l$), so the chosen q
uadrant extends toward $A_j=A_p$.

**In every case the chosen quadrant extends toward $A_p$, the smaller-indexed en
dpoint of $d$.**

Now let $X_1,X_2$ be consecutive on $d=A_pA_q$. Both chosen quadrants extend tow
ard $A_p$. One point, say $X_1$, is closer to $A_p$; the other, $X_2$, is farthe
r. Then:

- $X_1$'s quadrant extends toward $A_p$ — **away** from $X_2$.
- $X_2$'s quadrant extends toward $A_p$ — **toward** $X_1$.

For both to select the same shared cell, **both** must extend toward each other
along $d$. But $X_1$ extends away from $X_2$, so $X_1$ selects a cell on the $A_
p$-side, **not** a shared cell between $X_1$ and $X_2$. No conflict. (The symmet
ric case is identical.)

Since the only possible conflicts are between consecutive pairs on a common diag
onal, and no such conflict arises, the assignment is **injective**. ✓

## Step 6: Completion

For each $Q_{ijkl}$ pick any point $p_{ijkl}$ in the interior of its assigned ce
ll. Then:

- **Inside the quadrilateral**: by Step 4. ✓
- **Not on any diagonal**: the point is in a cell interior. ✓
- **All $\binom n4$ points distinct**: they occupy distinct cells (Step 5). ✓
- **Every joining segment meets a diagonal**: distinct cells ⟹ segment crosses a
 diagonal (Step 1). ✓

This gives the required $\binom n4$ points. $\blacksquare$
