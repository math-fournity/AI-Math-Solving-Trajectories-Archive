## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. Every non-empty open subset of a perfect Polish space is uncount
able.

**Setup.** Let $X$ be a perfect Polish space (Polish = separable, completely met
rizable; perfect = no isolated points), and let $U \subseteq X$ be a non-empty o
pen set. We show $U$ is uncountable.

**Step 1 — $U$ is a perfect Polish space.**

- *Polish:* An open subset of a Polish space is Polish. Separability is inherite
d by subspaces. Complete metrizability of an open subset $U$ of a completely met
rizable space $X$ is standard: if $d$ is a compatible complete metric on $X$, th
en
$$d'(x,y) = d(x,y) + \left|\tfrac{1}{d(x,X\setminus U)} - \tfrac{1}{d(y,X\setmin
us U)}\right|$$
(with $d(\cdot, X\setminus U)$ understood appropriately when $X\setminus U = \em
ptyset$, in which case $U = X$ is already complete) is a compatible metric on $U
$ under which $U$ is complete. Hence $U$ is Polish.

- *Perfect:* Suppose $U$ had an isolated point $x$. Then $\{x\}$ is open in $U$.
 Since $U$ is open in $X$, $\{x\}$ is open in $X$, making $x$ isolated in $X$ —
contradicting that $X$ is perfect. So $U$ is perfect.

**Step 2 — A non-empty perfect Polish space is uncountable.**

Since $U$ is Polish, it is completely metrizable, and therefore a *Baire space*
by the Baire Category Theorem: $U$ is not the countable union of nowhere-dense c
losed sets.

Suppose for contradiction that $U$ is countable, say $U = \{x_1, x_2, \dots\}$.
Each singleton $\{x_n\}$ is closed (metric spaces are $T_1$). Because $U$ is per
fect, no point is isolated, so each $\{x_n\}$ has empty interior; equivalently,
each $\{x_n\}$ is nowhere dense. Thus
$$U = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} \{x_n\}$$
expresses the non-empty Baire space $U$ as a countable union of nowhere-dense cl
osed sets — a contradiction.

**Conclusion.** Therefore $U$ is uncountable. Since $U$ was an arbitrary non-emp
ty open subset of the perfect Polish space $X$, every non-empty open subset of a
 perfect Polish space is uncountable. $\blacksquare$
