## Proof

Define $f(n) = \begin{cases} n/3 & 3\mid n \\ 4n-10 & 3\nmid n\end{cases}$. We s
eek all positive integers $c$ with $f^5(c)=2$.

**Backward step.** Given a value $v = n_{k+1}$, the possible predecessors $n_k$
are:
- **(A) Ternary reverse:** $n_k = 3v$. Always valid, since $3v$ is divisible by
$3$.
- **(B) Quad-Decima reverse:** $n_k = (v+10)/4$. Valid iff $v\equiv 2\pmod 4$ (s
o the result is an integer) **and** $(v+10)/4\not\equiv 0\pmod 3$ (so it was gen
uinely a non-multiple of $3$).

**Building the tree from $n_5=2$:**

**Step 1** ($n_5=2\to n_4$):
- (A): $n_4=6$. ✓
- (B): $n_4=(2+10)/4=3$, but $3\mid 3$ → invalid.

$n_4\in\{6\}$.

**Step 2** ($n_4=6\to n_3$):
- (A): $n_3=18$. ✓
- (B): $n_3=(6+10)/4=4$; $6\equiv2\pmod4$ ✓, $3\nmid4$ ✓.

$n_3\in\{18,4\}$.

**Step 3** ($n_3\to n_2$):
- From $18$: (A) $54$ ✓; (B) $(18+10)/4=7$; $18\equiv2\pmod4$ ✓, $3\nmid7$ ✓.
- From $4$: (A) $12$ ✓; (B) $(4+10)/4=3.5$ — not integer ✗.

$n_2\in\{54,7,12\}$.

**Step 4** ($n_2\to n_1$):
- From $54$: (A) $162$ ✓; (B) $(54+10)/4=16$; $54\equiv2\pmod4$ ✓, $3\nmid16$ ✓.
- From $7$: (A) $21$ ✓; (B) $(7+10)/4=4.25$ ✗.
- From $12$: (A) $36$ ✓; (B) $(12+10)/4=5.5$ ✗.

$n_1\in\{162,16,21,36\}$.

**Step 5** ($n_1\to n_0=c$):
- From $162$: (A) $486$ ✓; (B) $(162+10)/4=43$; $162\equiv2\pmod4$ ✓, $3\nmid43$
 ✓.
- From $16$: (A) $48$ ✓; (B) $(16+10)/4=6.5$ ✗.
- From $21$: (A) $63$ ✓; (B) $(21+10)/4=7.75$ ✗.
- From $36$: (A) $108$ ✓; (B) $(36+10)/4=11.5$ ✗.

$c\in\{486,43,48,63,108\}$.

**Forward verification:**
- $486\to162\to54\to18\to6\to2$ ✓
- $43\to162\to54\to18\to6\to2$ ✓
- $48\to16\to54\to18\to6\to2$ ✓
- $63\to21\to7\to18\to6\to2$ ✓
- $108\to36\to12\to4\to6\to2$ ✓

All five are positive integers yielding final value $2$.

**Sum:** $486+43+48+63+108 = 748$.
