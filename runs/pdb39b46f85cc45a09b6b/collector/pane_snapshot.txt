**问题.** 用多少种方法可以将 100 块石头分成若干堆，使得任意两堆的石头数量之差不
超过 1？

**答案：100.**

---

## Proof

**Setup.** Since the stones are identical, a "way" of dividing is an integer par
tition of 100 into positive parts (pile sizes) such that the largest part and th
e smallest part differ by at most 1. Hence every pile has size either $s$ or $s+
1$ for some integer $s \ge 1$.

**Key observation.** Fix the number of piles $n$ (with $n \ge 1$). I claim there
 is *exactly one* valid configuration with $n$ piles.

Indeed, let $a$ be the number of piles of size $s+1$ and $b$ the number of piles
 of size $s$, so $a + b = n$ and
$$
a(s+1) + bs = sn + a = 100.
$$
Dividing, $s = \lfloor 100/n \rfloor$ and $a = 100 \bmod n$. These are uniquely
determined by $n$. We must verify this configuration is valid:

- $s \ge 1$: this holds exactly when $n \le 100$ (since $\lfloor 100/n\rfloor \g
e 1 \iff n \le 100$).
- $0 \le a \le n$: by definition $a = 100 \bmod n \in \{0, 1, \dots, n-1\} \subs
eteq [0, n]$. ✓
- All pile sizes positive: piles of size $s+1 \ge 2$ are positive; piles of size
 $s \ge 1$ are positive. ✓
- The difference condition holds automatically since all sizes are in $\{s, s+1\
}$. ✓

So for every $n$ with $1 \le n \le 100$ there is exactly one valid division, and
 it is given concretely by "$a = 100 \bmod n$ piles of size $\lfloor 100/n\rfloo
r + 1$ and the remaining $n - a$ piles of size $\lfloor 100/n\rfloor$."

**Distinctness.** Two configurations with different numbers of piles $n \ne n'$
are obviously different (they have different total numbers of piles). Hence the
configurations for $n = 1, 2, \dots, 100$ are all distinct.

**No other configurations.** Conversely, any valid division has some number of p
iles $n \ge 1$ with all sizes in $\{s, s+1\}$, which forces $s = \lfloor 100/n \
rfloor$ and $a = 100 \bmod n$ as above, so it is one of the configurations we al
ready counted. Also $n$ cannot exceed $100$ (since each pile has at least one st
one, $n \le 100$), and $n \ge 101$ would force $s = 0$, giving piles of size $0$
, which is forbidden.

**Count.** The valid values of $n$ are precisely $1, 2, \dots, 100$, each contri
buting exactly one configuration. Therefore the number of ways is
$$
\boxed{100}.
$$

**Sanity checks.**
- $n=1$: $s=100, a=0$ → one pile of $100$. ✓ (vacuously satisfies the condition)
- $n=2$: $s=50, a=0$ → two piles of $50$. ✓
- $n=3$: $s=33, a=1$ → one pile of $34$, two piles of $33$; $34-33=1\le 1$. ✓
- $n=7$: $s=14, a=2$ → two piles of $15$, five piles of $14$; total $30+70=100$.
 ✓
- $n=100$: $s=1, a=0$ → one hundred piles of $1$. ✓

All consistent with exactly one configuration per $n \in \{1,\dots,100\}$.
