I'll solve this directly using the characteristic function method (equivalently
Isserlis' theorem for jointly Gaussian zero-mean variables).

## Setup

$X_1, X_2, X_3$ are jointly normal with $\mathbb{E}[X_i]=0$, $\mathbb{E}[X_i^2]=
\sigma^2$, and $\mathbb{E}[X_iX_j]=k_{ij}$ for $i\neq j$. The joint characterist
ic function is

$$\varphi(t_1,t_2,t_3)=\exp\!\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\sigma^2(t_1^2+t_2^2+t_3^2
)+2k_{12}t_1t_2+2k_{13}t_1t_3+2k_{23}t_2t_3\bigr]\Bigr).$$

Moments are recovered via $\mathbb{E}[X_1^a X_2^b X_3^c]=\frac{1}{i^{a+b+c}}\fra
c{\partial^{a+b+c}\varphi}{\partial t_1^a\partial t_2^b\partial t_3^c}\big|_{0}$
. Expanding the exponential and differentiating is exactly **Isserlis' (Wick's)
theorem**: every joint moment of centered Gaussians equals the sum over all pair
ings of products of pairwise covariances.

---

## (a) $\mathbb{M}[X_1^2 X_2^2 X_3^2]$

We pair the six factors $X_1,X_1,X_2,X_2,X_3,X_3$ (label positions $1,2,3,4,5,6$
). There are $15$ pairings. Tallying by the type of covariance each pair produce
s:

| Pairing type | Count | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| $(X_1X_1)(X_2X_2)(X_3X_3)$ | $1$ | $\sigma^6$ |
| $(X_iX_i)(X_jX_k)(X_jX_k)$, $\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}$ | $2$ each, $3$ choices of $
i$ | $2\sigma^2(k_{12}^2+k_{13}^2+k_{23}^2)$ |
| $(X_iX_j)(X_iX_k)(X_jX_k)$, all distinct | $8$ | $8\,k_{12}k_{13}k_{23}$ |

The "all distinct" count: fixing the partner of the first $X_1$ among $\{X_2,X_2
,X_3,X_3\}$ gives 4 choices; each forces the remaining two pairs, yielding 4 pai
rings. Symmetrically the partner of the first $X_1$ can be the second $X_1$ (han
dled in row 1) or one of the 4 cross-terms. The 8 count = 4 (first $X_1$ pairs w
ith a $X_2$ or $X_3$) × 2 (the two copies). Concretely, the 8 pairings each cont
ribute $k_{12}k_{13}k_{23}$.

Therefore

$$\boxed{\mathbb{M}[X_1^2 X_2^2 X_3^2]=\sigma^6+2\sigma^2\!\left(k_{12}^2+k_{13}
^2+k_{23}^2\right)+8\,k_{12}k_{13}k_{23}.}$$

---

## (b) $\mathbb{M}[(X_1^2-\sigma^2)(X_2^2-\sigma^2)(X_3^2-\sigma^2)]$

Expand:

$$\mathbb{E}[X_1^2X_2^2X_3^2]-\sigma^2\!\sum_{i<j}\mathbb{E}[X_i^2X_j^2]+\sigma^
4\!\sum_i\mathbb{E}[X_i^2]-\sigma^6.$$

The fourth-order moments, again by Isserlis (3 pairings of 4 factors):

$$\mathbb{E}[X_i^2X_j^2]=\sigma^4+2k_{ij}^2.$$

Substitute everything:

$$\begin{aligned}
&\bigl[\sigma^6+2\sigma^2(k_{12}^2+k_{13}^2+k_{23}^2)+8k_{12}k_{13}k_{23}\bigr]
\\
&\quad-\sigma^2\bigl[(\sigma^4+2k_{12}^2)+(\sigma^4+2k_{13}^2)+(\sigma^4+2k_{23}
^2)\bigr] \\
&\quad+\sigma^4(\sigma^2+\sigma^2+\sigma^2)-\sigma^6 \\m]
&=\sigma^6+2\sigma^2(k_{12}^2+k_{13}^2+k_{23}^2)+8k_{12}k_{13}k_{23} \\
&\quad-3\sigma^6-2\sigma^2(k_{12}^2+k_{13}^2+k_{23}^2)+3\sigma^6-\sigma^6 \\m
]
&=(\sigma^6-3\sigma^6+3\sigma^6-\sigma^6)+\bigl(2\sigma^2-2\sigma^2\bigr)(k_{12}
^2+k_{13}^2+k_{23}^2)+8k_{12}k_{13}k_{23} \\m]
&=0+0+8k_{12}k_{13}k_{23}.
\end{aligned}$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{\mathbb{M}[(X_1^2-\sigma^2)(X_2^2-\sigma^2)(X_3^2-\sigma^2)]=8\,k_{12}\
,k_{13}\,k_{23}.}$$

---

**Interpretation.** Part (b) is the third joint cumulant of the squared variable
s $Y_i=X_i^2-\sigma^2$. The cancellation of all $\sigma$-terms and all $k_{ij}^2
$-terms is the hallmark of cumulant extraction: only the fully connected (triang
le) contraction $k_{12}k_{13}k_{23}$ survives, with multiplicity $8$ from the $8
$ pairings that link all three distinct indices simultaneously.
