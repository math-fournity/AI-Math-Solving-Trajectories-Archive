# Solution

**Yes, $T$ is continuous.**

## Proof

The key observation is that a conjugate linear map is **real-linear**, and the c
losed graph theorem applies to real Banach spaces.

**Step 1: View $X$ as a real Banach space.**

Let $X_{\mathbb{R}}$ denote $X$ viewed as a Banach space over $\mathbb{R}$: same
 underlying set, same norm, with scalar multiplication restricted to $\mathbb{R}
$. Completeness is preserved, so $X_{\mathbb{R}}$ is a real Banach space. Likewi
se, $X_{\mathbb{R}} \times X_{\mathbb{R}}$ is a real Banach space with the same
topology as $X \times X$ (the norm is identical).

**Step 2: $T$ is real-linear.**

Since $T$ is conjugate linear, $T(\alpha x + \beta y) = \bar{\alpha}\,T(x) + \ba
r{\beta}\,T(y)$. For $r \in \mathbb{R}$, $\bar{r} = r$, so:
- $T(x + y) = T(x) + T(y)$,
- $T(rx) = r\,T(x)$.

Thus $T: X_{\mathbb{R}} \to X_{\mathbb{R}}$ is a real-linear map.

**Step 3: $G(T)$ is a closed real-linear subspace.**

$G(T)$ is closed in $X \times X$ by hypothesis, hence closed in $X_{\mathbb{R}}
\times X_{\mathbb{R}}$ (same topology).

We verify $G(T)$ is a real-linear subspace. If $(x, Tx), (y, Ty) \in G(T)$ and $
r \in \mathbb{R}$:
$$r(x, Tx) + (y, Ty) = (rx + y,\; rTx + Ty).$$
Since $T(rx + y) = \bar{r}\,T(x) + T(y) = r\,T(x) + T(y)$ (as $r$ is real), we h
ave $(rx+y,\; T(rx+y)) = (rx+y,\; rTx+Ty) \in G(T)$.

Therefore $G(T)$ is a closed real-linear subspace of the real Banach space $X_{\
mathbb{R}} \times X_{\mathbb{R}}$, hence $G(T)$ is itself a real Banach space.

**Step 4: Apply the closed graph theorem (real version).**

Define the projection $\pi_1: G(T) \to X_{\mathbb{R}}$ by $\pi_1(x, Tx) = x$. Th
is map is:
- **Real-linear** (clear from the definition),
- **Continuous** (projections are bounded in the product norm),
- **Bijective** (since $T$ is defined on all of $X$, every $x \in X_{\mathbb{R}}
$ has a unique preimage $(x, Tx) \in G(T)$).

By the **Bounded Inverse Theorem** (which holds for real Banach spaces), $\pi_1^
{-1}: X_{\mathbb{R}} \to G(T)$ is continuous.

**Step 5: Conclude.**

Let $\pi_2: G(T) \to X_{\mathbb{R}}$ be the projection $\pi_2(x, Tx) = Tx$, whic
h is continuous. Then:
$$T = \pi_2 \circ \pi_1^{-1}.$$

As a composition of continuous maps, $T$ is continuous. $\blacksquare$
