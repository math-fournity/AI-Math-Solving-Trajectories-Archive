## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. If $f$ has positive topological entropy on a proper invariant su
bset $J \subset [0,1]$, then $h_{\mathrm{top}}(f; [0,1]) > 0$.

**Key fact (Monotonicity of topological entropy).** Let $X$ be a compact metric
space, $f: X \to X$ continuous, and $J \subset X$ a compact subset with $f(J) \s
ubset J$ (i.e. $J$ is $f$-invariant). Then

$$h_{\mathrm{top}}(f|_J) \;\leq\; h_{\mathrm{top}}(f).$$

*Proof of the fact.* Topological entropy can be defined via $(n,\varepsilon)$-se
parated sets: $h_{\mathrm{top}}(f) = \lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{1}{n}\log s_n(\varepsilon, f, X)$, where $s_n(\varepsilon,f,X)$ is the max
imal cardinality of an $(n,\varepsilon)$-separated set. Since $J\subset X$, ever
y $(n,\varepsilon)$-separated set for $f|_J$ is also $(n,\varepsilon)$-separated
 for $f$ on $X$ (the metric on $J$ is the restriction of the metric on $X$, and
invariance $f(J)\subset J$ guarantees the orbit separation condition is identica
l). Hence

$$s_n(\varepsilon, f|_J, J) \;\leq\; s_n(\varepsilon, f, X)$$

for all $n,\varepsilon$. Taking $\limsup_{n}$, then $\frac{1}{n}\log$, then $\li
m_{\varepsilon\to 0}$ preserves the inequality, giving $h_{\mathrm{top}}(f|_J) \
leq h_{\mathrm{top}}(f)$. $\square$

**Application.** The hypothesis "$f$ exhibits positive topological entropy on $J
$" means precisely that the restricted system $(J, f|_J)$ is a well-defined dyna
mical system, i.e. $J$ is $f$-invariant ($f(J)\subset J$), and $h_{\mathrm{top}}
(f|_J) > 0$. Applying monotonicity:

$$0 \;<\; h_{\mathrm{top}}(f|_J) \;\leq\; h_{\mathrm{top}}(f; [0,1]).$$

Therefore $h_{\mathrm{top}}(f; [0,1]) > 0$.

**Remark on the role of invariance.** The statement "topological entropy of $f$
on $J$" is meaningful only when $J$ is $f$-invariant; otherwise the orbit of a p
oint in $J$ leaves $J$ and the restriction $f|_J$ is not a self-map of $J$. So t
he hypothesis itself encodes the invariance required to apply monotonicity. The
properness of $J\subsetneq[0,1]$ is irrelevant to the conclusion — it only empha
sizes that positivity is detected on a smaller invariant piece and propagates up
ward to the whole space.

**Conclusion.** The topological entropy of $f$ on the full interval $[0,1]$ is p
ositive, since topological entropy is monotone under restriction to compact inva
riant subsets, and a positive quantity lower-bounds the entropy on any larger in
variant set containing it.
