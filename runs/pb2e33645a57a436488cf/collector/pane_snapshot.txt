**Answer: Yes**, provided $v$ is complete (which is implicitly assumed by the ph
rase "the one-parameter group of diffeomorphisms $\phi_t$ generated by $v$").

**Proof.**

Let $v$ be a complete vector field on $M$ with flow $\phi_t : M \to M$ (a one-pa
rameter group of diffeomorphisms, so $\phi_0 = \mathrm{id}$, $\phi_{t+s} = \phi_
t \circ \phi_s$, and $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0} \phi_t(p) = v_p$).

The Lie derivative of a smooth function $f$ along $v$ is
$$\mathcal{L}_v f \;=\; v(f) \;=\; df(v),$$
i.e., the directional derivative of $f$ in the direction of $v$. By hypothesis,
$$(\mathcal{L}_v f)(p) = 0 \quad \text{for all } p \in M. \tag{$\ast$}$$

Fix $p \in M$ and consider the curve $\gamma_p(t) = f(\phi_t(p))$ in $\mathbb{R}
$. We compute its derivative. By the chain rule and the definition of the flow,
$$\frac{d}{dt} f(\phi_t(p)) \;=\; df_{\phi_t(p)}\!\left(\frac{d}{dt}\phi_t(p)\ri
ght) \;=\; df_{\phi_t(p)}\!\big(v_{\phi_t(p)}\big) \;=\; v(f)\big(\phi_t(p)\big)
 \;=\; (\mathcal{L}_v f)\big(\phi_t(p)\big).$$

Applying $(\ast)$ at the point $\phi_t(p) \in M$,
$$\frac{d}{dt} f(\phi_t(p)) \;=\; (\mathcal{L}_v f)\big(\phi_t(p)\big) \;=\; 0 \
quad \text{for all } t \in \mathbb{R}.$$

Therefore $\gamma_p(t) = f(\phi_t(p))$ is constant in $t$. Evaluating at $t = 0$
 gives the constant value:
$$f(\phi_t(p)) = f(\phi_0(p)) = f(p) \quad \text{for all } t \in \mathbb{R}.$$

Since $p \in M$ was arbitrary,
$$f(\phi_t(p)) = f(p) \quad \text{for all } t \in \mathbb{R},\; p \in M.$$

This is precisely the statement that the flow $\phi_t$ preserves $f$. $\blacksqu
are$

**Remark on completeness.** The conclusion "for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$" requires
 that $v$ be complete, i.e., that its integral curves exist for all time so that
 $\phi_t$ is a globally defined one-parameter group. The problem's phrasing ("th
e one-parameter group of diffeomorphisms $\phi_t$ generated by $v$") presupposes
 this. If $v$ were only locally complete, the same argument would yield $f(\phi_
t(p)) = f(p)$ on the (possibly $p$-dependent) interval of existence of the integ
ral curve through $p$. Standard sufficient conditions for completeness include:
$M$ compact, or $v$ having compact support, or $v$ being bounded with respect to
 a complete Riemannian metric on $M$.
