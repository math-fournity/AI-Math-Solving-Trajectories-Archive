## Proof

**Step 1: Characterize the rotated lattice.**

The integer lattice $\mathbb{Z}^2$ rotated by $45°$ is the lattice
$$L = R_{45°}(\mathbb{Z}^2) = \left\{\left(\frac{m-n}{\sqrt{2}},\, \frac{m+n}{\s
qrt{2}}\right) : m, n \in \mathbb{Z}\right\}.$$

A basis is $\mathbf{e}_1 = \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)$
and $\mathbf{e}_2 = \left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)$, both
of length $1$, orthogonal to each other. Thus $L$ is a **square lattice with min
imum distance $1$**, isometric to $\mathbb{Z}^2$.

**Step 2: Compute the covering radius.**

The Voronoi cell of $L$ is a unit square (rotated $45°$) with side length $1$. T
he point farthest from all lattice points is the center of this Voronoi cell, at
 distance equal to half the diagonal:

$$\rho = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.$$

This is the **covering radius**: every point in the plane is within distance $\r
ho$ of some lattice point, and the centers of the Voronoi cells achieve this max
imum distance.

**Step 3: Upper bound — the constraint gives supremum $\leq \pi/2$.**

The problem imposes the constraint $\text{area} < \pi/2$. Therefore no shape wit
h area $\geq \pi/2$ is admissible, and the supremum of achievable areas is at mo
st $\pi/2$.

**Step 4: Lower bound — every area $A < \pi/2$ is achievable.**

For any target area $A < \pi/2$, set $r = \sqrt{A/\pi}$. Then:
$$r = \sqrt{\frac{A}{\pi}} < \sqrt{\frac{\pi/2}{\pi}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}} = \fr
ac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \rho.$$

Place a closed disk $D$ of radius $r$ centered at the center of a Voronoi cell o
f $L$. The nearest lattice points are at distance exactly $\rho$ from the center
. Since $r < \rho$, the disk $D$ does not intersect any lattice point. Its area
is $\pi r^2 = A$.

Since $A < \pi/2$ was arbitrary, every area strictly less than $\pi/2$ is achiev
able.

**Step 5: The supremum $\pi/2$ is not achieved.**

A disk of radius exactly $\rho = \sqrt{2}/2$ centered at a Voronoi cell center w
ould have area exactly $\pi/2$, but it would **touch** the four nearest lattice
points (at distance $\rho$), violating the non-intersection condition. Moreover,
 no other shape of area $\pi/2$ can avoid all lattice points either: any shape o
f area $\pi/2$ placed at the Voronoi center would need to extend to distance $\r
ho$ in some direction to achieve this area, and by the covering property of the
lattice, reaching distance $\rho$ forces contact with a lattice point.

**Conclusion.**

The set of achievable areas is the open interval $(0, \pi/2)$. The supremum is:

$$\boxed{\dfrac{\pi}{2}}$$

This is the largest area in the sense of a sharp supremum: any area strictly les
s than $\pi/2$ can be realized by a disk placed at a Voronoi cell center, while
area $\pi/2$ itself cannot be achieved without intersecting a lattice point.
