 (and therefore also with respect to its completion).

$$I_k^n = \Big[\tfrac{k}{2^n},\,\tfrac{k+1}{2^n}\Big),\qquad k=0,1,\dots,2^n-1,$

together with the singleton $\{1\}$. Define $f_n:[0,1]^2\to\mathbb{R}$ by
$$f_n(x,y)\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{2^n-1} f\!\Big(\tfrac{k}{2^n},\,y\Big)\,\mathbf{1}_{I
_k^n}(x)\;+\;f(1,y)\,\mathbf{1}_{\{1\}}(x).$$

every fixed $x_0\in[0,1]$, the map $y\mapsto f(x_0,y)$ is Lebesgue measurable, i
.e. $\mathcal{A}$-measurable. Hence each $y\mapsto f(k/2^n,y)$ and $y\mapsto f(1
,y)$ is $\mathcal{A}$-measurable. Each indicator $\mathbf{1}_{I_k^n}(x)$ and $\m
athbf{1}_{\{1\}}(x)$ is Borel (hence $\mathcal{A}$-) measurable as a function of

asurable (it is the pointwise limit of simple functions supported on measurable
rectangles). A finite sum of $\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}$-measurable functions

**Pointwise convergence $f_n\to f$.** Fix $(x,y)\in[0,1]^2$.

- If $x=1$: $f_n(1,y)=f(1,y)=f(1,y)$ for all $n$, so $f_n(1,y)\to f(1,y)$.
