Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum side length *b* of a regular
 hexagon that can contain a square of side 8 with all four corners touching four
 flat sides of the hexagon.

## Setup

Place the regular hexagon centered at the origin with side length *b*. Its six s
ides have outward normals at angles 30°, 90°, 150°, 210°, 270°, 330°, each at di
stance (apothem) *a* = *b*√3/2 from the center.

Place the square (side 8) centered at the origin, rotated by angle θ. Its four c
orners lie at distance 4√2 from the center, at angles θ, θ+90°, θ+180°, θ+270°.

## Finding the Optimal Configuration

For all four corners to touch four sides, each corner's projection onto its side
's normal must equal *a*:

$$4\sqrt{2}\cos(\alpha_i - \phi_i) = a$$

By the square's 180° rotational symmetry, the four touched sides must form two o
pposite pairs. The three available opposite pairs are {30°,210°}, {90°,270°}, {1
50°,330°}. We select two of these three pairs.

**Assigning corners to sides** {30°, 90°, 210°, 270°}: Corner at θ touches norma
l 30°, corner at θ+90° touches normal 90°, etc. This requires:

$$\cos(\theta - 30°) = \cos(\theta + 90° - 90°) = \cos(\theta)$$

Solving: |θ − 30°| = |θ| gives **θ = 15°**, with each corner 15° from its neares
t normal.

## Computing b

$$a = 4\sqrt{2}\cos(15°) = 4\sqrt{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{6}+\sqrt{2}}{4} = \sqrt{2}(
\sqrt{6}+\sqrt{2}) = 2\sqrt{3} + 2$$

$$b = \frac{2a}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{2(2\sqrt{3}+2)}{\sqrt{3}} = 4 + \frac{4}{\sqrt
{3}} = 4 + \frac{4\sqrt{3}}{3}$$

## Verifying Corners Lie Within Side Segments

At θ = 15°, corner 0 is at 4√2(cos 15°, sin 15°) ≈ (5.464, 1.464). With b ≈ 6.30

$$t = \frac{5.464 - 6.309}{3.155 - 6.309} = \frac{-0.845}{-3.155} \approx 0.268
\in [0,1] \checkmark$$

Corner 1 at 105° ≈ (−1.464, 5.464) lies on the top side (y = a ≈ 5.464) with x ∈
 [−3.155, 3.155]. ✓ By symmetry, corners 2 and 3 also lie within their segments.

**Checking the two untouched sides** (normals 150°, 330°): The maximum projectio

 pokes out. ✓

## Optimality

The corners are spaced 90° apart; the normals are spaced 60° apart. The minimum
achievable equal corner-to-normal distance is half of gcd(90°,60°) = 30°/2 = **1
5°**, which is exactly what we found. All three pair selections yield the same *
b* by the hexagon's 6-fold symmetry. Centering the square at the hexagon's cente
r is optimal (both shapes are centrally symmetric).

## Final Computation

$$b = 4 + \frac{4\sqrt{3}}{3} \approx 4 + 2.3094 = 6.3094$$

$$\lfloor 10^2 \cdot b \rfloor = \lfloor 630.94\ldots \rfloor = \boxed{630}$$
