The region of integration is the ball $B = \{(x,y,z): x^2+y^2+z^2 \le 4\}$ of ra
dius $2$ centered at the origin. The integral is

$$I = \iiint_B \bigl(z^3\cos(xyz) - 3\bigr)\,dV = \iiint_B z^3\cos(xyz)\,dV \;-\
; 3\iiint_B dV.$$

**Second term.** $\iiint_B dV$ is the volume of the ball of radius $2$:

$$\iiint_B dV = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi(2)^3 = \tfrac{32\pi}{3}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad 3
\iiint_B dV = 32\pi.$$

**First term.** Consider the substitution $(x,y,z)\mapsto(x,y,-z)$, which maps t
he ball $B$ onto itself (it is symmetric under $z\to -z$). Under this map,

$$z^3 \mapsto (-z)^3 = -z^3, \qquad \cos(xyz)\mapsto \cos(xy(-z)) = \cos(-xyz) =
 \cos(xyz).$$

Hence the integrand transforms as

$$z^3\cos(xyz) \;\mapsto\; -z^3\cos(xyz).$$

So $f(x,y,-z) = -f(x,y,z)$; the integrand is **odd in $z$** over a region symmet
ric in $z$. Therefore

$$\iiint_B z^3\cos(xyz)\,dV = 0.$$

**Combining.**

$$I = 0 - 32\pi = -32\pi.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
