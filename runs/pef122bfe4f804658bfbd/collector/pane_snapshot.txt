## Proof

**Counterexample.** Define on $[0,1]$:
$$f_n(x) = g_n(x) = \sin(2\pi n x).$$

**Step 1: Membership in all $L^p$.** Each $f_n$ is bounded ($|f_n| \leq 1$), so
$f_n \in L^\infty([0,1])$, and since $[0,1]$ has finite measure, $f_n \in L^p([0
,1])$ for every $p \in [1,\infty]$. Same for $g_n$.

**Step 2: Weak convergence $f_n \rightharpoonup 0$ and $g_n \rightharpoonup 0$ i
n $L^p$ for $1 \leq p < \infty$.**

For $1 \leq p < \infty$, weak convergence in $L^p$ means: for every $\varphi \in
 L^q([0,1])$ (where $\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1$, with $q=\infty$ when $p=1$),
$$\int_0^1 \sin(2\pi n x)\,\varphi(x)\,dx \to 0.$$

Since $[0,1]$ has finite measure, $L^q([0,1]) \subseteq L^1([0,1])$ for every $q
 \in [1,\infty]$. The **Riemann–Lebesgue lemma** states that for any $\varphi \i
n L^1([0,1])$, the Fourier coefficients $\int_0^1 \varphi(x)\sin(2\pi n x)\,dx \
to 0$ as $n\to\infty$. Therefore $f_n \rightharpoonup 0$ weakly in $L^p$ for eve
ry $1 \leq p < \infty$, and likewise $g_n \rightharpoonup 0$.

So $f = 0$ and $g = 0$.

**Step 3: Weak convergence of $f_n g_n$ in $L^p$ for $1 \leq p < \infty$.**

Compute the product:
$$f_n(x)\,g_n(x) = \sin^2(2\pi n x) = \frac{1 - \cos(4\pi n x)}{2}.$$

By the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma again, $\int_0^1 \cos(4\pi n x)\,\varphi(x)\,dx \t

$. Therefore:
$$\int_0^1 f_n g_n\,\varphi\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^1 \varphi\,dx - \frac{1}{2}\
int_0^1 \cos(4\pi n x)\,\varphi\,dx \;\longrightarrow\; \frac{1}{2}\int_0^1 \var
phi\,dx.$$

This means $f_n g_n \rightharpoonup \frac{1}{2}$ weakly in $L^p$ for every $1 \l
eq p < \infty$. So $h = \frac{1}{2}$.

**Step 4: The case $p = \infty$ (weak-* convergence).**

For $p = \infty$, the natural notion is weak-* convergence in $L^\infty = (L^1)^
*$, which requires $\int_0^1 f_n\,\varphi\,dx \to 0$ for all $\varphi \in L^1([0
,1])$. This is exactly the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma, so $f_n \rightharpoonup^* 0$,

nfty$.

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

We have:
- $f = 0$, $g = 0$, so $fg = 0$.
- $h = \frac{1}{2}$.

Therefore $h = \frac{1}{2} \neq 0 = fg$.

The failure occurs because weak convergence does not control the "energy" or osc
illatory behavior of the sequences. The functions $f_n$ and $g_n$ oscillate rapi
dly, and their weak limits capture only the average (which is zero), but the pro
duct $f_n g_n = \sin^2(2\pi n x)$ has a nontrivial average $\frac{1}{2}$ that su
rvives as the weak limit $h$. $\blacksquare$
