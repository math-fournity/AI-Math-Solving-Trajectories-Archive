## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. Under the stated hypotheses, every element of $S$ is a unit in $
D$.

**Setup.** Let $D$ be an integral domain, $S \subseteq D\setminus\{0\}$ a multip
licatively closed subset, and form the localization $S^{-1}D$ sitting between $D
$ and its fraction field $\operatorname{Frac}(D)$:
$$D \;\subseteq\; S^{-1}D \;\subseteq\; \operatorname{Frac}(D).$$
The hypothesis is that every $q \in S^{-1}D$ is **integral over $D$**: there exi
sts a monic polynomial $P_q(X)\in D[X]$ with $P_q(q)=0$.

**Key step.** Fix an arbitrary $s \in S$. Since $S$ is multiplicatively closed a
nd $0\notin S$, we have $s\neq 0$, so the element
$$q \;:=\; \frac{1}{s} \;\in\; S^{-1}D$$
is well-defined (it is the fraction $\frac{1}{s}$, with numerator $1\in S$ and d
enominator $s\in S$). By the integrality hypothesis, there exist $a_0,a_1,\dots,
a_{n-1}\in D$ such that
$$\left(\frac{1}{s}\right)^{n} + a_{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{s}\right)^{n-1} + \cdots
+ a_1\left(\frac{1}{s}\right) + a_0 \;=\; 0 \quad\text{in } S^{-1}D \subseteq \o
peratorname{Frac}(D).$$

**Clear denominators.** Multiplying both sides by $s^{n}$ (which is legal inside
 the fraction field, since $s\neq 0$) yields an equality that already lives in $
D$:
$$1 \;+\; a_{n-1}\,s \;+\; a_{n-2}\,s^{2} \;+\; \cdots \;+\; a_1\,s^{n-1} \;+\;
a_0\,s^{n} \;=\; 0.$$

**Extract a witness for the inverse.** Rearranging,
$$1 \;=\; -\,s\bigl(a_{n-1} + a_{n-2}\,s + \cdots + a_1\,s^{n-2} + a_0\,s^{n-1}\
bigr).$$
Set
$$t \;:=\; -\,a_{n-1} - a_{n-2}\,s - \cdots - a_1\,s^{n-2} - a_0\,s^{n-1} \;\in\
; D.$$
Then $s\cdot t = 1$ in $D$, i.e. $t$ is a multiplicative inverse of $s$ inside $
D$.

**Conclusion.** The element $s\in S$ was arbitrary, so every element of $S$ is a
 unit in $D$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on necessity of the hypotheses.** The argument uses only: (i) $S$ is mu
ltiplicatively closed and $0\notin S$, so that $1/s\in S^{-1}D$ for every $s\in
S$; (ii) the integrality hypothesis applied to $q=1/s$; (iii) $D$ being a domain
 so that the fraction field exists and $S^{-1}D$ embeds into it (guaranteeing th
at clearing denominators is legitimate). All three are satisfied here, so the co
nclusion holds.
