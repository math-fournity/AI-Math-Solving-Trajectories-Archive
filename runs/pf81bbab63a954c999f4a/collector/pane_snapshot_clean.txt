## Solution

We seek real $x$ with
$$\sqrt{x+\sqrt{2x-1}}+\sqrt{x-\sqrt{2x-1}}=A,$$
where all square-root arguments are non-negative.

### Domain

We need $2x-1\ge 0$, i.e. $x\ge \tfrac12$. Also $x-\sqrt{2x-1}\ge 0$ requires $x
\ge \sqrt{2x-1}$; squaring (both sides non-negative when $x\ge\tfrac12$) gives $
x^2\ge 2x-1\iff (x-1)^2\ge 0$, always true. Hence the domain is $x\ge \tfrac12$.

### Simplification

Set $u=\sqrt{2x-1}\ge 0$, so $x=\tfrac{u^2+1}{2}$. Then
$$x+\sqrt{2x-1}=\frac{u^2+1}{2}+u=\frac{(u+1)^2}{2},\qquad x-\sqrt{2x-1}=\frac{u
^2+1}{2}-u=\frac{(u-1)^2}{2}.$$
Therefore
$$\sqrt{x+\sqrt{2x-1}}=\frac{u+1}{\sqrt2},\qquad \sqrt{x-\sqrt{2x-1}}=\frac{|u-1
|}{\sqrt2},$$
and the left-hand side equals
$$\frac{u+1+|u-1|}{\sqrt2}.$$

**Case 1: $0\le u\le 1$ (i.e. $\tfrac12\le x\le 1$).** Here $|u-1|=1-u$, so the
sum is
$$\frac{u+1+1-u}{\sqrt2}=\frac{2}{\sqrt2}=\sqrt2.$$

**Case 2: $u\ge 1$ (i.e. $x\ge 1$).** Here $|u-1|=u-1$, so the sum is
$$\frac{u+1+u-1}{\sqrt2}=\frac{2u}{\sqrt2}=u\sqrt2=\sqrt{2}\sqrt{2x-1}=\sqrt{4x-
2}.$$

### Answers

**(a) $A=\sqrt2$:** In Case 1 the sum is identically $\sqrt2$, and every $x\in[\
tfrac12,1]$ is admissible. In Case 2, $\sqrt{4x-2}=\sqrt2\Rightarrow 4x-2=2\Righ
tarrow x=1$, already included. Hence
$$\boxed{x\in\left[\tfrac12,\,1\right]}.$$

**(b) $A=1$:** In Case 1 the sum is $\sqrt2>1$. In Case 2, $\sqrt{4x-2}\ge \sqrt
2>1$ for $x\ge 1$. So the sum is always $\ge\sqrt2>1$, and
$$\boxed{\text{no real solution}}.$$

**(c) $A=2$:** Case 1 gives $\sqrt2\ne 2$. In Case 2, $\sqrt{4x-2}=2\Rightarrow
4x-2=4\Rightarrow x=\tfrac32$. Since $\tfrac32\ge 1$, it is valid. Verification:
 $\sqrt{2\cdot\tfrac32-1}=\sqrt2$, and $\sqrt{\tfrac32+\sqrt2}+\sqrt{\tfrac32-\s
qrt2}=\sqrt{4\cdot\tfrac32-2}=\sqrt4=2$. Hence
$$\boxed{x=\tfrac32}.$$
