## Solution

The given joint density is the standard bivariate normal distribution with $\mu_
X = \mu_Y = 0$, $\sigma_X = \sigma_Y = 1$, and correlation $\rho = p$:

$$f(x,y)= \frac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{1-p^2}} \exp\!\left(\frac{-(x^2 - 2pxy + y^2)}{2(1
-p^2)}\right).$$

**Representation via independent normals.** Since $|p|<1$, we can decompose $Y$
as
$$Y = pX + \sqrt{1-p^2}\, Z,$$
where $X$ and $Z$ are independent standard normal random variables. (This is the
 standard Cholesky-type decomposition of a bivariate normal: $\text{Cov}(X,Y)=p$
, $\text{Var}(Y)=p^2+(1-p^2)=1$.)

**Compute $E[X^2Y^2]$.** Expanding:
$$X^2 Y^2 = X^2\bigl(pX + \sqrt{1-p^2}\,Z\bigr)^2 = p^2 X^4 + 2p\sqrt{1-p^2}\,X^
3 Z + (1-p^2)\,X^2 Z^2.$$

Taking expectations and using independence of $X$ and $Z$:

- $E[X^4] = 3$ (fourth moment of a standard normal),
- $E[X^3 Z] = E[X^3]\,E[Z] = 0\cdot 0 = 0$,
- $E[X^2 Z^2] = E[X^2]\,E[Z^2] = 1\cdot 1 = 1$.

Therefore:
$$E[X^2Y^2] = p^2\cdot 3 + 2p\sqrt{1-p^2}\cdot 0 + (1-p^2)\cdot 1 = 3p^2 + 1 - p
^2 = 1 + 2p^2.$$

**Answer:**
$$\boxed{E(X^2Y^2) = 1 + 2p^2}$$
