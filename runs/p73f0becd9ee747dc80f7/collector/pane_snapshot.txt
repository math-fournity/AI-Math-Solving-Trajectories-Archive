## Proof

**Setup.** Let $C$ be a simple closed $C^1$ curve in $\mathbb{R}^2$ with positiv
e (counterclockwise) orientation, and let $g:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2$ be a d
iffeomorphism with $\det(J_g)<0$ at every point of $C$.

**Step 1: $\det(J_g)<0$ everywhere on $\mathbb{R}^2$.**

Since $g$ is a diffeomorphism, $\det(J_g)$ is continuous and nonzero on all of $
\mathbb{R}^2$. Since $\mathbb{R}^2$ is connected and $\det(J_g)$ never vanishes,
 $\det(J_g)$ has constant sign. Given $\det(J_g)<0$ on $C$, we conclude $\det(J_
g)<0$ on all of $\mathbb{R}^2$. Thus $g$ is an **orientation-reversing** diffeom
orphism.

**Step 2: $g$ maps the interior of $C$ to the interior of $g(C)$.**

Since $g$ is a diffeomorphism of $\mathbb{R}^2$, it is a proper map (preimages o
f compact sets are compact), hence it maps unbounded sets to unbounded sets and
bounded sets to bounded sets. By the Jordan Curve Theorem, $\mathbb{R}^2\setminu
s C$ has two components: a bounded interior $\operatorname{int}(C)$ and an unbou
nded exterior. Their images under $g$ are the two components of $\mathbb{R}^2\se
tminus g(C)$. Since $g(\operatorname{ext}(C))$ is unbounded, it is the exterior
of $g(C)$; therefore $g(\operatorname{int}(C))=\operatorname{int}(g(C))$.

**Step 3: Winding number computation via degree theory.**

Let $D=\operatorname{int}(C)$ (the closed disk bounded by $C$) and let $p\in\ope
ratorname{int}(C)$. Then $g(p)\in\operatorname{int}(g(C))$.

The **winding number** of $g(C)$ (with the pushforward orientation induced by $g
$) around $g(p)$ equals the **topological degree** of the map $g\big|_D : D \to
\mathbb{R}^2$ at the point $g(p)$:

$$n\big(g(C),\, g(p)\big) \;=\; \deg\!\big(g\big|_D,\, g(p)\big).$$

This is the standard boundary–degree relation: the degree of a smooth map on a d
isk equals the winding number of the image of the boundary around the target poi
nt.

Since $g$ is a diffeomorphism, $g(p)$ has exactly one preimage in $D$, namely $p
$ itself. The local degree at this preimage is:

$$\deg\!\big(g\big|_D,\, g(p)\big) \;=\; \operatorname{sgn}\!\big(\det J_g(p)\bi
g) \;=\; -1,$$

using Step 1 ($\det J_g(p)<0$) and the fact that $p$ is the unique preimage of $
g(p)$ in $D$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

We have shown:

$$n\big(g(C),\, g(p)\big) = -1 \quad \text{for all } p\in\operatorname{int}(C).$
$

A winding number of $-1$ around interior points means $g(C)$ with the pushforwar
d orientation is **negatively oriented** (clockwise). Since $C$ was positively o
riented (counterclockwise, winding number $+1$), $g(C)$ and $C$ have **opposite
orientations**. $\blacksquare$
