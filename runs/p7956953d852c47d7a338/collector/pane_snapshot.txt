## Proof

**Setup.** A principal ultrafilter on $\omega$ has the form $U_n = \{A \subseteq
 \omega : n \in A\}$ for some fixed $n \in \omega$. The natural mapping identifi
es each subset $A \subseteq \omega$ with its characteristic function $\chi_A \in
 2^\omega$, where $\chi_A(k) = 1$ iff $k \in A$. Applying this identification to
 the ultrafilter itself, the image of $U_n$ in $2^\omega$ is

$$\widehat{U_n} \;=\; \{\chi_A \in 2^\omega : A \in U_n\} \;=\; \{\alpha \in 2^\
omega : \alpha(n) = 1\}.$$

**Claim.** $\widehat{U_n}$ is open in the Cantor space topology.

**Proof of claim.** Recall the basic open sets $N_s = \{\alpha \in 2^\omega : s
\text{ is an initial segment of } \alpha\}$ for $s \in 2^{<\omega}$. Consider al
l binary strings $s$ of length $n+1$ satisfying $s(n) = 1$. There are $2^n$ such
 strings (the first $n$ entries range freely over $\{0,1\}$, the $(n+1)$-st entr
y is fixed to $1$). For each such $s$, the condition "$s$ is an initial segment
of $\alpha$" forces $\alpha(0),\ldots,\alpha(n)$ to match $s$, and in particular
 forces $\alpha(n) = 1$. Conversely, if $\alpha(n) = 1$, then the initial segmen
t $s = \alpha{\upharpoonright}(n+1)$ has $s(n) = 1$, so $\alpha \in N_s$. Theref
ore

$$\widehat{U_n} \;=\; \bigcup_{\substack{s \in 2^{n+1} \\ s(n)=1}} N_s.$$

This expresses $\widehat{U_n}$ as a union of basic open sets, hence $\widehat{U_
n}$ is open. $\square$

**Remark (on non-principal ultrafilters).** For contrast, a non-principal ultraf
ilter $U$ maps to $\widehat{U} = \{\alpha \in 2^\omega : \{k : \alpha(k)=1\} \in
 U\}$. This set is not determined by any finite initial segment: membership of $
\alpha$ in $\widehat{U}$ depends on the global behavior of $\alpha$ (the entire
set $\{k:\alpha(k)=1\}$), and no finite prefix can decide it. Indeed $\widehat{U
}$ is not Borel (it is not even determined by countably many coordinates), so in
 particular it is not open. Thus principality is precisely what yields openness.
