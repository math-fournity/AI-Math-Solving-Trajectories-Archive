## Proof

**Step 1: The eigenvectors are orthonormal.**

Since $A$ is self-adjoint and the $\lambda_n$ are distinct eigenvalues, the corr
esponding unit eigenvectors $x_n$ are pairwise orthogonal. Indeed, for $m \neq n
$:
$$\lambda_m \langle x_m, x_n\rangle = \langle Ax_m, x_n\rangle = \langle x_m, Ax
_n\rangle = \lambda_n \langle x_m, x_n\rangle,$$
so $(\lambda_m - \lambda_n)\langle x_m, x_n\rangle = 0$, giving $\langle x_m, x_
n\rangle = 0$. Since each $\|x_n\|=1$, the sequence $(x_n)$ is **orthonormal** i

**Step 2: $S$ is well-defined.**

For any $(\alpha_n)\in \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$, the partial sums $s_N = \sum_{n=1}^N

$$\|s_M - s_N\|^2 = \left\|\sum_{n=N+1}^{M}\alpha_n x_n\right\|^2 = \sum_{n=N+1}
^{M}|\alpha_n|^2 \to 0$$
as $N,M\to\infty$. By completeness of $H$, the series converges, so every elemen
t of $S$ is a well-defined vector in $H$.

**Step 3: $S = \overline{\operatorname{span}}\{x_n : n\ge 1\}$.**

$$x = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \langle x, x_n\rangle\, x_n.$$

Since $S = \overline{\operatorname{span}}\{x_n : n\ge 1\}$, and the closed linea
