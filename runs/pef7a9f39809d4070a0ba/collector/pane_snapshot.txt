## Proof

The smallest scaled L2 gain is defined as:

$$\gamma^* = \inf \left\{ \gamma > 0 : \exists\, P > 0,\; S > 0 \text{ (diagonal
)} \text{ s.t. } \begin{bmatrix} A_i^T P + P A_i + C_{z,i}^T S C_{z,i} & P B_{w,
i} \\ B_{w,i}^T P & -\gamma^2 S \end{bmatrix} < 0,\; i=1,\ldots,L \right\}$$

**Step 1: For fixed γ, the feasibility problem is a convex SDP.**

Fix $\gamma = \bar{\gamma} > 0$. The LMI becomes:

$$M_i(P, S) := \begin{bmatrix} A_i^T P + P A_i + C_{z,i}^T S C_{z,i} & P B_{w,i}
 \\ B_{w,i}^T P & -\bar{\gamma}^2 S \end{bmatrix} < 0, \quad i = 1, \ldots, L$$

This matrix is **affine** in the decision variables $(P, S)$:

- $A_i^T P + P A_i$ is linear in $P$,
- $C_{z,i}^T S C_{z,i}$ is linear in $S$,
- $P B_{w,i}$ and $B_{w,i}^T P$ are linear in $P$,
- $-\bar{\gamma}^2 S$ is linear in $S$ (since $\bar{\gamma}^2$ is now a constant
).

Combined with the convex constraints $P \succ 0$ and $S \succ 0$ (diagonal), the
 feasibility problem is a **semidefinite program (SDP)** — a convex optimization
 problem solvable in polynomial time.

**Step 2: Monotonicity of feasibility in γ.**

Suppose $(P, S, \gamma)$ satisfies the LMI. For any $\gamma' > \gamma > 0$:

$$M_i(\gamma') - M_i(\gamma) = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -(\gamma'^2 - \gamma
^2)\, S \end{bmatrix}$$

Since $\gamma'^2 - \gamma^2 > 0$ and $S \succ 0$, the difference is negative sem
idefinite (in fact, negative definite in the $(2,2)$ block). Therefore:

$$M_i(\gamma') \prec M_i(\gamma) \prec 0$$

in the Loewner order. Hence **if $\gamma$ is feasible, every $\gamma' > \gamma$
is also feasible**. The feasible set of $\gamma$ values is a ray $(\gamma^*, \in
fty)$ or $[\gamma^*, \infty)$.

**Step 3: Bisection (line search) on γ.**

By Steps 1 and 2, $\gamma^*$ is computed by bisection:

1. **Bracket**: Find $[\gamma_{\mathrm{lo}}, \gamma_{\mathrm{hi}}]$ with SDP inf
easible at $\gamma_{\mathrm{lo}}$ and feasible at $\gamma_{\mathrm{hi}}$.
2. **Test**: Set $\gamma_{\mathrm{mid}} = (\gamma_{\mathrm{lo}} + \gamma_{\mathr
m{hi}})/2$. Solve the SDP feasibility problem at $\gamma_{\mathrm{mid}}$:
   - Feasible → $\gamma_{\mathrm{hi}} \leftarrow \gamma_{\mathrm{mid}}$
   - Infeasible → $\gamma_{\mathrm{lo}} \leftarrow \gamma_{\mathrm{mid}}$
3. **Repeat** until $\gamma_{\mathrm{hi}} - \gamma_{\mathrm{lo}} < \epsilon$ for
 the desired tolerance $\epsilon$.

The interval halves each iteration, so the algorithm converges linearly to $\gam
ma^*$.

**Why a single SDP does not suffice.** The term $-\gamma^2 S$ in the $(2,2)$ blo
ck is **bilinear** in the variables $\gamma^2$ and $S$. Substituting $R = \gamma
^2 S$ linearizes the $(2,2)$ block but reintroduces nonlinearity via $S = R/\gam
ma^2$ in the $(1,1)$ block ($C_{z,i}^T S C_{z,i}$). The homogeneity of the LMI i
n $(P, S)$ (scaling both by $\alpha > 0$ preserves the inequality) allows normal
ization of $S$, but does not eliminate the bilinearity. Hence the joint optimiza
tion over $(P, S, \gamma)$ is not directly convex, and the bisection approach is
 required.

**Conclusion.** The smallest scaled L2 gain $\gamma^*$ is computed by a **bisect
ion search on $\gamma$**, where each iteration solves an **SDP feasibility probl
em** in $P$ and $S$ (for fixed $\gamma$, the LMI is affine in $P$ and $S$). The
monotonicity of feasibility in $\gamma$ guarantees convergence of the bisection
to the optimal value $\gamma^*$.
