## Proof

**Yes, $T$ is necessarily invertible with $T^{-1}=S$.**

**Key fact (joint continuity of operator multiplication).** If $A_n \to A$ and $
B_n \to B$ in operator norm, then $A_n B_n \to AB$ in operator norm. Indeed,
$$\|A_n B_n - AB\| \le \|A_n - A\|\,\|B_n\| + \|A\|\,\|B_n - B\|.$$
Since $(B_n)$ converges, it is bounded, so both terms tend to $0$.

**Applying this.** By hypothesis $T_n \to T$ in $B(X,Y)$ and $T_n^{-1} \to S$ in
 $B(Y,X)$. For every $n \ge 0$ we have the identities
$$T_n T_n^{-1} = I_Y \in B(Y,Y), \qquad T_n^{-1} T_n = I_X \in B(X,X).$$

Taking the limit $n\to\infty$ and using joint continuity of multiplication (with
 $A_n = T_n$, $B_n = T_n^{-1}$ for the first, and $A_n = T_n^{-1}$, $B_n = T_n$
for the second):

$$T S = \lim_{n\to\infty} T_n T_n^{-1} = I_Y, \qquad S T = \lim_{n\to\infty} T_n
^{-1} T_n = I_X.$$

**Conclusion.** We have exhibited $S \in B(Y,X)$ with $ST = I_X$ and $TS = I_Y$.
 This is exactly the definition of $T$ being invertible in $B(X,Y)$ with $T^{-1}
 = S$. (The two identities show $T$ is injective via $ST=I_X$ and surjective via
 $TS=I_Y$, and $S$ is the unique two-sided inverse, which is bounded by hypothes
is.)

Therefore $T$ is necessarily invertible and $T^{-1} = S$.
