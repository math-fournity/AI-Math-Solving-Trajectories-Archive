## Proof

**Setup.** Let the symmetric trapezoid $ABCD$ have $AB$ (long front base, length
 $a$), $CD$ (short rear base, length $d<a$), and equal legs $AD=BC=a$. Place coo
rdinates symmetrically about the $y$-axis:
$$A=(-a/2,0),\quad B=(a/2,0),\quad D=(-d/2,h),\quad C=(d/2,h).$$

Let $s=(a-d)/2$. Then the leg condition $AD=a$ gives
$$s^2+h^2=a^2,\quad\text{so}\quad \sqrt{h^2+s^2}=a.$$

**Circle $k_1$ (tangent to $AD, AB, BC$).** By symmetry its center is $(0,r_1)$
(tangent to $AB: y=0$). The line $AD$ has equation $h(x+a/2)-s\,y=0$. The distan
ce from $(0,r_1)$ to $AD$ equals $r_1$:
$$\frac{ha/2 - s\,r_1}{a}=r_1 \implies r_1=\frac{ha}{2(a+s)}.$$

**Circle $k_2$ (tangent to $BC, CD, AD$).** By symmetry its center is $(0,h-r_2)
$ (tangent to $CD: y=h$). Distance to $AD$:
$$\frac{ha/2-sh+s\,r_2}{a}=r_2 \implies \frac{hd}{2}=r_2(a-s) \implies r_2=\frac
{hd}{2(a-s)}.$$

**External tangency.** The centers lie on the $y$-axis, so
$$h-r_1-r_2 = r_1+r_2 \implies h=2(r_1+r_2).$$

Substituting:
$$1=\frac{a}{a+s}+\frac{d}{a-s}.$$

**Solving.** Set $t=s/a$ and $u=d/a=1-2t$:
$$1=\frac{1}{1+t}+\frac{1-2t}{1-t}.$$
Multiplying by $(1-t^2)$:
$$1-t^2=(1-t)+(1-2t)(1+t)=2-2t-2t^2,$$
$$t^2+2t-1=0 \implies t=\sqrt{2}-1.$$

**Scaling factor.**
$$x=\frac{r_2}{r_1}=\frac{d(a+s)}{a(a-s)}=\frac{(1-2t)(1+t)}{1-t}.$$
With $t=\sqrt{2}-1$: $1+t=\sqrt{2}$, $1-t=2-\sqrt{2}$, $1-2t=3-2\sqrt{2}$.
$$x=\frac{(3-2\sqrt{2})\sqrt{2}}{2-\sqrt{2}}=\frac{3\sqrt{2}-4}{2-\sqrt{2}}\cdot
\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2+\sqrt{2}}=\frac{2\sqrt{2}-2}{2}=\sqrt{2}-1.$$

**Final value.**
$$x^2=(\sqrt{2}-1)^2=3-2\sqrt{2},$$
$$1000x^2=1000(3-2\sqrt{2})\approx 1000\cdot 0.171572875 = 171.572875.$$

Rounded to the nearest integer:

$$\boxed{172}$$
