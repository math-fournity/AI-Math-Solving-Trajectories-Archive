## Proof

**Setup.** The $m=1$ term is $(1-1)^n \cdot 1 = 0$, so the sum effectively start
s at $m=2$. Define
$$S_n = \sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)^n\frac{1}{m^2}, \qquad L =
 \lim_{n\to\infty} n\,S_n.$$

We prove $L = 1$ by establishing matching upper and lower bounds.

**Key function.** Set $g(x) = \dfrac{e^{-1/x}}{x^2}$ for $x > 0$ and $g(0) = 0$.
 Note:
- $g$ is continuous and bounded on $[0,\infty)$: since $g'(x) = \dfrac{e^{-1/x}}
{x^4}(1-2x)$, $g$ increases on $(0,\tfrac12)$ and decreases on $(\tfrac12,\infty
)$, with $\max g = g(\tfrac12) = 4e^{-2}$.
- $g(x) \le 1/x^2$ for all $x > 0$ (since $e^{-1/x}\le 1$), giving integrability
 at $+\infty$.
- $g(x)\to 0$ as $x\to 0^+$, giving integrability at $0$.

**Integral evaluation.** With the substitution $t = 1/x$ ($dt = -dx/x^2$):
$$\int_0^{\infty} g(x)\,dx = \int_0^{\infty}\frac{e^{-1/x}}{x^2}\,dx = \int_{\in
fty}^{0} e^{-t}(-dt) = \int_0^{\infty} e^{-t}\,dt = 1. \tag{$\star$}$$

---

**Upper bound.** Since $\log(1 - 1/m) \le -1/m$ for $m\ge 2$, we have $(1-1/m)^n
 \le e^{-n/m}$. Hence
$$n\,S_n \;\le\; \frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-n/m}}{(m/n)^2} \;=\; \f
rac{1}{n}\sum_{m=2}^{\infty} g\!\left(\frac{m}{n}\right) \;=:\; U_n.$$

We show $U_n \to \int_0^\infty g = 1$. Fix $0 < \varepsilon < R$.

- **Middle region** $[\varepsilon, R]$: $g$ is continuous on $[\varepsilon,R]$,
so by the standard Riemann-sum theorem,
$$\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m:\,\varepsilon \le m/n \le R} g(m/n) \;\longrightarrow\; \in
t_{\varepsilon}^{R} g(x)\,dx.$$

- **Lower tail** ($m/n < \varepsilon$): there are at most $\lfloor \varepsilon n
\rfloor$ terms, each $\le 4e^{-2}$, so the contribution is $\le \frac{1}{n}\cdot
 \varepsilon n \cdot 4e^{-2} = 4\varepsilon e^{-2}$, which is $o(1)$ as $\vareps
ilon\to 0$. Likewise $\int_0^{\varepsilon} g \to 0$.

- **Upper tail** ($m/n > R$): using $g(m/n)\le n^2/m^2$,
$$\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m > Rn} g(m/n) \;\le\; n\sum_{m>Rn}\frac{1}{m^2} \;\le\; n\cd
ot\frac{1}{Rn} \;=\; \frac{1}{R},$$
which is $o(1)$ as $R\to\infty$. Likewise $\int_R^\infty g \le 1/R \to 0$.

Combining the three regions and letting $n\to\infty$, then $\varepsilon\to 0$, $
R\to\infty$:
$$\limsup_{n\to\infty}\, n\,S_n \;\le\; \lim_{n\to\infty} U_n \;=\; \int_0^{\inf
ty} g(x)\,dx \;=\; 1. \tag{1}$$

---

**Lower bound.** For $m\ge 2$, using $\displaystyle\log(1-\tfrac1m) = -\frac1m -
 \sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k\,m^k}$ and
$$\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k\,m^k} \;\le\; \sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m^k} \
;=\; \frac{1}{m(m-1)} \;\le\; \frac{2}{m^2},$$
we obtain $\log(1-1/m)\ge -1/m - 2/m^2$, hence
$$(1-\tfrac1m)^n \;\ge\; e^{-n/m - 2n/m^2} \;=\; e^{-n/m}\cdot e^{-2n/m^2}.$$

Now restrict to the range $\lceil \varepsilon n\rceil \le m \le \lfloor Rn\rfloo
r$. For such $m$, $2n/m^2 \le 2/(\varepsilon^2 n)$, so $e^{-2n/m^2} \ge 1 - 2n/m
^2 \ge 1 - \frac{2}{\varepsilon^2 n}$. Therefore
$$(1-\tfrac1m)^n \;\ge\; e^{-n/m}\!\left(1 - \frac{2}{\varepsilon^2 n}\right).$$

Summing over this range:
$$n\,S_n \;\ge\; \left(1 - \frac{2}{\varepsilon^2 n}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum_
{m=\lceil\varepsilon n\rceil}^{\lfloor Rn\rfloor} g\!\left(\frac{m}{n}\right).$$

As $n\to\infty$, the prefactor $\to 1$ and the Riemann sum $\to \int_{\varepsilo
n}^{R} g(x)\,dx$ (continuity of $g$ on $[\varepsilon,R]$). Thus
$$\liminf_{n\to\infty}\, n\,S_n \;\ge\; \int_{\varepsilon}^{R} g(x)\,dx.$$

Letting $\varepsilon\to 0$ and $R\to\infty$ (monotone convergence, $g\ge 0$):
$$\liminf_{n\to\infty}\, n\,S_n \;\ge\; \int_0^{\infty} g(x)\,dx \;=\; 1. \tag{2
}$$

---

**Conclusion.** From (1) and (2):
$$1 \;\le\; \liminf_{n\to\infty} n\,S_n \;\le\; \limsup_{n\to\infty} n\,S_n \;\l
e\; 1,$$
so $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty} n\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac1m\right)^n\
frac{1}{m^2} = 1$. $\blacksquare$
