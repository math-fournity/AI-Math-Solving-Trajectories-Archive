**Answer: No.**

**Key observation.** From $\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}=(n)$ we get $\mathfrak{a}^m\
mathfrak{b}^m=(n)^m=(n^m)$. If $\mathfrak{a}^m=(u)$ with $u\in\mathbb{Z}$, then
$$(u)\,\mathfrak{b}^m=(n^m),\qquad\text{hence}\qquad \mathfrak{b}^m=(n^m/u),$$
where $n^m/u\in\mathbb{Z}$ (since $\mathfrak{b}^m$ is an integral ideal and $n^m
/u\in\mathbb{Q}\cap\mathcal{O}_K=\mathbb{Z}$). For the conclusion $\mathfrak{b}^
m=(u)$ to hold we would need $n^m/u=\pm u$, i.e. $u^2=\pm n^m$. There is no reas
on this identity must hold, and indeed it fails in a very simple example.

**Counterexample.** Take $d=-1$, so $\mathcal{O}_K=\mathbb{Z}[i]$. Set
$$\mathfrak{a}=(2),\qquad \mathfrak{b}=(3),\qquad m=2.$$

- **Coprime:** $(2)+(3)=\mathcal{O}_K$ since $1=3-2\in(2)+(3)$. ✓
- **Product is a rational principal ideal:** $\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}=(2)(3)=(6
)$, so $n=6\in\mathbb{Z}$. ✓
- **$\mathfrak{a}^m$ is generated by a rational integer:** $\mathfrak{a}^2=(2)^2
=(4)$, so $u=4\in\mathbb{Z}$. ✓
- **But $\mathfrak{b}^m\neq(u)$:** $\mathfrak{b}^2=(3)^2=(9)$, and $(9)\neq(4)=(
u)$ since $9$ and $4$ are not associate in $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ (the only units are $
\pm1,\pm i$, and $9/4\notin\mathcal{O}_K$).

Thus all the hypotheses are satisfied yet $\mathfrak{b}^2=(9)\neq(4)=(u)$, so th
e implication **does not hold**.

**Remark (why the failure is forced, not accidental).** Taking norms: $N(\mathfr
ak{a})\,N(\mathfrak{b})=N((n))=n^2$ and $N(\mathfrak{a})^m=N((u))=u^2$. The conc
lusion $\mathfrak{b}^m=(u)$ is equivalent (via $\mathfrak{b}^m=(n^m/u)$) to $n^m
/u=\pm u$, i.e. $N(\mathfrak{b})^m=u^2=N(\mathfrak{a})^m$, i.e. $N(\mathfrak{a})
=N(\mathfrak{b})$. But the hypotheses only give $N(\mathfrak{a})\,N(\mathfrak{b}
)=n^2$; they do **not** force $N(\mathfrak{a})=N(\mathfrak{b})=n$. In the exampl
e above, $N(\mathfrak{a})=N((2))=4$ and $N(\mathfrak{b})=N((3))=9$, with $4\cdot
 9=36=6^2=n^2$, yet $4\neq 9$. So the norm data already predict $\mathfrak{b}^2=
(9)\neq(4)$, confirming the counterexample.
