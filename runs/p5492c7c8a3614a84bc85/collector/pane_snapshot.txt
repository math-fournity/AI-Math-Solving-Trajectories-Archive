## Proof

Let $A: C[0,1]\to C[0,1]$ be defined by $(Af)(x)=f(x)+\int_0^x f(t)\,dt$. Write
$A = I + V$, where $V$ is the **Volterra operator**
$$(Vf)(x)=\int_0^x f(t)\,dt.$$

We determine $\sigma(V)$ first, then use $\sigma(I+V)=\{1+\lambda:\lambda\in\sig
ma(V)\}$.

### Step 1. $V$ is compact

$V$ maps the unit ball $B=\{f:\|f\|_\infty\le 1\}$ into itself, and for $f\in B$
,
$$|(Vf)(x)-(Vf)(y)|=\left|\int_x^y f(t)\,dt\right|\le |x-y|.$$
So $V(B)$ is equicontinuous and uniformly bounded; by Arzelà–Ascoli, $V(B)$ is r
elatively compact. Hence $V$ is compact.

### Step 2. $\sigma(V)=\{0\}$ (Volterra is quasinilpotent)

By iterating,
$$(V^n f)(x)=\int_0^x \frac{(x-t)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\,f(t)\,dt,$$
so $|(V^n f)(x)|\le \|f\|_\infty\,\dfrac{x^n}{n!}\le \dfrac{\|f\|_\infty}{n!}$,
giving $\|V^n\|\le \dfrac{1}{n!}$. Therefore the spectral radius
$$r(V)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|V^n\|^{1/n}\le \lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{n!}\rig
ht)^{1/n}=0.$$
Since $r(V)=0$ and $V$ is compact (hence $\sigma(V)$ is nonempty), $\sigma(V)=\{
0\}$.

### Step 3. $V$ has no eigenvalues

If $Vf=\lambda f$ with $\lambda\ne 0$: setting $g(x)=\int_0^x f(t)\,dt$ gives $g
'=\lambda^{-1}g$... more directly, $\int_0^x f(t)\,dt=\lambda f(x)$, so $f$ is $
C^1$ and $f(x)=\lambda^{-1}\int_0^x f(t)\,dt$, hence $f'(x)=\lambda^{-1}f(x)$ wi
th $f(0)=0$ (from $x=0$ in the equation). The unique solution is $f\equiv 0$. So
 $\lambda\ne 0$ is not an eigenvalue. Combined with $\sigma(V)=\{0\}$, $V$ has *
*no eigenvalues** at all (and $0$ is not an eigenvalue either, since $Vf=0\Right
arrow f=0$).

### Step 4. Spectrum of $A=I+V$

For any bounded operator $T$, $\mu\in\sigma(I+T)\iff \mu-1\in\sigma(T)$. Thus
$$\sigma(A)=\{1+\lambda:\lambda\in\sigma(V)\}=\{1+0\}=\{1\}.$$

### Step 5. Spectral type of the single point $1$

$A-I=V$. We check the nature of $1\in\sigma(A)$:

- **Not an eigenvalue (point spectrum):** $Af=f\iff Vf=0\iff f\equiv 0$, so $1$
is not an eigenvalue of $A$.
- **Range of $A-I=V$ is not dense:** Every $g$ in $\operatorname{Ran}(V)$ satisf
ies $g(0)=0$ and is $C^1$. Hence $\operatorname{Ran}(V)\subseteq\{h\in C[0,1]:h(
0)=0\}$, a proper closed subspace. So $\operatorname{Ran}(V)$ is **not dense** i
n $C[0,1]$.

Since $A-I$ is injective but its range is not dense, $1$ belongs to the **residu
al spectrum** of $A$.

### Conclusion

The spectrum of $A$ is the single point
$$\boxed{\sigma(A)=\{1\}}.$$
The point spectrum is empty, the continuous spectrum is empty, and $\{1\}$ is en
tirely residual spectrum.
