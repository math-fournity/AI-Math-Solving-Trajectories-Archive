## Proof

**Answer: Yes, it is possible.**

**Key fact.** In a Hilbert space, given any two unit vectors $x, y \in \mathcal{
H}$, there exists a unitary operator $U \in \mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})$ with $Ux
= y$.

*Proof of the key fact.* If $x$ and $y$ are linearly dependent, then $y = \lambd
a x$ for some $|\lambda|=1$; the operator $U = \lambda P + (I-P)$, where $P$ is
the orthogonal projection onto $\operatorname{span}\{x\}$, is unitary and satisf
ies $Ux = \lambda x = y$.

If $x$ and $y$ are linearly independent, extend $\{x\}$ to an orthonormal basis
$\{x\}\cup\{x_\alpha\}_{\alpha\in A}$ of $\mathcal{H}$ and $\{y\}\cup\{y_\alpha\
}_{\alpha\in A}$ to an orthonormal basis of $\mathcal{H}$ (using the same index
set, since both are bases of the same space). Define $U$ by $Ux = y$ and $Ux_\al
pha = y_\alpha$ for all $\alpha$. This maps an orthonormal basis to an orthonorm
al basis, hence extends uniquely to a unitary operator with $Ux = y$. $\square$

**Construction.** Since $\mathcal{H}$ is separable, it admits a countable orthon
ormal basis. Pick a unit vector $e$ and extend $\{e\}$ to a (countable) orthonor
mal basis $\{e_n\}_{n=1}^{N}$ of $\mathcal{H}$, where $N \in \mathbb{N}\cup\{\in
fty\}$ and $e_1 = e$.

By the key fact, for each $n$ there exists a unitary $U_n \in \mathfrak{B}(\math
cal{H})$ with $U_n e = e_n$ (for $n=1$ take $U_1 = I$).

Then $\{U_n e\}_{n=1}^{N} = \{e_n\}_{n=1}^{N}$, which is an orthonormal basis of
 $\mathcal{H}$, hence a maximal orthonormal set.

**Verification of orthonormality and maximality.** For $n \neq m$,
$$\langle U_n e,\, U_m e\rangle = \langle e_n, e_m\rangle = 0,$$
and $\|U_n e\| = \|e_n\| = 1$, so the set is orthonormal. It is maximal because
$\{e_n\}$ is a basis: the only vector orthogonal to every $e_n$ is $0$.

Therefore such unitary operators $U_n$ always exist for any unit vector $e$ in a
 separable Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$.
