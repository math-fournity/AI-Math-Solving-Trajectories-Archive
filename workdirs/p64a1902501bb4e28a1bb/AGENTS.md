# Problem

In a specialized greenhouse, a robotic planter operates on a square grid of 36 planting modules arranged in 6 rows and 6 columns, represented by coordinates $(x,y)$ where $x,y \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$. 

Due to a software glitch, the robot selects exactly 6 distinct planting modules from these 36 possible locations, with every possible combination of 6 modules being equally likely to be chosen. Once the 6 modules are selected, an automated irrigation system defines a "hydration zone" shaped as the smallest convex polygon that encloses all 6 selected modules. 

Let $A$ be the expected area of this hydration zone across all possible random selections. Calculate the estimate $N = \lfloor 10^4 A \rfloor$.
