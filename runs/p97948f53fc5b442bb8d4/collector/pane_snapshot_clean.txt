## Proof

Let $S = \{f \colon \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R} : f \text{ is injective}\}$. We sh
ow $|S| = 2^{\aleph_0}$.

**Upper bound.** Every injective function is in particular a function, so $S \su
bseteq \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}$. Hence
$$|S| \le |\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}| = |\mathbb{R}|^{|\mathbb{N}|} = (2^{\aleph_0
})^{\aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_0 \cdot \aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_0}.$$

**Lower bound.** We exhibit an injection $\mathbb{R} \hookrightarrow S$. For eac
h $r \in \mathbb{R}$, define
$$f_r \colon \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}, \qquad f_r(n) = r + n.$$
Since $n \mapsto r+n$ is strictly increasing, $f_r$ is injective, so $f_r \in S$
. Moreover, if $r \neq r'$ then $f_r(0) = r \neq r' = f_{r'}(0)$, so $f_r \neq f
_{r'}$. Thus $r \mapsto f_r$ is injective, giving
$$|S| \ge |\mathbb{R}| = 2^{\aleph_0}.$$

**Conclusion.** Combining both bounds,
$$|S| = 2^{\aleph_0}.$$
