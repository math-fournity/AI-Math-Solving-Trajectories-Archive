## Solution

**Claim:** The statement is true: under the hypotheses, $D^2 \geq 0$.

**Proof.**

Let $|D|$ be the complete linear system, assumed non-empty with no fixed compone
nt. Let $F$ denote the fixed part of $|D|$ (the greatest common divisor of all m
embers), so that $|D| = F + |M|$ where $|M|$ has no fixed part. The hypothesis "
no fixed component" means $F = 0$, i.e. $|D| = |M|$ has trivial fixed part.

We distinguish two cases.

**Case 1: $\dim |D| = 0$.** Then $|D| = \{D_0\}$ consists of a single effective
divisor. The fixed part of a linear system is the greatest common divisor of all
 its members; with only one member $D_0$, the fixed part equals $D_0$ itself. Si
nce $|D|$ has no fixed component, $D_0 = 0$, hence $D \sim 0$ and
$$D^2 = 0 \geq 0.$$

**Case 2: $\dim |D| \geq 1$.** Choose two distinct members $D_1, D_2 \in |D|$. S
ince $|D|$ has no fixed component, $D_1$ and $D_2$ share no common irreducible c
omponent: any common component would be a fixed component of $|D|$. Therefore $D
_1$ and $D_2$ intersect properly, and their intersection number is a sum of posi
tive local intersection multiplicities:
$$D_1 \cdot D_2 = \sum_{p \in \mathrm{Supp}(D_1) \cap \mathrm{Supp}(D_2)} (D_1 \
cdot D_2)_p \geq 0.$$
(Each local intersection multiplicity $(D_1 \cdot D_2)_p$ is a positive integer
because $D_1, D_2$ have no common component through $p$; the sum is $\geq 0$, an
d equals $0$ exactly when $D_1$ and $D_2$ are disjoint.)

Since $D_1 \sim D_2 \sim D$, linear equivalence preserves intersection numbers,
so
$$D^2 = D_1 \cdot D_2 \geq 0.$$

**Conclusion.** In both cases $D^2 \geq 0$. Hence the statement is **true**: a d
ivisor whose complete linear system is non-empty and has no fixed component alwa
ys has non-negative self-intersection. $\blacksquare$
