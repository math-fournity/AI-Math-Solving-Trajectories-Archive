## Solution

**Answer: 4**

### Step 1: Lower bound via modular arithmetic

Cubes modulo 9 take only the values $\{0, 1, 8\} \equiv \{0, 1, -1\} \pmod{9}$,
since:
$$0^3\equiv0,\; 1^3\equiv1,\; 2^3\equiv8\equiv-1,\; 3^3\equiv0,\; 4^3\equiv1,\;
5^3\equiv-1,\; 6^3\equiv0,\; 7^3\equiv1,\; 8^3\equiv-1 \pmod 9.$$

A sum of $k$ cubes is therefore congruent mod 9 to a sum of $k$ values each in $
\{0, \pm1\}$, which can produce residues in $\{-k, \ldots, k\} \pmod 9$.

- With $k \le 3$ cubes, the achievable residues mod 9 are $\{0, \pm1, \pm2, \pm3
\} = \{0,1,2,3,6,7,8\}$.
- The residue $4 \pmod 9$ is **not** in this set, so any number $\equiv 4 \pmod
9$ requires **at least 4** cubes.

By Step 1, **at least 4 cubes** are required.
