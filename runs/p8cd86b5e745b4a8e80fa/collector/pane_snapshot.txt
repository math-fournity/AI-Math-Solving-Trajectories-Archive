## Proof

**Claim.** For every non-empty topological space $X$, the space $C_p(X)$ of cont
inuous real-valued functions on $X$ (endowed with the topology of pointwise conv
ergence) contains a subspace homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}$.

**Construction.** Since $X$ is non-empty, fix a point $x_0 \in X$. Consider the
set of constant functions
$$
\mathcal{C} \;=\; \{\,c_t \in C_p(X) : t \in \mathbb{R}\,\}, \qquad c_t(x) = t \
;\text{ for all } x \in X.
$$
Every constant function is continuous regardless of the topology on $X$, so $\ma
thcal{C} \subseteq C_p(X)$. Define
$$
\phi : \mathbb{R} \longrightarrow C_p(X), \qquad \phi(t) = c_t.
$$
This is clearly an injection with image $\mathcal{C}$. We show $\phi$ is a topol
ogical embedding.

**$\phi$ is continuous.** The topology of pointwise convergence on $C_p(X)$ is t
he subspace topology inherited from the product $\mathbb{R}^X$, generated by the
 subbasic open sets
$$
[x, U] \;=\; \{\, f \in C_p(X) : f(x) \in U \,\}, \qquad x \in X,\; U \subseteq
\mathbb{R} \text{ open}.
$$
For any such subbasic set,
$$
\phi^{-1}([x, U]) \;=\; \{\, t \in \mathbb{R} : c_t(x) \in U \,\} \;=\; \{\, t \
in \mathbb{R} : t \in U \,\} \;=\; U,
$$
which is open in $\mathbb{R}$. Hence $\phi$ is continuous.

**The inverse (on the image) is continuous.** Consider the evaluation map at $x_
0$:
$$
e_{x_0} : C_p(X) \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}, \qquad e_{x_0}(f) = f(x_0).
$$
This is one of the coordinate projections of the product $\mathbb{R}^X$, hence c
ontinuous. Restricted to $\mathcal{C} = \phi(\mathbb{R})$, we have
$$
e_{x_0}\big|_{\mathcal{C}}(c_t) = t,
$$
so $e_{x_0}\big|_{\mathcal{C}} = \phi^{-1}$. Being the restriction of a continuo
us map, $\phi^{-1} : \mathcal{C} \to \mathbb{R}$ is continuous.

**Conclusion.** The map $\phi : \mathbb{R} \to \mathcal{C}$ is a continuous bije
ction with continuous inverse, hence a homeomorphism. Therefore $\mathcal{C}$ is
 a subspace of $C_p(X)$ isomorphic (homeomorphic) to $\mathbb{R}$.

Since the argument uses only the non-emptiness of $X$ (to guarantee the existenc
e of $x_0$ and to ensure the constant functions are well-defined and the evaluat
ion inverse works), this holds for **every** non-empty topological space $X$.

**Answer:** Yes, $C_p(X)$ always contains a subspace isomorphic to $\mathbb{R}$
— the subspace of constant functions.
