## Solution

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains only the equation and the
condition on $\alpha$; no solution, sketch, or answer is embedded. Proceeding.

---

**Claim.** A solution exists only for $\alpha = 1$, and in that case the unique
solution is $f(x)=x$ for all $x>0$.

**Verification.** With $f(x)=x$ and $\alpha=1$: $f(f(x)+y)=f(x+y)=x+y$ and $\alp
ha x+\frac{1}{f(1/y)}=x+\frac{1}{1/y}=x+y$. ✓

It remains to prove uniqueness.

---

### 1.  Injectivity of $f$ and the sign of $\alpha$

Write the equation as
$$f(f(x)+y)=\alpha x+g(y),\qquad g(y):=\frac{1}{f(1/y)}. \tag{1}$$

*Injectivity.* If $f(x_1)=f(x_2)$, then (1) gives $\alpha x_1+g(y)=\alpha x_2+g(
y)$ for all $y>0$, hence $\alpha(x_1-x_2)=0$; since $\alpha\neq 0$, $x_1=x_2$.

*Positivity of $\alpha.* If $\alpha<0$, fix $y>0$ and let $x\to\infty$ in (1): t
he right side $\alpha x+g(y)\to -\infty$, but the left side $f(\cdot)>0$. Contra
diction. Hence $\alpha>0$.

---

### 2.  Surjectivity of $f$

Let $R:=f(\mathbb R_{>0})\subseteq\mathbb R_{>0}$.

*$\sup R=+\infty$.* From (1), $f(f(x)+y)=\alpha x+g(y)\ge \alpha x\to+\infty$ as
 $x\to\infty$, and the left side lies in $R$.

*$\inf R=0$.* Suppose $\inf R=m>0$. Then $f\ge m$, so $g(y)=1/f(1/y)\le 1/m$, an
d (1) gives
$$f(f(x)+y)=\alpha x+g(y)\in \bigl(\alpha x,\;\alpha x+1/m\bigr].$$
As $y$ varies (with $x$ fixed), $1/y$ runs over all $\mathbb R_{>0}$, so $f(1/y)
$ runs over $R$ and $g(y)$ over $1/R$; thus
$$\alpha x+1/R\subseteq R\qquad\forall x>0. \tag{2}$$
Since $\sup R=+\infty$, $\inf(1/R)=0$, so $\inf(\alpha x+1/R)=\alpha x$. For (2)
 to hold with $R\subseteq[m,\infty)$ we need $\alpha x\ge m$ for **all** $x>0$,
impossible as $x\to0^+$. Hence $\inf R=0$.

*Conclusion.* From (2) and $\alpha>0$, $\mathbb R_{>0}+1/R\subseteq R$. Since $\
inf(1/R)=1/\sup R=0$, for any $r>0$ pick $t\in 1/R$ with $t<r$; then $r=(r-t)+t\
in\mathbb R_{>0}+1/R\subseteq R$. So $R\supseteq\mathbb R_{>0}$, i.e. $R=\mathbb
 R_{>0}$.

Together with injectivity, **$f$ is a bijection $\mathbb R_{>0}\to\mathbb R_{>0}
$**.

---

### 3.  A Pexider equation

Since $f$ is bijective, apply $f^{-1}$ to (1):
$$f^{-1}\bigl(\alpha x+g(y)\bigr)=f(x)+y,\qquad x,y>0. \tag{3}$$
Set $s=\alpha x\in\mathbb R_{>0}$ (as $\alpha>0$) and $t=g(y)=1/f(1/y)$. As $y$
runs over $\mathbb R_{>0}$, $1/y$ runs over $\mathbb R_{>0}$ and $f(1/y)$ over $
R=\mathbb R_{>0}$, so $t$ runs over all of $\mathbb R_{>0}$. Define
$$F(s):=f^{-1}(s),\quad A(s):=f(s/\alpha),\quad B(t):=g^{-1}(t).$$
Then (3) becomes the **Pexider equation**
$$F(s+t)=A(s)+B(t),\qquad s,t>0, \tag{4}$$
with $F,A,B:\mathbb R_{>0}\to\mathbb R_{>0}$ (in particular, all three are bound
ed below by $0$).

---

### 4.  Solving the Pexider equation

The general solution of (4) is (Pexider–Cauchy): there exist an additive functio
n $\chi:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ ($\chi(u+v)=\chi(u)+\chi(v)$) and constants $c_F,
c_A,c_B$ with $c_F=c_A+c_B$ such that
$$F(s)=\chi(s)+c_F,\quad A(s)=\chi(s)+c_A,\quad B(t)=\chi(t)+c_B.$$
Since $F(s)>0$ for all $s>0$, we have $\chi(s)>-c_F$ on $(0,\infty)$: $\chi$ is
**bounded below on an interval**. A classical theorem on Cauchy's equation then
forces $\chi$ to be linear: $\chi(s)=\lambda s$ for some $\lambda\in\mathbb R$.

Thus $F(s)=\lambda s+c_F$. Because $F:\mathbb R_{>0}\to\mathbb R_{>0}$ is a bije
ction:
- $\lambda>0$ (else the range is not $(0,\infty)$),
- the range $\{(\lambda s+c_F):s>0\}=(c_F,\infty)$ must equal $(0,\infty)$, so $
c_F=0$.

Hence $F(s)=\lambda s$ and $f(x)=F^{-1}(x)=x/\lambda$, i.e. $f(x)=\beta x$ with
$\beta=1/\lambda>0$.

---

### 5.  Determining $\beta$ and $\alpha$

Substitute $f(x)=\beta x$ into the original equation:
$$\text{LHS}=f(f(x)+y)=\beta(\beta x+y)=\beta^{2}x+\beta y,$$
$$\text{RHS}=\alpha x+\frac{1}{f(1/y)}=\alpha x+\frac{1}{\beta/y}=\alpha x+\frac
{y}{\beta}.$$
Comparing coefficients of $x$ and $y$:
$$\beta^{2}=\alpha,\qquad \beta=\frac{1}{\beta}\;\Longrightarrow\;\beta^{2}=1.$$
Since $\beta>0$, $\beta=1$, and then $\alpha=\beta^{2}=1$.

---

### 6.  Conclusion

The only value of $\alpha$ for which a solution exists is $\alpha=1$, and the un
ique function satisfying the equation is
$$\boxed{\,f(x)=x\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb R_{>0},\ \alpha=1\,}.$$
