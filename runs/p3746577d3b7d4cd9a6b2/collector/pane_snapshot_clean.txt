## Solution

**Parametrization.** The curve $\gamma_R$ is the right semicircle of radius $R$.
 Parametrize by
$$x_1 = R\cos\theta,\quad x_2 = R\sin\theta,\qquad \theta\in\left(-\tfrac{\pi}{2
},\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right),$$
so that $ds = R\,d\theta$ and $x_1^2+x_2^2 = R^2$. The integral becomes
$$I_R \;=\; \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+R^2}}\,\cos(R\sin\theta)\;R\,
d\theta \;=\; \frac{R}{\sqrt{1+R^2}}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos(R\sin\theta)\,d\th
eta.$$

**Step 1. The prefactor.** Clearly $\dfrac{R}{\sqrt{1+R^2}}\xrightarrow[R\to\inf
ty]{}1$.

**Step 2. The oscillatory integral vanishes.** Set
$$J(R) \;=\; \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos(R\sin\theta)\,d\theta.$$
We show $J(R)\to 0$ as $R\to+\infty$ by the **method of stationary phase** (equi
valently, the van der Corput lemma).

Consider the phase $\phi(\theta)=\sin\theta$ on $\left[-\tfrac{\pi}{2},\tfrac{\p
i}{2}\right]$. Its derivative $\phi'(\theta)=\cos\theta$ satisfies:
- $\phi'(\theta)>0$ strictly on the open interval $\left(-\tfrac{\pi}{2},\tfrac{
\pi}{2}\right)$, so there are **no interior stationary points**;
- $\phi'(\pm\tfrac{\pi}{2})=0$, i.e. the only stationary points are the **endpoi
nts**.

At each endpoint, $\phi''(\pm\tfrac{\pi}{2})=-\sin(\pm\tfrac{\pi}{2})=\mp 1\neq
0$, so the phase has a **non-degenerate critical point** at each endpoint. By th
e standard stationary-phase estimate at a non-degenerate endpoint (van der Corpu
t lemma with $|\phi''|\ge c>0$ near the endpoint), each endpoint contributes $O(
R^{-1/2})$. On any compact subinterval $[-\tfrac{\pi}{2}+\varepsilon,\,\tfrac{\p
i}{2}-\varepsilon]$ the phase has no stationary point and $\phi'\ge\sin\varepsil
on>0$, so integration by parts gives a contribution $O(R^{-1})$. Combining,
$$J(R) = O(R^{-1/2})\xrightarrow[R\to\infty]{}0.$$

*(Remark: $J(R)=\pi J_0(R)$, the Bessel function of the first kind, whose classi
cal asymptotic $J_0(R)\sim\sqrt{2/(\pi R)}\cos(R-\pi/4)$ confirms $J(R)\to 0$.)*

**Step 3. Combine.**
$$\lim_{R\to+\infty} I_R \;=\; \lim_{R\to+\infty}\frac{R}{\sqrt{1+R^2}}\;\cdot\;
\lim_{R\to+\infty}J(R) \;=\; 1\cdot 0 \;=\; 0.$$
