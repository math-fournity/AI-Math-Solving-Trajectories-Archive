## Proof

**Claim.** Every connected, simply connected, nilpotent $n$-dimensional Lie grou
p $G$ is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^n$. In fact, $G$ is diffeomorphic to $\math
bb{R}^n$.

Let $\mathfrak{g} = \mathrm{Lie}(G)$, so $\mathfrak{g}$ is an $n$-dimensional ni
lpotent Lie algebra (nilpotent because $G$ is nilpotent, and $\dim \mathfrak{g}
= n$). As a vector space $\mathfrak{g} \cong \mathbb{R}^n$. We will show the exp
onential map $\exp : \mathfrak{g} \to G$ is a global diffeomorphism.

### Step 1. The BCH series is finite

Since $\mathfrak{g}$ is nilpotent, its lower central series terminates: $\mathfr
ak{g}^{(1)} = [\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}],\; \mathfrak{g}^{(k+1)} = [\mathfrak{g
}, \mathfrak{g}^{(k)}]$, and $\mathfrak{g}^{(c+1)} = 0$ for some $c$ (the nilpot
ency class). The Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula expresses the product $\exp(X)
\exp(Y) = \exp(\mathrm{BCH}(X,Y))$ where

$$\mathrm{BCH}(X,Y) = X + Y + \tfrac{1}{2}[X,Y] + \tfrac{1}{12}[X,[X,Y]] - \tfra
c{1}{12}[Y,[Y,X]] + \cdots$$

Every term of degree $\geq 2$ in this series is an iterated bracket of total len
gth $\geq 2$, hence lies in some $\mathfrak{g}^{(k)}$. All terms of length $> c$
 vanish because $\mathfrak{g}^{(c+1)} = 0$. Therefore $\mathrm{BCH}(X,Y)$ is a *
*finite** polynomial in $X, Y$ (real-analytic, in fact polynomial), and is defin
ed for all $X, Y \in \mathfrak{g}$.

### Step 2. $\exp(\mathfrak{g})$ is a subgroup of $G$

By Step 1, for any $X, Y \in \mathfrak{g}$,
$$\exp(X)\exp(Y) = \exp(\mathrm{BCH}(X,Y)) \in \exp(\mathfrak{g}).$$
Also $\exp(X)^{-1} = \exp(-X) \in \exp(\mathfrak{g})$ and $e = \exp(0)$. Hence $
\exp(\mathfrak{g})$ is a subgroup of $G$.

### Step 3. $\exp$ is surjective

The differential $d(\exp)_0 : \mathfrak{g} \to T_e G \cong \mathfrak{g}$ is the
identity, so by the inverse function theorem $\exp$ is a local diffeomorphism at
 $0$. In particular $\exp(\mathfrak{g})$ contains an open neighborhood of $e$ in
 $G$. An open subgroup of a topological group is also closed (its complement is
a union of open cosets). Since $G$ is **connected**, the only nonempty open-and-
closed subset is $G$ itself. Therefore
$$\exp(\mathfrak{g}) = G,$$
i.e., $\exp$ is surjective.

### Step 4. $\exp$ is a covering map

Define a Lie group structure on the manifold $\mathfrak{g} \cong \mathbb{R}^n$ b
y the polynomial multiplication $X \ast Y := \mathrm{BCH}(X,Y)$. Then $\exp : (\
mathfrak{g}, \ast) \to G$ is a surjective Lie group homomorphism (by Step 2) and
 a local diffeomorphism (since $d(\exp)_0 = \mathrm{id}$, and by homogeneity $d(
\exp)_X$ is invertible for every $X$ — equivalently, $\exp$ is a local diffeomor
phism at every point because left-translation by $\exp(X)$ in $G$ and by $X$ in
$(\mathfrak{g},\ast)$ reduce to the situation at the identity).

A surjective local diffeomorphism that is a group homomorphism between Lie group
s is a **covering map**. Its kernel
$$K := \exp^{-1}(e) \subset (\mathfrak{g}, \ast)$$
is a discrete normal subgroup, and $G \cong (\mathfrak{g}, \ast)/K$ as a coverin
g.

### Step 5. The kernel is trivial; $\exp$ is a diffeomorphism

As a smooth manifold, $(\mathfrak{g}, \ast) \cong \mathbb{R}^n$, which is **simp
ly connected**. Hence $(\mathfrak{g}, \ast)$ is the universal cover of $G$, and
$K = \pi_1(G)$ (as a discrete group). Since $G$ is **simply connected**, $\pi_1(
G) = 0$, so $K = \{0\}$. Therefore $\exp$ is bijective.

A bijective local diffeomorphism is a global diffeomorphism. Thus
$$\exp : \mathfrak{g} \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} G$$
is a diffeomorphism.

### Step 6. Conclusion

Since $\mathfrak{g}$ is an $n$-dimensional real vector space, $\mathfrak{g} \con
g \mathbb{R}^n$ as a smooth manifold. Composing with $\exp$,
$$G \;\cong\; \mathfrak{g} \;\cong\; \mathbb{R}^n$$
as smooth manifolds. In particular, $G$ is **homeomorphic** to $\mathbb{R}^n$. $
\blacksquare$
