## Solution

**Setup.** Place the origin at the center of the concentric circles (radii 1, 2,
 3, …). Rainbow Dash travels along a straight line; let \(d\) be the perpendicul
ar distance from the origin to this line. Parameterize the line by signed distan
ce \(t\) from the foot of the perpendicular. The line meets the circle of radius
 \(n\) exactly when
\[
t^2 + d^2 = n^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t = \pm\sqrt{n^2-d^2},
\]
provided \(n > d\). Let \(m\) be the smallest integer with \(m > d\); then the c
rossed circles are \(m, m+1, m+2, \dots\), and the crossing positions (in increa
sing \(t\)) are
\[
\cdots,\; -\sqrt{(m{+}1)^2{-}d^2},\; -\sqrt{m^2{-}d^2},\; +\sqrt{m^2{-}d^2},\; +
\sqrt{(m{+}1)^2{-}d^2},\; +\sqrt{(m{+}2)^2{-}d^2},\;\dots
\]

**Two kinds of gaps.** Define \(u_n = \sqrt{n^2-d^2}\). Consecutive crossings on
 the same side give *outer gaps* \(u_{n+1}-u_n\), and the gap between \(-u_m\) a
nd \(+u_m\) (through the closest approach to the origin) is the *inner gap* \(2u
_m\). A key fact:
\[
u_{n+1}-u_n = \frac{2n+1}{u_{n+1}+u_n} \ge 1,
\]
since \(u_{n+1}+u_n \le u_{n+1}+u_n\) and at \(d=0\) the gap equals \(1\), incre
asing as \(d\to n\). So **every outer gap is \(\ge 1\)**, while the inner gap \(
2u_m\) can be arbitrarily small (as \(d\to m\)).

**Identifying the gaps.** The three given distances are \(\frac{\sqrt5}{5}=\frac
1{\sqrt5}\), \(\sqrt5\), \(\frac{3\sqrt5}{5}=\frac3{\sqrt5}\). The first (\(\fra
c1{\sqrt5}\approx 0.447 < 1\)) cannot be an outer gap, so it is the distance fro
m her **starting point** to the first crossing (not a full inter-crossing gap).
The two genuine consecutive inter-crossing gaps are
\[
\sqrt5 \quad\text{and}\quad \frac3{\sqrt5}.
\]
Both are \(\ge 1\), so both are outer gaps. Moreover outer gaps **decrease** as
\(n\) grows (tending to \(1\)), and indeed \(\sqrt5 > \frac3{\sqrt5}\), consiste
nt with moving outward on one side.

**Solving for \(d\) and the circle index.** Let the two outer gaps be \(u_{n+1}-
u_n = \sqrt5\) and \(u_{n+2}-u_{n+1} = \frac3{\sqrt5}\). Using \(u_{k+1}^2-u_k^2
 = 2k+1\):
\[
(u_{n+1}-u_n)(u_{n+1}+u_n)=2n+1 \;\Longrightarrow\; \sqrt5\,(u_{n+1}+u_n)=2n+1,
\]
\[
(u_{n+2}-u_{n+1})(u_{n+2}+u_{n+1})=2n+3 \;\Longrightarrow\; \tfrac3{\sqrt5}(u_{n
+2}+u_{n+1})=2n+3.
\]
From the first: \(u_{n+1}+u_n = \frac{2n+1}{\sqrt5}\), and with \(u_{n+1}-u_n=\s
qrt5\):
\[
2u_{n+1} = \sqrt5 + \frac{2n+1}{\sqrt5} = \frac{2n+6}{\sqrt5} \;\Longrightarrow\
; u_{n+1}=\frac{n+3}{\sqrt5}.
\]
Then \(u_{n+1}^2 = \frac{(n+3)^2}{5} = (n+1)^2-d^2\), giving \(d^2 = (n+1)^2 - \
frac{(n+3)^2}{5} = \frac{4(n^2+n-1)}{5}\).

Now enforce the second gap. \(u_{n+2} = u_{n+1}+\frac3{\sqrt5} = \frac{n+6}{\sqr
t5}\), so \(u_{n+2}^2 = \frac{(n+6)^2}{5} = (n+2)^2-d^2\). Substituting \(d^2\):
\[
(n+2)^2 - \frac{4(n^2+n-1)}{5} = \frac{(n+6)^2}{5}
\;\Longrightarrow\; 5(n+2)^2 - 4(n^2+n-1) = (n+6)^2.
\]
Expanding: \(5n^2+20n+20-4n^2-4n+4 = n^2+12n+36\), i.e. \(n^2+16n+24 = n^2+12n+3
6\), giving \(4n=12\), so
\[
n=3, \qquad d^2 = \frac{4(9+3-1)}{5} = \frac{44}{5}.
\]
Check: \(d=\sqrt{44/5}\approx 2.966\), so \(2<d<3\); the smallest crossed circle
 is \(m=3\). ✓ Consistent.

**Verifying the starting point.** The crossings on the positive side are at
\[

u_4=\sqrt{16-\tfrac{44}{5}}=\sqrt{\tfrac{36}{5}}=\tfrac6{\sqrt5},\quad

The inter-crossing gaps are \(\frac6{\sqrt5}-\frac1{\sqrt5}=\frac5{\sqrt5}=\sqrt
