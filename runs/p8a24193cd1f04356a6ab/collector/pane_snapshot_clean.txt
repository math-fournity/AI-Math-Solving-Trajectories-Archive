## Proof

Let $w_n = \frac{n^2}{n^2+1}$. Note that $w_1 = \frac{1}{2}$ and $w_n$ is strict
ly increasing with $\lim_{n\to\infty} w_n = 1$, so

$$\frac{1}{2} \le w_n < 1 \quad \text{for all } n \ge 1.$$

**Step 1: $\|\cdot\|_2'$ is a norm on $l^2$.**

Positive definiteness: if $\|x\|_2' = 0$ then $\sum w_n|x_n|^2 = 0$, and since e
very $w_n > 0$, this forces $x_n = 0$ for all $n$. Absolute homogeneity and the
triangle inequality (via Minkowski's inequality applied to the weighted inner pr
oduct $\langle x,y\rangle' = \sum w_n x_n \overline{y_n}$) are straightforward.
So $\|\cdot\|_2'$ is a norm.

**Step 2: The norms $\|\cdot\|_2'$ and $\|\cdot\|_2$ are equivalent.**

Using the bounds $\frac{1}{2} \le w_n \le 1$, for every $x \in l^2$:

$$\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|x_n|^2 \;\le\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} w_n|x_n|^2 \
;\le\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|x_n|^2,$$

hence

$$\boxed{\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\|x\|_2 \;\le\; \|x\|_2' \;\le\; \|x\|_2\;}$$

for all $x \in l^2$. This establishes norm equivalence.

**Step 3: Completeness.**

Since the two norms are equivalent, they induce the same topology and the same C
auchy sequences. Let $(x^{(k)})_{k\ge1}$ be a Cauchy sequence in $(l^2, \|\cdot\
|_2')$. By the equivalence above, it is also Cauchy in $(l^2, \|\cdot\|_2)$. Sin
ce $l^2$ with the standard norm is a Banach space, there exists $x \in l^2$ with
 $\|x^{(k)} - x\|_2 \to 0$. By norm equivalence, $\|x^{(k)} - x\|_2' \to 0$ as w
ell. Hence every Cauchy sequence converges, and $(l^2, \|\cdot\|_2')$ is complet
e.

**Conclusion:** The space $l^2$ equipped with the norm $\|x\|_2' = \left(\sum_{n
=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^2}{n^2+1}|x_n|^2\right)^{1/2}$ **is a Banach space**, since
this norm is equivalent to the standard $l^2$ norm (the weights satisfy $\frac{1
}{2} \le \frac{n^2}{n^2+1} \le 1$), and completeness is preserved under equivale
nt norms.
