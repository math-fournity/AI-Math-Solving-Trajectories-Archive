## Analysis

Let $\mu$ be Lebesgue measure on $[0,\infty]$. Recall each measure is given by a
 density w.r.t. $\mu$:

- $\mu_1(E) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^3}\int_{E\cap[n,n+1]} x\,d\mu$, so o
n $[n,n+1]$ we have $\dfrac{d\mu_1}{d\mu}=\dfrac{x}{n^3}$, and $\mu_1$ is zero o
n $[0,1)$.
- $\mu_2(E)=\int_{E\cap[1,\infty]}\frac{1}{x^2}\,d\mu$, so $\dfrac{d\mu_2}{d\mu}
=\dfrac{1}{x^2}\mathbf{1}_{[1,\infty)}(x)$, and $\mu_2$ is zero on $[0,1)$.

---

## 1. Is $\mu\ll\mu_2$? — **No.**

$\mu_2$ is supported on $[1,\infty]$, hence
$$\mu_2([0,1))=\int_{[0,1)\cap[1,\infty]}\frac{1}{x^2}\,d\mu=\int_{\varnothing}\
frac{1}{x^2}\,d\mu=0,$$
yet
$$\mu([0,1))=1\neq 0.$$
Therefore $\mu$ is **not** absolutely continuous w.r.t. $\mu_2$.

---

## 2. Is $\mu_2\ll\mu_1$? — **Yes.**

Both $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$ are $\sigma$-finite: $[0,\infty]=\bigcup_{n\ge 0}[n,n+1
]$ with $\mu_1([n,n+1])=\frac{2n+1}{2n^3}<\infty$ (for $n\ge1$) and $\mu_2([n,n+
1])=\int_n^{n+1}\frac{dx}{x^2}<\infty$.

Suppose $\mu_1(E)=0$. Then for every $n\ge1$,
$$0=\frac{1}{n^3}\int_{E\cap[n,n+1]}x\,d\mu.$$
Since $x\ge n\ge1>0$ on $[n,n+1]$, the function $x\mapsto x$ is strictly positiv
e a.e. on $[n,n+1]$, so the vanishing integral forces
$$\mu(E\cap[n,n+1])=0\quad\text{for all }n\ge1.$$
Summing, $\mu(E\cap[1,\infty))=0$. Consequently
$$\mu_2(E)=\int_{E\cap[1,\infty]}\frac{1}{x^2}\,d\mu=0.$$
Hence $\mu_2\ll\mu_1$.

### Derivative $\dfrac{d\mu_2}{d\mu_1}$

On each interval $[n,n+1]$ ($n\ge1$) both densities w.r.t. $\mu$ are strictly po
sitive, so by the chain rule for Radon–Nikodym derivatives:
$$\frac{d\mu_2}{d\mu_1}\bigg|_{[n,n+1]}=\frac{d\mu_2/d\mu}{d\mu_1/d\mu}\bigg|_{[
n,n+1]}=\frac{1/x^2}{\,x/n^3\,}=\frac{n^3}{x^3}.$$

Thus
$$\boxed{\frac{d\mu_2}{d\mu_1}(x)=\frac{n^3}{x^3}\quad\text{for }x\in[n,n+1],\ n
=1,2,3,\dots}$$

(On $[0,1)$ both $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$ vanish, so the derivative is irrelevant the
re; the formula above covers the support $[1,\infty)$ of $\mu_2$.)

---

## Summary

- $\mu\ll\mu_2$: **No** ($\mu_2$ vanishes on $[0,1)$ while $\mu$ does not).
- $\mu_2\ll\mu_1$: **Yes**, with $\displaystyle\frac{d\mu_2}{d\mu_1}(x)=\frac{n^
3}{x^3}$ for $x\in[n,n+1]$, $n\ge1$.
