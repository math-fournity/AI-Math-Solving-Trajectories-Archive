## Solution

**Key observation.** The function \(f(z)=e^{-e^{z}}\) is the composition of two
entire functions (\(z\mapsto e^{z}\) and \(w\mapsto e^{-w}\)), hence is entire.
Therefore its Laurent expansion about \(z=0\) has no principal part: it is exact
ly its Taylor series, and the residue (the coefficient of \(z^{-1}\)) is automat
ically \(0\).

It remains to write the expansion explicitly.

---

### Deriving the coefficients

Insert the Taylor series of \(e^{kz}\) into the defining expansion of the expone
ntial:

\[
e^{-e^{z}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\,e^{kz}
        =\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(kz)^{n}
}{n!}.
\]

Absolute convergence of both series (for all \(z\)) justifies exchanging the ord
er of summation:

\[
e^{-e^{z}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\underbrace{\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\inf
ty}\frac{(-1)^{k}k^{n}}{k!}\right)}_{=:\,A_{n}}.
\]

The inner sum \(A_{n}\) is a classical object. Using the exponential generating
function of the Touchard (a.k.a. exponential / Bell) polynomials,

\[
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{k^{n}}{k!}\,x^{k}=e^{x}\,T_{n}(x),
\qquad
T_{n}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}S(n,j)\,x^{j},
\]

where \(S(n,j)\) are the Stirling numbers of the second kind. Setting \(x=-1\) g
ives

\[
A_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}k^{n}}{k!}=e^{-1}\,T_{n}(-1)
     =e^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n}S(n,j)\,(-1)^{j}.
\]

Hence the coefficient of \(z^{n}\) is

\[
\boxed{\;c_{n}=\frac{e^{-1}}{n!}\,T_{n}(-1)
      =\frac{e^{-1}}{n!}\sum_{j=0}^{n}S(n,j)\,(-1)^{j}\;}
\]

and the full expansion is

\[
\boxed{\;e^{-e^{z}}=e^{-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{n}(-1)}{n!}\,z^{n}
      =\frac{1}{e}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\sum_{j=0}^{n}S(n,j)\,(-1)^
{j}.\;}
\]

---

### Verification of the first few terms

Compute \(T_{n}(-1)\) from \(T_{0}=1,\;T_{1}=x,\;T_{2}=x+x^{2},\;T_{3}=x+3x^{2}+
x^{3},\;T_{4}=x+7x^{2}+6x^{3}+x^{4}\):

\[
T_{0}(-1)=1,\quad T_{1}(-1)=-1,\quad T_{2}(-1)=0,\quad T_{3}(-1)=1,\quad T_{4}(-
1)=1.
\]

Thus

\[
e^{-e^{z}}=\frac{1}{e}\left(1-z+0\cdot z^{2}+\frac{z^{3}}{6}+\frac{z^{4}}{24}+\c
dots\right).
\]

Cross-check independently by substituting \(w=e^{z}-1=z+\tfrac{z^{2}}{2}+\tfrac{
z^{3}}{6}+\tfrac{z^{4}}{24}+\cdots\), so that \(e^{-e^{z}}=e^{-1}e^{-w}\), and e
xpanding \(e^{-w}=1-w+\tfrac{w^{2}}{2}-\tfrac{w^{3}}{6}+\cdots\):

- coefficient of \(z\): \(-1\);
- coefficient of \(z^{2}\): \(-\tfrac12+\tfrac12=0\);
- coefficient of \(z^{3}\): \(-\tfrac16+\tfrac12-\tfrac16=\tfrac16\);
- coefficient of \(z^{4}\): \(-\tfrac1{24}+\tfrac7{24}-\tfrac14+\tfrac1{24}=\tfr
ac1{24}\).

These agree with the Touchard-polynomial formula, confirming the expansion. The
constant term \(e^{-1}\) also matches \(f(0)=e^{-e^{0}}=e^{-1}\).

---

### Residue

Since \(f\) is entire, its Laurent series about \(z=0\) contains no negative pow
ers of \(z\). In particular the coefficient of \(z^{-1}\) vanishes:

\[
\boxed{\;\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} e^{-e^{z}}=0.\;}
\]
