## Proof

**Setup.** Let $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ be a smooth function, and let $a
 = (a_1, \dots, a_n)$ be a point with $\nabla f(a) \neq 0$ (so that the level su
rface of $f$ through $a$ is a regular level set and admits a well-defined tangen
t plane). The **tangent plane of $f$ at $a$** is the affine function

$$z(x) = f(a) + \nabla f(a)\cdot(x - a) = f(a) + \sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}
{\partial x_i}(a)(x_i - a_i).$$

**Determining $k$.** We are told $a \in N_k = \{x : z(x) = k\}$. Evaluating,

$$z(a) = f(a) + \nabla f(a)\cdot(a - a) = f(a).$$

Hence $k = z(a) = f(a)$.

**Level surface of $z$ at $k = f(a)$.** This is

$$N_k^{(z)} = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : z(x) = f(a)\} = \{x : f(a) + \nabla f(a)\cd
ot(x-a) = f(a)\} = \{x : \nabla f(a)\cdot(x - a) = 0\}.$$

**Tangent plane of the level surface $\{f = k\}$ at $a$.** The level surface of
$f$ through $a$ is $N_k^{(f)} = \{x : f(x) = f(a)\}$. Since $\nabla f(a) \neq 0$
, this is a regular hypersurface, and its tangent plane at $a$ is the set of vec
tors $v$ such that $\nabla f(a)\cdot v = 0$, translated to pass through $a$:

$$p = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : \nabla f(a)\cdot(x - a) = 0\}.$$

**Comparison.** Both sets are described by the identical equation

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i}(a)(x_i - a_i) = 0.$$

Therefore $N_k^{(z)} = p$.

**Geometric interpretation.** The tangent plane $z(x)$ of $f$ at $a$ is the best
 affine approximation of $f$. Its level set at height $f(a)$ consists of exactly
 those directions in which the *linear part* of $f$ does not change — i.e., the
directions orthogonal to $\nabla f(a)$. The tangent plane of the level surface $
\{f = f(a)\}$ at $a$ is likewise the set of directions orthogonal to $\nabla f(a
)$ (the normal to the level surface). Both constructions extract the same hyperp
lane through $a$ orthogonal to the gradient, so they coincide.

**Conclusion.** The level surface of the tangent plane $z$ at the value $k = f(a
)$ is equal to the tangent plane of the level surface $\{f = k\}$ of $f$ at $a$,
 provided $\nabla f(a) \neq 0$ (which is the necessary and sufficient condition
for both objects to be well-defined as $(n-1)$-dimensional hyperplanes).
