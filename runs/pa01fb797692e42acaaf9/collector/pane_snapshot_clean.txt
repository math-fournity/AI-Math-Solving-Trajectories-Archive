**Claim:** $SO(3)$ has no 2-dimensional Lie subgroups.

**Proof.**

By the Lie subgroup–subalgebra correspondence (Frobenius theorem / Lie's third t
heorem applied to Lie groups), connected Lie subgroups of a Lie group $G$ are in
 bijection with Lie subalgebras of its Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$. Any Lie subgr
oup $H$ of $SO(3)$ has a Lie algebra $\mathfrak{h}$ that is a Lie subalgebra of
$\mathfrak{so}(3)$, with $\dim \mathfrak{h} = \dim H$. Thus it suffices to show
that $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ has **no 2-dimensional Lie subalgebra**.

---

**Setup.** The Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ consists of $3\times 3$ real skew-
symmetric matrices. It is compact and simple, with basis $\{X,Y,Z\}$ satisfying
$$[X,Y]=Z,\quad [Y,Z]=X,\quad [Z,X]=Y.$$
Equivalently, $\mathfrak{so}(3)\cong(\mathbb{R}^3,\times)$ via the cross product
. Its Killing form $B$ is **negative definite** (a standard fact for compact sim
ple Lie algebras), and $B$ is **invariant**: $B([a,b],c)=B(a,[b,c])$.

---

**Key observation.** For any $A\in\mathfrak{so}(3)$, the operator $\operatorname
{ad}(A):\mathfrak{so}(3)\to\mathfrak{so}(3)$ is $B$-skew-symmetric:
$$B(\operatorname{ad}(A)\,x,\, y) = B([A,x],y) = B(A,[x,y]) = -B([x,A],y) = -B(x
,[A,y]) = -B(x,\operatorname{ad}(A)\,y).$$
Since $-B$ is positive definite, in a $(-B)$-orthonormal basis $\operatorname{ad
}(A)$ is represented by a **real skew-symmetric matrix**. Such a matrix has eige
nvalues that are purely imaginary or $0$; in particular, **its only real eigenva
lue is $0$**.

2-dimensional subalgebra with basis $\{A,B\}$. Then $[A,B]\in\mathfrak{h}$, so $
[A,B]=\alpha A+\beta B$ for some $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}$.

athbb{R}^3,\times)$, the bracket is the cross product. Two vectors $A,B\in\mathb

$$[e_1,e_2]=[A,\alpha A+\beta B]=\beta[A,B]=\beta(\alpha A+\beta B)=\beta\, e_2.
