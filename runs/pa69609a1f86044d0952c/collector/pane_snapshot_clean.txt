## Proof

We evaluate $\displaystyle \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\left(\frac{6}{e}\right)^x}
{\Gamma(x+1)}$.

**Stirling's approximation.** As $x \to \infty$,
$$\Gamma(x+1) \sim \sqrt{2\pi x}\left(\frac{x}{e}\right)^x.$$

Substituting,

$$\frac{\left(\frac{6}{e}\right)^x}{\Gamma(x+1)} \sim \frac{\left(\frac{6}{e}\ri
ght)^x}{\sqrt{2\pi x}\left(\frac{x}{e}\right)^x} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi x}} \cdot
 \frac{\left(\frac{6}{e}\right)^x}{\left(\frac{x}{e}\right)^x} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{
2\pi x}} \left(\frac{6}{x}\right)^x.$$

**Analysis of the two factors.**

- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi x}} \to 0$ as $x \to \infty$.

- For $\left(\dfrac{6}{x}\right)^x$: write $\left(\dfrac{6}{x}\right)^x = \exp\!
\bigl(x \ln(6/x)\bigr)$. As $x \to \infty$, $\ln(6/x) \to -\infty$, and since $x
 \to \infty$ while $\ln(6/x) \to -\infty$, the product $x\ln(6/x) \to -\infty$.
Hence $\left(\dfrac{6}{x}\right)^x \to 0$.

More concretely, for $x > 6$ we have $0 < 6/x < 1$, so $\left(\frac{6}{x}\right)
^x \le \left(\frac{6}{x}\right)^{6} \to 0$ (in fact it decays far faster, but th
is already suffices).

**Combining.** Both factors are non-negative and tend to $0$, so their product t
ends to $0$:

$$\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\left(\frac{6}{e}\right)^x}{\Gamma(x+1)} = 0.$$
