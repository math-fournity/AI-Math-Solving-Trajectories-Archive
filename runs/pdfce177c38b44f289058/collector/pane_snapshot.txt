## Proof

**Step 1: Base formula.** I use the well-known result (derived via the Laplace r
epresentation $\frac{1}{x^2+a^2}=\int_0^\infty e^{-(x^2+a^2)t}\,dt$ and Fubini):

$$I(a) := \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-x^2}}{x^2+a^2}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{2a}\,e^{a^2}\o
peratorname{erfc}(a), \qquad a>0.$$

*Derivation sketch:* Substituting the representation and swapping integrals give
s $\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-a^2 t}}{\sqrt{1+t}}\,dt$. With $u
=1+t$ then $v=a^2 u$ then $v=s^2$, this becomes $\frac{\pi}{2a}e^{a^2}\operatorn
ame{erfc}(a)$.

**Step 2: Differentiate to get the squared denominator.** Since

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\frac{1}{x^2+a^2} = \frac{-2a}{(x^2+a^2)^2},$$

differentiating under the integral sign:

$$I'(a) = -2a\int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-x^2}}{(x^2+a^2)^2}\,dx \quad\Longrightarrow
\quad \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-x^2}}{(x^2+a^2)^2}\,dx = -\frac{I'(a)}{2a}.$$

**Step 3: Compute $I'(a)$ from the closed form.** Write $I(a)=\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot
\frac{h(a)}{a}$ with $h(a)=e^{a^2}\operatorname{erfc}(a)$. Using $\operatorname{
erfc}'(a)=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-a^2}$:

$$h'(a)=2a\,e^{a^2}\operatorname{erfc}(a)+e^{a^2}\!\left(-\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}e
^{-a^2}\right)=2a\,h(a)-\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}.$$

By the quotient rule:

$$I'(a)=\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{h'(a)\,a-h(a)}{a^2}=\frac{\pi}{2a^2}\Big[(2a^2-1
)\,h(a)-\tfrac{2a}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Big].$$

**Step 4: Assemble.**

$$\int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-x^2}}{(x^2+a^2)^2}\,dx = -\frac{1}{2a}\cdot\frac{\pi}{
2a^2}\Big[(2a^2-1)\,e^{a^2}\operatorname{erfc}(a)-\tfrac{2a}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Big]$$

$$= -\frac{\pi(2a^2-1)}{4a^3}\,e^{a^2}\operatorname{erfc}(a)+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2
a^2}.$$

**Step 5: Substitute $a=1/\sqrt{2}$ (so $a^2=1/2$).** The crucial observation:

$$2a^2-1 = 2\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}-1 = 0.$$

The entire $\operatorname{erfc}$ term vanishes, leaving

$$\int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-x^2}}{(x^2+1/2)^2}\,dx = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\cdot\frac
{1}{2}} = \sqrt{\pi}.$$
