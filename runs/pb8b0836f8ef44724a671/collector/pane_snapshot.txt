## Proof

We determine all $A \in SU_2$ such that $AXA^{-1} = X$ for every traceless Hermi
tian $2\times 2$ matrix $X$, i.e. $\ker P$.

### Step 1. A convenient basis of $E$

The space $E$ of traceless Hermitian matrices is a $3$-dimensional real vector s
pace. A standard basis is given by the Pauli matrices
$$
\sigma_1 = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\sigma_2 = \begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\ i&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\sigma_3 = \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},
$$
which correspond respectively to $(x_1,x_2,x_3) = (0,1,0), (0,0,1), (1,0,0)$. Ev
ery $X \in E$ is a real linear combination $X = x_1\sigma_3 + x_2\sigma_1 + x_3\
sigma_2$.

### Step 2. Commuting with $E$ forces $A$ to be scalar

Suppose $A \in SU_2$ satisfies $AXA^{-1} = X$ for all $X \in E$, i.e. $A$ commut
es with every element of $E$. In particular $A$ commutes with $\sigma_1, \sigma_
2, \sigma_3$.

Together with the identity $I$, the four matrices $\{I, \sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sig
ma_3\}$ form a basis of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ over $\mathbb{C}$. Hence any $2\times
2$ complex matrix is a complex linear combination of $I$ and the Pauli matrices.
 Since $A$ commutes with each $\sigma_j$ and trivially with $I$, $A$ commutes wi
th every $2\times 2$ complex matrix.

By the standard Schur/center argument, the center of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ consists
exactly of the scalar matrices. Concretely: writing $A = \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d
\end{pmatrix}$, the condition $A\sigma_3 = \sigma_3 A$ gives $b = c = 0$; the co
ndition $A\sigma_1 = \sigma_1 A$ then gives $a = d$. Thus $A = aI$ for some $a \
in \mathbb{C}$.

### Step 3. Impose the $SU_2$ conditions

Since $A \in SU_2$ we require $A \in SL_2(\mathbb{C})$ and $A$ unitary. From $A
= aI$:
- $\det A = a^2 = 1$, so $a = \pm 1$;
- $A^*A = |a|^2 I = I$ is automatic for $a = \pm 1$.

Both $I$ and $-I$ lie in $SU_2$ and both commute with every matrix, so both belo
ng to $\ker P$.

### Step 4. Conclusion

$$
\boxed{\ker P \;=\; \{\, I,\; -I\,\} \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.}
$$

The adjoint representation $P: SU_2 \to SO_3(\mathbb{R})$ is a surjective homomo
rphism with kernel $\{\pm I\}$, exhibiting $SU_2/\{\pm I\} \cong SO_3(\mathbb{R}
)$.
