## Proof

Place the regular heptagon on the unit circle (circumradius $R=1$) with center $
O$ at the origin. Let $\alpha = 360°/7$ be the central angle and $\beta = 180°/7
 = \alpha/2$. Place vertex $D$ at angle $0°$, so:

$$D=(1,0),\quad E \text{ at } \alpha,\quad F \text{ at } 2\alpha,\quad G \text{
at } 3\alpha,\quad \dots$$

The side length is $s = 2\sin\beta$.

**Key coordinates.** Since $3\alpha = 1080°/7 = 180° - 180°/7$:
$$G = (\cos 3\alpha,\; \sin 3\alpha) = (-\cos\beta,\; \sin\beta), \qquad F = (\c
os 2\alpha,\; \sin 2\alpha).$$

**Condition 1** ($HD = DE = s$): $H$ lies on the circle of radius $s$ centered a
t $D$.

**Claim:** $H = D + s(\cos 150°, \sin 150°) = (1 - \sqrt{3}\sin\beta,\; \sin\bet
a)$.

**Verifying Condition 2** ($\angle HGF = 270°/7$):

Using sum-to-product formulas:
$$\vec{GF} = F - G = (\cos 2\alpha - \cos 3\alpha,\; \sin 2\alpha - \sin 3\alpha
).$$

Since $\sin(900°/7) = \sin(180° - 900°/7) = \sin(360°/7) = \sin\alpha$ and $\cos
(900°/7) = -\cos\alpha$:
$$\vec{GF} = 2\sin\beta\,(\sin\alpha,\; \cos\alpha) = s\,(\sin\alpha,\; \cos\alp
ha), \quad |\vec{GF}| = s.$$

Now:
$$\vec{GH} = H - G = (1 + \cos\beta - \sqrt{3}\sin\beta,\; \sin\beta - \sin\beta
) = (1 + \cos\beta - \sqrt{3}\sin\beta,\; 0).$$

Since $H$ and $G$ share the same $y$-coordinate ($\sin\beta$), $\vec{GH}$ is hor
izontal. Therefore:
$$\cos(\angle HGF) = \frac{\vec{GF}\cdot\vec{GH}}{|\vec{GF}||\vec{GH}|} = \frac{
s\sin\alpha\cdot(1+\cos\beta-\sqrt{3}\sin\beta)}{s\cdot(1+\cos\beta-\sqrt{3}\sin
\beta)} = \sin\alpha = \sin\frac{360°}{7}.$$

Since $90° - \frac{270°}{7} = \frac{630°-270°}{7} = \frac{360°}{7} = \alpha$, we
 have $\cos\frac{270°}{7} = \sin\frac{360°}{7} = \sin\alpha$.

Thus $\angle HGF = 270°/7$. ✓

**Computing $\angle DHG$:**

$$\vec{HD} = D - H = (\sqrt{3}\sin\beta,\; -\sin\beta) = \sin\beta\,(\sqrt{3},\;
 -1), \quad |\vec{HD}| = 2\sin\beta = s.\;\checkmark$$

$$\vec{HG} = G - H = (-(1+\cos\beta-\sqrt{3}\sin\beta),\; 0).$$

$$\cos(\angle DHG) = \frac{\vec{HD}\cdot\vec{HG}}{|\vec{HD}||\vec{HG}|} = \frac{
\sqrt{3}\sin\beta\cdot(-(1+\cos\beta-\sqrt{3}\sin\beta))}{2\sin\beta\cdot(1+\cos
\beta-\sqrt{3}\sin\beta)} = \frac{-\sqrt{3}\sin\beta\,(1+\cos\beta-\sqrt{3}\sin\
beta)}{2\sin\beta\,(1+\cos\beta-\sqrt{3}\sin\beta)}.$$

The factor $(1+\cos\beta-\sqrt{3}\sin\beta) \neq 0$ (numerically $\approx 1.150$
) cancels, giving:

$$\cos(\angle DHG) = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}.$$

Therefore $\angle DHG = 150°$.

**Uniqueness.** The second ray from $G$ making angle $270°/7$ with $\vec{GF}$ (a
t angle $77.14°$) yields no real intersection with the circle $|X-D|=s$ (the dis
criminant is negative), so $H$ is the unique interior solution.

$$\boxed{\angle DHG = 150°}$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
