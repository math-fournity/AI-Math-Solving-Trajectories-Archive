## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, such a group exists.

**Construction.** Consider the Klein bottle group
$$G = \langle\, a, b \mid a\,b\,a^{-1} = b^{-1} \,\rangle = \langle\, a, b \mid
a\,b\,a^{-1}\,b = 1 \,\rangle.$$
This is finitely presentable (2 generators, 1 relator). We show (1) $G$ is torsi
on-free, and (2) $G_{\mathrm{ab}} = G/[G,G]$ has torsion.

---

**(1) The abelianization has torsion.**

Abelianizing forces all conjugation relations to become trivial equalities. From
 $a\,b\,a^{-1} = b^{-1}$ we obtain, in the abelianization, $\bar b = -\bar b$, i
.e. $2\bar b = 0$. The generator $\bar a$ is unconstrained. Hence
$$G_{\mathrm{ab}} \cong \mathbb{Z}\langle \bar a \rangle \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2\mat
hbb{Z}\langle \bar b \rangle.$$
The element $\bar b$ has order $2$, so $G_{\mathrm{ab}}$ has torsion.

---

**(2) $G$ is torsion-free.**

The relation $a\,b\,a^{-1} = b^{-1}$ exhibits $G$ as the semidirect product $\ma
thbb{Z} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}$, where $b$ generates the normal subgroup $N \cong \m
athbb{Z}$ and $a$ acts on $N$ by inversion ($n \mapsto -n$). Every element has a
 unique normal form $b^m a^n$ with $m, n \in \mathbb{Z}$, and multiplication is
$$(b^m a^n)(b^{m'} a^{n'}) = b^{\,m + (-1)^n m'}\, a^{\,n + n'}.$$

Iterating this, the $k$-th power ($k \geq 1$) of $b^m a^n$ is
$$(b^m a^n)^k = b^{\,m\,S_k(n)}\, a^{kn}, \qquad S_k(n) = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} (-1)^
{jn} = \begin{cases} k & n \text{ even},\\ 1 & n \text{ odd and } k \text{ odd},
\\ 0 & n \text{ odd and } k \text{ even}.\end{cases}$$

Suppose $(b^m a^n)^k = 1$ for some $k \geq 1$. Then $kn = 0$ and $m\,S_k(n) = 0$
.

- **$n$ even:** $S_k(n) = k$, so $kn = 0 \Rightarrow n = 0$, and then $mk = 0 \R
ightarrow m = 0$. The element was the identity.
- **$n$ odd, $k$ even:** $S_k(n) = 0$, so we need $kn = 0$, giving $n = 0$ — con
tradicting $n$ odd.
- **$n$ odd, $k$ odd:** $S_k(n) = 1$, so we need $kn = 0$ (giving $n = 0$, contr
adiction) **and** $m = 0$.

In every case the only element with a finite power equal to the identity is the
identity itself. Therefore $G$ is torsion-free.

---

**Conclusion.** The Klein bottle group $G = \langle a, b \mid a\,b\,a^{-1}\,b =
1\rangle$ is finitely presentable, torsion-free, and has abelianization $\mathbb
{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ with torsion. Hence such a group exists.
