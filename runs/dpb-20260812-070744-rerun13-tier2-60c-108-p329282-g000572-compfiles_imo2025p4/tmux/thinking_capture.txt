Proof

Notation. For a positive integer $N$ with at least three proper divisors, let
$S(N)$ denote the sum of the three largest proper divisors of $N$. The proper
divisors of $N$ that are $\geq \sqrt{N}$ are exactly $N/d$ for divisors $d$ of
$N$ with $1 < d \leq \sqrt{N}$. Thus the three largest proper divisors are $N/
d_1, N/d_2, N/d_3$ where $d_1 < d_2 < d_3$ are the three smallest divisors of
$N$ greater than $1$, and $$S(N) = N!\left(\tfrac{1}{d_1}+\tfrac{1}{d_2}+
\tfrac{1}{d_3}\right).$$

We write $v_p(N)$ for the $p$-adic valuation of $N$.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 1. Classification of fixed points

$S(N)=N$ iff $\frac{1}{d_1}+\frac{1}{d_2}+\frac{1}{d_3}=1$. With $2\le
d_1<d_2<d_3$ integers, the only solution is $(d_1,d_2,d_3)=(2,3,6)$: since
$d_1\ge 3$ gives $\frac{1}{d_1}+\frac{1}{d_2}+\frac{1}{d_3}\le \frac{1}{3}+
\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{5}<1$, we need $d_1=2$; then $\frac{1}{d_2}+\frac{1}{d_3}
=\frac{1}{2}$ forces $d_2=3, d_3=6$.

So $S(N)=N$ iff the three smallest divisors of $N$ greater than $1$ are $2,3,6$,
which holds iff $6\mid N$, $4\nmid N$, and $5\nmid N$ (equivalently, $v_2(N)=1$,
$v_3(N)\ge 1$, $5\nmid N$). These are the fixed points, and each gives a valid
constant sequence.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 2. If $6\nmid N$ then $6\nmid S(N)$

We prove this by cases.

Case A: $N$ odd. Then all divisors of $N$ are odd, so $N/d_1, N/d_2, N/d_3$ are
all odd, and $S(N)$ is a sum of three odd numbers, hence odd. So $2\nmid S(N)$,
thus $6\nmid S(N)$.

Case B: $2\mid N$, $3\nmid N$. Then $d_1=2$.

  • If $v_2(N)\le 3$: We check that $S(N)$ is odd. Write $S(N)=N/2+N/d_2+N/d_3$.
  When $v_2(N)=1$: $N/2$ is odd, $d_2$ is odd (since $4\nmid N$), so $N/d_2$ and
  $N/d_3$ are even; $S$ is odd. When $v_2(N)=2$: $d_2=4$, $N/4$ is odd, $d_3$ is
  odd (since $8\nmid N$), so $N/2$ and $N/d_3$ are even; $S$ is odd. When
  $v_2(N)=3$: $d_2=4, d_3=8$, $N/8$ is odd, $N/2$ and $N/4$ are even; $S$ is
  odd.
  • If $v_2(N)\ge 4$: Then $d_1=2, d_2=4$, and $d_3=\min(8, p)$ where $p\ge 5$
  is the smallest odd prime factor of $N$ (or $d_3=8$ if no odd prime $<8$
  divides $N$). In either case, $S(N)=N!\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}
  {d_3}\right)$. Since $3\nmid N$ and $3\nmid d_3$ (as $d_3\in{5,7,8}$ and
  $3\nmid N$ forces $3\nmid d_3$), we get $3\nmid S(N)$: indeed
  $S(N)=\frac{N\cdot(2d_3+4+d_3\text{-terms})}{4d_3}$ and since $
  \gcd(3,N)=\gcd(3,4d_3)=1$, we have $3\mid S(N)\iff 3\mid N$, contradiction. So
  $6\nmid S(N)$.

Step 3. If $6\nmid a_1$, the sequence is invalid

By Step 2, if $6\nmid a_n$ then $6\nmid a_{n+1}$. Also, when $6\nmid N$, we have
$\frac{1}{d_1}+\frac{1}{d_2}+\frac{1}{d_3}<1$ (since $d_1\ge 3$ gives sum $<1$,
and $d_1=2, 3\nmid N$ gives $d_2\ge 4, d_3\ge 5$ so sum $\le \frac{1}{2}+

decreasing. Since it never reaches a multiple of $6$, and the smallest integer
with $\ge 3$ proper divisors is $6$, the sequence must eventually reach an
integer with fewer than $3$ proper divisors. Invalid.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  • $v_2$ drops to $1$ with $v_3\ge 1$ and $5\mid S$: enters the $30\mid S,

  • $v_3$ drops to $0$: $3\nmid S$, $6\nmid S$, breaks by Step 3.

So $5\mid N$ always leads to an invalid sequence.

Combining all cases:
