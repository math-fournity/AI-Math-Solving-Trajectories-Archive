**Triangle angles.** For three lines with directions α < β < γ in [0, π), the in
terior angles of the triangle they form are the three "gaps":
- g₁ = β − α,  g₂ = γ − β,  g₃ = π − γ + α

with g₁ + g₂ + g₃ = π. (This follows because the three lines partition the direc
tion circle [0,π) into three arcs, and the interior angles of the triangle equal
 these arcs.)

The triangle is **acute** iff all gaps < π/2, and **obtuse** iff exactly one gap
 > π/2 (right angles are excluded by hypothesis).

**Counting obtuse triangles.** A triple is obtuse iff all three directions lie i
n some open arc of length π/2 on the circle [0,π). Each obtuse triple has a uniq
ue gap exceeding π/2, hence a unique "starting point" — the direction immediatel

Define mᵢ = |{j ≠ i : θⱼ ∈ (θᵢ, θᵢ + π/2) mod π}|. Then:

$$\text{Obtuse} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \binom{m_i}{2}$$

**Key constraint.** Each unordered pair {i,j} contributes exactly 1 to Σmᵢ, sinc
e the angular distance between any two directions is ≠ π/2, so exactly one of th

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i = \binom{n}{2}$$

**Minimizing obtuse triangles.** Since f(x) = x(x−1)/2 is convex, by Jensen's in
equality:

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} \binom{m_i}{2} \geq n \cdot \binom{\bar{m}}{2}, \quad \bar{m} =
