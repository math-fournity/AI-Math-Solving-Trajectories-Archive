## Solution

We want to evaluate
$$I = \int_0^{\infty} \frac{2\sin x\,\cos^2 x}{x\,e^{x\sqrt{3}}}\,dx.$$

**Step 1: Expand $\cos^2 x$.** Using $\cos^2 x = \frac{1+\cos 2x}{2}$,
$$I = \int_0^{\infty} \frac{\sin x\,(1+\cos 2x)}{x}\,e^{-x\sqrt{3}}\,dx = \int_0
^{\infty} \frac{\sin x}{x}\,e^{-x\sqrt{3}}\,dx + \int_0^{\infty} \frac{\sin x\,\
cos 2x}{x}\,e^{-x\sqrt{3}}\,dx.$$

**Step 2: Product-to-sum on the second integral.** Since
$$\sin x\,\cos 2x = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\sin(3x) - \sin x\bigr],$$
the second integral becomes
$$\frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\infty}\frac{\sin 3x}{x}\,e^{-x\sqrt{3}}\,dx - \frac{1}{2}\
int_0^{\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x}\,e^{-x\sqrt{3}}\,dx.$$

Combining with the first integral,
$$I = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x}\,e^{-x\sqrt{3}}\,dx + \frac{1}{
2}\int_0^{\infty}\frac{\sin 3x}{x}\,e^{-x\sqrt{3}}\,dx.$$

**Step 3: Standard identity.** For $a,b>0$,
$$\int_0^{\infty}\frac{\sin(ax)}{x}\,e^{-bx}\,dx = \arctan\!\left(\frac{a}{b}\ri
ght).$$

*Proof of identity:* Differentiate w.r.t. $a$:
$$\frac{d}{da}\int_0^{\infty}\frac{\sin(ax)}{x}\,e^{-bx}\,dx = \int_0^{\infty}\c
os(ax)\,e^{-bx}\,dx = \frac{b}{a^2+b^2}.$$
Integrating from $0$ to $a$ (the integral vanishes at $a=0$):
$$\int_0^{a}\frac{b}{t^2+b^2}\,dt = \arctan\!\left(\frac{a}{b}\right). \qquad\ch
eckmark$$

**Step 4: Apply with $b=\sqrt{3}$.**

- $a=1$: $\;\arctan\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\bigr) = \arctan\!\bigl(\tfrac{\sq
rt{3}}{3}\bigr) = \dfrac{\pi}{6}.$
- $a=3$: $\;\arctan\!\bigl(\tfrac{3}{\sqrt{3}}\bigr) = \arctan(\sqrt{3}) = \dfra
c{\pi}{3}.$

Therefore
$$I = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\pi}{6} + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{\pi}
{12} + \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{\pi}{12} + \frac{2\pi}{12} = \frac{3\pi}{12} = \fra
c{\pi}{4}.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_0^{\infty} \frac{2\sin x\,\cos^2 x}{x\,e^{x\sqrt{3}}}
\,dx = \frac{\pi}{4}}$$
