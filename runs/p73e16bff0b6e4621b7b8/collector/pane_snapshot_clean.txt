## Solution

We evaluate $\int_{\mathcal{C}} \log(z)\,dz$ where $\log$ is the branch with cut
 along the negative real axis, i.e., $\log(z) = \ln|z| + i\arg(z)$ with $\arg(z)
 \in (-\pi, \pi)$, and $\mathcal{C}(t) = e^{it}$, $t \in [-\pi, \pi]$.

**Parametrization.** On the contour, $z = e^{it}$ with $|z|=1$ and $\arg(z) = t$
 for $t \in (-\pi,\pi)$. Hence
$$\log(e^{it}) = \ln 1 + it = it, \qquad dz = ie^{it}\,dt.$$

The integral becomes
$$\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} (it)\,(ie^{it})\,dt = \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} -t\,e^{it}\,dt.$$

**Evaluating by parts.** With $u = t$, $dv = e^{it}dt$:
$$\int t\,e^{it}\,dt = e^{it}(1 - it).$$

Therefore
$$\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} t\,e^{it}\,dt = \Big[e^{it}(1-it)\Big]_{-\pi}^{\pi}.$$

At $t = \pi$: $e^{i\pi}(1 - i\pi) = (-1)(1-i\pi) = -1 + i\pi$.

At $t = -\pi$: $e^{-i\pi}(1 + i\pi) = (-1)(1+i\pi) = -1 - i\pi$.

Subtracting:
$$\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} t\,e^{it}\,dt = (-1+i\pi) - (-1-i\pi) = 2\pi i.$$

**Conclusion.**
$$\int_{\mathcal{C}} \log(z)\,dz = -\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} t\,e^{it}\,dt = -2\pi i.$$

**Remark on convergence.** The contour begins and ends at $z = -1$, which lies o
n the branch cut. Approaching $-1$ from below ($t \to -\pi^+$) gives $\log \to -
i\pi$; approaching from above ($t \to \pi^-$) gives $\log \to i\pi$. The integra
nd $-t\,e^{it}$ is bounded and continuous on $(-\pi,\pi)$, so the integral exist
s as an improper integral and equals $-2\pi i$.

$$\boxed{\int_{\mathcal{C}} \log(z)\,dz = -2\pi i.}$$
