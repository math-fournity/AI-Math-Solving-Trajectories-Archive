## Proof

Let $\{A_i\}_{i \in I}$ be an uncountable collection of pairwise disjoint sets,
where the index set $I$ is uncountable, and $|A_i| = \mathfrak{c} = 2^{\aleph_0}
$ for every $i \in I$. We determine the cardinality of $\bigcup_{i \in I} A_i$.

**Step 1: Cardinality of a disjoint union.** Since the sets $A_i$ are pairwise d
isjoint, the cardinality of the union equals the sum of the cardinalities:

$$\left|\bigcup_{i \in I} A_i\right| = \sum_{i \in I} |A_i| = |I| \cdot \mathfra
k{c}.$$

**Step 2: Cardinal arithmetic for infinite cardinals.** We use the standard theo
rem (assuming the Axiom of Choice) that for any two infinite cardinals $\kappa$
and $\lambda$,

$$\kappa \cdot \lambda = \max(\kappa, \lambda).$$

*Proof of this fact:* Without loss of generality assume $\kappa \leq \lambda$. T
hen:
- $\max(\kappa, \lambda) = \lambda \leq \kappa \cdot \lambda$ (since $\kappa \ge
q 1$, we have $\lambda \leq \kappa \cdot \lambda$).
- $\kappa \cdot \lambda \leq \lambda \cdot \lambda = \lambda$ (using $\kappa \le
q \lambda$ and the fact that $\lambda \cdot \lambda = \lambda$ for infinite $\la
mbda$, which follows from AC via the well-ordering of $\lambda$ and the bijectio
n $\lambda \times \lambda \cong \lambda$).

By Cantor–Bernstein, $\kappa \cdot \lambda = \lambda = \max(\kappa, \lambda)$.

**Step 3: Apply to our situation.** Both $|I|$ and $\mathfrak{c}$ are infinite c
ardinals (indeed both are uncountable: $|I|$ is uncountable by hypothesis, and $
\mathfrak{c} \geq \aleph_1 > \aleph_0$). Therefore:

$$\left|\bigcup_{i \in I} A_i\right| = |I| \cdot \mathfrak{c} = \max(|I|, \mathf
rak{c}).$$

**Conclusion.** The cardinality of the union is $\max(|I|,\, \mathfrak{c})$, whe
re $|I|$ is the cardinality of the (uncountable) index set. In particular:

- If $|I| \leq \mathfrak{c}$ (e.g., $|I| = \aleph_1 \leq \mathfrak{c}$, or $|I|
= \mathfrak{c}$), then the union has cardinality $\mathfrak{c}$.
- If $|I| > \mathfrak{c}$, then the union has cardinality $|I|$.

$$\boxed{\left|\bigcup_{i \in I} A_i\right| = \max(|I|,\, \mathfrak{c})}$$
