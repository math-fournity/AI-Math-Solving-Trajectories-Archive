## Proof

Let $X = \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(3,0)\}$.

**Step 1: Identify $V/W$ with de Rham cohomology.**

A vector field $\mathbf{F} = (P,Q)$ on $X$ is *irrotational* iff
$$\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = 0,$$
which is precisely the condition that the associated 1-form $\omega = P\,dx + Q\
,dy$ is **closed** ($d\omega = 0$). Thus $V = Z^1(X)$, the space of closed 1-for
ms on $X$.

A vector field is *conservative* iff $\mathbf{F} = \nabla f$ for some smooth $f:
X\to\mathbb{R}$, i.e. $\omega = df$ is **exact**. Thus $W = B^1(X)$, the space o
f exact 1-forms.

Therefore
$$V/W = Z^1(X)/B^1(X) = H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X),$$
the first de Rham cohomology of $X$.

**Step 2: Compute $H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)$.**

The space $X = \mathbb{R}^2$ with $k=4$ points removed deformation-retracts onto
 a bouquet (wedge) of $k$ circles. Concretely, one may construct a deformation r
etraction of $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{p_1,\dots,p_4\}$ onto a graph consisting
of 4 loops joined at a common base point (one loop encircling each puncture), wh
ich is homotopy equivalent to $\bigvee_{i=1}^{4} S^1$.

By the de Rham theorem (or directly, since de Rham cohomology is a homotopy inva
riant),
$$H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X) \cong H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}\!\left(\bigvee_{i=1}^{4} S^1\righ
t).$$

For a wedge of $k$ circles, $H^1 \cong \mathbb{R}^k$. Hence
$$H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X) \cong \mathbb{R}^4.$$

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

$$\dim(V/W) = \dim H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X) = 4.$$

A basis is given by the cohomology classes of the forms $\omega_i = \dfrac{-(y-y
_i)\,dx + (x-x_i)\,dy}{(x-x_i)^2+(y-y_i)^2}$ for $p_i = (i,0)$, $i=0,1,2,3$ (the
 angular forms around each puncture), each closed but not exact on $X$, and line
arly independent modulo exact forms.

$$\boxed{4}$$
