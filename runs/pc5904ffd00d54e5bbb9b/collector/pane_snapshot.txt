## Central Slice Theorem (Fourier Slice Theorem)

**Setup and definitions.**

Let $f(x,y)$ be a sufficiently nice function (say $f \in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^
2)$, Schwartz class). The **Radon transform** of $f$ at angle $\theta$ and offse
t $t \in \mathbb{R}$ is

$$
\mathcal{R}f(\theta, t) \;=\; \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
 f(x,y)\,\delta\!\bigl(t - x\cos\theta - y\sin\theta\bigr)\,dx\,dy.
$$

Geometrically, this integrates $f$ over the line $\ell_{\theta,t}:=\{(x,y): x\co
s\theta + y\sin\theta = t\}$, i.e. over the line making angle $\theta$ with the
$x$-axis at signed distance $t$ from the origin.

Define the **1-D Fourier transform of the Radon transform in the $t$ variable**:

$$
\widehat{\mathcal{R}f}\,(\theta,\omega) \;=\; \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \mathcal{R
}f(\theta,t)\,e^{-i\omega t}\,dt.
$$

The **2-D Fourier transform of $f$** is

$$
\widehat{f}(k_x,k_y) \;=\; \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(
x,y)\,e^{-i(k_x x + k_y y)}\,dx\,dy.
$$

**Claim (Central Slice Theorem).**

$$
\boxed{\;\widehat{\mathcal{R}f}\,(\theta,\omega) \;=\; \widehat{f}\bigl(\omega\c
os\theta,\;\omega\sin\theta\bigr)\;}
$$

---

### Proof

Substitute the definition of the Radon transform into the 1-D Fourier transform
in $t$:

$$
\widehat{\mathcal{R}f}\,(\theta,\omega)
= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\left[\iint_{\mathbb{R}^2} f(x,y)\,\delta\!\bigl(t -
x\cos\theta - y\sin\theta\bigr)\,dx\,dy\right] e^{-i\omega t}\,dt.
$$

Assuming $f$ is Schwartz (so Fubini/Tonelli applies), swap the order of integrat
ion — do the $t$-integral innermost:

$$
\widehat{\mathcal{R}f}\,(\theta,\omega)
= \iint_{\mathbb{R}^2} f(x,y)\!\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \delta\!\bigl(t - x
\cos\theta - y\sin\theta\bigr)\,e^{-i\omega t}\,dt\right] dx\,dy.
$$

Evaluate the inner integral using the sifting property of the delta distribution
. The delta centers the integral at $t = x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta$, so

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \delta\!\bigl(t - x\cos\theta - y\sin\theta\bigr)\,e^{-i
\omega t}\,dt
= e^{-i\omega(x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta)}.
$$

Substituting back,

$$
\widehat{\mathcal{R}f}\,(\theta,\omega)
= \iint_{\mathbb{R}^2} f(x,y)\,e^{-i\omega(x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta)}\,dx\,dy.
$$

Distribute the exponent:

$$
\widehat{\mathcal{R}f}\,(\theta,\omega)
= \iint_{\mathbb{R}^2} f(x,y)\,e^{-i\bigl((\omega\cos\theta)\,x \;+\; (\omega\si
n\theta)\,y\bigr)}\,dx\,dy.
$$

The right-hand side is precisely the 2-D Fourier transform of $f$ evaluated at t
he frequency point $(k_x,k_y) = (\omega\cos\theta,\;\omega\sin\theta)$:

$$
\widehat{\mathcal{R}f}\,(\theta,\omega) \;=\; \widehat{f}\bigl(\omega\cos\theta,
\;\omega\sin\theta\bigr).
\qquad\blacksquare
$$

---

### Interpretation

The frequency point $(\omega\cos\theta,\omega\sin\theta)$ traces out, as $\omega
$ ranges over $\mathbb{R}$, the **radial line through the origin at angle $\thet
a$** in the 2-D frequency plane. Hence:

> *The 1-D Fourier transform (in $t$) of each projection $\mathcal{R}f(\theta,\c
dot)$ equals the 2-D Fourier transform of $f$ restricted to the central slice (t
he line through the origin at angle $\theta$) of frequency space.*

This is the foundation of **filtered back-projection** reconstruction: collect p
rojections for all $\theta \in [0,\pi)$, Fourier-transform each in $t$, place th
e result along the corresponding radial line in $\widehat{f}$-space, then invers
e-2-D-Fourier-transform to recover $f$.
