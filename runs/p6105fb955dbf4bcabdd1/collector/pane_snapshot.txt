## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. If $f$ is analytic in the punctured disk $D'=\{z:0<|z-a|<R\}$ an
d $f'$ is bounded on $D'$, then $f$ is bounded on $D'$.

**Key idea.** The punctured disk is connected and has *finite geodesic diameter*
: any two points can be joined by a path inside $D'$ whose length is bounded by
a constant depending only on $R$. A bound on $f'$ then turns into a bound on $f$
 by integrating $f'$ along such paths.

---

**Step 1 – A convenient base point.**
Without loss of generality take $a=0$. Fix the base point $w_0$ with $|w_0|=R/2$
 (any such point lies in $D'$). Let $M=\sup_{z\in D'}|f'(z)|<\infty$.

**Step 2 – Bounding the geodesic distance from $w_0$ to any point.**
Let $z\in D'$ be arbitrary, $0<|z|<R$. Write $u=z/|z|$ (the unit direction of $z
$) and let $p=(R/2)\,u$, which lies on the circle $|\zeta|=R/2$.

Construct a path $\gamma$ from $w_0$ to $z$ in two pieces:

1. **Arc:** travel along $|\zeta|=R/2$ from $w_0$ to $p$. Length $\le \pi\cdot(R
/2)=\pi R/2$.
2. **Radial segment:** travel along the ray $\zeta=t\,u$ for $t$ ranging from $R
/2$ to $|z|$. Every point on this segment has modulus between $|z|$ and $R/2$, h
ence lies strictly between $0$ and $R$, so the segment stays in $D'$. Its length
 is $|R/2-|z||\le R/2$.

The concatenated path $\gamma$ lies entirely in $D'$ and has length
$$L(\gamma)\le \frac{\pi R}{2}+\frac{R}{2}=\frac{(\pi+1)R}{2}.$$

**Step 3 – Integrating $f'$.**
Since $f$ is analytic on $D'$, $f'$ is continuous on $D'$, and $\gamma$ is a pie
cewise-$C^1$ path in $D'$ from $w_0$ to $z$. By the fundamental theorem of conto
ur integration,
$$f(z)-f(w_0)=\int_\gamma f'(\zeta)\,d\zeta.$$
Therefore
$$|f(z)-f(w_0)|\le \int_\gamma |f'(\zeta)|\,|d\zeta|\le M\cdot L(\gamma)\le M\cd
ot\frac{(\pi+1)R}{2}.$$

**Step 4 – Conclusion.**
By the triangle inequality,
$$|f(z)|\le |f(w_0)|+\frac{(\pi+1)R}{2}\,M\qquad\text{for every }z\in D'.$$
The right-hand side is a finite constant independent of $z$. Hence $f$ is bounde
d on the punctured disk. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The argument uses only that $f'$ exists, is continuous (automatic fr
om analyticity), and is bounded, together with the finite geodesic diameter of t
he punctured disk. In particular, the possible singularity at the center is forc
ed to be removable *a posteriori*: since $f$ is bounded near $0$, Riemann's remo
vable-singularity theorem implies $f$ extends analytically through $0$. But boun
dedness itself follows directly from the derivative bound, without first classif
ying the singularity.
