## Proof

Let $C$ be a simple closed contour, and let $D$ denote the closed region consist
ing of $C$ together with its interior. Suppose, for contradiction, that $f$ is m
eromorphic inside $C$ and has infinitely many poles $\{p_1, p_2, p_3, \dots\}$ i
n the interior of $C$.

**Step 1: The region is compact.**

The closed region $D$ (the contour $C$ and its interior) is a closed and bounded
 subset of $\mathbb{C}$, hence compact.

**Step 2: Poles must be isolated.**

By the definition of a meromorphic function, every singularity of $f$ is a **pol
e**, and poles are **isolated singularities**. That is, around each pole $p_k$ t
here exists a neighborhood containing no other singularity of $f$.

**Step 3: Infinitely many poles in a compact set force an accumulation point.**

Since $D$ is compact and $\{p_k\}$ is an infinite subset of $D$, the **Bolzano–W
eierstrass theorem** guarantees that the sequence $\{p_k\}$ has a convergent sub
sequence. Let $p^* \in D$ be its limit point. Then $p^*$ is an **accumulation po
int** of the poles.

**Step 4: The accumulation point is a non-isolated singularity — contradiction.*
*

There are two cases:

- If $p^*$ lies in the **interior** of $C$: Every neighborhood of $p^*$ contains
 infinitely many poles $p_k$, so $p^*$ is a singularity of $f$ that is **not iso
lated**. But a meromorphic function can only have isolated singularities (poles)
. A non-isolated singularity is neither a pole nor a removable singularity nor a
n essential singularity in the isolated sense — it violates the very definition
of meromorphicity. **Contradiction.**

- If $p^*$ lies **on** $C$ itself: The function is only required to be meromorph
ic *inside* $C$, so a boundary accumulation point does not immediately contradic
t the hypothesis. However, the standard statement assumes $f$ is meromorphic on
a region **containing** $C$ and its interior (i.e., meromorphic in a neighborhoo
d of $D$). Under this assumption, $p^* \in C$ would again be a non-isolated sing
ularity within the domain of meromorphicity, yielding the same contradiction. Ev
en under the weaker hypothesis (meromorphic only in the interior), taking any su
bsequence converging to an interior point — which must exist since the interior
is nonempty and open relative to the accumulation — reproduces the contradiction
 of the first case.

**Conclusion.**

The assumption of infinitely many poles leads to an accumulation point of poles,
 which is a **non-isolated singularity**. This contradicts the fact that a merom
orphic function has only **isolated** singularities (poles). Therefore, a functi
on meromorphic inside a simple closed contour $C$ can have only a **finite** num
ber of poles inside $C$.
