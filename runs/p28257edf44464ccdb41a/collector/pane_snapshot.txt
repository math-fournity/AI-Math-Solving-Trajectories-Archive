**Answer: Yes**, such functions are dense in $\mathcal{D}((0,T);V)$ under the $W
$-norm.

---

**Proof.**

Let $u \in \mathcal{D}((0,T);V)$. We construct an explicit approximating sequenc
e of the required form.

**Step 1: Reduction to a separable subspace.**

Since $u$ is continuous with compact support $K \Subset (0,T)$, the image $u(K)$
 is compact in $V$. The closed linear span $V_0 := \overline{\operatorname{span}
}\, u(K)$ is a separable closed subspace of $V$ (a compact subset of a metric sp
ace is separable, and the closed linear span of a separable set in a Hilbert spa
ce is separable). Let $\{e_k\}_{k=1}^\infty$ be a complete orthonormal system in
 $V_0$.

**Step 2: Fourier expansion.**

For each $k$, define the scalar function
$$u_k(t) := \langle u(t), e_k \rangle_V, \qquad t \in (0,T).$$
Since $t \mapsto u(t)$ is $C^\infty$ with compact support in $(0,T)$ and the inn
er product with a fixed vector is a bounded linear functional, each $u_k$ is $C^
\infty$ with compact support in $(0,T)$; thus $u_k \in \mathcal{D}(0,T)$. Moreov
er $u_k'(t) = \langle u'(t), e_k\rangle_V$.

For each $N \geq 1$ define the partial sum
$$u_N(t) := \sum_{k=1}^{N} u_k(t)\, e_k.$$
This is a finite sum of the form $\sum_n \psi_n(t)\, w_n$ with $\psi_n = u_n \in
 \mathcal{D}(0,T)$ and $w_n = e_n \in V$.

**Step 3: Convergence in the $W$-norm.**

For each $t$, by Parseval's identity applied in $V_0$:
$$\|u(t) - u_N(t)\|_V^2 = \sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty} |u_k(t)|^2, \qquad \|u'(t) - u_N
'(t)\|_V^2 = \sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty} |u_k'(t)|^2.$$
All terms are non-negative, so by the monotone convergence theorem:
$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \int_0^T |u_k(t)|^2\,dt = \int_0^T \|u(t)\|_V^2\,dt < \inf
ty,$$
$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \int_0^T |u_k'(t)|^2\,dt = \int_0^T \|u'(t)\|_V^2\,dt < \i
nfty,$$
where finiteness holds because $u$ is smooth with compact support, hence both $u
$ and $u'$ are bounded $V$-valued functions on a bounded interval. Therefore
$$\|u - u_N\|_W^2 = \int_0^T \|u(t)-u_N(t)\|_V^2 + \|u'(t)-u_N'(t)\|_V^2\,dt = \
sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty} \int_0^T \bigl(|u_k(t)|^2 + |u_k'(t)|^2\bigr)\,dt.$$
The right-hand side is the tail of a convergent series (of non-negative terms),
so it tends to $0$ as $N \to \infty$.

Hence $u_N \to u$ in the $W$-norm, and each $u_N$ is of the required separated f
orm. $\blacksquare$
