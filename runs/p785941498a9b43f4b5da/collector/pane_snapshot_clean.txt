Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there exists a strictly inc
reasing sequence $(t_n)$ with $t_0=0$, $t_n\to\infty$, such that $S_n = \sum_{i=
0}^{n-1}\frac{t_{i+1}-t_i}{t_n-t_i}$ has finite supremum.

**Answer: Yes, such a sequence exists.**

## Proof

**Construction.** Take $t_n = c^n - 1$ for a constant $c > 1$. Then $t_0 = 0$, t
he sequence is strictly increasing, and $t_n \to \infty$.

**Computation of $S_n$.** For each $i$:
$$t_{i+1} - t_i = (c^{i+1}-1)-(c^i-1) = c^i(c-1), \qquad t_n - t_i = (c^n-1)-(c^
i-1) = c^n - c^i = c^i(c^{n-i}-1).$$

Therefore
$$S_n = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{c^i(c-1)}{c^i(c^{n-i}-1)} = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{
c-1}{c^{n-i}-1}.$$

Substituting $j = n - i$ (so $j$ ranges from $1$ to $n$):
$$S_n = \sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{c-1}{c^{j}-1}.$$

**Convergence.** Since each term $\frac{c-1}{c^j - 1}$ is positive, $(S_n)$ is i
ncreasing, so $\sup_n S_n = \lim_{n\to\infty} S_n = \sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{c-1
}{c^j-1}$, provided this series converges.

For $j \ge j_0$ where $c^{j_0} \ge 2$, we have $c^j - 1 \ge c^j/2$, hence
$$\frac{c-1}{c^j - 1} \le \frac{2(c-1)}{c^j}.$$

Since $\sum_{j=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{c^j} = \frac{1}{c-1} < \infty$, by compariso
n the series $\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{c-1}{c^j-1}$ converges.

**Explicit bound.** For $j \ge 2$: $c^j - 1 = (c-1)(c^{j-1}+c^{j-2}+\cdots+1) \g
e (c-1)\,c^{j-1}$, so $\frac{c-1}{c^j-1} \le \frac{1}{c^{j-1}}$. Thus
$$S_n \le 1 + \sum_{j=2}^{n}\frac{1}{c^{j-1}} < 1 + \frac{1}{c-1} = \frac{c}{c-1
}.$$

So $\sup_n S_n \le \frac{c}{c-1} < \infty$.

**Concrete example.** With $c=2$, i.e., $t_n = 2^n - 1$, we get $S_n = \sum_{j=1
}^{n}\frac{1}{2^j-1} \le 2$, and in fact $\sup_n S_n = \sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{
1}{2^j-1} \approx 1.6067 < \infty$.

## Conclusion

There exists a strictly increasing sequence $(t_n)_{n\ge 0}$ with $t_0=0$ and $t
_n\to\infty$ such that $\sup_{n\ge 1} S_n < \infty$. The geometric-type sequence
 $t_n = c^n - 1$ (for any $c>1$) provides an explicit example, with $\sup_n S_n
\le \frac{c}{c-1}$.
