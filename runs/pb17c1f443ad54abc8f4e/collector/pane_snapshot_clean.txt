## Proof

**Example.** Let $k = \mathbb{R}$ and let $f: \mathbb{A}^1_k \to \mathbb{A}^1_k$
 be the morphism corresponding to the $k$-algebra homomorphism $f^*: k[t] \to k[
t]$ defined by $f^*(t) = t^2$. Concretely, on $k$-rational points, $f(a) = a^2$.

**Claim.** This $f$ satisfies all three required properties.

**Property 1: $f^*$ is injective.**

The map $f^*: \mathbb{R}[t] \to \mathbb{R}[t]$ sends a polynomial $g(t) = a_0 +
a_1 t + \cdots + a_n t^n$ to $g(t^2) = a_0 + a_1 t^2 + \cdots + a_n t^{2n}$. Sin
ce $t^2$ is transcendental over $\mathbb{R}$ (it is non-constant), the substitut
ion $t \mapsto t^2$ gives an isomorphism $\mathbb{R}[t] \xrightarrow{\sim} \math
bb{R}[t^2]$, and the inclusion $\mathbb{R}[t^2] \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}[t]$ i
s injective. Composing these, $f^*$ is injective. Concretely, if $g(t^2) = 0$, t
hen all coefficients $a_i = 0$ (the even powers $t^{2i}$ are linearly independen
t over $\mathbb{R}$), so $g = 0$.

**Property 2: $f(\mathbb{A}^1_k) \neq \mathbb{A}^1_k$.**

On $k$-rational points, $f(\mathbb{A}^1(\mathbb{R})) = \{a^2 : a \in \mathbb{R}\
} = [0, \infty)$. Since $-1 \notin [0,\infty)$, we have $f(\mathbb{A}^1_k) \neq
\mathbb{A}^1_k$.

**Property 3: $f(\mathbb{A}^1_k)$ is not a closed subset of $\mathbb{A}^1_k$.**

In the Zariski topology on $\mathbb{A}^1(\mathbb{R}) = \mathbb{R}$, the closed s
ets are the zero loci $V(I)$ of ideals $I \subseteq \mathbb{R}[t]$. Since $\math
bb{R}[t]$ is a PID, every ideal is principal: $I = (h(t))$ for some $h \in \math
bb{R}[t]$. If $h \neq 0$, then $V(h) = \{a \in \mathbb{R} : h(a) = 0\}$ is finit
e (a nonzero polynomial has finitely many real roots). If $h = 0$, then $V(0) =
\mathbb{R}$. Thus the Zariski-closed subsets of $\mathbb{A}^1(\mathbb{R})$ are e
xactly the finite sets and $\mathbb{R}$ itself.

Now $f(\mathbb{A}^1(\mathbb{R})) = [0,\infty)$ is infinite (it contains $0, 1, 4
, 9, 16, \ldots$), so if it were Zariski-closed, it would have to equal $\mathbb
{R}$. But $[0,\infty) \neq \mathbb{R}$, so $f(\mathbb{A}^1_k)$ is **not** closed
.

**Remark.** The same argument works for any infinite field $k$ that is not algeb
raically closed: take $f^*(t) = t^2$. Then $f^*$ is injective ($t^2$ non-constan
t), $f(\mathbb{A}^1(k)) = \{a^2 : a \in k\}$ is a proper subset of $k$ (since $k
$ is not algebraically closed, some element is not a square), and it is infinite
 (hence not Zariski-closed, as the only infinite closed subset of $\mathbb{A}^1(
k)$ is $\mathbb{A}^1(k)$ itself). For instance, $k = \mathbb{Q}$ with $f(t) = t^
2$ also works: $\{a^2 : a \in \mathbb{Q}\}$ is an infinite proper subset of $\ma
thbb{Q}$, hence not Zariski-closed.
