**Answer: No.** Finitely generated submodules of a $p$-adic module need not be d
irect summands.

**Counterexample.** Let $R = \mathbb{Z}_p$ and take $M = \mathbb{Q}_p$, the frac
tion field of $\mathbb{Z}_p$, regarded as an $R$-module. The submodule $N = \mat
hbb{Z}_p \subset \mathbb{Q}_p$ is finitely generated (by the single element $1$)
. I claim $N$ is **not** a direct summand of $M$.

**Proof.** Suppose for contradiction that $\mathbb{Q}_p = \mathbb{Z}_p \oplus L$
 as $\mathbb{Z}_p$-modules for some submodule $L$. Then $L \cong \mathbb{Q}_p /
\mathbb{Z}_p$.

*Step 1: $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is torsion-free over $\mathbb{Z}_p$.* Since $\mathbb{Q}_
p$ is a field containing $\mathbb{Z}_p$, if $r \in \mathbb{Z}_p \setminus \{0\}$
 and $x \in \mathbb{Q}_p$ with $rx = 0$, then (viewing $r$ as a nonzero element
of the field $\mathbb{Q}_p$) we get $x = 0$.

*Step 2: A direct summand of a torsion-free module is torsion-free.* If $r \in R
 \setminus \{0\}$ and $\ell \in L$ with $r\ell = 0$, write $\ell = (0, \ell)$ in
 the decomposition $M = \mathbb{Z}_p \oplus L$; then $r\ell = (0, r\ell) = 0$ in
 $M$, so $r\ell = 0$ in the torsion-free module $M$, giving $\ell = 0$. Hence $L
$ is torsion-free.

*Step 3: $\mathbb{Q}_p / \mathbb{Z}_p$ is a torsion module.* Every $x \in \mathb
b{Q}_p$ can be written as $x = p^{-n} a$ for some $a \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ and $n \g
eq 0$ (since $\mathbb{Q}_p = \mathbb{Z}_p[1/p]$). Then $p^n x = a \in \mathbb{Z}
_p$, so $p^n(x + \mathbb{Z}_p) = 0$ in $\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$. Thus every e
lement of $\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$ is annihilated by a power of $p$.

*Step 4: Contradiction.* We have $L \cong \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$. Since $\ma
thbb{Z}_p \subsetneq \mathbb{Q}_p$ (e.g. $p^{-1} \in \mathbb{Q}_p \setminus \mat
hbb{Z}_p$), the quotient $\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$ is nonzero. So $L$ is a **n
onzero torsion** module. But by Step 2, $L$ is torsion-free. A nonzero module ca
nnot be simultaneously torsion and torsion-free: if $\ell \neq 0$ is torsion, th
en $r\ell = 0$ for some $r \neq 0$, contradicting torsion-freeness. Contradictio
n.

Therefore no such splitting $L$ exists, and $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is not a direct summa
nd of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. $\square$

**Remark.** The obstruction is torsion: $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is torsion-free while the
 complementary summand would have to be $\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$, which is to
rsion. Note that $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is a DVR (hence a PID), so over it submodules of
 *free* modules are free and finitely generated modules split nicely in many set
tings — but for *arbitrary* modules the splitting of finitely generated submodul
es fails, as shown.
