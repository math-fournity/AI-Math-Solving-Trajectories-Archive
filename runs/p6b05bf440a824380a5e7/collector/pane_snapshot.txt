## Solution

We solve the inhomogeneous heat equation for a rod with Dirichlet boundary condi
tions.

### Setup

The temperature $u(x,t)$ satisfies

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = a^2 \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + \Phi(t
)\sin\frac{\pi x}{l}, \qquad 0 < x < l,\; t>0,$$

with boundary conditions $u(0,t)=u(l,t)=0$ and initial condition $u(x,0)=f(x)$.

### Eigenfunction expansion

The eigenfunctions of $-\frac{d^2}{dx^2}$ on $[0,l]$ with Dirichlet boundary con
ditions are

$$X_n(x)=\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}, \qquad \lambda_n=\frac{n^2\pi^2}{l^2}, \qquad n=1
,2,\dots$$

We expand $u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}T_n(t)\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}$ and the initial
 data $f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}$, where

$$f_n=\frac{2}{l}\int_0^l f(x)\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}\,dx.$$

Substituting into the PDE and using orthogonality, the source $\Phi(t)\sin\frac{
\pi x}{l}$ contributes only to the $n=1$ mode:

- **For $n=1$:** $\;T_1'(t)+a^2\lambda_1 T_1(t)=\Phi(t)$, $\;T_1(0)=f_1$.
- **For $n\ge 2$:** $\;T_n'(t)+a^2\lambda_n T_n(t)=0$, $\;T_n(0)=f_n$.

### Solving the ODEs

For $n\ge 2$, the solution is immediate:

$$T_n(t)=f_n\,e^{-a^2\lambda_n t}=f_n\,e^{-a^2 n^2\pi^2 t/l^2}.$$

For $n=1$, we solve the first-order linear ODE via the integrating factor $e^{a^
2\lambda_1 t}$:

$$T_1(t)=f_1\,e^{-a^2\lambda_1 t}+\int_0^t e^{-a^2\lambda_1(t-\tau)}\,\Phi(\tau)
\,d\tau.$$

### Final answer

Combining all modes, the temperature of the rod is

$$\boxed{u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_n\,e^{-a^2 n^2\pi^2 t/l^2}\sin\frac{n\pi x}
{l}+\left[\int_0^t e^{-a^2\pi^2(t-\tau)/l^2}\,\Phi(\tau)\,d\tau\right]\sin\frac{
\pi x}{l}}$$

where

$$f_n=\frac{2}{l}\int_0^l f(\xi)\sin\frac{n\pi\xi}{l}\,d\xi.$$

### Interpretation

The solution decomposes into two parts:

1. **Homogeneous part** $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_n\,e^{-a^2 n^2\pi^2 t
/l^2}\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}$: the free decay of the initial temperature distributi
on $f(x)$, with each Fourier mode decaying at its own rate $e^{-a^2 n^2\pi^2 t/l
^2}$.

2. **Particular part** $\displaystyle\left[\int_0^t e^{-a^2\pi^2(t-\tau)/l^2}\Ph
i(\tau)\,d\tau\right]\sin\frac{\pi x}{l}$: the forced response driven by the hea
t source. Since the source has spatial profile $\sin(\pi x/l)$ (the first eigenm
ode), only the first mode is excited. The response is a causal convolution of $\
Phi(\tau)$ with the exponential kernel $e^{-a^2\pi^2(t-\tau)/l^2}$, which is pre
cisely the Duhamel principle: the source at time $\tau$ contributes a decaying r
esponse from $\tau$ to $t$.

One can verify directly: substituting into the PDE, the homogeneous part satisfi
es the heat equation with zero source (giving $f(x)$ at $t=0$ and zero at the bo
undaries), while the particular part satisfies the heat equation with source $\P
hi(t)\sin(\pi x/l)$ (zero initial value, zero boundary values). The sum satisfie
s all conditions of the problem.
