## Answer: No

**Proof.**

Let $k$ be the base field, and let $H = V(f) \subset \mathbb{A}^n$ be a hypersur
face, where $f \in k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ is a nonconstant polynomial and $n > 1$. It
s complement is

$$U = \mathbb{A}^n \setminus V(f) = D(f) = \{p \in \mathbb{A}^n : f(p) \neq 0\},
$$

a distinguished (principal) open set in the Zariski topology.

Recall that an **algebraic variety** (affine algebraic set) in $\mathbb{A}^n$ is
 a subset of the form $V(S) = \{p : g(p)=0 \text{ for all } g \in S\}$ for some
collection of polynomials $S \subseteq k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$. Such a set is Zariski-
**closed** by definition.

We show $U$ is **not** Zariski-closed, hence not an algebraic variety.

**Step 1: $U$ is a nonempty proper open subset.** Since $f$ is nonconstant and $
n > 1$, $V(f)$ is a proper, nonempty closed subset of $\mathbb{A}^n$ (over an al
gebraically closed field, a nonconstant polynomial in $n \geq 1$ variables has z
eros, and $V(f) \neq \mathbb{A}^n$ since $f$ is not identically zero on $\mathbb
{A}^n$). Hence $U = D(f)$ is nonempty and $U \neq \mathbb{A}^n$.

**Step 2: $U$ is dense in $\mathbb{A}^n$.** The closure $\overline{U}$ is a clos
ed set containing $U$. Since $U = D(f)$ is open and nonempty, and $\mathbb{A}^n$
 is irreducible (its coordinate ring $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ is an integral domain),
every nonempty open subset is dense. Thus $\overline{U} = \mathbb{A}^n$.

**Step 3: $U$ is not closed.** If $U$ were closed, then $U = \overline{U} = \mat
hbb{A}^n$ by Step 2. But $U \neq \mathbb{A}^n$ by Step 1 (since $V(f) \neq \empt
yset$). Contradiction.

Therefore $U = \mathbb{A}^n \setminus V(f)$ is not Zariski-closed, and consequen
tly **not an algebraic variety**.

**Concrete illustration.** Take $n=2$ and $f = x$. Then $V(x) = \{x = 0\}$ is th
e $y$-axis, and $U = \{(x,y) : x \neq 0\}$. If $U$ were an algebraic set $V(g_1,
 g_2, \dots)$, every $g_i$ would vanish on all of $U$. But $U$ is Zariski-dense
in $\mathbb{A}^2$, so each $g_i$ vanishes on all of $\mathbb{A}^2$, giving $V(g_
1,\dots) = \mathbb{A}^2 \neq U$. Hence $U$ is not an algebraic set.
