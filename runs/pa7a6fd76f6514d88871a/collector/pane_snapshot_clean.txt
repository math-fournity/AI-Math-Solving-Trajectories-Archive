## Proof

**Setup.** Let $M = D = \{(x,y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 : x^2 + y^2 \le 1\}$, the close
d unit disk, a compact smooth 2-manifold with boundary $\partial M = S^1$. Let $
f(x,y) = x^2 + y^2$.

**Step 1: Critical points and Morse indices.**

The gradient is $\nabla f = (2x, 2y)$, which vanishes only at the origin $p = (0
,0)$. The Hessian is
$$H_f = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix},$$
with eigenvalues $2, 2 > 0$. Thus $p$ is a non-degenerate critical point with **
Morse index** $\lambda(p) = 0$ (a non-degenerate local minimum). There are no ot
her critical points in the interior of $M$.

**Step 2: Boundary behavior.**

On $\partial M = \{x^2 + y^2 = 1\}$, the outward-pointing unit normal is $\nu =
(x, y)$. We have
$$\nabla f\big|_{\partial M} = (2x, 2y) = 2\,\nu,$$
so $\nabla f$ points **strictly outward** along $\partial M$. In particular, $f$
 has no critical points on $\partial M$.

**Step 3: Applicable theorem (Morse homology on a manifold with boundary).**

> **Theorem.** Let $M$ be a compact smooth manifold with boundary, and $f: M \to
 \mathbb{R}$ a Morse function such that (i) $f$ has no critical points on $\part
ial M$, and (ii) $\nabla f$ points outward along $\partial M$. Then the Morse ho
mology $HM_*(f)$ — defined using the negative gradient flow $dx/dt = -\nabla f(x
)$ and counting flow lines that remain in $M$ — is naturally isomorphic to the s
ingular homology $H_*(M; \mathbb{Z})$.

*Justification of the hypotheses.* Since $\nabla f = 2\nu$ on $\partial M$, the
negative gradient $-\nabla f$ points **inward** along $\partial M$. Therefore, f
or the negative gradient flow, the forward flow ($t \to +\infty$) cannot exit $M
$: any trajectory starting in $M$ remains in $M$ for all $t \ge 0$. Consequently
, every unstable manifold $W^u(p)$ (defined via the negative gradient flow) is c
ontained in $M$, and the Morse–Smale–Witten complex is well-defined using only i
nterior critical points. The standard compactification and gluing arguments then
 yield a chain complex whose homology is $H_*(M)$.

**Step 4: The Morse complex.**

The chain groups are
$$C_k = \bigoplus_{\substack{p \in \mathrm{Crit}(f) \\ \lambda(p)=k}} \mathbb{Z}
\langle p \rangle.$$

Since the only critical point is $p = (0,0)$ with $\lambda(p) = 0$:
$$C_0 = \mathbb{Z}\langle p \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}, \qquad C_k = 0 \quad \text
{for all } k \ge 1.$$

All boundary operators $\partial_k : C_k \to C_{k-1}$ are zero (either the domai
n or codomain is trivial). Hence the Morse homology is:
$$HM_k(f) = \ker \partial_k \,/\, \operatorname{im} \partial_{k+1}.$$

- $HM_0 = \ker(\partial_0 : C_0 \to 0) \,/\, \operatorname{im}(\partial_1 : 0 \t
o C_0) = \mathbb{Z} / 0 = \mathbb{Z}.$
- $HM_k = 0$ for all $k \ge 1$.

**Step 5: Consistency check via singular homology.**

The closed disk $D$ is contractible, so
$$H_0(D; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}, \qquad H_k(D; \mathbb{Z}) = 0 \quad (k \ge 1)
.$$

This agrees with the Morse homology computed above, confirming the theorem.
