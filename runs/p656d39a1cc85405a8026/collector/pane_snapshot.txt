# Uniqueness of the Artin-Wedderburn Decomposition

**Theorem.** Let $R$ be a semisimple ring (left Artinian with Jacobson radical z
ero, equivalently a semisimple left $R$-module over itself). Suppose
$$R \cong M_{n_1}(D_1) \times \cdots \times M_{n_k}(D_k) \cong M_{n'_1}(D'_1) \t
imes \cdots \times M_{n'_{k'}}(D'_{k'}),$$
where each $D_i, D'_j$ is a division ring. Then $k = k'$, and after reordering,
$n_i = n'_i$ and $D_i \cong D'_i$ for every $i$.

---

## Proof

### Step 1. Reduction to simple components via the center

The center $Z(R)$ of a finite direct product is the direct product of the center
s:
$$Z(R) \cong Z(M_{n_1}(D_1)) \times \cdots \times Z(M_{n_k}(D_k)).$$

For a matrix ring over a division ring, $Z(M_{n_i}(D_i)) \cong Z(D_i)$, the cent
er of the division ring, which is a field. Hence
$$Z(R) \cong Z(D_1) \times \cdots \times Z(D_k) \cong Z(D'_1) \times \cdots \tim
es Z(D'_{k'}).$$

Each $Z(D_i)$ is a field, and a product of fields decomposes uniquely into its c
onnected components (its primitive idempotents correspond bijectively to the fac
tors). Therefore $k = k'$, and after reordering, $Z(D_i) \cong Z(D'_i)$ as field
s. This pins down the number of simple components and matches them up. **Hencefo
rth we identify the two decompositions component-by-component** (after reorderin
g) and prove $n_i = n'_i$ and $D_i \cong D'_i$ for each $i$.

### Step 2. The simple components are intrinsically determined

The primitive central idempotents $e_1, \dots, e_k \in Z(R)$ are uniquely determ
ined by $R$ (they are the identity elements of the connected components of $Z(R)
$). Each simple component $R_i := e_i R$ is therefore an *intrinsic* two-sided i
deal of $R$, depending only on $R$, not on the chosen decomposition. So both dec
ompositions must pair up the *same* collection of simple components. It suffices
 to prove uniqueness for a single **simple** Artinian ring.

### Step 3. Uniqueness for a simple Artinian ring

Now suppose $R$ is simple (a single component) with
$$R \cong M_n(D) \cong M_{n'}(D'), \qquad D, D' \text{ division rings}.$$

**Claim 1: $D \cong D'$.** Consider $R$ as a left $R$-module. Under $R \cong M_n
(D)$, the natural left module $D^n$ (column vectors) is a simple left $R$-module
, and $R \cong (D^n)^n$ as left $R$-modules, so $D^n$ is the unique simple left
$R$-module up to isomorphism. Its endomorphism ring is
$$\operatorname{End}_R(D^n) \cong D^{\mathrm{op}}.$$
(Endomorphisms of the natural $M_n(D)$-module $D^n$ are right multiplications by
 elements of $D$, giving $D^{\mathrm{op}}$.) Since $D^n$ is determined intrinsic
ally as the unique simple left $R$-module, $\operatorname{End}_R(D^n)$ is an inv
ariant of $R$. The same computation under $R \cong M_{n'}(D')$ gives $\operatorn
ame{End}_R((D')^{n'}) \cong (D')^{\mathrm{op}}$. Therefore
$$D^{\mathrm{op}} \cong (D')^{\mathrm{op}},$$
and taking opposite rings (an involution on the class of division rings) yields
$$D \cong D'.$$

**Claim 2: $n = n'$.** The simple left $R$-module $S = D^n$ has $\operatorname{l
ength}_R(R) = n$ (since $R \cong S^n$). This composition length is an invariant
of $R$ as a left $R$-module. Under the second decomposition, $\operatorname{leng
th}_R(R) = n'$. Hence $n = n'$.

(Equivalently, $\dim_D R = n^2$ and $\dim_{D'} R = (n')^2$; with $D \cong D'$ an
d the same underlying ring, $n^2 = (n')^2$ gives $n = n'$ since $n, n' > 0$.)

### Step 4. Assembling

Combining Steps 1–3: the number of simple components is $k = k'$ (from the cente
r), the components pair up intrinsically (Step 2), and within each paired compon
ent $n_i = n'_i$ and $D_i \cong D'_i$ (Step 3). The pairing is unique up to the
permutation already used in Step 1.

---

**Conclusion.** The Artin–Wedderburn decomposition is unique up to permutation o
f the factors: $k = k'$, and after reindexing, $n_i = n'_i$ and $D_i \cong D'_i$
 for all $i$. $\blacksquare$
