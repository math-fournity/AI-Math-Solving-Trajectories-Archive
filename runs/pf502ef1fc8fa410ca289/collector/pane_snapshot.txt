## Proof

**Setup and convention.** We work in $S'(\mathbb{R})$. The Fourier transform and
 its conjugate are defined by
$$F\phi(\xi) = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \phi(x)\,e^{-ix\xi}\,dx, \qquad \overline{F}\ph
i(\xi) = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \phi(x)\,e^{ix\xi}\,dx,$$
so that the inversion formula reads $\phi = \frac{1}{2\pi}\,\overline{F}(F\phi)
= \frac{1}{2\pi}\,F(\overline{F}\phi)$, i.e.,
$$F\,\overline{F} = \overline{F}\,F = 2\pi\,\mathrm{Id} \quad \text{on } S'(\mat
hbb{R}). \tag{1}$$

$$Ff = F\bigl(\overline{F}\,\delta_a\bigr) = (F\,\overline{F})\,\delta_a = 2\pi\
