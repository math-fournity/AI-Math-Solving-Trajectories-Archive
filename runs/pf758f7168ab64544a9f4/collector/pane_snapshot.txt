$$f(t) \geq \tfrac{1}{2}\, t^{1/2}\, f(t^{1/2}) + \tfrac{1}{2}\, t^{1/2}.$$

Now substitute $x = t^{1/4}$ to bound $f(t^{1/2})$, then $x = t^{1/8}$ to bound
$f(t^{1/4})$, and so on. After $m$ iterations, by induction:

$$f(t) \;\geq\; \frac{1}{2^m}\, t^{1-1/2^m}\, f\!\bigl(t^{1/2^m}\bigr) \;+\; \su
m_{j=1}^{m} \frac{1}{2^j}\, t^{1-1/2^j}.$$

**Verification of the pattern:** For $m=1$ this is exactly the first substitutio
n. Assuming it holds for $m$, replace $f(t^{1/2^m})$ using $x = t^{1/2^{m+1}}$:
$$f(t^{1/2^m}) \geq \tfrac{1}{2}\, t^{1/2^{m+1}}\, f(t^{1/2^{m+1}}) + \tfrac{1}{
2}\, t^{1/2^{m+1}}.$$
Substituting back, the remainder term becomes $\frac{1}{2^{m+1}} t^{1-1/2^{m+1}}
 f(t^{1/2^{m+1}})$ and a new summand $\frac{1}{2^{m+1}} t^{1-1/2^{m+1}}$ appears
, confirming the induction.

**Step 2: Take $m \to \infty$.**

For any fixed $t > 0$: $t^{1/2^m} \to 1$, so $f(t^{1/2^m}) \to f(1)$, which is a

the remainder term vanishes, giving:

$$f(t) \;\geq\; \sum_{j=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^j}\, t^{1-1/2^j}, \qquad \forall\
, t > 0.$$

**Step 3: Apply Jensen's inequality to extract a power lower bound.**

The weights $w_j = 1/2^j$ satisfy $\sum w_j = 1$. The function $g(s) = t^s$ is *
*convex** in $s$ for every $t > 0$ (since $\frac{d^2}{ds^2} t^s = t^s (\ln t)^2
\geq 0$). By Jensen's inequality:

$$\sum_{j=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^j}\, t^{1-1/2^j} \;\geq\; t^{\,\bar{s}}, \quad
\text{where } \bar{s} = \sum_{j=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^j}\!\left(1 - \frac{1}{2^
j}\right).$$

Compute the weighted average exponent:

$$\bar{s} = \sum_{j=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^j} - \sum_{j=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{4^j
} = 1 - \frac{1/4}{1-1/4} = 1 - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{3}.$$

Therefore:

$$\boxed{f(t) \geq t^{2/3} \quad \text{for all } t > 0.}$$

**Step 4: Show $2/3$ is optimal (no larger exponent works).**

Set $t = e^{\varepsilon}$ for small $\varepsilon$. Expand each term:

$$t^{1-1/2^j} = e^{\varepsilon(1-1/2^j)} = 1 + \varepsilon\!\left(1-\tfrac{1}{2^
j}\right) + \tfrac{\varepsilon^2}{2}\!\left(1-\tfrac{1}{2^j}\right)^2 + \cdots$$

Summing with weights $1/2^j$:

$$\sum_{j=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^j}\, t^{1-1/2^j} = 1 + \frac{2}{3}\,\varepsilon
 + O(\varepsilon^2).$$

Meanwhile $t^{\alpha} = e^{\alpha\varepsilon} = 1 + \alpha\,\varepsilon + O(\var
epsilon^2)$. For the inequality $\sum \frac{1}{2^j} t^{1-1/2^j} \geq t^{\alpha}$
 to hold for **all** $t > 0$ (i.e., both $\varepsilon > 0$ and $\varepsilon < 0$

alpha \leq \frac{2}{3}$, confirming that $t^{2/3}$ is the tightest power-functio
n lower bound.

**Step 5: Apply to $f(x^{15})$.**

Setting $t = x^{15}$:

$$f(x^{15}) \geq (x^{15})^{2/3} = x^{10}.$$

Since $2/3$ is the optimal exponent, $k = 15 \cdot \frac{2}{3} = 10$ is the larg
est value guaranteed.

$$\boxed{k = 10}$$
