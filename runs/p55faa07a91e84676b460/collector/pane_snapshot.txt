## Proof

Let $\alpha = 1+\sqrt{2}$ and $\beta = 1-\sqrt{2}$. Then

$$\alpha+\beta = 2,\qquad \alpha\beta = -1,$$

and both numbers satisfy $x^2 = 2x+1$ (since $(1\pm\sqrt2)^2 = 3\pm 2\sqrt2 = 2(
1\pm\sqrt2)+1$).

**Define the conjugate sum.** Let

$$S_n = \alpha^n + \beta^n, \qquad n\ge 0.$$

Because $\alpha^2=2\alpha+1$ and $\beta^2=2\beta+1$, multiplying by $\alpha^{n-2
}$, $\beta^{n-2}$ and adding yields the recurrence

$$S_n = 2S_{n-1}+S_{n-2}\quad(n\ge 2),\qquad S_0=2,\; S_1=2.$$

Since the recurrence has integer coefficients and integer initial values, **$S_n
\in\mathbb Z$ for every $n\ge 0$**.

**Size of $\beta^n$.** We have $|\beta| = \sqrt2-1 \approx 0.414\in(0,1)$, so

$$0<|\beta|^n<1\quad\text{for all }n\ge 1,\qquad \beta^n\to 0.$$

**Sign of $\beta^{2021}$.** Since $\beta = 1-\sqrt2<0$ and $2021$ is odd, $\beta
^{2021}<0$.

**Relating $\alpha^{2021}$ to $S_{2021}$.** From $S_{2021}=\alpha^{2021}+\beta^{
2021}$ and the negativity of $\beta^{2021}$,

$$\alpha^{2021} = S_{2021}-\beta^{2021}=S_{2021}+|\beta|^{2021}.$$

Because $0<|\beta|^{2021}<1$,

$$S_{2021} < \alpha^{2021} < S_{2021}+1.$$

**Conclusion.** $S_{2021}$ is an integer strictly less than $\alpha^{2021}$ and
strictly greater than $\alpha^{2021}-1$, so

$$\boxed{\bigl\lfloor (1+\sqrt2)^{2021}\bigr\rfloor = S_{2021} = (1+\sqrt2)^{202
1}+(1-\sqrt2)^{2021},}$$

the integer determined by $S_0=2,\;S_1=2,\;S_n=2S_{n-1}+S_{n-2}$.
