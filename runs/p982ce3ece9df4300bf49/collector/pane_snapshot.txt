## Solution

We evaluate
$$I(a)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x}{x^{2}+1}\sin(ax)\,dx.$$

**Step 1. Reduce to a complex exponential integral.**

Note $\frac{x}{x^{2}+1}\cos(ax)$ is odd (odd × even), hence integrates to $0$ ov
er $\mathbb{R}$, while $\frac{x}{x^{2}+1}\sin(ax)$ is even (odd × odd). Therefor
e, writing $e^{iax}=\cos(ax)+i\sin(ax)$,

$$J(a):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x}{x^{2}+1}e^{iax}\,dx=\int_{-\infty}^{\inf
ty}\frac{x}{x^{2}+1}\cos(ax)\,dx+i\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x}{x^{2}+1}\sin(a
x)\,dx=0+i\,I(a),$$

so
$$I(a)=-i\,J(a). \tag{1}$$

**Step 2. Evaluate $J(a)$ by residues (case $a>0$).**

Consider $f(z)=\dfrac{z}{z^{2}+1}e^{iaz}=\dfrac{z}{(z-i)(z+i)}e^{iaz}$ and the c
losed contour $\Gamma_{R}$ = real segment $[-R,R]$ + upper semicircle $C_{R}$ ($
|z|=R$, $\operatorname{Im}z\ge 0$). For $a>0$, $e^{iaz}=e^{iax-ay}$ decays in th
e upper half-plane. The only pole enclosed is $z=i$ (simple). Its residue:

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=i}f(z)=\lim_{z\to i}(z-i)\frac{z\,e^{iaz}}{(z-i)(z+i)}=\
frac{i}{2i}e^{ia\cdot i}=\frac{1}{2}e^{-a}.$$

By the residue theorem,
$$\oint_{\Gamma_{R}}f(z)\,dz=2\pi i\cdot\frac{1}{2}e^{-a}=\pi i\,e^{-a}.$$

On $C_{R}$, $|z/(z^{2}+1)|=O(1/R)\to 0$ and $a>0$, so by **Jordan's lemma** $\di
splaystyle\int_{C_{R}}f(z)\,dz\to 0$ as $R\to\infty$. Hence

$$J(a)=\pi i\,e^{-a},\qquad a>0.$$

Using (1):
$$I(a)=-i\cdot\pi i\,e^{-a}=\pi e^{-a},\qquad a>0. \tag{2}$$

**Step 3. Case $a<0$.**

Write $a=-|a|$. Then $\sin(ax)=-\sin(|a|x)$, so $I(a)=-I(|a|)=-\pi e^{-|a|}$ by
(2). Equivalently, closing the contour in the **lower** half-plane (clockwise) e
ncloses the pole $z=-i$ with residue $\frac{1}{2}e^{a}$, giving $J(a)=-\pi i\,e^
{a}$ and $I(a)=-i(-\pi i\,e^{a})=-\pi e^{a}=-\pi e^{-|a|}$, consistent.

**Step 4. Case $a=0$.**

$I(0)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x}{x^{2}+1}\cdot 0\,dx=0$.

**Combining all cases:**

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x}{x^{2}+1}\sin(ax)\,dx=\pi\,
\operatorname{sgn}(a)\,e^{-|a|}},\qquad a\in\mathbb{R},$$

with the value $0$ at $a=0$ (consistent with $\operatorname{sgn}(0)=0$).
