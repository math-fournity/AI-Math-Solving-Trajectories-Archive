## Proof

### Setup and notation

Let $\sigma^n$ be the standard $n$-simplex with vertices $v_0, \dots, v_n$, and
$\partial\sigma^n$ its boundary. We are given a continuous, fixed-point-free map
 $f: \sigma^n \to \sigma^n$, i.e., $f(x) \neq x$ for every $x \in \sigma^n$.

### Construction of the retraction $r: \sigma^n \to \partial\sigma^n$

For each $x \in \sigma^n$, consider the **ray emanating from $f(x)$ and passing
through $x$**:
$$\rho_x(t) = f(x) + t\bigl(x - f(x)\bigr), \qquad t \ge 0.$$
This ray is well-defined because $f(x) \neq x$, so the direction $x - f(x)$ is n
onzero. Note $\rho_x(0) = f(x) \in \sigma^n$ and $\rho_x(1) = x \in \sigma^n$.

Since $\sigma^n$ is **compact and convex**, the intersection of the ray with $\s
igma^n$ is a closed interval $[0, T(x)]$ for some $T(x) \ge 1$ (the bound $T(x)
\ge 1$ holds because both endpoints $f(x)$ at $t=0$ and $x$ at $t=1$ lie in $\si
gma^n$, and convexity forces the entire segment between them to lie in $\sigma^n
$). The point $\rho_x(T(x))$ lies on $\partial\sigma^n$.

**Define:**
$$\boxed{r(x) = \rho_x\bigl(T(x)\bigr) = f(x) + T(x)\bigl(x - f(x)\bigr).}$$

Geometrically, $r(x)$ is the unique point where the ray from $f(x)$ through $x$
exits $\sigma^n$.

### $r$ is well-defined and continuous

Express $\sigma^n$ via barycentric coordinates: $\sigma^n = \bigl\{\sum_{i=0}^n
\lambda_i v_i : \lambda_i \ge 0,\ \sum \lambda_i = 1\bigr\}$. Each barycentric c
oordinate $\lambda_i$ is an affine (hence continuous) function. For a point $\rh
o_x(t)$, the $i$-th barycentric coordinate is
$$\lambda_i\bigl(\rho_x(t)\bigr) = \lambda_i\bigl(f(x)\bigr) + t\bigl[\lambda_i(
x) - \lambda_i(f(x))\bigr],$$
which is affine in $t$. The ray exits $\sigma^n$ through the facet $\lambda_i =
0$ at the parameter value
$$t_i(x) = \frac{\lambda_i(f(x))}{\lambda_i(f(x)) - \lambda_i(x)},$$
whenever the denominator is nonzero and $t_i(x) \ge 1$. Then
$$T(x) = \min\bigl\{ t_i(x) : t_i(x) \ge 1 \bigr\},$$
which is a minimum of finitely many continuous functions, hence **continuous**.
Since $f$ is continuous and $T$ is continuous, $r(x) = f(x) + T(x)(x - f(x))$ is
 continuous.

### $r$ is a retraction: $r\big|_{\partial\sigma^n} = \mathrm{id}$

Let $x \in \partial\sigma^n$. Since $\sigma^n$ is a compact convex set, there ex
ists a **supporting hyperplane** $H$ at $x$: a hyperplane passing through $x$ su
ch that $\sigma^n$ lies entirely in one closed half-space determined by $H$. The
 point $f(x) \in \sigma^n$ lies in this closed half-space.

For $t > 1$, the point $\rho_x(t) = x + (t-1)(x - f(x))$ lies on the **opposite
side** of $H$ from $f(x)$ (it moves beyond $x$ in the direction away from $f(x)$
, crossing $H$). Hence $\rho_x(t) \notin \sigma^n$ for all $t > 1$, which forces
 $T(x) = 1$ and therefore
$$r(x) = \rho_x(1) = x.$$
Thus $r\big|_{\partial\sigma^n} = \mathrm{id}_{\partial\sigma^n}$, confirming $r
$ is a retraction. $\checkmark$

### Proof of the equivalence

We now establish **Brouwer Fixed Point Theorem $\iff$ No Retraction Theorem**.

**($\Leftarrow$) No Retraction Theorem $\Rightarrow$ Brouwer Fixed Point Theorem
.**

Suppose, for contradiction, that Brouwer's theorem fails: there exists a continu
ous, fixed-point-free map $f: \sigma^n \to \sigma^n$. By the construction above,
 $f$ yields a continuous retraction $r: \sigma^n \to \partial\sigma^n$. This con
tradicts the No Retraction Theorem (which states no such retraction exists). Hen
ce every continuous self-map of $\sigma^n$ has a fixed point. $\checkmark$

**($\Rightarrow$) Brouwer Fixed Point Theorem $\Rightarrow$ No Retraction Theore
m.**

Suppose, for contradiction, that a retraction $r: \sigma^n \to \partial\sigma^n$
 exists. Consider the inclusion $i: \partial\sigma^n \hookrightarrow \sigma^n$.
The composition
$$\partial\sigma^n \xrightarrow{\;i\;} \sigma^n \xrightarrow{\;r\;} \partial\sig
ma^n$$
equals $\mathrm{id}_{\partial\sigma^n}$ (by the retraction property). Passing to
 $(n{-1})$-th homology:
$$\mathrm{id}_{H_{n-1}(\partial\sigma^n)} = r_* \circ i_*.$$
Since $\partial\sigma^n \cong S^{n-1}$, we have $H_{n-1}(\partial\sigma^n) \cong
 \mathbb{Z}$. Since $\sigma^n$ is contractible, $H_{n-1}(\sigma^n) = 0$, so the
map $i_*: H_{n-1}(\partial\sigma^n) \to H_{n-1}(\sigma^n)$ is the zero map. Ther
efore $r_* \circ i_* = 0 \neq \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{Z}}$, a contradiction. Hence
no retraction exists. $\checkmark$

(Alternatively, one can argue: if a retraction $r$ existed, then $r: \sigma^n \t
o \sigma^n$ is a continuous self-map, so by Brouwer it has a fixed point $x_0 =
r(x_0) \in \partial\sigma^n$. But one then constructs a fixed-point-free map on
$\sigma^n$ by a radial deformation using $r$, contradicting Brouwer. The homolog
y argument is the standard clean route.)

### Conclusion

Given any fixed-point-free continuous map $f: \sigma^n \to \sigma^n$, the ray co
nstruction $r(x) = f(x) + T(x)(x - f(x))$ produces a continuous retraction $\sig
ma^n \to \partial\sigma^n$. The existence of such a retraction is equivalent (vi
a the two implications above) to the failure of Brouwer's theorem. Therefore:

- **Brouwer Fixed Point Theorem holds $\iff$ No Retraction Theorem holds**, and
the two are equivalent. $\blacksquare$
