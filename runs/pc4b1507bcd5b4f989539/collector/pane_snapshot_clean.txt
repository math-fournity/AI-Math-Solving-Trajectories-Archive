## Proof

The answer is **yes**: such a $\lambda$ exists.

**Setup.** Define the continuous function
$$
g(x) \;=\; \sum_{1\le i<j\le n}(x_i-x_j)^2,
$$
which measures the squared deviation of $x$ from the diagonal $\{(\lambda,\dots,
\lambda):\lambda\in\mathbb R\}$. Since $X$ is nonempty and compact and $g$ is co
ntinuous, $g$ attains its minimum at some point $x^*\in X$.

**Key claim.** If $x^*$ is not on the diagonal, we can strictly decrease $g$ whi
le staying in $X$, contradicting minimality.

Suppose $x^*$ is not on the diagonal. Then there exist indices $i<j$ with $x_i^*
\neq x_j^*$. By hypothesis the point
$$
y \;=\; T_{ij}(x^*) \;=\; \bigl(x_1^*,\dots,x_{i-1}^*,\,\tfrac{x_i^*+x_j^*}{2},\
,x_{i+1}^*,\dots,x_{j-1}^*,\,\tfrac{x_i^*+x_j^*}{2},\,x_{j+1}^*,\dots,x_n^*\bigr
)
$$
belongs to $X$. We compare $g(y)$ with $g(x^*)$.

Let $s=\frac{x_i^*+x_j^*}{2}$. The contribution of the pair $(i,j)$ changes from
 $(x_i^*-x_j^*)^2>0$ to $0$, a strict decrease. For any $k\notin\{i,j\}$, the co
mbined contribution of pairs $(i,k)$ and $(j,k)$ changes from
$$
(x_i^*-x_k^*)^2+(x_j^*-x_k^*)^2
$$
to
$$
2(s-x_k^*)^2.
$$
Writing $a=x_i^*-x_k^*$ and $b=x_j^*-x_k^*$, we have $s-x_k^*=\frac{a+b}{2}$, an
d
$$
a^2+b^2 - 2\Bigl(\frac{a+b}{2}\Bigr)^2 \;=\; \frac{(a-b)^2}{2}\;\ge\;0,
$$
so the combined contribution of pairs involving $k$ does not increase. All pairs
 not involving $i$ or $j$ are unchanged.

Therefore
$$
g(y) \;<\; g(x^*),
$$
a strict inequality driven solely by the $(i,j)$ term. This contradicts the mini
mality of $x^*$.

**Conclusion.** The minimizer $x^*$ cannot have any two coordinates unequal, so
$x_1^*=\cdots=x_n^*=\lambda$ for some $\lambda\in\mathbb R$, and $x^*=(\lambda,\
dots,\lambda)\in X$. $\blacksquare$
