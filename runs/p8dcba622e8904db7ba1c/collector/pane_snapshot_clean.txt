## Proof

We wish to compute
$$L = \lim_{n\to\infty} n\int_X \log\!\left(1+\frac{f(x)}{n}\right)d\mu.$$

**Step 1: Pointwise limit of the integrand.**

Define $g_n(x) = n\log\!\left(1+\frac{f(x)}{n}\right)$. We claim $g_n(x)\to f(x)
$ pointwise on $X$.

- If $f(x)=0$: $g_n(x)=n\log(1+0)=0=f(x)$ for all $n$.
- If $0<f(x)<\infty$: writing $t=f(x)/n\to 0$,
$$g_n(x)=f(x)\cdot\frac{\log(1+t)}{t}\;\xrightarrow{t\to 0}\; f(x)\cdot 1=f(x),$
$
since $\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\log(1+t)}{t}=1$.
- If $f(x)=\infty$: $g_n(x)=n\log(1+\infty/n)=n\cdot\infty=\infty=f(x)$ for ever
y $n$.

Hence $g_n\to f$ pointwise.

**Step 2: Dominating function.**

For all $t\ge 0$, the concavity of $\log$ (or the standard inequality $\log(1+t)
\le t$) gives
$$\log(1+t)\le t.$$
Applying this with $t=f(x)/n\ge 0$:
$$g_n(x)=n\log\!\left(1+\frac{f(x)}{n}\right)\le n\cdot\frac{f(x)}{n}=f(x).$$

Thus $0\le g_n(x)\le f(x)$ for all $x$ and all $n$. Since $f\ge 0$ and $\int_X f
\,d\mu=1<\infty$, the function $f$ is integrable and serves as an $L^1$-dominati
ng function.

**Step 3: Apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem.**

The sequence $\{g_n\}$ satisfies:
- $g_n\to f$ pointwise (Step 1),
- $|g_n|\le f\in L^1(\mu)$ (Step 2).

By the Dominated Convergence Theorem,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_X g_n\,d\mu=\int_X f\,d\mu=1.$$

That is,
$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} n\int_X \log\!\left(1+\frac{f(x)}{n}\right)d\mu = 1.}
$$
