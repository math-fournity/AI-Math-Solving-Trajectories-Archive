## Proof

Let $H$ be a Hilbert space with orthonormal basis $(e_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}$, and l
et $F$ be a bounded linear operator whose matrix entries in this basis are
$$
\langle e_k, F e_n\rangle = \phi(n,k), \qquad n,k\in\mathbb N .
$$

### 1. General estimate (Schur's test)

For any sequence of **positive weights** $(p_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}$, $p_n>0$, defin
e the two quantities
$$
\alpha \;=\; \sup_{n\ge 1}\;\frac{1}{p_n}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigl|\phi(n,k)\bigr
|\,p_k,
\qquad
\beta \;=\; \sup_{k\ge 1}\;\frac{1}{p_k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl|\phi(n,k)\bigr|
\,p_n .
$$

**Schur's Test.** *If $\alpha,\beta<\infty$, then $F$ is bounded on $\ell^2(\mat
hbb N)$ and*
$$
\boxed{\;\lVert F\rVert \;\le\; \sqrt{\alpha\,\beta}\;}
\qquad\text{(Schur estimate).}
$$

*Proof of the test.* For $x=(x_n)\in\ell^2$,
$$
\lVert Fx\rVert^2 \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Bigl|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\phi(n,k)\,x
_n\Bigr|^{2}
\;\le\; \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\phi(n,k)|\,|x_n|\Bigr)^{2}
.
$$
By Cauchy–Schwarz with the weights $p_n$,
$$
\Bigl(\sum_{n}|\phi(n,k)|\,|x_n|\Bigr)^{2}
= \Bigl(\sum_{n}\bigl(|\phi(n,k)|\,p_n\bigr)\,\frac{|x_n|}{p_n}\Bigr)^{2}
\le \Bigl(\sum_{n}|\phi(n,k)|\,p_n\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{n}|\phi(n,k)|\,\frac{|x_n|^{
2}}{p_n}\Bigr).
$$
The first factor is $\le\beta\,p_k$ by definition of $\beta$. Hence
$$
\lVert Fx\rVert^{2}\;\le\;\beta\sum_{k}p_k\sum_{n}|\phi(n,k)|\,\frac{|x_n|^{2}}{
p_n}
\;=\;\beta\sum_{n}\frac{|x_n|^{2}}{p_n}\sum_{k}|\phi(n,k)|\,p_k
\;\le\;\beta\sum_{n}\frac{|x_n|^{2}}{p_n}\,\alpha\,p_n
\;=\;\alpha\beta\,\lVert x\rVert^{2}.
$$
Taking square roots gives $\lVert F\rVert\le\sqrt{\alpha\beta}$. $\square$

**Specialisation $p_n\equiv 1$.** When all row and column sums of $|\phi|$ are f
inite, Schur's test with $p_n\equiv 1$ yields the simple bound
$$
\lVert F\rVert \;\le\; \sqrt{\Bigl(\sup_{n}\sum_{k}|\phi(n,k)|\Bigr)\Bigl(\sup_{
k}\sum_{n}|\phi(n,k)|\Bigr)} .
$$

**Hilbert–Schmidt bound.** Independently, when $\sum_{n,k}|\phi(n,k)|^{2}<\infty
$,
$$
\lVert F\rVert \;\le\; \lVert F\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}} \;=\; \Bigl(\sum_{n,k}|\phi(
n,k)|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}.
$$

### 2. Application: $\phi(n,k)=\dfrac{1}{n+k}$, $\ n,k\ge 1$

The matrix $a_{kn}=\phi(n,k)=1/(n+k)$ is the **Hilbert matrix** (shifted). With
the trivial weights $p_n\equiv 1$ the column sums $\sum_{k\ge 1}1/(n+k)$ diverge
 (harmonic tail), so we use **weighted Schur test** with
$$
p_n \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}, \qquad n\ge 1.
$$

Because the matrix is symmetric ($\phi(n,k)=\phi(k,n)$), the row and column cond
itions coincide, so $\alpha=\beta$ and it suffices to bound
$$
\alpha \;=\; \sup_{n\ge 1}\; \sqrt{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+k)\sqrt{k}}
.
$$

**Estimating the sum.** For fixed $n\ge 1$ set $f(x)=\dfrac{1}{(n+x)\sqrt{x}}$ o
n $(0,\infty)$. Since
$$
f'(x)=-\frac{1}{(n+x)^{2}\sqrt{x}}-\frac{1}{2(n+x)x^{3/2}}<0,
$$
$f$ is strictly decreasing. Therefore, for every $k\ge 1$,
$$
f(k)\;\le\;\int_{k-1}^{k}f(x)\,dx,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}f(k)\;\le\;\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\,dx .
$$

Evaluate the integral with the substitution $u=\sqrt{x}$ ($x=u^{2},\;dx=2u\,du$)
:
$$
\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{(n+x)\sqrt{x}}
\;=\;\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{2u\,du}{(n+u^{2})\,u}
\;=\;2\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{du}{n+u^{2}}
\;=\;\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl[\arctan\frac{u}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr]_{0}^{\infty}
\;=\;\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}
\;=\;\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{n}} .
$$

Hence
$$
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+k)\sqrt{k}}\;\le\;\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{n}},
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
\sqrt{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+k)\sqrt{k}}\;\le\;\pi
\quad\text{for all }n\ge 1.
$$

So $\alpha\le\pi$, and by symmetry $\beta\le\pi$. Schur's test gives
$$
\boxed{\;\lVert F\rVert \;\le\; \sqrt{\pi\cdot\pi} \;=\; \pi\;}.
$$

**Remark (sharpness).** The bound $\pi$ is in fact **exact**: the norm of the Hi
lbert matrix equals $\pi$. This can be seen by testing $F$ against the approxima
te eigenvectors $x^{(N)}_n=n^{-1/2}\mathbf 1_{1\le n\le N}$ and using the integr
al asymptotics above, which shows $\lVert F\rVert\ge\pi$ in the limit. Combined
with the upper bound, $\lVert F\rVert=\pi$.

---

**Summary.** The general Schur estimate is
$$
\lVert F\rVert\le\sqrt{\alpha\beta},\quad
\alpha=\sup_n\frac{1}{p_n}\sum_k|\phi(n,k)|p_k,\quad
\beta=\sup_k\frac{1}{p_k}\sum_n|\phi(n,k)|p_n,
$$
for any positive weights $(p_n)$. Applied to $\phi(n,k)=1/(n+k)$ with weights $p
_n=1/\sqrt{n}$ and the integral evaluation $\int_0^\infty\frac{dx}{(n+x)\sqrt{x}
}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{n}}$, this yields
$$
\lVert F\rVert\le\pi,
$$
which is sharp.
