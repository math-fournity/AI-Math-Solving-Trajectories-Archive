## Proof

Consider the transformation $\sigma: (x, y, z) \mapsto (y, x, z)$, which swaps $
x$ and $y$ while fixing $z$.

**Step 1: The region $N$ is invariant under $\sigma$.**

The region is defined by three conditions:
- $1 \le x^2 + y^2 - z^2 \le 9$: Under $\sigma$, this becomes $y^2 + x^2 - z^2$,
 which is identical. ✓
- $2 \le xz + yz \le 4$: Since $xz + yz = z(x+y)$, under $\sigma$ this becomes $
yz + xz = z(y+x)$, identical. ✓
- $x, y, z \ge 0$: Under $\sigma$ this becomes $y, x, z \ge 0$, identical. ✓

Therefore $\sigma(N) = N$; the region is symmetric under the swap $x \leftrighta
rrow y$.

**Step 2: The integrand is antisymmetric under $\sigma$.**

$$f(x,y,z) = x^3 - y^3 \implies f(\sigma(x,y,z)) = f(y,x,z) = y^3 - x^3 = -(x^3
- y^3) = -f(x,y,z).$$

**Step 3: The integral vanishes.**

The Jacobian of $\sigma$ is $|\det D\sigma| = 1$ (it is a permutation matrix). S
ince $\sigma$ is a smooth bijection of $N$ onto itself,

$$I = \iiint_N (x^3 - y^3)\,dV = \iiint_N f(\sigma(x,y,z))\,|\det D\sigma|\,dV =
 \iiint_N (-f(x,y,z))\,dV = -I.$$

Hence $2I = 0$, so

$$\boxed{I = 0}.$$
