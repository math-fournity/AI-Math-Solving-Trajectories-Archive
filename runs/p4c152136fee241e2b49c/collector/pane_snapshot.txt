## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle I = \int_\gamma \frac{\sinh(3z)}{z^4}\,dz$, where $\g
amma$ is the positively oriented circle $|z|=2$.

**Step 1: Identify the singularity.** The integrand $f(z) = \dfrac{\sinh(3z)}{z^
4}$ is meromorphic. The only singularity is at $z=0$, which lies inside $\gamma$
 (since $|0|=0<2$). Since $\sinh(3z)$ has a simple zero at $z=0$ and the denomin
ator is $z^4$, the integrand has a pole of order $3$ at $z=0$.

**Step 2: Laurent expansion.** Using the Taylor series
$$\sinh(3z) = 3z + \frac{(3z)^3}{3!} + \frac{(3z)^5}{5!} + \cdots = 3z + \frac{2
7z^3}{6} + \frac{243z^5}{120} + \cdots = 3z + \frac{9z^3}{2} + \frac{81z^5}{40}
+ \cdots,$$
we divide by $z^4$:
$$\frac{\sinh(3z)}{z^4} = \frac{3}{z^3} + \frac{9}{2z} + \frac{81z}{40} + \cdots
.$$

**Step 3: Extract the residue.** The residue of $f$ at $z=0$ is the coefficient
of the $z^{-1}$ term:
$$\operatorname{Res}(f, 0) = \frac{9}{2}.$$

**Step 4: Apply the Residue Theorem.** Since $z=0$ is the only singularity enclo
sed by $\gamma$,
$$I = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}(f,0) = 2\pi i \cdot \frac{9}{2} = 9\pi i.$
$

**Verification via Cauchy's integral formula for derivatives.** Equivalently, si
nce
$$\frac{\sinh(3z)}{z^4} = \frac{1}{z^4}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(3z)^{2n+1}}{(2n
+1)!},$$
the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ corresponds to the term with $2n+1 = 3$, i.e. $n=1$,
 giving $\dfrac{3^3}{3!} = \dfrac{27}{6} = \dfrac{9}{2}$. This confirms the resi
due.

Alternatively, by Cauchy's differentiation formula,
$$\oint_{|z|=2} \frac{\sinh(3z)}{z^4}\,dz = \frac{2\pi i}{3!}\cdot \left.\frac{d
^3}{dz^3}\sinh(3z)\right|_{z=0}.$$
Since $\frac{d^3}{dz^3}\sinh(3z) = 27\cosh(3z)$ and $\cosh(0)=1$,
$$I = \frac{2\pi i}{6}\cdot 27 = \frac{54\pi i}{6} = 9\pi i.$$

Both methods agree.

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \int_\gamma \frac{\sinh(3z)}{z^4}\,dz = 9\pi i}$$
