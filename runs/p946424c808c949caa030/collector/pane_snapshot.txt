## Proof

We evaluate
$$I = \int_{\gamma} \frac{z^5}{z^6-1}\,dz,$$
where $\gamma$ is the ellipse $x^2+4y^2=16$, i.e. $\dfrac{x^2}{16}+\dfrac{y^2}{4
}=1$, traversed once counterclockwise (standard positive orientation).

**Step 1: Locate the singularities.**

The integrand $f(z)=\dfrac{z^5}{z^6-1}$ has poles exactly at the six roots of $z
^6=1$:
$$z_k = e^{2\pi i k/6}, \qquad k=0,1,2,3,4,5,$$
namely $z_k\in\{1,\,e^{i\pi/3},\,e^{2i\pi/3},\,-1,\,e^{4i\pi/3},\,e^{5i\pi/3}\}$
. Each satisfies $|z_k|=1$.

**Step 2: Verify all poles lie inside $\gamma$.**

The ellipse is $\dfrac{x^2}{16}+\dfrac{y^2}{4}=1$, with semi-major axis $4$ (alo
ng $x$) and semi-minor axis $2$ (along $y$). For any point on the unit circle, $
x^2+y^2=1$, we have
$$\frac{x^2}{16}+\frac{y^2}{4}\le \frac{x^2}{4}+\frac{y^2}{4}=\frac{x^2+y^2}{4}=
\frac{1}{4}<1.$$
Hence the entire unit circle lies strictly inside the ellipse, so **all six pole
s $z_k$ are enclosed by $\gamma$**.

**Step 3: Compute the residue at each pole.**

Each $z_k$ is a simple zero of $z^6-1$ (since $(z^6-1)'=6z^5\neq 0$ at $z_k\neq
0$). Thus $z_k$ is a simple pole of $f$. The residue is
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,z_k)=\lim_{z\to z_k}(z-z_k)\frac{z^5}{z^6-1}=\frac{z_k^5}
{(z^6-1)'\big|_{z=z_k}}=\frac{z_k^5}{6z_k^5}=\frac{1}{6}.$$

**Step 4: Apply the Residue Theorem.**

All six poles lie inside $\gamma$, so
$$I = 2\pi i \sum_{k=0}^{5}\operatorname{Res}(f,z_k)=2\pi i\cdot 6\cdot\frac{1}{
6}=2\pi i.$$

**Sanity check via the logarithmic-derivative observation.** Since $\dfrac{z^5}{
z^6-1}=\dfrac{1}{6}\dfrac{(z^6-1)'}{z^6-1}=\dfrac{1}{6}\dfrac{d}{dz}\log(z^6-1)$
, the integral equals $\dfrac{1}{6}\cdot 2\pi i\cdot(\text{number of zeros of }z
^6-1\text{ inside }\gamma)=\dfrac{1}{6}\cdot 2\pi i\cdot 6=2\pi i$, confirming t
he result.

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}\frac{z^5}{z^6-1}\,dz = 2\pi i.}$$
