## Proof

**Setup.** Let $B = \{1, 2, \ldots, 6\}$ be vertices of a convex hexagon in cycl
ic order. A broken line is a non-self-intersecting Hamiltonian path on $B$. I co
unt geometric broken lines (undirected paths), so a sequence and its reverse are
 the same broken line.

**Key Lemma.** *In any non-crossing Hamiltonian path on $n \geq 3$ points in con
vex position, the edge incident to each endpoint is a boundary edge of the conve
x hull.*

*Proof of Lemma.* Let the path be $v_1, v_2, \ldots, v_n$ with $v_1$ an endpoint
. Suppose for contradiction that $v_1v_2$ is a diagonal. This diagonal partition
s the remaining $n - 2$ vertices into two non-empty sets $A$ and $B$ on opposite
 sides of the chord $v_1v_2$. The edge $v_2v_3$ shares vertex $v_2$ with $v_1v_2
$, so it does not cross $v_1v_2$ regardless of which side $v_3$ is on. However,
for every $i \geq 3$, the edge $v_iv_{i+1}$ shares no endpoint with $v_1v_2$ (si
nce $v_1$ appears only at position 1 and $v_2$ only at position 2). Thus $v_iv_{
i+1}$ must not cross $v_1v_2$, forcing $v_i$ and $v_{i+1}$ onto the same side. S
ince $v_3$ is on one side, all of $v_3, \ldots, v_n$ must lie on that same side.
 But the path must visit vertices in both $A$ and $B$—contradiction. By symmetry
 (reversing the path), the last edge is also a boundary edge. $\square$

**Recursion.** Let $g(n)$ denote the number of *directed* (ordered) non-crossing
 Hamiltonian paths on $n$ convex points, and let $g(n, s)$ denote the count of t
hose starting at a specific vertex $s$. By the rotational symmetry of convex pos
ition, $g(n, s) = g(n)/n$ for every $s$.

By the Lemma, the first edge $v_1v_2$ must be a boundary edge. Each vertex has e
xactly 2 boundary neighbors, so there are 2 choices for $v_2$. After fixing $v_1
v_2$, the remaining $n-1$ points form a convex $(n{-}1)$-gon, and $v_2, v_3, \ld
ots, v_n$ is a directed non-crossing Hamiltonian path on these $n-1$ points star
ting at $v_2$. By symmetry, the number of such paths is $g(n-1)/(n-1)$. Therefor
e:

$$\frac{g(n)}{n} = 2 \cdot \frac{g(n-1)}{n-1}, \qquad \text{i.e.,} \quad g(n) =
\frac{2n}{n-1}\, g(n-1).$$

**Base case.** $g(2) = 2$ (the two directed paths $1 \to 2$ and $2 \to 1$).

**Solving the recursion.**

$$g(n) = \frac{2n}{n-1} \cdot g(n-1) = \frac{2n}{n-1} \cdot \frac{2(n-1)}{n-2} \
cdot g(n-2) = \cdots = 2^{n-2} \cdot \frac{n!}{2!\cdot(n-2)!}\cdot \frac{g(2)}{2
} \cdot 2$$

More cleanly, by induction: $g(n) = n \cdot 2^{n-2}$.

*Verification:* $g(2) = 2 \cdot 2^0 = 2$ ✓. $g(3) = 3 \cdot 2^1 = 6 = 3!$ ✓ (all
 permutations of 3 points give non-crossing paths). $g(4) = 4 \cdot 2^2 = 16$ ✓
(verified: $24 - 8 = 16$, where 8 is the number of permutations containing both
diagonals). $g(5) = 5 \cdot 2^3 = 40$ ✓ (verified by explicit enumeration). $g(6
) = 6 \cdot 2^4 = 96$.

**Undirected count.** Each geometric broken line corresponds to exactly 2 direct
ed sequences (forward and reverse), so:

$$T(B) = f(n) = \frac{g(n)}{2} = n \cdot 2^{n-3}.$$

**Verification of the formula:**

- $f(4) = 4 \cdot 2 = 8$ ✓ (4 boundary paths + 2 paths using each diagonal = 8)
- $f(5) = 5 \cdot 4 = 20$ ✓

**Final evaluation for $n = 6$:**

$$T(B) = 6 \cdot 2^{6-3} = 6 \cdot 2^3 = 6 \cdot 8 = \boxed{48}.$$
