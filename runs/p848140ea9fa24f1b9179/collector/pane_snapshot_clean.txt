## Solution

We determine all semidirect products $C_p \rtimes_\phi C_p$ of the cyclic group
$C_p$ (of prime order $p$) by $C_p$.

### Setup

A semidirect product $G = N \rtimes_\phi H$ is determined (up to isomorphism of
the construction) by a homomorphism
$$\phi: H \to \operatorname{Aut}(N).$$
Here $N \cong C_p$ and $H \cong C_p$, so we must classify all homomorphisms
$$\phi: C_p \to \operatorname{Aut}(C_p).$$

### Step 1: The automorphism group of $C_p$

Let $C_p = \langle a \rangle$ with $a^p = e$. An automorphism of $C_p$ is determ
ined by the image of the generator $a$, which must be another generator. The gen
erators of $C_p$ are exactly $a, a^2, \dots, a^{p-1}$, i.e. the elements $a^k$ w
ith $\gcd(k,p)=1$. Since $p$ is prime, every $k \in \{1,\dots,p-1\}$ is coprime
to $p$, giving $|\operatorname{Aut}(C_p)| = p-1$.

Concretely, each $k \in (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^\times$ defines $\sigma_k \in \
operatorname{Aut}(C_p)$ by $\sigma_k(a) = a^k$, and the map $k \mapsto \sigma_k$
 is a group isomorphism
$$\operatorname{Aut}(C_p) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^\times \cong C_{p-1},$$
which is cyclic of order $p-1$.

### Step 2: Classifying homomorphisms $\phi: C_p \to \operatorname{Aut}(C_p)$

A homomorphism from $C_p = \langle b \rangle$ (with $b^p = e$) into $\operatorna
me{Aut}(C_p) \cong C_{p-1}$ is determined by the image $\phi(b)$, which must be
an element of $\operatorname{Aut}(C_p)$ whose order divides $p$ (since $\phi(b)^
p = \phi(b^p) = \phi(e) = \mathrm{id}$).

The order of $\phi(b)$ divides both:
- $p$ (because $\phi(b)^p = \mathrm{id}$), and
- $p - 1$ (because $\phi(b) \in \operatorname{Aut}(C_p) \cong C_{p-1}$, whose el
ements all have order dividing $p-1$).

Since $\gcd(p,\, p-1) = 1$, the only possibility is
$$\operatorname{ord}(\phi(b)) = 1, \quad\text{i.e.}\quad \phi(b) = \mathrm{id}.$
$

Therefore the **only** homomorphism $\phi: C_p \to \operatorname{Aut}(C_p)$ is t
he **trivial** homomorphism $\phi \equiv \mathrm{id}$.

### Step 3: The resulting group

When $\phi$ is trivial, the semidirect product construction reduces to the direc
t product:
$$C_p \rtimes_{\mathrm{id}} C_p \;=\; C_p \times C_p.$$

Since $p$ is prime, $C_p \times C_p$ is **not** cyclic (its order is $p^2$ but e
very non-identity element has order $p$), so
$$C_p \rtimes C_p \;\cong\; C_p \times C_p \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} \tim
es \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z},$$
the elementary abelian group of order $p^2$.

### Conclusion

There is exactly **one** semidirect product of $C_p$ by $C_p$ (up to isomorphism
), namely the direct product. The reason is that $\operatorname{Aut}(C_p) \cong
C_{p-1}$ has order $p-1$, which is coprime to $p = |C_p|$, forcing every homomor
phism $C_p \to \operatorname{Aut}(C_p)$ to be trivial. Hence the only semidirect
 product is
$$\boxed{C_p \rtimes C_p \;\cong\; C_p \times C_p \;\cong\; (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb
{Z})^2,}$$
the elementary abelian group of order $p^2$. In particular, no non-trivial (non-
abelian) semidirect product exists in this case.
