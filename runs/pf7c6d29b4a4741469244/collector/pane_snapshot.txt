## Solution

**Yes.** The group $H^4(PSL(2,\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{Z})$ is known, and in fact the
 entire integral cohomology ring of $PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})$ is classically computabl
e. We show

$$H^4(PSL(2,\mathbb{Z}),\,\mathbb{Z}) \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}.$$

### Step 1. Structure of $PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})$

The modular group admits the classical free-product decomposition

$$PSL(2,\mathbb{Z}) \;\cong\; C_2 * C_3,$$

generated by the order‑2 element $S:z\mapsto -1/z$ and the order‑3 element $T:z\
mapsto -1/(z+1)$ (equivalently, the standard presentation $\langle S,T \mid S^2
= (ST)^3 = 1\rangle$). This is a free product of the finite cyclic groups $C_2$
and $C_3$.

### Step 2. Cohomology of a free product

Let $G = A * B$ be a free product. By Bass–Serre theory, $G$ acts on a tree with
 two vertex orbits (with stabilizers conjugate to $A$ and $B$) and one edge orbi
t with trivial stabilizer. The associated Mayer–Vietoris (equivariant spectral-s
equence) long exact sequence in group cohomology with trivial coefficients $M$ i
s

$$\cdots \to H^n(G,M) \to H^n(A,M)\oplus H^n(B,M) \to H^n(\{1\},M) \to H^{n+1}(G
,M) \to \cdots$$

Since $H^n(\{1\},M)=0$ for all $n\ge 1$, the boundary maps $H^n(\{1\},M)\to H^{n
+1}(G,M)$ vanish and the restriction map becomes an isomorphism for $n\ge 2$:

$$\boxed{H^n(A*B,\,M) \;\cong\; H^n(A,M)\oplus H^n(B,M), \qquad n\ge 2.}$$

(The low‑degree terms $n=0,1$ require separate care because $H^0(\{1\},M)=M$ is
nonzero, but we only need $n=4$.)

Applying this with $A=C_2$, $B=C_3$, $M=\mathbb{Z}$:

$$H^4(PSL(2,\mathbb{Z}),\,\mathbb{Z}) \;\cong\; H^4(C_2,\mathbb{Z})\;\oplus\; H^
4(C_3,\mathbb{Z}).$$

### Step 3. Cohomology of a finite cyclic group

For a cyclic group $C_m$ with trivial $\mathbb{Z}$‑coefficients, the standard pe
riodic resolution of $\mathbb{Z}$ over $\mathbb{Z}[C_m]$ gives the well‑known 2‑
periodic pattern

$$H^n(C_m,\mathbb{Z}) \;=\; \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z}, & n=0,\\[2pt] 0, & n\ \tex
t{odd},\\[2pt] \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}, & n\ge 2\ \text{even}. \end{cases}$$

Hence

$$H^4(C_2,\mathbb{Z}) \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \qquad H^4(C_3,\mathbb{Z
}) \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}.$$

### Step 4. Combine

$$H^4(PSL(2,\mathbb{Z}),\,\mathbb{Z}) \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\;\oplus\;
\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z},$$

the last isomorphism being the Chinese‑remainder theorem (since $\gcd(2,3)=1$).

### Conclusion

The fourth integral cohomology group of $PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})$ is known; it is

$$\boxed{H^4(PSL(2,\mathbb{Z}),\,\mathbb{Z})\;\cong\;\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}.}$$

This follows from the free‑product decomposition $PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})\cong C_2*C_3
$, the Mayer–Vietoris isomorphism for free products in degrees $\ge 2$, and the
2‑periodic cohomology of finite cyclic groups. More generally the same argument
yields $H^{2k}(PSL(2,\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ for all
 $k\ge 1$ and $H^{2k+1}(PSL(2,\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{Z})=0$ for $k\ge 1$ (with $H^1
\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\oplus\mathbb{Z}/3\cong\mathbb{Z}/6$ recoverable from the low‑d
egree part of the sequence), so the full integral cohomology is periodic of peri
od 2 — consistent with $PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})$ being virtually free.
