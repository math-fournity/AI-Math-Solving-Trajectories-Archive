## Proof

**Claim.** Yes, $M$ and $N$ are necessarily equal.

Let $i: N \hookrightarrow M$ denote the inclusion, which is an embedding (hence
an immersion). Since $\dim N = \dim M$, for every $p \in N$ the differential
$$di_p : T_p N \to T_{i(p)} M$$
is an injective linear map between vector spaces of equal dimension, hence an is
omorphism.

By the **Inverse Function Theorem**, $i$ is a local diffeomorphism at every poin
t $p \in N$. Concretely, for each $p \in N$ there exist open neighborhoods $U_p$
 of $p$ in $N$ and $V_p$ of $i(p)$ in $M$ such that $i|_{U_p}: U_p \to V_p$ is a
 diffeomorphism. In particular $V_p \subseteq i(N)$, so every point of $i(N)$ ha
s an open neighborhood (in $M$) contained in $i(N)$. Therefore

$$i(N) \text{ is open in } M. \tag{1}$$

On the other hand, $N$ is a **closed** embedded submanifold, which by definition
 means $i(N)$ is a closed subset of $M$:

$$i(N) \text{ is closed in } M. \tag{2}$$

Since $i$ is an embedding, $i: N \to i(N)$ is a homeomorphism (in fact a diffeom
orphism onto its image), so it suffices to show $i(N) = M$.

From (1) and (2), $i(N)$ is both open and closed in $M$. The manifold $M$ is **c
onnected**, so the only subsets of $M$ that are simultaneously open and closed a
re $\emptyset$ and $M$ itself. Since $N$ is a (non-empty) manifold, $i(N) \neq \
emptyset$. Hence

$$i(N) = M.$$

As $i: N \to i(N) = M$ is a diffeomorphism, $N$ and $M$ are equal as smooth mani
folds (and $N = M$ as subsets of $M$). $\blacksquare$
