Looking at this problem, I need to prove that all coefficients $b_k$ in $e^{P(x)
} = \sum b_k x^k$ are nonzero, given $P(x) = a_1 x + \cdots + a_n x^n$ with inte
ger coefficients and $a_1$ odd.

**Key idea:** Show that $d_k := k! \cdot b_k$ is always odd (a 2-adic unit), whi
ch forces $b_k \neq 0$.

---

**Proof.**

Since $P(0)=0$, we have $b_0 = e^{P(0)} = 1$, so $d_0 = 0! \cdot b_0 = 1$, which
 is odd.

**Deriving the recurrence.** From $B(x) = e^{P(x)}$ we get $B'(x) = P'(x)\,B(x)$
. Writing $B(x) = \sum_{k\ge 0} b_k x^k$ and $P'(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} i\,a_i\, x^
{i-1}$, equating coefficients of $x^{k-1}$ (for $k \ge 1$) gives:

$$k\, b_k = \sum_{i=1}^{\min(n,k)} i\, a_i\, b_{k-i}. \tag{1}$$

**Introducing $d_k = k!\, b_k$.** Substituting $b_k = d_k/k!$ and $b_{k-i} = d_{
k-i}/(k-i)!$ into (1):

$$k \cdot \frac{d_k}{k!} = \sum_{i=1}^{\min(n,k)} i\, a_i\, \frac{d_{k-i}}{(k-i)
!},$$

$$d_k = \sum_{i=1}^{\min(n,k)} \frac{(k-1)!}{(k-i)!}\cdot i \cdot a_i \cdot d_{k
-i}. \tag{2}$$

**Analyzing the coefficients modulo 2.** For each term in (2), the coefficient o
f $a_i\, d_{k-i}$ is:

$$\alpha_{k,i} := \frac{(k-1)!}{(k-i)!}\cdot i = i \cdot (k-1)(k-2)\cdots(k-i+1)
,$$

a product of $i$ and $(i-1)$ consecutive integers.

- **$i = 1$:** $\alpha_{k,1} = 1$, so the term is $a_1\, d_{k-1}$.
- **$i \ge 2$:** We claim $\alpha_{k,i}$ is even.
  - If $i$ is even, then $\alpha_{k,i}$ is even. ✓
  - If $i$ is odd with $i \ge 3$, then $\alpha_{k,i} = i \cdot \prod_{j=k-i+1}^{
k-1} j$ is the product of $i$ (odd) and $i-1 \ge 2$ consecutive integers. Among
any two or more consecutive integers, at least one is even, so the product is ev
en. ✓

Therefore, reducing (2) modulo 2:

$$d_k \equiv a_1\, d_{k-1} \pmod{2}. \tag{3}$$

**Induction.** Since $a_1$ is odd, $a_1 \equiv 1 \pmod{2}$, so (3) gives $d_k \e
quiv d_{k-1} \pmod{2}$.

- **Base case:** $d_0 = 1$ is odd.
- **Inductive step:** If $d_{k-1}$ is odd, then $d_k \equiv d_{k-1} \equiv 1 \pm
od{2}$, so $d_k$ is odd.

By induction, $d_k = k!\, b_k$ is odd for every $k \ge 0$.

**Conclusion.** Since $d_k = k!\, b_k$ is an odd (hence nonzero) integer, we hav
e $b_k = d_k / k! \neq 0$ for all $k \ge 0$. $\blacksquare$
