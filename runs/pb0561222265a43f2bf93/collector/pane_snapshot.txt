## Solution

**Setup.** Let $z = e^{2\pi i/101}$ (primitive 101st root of unity) and $w = e^{
2\pi i/10}$ (primitive 10th root of unity). The product
$$A = \prod_{a=0}^{9}\prod_{b=0}^{100}\prod_{c=0}^{100}(w^a + z^b + z^c)$$
is invariant under the Galois actions $z \mapsto z^k\ (\gcd(k,101)=1)$ and $w \m
apsto w^j\ (\gcd(j,10)=1)$, hence $A \in \mathbb{Z}$. We compute $A \pmod{101}$.

**Step 1: Collapse the $c$-product.** For any $\beta$, since $-z^c$ ($c=0,\dots,
100$) are exactly the roots of $t^{101}+1$ (as $(-z^c)^{101}=-1$ and they are di
stinct):
$$\prod_{c=0}^{100}(\beta + z^c) = \beta^{101} + 1.$$
Setting $\beta = w^a + z^b$:
$$Q_{a,b} := \prod_{c=0}^{100}(w^a + z^b + z^c) = (w^a + z^b)^{101} + 1.$$

**Step 2: Reduce mod 101.** Expand:
$$(w^a + z^b)^{101} = \sum_{k=0}^{101}\binom{101}{k}w^{a(101-k)}z^{bk} = w^{101a
} + z^{101b} + \sum_{k=1}^{100}\binom{101}{k}w^{a(101-k)}z^{bk}.$$
Since $\binom{101}{k}\equiv 0\pmod{101}$ for $1\le k\le 100$, and $z^{101b}=1$:
$$(w^a + z^b)^{101} + 1 \equiv w^{101a} + 2 \pmod{101},$$
which is **independent of $b$**. (This is justified rigorously by working in $\m
athbb{F}_{101}[z]/((z-1)^{100})$ where $z=1+\epsilon$, $\epsilon^{100}=0$; Fresh
man's dream gives $(w^a+z^b)^{101}=w^{101a}+z^{101b}=w^{101a}+1$ since $z^{101}=
(1+\epsilon)^{101}=1+\epsilon^{101}=1$.)

**Step 3: Collapse the $b$-product.**
$$P_a = \prod_{b=0}^{100}Q_{a,b} \equiv (w^{101a}+2)^{101} \pmod{101}.$$
By Freshman's dream in characteristic 101, $(w^{101a}+2)^{101} \equiv w^{101^2 a
} + 2^{101}$. Since $101\equiv 1\pmod{10}$, $w^{101^2 a}=w^a$; by Fermat, $2^{10
1}\equiv 2\pmod{101}$. Therefore:
$$P_a \equiv w^a + 2 \pmod{101}.$$

**Step 4: Collapse the $a$-product.**
$$A \equiv \prod_{a=0}^{9}(w^a + 2) \pmod{101}.$$
Since $w^0,w^1,\dots,w^9$ are all 10th roots of unity, $\prod_{a=0}^{9}(t-w^a)=t
^{10}-1$. Evaluating at $t=-2$:
$$\prod_{a=0}^{9}(-2-w^a)=(-2)^{10}-1=1023.$$
Since $(-1)^{10}=1$, $\prod_{a=0}^{9}(2+w^a)=1023$.

**Step 5: Final reduction.** $1023 = 101\cdot 10 + 13$, so
$$A \equiv 13 \pmod{101}.$$
