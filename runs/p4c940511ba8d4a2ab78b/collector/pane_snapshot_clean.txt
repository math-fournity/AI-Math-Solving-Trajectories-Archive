## Fundamental Group of a Double Torus with a Point Removed

**Setup.** Let $P$ be a closed orientable surface of genus 2 (the double torus),
 and let $x \in P$. We determine $\pi_1(P \setminus \{x\})$ using the Seifert–va
n Kampen theorem.

**CW model.** The double torus admits the standard CW structure: a single 0-cell
 $v$, four 1-cells $a, b, c, d$ (all loops at $v$), and a single 2-cell attached
 via the word
$$[a,b][c,d] = a\,b\,a^{-1}b^{-1}\,c\,d\,c^{-1}d^{-1}.$$
Equivalently, $P$ is the quotient of a regular octagon whose boundary is labeled
 $a\,b\,a^{-1}b^{-1}\,c\,d\,c^{-1}d^{-1}$, with paired edges identified. Choose
$x$ to be a point in the interior of the 2-cell (the center of the octagon).

**Open cover of $P \setminus \{x\}$.** Define two open subsets of $P \setminus \
{x\}$:

- $U_1$: a small open collar neighborhood of the 1-skeleton (the image of the bo
undary of the octagon after identification), thickened slightly into the interio
r. This deformation retracts onto the 1-skeleton, which is a wedge $\bigvee_{i=1
}^{4} S^1$ of four circles. Hence
$$\pi_1(U_1) \cong F_4 = \langle a, b, c, d \rangle,$$
the free group on four generators.

- $U_2$: the interior of the octagon with the point $x$ removed — an open punctu
red disk. This deformation retracts onto a circle $\gamma$ encircling $x$. Hence
$$\pi_1(U_2) \cong \mathbb{Z} = \langle \gamma \rangle.$$

- $U_1 \cap U_2$: an open annular region between the collar and the puncture. Th
is deformation retracts onto a circle, so
$$\pi_1(U_1 \cap U_2) \cong \mathbb{Z} = \langle \delta \rangle.$$

**Inclusion maps.** We track the images of the generator $\delta$ of $\pi_1(U_1
\cap U_2)$ under the two inclusions:

- The inclusion $U_1 \cap U_2 \hookrightarrow U_2$ sends $\delta \mapsto \gamma$
 (both represent the loop going once around the puncture), an isomorphism.

- The inclusion $U_1 \cap U_2 \hookrightarrow U_1$ sends $\delta$ to the loop tr
acing the boundary of the octagon, read in the 1-skeleton. Traversing the octago
n boundary gives exactly the attaching word of the 2-cell:
$$(\iota_1)_*(\delta) = [a,b][c,d] = a\,b\,a^{-1}b^{-1}\,c\,d\,c^{-1}d^{-1} \in
\pi_1(U_1) = F_4.$$

**Applying van Kampen.** Since $U_1 \cup U_2 = P \setminus \{x\}$ and the inters
ection is path-connected, the Seifert–van Kampen theorem gives
$$\pi_1(P \setminus \{x\}) \cong \pi_1(U_1) *_{\pi_1(U_1 \cap U_2)} \pi_1(U_2) =
 F_4 *_{\mathbb{Z}} \,\mathbb{Z}.$$

Concretely, this is the free product $\langle a,b,c,d\rangle * \langle \gamma\ra
ngle$ modulo the single relation identifying the two images of $\delta$:
$$[a,b][c,d] = \gamma.$$

Thus
$$\pi_1(P \setminus \{x\}) \cong \langle a, b, c, d, \gamma \;\mid\; [a,b][c,d]
= \gamma \rangle.$$

**Eliminating the redundant generator.** The relation $\gamma = [a,b][c,d]$ expr
esses $\gamma$ as a word in $a,b,c,d$; in particular $\gamma$ is not an independ
ent generator. By Tietze transformation (eliminating $\gamma$), we obtain
$$\pi_1(P \setminus \{x\}) \cong \langle a, b, c, d \rangle = F_4.$$

There are **no remaining relations** among $a, b, c, d$.

**Consistency check.** Recovering $P$ from $P \setminus \{x\}$ amounts to fillin
g the puncture, which makes the loop $\gamma$ around $x$ contractible. This impo
ses $\gamma = 1$, i.e. $[a,b][c,d] = 1$, yielding
$$\pi_1(P) \cong \langle a, b, c, d \mid [a,b][c,d] = 1\rangle,$$
the well-known fundamental group of the genus-2 surface. This confirms our compu
tation.

**Geometric interpretation.** Removing a point from the interior of the 2-cell p
unctures the cell, so it deformation retracts onto its boundary. The entire spac
e $P \setminus \{x\}$ therefore deformation retracts onto the 1-skeleton, a wedg
e of four circles — independently confirming $\pi_1(P\setminus\{x\}) \cong F_4$.

**Conclusion.** The fundamental group of the double torus with a point removed i
s the free group on four generators:

$$\boxed{\pi_1(P \setminus \{x\}) \cong F_4 = \langle a, b, c, d \rangle.}$$
