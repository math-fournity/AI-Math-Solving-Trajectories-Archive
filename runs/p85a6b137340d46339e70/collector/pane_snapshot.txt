## Proof

This is the classical theorem that the mapping cone of a quasi-isomorphism is ac
yclic. We prove it via the long exact homology sequence of the short exact seque
nce of chain complexes associated to the cone.

### Step 1. $C_f$ is a chain complex

We verify $\overline{\partial}_{n-1}\overline{\partial}_n=0$. For $(x,y)\in D_n=
C_{n-1}\oplus C_n'$,
$$\overline{\partial}_n(x,y)=\bigl(-\partial_{n-1}x,\;\partial_n' y+f_{n-1}x\big
r).$$
Applying $\overline{\partial}_{n-1}$:
$$\overline{\partial}_{n-1}\bigl(-\partial_{n-1}x,\;\partial_n'y+f_{n-1}x\bigr)=
\Bigl(-\partial_{n-2}(-\partial_{n-1}x),\;\partial_{n-1}'(\partial_n'y+f_{n-1}x)
+f_{n-2}(-\partial_{n-1}x)\Bigr).$$
The first component is $\partial_{n-2}\partial_{n-1}x=0$. The second component i
s
$$\partial_{n-1}'\partial_n' y+\partial_{n-1}'f_{n-1}x-f_{n-2}\partial_{n-1}x=0+
\partial_{n-1}'f_{n-1}x-f_{n-2}\partial_{n-1}x.$$
Since $f$ is a chain map, $\partial_{n-1}'f_{n-1}=f_{n-2}\partial_{n-1}$, so thi
s vanishes. Hence $\overline{\partial}^2=0$.

### Step 2. A short exact sequence of chain complexes

Define chain maps
$$0\longrightarrow C'\xrightarrow{\;i\;} C_f\xrightarrow{\;p\;} C[-1]\longrighta
rrow 0$$
by $i_n(y)=(0,y)$ and $p_n(x,y)=x$, where $C[-1]$ is the shifted complex with $C
[-1]_n=C_{n-1}$ and differential $-\partial_{n-1}$ (the sign ensuring $p$ is a c
hain map: $p_{n-1}\overline{\partial}_n(x,y)=-\partial_{n-1}x$, matching the dif
ferential of $C[-1]$).

These are chain maps, $i$ is injective, $p$ is surjective, and $\ker p=\operator
name{im}i$ (both equal $\{(0,y):y\in C_n'\}$). So the sequence is exact.

### Step 3. The associated long exact homology sequence

The short exact sequence of chain complexes yields the long exact sequence
$$\cdots\longrightarrow H_n(C')\xrightarrow{\;i_*\;} H_n(C_f)\xrightarrow{\;p_*\
;} H_n(C[-1])\xrightarrow{\;\delta\;} H_{n-1}(C')\longrightarrow\cdots$$
Since $H_n(C[-1])=H_{n-1}(C)$, the relevant segment is
$$H_n(C)\xrightarrow{\;f_*\;} H_n(C')\xrightarrow{\;i_*\;} H_n(C_f)\xrightarrow{
\;p_*\;} H_{n-1}(C)\xrightarrow{\;f_*\;} H_{n-1}(C').$$
Here the connecting homomorphism $\delta:H_n(C[-1])\to H_{n-1}(C')$ is identifie
d with $f_*$ as follows.

### Step 4. The connecting homomorphism is $f_*$

Let $[x]\in H_n(C[-1])=H_{n-1}(C)$, so $x\in C_{n-1}$ with $\partial_{n-1}x=0$.
Lift $x$ to $(x,0)\in D_n$ (a lift under $p$). Apply $\overline{\partial}_n$:
$$\overline{\partial}_n(x,0)=\bigl(-\partial_{n-1}x,\;f_{n-1}x\bigr)=(0,\,f_{n-1
}x).$$
This lies in $\operatorname{im}i$; the corresponding element of $C_{n-1}'$ is $f
_{n-1}x$. Since $x$ is a cycle and $f$ is a chain map, $f_{n-1}x$ is a cycle in
$C_{n-1}'$. Thus
$$\delta([x])=[f_{n-1}x]=f_*[x].$$
So $\delta=f_*$.

### Step 5. $f_*$ an isomorphism forces $H_n(C_f)=0$

Assume $f_*:H_k(C)\to H_k(C')$ is an isomorphism for every $k$ (in particular fo
r $k=n$ and $k=n-1$). From the exact segment
$$H_n(C)\xrightarrow{\;f_*\;} H_n(C')\xrightarrow{\;i_*\;} H_n(C_f)\xrightarrow{
\;p_*\;} H_{n-1}(C)\xrightarrow{\;f_*\;} H_{n-1}(C'),$$

- **Exactness at $H_n(C')$:** $\ker i_*=\operatorname{im}f_*$. Since $f_*$ is su
rjective at degree $n$, $\operatorname{im}f_*=H_n(C')$, so $\ker i_*=H_n(C')$, i
.e. $i_*=0$. Hence $\operatorname{im}i_*=0$.

- **Exactness at $H_n(C_f)$:** $\ker p_*=\operatorname{im}i_*=0$, so $p_*$ is in
jective.

- **Exactness at $H_{n-1}(C)$:** $\operatorname{im}p_*=\ker f_*$. Since $f_*$ is
 injective at degree $n-1$, $\ker f_*=0$, so $\operatorname{im}p_*=0$, i.e. $p_*
=0$.

Since $p_*:H_n(C_f)\to H_{n-1}(C)$ is both injective and the zero map, its domai
n must be trivial:
$$H_n(C_f)=0.$$

This holds for every $n$, completing the proof. $\blacksquare$
