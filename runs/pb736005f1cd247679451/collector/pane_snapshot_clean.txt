## Solution

**Residue analysis.** For a pair $(a,b)$ to satisfy $a+b \equiv 0 \pmod 3$, the
residues $(a \bmod 3, b \bmod 3)$ must be either $(0,0)$ or $(1,2)$ (in some ord
er). No other combination works: $0+1\equiv1$, $0+2\equiv2$, $1+1\equiv2$, $2+2\
equiv1$.

Let $c_0, c_1, c_2$ be the counts of numbers in $\{1,2,\dots,n\}$ congruent to $
0,1,2 \pmod 3$ respectively.

**Necessary and sufficient conditions.** Each pair is either a $(0,0)$-pair or a
 $(1,2)$-pair. The $(1,2)$-pairs consume equal numbers from classes $1$ and $2$,
 so after forming as many as possible, any leftover in class $1$ or class $2$ ca
n never be paired (it cannot pair with class $0$, nor with itself). Hence we nee
d $c_1 = c_2$. The $(0,0)$-pairs consume class $0$ two at a time, so we need $c_
0$ even. These conditions are also sufficient: pair the $c_1$ numbers of class $
1$ with the $c_2 = c_1$ numbers of class $2$ arbitrarily, and pair the $c_0$ num
bers of class $0$ among themselves.

**Computing the counts.** Write $n = 3q + r$ with $r \in \{0,1,2\}$.

- $r=0$: $c_0 = c_1 = c_2 = q$. Then $c_1 = c_2$ automatically, and $c_0 = q$ mu
st be even. Since $n=3q$ is even and $3$ is odd, $q$ is even. So **$n \equiv 0 \
pmod 6$**.
- $r=1$: $c_0 = q,\ c_1 = q+1,\ c_2 = q$. Then $c_1 \neq c_2$; **invalid**.
- $r=2$: $c_0 = q,\ c_1 = q+1,\ c_2 = q+1$. Then $c_1 = c_2$, and $c_0 = q$ must
 be even. Since $n = 3q+2$ is even, $q$ is even. So **$n \equiv 2 \pmod 6$**.

Thus the valid even $n$ are exactly those with $n \bmod 6 \in \{0, 2\}$ (equival
ently, $n \not\equiv 4 \pmod 6$).

**Verification on small cases.**
- $n=2$: $\{1,2\}\to(1,2)$, sum $3$. ✓ ($2 \bmod 6 = 2$)
- $n=4$: $c_0=1, c_1=2, c_2=1$, $c_1\neq c_2$. ✗ ($4 \bmod 6 = 4$)
- $n=6$: $c_0=c_1=c_2=2$; e.g. $(1,5),(2,4),(3,6)$. ✓ ($6 \bmod 6 = 0$)
- $n=8$: $c_0=2, c_1=c_2=3$; e.g. $(1,8),(4,5),(2,7),(3,6)$. ✓ ($8 \bmod 6 = 2$)
- $n=10$: $c_0=3, c_1=4, c_2=3$, $c_1\neq c_2$. ✗ ($10 \bmod 6 = 4$)

All consistent.

**Counting $n \le 2024$.**

- $n \equiv 0 \pmod 6$: $n \in \{6, 12, \dots, 2022\}$. Since $2022 = 6 \times 3
37$, there are **337** values.
- $n \equiv 2 \pmod 6$: $n \in \{2, 8, \dots, 2024\}$. Since $2024 = 6 \times 33
7 + 2$, there are **338** values (from $k=0$ to $k=337$).

Total: $337 + 338 = \boxed{675}$.
