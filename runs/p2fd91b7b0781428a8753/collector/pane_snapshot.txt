## Proof that the statement is TRUE

The claim is equivalent to showing $\lambda(D_{2n}) \mid 2n$, where $\lambda$ is
 the Carmichael function. I establish this by determining the exact prime factor
ization of $D_{2n}$ and verifying the divisibility for each prime power factor.

### Step 1: Denominator of $\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}$

**Theorem.** *The denominator of $\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}$ (in lowest terms) is $E_{2n
} = \prod_{p-1\mid 2n} p^{v_p(2n)+1}$, where the product ranges over all primes
$p$ with $p-1 \mid 2n$.*

**Proof.** We analyze the $p$-adic valuation of $\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}$ for each pri
me $p$.

**Case (i): $p-1 \mid 2n$.** By the von Staudt–Clausen theorem, $B_{2n} = A - \s
um_{q-1\mid 2n}\frac{1}{q}$ for $A\in\mathbb{Z}$, so the denominator of $B_{2n}$
 is $\prod_{q-1\mid 2n} q$ (a squarefree product), giving $v_p(B_{2n})=-1$ exact
ly. Thus $v_p\!\left(\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}\right) = -1 - v_p(2n) = -(v_p(2n)+1)$, so
 $p^{v_p(2n)+1}$ exactly divides the denominator. ✓

**Case (ii): $p-1 \nmid 2n$ (so $p$ is odd, since $1\mid 2n$ always).** We show
$v_p\!\left(\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}\right)\geq 0$ using **Kummer's congruence**: *if $
a\equiv b \pmod{\phi(p^k)}$ with $a,b$ positive even integers and $p-1\nmid a,b$
, then $(1-p^{a-1})\frac{B_a}{a}\equiv (1-p^{b-1})\frac{B_b}{b}\pmod{p^k}$.*

Set $k=1$, so $\phi(p)=p-1$. Let $r$ be the unique integer with $2n\equiv r\pmod
{p-1}$ and $0<r<p-1$. Then $r$ is positive, even (since $2n$ and $p-1$ are both
even), and satisfies $p-1\nmid r$ (as $0<r<p-1$). By von Staudt–Clausen, $v_p(B_
r)\geq 0$; and since $r<p$, $v_p(r)=0$, so $v_p\!\left(\frac{B_r}{r}\right)\geq
0$.

Kummer's congruence gives $(1-p^{2n-1})\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}\equiv(1-p^{r-1})\frac{B
_r}{r}\pmod{p}$. Since $2n,r\geq 2$, both $1-p^{2n-1}$ and $1-p^{r-1}$ are $\equ
iv 1\pmod{p}$ (hence $p$-adic units). Therefore $\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}\equiv u\cdot\
frac{B_r}{r}\pmod{p}$ for a $p$-adic unit $u$. If $v_p\!\left(\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}\
right)<0$, then $v_p\!\left(\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}-u\cdot\frac{B_r}{r}\right)=v_p\!\l
eft(\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}\right)<0<1$, contradicting the congruence mod $p$. Hence $
v_p\!\left(\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}\right)\geq 0$. ✓

Combining both cases, the denominator of $\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}$ is exactly $E_{2n}=
\prod_{p-1\mid 2n}p^{v_p(2n)+1}$. $\square$

### Step 2: From $\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}$ to $\frac{B_{2n}}{4n}$

Write $\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}=\frac{N}{E_{2n}}$ with $\gcd(N,E_{2n})=1$. Then $\frac{
B_{2n}}{4n}=\frac{N}{2E_{2n}}$, so $D_{2n}=\frac{2E_{2n}}{\gcd(N,2E_{2n})}=\frac
{2E_{2n}}{\gcd(N,2)}$ (since $\gcd(N,E_{2n})=1$).

**$N$ is odd:** From Case (i) with $p=2$: $v_2(B_{2n})=-1$ and $v_2(2n)=v_2(n)+1
$, so $v_2\!\left(\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}\right)=-(v_2(n)+2)$. Also $v_2(E_{2n})=v_2(2
n)+1=v_2(n)+2$, so $v_2(N)=v_2\!\left(\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}\right)+v_2(E_{2n})=0$. T
hus $N$ is odd, $\gcd(N,2)=1$, and:

$$\boxed{D_{2n} = 2\cdot E_{2n} = 2^{v_2(n)+3}\cdot\prod_{\substack{p\text{ odd}
\\p-1\mid 2n}}p^{v_p(n)+1}}$$

(using $v_2(2n)+2=v_2(n)+3$ and $v_p(2n)+1=v_p(n)+1$ for odd $p$).

### Step 3: Verifying $\lambda(D_{2n})\mid 2n$

Since $\lambda$ is multiplicative over coprime factors, it suffices to check eac
h prime power $q^e\|D_{2n}$.

**Prime $q=2$:** The power is $2^{v_2(n)+3}$ with $v_2(n)+3\geq 3$, so $\lambda(
2^{v_2(n)+3})=2^{v_2(n)+1}$. Since $v_2(2n)=v_2(n)+1$, we have $2^{v_2(n)+1}\mid
 2n$. ✓

**Odd prime $q$ with $q-1\mid 2n$:** The power is $q^{v_q(n)+1}$, so $\lambda(q^

and $\gcd(q^{v_q(n)},q-1)=1$ (as $q\nmid q-1$), we conclude $q^{v_q(n)}(q-1)\mid
 2n$. ✓

No other primes divide $D_{2n}$ (by Step 1, Case (ii)). Therefore:

$$\lambda(D_{2n})\mid 2n.$$

### Step 4: Conclusion

By definition of the Carmichael function, $\lambda(D_{2n})\mid 2n$ implies that
for every integer $a$ with $\gcd(a,D_{2n})=1$:

$$a^{2n}\equiv 1\pmod{D_{2n}}.$$

**The statement is TRUE.** $\square$
