**Answer: No.** $X\setminus\{x_0\}$ is **not** always dense in an arbitrary Haus
dorff space $X$.

## Proof

Recall that a subset $A\subseteq X$ is dense in $X$ iff $\overline{A}=X$. For $A
=X\setminus\{x_0\}$, we have
$$\overline{X\setminus\{x_0\}}=X \iff x_0\in\overline{X\setminus\{x_0\}} \iff x_
0\text{ is not an isolated point of }X.$$
Equivalently, $X\setminus\{x_0\}$ fails to be dense exactly when $\{x_0\}$ is op
en in $X$ (i.e., $x_0$ is an isolated point).

Hausdorffness only guarantees that singletons are **closed** ($T_1$); it does **
not** guarantee that singletons are non-open. A Hausdorff space may have isolate
d points.

**Counterexample.** Let $X=\{0,1\}$ equipped with the discrete topology $\tau=\m
athcal{P}(X)=\{\emptyset,\{0\},\{1\},\{0,1\}\}$. This is Hausdorff (every discre
te space is Hausdorff: any two distinct points can be separated by their singlet
on neighborhoods). Take $x_0=0$. Then
$$X\setminus\{x_0\}=X\setminus\{0\}=\{1\}.$$
Since $\{1\}$ is open (the topology is discrete), its complement $\{0\}$ is clos
ed, so $\{1\}$ is also closed. Hence
$$\overline{X\setminus\{x_0\}}=\overline{\{1\}}=\{1\}\neq\{0,1\}=X.$$
Therefore $X\setminus\{x_0\}$ is **not** dense in $X$.

More generally, in **any** discrete space with at least two points, every point
is isolated, so $X\setminus\{x_0\}$ is closed and proper, hence not dense. Since
 every discrete space is Hausdorff, this provides an infinite family of countere
xamples.

**Conclusion.** The statement "$X\setminus\{x_0\}$ is always dense in any Hausdo
rff space $X$" is **false**. Density of $X\setminus\{x_0\}$ holds precisely when
 $x_0$ is a non-isolated (limit) point of $X$; Hausdorffness alone does not prec
lude isolated points.
