Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a compact metric space can
be $\omega$-homogeneous (finite partial isometries extend) without being $\omega
_1$-homogeneous (countable partial isometries extend).

**Answer: No. Every compact $\omega$-homogeneous metric space is $\omega_1$-homo
geneous.**

## Proof

**Key Fact.** If $(X,d)$ is a compact metric space, then its isometry group $\op
eratorname{Iso}(X,d)$ is compact and metrizable (under the uniform metric $D(\ph
i,\psi)=\sup_{x\in X}d(\phi(x),\psi(x))$).

*Justification.* $\operatorname{Iso}(X)$ is equicontinuous (every isometry is $1
$-Lipschitz). Since $X$ is compact, the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem gives that every s
equence in $\operatorname{Iso}(X)$ has a uniformly convergent subsequence. The u
niform limit of isometries is an isometry (if $\phi_n\to\phi$ uniformly and each
 $\phi_n$ is an isometry, then $d(\phi(x),\phi(y))=\lim_n d(\phi_n(x),\phi_n(y))
=d(x,y)$, and surjectivity follows by compactness). Hence $\operatorname{Iso}(X)
$ is sequentially compact, therefore compact. Metrizability follows from the sup
-metric $D$, which is well-defined since $X$ is compact. $\square$

**Main Theorem.** Let $(X,d)$ be a compact $\omega$-homogeneous metric space. Th
en $(X,d)$ is $\omega_1$-homogeneous.

*Proof.* Let $X_1, X_2 \subseteq X$ be countable (possibly finite) subsets and l
et $h:X_1\to X_2$ be an isometry. We show $h$ extends to an isometry of $X$.

**Case 1: $X_1$ is finite.** This is immediate from $\omega$-homogeneity.

**Case 2: $X_1$ is countably infinite.** Enumerate $X_1=\{a_1,a_2,a_3,\ldots\}$.
 For each $n\geq 1$, set $F_n=\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}$. The restriction $h|_{F_n}:F_n
\to h(F_n)$ is an isometry between finite subsets. By $\omega$-homogeneity, ther
e exists $\phi_n\in\operatorname{Iso}(X)$ extending $h|_{F_n}$, i.e., $\phi_n(a_
i)=h(a_i)$ for all $1\leq i\leq n$.

Since $\operatorname{Iso}(X)$ is compact and metrizable, it is sequentially comp
act. Thus the sequence $\{\phi_n\}_{n\geq 1}$ admits a convergent subsequence $\
phi_{n_k}\to\phi\in\operatorname{Iso}(X)$ (convergence in the uniform metric $D$
).

We claim $\phi$ extends $h$. Fix any $a_i\in X_1$. For all $k$ sufficiently larg
e (namely $n_k\geq i$), we have $\phi_{n_k}(a_i)=h(a_i)$. Therefore:
$$\phi(a_i)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\phi_{n_k}(a_i)=h(a_i),$$
where the first equality uses uniform convergence (which implies pointwise conve
rgence). Since $i$ was arbitrary, $\phi|_{X_1}=h$.

Thus $h$ extends to the global isometry $\phi\in\operatorname{Iso}(X)$. $\square
$

**Conclusion.** The compactness of the isometry group provides the crucial bridg
e: any family of finite partial extensions, indexed by growing finite subsets, m
ust accumulate to a global isometry extending the countable partial isometry. Th
erefore **no** compact metric space is $\omega$-homogeneous but not $\omega_1$-h
omogeneous. The two notions coincide for compact metric spaces.
