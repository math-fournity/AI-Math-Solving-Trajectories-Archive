## Proof

ol is an ordered sequence of $n$ distinct keys, which omits exactly one key. We
establish a bijection between protocols and permutations of $S_{n+1}$: a protoco

rresponds to the permutation $\pi \in S_{n+1}$ where $\pi(i) = k_i$ for $i = 1,
\ldots, n$ and $\pi(n+1) = o$.

**Characterizing non-Cross-Linked pairs.** Two distinct protocols $\pi, \sigma$
are *not* Cross-Linked iff every key in their common image occupies the same pos
ition in both.

- **Same omitted key** ($\pi(n+1) = \sigma(n+1)$): They share all $n$ keys. Bein
g not Cross-Linked forces all positions to agree, so $\pi = \sigma$. Thus distin
ct protocols omitting the same key are **always** Cross-Linked.

- **Different omitted keys** ($\pi(n+1) = a \neq b = \sigma(n+1)$): They share $
n-1$ keys. Not Cross-Linked means these $n-1$ shared keys occupy identical posit
ions. The remaining position $p$ has $\pi(p) = b$ and $\sigma(p) = a$, with all
other positions equal. Equivalently, $\sigma$ is obtained from $\pi$ by swapping
 the values at positions $p$ and $n+1$, i.e., $\sigma = \pi \circ (p\;\; n{+}1)$
.

Setting $T = \{(i\;\; n{+}1) : 1 \le i \le n\}$, two distinct permutations $\pi,
 \sigma$ are not Cross-Linked **iff** $\pi^{-1}\sigma \in T$.

**Cayley graph formulation.** The "not-Cross-Linked" graph is the Cayley graph $
\Gamma = \operatorname{Cay}(S_{n+1}, T)$, with $(n+1)!$ vertices and degree $n$.
 We seek the maximum independent set of $\Gamma$.

**$\Gamma$ is bipartite.** Every generator in $T$ is a transposition (an odd per
mutation). The sign homomorphism $\operatorname{sgn}: S_{n+1} \to \mathbb{Z}/2\m
athbb{Z}$ satisfies $\operatorname{sgn}(t) = 1$ for all $t \in T$. This gives a
proper 2-coloring: even permutations ($A_{n+1}$) and odd permutations ($S_{n+1}
\setminus A_{n+1}$), each of size $(n+1)!/2$. Hence $\Gamma$ is bipartite.

**$\Gamma$ has a perfect matching.** $\Gamma$ is $n$-regular and bipartite with
both parts of equal size $(n+1)!/2$. By Hall's theorem: for any subset $S$ of on
e part, the $n|S|$ edges from $S$ enter $N(S)$, which receives at most $n|N(S)|$
 edges, so $|S| \le |N(S)|$. Hall's condition holds, so a perfect matching of si
ze $(n+1)!/2$ exists.

**Maximum independent set = $(n+1)!/2$.** By König's theorem (bipartite graphs:
max independent set $= |V| -$ min vertex cover $= |V| -$ max matching):

$$\alpha(\Gamma) = (n+1)! - \frac{(n+1)!}{2} = \frac{(n+1)!}{2}.$$

**Achievability.** The set $A_{n+1}$ of even permutations is an independent set
of size $(n+1)!/2$: for any distinct $\pi, \sigma \in A_{n+1}$, the quotient $\p
i^{-1}\sigma$ is even, while every element of $T$ is odd, so $\pi^{-1}\sigma \no
tin T$, meaning $\pi$ and $\sigma$ are Cross-Linked.
