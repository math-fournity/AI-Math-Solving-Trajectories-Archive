## Solution

**Claim.** The solutions are exactly $f(y)=Ay+\dfrac{B}{y}$ for arbitrary consta
nts $A,B\in\mathbb{R}$.

### Verification (these work)

For $f(y)=Ay+B/y$:
$$\Big(x+\tfrac1x\Big)f(y)=\Big(x+\tfrac1x\Big)\Big(Ay+\tfrac{B}{y}\Big)=Axy+\fr
ac{Ax}{y}+\frac{By}{x}+\frac{B}{xy},$$
$$f(xy)+f\!\Big(\tfrac{y}{x}\Big)=\Big(Axy+\frac{B}{xy}\Big)+\Big(\frac{Ay}{x}+\
frac{Bx}{y}\Big)=Axy+\frac{Ax}{y}+\frac{By}{x}+\frac{B}{xy}.\quad\checkmark$$

### Necessity

Set $g(t)=f(e^t)$, so $g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$. With $x=e^a,\;y=e^b$ the equa
tion becomes
$$2\cosh(a)\,g(b)=g(a+b)+g(b-a),\qquad\forall\,a,b\in\mathbb{R}.\tag{$\star$}$$

**Step 1 — Split into even and odd parts.** Setting $b=0$ in $(\star)$:
$$g(a)+g(-a)=2g(0)\cosh(a).\tag{1}$$
Define $E(t)=\frac{g(t)+g(-t)}{2}$ and $L(t)=\frac{g(t)-g(-t)}{2}$. By (1),
$$E(t)=g(0)\cosh(t).\tag{2}$$
Substituting $g=E+L$ into $(\star)$ and using the identity $2\cosh(a)\cosh(b)=\c
osh(a+b)+\cosh(b-a)$ (which cancels the $E$-part), the $L$-part satisfies
$$2\cosh(a)\,L(b)=L(a+b)+L(b-a),\qquad L\text{ odd},\;L(0)=0.\tag{3}$$

**Step 2 — Integer multiples.** Setting $b=a$ in (3): $L(2a)=2\cosh(a)L(a)$. Set
ting $b=na$ in (3) and using induction:
$$L(na)=\frac{\sinh(na)}{\sinh(a)}\,L(a),\qquad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},
\;a\neq 0.\tag{4}$$
*Induction step:* $L((n{+}1)a)=2\cosh(a)L(na)-L((n{-}1)a)=\frac{2\cosh(a)\sinh(n
a)-\sinh((n-1)a)}{\sinh(a)}L(a)=\frac{\sinh((n+1)a)}{\sinh(a)}L(a)$, using $2\co
sh(a)\sinh(na)=\sinh((n{+}1)a)+\sinh((n{-}1)a)$.

**Step 3 — Rational multiples.** Replacing $a$ by $a/n$ in (4): $L(a)=\frac{\sin
h(a)}{\sinh(a/n)}L(a/n)$, so $L(a/n)=\frac{\sinh(a/n)}{\sinh(a)}L(a)$. Combining
 with (4), for every positive rational $q$:
$$L(qa)=\frac{\sinh(qa)}{\sinh(a)}\,L(a).\tag{5}$$

**Step 4 — Pin down the constant.** Fix $a_0=1$ and set $\alpha:=\dfrac{L(1)}{\s
inh(1)}$. By (5) with $a=1$:
$$L(q)=\alpha\sinh(q),\qquad\forall\,q\in\mathbb{Q}.\tag{6}$$

**Step 5 — Extend to all reals.** Define $r(t):=L(t)-\alpha\sinh(t)$. Then $r$ i
s odd, $r(q)=0$ for all $q\in\mathbb{Q}$. Use $(\star)$ for $L$ with $a=q\in\mat
hbb{Q}$, $b=t\in\mathbb{R}$:
$$L(t+q)+L(t-q)=2\cosh(q)L(t).$$
Using $L(t\pm q)=r(t\pm q)+\alpha\sinh(t\pm q)$ and the identity $\sinh(t+q)+\si
nh(t-q)=2\cosh(t)\sinh(q)$:
$$r(t+q)+r(t-q)+2\alpha\cosh(t)\sinh(q)=2\cosh(q)\big(r(t)+\alpha\sinh(t)\big).$
$
Using $2\cosh(q)\sinh(t)=\sinh(t+q)+\sinh(t-q)$, the $\alpha$-terms cancel, givi
ng
$$r(t+q)+r(t-q)=2\cosh(q)\,r(t).\tag{7}$$
Now use $(\star)$ for $L$ with $a=t,\;b=q\in\mathbb{Q}$: $2\cosh(t)L(q)=L(t+q)+L
(q-t)=L(t+q)-L(t-q)$ (oddness). Since $L(q)=\alpha\sinh(q)$:
$$L(t+q)-L(t-q)=2\alpha\cosh(t)\sinh(q).\tag{8}$$
From (7) and (8): $r(t+q)+r(t-q)=2\cosh(q)r(t)$ and $r(t+q)+r(t-q)=2\cosh(q)r(t)
$ (the $L$-version of (7)); subtracting the $\alpha$-identity (8) from the $L$-s
um:
$$r(t+q)=\cosh(q)\,r(t),\qquad r(t-q)=\cosh(q)\,r(t).\tag{9}$$
From $r(t+q)=r(t-q)$ (both equal $\cosh(q)r(t)$): setting $s=t-q$ gives $r(s+2q)
=r(s)$, so **every rational $2q$ is a period of $r$**, i.e., $r$ has every ratio
nal as a period. But (9) also gives $r(t+q)=\cosh(q)r(t)$; combined with periodi
city $r(t+q)=r(t)$:
$$(\cosh(q)-1)\,r(t)=0,\qquad\forall\,q\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}.$$
Since $\cosh(q)\neq 1$ for $q\neq 0$, we conclude $r(t)=0$ for all $t$, i.e.,
$$L(t)=\alpha\sinh(t),\qquad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}.\tag{10}$$

**Step 6 — Combine.** From (2) and (10):
$$g(t)=E(t)+L(t)=g(0)\cosh(t)+\alpha\sinh(t)=\frac{g(0)+\alpha}{2}e^t+\frac{g(0)
-\alpha}{2}e^{-t}.$$
Setting $A=\frac{g(0)+\alpha}{2}$, $B=\frac{g(0)-\alpha}{2}$, and $y=e^t$:
$$\boxed{f(y)=Ay+\frac{B}{y}},\qquad A,B\in\mathbb{R}\text{ arbitrary.}$$
