Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether Brouwer's invariance of dim
ension theorem extends from open sets to closed domains in ℝⁿ.

**Answer: Yes, the theorem holds for closed domains.**

## Proof

**Definitions.** A *closed domain* in ℝⁿ is a set D = cl(Ω) where Ω is a nonempt
y open subset of ℝⁿ. In particular, int(D) ⊇ Ω is a nonempty open subset of ℝⁿ.

**Theorem (Invariance of dimension for closed domains).** If D ⊆ ℝⁿ and D' ⊆ ℝᵐ
are closed domains and h: D → D' is a homeomorphism, then n = m.

**Proof.** The strategy is to show that the closed-domain case reduces to the st
andard (open-set) invariance of dimension.

---

**Step 1: Constructing a homeomorphism between an open subset of ℝⁿ and an open
subset of ℝᵐ.**

Since D is a closed domain in ℝⁿ, int(D) is nonempty and open in ℝⁿ. Pick x ∈ in
t(D) and r > 0 such that the open ball B = B(x, r) ⊆ int(D) ⊆ D.

Since h: D → D' is a homeomorphism and B is open in D (subspace topology), h(B)
is open in D'. Therefore there exists an open set W ⊆ ℝᵐ such that

$$h(B) = D' \cap W.$$

Now, D' = cl(Ω') for some nonempty open Ω' ⊆ ℝᵐ. Since h(B) = D' ∩ W is nonempty
, W intersects D' = cl(Ω'). Because W is open in ℝᵐ and W ∩ cl(Ω') ≠ ∅, we concl
ude W ∩ Ω' ≠ ∅ (an open set meeting the closure of Ω' must meet Ω' itself). Sinc
e Ω' ⊆ D', we have:

$$h(B) \cap \Omega' = D' \cap W \cap \Omega' = W \cap \Omega' \neq \emptyset.$$

Pick y ∈ h(B) ∩ Ω'. Since Ω' is open in ℝᵐ and y ∈ Ω', there exists δ > 0 with B
(y, δ) ⊆ Ω' ⊆ D'. Since y ∈ W and W is open, shrink δ so that B(y, δ) ⊆ W. Then:

$$B(y, \delta) \subseteq D' \cap W = h(B).$$

Now observe:
- B(y, δ) is an open ball in ℝᵐ, hence a **nonempty open subset of ℝᵐ**.
- h⁻¹(B(y, δ)) is open in B (since h is a homeomorphism and B(y, δ) is open in h
(B)), and B is open in ℝⁿ, so h⁻¹(B(y, δ)) is a **nonempty open subset of ℝⁿ**.
- The restriction h: h⁻¹(B(y, δ)) → B(y, δ) is a **homeomorphism** between an op
en subset of ℝⁿ and an open subset of ℝᵐ.

---

**Step 2: Applying invariance of dimension for open sets.**

We now invoke the standard Brouwer invariance of dimension theorem for open sets
:

> **(Invariance of dimension, open-set version).** If U ⊆ ℝⁿ and V ⊆ ℝᵐ are none
mpty open subsets and U ≅ V, then n = m.

*Proof of the open-set version (via local homology).* Let f: U → V be a homeomor
phism. Pick u ∈ U and a small open ball B_u ⊆ U containing u. Then f(B_u) is ope
n in V (homeomorphism maps open to open), and V is open in ℝᵐ, so f(B_u) is open
 in ℝᵐ. Now B_u is an open subset of ℝⁿ and f(B_u) is an open subset of ℝᵐ, with
 B_u ≅ f(B_u).

For any point p ∈ B_u, by excision (excise B_u \ B(p, ε) for small ε > 0):

$$H_k(B_u,\, B_u \setminus \{p\};\,\mathbb{Z}) \cong H_k(\mathbb{R}^n,\, \mathbb
{R}^n \setminus \{0\};\,\mathbb{Z}) \cong \widetilde{H}_{k-1}(S^{n-1}) = \begin{
cases} \mathbb{Z} & k = n \\ 0 & k \neq n \end{cases}$$

Similarly, since f(B_u) is an open subset of ℝᵐ, for the point f(p) ∈ f(B_u):

$$H_k(f(B_u),\, f(B_u) \setminus \{f(p)\};\,\mathbb{Z}) = \begin{cases} \mathbb{
Z} & k = m \\ 0 & k \neq m \end{cases}$$

Since f is a homeomorphism, these local homology groups are isomorphic for all k
. The unique degree at which the group is ℤ gives **n = m**. ∎

Applying this to U = h⁻¹(B(y, δ)) ⊆ ℝⁿ and V = B(y, δ) ⊆ ℝᵐ, which are nonempty
open subsets connected by the homeomorphism h, we conclude:

$$n = m. \qquad \blacksquare$$

---

**Remark.** The key insight is that while a homeomorphism between closed domains
 need not obviously preserve interiors (invariance of domain in its injective-ma
p form requires equal ambient dimensions, which is what we seek to prove), we ca
n nevertheless find *some* open ball of ℝᵐ sitting inside the image h(B) by expl
oiting that h(B) is open in D' and must therefore meet the open set Ω' = int(D')
. This single open ball, together with its preimage, provides the open-set homeo
morphism to which the standard invariance of dimension applies.
