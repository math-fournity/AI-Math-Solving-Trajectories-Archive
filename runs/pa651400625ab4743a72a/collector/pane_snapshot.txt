**Claim.** Yes, $\sigma$ necessarily fixes the composite field $KF$ pointwise.

**Proof.**

Recall that the composite field $KF$ is the smallest subfield of $L$ containing
both $K$ and $F$. Concretely, let $R = K\cdot F = \left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i b_i
: a_i \in K,\; b_i \in F,\; n \geq 0\right\}$ be the ring generated by $K$ and $
F$ inside $L$ (finite sums of products). Then $KF$ is the field of fractions of
$R$ inside $L$:

$$KF = \left\{\frac{p}{q} : p, q \in R,\; q \neq 0\right\}.$$

Every element $x \in KF$ can thus be written as $x = p/q$ with $p = \sum_i a_i b
_i$ and $q = \sum_j c_j d_j$ where $a_i, c_j \in K$ and $b_i, d_j \in F$, and $q
 \neq 0$.

Since $\sigma$ is a field automorphism of $L$, it respects addition, multiplicat
ion, and inverses. Since $\sigma$ fixes $K$ pointwise ($\sigma(a) = a$ for all $
a \in K$) and fixes $F$ pointwise ($\sigma(b) = b$ for all $b \in F$), we comput
e:

$$\sigma(p) = \sigma\!\left(\sum_i a_i b_i\right) = \sum_i \sigma(a_i)\,\sigma(b
_i) = \sum_i a_i b_i = p,$$

and likewise $\sigma(q) = q$. Therefore

$$\sigma(x) = \sigma\!\left(\frac{p}{q}\right) = \frac{\sigma(p)}{\sigma(q)} = \
frac{p}{q} = x.$$

Since $x \in KF$ was arbitrary, $\sigma$ fixes every element of $KF$ pointwise.
$\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The key point is that $KF$ is generated (as a field) by $K \cup F$,
and field automorphisms are completely determined by their action on a generatin
g set. An automorphism that fixes a generating set fixes the entire generated fi
eld. This is the special case of the general principle: if $E = k(S)$ is a field
 generated by a set $S$ over $k$, and an automorphism fixes $k$ and $S$ pointwis
e, then it fixes $E$ pointwise.
