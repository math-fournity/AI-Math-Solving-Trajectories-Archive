## Proof

**Setting up the equation.** The total power consumption is

$$S = \sum_{k=0}^{2000} x^{2k} = 1 + x^2 + x^4 + \cdots + x^{4000},$$

and the condition is $S = 2001\,x^{2000}$.

**Substitution.** Let $y = x^2$ (so $y \geq 0$ for real $x$). The equation becom
es

$$\sum_{k=0}^{2000} y^k = 2001\,y^{1000}, \qquad y \geq 0. \tag{$\star$}$$

**Checking $y=0$:** LHS $= 1$, RHS $= 0$; not a solution. Hence $y > 0$.

**Applying AM–GM.** The 2001 terms $y^0, y^1, y^2, \ldots, y^{2000}$ are all pos
itive. By the AM–GM inequality,

$$\frac{y^0 + y^1 + y^2 + \cdots + y^{2000}}{2001} \;\geq\; \Bigl(y^0 \cdot y^1
\cdot y^2 \cdots y^{2000}\Bigr)^{1/2001}.$$

The geometric mean is

$$\Bigl(y^{\,0+1+2+\cdots+2000}\Bigr)^{1/2001} = y^{\,\frac{0+1+\cdots+2000}{200
1}} = y^{\,\frac{2000\cdot 2001/2}{2001}} = y^{1000}.$$

Therefore

$$\sum_{k=0}^{2000} y^k \;\geq\; 2001\,y^{1000},$$

with **equality if and only if all terms are equal**, i.e., $y^0 = y^1 = \cdots
= y^{2000}$, which forces $y = 1$.

**Conclusion.** The unique positive solution to ($\star$) is $y = 1$, i.e., $x^2
 = 1$, giving $x = \pm 1$. Since an efficiency factor is taken to be positive, t
he specific efficiency factor is

$$\boxed{x = 1}.$$

**Verification:** At $x = 1$, every term $x^{2k} = 1$, so $S = 2001$ and $2001\,
x^{2000} = 2001\cdot 1 = 2001$. ✓
