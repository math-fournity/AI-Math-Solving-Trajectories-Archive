## Solution

Let **C** and **C′** be two categories. An **adjunction** between them is a trip
le \((F, G, \zeta)\) where:

- \(F : \mathbf{C} \to \mathbf{C}^{\prime}\) is a functor (the **left adjoint**)
,
- \(G : \mathbf{C}^{\prime} \to \mathbf{C}\) is a functor (the **right adjoint**
),
- \(\zeta\) is a **natural isomorphism** of bifunctors
\[
\zeta : \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}^{\prime}}(F(-),\, -) \;\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\
; \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(-,\, G(-)),
\]
i.e., for every object \(X \in \mathbf{C}\) and every object \(Y \in \mathbf{C}^
{\prime}\) a bijection of sets
\[
\zeta_{X,Y} : \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}^{\prime}}(F X,\, Y) \;\xrightarrow{\;\con
g\;}\; \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(X,\, G Y),
\]
which is natural in both \(X\) and \(Y\).

### Role of \(F\) (the left adjoint)

\(F\) is the **left adjoint**. It maps objects and morphisms of **C** into **C′*
*. Its role is to provide the "free" or "universal" construction: given an objec
t \(X\) of **C**, the object \(F X\) of **C′** is the target of those morphisms
in **C′** that correspond, via \(\zeta\), to morphisms in **C** out of \(X\) int
o objects of the form \(G Y\). Concretely, \(F\) encodes the **left/universal si
de** of the adjunction — it builds the object that satisfies the universal prope
rty. The fact that \(F\) is a *left* adjoint is reflected in its appearing in th
e **source** position of the hom-set on the left-hand side of \(\zeta\).

### Role of \(G\) (the right adjoint)

\(G\) is the **right adjoint**. It maps objects and morphisms of **C′** back int
o **C**. Its role is to provide the "cofree" or "forgetful/representing" constru
ction: given an object \(Y\) of **C′**, the object \(G Y\) of **C** represents t
he functor \(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}^{\prime}}(F(-), Y) : \mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{o
p}} \to \mathbf{Set}\), in the sense that
\[
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}^{\prime}}(F X, Y) \;\cong\; \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(X
, G Y)
\]
naturally in \(X\). Thus \(G Y\) is the object of **C** that **represents** the
presheaf \(X \mapsto \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}^{\prime}}(F X, Y)\). The fact that
 \(G\) is a *right* adjoint is reflected in its appearing in the **target** posi
tion of the hom-set on the right-hand side of \(\zeta\).

### Role of \(\zeta\) (the natural isomorphism)

\(\zeta\) is the **adjunction isomorphism** (the "adjunction correspondence" or
"transposition"). Its role is to **witness** the adjunction by establishing, for
 each pair \((X, Y)\), a bijection between morphisms \(F X \to Y\) in **C′** and
 morphisms \(X \to G Y\) in **C**. The two **naturality conditions** are the ess
ential content:

- **Naturality in \(X\):** for every \(f : X' \to X\) in **C** and every \(g : F
 X \to Y\) in **C′**,
\[
\zeta_{X',Y}(g \circ F f) \;=\; \zeta_{X,Y}(g) \circ f.
\]

- **Naturality in \(Y\):** for every \(h : Y \to Y'\) in **C′** and every \(g :
F X \to Y\) in **C′**,
\[
\zeta_{X,Y'}(h \circ g) \;=\; G h \circ \zeta_{X,Y}(g).
\]

These two conditions say precisely that \(\zeta\) is a natural isomorphism of bi
functors
\[
\mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \times \mathbf{C}^{\prime} \;\longrightarrow\; \mathbf{
Set}.
\]

Without \(\zeta\), the pair \((F, G)\) of functors would carry no adjunction dat
a — many functor pairs admit no adjunction, and among those that do, the isomorp
hism \(\zeta\) is the specific structure that makes \((F, G)\) an adjoint pair.
The naturality of \(\zeta\) is what ties the bijection coherently to the functor
ial actions of \(F\) and \(G\), ensuring that the correspondence respects compos
ition on both sides.

### How the three roles fit together

The triple \((F, G, \zeta)\) packages three logically distinct pieces:

1. **\(F\)** specifies the *left/universal* construction in **C′**.
2. **\(G\)** specifies the *right/representing* construction in **C**.
3. **\(\zeta\)** specifies the *correspondence* that identifies morphisms \(F X
\to Y\) with morphisms \(X \to G Y\), coherently with the functorial structures
of \(F\) and \(G\).

Equivalently, \(\zeta\) being a natural isomorphism means that \(G Y\) **represe
nts** the functor \(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}^{\prime}}(F(-), Y)\) for each \(Y\)
, and dually \(F X\) **corepresents** the functor \(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(X,
 G(-))\) for each \(X\). From \(\zeta\) one recovers the **unit** \(\eta : \math
rm{Id}_{\mathbf{C}} \Rightarrow G F\) (by setting \(Y = F X\) and taking the ima
ge of \(\mathrm{id}_{F X}\)) and the **counit** \(\varepsilon : F G \Rightarrow
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{C}^{\prime}}\) (by setting \(X = G Y\) and taking the preim
age of \(\mathrm{id}_{G Y}\)), satisfying the triangle identities — confirming t
hat the triple definition is equivalent to the unit–counit formulation of adjunc
tion.
