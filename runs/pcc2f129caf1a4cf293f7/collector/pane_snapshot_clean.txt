## Proof

Define $f(x) = \sin\!\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3}x\right)$. The integrand is the $n$-fol
d composition $f^{\circ n}(x)$, and we seek
$$L = \lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^3 f^{\circ n}(x)\,dx.$$

**Step 1: Fixed points of $f$.** Solving $f(x)=x$, i.e. $\sin(\pi x/3)=x$. The p
oint $x=0$ is a fixed point. We observe that $x=\tfrac12$ is also a fixed point,
 since
$$f\!\left(\tfrac12\right)=\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)=\frac12.$$

**Step 2: Attractiveness of $\tfrac12$.** The derivative is $f'(x)=\tfrac{\pi}{3
}\cos(\pi x/3)$. At $x=\tfrac12$:
$$|f'(\tfrac12)|=\frac{\pi}{3}\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)=\frac{\pi}{3}\cdo
t\frac{\sqrt3}{2}=\frac{\pi\sqrt3}{6}\approx 0.907<1.$$
So $\tfrac12$ is an attractive fixed point. In contrast, $|f'(0)|=\pi/3>1$, so $
0$ is repelling.

**Step 3: Range contraction.** For $x\in[0,3]$, $\pi x/3\in[0,\pi]$, so $f(x)\in
[0,1]$. Then $f$ maps $[0,1]$ into $[0,\sin(\pi/3)]=[0,\sqrt3/2]\subset[0,1]$. T
hus after one iteration, every point of $[0,3]$ lands in $[0,\sqrt3/2]\subset(0,
1]$ (except $x=0$ and $x=3$, which map to $0$).

**Step 4: Pointwise convergence to $\tfrac12$ on $(0,3)$.** We analyze the dynam
ics on $(0,1]$ (where all non-endpoint points land after one step).

- For $x\in(0,\tfrac12)$: $f$ is increasing on $(0,\tfrac32)$ with $f(0)=0$, $f(
\tfrac12)=\tfrac12$, and $f'(0)=\pi/3>1$ so $f(x)>x$ near $0$. Since $f(x)-x$ ha
s no zero in $(0,\tfrac12)$ (the only positive fixed point is $\tfrac12$), we ha
ve $f(x)>x$ and $f(x)\in(0,\tfrac12)$. The sequence $x, f(x), f^2(x),\dots$ is i
ncreasing and bounded above by $\tfrac12$, hence converges; its limit is a fixed
 point in $(0,\tfrac12]$, which must be $\tfrac12$.

- For $x\in(\tfrac12,1]$: $f(x)\in(\tfrac12,1]\subset(\tfrac12,\tfrac32)$ (since
 $\sin(\pi x/3)>\tfrac12$ for $x\in(\tfrac12,\tfrac52)$). One checks $f(x)<x$ on
 $(\tfrac12,\tfrac32)$ (e.g. $g(x)=f(x)-x$ has $g(\tfrac12)=0$, $g'(\tfrac12)=f'
(\tfrac12)-1<0$). The sequence is decreasing and bounded below by $\tfrac12$, he
nce converges to $\tfrac12$.

For $x\in(\tfrac52,3)$: $f(x)\in(0,\tfrac12)$, reducing to the first case after
one step.

Therefore $f^{\circ n}(x)\to \tfrac12$ for every $x\in(0,3)$. The endpoints $x=0
,3$ (measure zero) satisfy $f^{\circ n}\to 0$.

**Step 5: Interchange limit and integral.** Since $|f^{\circ n}(x)|\le 1$ for al
l $n$ and all $x\in[0,3]$, and the constant function $1$ is integrable on $[0,3]
$, the **Dominated Convergence Theorem** gives
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^3 f^{\circ n}(x)\,dx = \int_0^3 \lim_{n\to\infty}f^{\c
irc n}(x)\,dx = \int_0^3 \frac12\,dx = \frac32.$$

(The values at $x=0$ and $x=3$ do not affect the integral.)
