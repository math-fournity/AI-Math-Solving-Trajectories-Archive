**Answer: Yes, S\* is necessarily second countable.**

## Proof

Let $S$ be locally compact, Hausdorff, second countable, and non-compact, and le
t $S^* = S \cup \{\infty\}$ be its one-point compactification. We construct a co
untable base for $S^*$.

**Step 1: A countable base for $S$ consisting of relatively compact sets.**

Let $\mathcal{B} = \{B_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ be a countable base for $S$. Since $S$
is locally compact and Hausdorff, every point $x \in S$ has an open neighborhood
 $U_x$ with $\overline{U_x}$ compact. Since $\mathcal{B}$ is a base, there exist
s $B_n$ with $x \in B_n \subseteq U_x$, hence $\overline{B_n} \subseteq \overlin
e{U_x}$, which is compact; so $\overline{B_n}$ is compact. Thus the subcollectio
n
$$\{B_n \in \mathcal{B} : \overline{B_n} \text{ is compact}\}$$
covers $S$. Enumumerate the corresponding compact closures as $\{C_k\}_{k=1}^\in
fty$; this is a countable family of compact sets covering $S$.

**Step 2: A compact exhaustion of $S$.**

Define $K_n = C_1 \cup \cdots \cup C_n$. Each $K_n$ is compact (finite union of
compact sets in a Hausdorff space), $K_n \subseteq K_{n+1}$, and $\bigcup_{n=1}^
\infty K_n = S$.

**Step 3: A countable neighborhood base at $\infty$.**

Recall the topology of $S^*$: open sets are (i) all open subsets of $S$, and (ii
) sets of the form $\{\infty\} \cup (S \setminus K)$ where $K \subseteq S$ is co
mpact. Define
$$U_n = \{\infty\} \cup (S \setminus K_n), \qquad n = 1, 2, \ldots$$

We claim $\{U_n\}$ is a neighborhood base at $\infty$. Let $V = \{\infty\} \cup
(S \setminus K)$ be any open neighborhood of $\infty$, with $K \subseteq S$ comp
act. Since $K \subseteq S = \bigcup_n K_n$ and the $K_n$ are increasing, compact
ness of $K$ gives $K \subseteq K_N$ for some $N$. Then $S \setminus K_N \subsete
q S \setminus K$, so
$$U_N = \{\infty\} \cup (S \setminus K_N) \subseteq \{\infty\} \cup (S \setminus
 K) = V.$$
Thus $\{U_n\}$ is a countable neighborhood base at $\infty$.

**Step 4: A countable base for $S^*$.**

Set $\mathcal{B}^* = \mathcal{B} \cup \{U_n : n \geq 1\}$. This is countable. We
 verify it is a base for $S^*$:

- *Coverage and refinement of $S$-open sets:* handled by $\mathcal{B}$, which is
 a base for $S$.
- *Refinement of neighborhoods of $\infty$:* by Step 3, every open set containin
g $\infty$ contains some $U_n$.
- *Intersection property:*
  - $B_i \cap B_j$ is open in $S$, refined by $\mathcal{B}$.
  - $U_n \cap B_m = (S \setminus K_n) \cap B_m$, open in $S$, refined by $\mathc
al{B}$.
  - $U_n \cap U_m = \{\infty\} \cup (S \setminus (K_n \cup K_m)) = \{\infty\} \c
up (S \setminus K_{\max(n,m)}) = U_{\max(n,m)} \in \mathcal{B}^*$.

Hence $\mathcal{B}^*$ is a countable base for $S^*$.

**Conclusion.** The one-point compactification $S^*$ of a locally compact, Hausd
orff, second countable, non-compact space $S$ is second countable. $\blacksquare
$
