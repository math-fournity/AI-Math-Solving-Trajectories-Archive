Looking at this problem, I need to analyze what $L_{ij}u = 0$ for all $i,j$ impl
ies on a domain of the unit sphere.

## Analysis

**The operators $L_{ij}$ are the generators of $SO(n+1)$.** The operator $L_{ij}
 = x_i \partial_j - x_j \partial_i$ generates rotations in the $(x_i, x_j)$-plan
e. Together, the collection $\{L_{ij}\}_{i<j}$ generates the full rotation group
 $SO(n+1)$ acting on $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$.

**On $\mathbb{S}^n$, these are tangent vector fields.** Since $L_{ij}(|x|^2) = 0
$, each $L_{ij}$ is tangent to every sphere centered at the origin, in particula
r to $\mathbb{S}^n$. They are precisely the angular derivative operators on the
sphere — the infinitesimal generators of the isometry group of $\mathbb{S}^n$.

**Vanishing of all $L_{ij}u$ means $u$ is $SO(n+1)$-invariant.** If $L_{ij}u = 0
$ for all $i, j$, then $u$ is invariant under the flow of every $L_{ij}$, hence
under all one-parameter rotation subgroups, hence under the entire connected gro
up $SO(n+1)$. The orbit of any point $x \in \mathbb{S}^n$ under $SO(n+1)$ is the
 entire sphere $\mathbb{S}^n$. Therefore, if $\Omega$ is connected, $u$ is **con
stant** on $\Omega$.

A constant function is trivially radially symmetric (it can be written as $u(x)
= f(d(x,p))$ for any pole $p$, with $f$ constant).

**However, the shape of $\Omega$ is unconstrained.** The condition $L_{ij}u = 0$
 is a condition on the function $u$, not on the domain $\Omega$. For **any** smo
oth bounded domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{S}^n$ — whether or not it is a geodes
ic ball — the constant function $u \equiv c$ satisfies $L_{ij}u = 0$ for all $i,
j$. The vanishing of the angular derivatives imposes no geometric restriction on
 $\Omega$ whatsoever.

## Answer

**No.**

The vanishing of all $L_{ij}u$ does imply that $u$ is radially symmetric (in fac
t, $u$ is constant on each connected component of $\Omega$, since $SO(n+1)$ acts
 transitively on $\mathbb{S}^n$ and the $L_{ij}$ generate this action). However,
 it does **not** imply that $\Omega$ is a ball.

**Counterexample:** Let $\Omega$ be any smooth bounded domain on $\mathbb{S}^n$
that is **not** a geodesic ball (for instance, a non-convex region, or a region
whose boundary is not a level set of the geodesic distance from any single point
). Define $u \equiv 1$ on $\Omega$. Then $L_{ij}u = 0$ for every $i,j$ (since $u
$ is constant), yet $\Omega$ is not a ball.

The essential point is that $L_{ij}u = 0$ constrains only $u$ (forcing it to be
rotation-invariant, hence constant on the sphere), while $\Omega$ enters the pro
blem merely as the set on which $u$ is defined. No elliptic PDE, boundary condit
ion, or overdetermined system links the vanishing of $L_{ij}u$ to the geometry o
f $\Omega$. This is fundamentally different from results like those of Gidas–Ni–
Nirenberg or Serrin's overdetermined boundary value problem, where a PDE *togeth
er with* a boundary condition creates the link between symmetry of the solution
and symmetry of the domain. Here, no such link exists.
