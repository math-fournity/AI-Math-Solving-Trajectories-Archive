## Answer

**No**, it does not follow that \(X = Y\) when \(Y = X^T\). The condition is aut
omatically satisfied for *every* \(X\), symmetric or not.

### Proof

Recall the Frobenius inner product \(\langle A, B\rangle = \operatorname{tr}(A^T
 B)\).

**Step 1: The condition only constrains the symmetric part.**

Setting \(Z = X - Y\), the hypothesis \(\langle X, P\rangle = \langle Y, P\rangl
e\) for all \(P \succ 0\) becomes
\[
\operatorname{tr}(Z^T P) = 0 \quad \forall\, P \succ 0.
\]
Since every positive definite \(P\) is **symmetric**, we decompose \(Z = S + K\)
 into symmetric part \(S = \tfrac{Z+Z^T}{2}\) and skew-symmetric part \(K = \tfr
ac{Z-Z^T}{2}\). Then
\[
\operatorname{tr}(Z^T P) = \operatorname{tr}(S\,P) + \operatorname{tr}(K^T P).
\]
But \(K^T = -K\) is skew-symmetric and \(P\) is symmetric, so \(\operatorname{tr
}(K^T P) = 0\) identically (trace of a skew-symmetric–symmetric product vanishes
). Hence the hypothesis reduces to
\[
\operatorname{tr}(S\,P) = 0 \quad \forall\, P \succ 0.
\]
The positive-definite cone is **open** in the space of symmetric matrices, so by
 continuity \(\operatorname{tr}(S\,P)=0\) for all symmetric \(P\), giving \(S =
0\). Thus the hypothesis forces only \(X - Y\) to be **skew-symmetric**, not zer
o.

**Step 2: Specialize to \(Y = X^T\).**

With \(Y = X^T\), the hypothesis reads
\[
\operatorname{tr}(X^T P) = \operatorname{tr}(X\,P) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{tr}\!\big((X^T - X)\,P\big) = 0 \quad \forall\, P \succ 0.
\]
Here \(W := X^T - X\) is **skew-symmetric** (\(W^T = -W\)), and \(P\) is symmetr
ic, so
\[
\operatorname{tr}(W\,P) = 0
\]
holds **identically for every \(X\)** — no constraint on \(X\) whatsoever. The h
ypothesis is therefore vacuous when \(Y = X^T\); it cannot force \(X = X^T\).

**Step 3: Explicit counterexample.**

Take \(n = 2\) and
\[
X = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad Y = X^T = \begin{pmatri
x} 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Clearly \(X \neq Y\). For any symmetric \(P = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ b & c \en
d{pmatrix} \succ 0\),
\[
\langle X, P\rangle = \operatorname{tr}(X^T P) = \operatorname{tr}\!\begin{pmatr
ix} 0 & 0 \\ a & b \end{pmatrix} = b,
\]
\[
\langle Y, P\rangle = \operatorname{tr}(Y^T P) = \operatorname{tr}\!\begin{pmatr
ix} b & c \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = b.
\]
So \(\langle X, P\rangle = \langle Y, P\rangle = b\) for **every** \(P \succ 0\)
, yet \(X \neq Y\).

### Conclusion

The implication **fails**. The Frobenius inner product against all positive defi
nite (hence symmetric) \(P\) is blind to the skew-symmetric component of \(X - Y
\). When \(Y = X^T\), the difference \(X - Y = X - X^T\) is purely skew-symmetri
c, so the hypothesis \(\langle X, P\rangle = \langle Y, P\rangle\) is satisfied
trivially for all \(X\) and provides no information forcing \(X = X^T\).
