## Solution

Let $\alpha = \sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}$ and $\beta = \sqrt{3}-\sqrt{2}$. Then $\alpha\b
eta = 1$, so $\beta = \alpha^{-1}$, and $0 < \beta < 1$.

**Step 1: Reduce to an integer recurrence.**

Set $u = \alpha^2 = 5+2\sqrt{6}$ and $v = \beta^2 = 5-2\sqrt{6}$. Then
$$u+v = 10, \qquad uv = 1,$$
so $u, v$ are the roots of $x^2 - 10x + 1 = 0$. Define
$$T_k = u^k + v^k.$$
Then $T_k$ satisfies
$$T_k = 10\,T_{k-1} - T_{k-2}, \qquad T_0 = 2,\; T_1 = 10.$$
Since the recurrence has integer coefficients and integer initial values, **ever
y $T_k$ is a positive integer**.

**Step 2: Locate $\alpha^{2012}$ relative to an integer.**

Since $2012 = 2\cdot 1006$,
$$\alpha^{2012} = u^{1006} = T_{1006} - v^{1006}.$$
Now $v = 5 - 2\sqrt{6} \approx 0.10102$, so $0 < v < 1$, hence $0 < v^{1006} < 1
$. Therefore
$$\alpha^{2012} = T_{1006} - v^{1006} \in (T_{1006}-1,\; T_{1006}).$$
So the integer part of $\alpha^{2012}$ is $T_{1006}-1$, and the fractional part
is $1 - v^{1006}$.

**Step 3: Digit immediately to the right of the decimal point.**

The fractional part is $1 - v^{1006}$. Since $v^{1006}$ is a positive number far
 smaller than $0.1$ (indeed $v^{1006} < 0.102^{1006} < 10^{-1000}$), we have
$$1 - v^{1006} = 0.\underbrace{999\ldots 9}_{\text{hundreds of 9's}}\ldots$$
In particular, the first digit after the decimal point is **9**.

**Step 4: Digit immediately to the left of the decimal point.**

This is the units digit of $T_{1006}-1$, i.e. determined by $T_{1006} \pmod{10}$
.

Modulo 10, the recurrence gives $T_k \equiv -T_{k-2} \pmod{10}$. With $T_0 \equi
v 2$, $T_1 \equiv 0$:
$$T_2 \equiv -2 \equiv 8,\quad T_3 \equiv 0,\quad T_4 \equiv -8 \equiv 2,\quad T
_5 \equiv 0,\quad T_6 \equiv 8,\ldots$$
The sequence mod 10 is periodic with period 4:
$$2,\;0,\;8,\;0,\;2,\;0,\;8,\;0,\;\ldots$$
For even $k$: $k \equiv 0 \pmod 4 \Rightarrow 2$; $\;k \equiv 2 \pmod 4 \Rightar
row 8$.

Since $1006 = 4\cdot 251 + 2$, we have $1006 \equiv 2 \pmod 4$, so
$$T_{1006} \equiv 8 \pmod{10}.$$
Thus $T_{1006}$ ends in 8, and $T_{1006}-1$ ends in **7**.

**Conclusion.**

The digit immediately to the left of the decimal point in $(\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3})^{
2012}$ is $\boxed{7}$, and the digit immediately to the right is $\boxed{9}$.
