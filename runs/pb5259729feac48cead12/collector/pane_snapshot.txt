# Solution

We seek extremals of the isoperimetric problem
$$J(y)=\int_0^1 \dot y^2\,dt,\qquad y(0)=1,\; y(1)=4,\; \int_0^1 y\,dt=2.$$

## Setting up the augmented functional

For an isoperimetric constraint $K(y)=\int_0^1 y\,dt=2$, we introduce a Lagrange
 multiplier $\lambda\in\mathbb{R}$ and form the augmented Lagrangian
$$\mathcal{L}(t,y,\dot y)=\dot y^2+\lambda\,y.$$
The Euler–Lagrange equation for the augmented problem is a necessary condition f
or an extremal.

## Deriving the Euler–Lagrange equation

Computing the partial derivatives:
$$\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial y}=\lambda,\qquad \frac{\partial\mathcal{L
}}{\partial\dot y}=2\dot y.$$
The Euler–Lagrange equation $\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial y}-\frac{d}{dt}
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot y}=0$ gives
$$\lambda-\frac{d}{dt}(2\dot y)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \ddot y=\frac{\lambda
}{2}.$$

## General solution

Integrating twice:
$$\dot y(t)=\frac{\lambda}{2}\,t+a,\qquad y(t)=\frac{\lambda}{4}\,t^2+at+b,$$
where $a,b$ are constants of integration.

## Applying the boundary conditions

From $y(0)=1$: $\;b=1$.

From $y(1)=4$: $\;\frac{\lambda}{4}+a+1=4$, i.e.
$$\frac{\lambda}{4}+a=3.\tag{1}$$

## Applying the integral constraint

$$\int_0^1 y\,dt=\int_0^1\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{4}t^2+at+1\right)dt=\frac{\lambd
a}{12}+\frac{a}{2}+1=2,$$
so
$$\frac{\lambda}{12}+\frac{a}{2}=1.\tag{2}$$

## Solving the linear system

From (1): $a=3-\frac{\lambda}{4}$. Substituting into (2):
$$\frac{\lambda}{12}+\frac{1}{2}\!\left(3-\frac{\lambda}{4}\right)=1
\;\Longrightarrow\;\frac{\lambda}{12}+\frac{3}{2}-\frac{\lambda}{8}=1.$$
Collecting the $\lambda$-terms:
$$\lambda\!\left(\frac{1}{12}-\frac{1}{8}\right)=1-\frac{3}{2}=-\frac{1}{2}
\;\Longrightarrow\;\lambda\!\left(-\frac{1}{24}\right)=-\frac{1}{2}
\;\Longrightarrow\;\lambda=12.$$
Then $a=3-\frac{12}{4}=3-3=0$.

## The extremal

$$\boxed{\,y(t)=3t^2+1\,}.$$

## Verification

- $y(0)=1$ ✓
- $y(1)=3+1=4$ ✓
- $\int_0^1(3t^2+1)\,dt=\big[t^3+t\big]_0^1=1+1=2$ ✓
- $\ddot y=6=\lambda/2=12/2$ ✓ (satisfies the Euler–Lagrange equation)

## Uniqueness (sufficiency)

The augmented Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}=\dot y^2+\lambda y$ is convex in $\dot y$
(in fact strictly convex, since $\partial^2\mathcal{L}/\partial\dot y^2=2>0$) an
d affine in $y$. Hence the Euler–Lagrange equation together with the constraints
 gives a global minimizer of $J$ subject to the constraints. Since the second va
riation $\delta^2 J=2\int_0^1 \eta^{\prime\,2}\,dt\ge 0$ is non-negative (and th
e constraint is linear), the extremal $y(t)=3t^2+1$ is in fact the unique global
 minimizer.
