We study the general term
$$a_n = \left(1 - \frac{p\,\ln n}{n}\right)^n, \qquad n \ge n_p.$$

The role of $n_p$ is purely to ensure the base is positive (so the term is real
and positive); since $\frac{\ln n}{n}\to 0$, for every fixed $p$ such an $n_p$ e
xists, and it does not affect convergence. Hence we analyze the tail behavior as
 $n\to\infty$.

### Asymptotic expansion

Set $x_n = \frac{p\ln n}{n}\to 0$. Taking logarithms and using $\ln(1-x) = -x -
\frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^3}{3}-\cdots$:

$$\ln a_n = n\ln\!\left(1 - \frac{p\ln n}{n}\right) = n\left(-\frac{p\ln n}{n} -
 \frac{p^2\ln^2 n}{2n^2} - \frac{p^3\ln^3 n}{3n^3} - \cdots\right).$$

Therefore
$$\ln a_n = -p\ln n \;-\; \frac{p^2\ln^2 n}{2n} \;-\; \frac{p^3\ln^3 n}{3n^2} \;
-\;\cdots$$

and exponentiating,
$$a_n = n^{-p}\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{p^2\ln^2 n}{2n} - \frac{p^3\ln^3 n}{3n^2}-\cd
ots\right).$$

### The exponential factor tends to 1

Since $\frac{\ln^2 n}{n}\to 0$ and $\frac{\ln^k n}{n^{k-1}}\to 0$ for every $k\g
e 2$, every term in the exponent vanishes, so
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_n}{n^{-p}} = \lim_{n\to\infty}\exp\!\left(-\frac{p^2\
ln^2 n}{2n}-\cdots\right) = 1.$$

### Limit comparison test

Since $\frac{a_n}{n^{-p}}\to 1 \in (0,\infty)$, the limit comparison test gives
$$\sum_{n} a_n \text{ converges} \iff \sum_{n} \frac{1}{n^{p}} \text{ converges}
.$$

The $p$-series $\sum n^{-p}$ converges if and only if $p > 1$.

### Verification of boundary cases

- **$p\le 0$:** then $1-\frac{p\ln n}{n}\ge 1$, so $a_n\ge 1$; the general term
does not tend to $0$. Divergent.
- **$0<p\le 1$:** $a_n\sim n^{-p}$ with $p\le 1$; divergent by comparison with t
he harmonic-type series.
- **$p>1$:** $a_n\sim n^{-p}$ with $p>1$; convergent.

### Conclusion

The series converges if and only if

$$\boxed{p > 1.}$$
