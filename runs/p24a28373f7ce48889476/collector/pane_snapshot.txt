Looking at this problem, I need to evaluate the asymptotic behavior of an integr
al dominated by regions where $x^3 + y^3$ is maximized.

## Proof

**Setup.** The triangle is $T = \{(x,y) : x \ge 0,\; y \ge 0,\; x+y \le a\}$. On
 $T$, the function $f(x,y) = x^3 + y^3$ achieves its maximum $a^3$ at the two co
rners $(a,0)$ and $(0,a)$. Indeed, on the hypotenuse $x+y=a$ we have $x^3+y^3 =
a^3 - 3ax(a-x) \le a^3$, with equality only at the endpoints. In the interior, $
x^3+y^3 < a^3$ strictly. The integral is therefore dominated by neighborhoods of
 the two corners.

**Symmetry.** Since $T$ and the integrand are symmetric under $x \leftrightarrow
 y$, the contributions from neighborhoods of $(a,0)$ and $(0,a)$ are equal. Thus
 $I(a) = 2\,J(a) + R(a)$, where $J(a)$ is the contribution near $(a,0)$ and $R(a
)$ is the remainder.

**Bounding the remainder.** Away from the two corners, $x^3+y^3 \le a^3 - c\,a^3
$ for some constant $c > 0$ (e.g., on the hypotenuse near the midpoint, $x^3+y^3
 = a^3/4$). The area of $T$ is $a^2/2$, so $R(a) = O(a^2 \, e^{a^3/4}) = o(e^{a^
3}/a^4)$. Hence $R(a)$ is negligible.

**Computing $J(a)$.** Substitute $x = a - u,\; y = v$ near $(a,0)$. The constrai
nts become $u \ge 0$, $v \ge 0$, $v \le u$, $u \le a$. Then:
$$(a-u)^3 + v^3 = a^3 - 3a^2 u + 3au^2 - u^3 + v^3.$$

So:
$$J(a) = e^{a^3}\int_0^a \int_0^u e^{-3a^2 u + 3au^2 - u^3 + v^3}\,dv\,du.$$

**Laplace scaling.** The factor $e^{-3a^2 u}$ localizes $u$ to scale $1/(3a^2)$.
 Set $u = \frac{t}{3a^2}$, $du = \frac{dt}{3a^2}$:
$$J(a) = \frac{e^{a^3}}{3a^2}\int_0^{3a^3}\int_0^{t/(3a^2)} e^{-t + \frac{t^2}{3
a^3} - \frac{t^3}{27a^6} + v^3}\,dv\,dt.$$

For the inner integral, $v$ ranges over $[0, t/(3a^2)]$. Since $v^3 \le t^3/(27a
^6) = O(a^{-6})$ for $t = O(1)$, we have $e^{v^3} = 1 + O(a^{-6})$ uniformly, gi
ving:
$$\int_0^{t/(3a^2)} e^{v^3}\,dv = \frac{t}{3a^2}\bigl(1 + O(a^{-6})\bigr).$$

For the remaining exponential, $e^{t^2/(3a^3) - t^3/(27a^6)} = 1 + \frac{t^2}{3a
^3} + O(a^{-6})$ for $t = O(1)$. Extending the integral to $[0,\infty)$ introduc
es only exponentially small error. Therefore:
$$J(a) = \frac{e^{a^3}}{9a^4}\int_0^{\infty} t\,e^{-t}\left(1 + \frac{t^2}{3a^3}
 + O(a^{-6})\right)dt.$$

Using $\int_0^\infty t\,e^{-t}\,dt = \Gamma(2) = 1$ and $\int_0^\infty t^3 e^{-t
}\,dt = \Gamma(4) = 6$:
$$J(a) = \frac{e^{a^3}}{9a^4}\left(1 + \frac{6}{3a^3} + O(a^{-6})\right) = \frac
{e^{a^3}}{9a^4}\left(1 + O(a^{-3})\right).$$

**Assembling.** Since $I(a) = 2\,J(a) + o(e^{a^3}/a^4)$:
$$a^4\,e^{-a^3}\,I(a) = a^4\,e^{-a^3}\cdot\frac{2\,e^{a^3}}{9a^4}\left(1 + O(a^{
-3})\right) = \frac{2}{9}\left(1 + O(a^{-3})\right).$$

Taking the limit as $a \to \infty$:

$$\lim_{a\to\infty} a^4\,e^{-a^3}\int_T e^{x^3+y^3}\,dx\,dy = \boxed{\dfrac{2}{9
}}.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
