Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the isomorphism $M \otimes
M^\vee \xrightarrow{\sim} \operatorname{End}(M)$ forces the canonical double-dua
l map $M \to (M^\vee)^\vee$ to be an isomorphism.

**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

Let $\Phi: M \otimes_A M^\vee \to \operatorname{End}_A(M)$ be the canonical map
sending $m \otimes \varphi$ to the endomorphism $x \mapsto \varphi(x)\,m$.

**Step 1: $\Phi$ surjective $\Rightarrow$ $M$ is finitely generated projective.*
*

Since $\Phi$ is an isomorphism, it is surjective, so $\operatorname{id}_M$ lies
in its image. Thus there exist $m_1, \ldots, m_n \in M$ and $\varphi_1, \ldots,
\varphi_n \in M^\vee$ (a finite sum, since tensors are finite sums by definition
) such that
$$\operatorname{id}_M = \Phi\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^n m_i \otimes \varphi_i\right),$$
i.e., for every $x \in M$,
$$x = \sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_i(x)\, m_i. \tag{$\ast$}$$

This is precisely the **dual basis condition**. By the dual basis lemma, $(\ast)
$ holds for some finite family $\{(m_i, \varphi_i)\}_{i=1}^n$ if and only if $M$
 is finitely generated projective.

*Brief justification of the lemma (converse direction, which is what we use):* G
iven $(\ast)$, define $s: M \to A^n$ by $s(x) = (\varphi_1(x), \ldots, \varphi_n
(x))$ and $\pi: A^n \to M$ by $\pi(a_1,\ldots,a_n) = \sum a_i m_i$. Then $\pi \c
irc s = \operatorname{id}_M$ by $(\ast)$, so $M$ is a direct summand of the fini
te free module $A^n$, hence finitely generated projective.

**Step 2: Finitely generated projective $\Rightarrow$ $M \to M^{\vee\vee}$ is an
 isomorphism.**

Since $M$ is finitely generated projective, there exists an $A$-module $N$ with
$M \oplus N \cong A^r$ for some finite $r$. The dual functor $(-)^\vee = \operat
orname{Hom}_A(-,A)$ and the double-dual functor $(-)^{\vee\vee}$ are additive, h
ence preserve finite direct sums:
$$(M \oplus N)^{\vee\vee} \cong M^{\vee\vee} \oplus N^{\vee\vee}.$$
The canonical map $\eta$ is natural in $M$, so
$$\eta_{M \oplus N} = \eta_M \oplus \eta_N.$$
For the finite free module $A^r$, the map $\eta_{A^r}: A^r \to (A^r)^{\vee\vee}$
 is an isomorphism (this is the standard fact for finite free modules: $A^r \con
g (A^r)^\vee \cong (A^r)^{\vee\vee}$, and $\eta$ is the composite of these canon
ical isomorphisms). Since $M \oplus N \cong A^r$, the map $\eta_{M\oplus N}$ is
an isomorphism. As $\eta_{M \oplus N} = \eta_M \oplus \eta_N$ under the direct-s
um decomposition, both $\eta_M$ and $\eta_N$ are isomorphisms.

**Conclusion.** The canonical map $\eta: M \to (M^\vee)^\vee$ is an isomorphism.
 $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** Only the *surjectivity* of $\Phi$ was used, not the full strength of
 $\Phi$ being an isomorphism. The injectivity of $\Phi$ is automatic once $M$ is
 finitely generated projective (since then $M \otimes M^\vee \cong \operatorname
{End}(M)$ is the standard isomorphism for finitely generated projective modules)
, so the hypothesis "$\Phi$ is an isomorphism" is in fact equivalent to "$M$ is
finitely generated projective."
