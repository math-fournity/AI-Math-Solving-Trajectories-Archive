**Lemma (Divisibility is preserved by surjective homomorphic images).** Let $\va
rphi : G \to H$ be a surjective group homomorphism. If $G$ is divisible, then $H

*Proof of Lemma.* Let $h \in H$ and let $m$ be a positive integer. By surjectivi
ty, choose $g \in G$ with $\varphi(g) = h$. Since $G$ is divisible, there exists
 $g' \in G$ with $(g')^m = g$. Setting $h' := \varphi(g') \in H$, we compute
$$
(h')^m = \varphi(g')^m = \varphi\!\big((g')^m\big) = \varphi(g) = h.
$$
Thus every element of $H$ has an $m$-th root in $H$ for every $m \geq 1$, so $H$
 is divisible. $\square$

**Main proof.** Consider the determinant map
$$
\det : GL_n(F) \longrightarrow F^*.
$$

es n$ matrices over $F$ to the multiplicative group of $F$. It is surjective: fo

in GL_n(F)$ has determinant $a$.

By hypothesis $GL_n(F)$ is divisible. Applying the Lemma to the surjective homom
orphism $\det$, we conclude that $F^*$ is divisible. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The case $n = 1$ is trivial since $GL_1(F) = F^*$. The argument abov
