Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a finite local ring $R$ (no
t a field) with $p \mid |R^\times|$ ($p > 2$) can contain a primitive $p$th root
 of unity $u$ with $u - 1 \in R^\times$.

**Answer: Yes, it is possible.**

## Proof

**Construction.** Choose a prime power $q$ such that $q \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$ (such
 $q$ exists; for instance, take any prime $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$ by Dirichlet'
s theorem, and set $q = \ell$). Define

$$R = \mathbb{F}_q[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2),$$

the ring of dual numbers over $\mathbb{F}_q$.

**$R$ is a finite local ring, not a field.** The ideal $\mathfrak{m} = (\varepsi
lon)$ is the unique maximal ideal: every element $a + b\varepsilon$ with $a \neq
 0$ is a unit (with inverse $a^{-1} - a^{-2}b\,\varepsilon$), while every elemen
t with $a = 0$ lies in $\mathfrak{m}$ and is nilpotent. The residue field is $R/
\mathfrak{m} \cong \mathbb{F}_q$. Since $\varepsilon \neq 0$ but $\varepsilon^2
= 0$, the ring $R$ is not a field.

**$p$ divides $|R^\times|$.** The units are exactly the elements $a + b\varepsil
on$ with $a \in \mathbb{F}_q^\times$, $b \in \mathbb{F}_q$, so

$$|R^\times| = (q - 1)\cdot q.$$

Since $p \mid (q - 1)$ by construction, we have $p \mid |R^\times|$.

**$R$ contains a primitive $p$th root of unity.** Since $p \mid (q-1) = |\mathbb
{F}_q^\times|$ and $\mathbb{F}_q^\times$ is cyclic, there exists $\zeta \in \mat
hbb{F}_q^\times$ of order exactly $p$. Viewing $\mathbb{F}_q$ as a subring of $R
$ (via the natural embedding $a \mapsto a + 0\varepsilon$), the element $\zeta \
in R$ satisfies $\zeta^p = 1$ and $\zeta \neq 1$. The inclusion $\mathbb{F}_q^\t
imes \hookrightarrow R^\times$ is an injective group homomorphism, so $\zeta$ re
tains order exactly $p$ in $R^\times$. Thus $u := \zeta$ is a primitive $p$th ro
ot of unity in $R$.

**$u - 1$ is a unit.** We have $u - 1 = \zeta - 1$, viewed as the constant eleme
nt $(\zeta - 1) + 0\varepsilon \in R$. Since $\zeta$ has order $p > 2$ in $\math
bb{F}_q^\times$, we have $\zeta \neq 1$ in $\mathbb{F}_q$, so $\zeta - 1 \neq 0$
 in $\mathbb{F}_q$. An element $a + b\varepsilon \in R$ is a unit if and only if
 its image $a$ in the residue field $\mathbb{F}_q$ is nonzero. Since $\zeta - 1
\neq 0$ in $\mathbb{F}_q$, the element $u - 1 = (\zeta - 1) + 0\varepsilon$ is a
 unit.

**Concrete example.** Taking $p = 3$ and $q = 7$ (since $3 \mid 6$), the ring $R
 = \mathbb{F}_7[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ is a finite local ring (not a fiel
d) with $|R^\times| = 42 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 7$, so $3 \mid |R^\times|$. The eleme
nt $\zeta = 2 \in \mathbb{F}_7^\times$ has order $3$ (since $2^3 = 8 \equiv 1 \p
mod{7}$), and $u - 1 = 1 \in R^\times$.

This completes the proof that such a ring exists. $\blacksquare$
