## Proof

We work over a field $k$ of characteristic $0$. The Hopf algebra structure on $U
(L)$ is given by:
- **Coproduct:** $\Delta(x) = x \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes x$ for $x \in L$, extended
 as an algebra homomorphism.
- **Counit:** $\varepsilon(x) = 0$ for $x \in L$, extended as an algebra homomor
phism.
- **Antipode:** $S(x) = -x$ for $x \in L$, extended as an anti-homomorphism.

**Claim:** The only grouplike element of $U(L)$ is $1$. Hence the group of group
like elements is the trivial group $\{1\}$.

---

### Step 1: Reduction to the augmentation ideal

Let $g \in U(L)$ be grouplike: $\Delta(g) = g \otimes g$, $g \neq 0$. Apply $\va
repsilon \otimes \mathrm{id}$ to both sides:

$$g = (\varepsilon \otimes \mathrm{id})(\Delta(g)) = (\varepsilon \otimes \mathr
m{id})(g \otimes g) = \varepsilon(g)\, g.$$

Since $g \neq 0$, we get $\varepsilon(g) = 1$. Set $I = \ker \varepsilon$ (the a
ugmentation ideal) and write $g = 1 + h$ with $h \in I$.

Computing both sides of $\Delta(g) = g \otimes g$:

$$1 \otimes 1 + \Delta(h) = (1+h)\otimes(1+h) = 1\otimes 1 + 1\otimes h + h \oti
mes 1 + h \otimes h.$$

So $h$ satisfies:

$$\Delta(h) = 1 \otimes h + h \otimes 1 + h \otimes h. \tag{$\ast$}$$

### Step 2: PBW filtration and the associated graded

The PBW filtration $U_n(L)$ (span of products of at most $n$ elements of $L$) gi
ves $\mathrm{gr}\, U(L) \cong S(L)$, the symmetric algebra. The coproduct is com
patible with this filtration and induces on $S(L)$ the standard coproduct where
every $x \in L$ is **primitive**: $\bar{\Delta}(x) = x \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes x$.

Suppose $h \neq 0$. Write $h = h_d + h_{d+1} + \cdots + h_N$ with $h_i \in S^i(L
)$ and $h_d \neq 0$, where $d \geq 1$ (since $h \in I = \ker \varepsilon$ means
$h$ has no degree-$0$ part).

---

### Step 3: The leading term is primitive

Extract the **degree-$d$ component** of $(\ast)$ in $\mathrm{gr}\, U(L)$:

- **Left side:** $\bar{\Delta}(h_d)$, which lives in $\bigoplus_{i+j=d} S^i(L) \
otimes S^j(L)$.
- **Right side:** $1 \otimes h_d + h_d \otimes 1$ (from $1 \otimes h$ and $h \ot
imes 1$). The term $h \otimes h$ has lowest degree $2d > d$ (since $d \geq 1$),
so it contributes nothing at degree $d$.

Therefore $\bar{\Delta}(h_d) = 1 \otimes h_d + h_d \otimes 1$, i.e., **$h_d$ is
primitive in $S(L)$**.

**Key fact (characteristic $0$):** The primitive elements of $S(L)$ with the shu
ffle coproduct are exactly $L = S^1(L)$.

*Proof of key fact:* The symmetrization map $\sigma: S(L) \to U(L)$, $x_1 \cdots
 x_n \mapsto \frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\pi \in S_n} x_{\pi(1)} \cdots x_{\pi(n)}$, is an
 isomorphism of Hopf algebras in characteristic $0$. The primitive elements of $
U(L)$ are precisely $L$ (a standard result: if $u \in U(L)$ is primitive, applyi
ng $(\varepsilon \otimes \mathrm{id})$ and degree analysis shows $u \in L$). Hen
ce $\mathrm{Prim}(S(L)) \cong \mathrm{Prim}(U(L)) = L$.

Therefore $d = 1$ and $h_1 \in L$ with $h_1 \neq 0$.

### Step 4: All higher terms are forced by induction

We claim $h_n = \frac{h_1^n}{n!}$ in $S^n(L)$ for all $n \geq 1$.

**Base case** $n=1$: $h_1 = h_1^1/1!$ ✓.

**Inductive step:** Assume $h_k = h_1^k/k!$ for $1 \leq k < n$. Extract the degr
ee-$n$ component of $(\ast)$:

$$\bar{\Delta}(h_n) = 1 \otimes h_n + h_n \otimes 1 + \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} h_i \otim
es h_{n-i} = 1 \otimes h_n + h_n \otimes 1 + \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} \frac{h_1^i}{i!} \
otimes \frac{h_1^{n-i}}{(n-i)!}.$$

Now compute $\bar{\Delta}\!\left(\frac{h_1^n}{n!}\right)$ in $S(L)$. Since $h_1$
 is primitive:

$$\bar{\Delta}\!\left(\frac{h_1^n}{n!}\right) = \frac{1}{n!}(h_1 \otimes 1 + 1 \
otimes h_1)^n = \frac{1}{n!}\sum_{i=0}^{n} \binom{n}{i} h_1^i \otimes h_1^{n-i}
= \sum_{i=0}^{n} \frac{h_1^i}{i!} \otimes \frac{h_1^{n-i}}{(n-i)!}.$$

This equals $1 \otimes \frac{h_1^n}{n!} + \frac{h_1^n}{n!} \otimes 1 + \sum_{i=1
}^{n-1} \frac{h_1^i}{i!} \otimes \frac{h_1^{n-i}}{(n-i)!}$, which is exactly the
 right-hand side of the equation for $h_n = h_1^n/n!$.

Since the coproduct is injective on each homogeneous component (applying $(\vare
psilon \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \bar{\Delta} = \mathrm{id}$), $h_n$ is uniquel
y determined:

$$h_n = \frac{h_1^n}{n!}.$$

### Step 5: Contradiction with finite support

Since $h \in U(L)$, it is a **finite** sum: $h_n = 0$ for all $n > N$ (some $N$)
.

But $h_1 \neq 0$ and $S(L)$ is an integral domain (a polynomial algebra), so $h_
1^n \neq 0$ in $S^n(L)$ for every $n$. In characteristic $0$, $1/n! \neq 0$, so
$h_n = h_1^n/n! \neq 0$ for all $n \geq 1$.

This contradicts the finiteness of $h$. Therefore our assumption $h \neq 0$ is f
alse, so $h = 0$ and $g = 1$.

### Conclusion

The only grouplike element of $U(L)$ is the identity $1$ (which indeed satisfies
 $\Delta(1) = 1 \otimes 1$). The group of grouplike elements is therefore the **
trivial group $\{1\}$**.
