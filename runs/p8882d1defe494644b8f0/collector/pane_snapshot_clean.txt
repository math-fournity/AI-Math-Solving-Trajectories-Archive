## Proof

We evaluate $I = \int_0^{2\pi} e^{e^{ix}}\,dx$.

**Step 1: Series expansion.** For all $z \in \mathbb{C}$, $e^z = \sum_{n=0}^{\in
fty} \frac{z^n}{n!}$, and this series converges absolutely and uniformly on ever
y compact set. Setting $z = e^{ix}$ (which lies on the unit circle, a compact se
t), we may interchange summation and integration:

$$e^{e^{ix}} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{e^{inx}}{n!}.$$

**Step 2: Term-by-term integration.**

$$I = \int_0^{2\pi} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{e^{inx}}{n!}\,dx = \sum_{n=0}^{\in
fty} \frac{1}{n!}\int_0^{2\pi} e^{inx}\,dx.$$

The interchange is justified by uniform convergence of the series on $[0,2\pi]$
(since $|e^{inx}/n!| \le 1/n!$ and $\sum 1/n!$ converges, giving uniform converg
ence by the Weierstrass $M$-test).

**Step 3: Evaluate each integral.**

- For $n = 0$: $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} e^{0}\,dx = \int_0^{2\pi} 1\,dx = 2\p
i$.

- For $n \geq 1$: $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} e^{inx}\,dx = \left[\frac{e^{inx}}
{in}\right]_0^{2\pi} = \frac{e^{2\pi i n}-1}{in} = \frac{1-1}{in} = 0,$

  since $e^{2\pi i n} = 1$ for every integer $n$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.** All terms with $n \geq 1$ vanish, leaving only the $n=0$
 term:

$$I = \frac{1}{0!}\cdot 2\pi + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\cdot 0 = 2\pi.$$

$$\boxed{\int_0^{2\pi} e^{e^{ix}}\,dx = 2\pi}$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
