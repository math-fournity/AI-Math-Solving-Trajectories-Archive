## Proof

Let $H_g$ be the genus-$g$ handlebody (a 3-manifold with boundary $\partial H_g
= \Sigma_g$, the closed orientable surface of genus $g$). The double is
$$W = H_g \cup_{\mathrm{id}} H_g,$$
two copies of $H_g$ glued along $\partial H_g$ via the identity map. Since $H_g$
 is orientable, $W$ is a **closed orientable 3-manifold**.

### Step 1: Fundamental group via Seifert–van Kampen

We have $\pi_1(H_g) \cong F_g = \langle x_1, \dots, x_g\rangle$ (free group on $
g$ generators). The boundary surface has
$$\pi_1(\Sigma_g) = \langle a_1, b_1, \dots, a_g, b_g \mid \textstyle\prod_{i=1}
^g [a_i, b_i] = 1\rangle.$$

The inclusion $\iota: \Sigma_g \hookrightarrow H_g$ induces $\iota_*: \pi_1(\Sig
ma_g) \to F_g$ sending $a_i \mapsto x_i$ and $b_i \mapsto 1$ (the $b_i$-curves b
ound meridian disks in $H_g$).

By Seifert–van Kampen (amalgamated product over $\pi_1(\Sigma_g)$, with **both**
 inclusion maps equal to $\iota_*$ since the gluing is the identity):
$$\pi_1(W) = F_g^{(1)} *_{\pi_1(\Sigma_g)} F_g^{(2)}.$$

Writing $F_g^{(1)} = \langle x_1,\dots,x_g\rangle$ and $F_g^{(2)} = \langle y_1,
\dots,y_g\rangle$, the amalgamation relations are:
- From $a_i$: $x_i = y_i$ for each $i$,
- From $b_i$: $1 = 1$ (trivial).

Therefore
$$\pi_1(W) = \langle x_1, \dots, x_g, y_1, \dots, y_g \mid x_i = y_i \rangle \co
ng F_g.$$

### Step 2: No closed irreducible 3-manifold has nontrivial free fundamental gro
up

Suppose $M$ is a **closed, orientable, irreducible** 3-manifold with $\pi_1(M) \
cong F_r$ ($r \geq 1$). By the Sphere Theorem, irreducibility gives $\pi_2(M) =
0$. Since $\pi_1(M)$ is infinite, the universal cover $\widetilde{M}$ is non-com
pact and simply connected with $\pi_2(\widetilde{M}) = \pi_2(M) = 0$; by Hurewic
z, $H_2(\widetilde{M}) = 0$, and $\widetilde{M}$ is contractible (a standard con
sequence for irreducible 3-manifolds with infinite $\pi_1$). Thus $M$ is aspheri
cal: $M \simeq K(F_r, 1)$.

But $H_3(K(F_r,1)) = H_3(\text{wedge of } r \text{ circles}) = 0$, while $H_3(M)
 = \mathbb{Z}$ (since $M$ is closed and orientable). **Contradiction.** Hence no
 such $M$ exists.

### Step 3: Prime decomposition

By the Kneser–Milnor prime decomposition, $W = M_1 \# \cdots \# M_k$ with each $
M_i$ prime. Then
$$\pi_1(W) = \pi_1(M_1) * \cdots * \pi_1(M_k) \cong F_g.$$

Each prime factor $M_i$ is either:
- $S^3$ ($\pi_1 = 1$, trivial summand), or
- $S^1 \times S^2$ ($\pi_1 = \mathbb{Z}$, the unique prime **reducible** 3-manif
old), or
- irreducible with $\pi_1 \neq 1$.

By Step 2, no irreducible factor can have nontrivial free $\pi_1$. Since $F_g$ i
s a free product of infinite cyclic groups, the only possibility is:
$$W \cong \underbrace{S^1 \times S^2 \# \cdots \# S^1 \times S^2}_{g \text{ copi
es}} = \#_g (S^1 \times S^2).$$

- **$g = 0$:** $H_0 = B^3$, double is $S^3 = \#_0(S^1 \times S^2)$. ✓
- **$g = 1$:** $H_1$ is a solid torus. The identity gluing identifies $\mu_1 \le

- **Homology (general $g$):** $H_1(W) \cong \mathbb{Z}^g$ and by Poincaré dualit

### Conclusion

The double of the genus-$g$ handlebody is the connected sum of $g$ copies of $S^
1 \times S^2$:

$$\boxed{W \cong \#_g (S^1 \times S^2).}$$
