**Answer: Yes** — in the natural $L^{1}$ sense appropriate to Riemann integrable
 functions, every Riemann integrable function can be approximated arbitrarily cl
osely by a linear combination of monotone functions.

---

### Key identification

A function is a **linear combination of monotone functions** if and only if it i
s of **bounded variation**.

*($\Leftarrow$)* By the Jordan decomposition theorem, every function $f$ of boun
ded variation on $[a,b]$ can be written as $f = g - h$ where $g,h$ are increasin
g. This is a linear combination (coefficients $+1,-1$) of two monotone functions
.

*($\Rightarrow$)* Let $f = \sum_{i} c_{i}\,m_{i}$ with each $m_{i}$ monotone. Re
placing every decreasing $m_{i}$ by $-m_{i}$ (absorbing the sign into $c_{i}$),
we may assume all $m_{i}$ are increasing. Splitting the sum into positive and ne
gative coefficients,
$$P \;=\; \sum_{c_{i}>0} c_{i}\,m_{i}, \qquad N \;=\; \sum_{c_{i}<0} (-c_{i})\,m
_{i},$$
both $P$ and $N$ are increasing (positive combinations of increasing functions).
 Hence $f = P - N$ is a difference of two increasing functions, so $f$ has bound
ed variation. $\square$

Thus the question becomes: *are functions of bounded variation dense among Riema
nn integrable functions?*

---

### Step functions are linear combinations of monotone functions

Let $s$ be a step function on $[a,b]$, constant on the subintervals of a partiti
on $a=x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}=b$, with $s\equiv c_{k}$ on $(x_{k-1},x_{k}]$. Th
en
$$s \;=\; c_{0} \;+\; \sum_{k=1}^{n}(c_{k}-c_{k-1})\,\mathbf{1}_{[x_{k},\,b]}.$$
Each indicator $\mathbf{1}_{[x_{k},b]}$ is **decreasing** (hence monotone), and
the constant $c_{0}$ is monotone. Therefore every step function is a linear comb
ination of monotone functions (in particular, of bounded variation).

---

### Step functions are $L^{1}$-dense in the Riemann integrable functions

Let $f$ be Riemann integrable on $[a,b]$ and let $\varepsilon>0$. By the Darboux
 criterion for Riemann integrability, there exists a partition $P=\{a=x_{0}<\cdo
ts<x_{n}=b\}$ such that the upper and lower Darboux sums satisfy
$$U(P,f)-L(P,f) \;<\; \varepsilon.$$
Define the lower step function
$$s(x) \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{n} m_{k}\,\mathbf{1}_{(x_{k-1},x_{k}]}(x), \qquad m_{k}
=\inf_{(x_{k-1},x_{k}]} f.$$
Then $s\le f$, $\int_{a}^{b} s = L(P,f)$, and
$$\|f-s\|_{1} \;=\; \int_{a}^{b}|f-s| \;=\; \int_{a}^{b}(f-s) \;=\; \int_{a}^{b}
f - L(P,f) \;\le\; U(P,f)-L(P,f) \;<\; \varepsilon.$$
Since $s$ is a step function, it is a linear combination of monotone functions.
As $\varepsilon>0$ was arbitrary, $f$ can be approximated arbitrarily closely in
 $L^{1}$ by linear combinations of monotone functions.

---

### Conclusion

Every Riemann integrable function on $[a,b]$ is an $L^{1}$-limit of step functio
ns, and step functions are linear combinations of monotone functions. Equivalent
ly — and more structurally — the linear combinations of monotone functions are e
xactly the functions of bounded variation, and these are $L^{1}$-dense in the sp
ace of Riemann integrable functions (since the even smaller class of step functi
ons already is). Hence **yes**, every Riemann integrable function can be approxi
mated arbitrarily closely (in $L^{1}$) by a linear combination of monotone funct
ions.

---

**Remark (uniform norm).** The answer is sensitive to the choice of topology. Un
der *uniform* approximation the answer would be **no**: the uniform closure of t
he functions of bounded variation is the space of *regulated* functions (those p
ossessing left and right limits at every point), which is a proper subclass of t
he Riemann integrable functions. For instance, $f(x)=\sin(1/x)$ for $x\in(0,1]$,
 $f(0)=0$, is Riemann integrable (bounded, discontinuous only at $0$) but is not
 regulated — the right limit at $0$ does not exist — so it cannot be uniformly a
pproximated by linear combinations of monotone functions. The positive result ab
ove is therefore the natural $L^{1}$ statement.
