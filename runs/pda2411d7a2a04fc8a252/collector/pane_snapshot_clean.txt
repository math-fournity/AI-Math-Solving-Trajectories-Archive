## Solution

The extension $\mathbb{E} = \mathbb{Q}[\sqrt{p_1}, \dots, \sqrt{p_n}]$ is a Galo
is extension of $\mathbb{Q}$, being the splitting field of the separable polynom
ial $\prod_{i=1}^n (x^2 - p_i)$.

**Step 1: Determine the Galois group.**

Each $\sqrt{p_i}$ is a root of $x^2 - p_i$, and the automorphisms act by indepen
dently sending $\sqrt{p_i} \mapsto \pm\sqrt{p_i}$. The extensions $\mathbb{Q}[\s
qrt{p_i}]/\mathbb{Q}$ are linearly disjoint because the classes of $p_1, \dots,
p_n$ are independent in $\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/(\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^2$ (each $p_i
$ is a distinct prime, so no product of a subset of them is a square). Therefore
:

$$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^n,$$

an elementary abelian $2$-group of order $2^n$.

**Step 2: Count subgroups of index 3.**

A subgroup $H \leq G$ of index $3$ would satisfy $|G|/|H| = 3$, i.e., $|H| = 2^n
/3$. But $|G| = 2^n$ is a power of $2$, and $3 \nmid 2^n$ for any $n \geq 0$. He
nce $|H|$ is not an integer, and no such subgroup can exist.

Equivalently, a subgroup of index $3$ corresponds to a surjective homomorphism $
G \to \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$, but since $G$ is a $2$-group, every homomorphic i
mage has order a power of $2$, so no surjection onto a group of order $3$ exists
.

**Conclusion.** The number of subgroups of index $3$ in $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{E}
/\mathbb{Q})$ is:

$$\boxed{0}$$
