## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $(X, \mathcal{D})$ is Hausdorff.

**Key characterization.** A uniform space $(X, \mathcal{D})$ is Hausdorff if and
 only if
$$\bigcap_{D \in \mathcal{D}} D = \Delta := \{(x,x) : x \in X\}.$$
Indeed, if $\bigcap D = \Delta$, then for any $x \neq y$ there exists $D \in \ma
thcal{D}$ with $(x,y) \notin D$, so $y \notin D[x]$; since $D[x]$ is a neighborh
ood of $x$ excluding $y$, the space is Hausdorff. Conversely, if the space is Ha
usdorff and $x \neq y$, there exist entourages $D, E$ with $D[x] \cap E[y] = \em
ptyset$; then $(x,y) \notin D$, so $(x,y) \notin \bigcap D$.

**Suppose, for contradiction, that $(X, \mathcal{D})$ is not Hausdorff.** Then $
\bigcap_{D \in \mathcal{D}} D \supsetneq \Delta$, so there exist distinct points
 $x \neq y$ with
$$(x, y) \in D \quad \text{for all } D \in \mathcal{D}. \tag{$\star$}$$

Since $D^{-1}$ is an entourage whenever $D$ is, condition $(\star)$ also gives $
(x,y) \in D^{-1}$ for all $D$, i.e., $(y,x) \in D$ for all $D \in \mathcal{D}$.

**The singleton $\{x\}$ is complete.** The subspace uniformity on $\{x\}$ has en
tourage $\{(x,x)\}$ (the trace of any $D$ on $\{x\} \times \{x\}$). The only fil
ter on $\{x\}$ is the principal filter $\mathcal{F} = \{\{x\}\}$. This filter is
 Cauchy: for every entourage $D$, $\{x\} \times \{x\} = \{(x,x)\} \subseteq D$ (
since every entourage contains the diagonal). It converges to $x \in \{x\}$: eve
ry neighborhood of $x$ contains $\{x\}$. Hence every Cauchy filter on $\{x\}$ co
nverges in $\{x\}$, so $\{x\}$ is complete.

**The singleton $\{x\}$ is not closed.** A neighborhood of $y$ contains $D[y] =
\{z : (y,z) \in D\}$ for some entourage $D$. By the symmetry observation above,
$(y,x) \in D$ for all $D$, so $x \in D[y]$ for every entourage $D$. Thus $x$ bel
ongs to every neighborhood of $y$, which means $x \in \overline{\{y\}}$ and equi
valently $y \in \overline{\{x\}}$. Since $y \neq x$ and $y \in \overline{\{x\}}
\setminus \{x\}$, the set $\{x\}$ is **not closed**.

**Contradiction.** We have produced a complete subset $\{x\}$ that is not closed
, contradicting the hypothesis that every complete subset is closed.

Therefore $(X, \mathcal{D})$ must be Hausdorff. $\blacksquare$
