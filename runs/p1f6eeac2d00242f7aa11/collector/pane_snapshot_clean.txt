$$0 = \mathrm{tr}(yx) - \mathrm{tr}(xy) = \mathrm{tr}(yx - xy) = \mathrm{tr}(\ma
thrm{id}_V) = \dim V.$$
The first equality uses $\mathrm{tr}(AB) = \mathrm{tr}(BA)$. Since $\mathrm{char

### Two-sided ideals

$$[y, x^i y^j] = i\, x^{i-1} y^j, \qquad [x, x^i y^j] = j\, x^i y^{j-1}.$$

Let $I \neq \{0\}$ be a two-sided ideal. Choose $0 \neq a \in I$ of minimal degr

j=d} c_{ij} x^i y^j$.

- If some term in $a_d$ has $i > 0$: then $[y, a]$ has leading term $\sum_{i+j=d

- If all terms in $a_d$ have $i = 0$ (i.e., $a_d = c_{0,d}\, y^d$ with $c_{0,d}

## (b) Characteristic $p$: the center

**Claim:** $Z(A) = k[x^p, y^p]$.

**Proof.** The commutator identities $[y, x^i y^j] = i\, x^{i-1} y^j$ and $[x, x
^i y^j] = j\, x^i y^{j-1}$ hold in any characteristic. Setting $i = p$ (resp. $j
 = p$):
$$[y, x^p] = p\, x^{p-1} = 0, \qquad [x, y^p] = p\, y^{p-1} = 0,$$
since $\mathrm{char}\, k = p$. Clearly $x^p$ commutes with $x$ and $y^p$ commute
s with $y$. So $x^p, y^p \in Z(A)$, hence $k[x^p, y^p] \subseteq Z(A)$.

For the reverse inclusion, let $z = \sum c_{ij}\, x^i y^j \in Z(A)$.

**Commuting with $x$:** $[x, z] = \sum c_{ij}\, j\, x^i y^{j-1} = 0$. Collecting
 the coefficient of $x^i y^m$: it equals $c_{i,\, m+1}\,(m+1)$. For this to vani
sh for all $i, m$: if $m + 1 \not\equiv 0 \pmod{p}$, then $c_{i,\, m+1} = 0$. So
 $c_{ij} = 0$ unless $j \equiv 0 \pmod{p}$.

**Commuting with $y$:** $[y, z] = \sum c_{ij}\, i\, x^{i-1} y^j = 0$. Similarly,
 $c_{ij} = 0$ unless $i \equiv 0 \pmod{p}$.

Combining: $z = \sum c_{ij}\, x^i y^j$ with $i \equiv 0 \pmod{p}$ and $j \equiv
0 \pmod{p}$, i.e., $z \in k[x^p, y^p]$. $\square$

## (c) Characteristic $p$: irreducible finite-dimensional representations

Let $V = k[t]/(t^p)$, a $p$-dimensional $k$-vector space with basis $\{1, t, \ld
ots, t^{p-1}\}$. Define operators:
$$\rho(x) = \text{multiplication by } \alpha + t, \qquad \rho(y) = \beta + \frac
{d}{dt}.$$

**Verification of the relation $yx - xy = 1$:** For any $f \in k[t]/(t^p)$:
$$(\rho(y)\rho(x) - \rho(x)\rho(y))(f) = (\beta + \tfrac{d}{dt})((\alpha + t)f)
- (\alpha + t)(\beta f + f')$$
$$= \beta(\alpha+t)f + (\alpha+t)f' + f - (\alpha+t)\beta f - (\alpha+t)f' = f.$
$

**Verification of $x^p = a$, $y^p = b$ (as scalars):** In $\mathrm{char}\, k = p

**Irreducibility:** Let $W \subseteq V$ be a nonzero $A$-submodule. Since $\rho(
x) - \alpha = \text{mult by } t$ and $\rho(y) - \beta = \frac{d}{dt}$, the space
 $W$ is stable under multiplication by $t$ and under $\frac{d}{dt}$. Take $0 \ne
q w \in W$, write $w = c_j t^j + \cdots + c_{p-1} t^{p-1}$ with $c_j \neq 0$ and

\neq 0$ in $k$), giving a nonzero constant in $W$. Multiplying by $t^i$ for $i =

### Step 3: These are all the irreducible finite-dimensional representations.

Let $V$ be an irreducible finite-dimensional $A$-module. By Schur's lemma, $Z(A)
 = k[x^p, y^p]$ acts on $V$ via a character, i.e., $x^p$ acts as some scalar $a
\in k$ and $y^p$ as some scalar $b \in k$. (Since $k$ is algebraically closed, e
very irreducible representation of the commutative algebra $k[x^p, y^p]$ is 1-di
mensional, given by a maximal ideal $(x^p - a, y^p - b)$.)

Let $\mathfrak{m} = (x^p - a, y^p - b) \subseteq Z(A)$. Then $V$ is an irreducib

The representation $\rho_{a,b}$ constructed above gives a surjective homomorphis
m $A/\mathfrak{m}A \to M_p(k)$ (surjective by **Burnside's theorem**: over an al

{x^i y^j : 0 \leq i, j \leq p-1\}$ over $k$), so this is an isomorphism:
$$A/\mathfrak{m}A \cong M_p(k).$$

The algebra $M_p(k)$ has a unique irreducible module (up to isomorphism), namely

somorphic to the representation $\rho_{a,b}$ constructed above.
