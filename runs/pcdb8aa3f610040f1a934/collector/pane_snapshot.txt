Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the condition "$x$ maps to
zero in $M/\mathfrak{m}M$ for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$" forces $x = 0$
.

**Answer: No, $x$ is not necessarily $0$.**

**Counterexample.**

Let $k$ be algebraically closed, $R = k[t]$, which is an integral domain and a f
initely generated $k$-algebra. Let $M = R/(t^2) = k[t]/(t^2)$, which is a finite
ly generated (cyclic) $R$-module. Set $x = \bar{t} \in M$, which is nonzero sinc
e $t \notin (t^2)$ in $k[t]$.

Since $k$ is algebraically closed, the maximal ideals of $R = k[t]$ are $\mathfr
ak{m}_a = (t - a)$ for $a \in k$.

**Case 1: $a \neq 0$.** Then $t - a$ is a unit in $M = k[t]/(t^2)$. Indeed,
$$(t - a)\left(-\tfrac{1}{a} - \tfrac{t}{a^2}\right) = -\tfrac{t}{a} - \tfrac{t^
2}{a^2} + 1 + \tfrac{t}{a} = 1 - \tfrac{t^2}{a^2} \equiv 1 \pmod{t^2}.$$
Hence $\mathfrak{m}_a M = M$, so $x \in \mathfrak{m}_a M$ trivially, and $x$ map
s to $0$ in $M/\mathfrak{m}_a M = 0$.

**Case 2: $a = 0$, i.e., $\mathfrak{m} = (t)$.** Then $\mathfrak{m}M = t \cdot k
[t]/(t^2) = k\cdot\bar{t}$, which contains $x = \bar{t}$. The quotient $M/\mathf
rak{m}M \cong k[t]/(t) \cong k$, and the image of $x = \bar{t}$ in this quotient
 is $0$.

**Conclusion.** The element $x = \bar{t} \neq 0$ maps to zero in $M/\mathfrak{m}
M$ for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ of $R$, yet $x \neq 0$ in $M$.

**Why the naive expectation fails.** One might hope to use the Nullstellensatz:
since $R$ is a finitely generated domain over an algebraically closed field, the
 Jacobson radical $J(R) = \bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}} \mathfrak{m} = \sqrt{(0)} = (0)
$. For a **free** module $M = R^n$, one would then get $\bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}} \
mathfrak{m}M = (\bigcap \mathfrak{m})M = J(R)M = 0$, and the result would hold.
However, for a general finitely generated module $M$ (which may have torsion), t
he equality $\bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}} \mathfrak{m}M = J(R)\cdot M$ **fails**. The
torsion submodule of $M$ supported at a single closed point is annihilated by th
e corresponding maximal ideal (hence invisible in $M/\mathfrak{m}M$ at that poin
t) and becomes all of $M$ locally at every other point (where $\mathfrak{m}$ act
s as a unit), making it invisible in every fiber $M/\mathfrak{m}M$.
