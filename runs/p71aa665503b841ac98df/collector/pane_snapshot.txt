## Answer

**Yes**, the Sobolev inequality is valid for a cube $Q$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ (in th
e maximum norm). Below is the complete proof.

---

### Setup

Let $Q \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ be a cube in the maximum norm, i.e., $Q = \prod_{i=
1}^{n}[a_i, b_i]$ with $a_i < b_i$. Let $1 \le p < n$ and $p^* = \frac{np}{n-p}$
. We must show there exists $C > 0$ such that

$$\|u\|_{L^{p^*}(Q)} \le C\,\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(Q)} \quad \text{for all } u \in W^{1,
p}(Q).$$

The proof proceeds in four steps.

---

### Step 1: $Q$ is a bounded Lipschitz domain

A cube in the maximum norm is a bounded convex polyhedron. Its boundary $\partia
l Q$ consists of finitely many flat faces, each of which is locally the graph of
 an affine (hence Lipschitz) function. Therefore $Q$ is a **bounded Lipschitz do
main**.

---

### Step 2: Bounded Lipschitz domains are extension domains

By the **Calderón–Stein extension theorem**, every bounded Lipschitz domain $\Om
ega \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ admits a bounded linear extension operator

$$E : W^{1,p}(\Omega) \to W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)$$

such that $(Eu)|_\Omega = u$ and

$$\|Eu\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)} \le C_1\,\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}$$

for a constant $C_1 = C_1(\Omega, p, n) > 0$.

---

### Step 3: Sobolev inequality on $\mathbb{R}^n$

For $1 \le p < n$, the **Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev inequality** on $\mathbb{R}
^n$ states that for every $v \in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)$,

$$\|v\|_{L^{p^*}(\mathbb{R}^n)} \le C_2\,\|\nabla v\|_{L^p(\mathbb{R}^n)} \le C_
2\,\|v\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)},$$

where $C_2 = C_2(n, p) > 0$ is a universal constant depending only on $n$ and $p
$.

---

### Step 4: Composition

Given any $u \in W^{1,p}(Q)$, set $v = Eu \in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)$. Since $v|_
Q = u$,

$$\|u\|_{L^{p^*}(Q)} = \|v\|_{L^{p^*}(Q)} \le \|v\|_{L^{p^*}(\mathbb{R}^n)} \le
C_2\,\|v\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)} \le C_1 C_2\,\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(Q)}.$$

Setting $C = C_1 C_2 > 0$, we obtain

$$\boxed{\|u\|_{L^{p^*}(Q)} \le C\,\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(Q)} \quad \text{for all } u \i
n W^{1,p}(Q).}$$

The constant $C$ depends on $n$, $p$, and the geometry of $Q$ (via the extension
 constant $C_1$), but is independent of $u$.

---

### Conclusion

The Sobolev inequality $\|u\|_{L^{p^*}(Q)} \le C\,\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(Q)}$ **holds**
for a cube $Q$ in the maximum norm, for all $1 \le p < n$. The essential reason
is that a cube is a bounded Lipschitz (hence extension) domain, so the Sobolev e
mbedding on $\mathbb{R}^n$ can be pulled back to $Q$ via the extension operator.
