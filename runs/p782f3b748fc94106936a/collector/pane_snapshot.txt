## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $a_n = 0$ for every $n$.

**Key substitution.** Set $w = e^{2\pi i z}$. Since $e^{2\pi i n z} = (e^{2\pi i
 z})^n = w^n$, the series becomes

$$f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n w^n =: g(w), \qquad w = e^{2\pi i z}.$$

So $g(w) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n w^n$ is a power series in $w$ centered at the o
rigin, and $f(z) = g(e^{2\pi i z})$.

**The image $e^{2\pi i U}$ is a non-empty open set.** The map $\varphi(z) = e^{2
\pi i z}$ is a non-constant entire function, hence an open map. Since $U \subset
eq \mathbb{C}$ is non-empty and open, $\Omega := \varphi(U) = e^{2\pi i U}$ is a
 non-empty open subset of $\mathbb{C}^* \subseteq \mathbb{C}$.

**The series converges on $\Omega$.** By hypothesis, $\sum a_n e^{2\pi i nz}$ co
nverges at every $z \in U$, so $\sum a_n w^n$ converges at every $w \in \Omega$.

**$\Omega$ lies inside the disk of convergence.** Let $R \in [0, \infty]$ be the
 radius of convergence of $\sum a_n w^n$. The set of convergence is $\{|w| < R\}
 \cup T$ for some $T \subseteq \{|w| = R\}$. Since $\Omega$ is open and containe
d in this set, and the circle $\{|w|=R\}$ has empty interior, the intersection

$$V := \Omega \cap \{|w| < R\}$$

is a **non-empty open set**. (If $R = 0$, the series converges only at $w=0$, fo
rcing $\Omega = \{0\}$, contradicting that $\Omega$ is open and non-empty; so $R
 > 0$.)

**Apply the identity theorem.** The function $g$ is analytic (holomorphic) on th
e disk $D = \{|w| < R\}$, which is connected. On $V \subseteq D$, which is a non
-empty open set (hence has limit points in $D$), we have

$$g(w) = f(z) = 0 \quad \text{for all } w = e^{2\pi i z} \in V.$$

By the **identity theorem** for holomorphic functions, $g \equiv 0$ on $D$.

**Conclusion.** Since $g(w) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n w^n \equiv 0$ on the disk $D
$, all Taylor coefficients vanish:

$$a_n = \frac{g^{(n)}(0)}{n!} = 0 \quad \text{for every } n \geq 0. \qquad \blac
ksquare$$
