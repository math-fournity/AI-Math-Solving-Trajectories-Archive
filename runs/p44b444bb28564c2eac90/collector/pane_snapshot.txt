**Theorem.** Let $M$ be a closed, translation-invariant linear subspace of $L^2(
\mathbb{R}^n)$. Then there exists a measurable set $E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n$ such
 that
$$M=\{f\in L^2(\mathbb{R}^n):\hat f=0\text{ a.e. on }E\}.$$

**Proof.**

**Step 1 (Fourier transform reduces translation to modulation).** The Fourier tr
ansform $\mathcal F:f\mapsto\hat f$ is a unitary operator on $L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)$
 (Plancherel). A direct computation gives
$$\widehat{f_a}(y)=\int f(x+a)e^{-2\pi i x\cdot y}\,dx=e^{2\pi i a\cdot y}\hat f
(y).$$
Let $\widehat M:=\mathcal F(M)=\{\hat f:f\in M\}$. Then $\widehat M$ is a closed
 subspace of $L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)$, and translation-invariance of $M$ is equivalen
t to:
$$g\in\widehat M\implies e^{2\pi i a\cdot y}\,g\in\widehat M\quad\text{for all }
a\in\mathbb{R}^n.$$
Let $P$ be the orthogonal projection of $L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)$ onto $\widehat M$. T
he invariance says $P$ commutes with the multiplication operator $M_{e^{2\pi i a
\cdot y}}$ for every $a\in\mathbb{R}^n$.

**Step 2 ($P$ commutes with multiplication by every bounded measurable function)
.** By linearity, $P$ commutes with $M_\psi$ for every trigonometric polynomial
$\psi$ (finite linear combinations of characters $e^{2\pi i a\cdot y}$).

We extend this to all $\phi\in L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$. Take $g\in L^2\cap L^\in
fty$ with compact support $K$. The algebra of trigonometric polynomials restrict

 by Stone–Weierstrass it is dense in $C(K)$, hence in $L^2(K)$. So there exist t
rigonometric polynomials $\psi_j\to\phi|_K$ in $L^2(K)$. Since $g$ is bounded an
d supported in $K$,
$$\|\psi_j g-\phi g\|_2^2=\int_K|\psi_j-\phi|^2|g|^2\le\|g\|_\infty^2\|\psi_j-\p
hi\|_{L^2(K)}^2\to0.$$
As $P M_{\psi_j}=M_{\psi_j}P$, we get $P(\psi_j g)=\psi_j P(g)$; passing to the
$L^2$-limit, $P(\phi g)=\phi P(g)$.

Now approximate any $g\in L^2$ by $g_k\in L^2\cap L^\infty$ with compact support
, $g_k\to g$ in $L^2$. Then $\phi g_k\to\phi g$ in $L^2$ and $P(\phi g_k)=\phi P
(g_k)$; taking limits yields $P(\phi g)=\phi P(g)$. Thus
$$P\,M_\phi=M_\phi\,P\quad\text{for all }\phi\in L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n).$$

**Step 3 ($P$ is multiplication by a characteristic function).** Let $B_k=\{y:|y

k$, using Step 2 with $\phi=\chi_A$:
$$P(\chi_A)=P(\chi_A\chi_{B_k})=\chi_A\,P(\chi_{B_k})=\chi_A h_k.$$
For $A\subseteq B_k$ we also have $P(\chi_A)=\chi_A h_{k+1}$, so $h_k=h_{k+1}$ a
.e. on $B_k$. The $h_k$ are therefore consistent, and $h(y):=h_k(y)$ for $y\in B
_k$ is a well-defined measurable function.

For any simple function $s=\sum_i c_i\chi_{A_i}$ supported in $B_k$, $P(s)=s\,h_
k$ by linearity; by density, $P(g)=g\,h_k$ for every $g\in L^2$ supported in $B_
k$, hence $P(g)=g\,h$ for every such $g$.

Since $P$ is a projection ($P^2=P$): for $g$ supported in $B_k$,
$$g\,h=P(g)=P^2(g)=P(g\,h)=(g\,h)\,h=g\,h^2,$$
