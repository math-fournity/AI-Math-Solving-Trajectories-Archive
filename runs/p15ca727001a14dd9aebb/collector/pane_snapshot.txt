# Necessary Conditions for Nash Equilibrium in a Continuous-Time Dynamic Game

## Setup

We have $N$ players over a finite horizon $[0,T]$. The state evolves according t
o

$$x'(t) = f\bigl(t, x(t), u^1(t), \ldots, u^N(t)\bigr), \qquad x(0)=x_0,$$

and player $i$'s payoff is

$$J^i(u^1, \ldots, u^N) = \int_0^T g^i\bigl(t, x(t), u^1(t), \ldots, u^N(t)\bigr
)\,dt + G^i\bigl(x(T)\bigr).$$

A strategy profile $(u^{1*}, \ldots, u^{N*})$ is a **Nash equilibrium** if for e
very player $i$ and every admissible control $\tilde u^i$,

$$J^i(u^{1*}, \ldots, u^{N*}) \;\ge\; J^i(u^{1*}, \ldots, u^{i-1*}, \tilde u^i,
u^{i+1*}, \ldots, u^{N*}).$$

That is, each player's strategy is a best response to the others.

## Derivation of Necessary Conditions

Fix player $i$. At a Nash equilibrium, $u^{i*}$ maximizes $J^i$ **given** the ot
her players' controls $u^{-i*} = (u^{j*})_{j\ne i}$. This is a single-agent opti
mal control problem with the "frozen" opponents $u^{-i*}$ appearing as exogenous
 inputs. Applying the **Pontryagin Maximum Principle** to each player's best-res
ponse problem yields the necessary conditions.

### Step 1 — The Hamiltonian for player $i$

Define player $i$'s Hamiltonian (with costate $\lambda^i \in \mathbb{R}^n$):

$$H^i\bigl(t, x, u^1, \ldots, u^N, \lambda^i\bigr) = g^i\bigl(t, x, u^1, \ldots,
 u^N\bigr) + \bigl(\lambda^i\bigr)^\top f\bigl(t, x, u^1, \ldots, u^N\bigr).$$

### Step 2 — State equation (common to all players)

The state trajectory is shared by all players, so there is a single state equati
on evaluated at the equilibrium profile:

$$\boxed{\;x'^*(t) = f\bigl(t, x^*(t), u^{1*}(t), \ldots, u^{N*}(t)\bigr) = \fra
c{\partial H^i}{\partial \lambda^i}, \qquad x^*(0) = x_0.\;}$$

### Step 3 — Costate (adjoint) equation for each player

Each player $i$ has their **own** costate $\lambda^i(t)$, evolving according to:

$$\boxed{\;\lambda^{i\,\prime}(t) = -\,\frac{\partial H^i}{\partial x}\Bigl(t, x
^*(t), u^{1*}(t), \ldots, u^{N*}(t), \lambda^i(t)\Bigr).\;}$$

In expanded form:

$$\lambda^{i\,\prime}(t) = -\,\frac{\partial g^i}{\partial x} - \bigl(\lambda^i\
bigr)^\top \frac{\partial f}{\partial x},$$

where the partials are evaluated at the equilibrium. Note that $\partial f/\part
ial x$ and $\partial g^i/\partial x$ depend on **all** players' controls (throug
h $f$ and $g^i$), so each player's costate dynamics are coupled to the others' s
trategies.

### Step 4 — Stationarity / maximum condition (best-response optimality)

For each player $i$, the equilibrium control $u^{i*}(t)$ must maximize $H^i$ wit
h respect to $u^i$ (holding $u^{-i*}$ fixed). For an **interior** optimum with u
nconstrained or open controls:

$$\boxed{\;\frac{\partial H^i}{\partial u^i}\Bigl(t, x^*(t), u^{1*}(t), \ldots,
u^{N*}(t), \lambda^i(t)\Bigr) = 0, \qquad i = 1, \ldots, N.\;}$$

More generally (when controls lie in a constraint set $U^i$):

$$u^{i*}(t) \in \arg\max_{u^i \in U^i} H^i\bigl(t, x^*(t), u^{1*}(t), \ldots, u^
i, \ldots, u^{N*}(t), \lambda^i(t)\bigr).$$

This is the **coupling condition**: each player's first-order condition depends
on the others' equilibrium controls, producing a system of $N$ simultaneous opti
mality conditions.

### Step 5 — Transversality (terminal) condition

Since the horizon is fixed at $T$ and the terminal payoff is $G^i(x(T))$, the co
state terminal condition for each player is:

$$\boxed{\;\lambda^i(T) = \nabla_x G^i\bigl(x^*(T)\bigr), \qquad i = 1, \ldots,
N.\;}$$

(If the terminal state were free with no terminal cost, this would be $\lambda^i
(T)=0$; here the terminal payoff $G^i$ pins down the terminal costate.)

## Summary: The Complete System of Necessary Conditions

A Nash equilibrium $(u^{1*}, \ldots, u^{N*})$ with associated state $x^*$ and co
states $\lambda^1, \ldots, \lambda^N$ must satisfy, for $i = 1, \ldots, N$:

| Condition | Equation |
|---|---|
| **State dynamics** | $x'^*(t) = f(t, x^*, u^{1*}, \ldots, u^{N*})$, $\;x^*(0)
= x_0$ |
| **Costate dynamics** | $\lambda^{i\,\prime}(t) = -\partial H^i/\partial x\big|
_{*}$ |
| **Stationarity** | $\partial H^i/\partial u^i\big|_{*} = 0$ (interior case), o
r $u^{i*} \in \arg\max_{u^i \in U^i} H^i$ |
| **Transversality** | $\lambda^i(T) = \nabla G^i(x^*(T))$ |

where

$$H^i = g^i(t, x, u^1, \ldots, u^N) + (\lambda^i)^\top f(t, x, u^1, \ldots, u^N)
.$$

## Key Structural Remarks

1. **One state, $N$ costates.** There is a single shared state $x(t)$ but each p
layer carries an independent costate $\lambda^i(t)$ reflecting their own valuati
on of the state. The system is a **two-point boundary-value problem** with $n$ s
tate variables and $nN$ costate variables.

2. **Coupling through the Hamiltonian.** The stationarity condition $\partial H^
i/\partial u^i = 0$ for player $i$ depends on $u^{-i*}$ because both $g^i$ and $
f$ depend on all controls. Thus the $N$ optimality conditions are **simultaneous
** and must be solved together — this is the essence of the Nash structure, as o
pposed to a Stackelberg (leader–follower) hierarchy.

3. **The adjoint equation is also coupled.** Since $\partial f/\partial x$ and $
\partial g^i/\partial x$ are evaluated at the full equilibrium profile, each $\l
ambda^i$'s dynamics depend on all players' controls. The costates are not indepe
ndent across players.

4. **These are necessary, not sufficient.** They follow from the Pontryagin Maxi
mum Principle applied to each player's best-response problem under the regularit
y assumptions (continuous differentiability of $f$, $g^i$, $G^i$; interiority of
 controls; appropriate constraint qualifications). Sufficiency requires addition
al concavity/convexity structure (e.g., $H^i$ concave in $(x, u^i)$ and $G^i$ co
ncave in $x$).

5. **Open-loop vs. feedback.** The conditions above are stated for **open-loop**
 Nash equilibria, where each $u^i(t)$ is a function of time alone. For **feedbac
k** (closed-loop / Markovian) Nash equilibria, where strategies are $u^i(t, x(t)
)$, the costate is replaced by the gradient of player $i$'s value function $V^i(
t, x)$, and the necessary conditions take the form of a system of coupled **Hami
lton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) PDEs**:

$$-\frac{\partial V^i}{\partial t} = \max_{u^i \in U^i}\Bigl[g^i\bigl(t, x, u^i,
 u^{-i*}(t,x)\bigr) + \nabla_x V^i \cdot f\bigl(t, x, u^i, u^{-i*}(t,x)\bigr)\Bi
gr],$$

with terminal condition $V^i(T, x) = G^i(x)$, and the Nash property requiring ea
ch $u^{i*}(t,x)$ to achieve the maximum on the right-hand side simultaneously fo
r all $i$.
