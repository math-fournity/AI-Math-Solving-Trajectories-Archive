## Solution

The integrand factors as

$$\frac{\cos z}{z^7-2z^6}=\frac{\cos z}{z^6(z-2)}.$$

The singularities are $z=0$ (pole of order $6$) and $z=2$ (simple pole).

**Which singularities lie inside $\gamma$?** The contour is $|z-\tfrac14|=\tfrac
12$, enclosing the disk $\{z:|z-\tfrac14|<\tfrac12\}$, i.e. the interval $(-\tfr
ac14,\tfrac34)$ on the real axis. Hence $z=0$ is inside, while $z=2$ is outside.

By the residue theorem,

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_\gamma \frac{\cos z}{z^6(z-2)}\,dz = \operatorname{Res}_{
z=0}\frac{\cos z}{z^6(z-2)}.$$

**Computing the residue at $z=0$.** For the order-$6$ pole,

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}=\frac{1}{5!}\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{d^5}{dz^5}\!\left[\fra
c{\cos z}{z-2}\right].$$

Equivalently, the residue equals the coefficient of $z^5$ in the Laurent expansi
on of $\frac{\cos z}{z^6(z-2)}$, i.e. the coefficient of $z^5$ in $g(z):=\frac{\
cos z}{z-2}$.

Expand both factors around $z=0$:

$$\cos z = 1-\frac{z^2}{2}+\frac{z^4}{24}-\cdots,\qquad \frac{1}{z-2}=-\frac{1}{
2}\cdot\frac{1}{1-z/2}=-\sum_{k\ge 0}\frac{z^k}{2^{k+1}}.$$

The coefficient of $z^5$ in the product comes from pairs $(j,k)$ with $j+k=5$ an
d $j$ even:

| $j$ (from $\cos z$) | $k$ (from $\frac1{z-2}$) | contribution |
|---|---|---|
| $0$: $1$ | $5$: $-\frac{1}{2^6}=-\frac1{64}$ | $-\frac1{64}$ |
| $2$: $-\frac12$ | $3$: $-\frac{1}{2^4}=-\frac1{16}$ | $+\frac1{32}$ |
| $4$: $\frac1{24}$ | $1$: $-\frac{1}{2^2}=-\frac14$ | $-\frac1{96}$ |

Summing:

$$-\frac1{64}+\frac1{32}-\frac1{96}=\frac{-3+6-2}{192}=\frac{1}{192}.$$

Therefore

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{\cos z}{z^6(z-2)}=\frac{1}{192},$$

and

$$\boxed{\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}\frac{\cos z}{z^7-2z^6}\,dz=\frac{1}{192}}
.$$
