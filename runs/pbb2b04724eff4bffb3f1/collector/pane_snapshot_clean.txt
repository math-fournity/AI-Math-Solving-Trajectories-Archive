Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether, given that $\sum n^q a_n$
converges for every $q > 0$, the series $\sum n^p \log(n)\sqrt{a_n}$ converges f
or $p \ge 1$.

**Answer: Yes, such a $q$ exists.** In fact, any $q > 2p+1$ suffices.

## Proof

**Hypothesis:** $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^q a_n$ converges for every $q > 0$ (i.e.,
 for arbitrarily small positive $q$, hence for all $q > 0$).

**Goal:** For any given $p \ge 1$, show $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^p \log(n)\sqrt{a_
n}$ converges.

---

**Step 1: Choose $q$.**

Set $q = 2p + 2$. Since $p \ge 1$, we have $q = 2p+2 \ge 4 > 0$, so by hypothesi
s the series
$$S_1 := \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^{2p+2}\, a_n$$
converges.

**Step 2: Factor the summand for Cauchy–Schwarz.**

Observe the algebraic identity:
$$n^p \log(n)\,\sqrt{a_n} \;=\; \underbrace{\sqrt{n^{2p+2}\,a_n}}_{=\,n^{p+1}\sq
rt{a_n}}\;\cdot\;\underbrace{\frac{\log n}{n}}_{=\,n^{-1}\log n}.$$

Indeed, $\sqrt{n^{2p+2}a_n}\cdot\frac{\log n}{n} = n^{p+1}\sqrt{a_n}\cdot\frac{\
log n}{n} = n^p\log(n)\,\sqrt{a_n}$. ✓

**Step 3: Apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.**

$$\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^p \log(n)\,\sqrt{a_n}\right)^{\!2} \;\le\; \left(\
sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^{2p+2}\,a_n\right)\!\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\log^2
n}{n^2}\right).$$

**Step 4: Verify both factors on the right-hand side are finite.**

- **First factor:** $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^{2p+2}\,a_n = S_1 < \infty$ by the hy
pothesis (applied with $q = 2p+2 > 0$) and $a_n \ge 0$.

- **Second factor:** $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\log^2 n}{n^2}$ converges. To se
e this, note that for any $\varepsilon \in (0,1)$ and all sufficiently large $n$
, $\log^2 n \le n^{\varepsilon}$, so $\frac{\log^2 n}{n^2} \le \frac{1}{n^{2-\va
repsilon}}$. Choosing $\varepsilon < 1$ gives $2 - \varepsilon > 1$, so $\sum \f
rac{1}{n^{2-\varepsilon}}$ converges (p-series with exponent $> 1$). By comparis
on, $\sum \frac{\log^2 n}{n^2} < \infty$.

(Equivalently, the integral test gives $\int_1^{\infty} \frac{\log^2 x}{x^2}\,dx
 = 2 < \infty$.)

**Step 5: Conclude.**

Since both factors are finite and $a_n \ge 0$ (so all terms are non-negative), t
he Cauchy–Schwarz inequality yields:
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^p \log(n)\,\sqrt{a_n} \;\le\; \sqrt{S_1 \cdot \sum_{n=1}
^{\infty}\frac{\log^2 n}{n^2}} \;<\; \infty.$$

(The $n=1$ term vanishes since $\log 1 = 0$, so there is no issue at the lower l
imit.)

---

**Remark on the choice of $q$.** The Cauchy–Schwarz factorization $n^p\log(n)\sq
rt{a_n} = \sqrt{n^q a_n}\cdot n^{p-q/2}\log(n)$ requires $\sum n^{2p-q}\log^2(n)
 < \infty$, i.e., $2p - q < -1$, i.e., $q > 2p+1$. Any such $q > 0$ works, and t
he hypothesis guarantees convergence for all $q > 0$, so the choice is always av
ailable. $\blacksquare$
