The function $f$ is $2$-periodic: $f(x+2)=f(x)$ for all $x\ge 0$, with one perio

$$
f(x)=\begin{cases}x & 0\le x<1\\ 2-x & 1\le x<2.\end{cases}

### Step 1. Reduce to one period

Write the integral as a sum over periods:

$$
\int_0^{2n} f(x)e^{-x}\,dx=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{2k}^{2k+2}f(x)e^{-x}\,dx.
$$

Substituting $x=t+2k$ and using $f(t+2k)=f(t)$,

$$
\int_{2k}^{2k+2}f(x)e^{-x}\,dx=\int_0^2 f(t)\,e^{-(t+2k)}\,dt=e^{-2k}\int_0^2 f(
t)e^{-t}\,dt.
$$

Let $I:=\int_0^2 f(t)e^{-t}\,dt$. Then

$$
\int_0^{2n}f(x)e^{-x}\,dx=I\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e^{-2k}=I\cdot\frac{1-e^{-2n}}{1-e^{-
2}}.
$$

Since $e^{-2n}\to 0$,

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^{2n}f(x)e^{-x}\,dx=\frac{I}{1-e^{-2}}.
$$

### Step 2. Compute $I$

$$
I=\int_0^1 x\,e^{-x}\,dx+\int_1^2(2-x)e^{-x}\,dx.
$$

**First piece.** Integration by parts gives $\int x e^{-x}dx=-xe^{-x}-e^{-x}$, s
o

$$
\int_0^1 x e^{-x}\,dx=\bigl[-xe^{-x}-e^{-x}\bigr]_0^1=(-e^{-1}-e^{-1})-(-1)=1-2e
^{-1}.
$$

**Second piece.** Substitute $u=2-x$ ($dx=-du$, $x:1\to 2$ becomes $u:1\to 0$):

$$
\int_1^2(2-x)e^{-x}\,dx=\int_1^0 u\,e^{-(2-u)}(-du)=e^{-2}\int_0^1 u\,e^{u}\,du.
$$

Integration by parts: $\int u e^u du = ue^u-e^u$, hence

$$
\int_0^1 u e^u\,du=\bigl[ue^u-e^u\bigr]_0^1=(e-e)-(0-1)=1.
$$

So $\int_1^2(2-x)e^{-x}\,dx=e^{-2}$.

Therefore

$$
I=1-2e^{-1}+e^{-2}=\bigl(1-e^{-1}\bigr)^2.
$$

### Step 3. Take the limit

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^{2n}f(x)e^{-x}\,dx
=\frac{(1-e^{-1})^2}{1-e^{-2}}
=\frac{(1-e^{-1})^2}{(1-e^{-1})(1+e^{-1})}

$$
