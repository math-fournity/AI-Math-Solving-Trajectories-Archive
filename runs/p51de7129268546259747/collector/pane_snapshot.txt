$$E(y) ;=; \zeta(s) ;+; \tfrac{1}{2},N^{-s} ;-; \sum_{n=1}^{N} n^{-s},$$

where $N = \lfloor y/\pi\rfloor \sim y/\pi \to \infty$ as $y\to\infty$.

Step 1. Euler–Maclaurin summation formula

The classical Euler–Maclaurin formula (see Titchmarsh, The Theory of the Riemann
Zeta-Function, §2.9) gives, for $\Re(s)>0$, $s\neq 1$, and any positive integer
$N$:

$$\zeta(s) ;=; \sum_{n=1}^{N} n^{-s} ;+; \frac{N^{1-s}}{s-1} ;-; \frac{1}
{2},N^{-s} ;+; O!\bigl(|s|,N^{-\sigma-1}\bigr), \tag{1}$$

where $\sigma = \Re(s)$. The sign of the $\frac{1}{2}N^{-s}$ term is negative:
this can be verified numerically (e.g., $s=2$, $N=10$: $\zeta(2)-\sum_{n=1}^{10}
n^{-2} \approx 0.0951$, while $N^{1-s}/(s-1) = 0.1$ and $0.1 - 0.005 = 0.095$
matches to high precision) and follows from the standard derivation via

$$\zeta(s) - \sum_{n=1}^{N}n^{-s} = \frac{N^{1-s}}{s-1} - \frac{N^{-s}}{2} -
\sum_{k\ge 1}\frac{B_{2k}}{(2k)!},f^{(2k-1)}(N) + \cdots$$

with $f(x)=x^{-s}$.

Step 2. Substitution into $E(y)$

Substituting (1) into $E(y)$:

$$E(y) = \left[\sum_{n=1}^{N} n^{-s} + \frac{N^{1-s}}{s-1} - \frac{1}{2}N^{-s} +
O(|s|,N^{-\sigma-1})\right] + \frac{1}{2}N^{-s} - \sum_{n=1}^{N} n^{-s}.$$

The partial sums cancel, and the $\pm\frac{1}{2}N^{-s}$ terms cancel, leaving:

$$E(y) = \frac{N^{1-s}}{s-1} + O!\bigl(|s|,N^{-\sigma-1}\bigr). \tag{2}$$

Step 3. Estimating both terms

Set $s = \tfrac{1}{2}+iy$, so $\sigma = \tfrac{1}{2}$, $|s| \sim y$, and $N =
\lfloor y/\pi\rfloor \sim y/\pi$.

Main term:

$$\left|\frac{N^{1-s}}{s-1}\right| = \frac{N^{1/2}}{|s-1|} = \frac{N^{1/2}}{|iy
- \tfrac{1}{2}|} = \frac{N^{1/2}}{\sqrt{y^2+\tfrac{1}{4}}} ;\sim; \frac{(y/
\pi)^{1/2}}{y} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi,y}} = O(y^{-1/2}).$$

Remainder term:

$$\bigl|O(|s|,N^{-\sigma-1})\bigr| = O!\bigl(y \cdot N^{-3/2}\bigr) = O!\bigl(y
\cdot (y/\pi)^{-3/2}\bigr) = O(y^{-1/2}).$$

From (2) and the estimates above:

$$|E(y)| = O(y^{-1/2}) ;\longrightarrow; 0 \quad\text{as } y\to\infty.$$
