1. Every prime factor of $p - 1$ lies in $\mathcal{P}$.
2. If $q \in \mathcal{P}$, then every prime factor of $q - 1$ lies in $\mathcal{
P}$.

This is well-defined and finite: condition (2) only adds primes strictly smaller

Set $M = \prod_{q \in \mathcal{P}} q$ (with $M = 1$ if $\mathcal{P} = \emptyset$

cal{P}$, so $\gcd(p, M) = 1$.

- **$n \notin S$:** Since $p \mid n$, we have $\gcd(n, p) \geq p > 1$, so $\gcd(
