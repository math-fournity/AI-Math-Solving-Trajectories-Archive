## Setup

**Key fact:** Over $\mathbb{F}_3$, $x_i^3 = x_i$ for all $x_i \in \mathbb{F}_3$,
 so the ideal $I = (x_1^3-x_1, \dots, x_{2016}^3-x_{2016})$ consists exactly of
polynomials vanishing on $\mathbb{F}_3^{2016}$. The quotient $R/I$ is the ring o
f polynomial functions, with basis = reduced monomials ($e_i \in \{0,1,2\}$), gi
ving $\dim(R/I) = 3^{2016}$.

**Counting valid protocols:** $P$ is valid iff $P - 1 \in I$. Setting $Q = P-1$,
 we need $Q \in I$ with $\deg Q \leq 2015$ (the constant 1 has degree 0, so $\de
g P = \deg Q$ when $Q \neq 0$). Thus:

$$N = |\ker(\phi)|$$

where $\phi: V \to \text{Functions}(\mathbb{F}_3^{2016} \to \mathbb{F}_3)$ is th
e evaluation map on $V = \{\text{polynomials of degree} \leq 2015\}$.

Since $N = 3^{\dim(\ker \phi)}$, we get $v_3(N) = \dim(\ker \phi) = \dim V - \di
m(\text{im}\, \phi)$.

## Computing $\dim V$

Number of monomials of total degree $\leq 2015$ in 2016 variables:

$$\dim V = \binom{2015+2016}{2016} = \binom{4031}{2016}$$

## Computing $\dim(\text{im}\, \phi)$

A function is representable by a degree $\leq 2015$ polynomial iff its **unique
reduced form** has total degree $\leq 2015$. So $\dim(\text{im}\, \phi)$ = numbe
r of $(e_1,\dots,e_{2016}) \in \{0,1,2\}^{2016}$ with $\sum e_i \leq 2015$.

**Symmetry:** The map $e_i \mapsto 2 - e_i$ is a bijection sending $\sum e_i = s
$ to $\sum e_i = 4032 - s$. So:
- Count with $\sum e_i \leq 2015$ = count with $\sum e_i \geq 2017$ (call these
$A$ and $C$, with $A = C$)
- Count with $\sum e_i = 2016$ = $B$

Then $2A + B = 3^{2016}$, so $\dim(\text{im}\,\phi) = A = \frac{3^{2016} - B}{2}
$, where $B = [x^{2016}](1+x+x^2)^{2016}$.

## Computing $v_3(N) \bmod 2011$

$$v_3(N) = \binom{4031}{2016} - \frac{3^{2016} - B}{2}$$

**2011 is prime** (verified: no prime factor $\leq \sqrt{2011} \approx 44.8$).

### Term 1: $\binom{4031}{2016} \bmod 2011$

Base 2011: $4031 = (2, 9)_{2011}$, $2016 = (1, 5)_{2011}$.

By **Lucas' theorem**: $\binom{4031}{2016} \equiv \binom{2}{1}\binom{9}{5} = 2 \
cdot 126 = 252 \pmod{2011}$.

### Term 2: $3^{2016} \bmod 2011$

By **Fermat's little theorem**: $3^{2010} \equiv 1 \pmod{2011}$, so $3^{2016} \e
quiv 3^6 = 729 \pmod{2011}$.

### Term 3: $B \bmod 2011$

$(1+x+x^2)^{2016} = (1+x+x^2)^{2011} \cdot (1+x+x^2)^5$.

By **Freshman's dream** in $\mathbb{F}_{2011}$: $(1+x+x^2)^{2011} \equiv 1 + x^{
2011} + x^{4022} \pmod{2011}$.

Coefficient of $x^{2016} = x^{2011+5}$: only from $x^{2011} \cdot [x^5](1+x+x^2)
^5$.

Computing $[x^5](1+x+x^2)^5$ with $b + 2c = 5$, $a+b+c=5$, $a=c$:

| $c$ | $b$ | $a$ | $\frac{5!}{a!\,b!\,c!}$ |
|-----|-----|-----|--------------------------|
| 0   | 5   | 0   | 1                        |
| 1   | 3   | 1   | 20                       |
| 2   | 1   | 2   | 30                       |

$B \equiv 1 + 20 + 30 = 51 \pmod{2011}$.

### Combining

$$\frac{3^{2016} - B}{2} \equiv \frac{729 - 51}{2} = \frac{678}{2} = 339 \pmod{2
011}$$

$$v_3(N) \equiv 252 - 339 = -87 \equiv 1924 \pmod{2011}$$

## Answer

The remainder when $v_3(N)$ is divided by 2011 is $\boxed{1924}$.
