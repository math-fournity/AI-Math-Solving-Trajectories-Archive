## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $F(0) = 0$ must hold.

**Key tool (Lipschitz truncation preserves $H^1_0$).** If $u \in W^{1,2}_0(\Omeg
a)$ and $T:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ is Lipschitz with $T(0)=0$, then $T\circ u \
in W^{1,2}_0(\Omega)$.

*Proof of tool.* $W^{1,2}_0(\Omega)$ is the closure of $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ in $
W^{1,2}$. Take $u_n\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ with $u_n\to u$ in $W^{1,2}$. Since $
T(0)=0$ and $u_n$ vanishes on a neighborhood of $\partial\Omega$, $T\circ u_n$ a
lso vanishes near $\partial\Omega$, hence $T\circ u_n\in W^{1,2}_0(\Omega)$. By
the Lipschitz property, $\|T\circ u_n - T\circ u\|_{L^2}\le L\|u_n-u\|_{L^2}$ an
d $\nabla(T\circ u_n)=T'(u_n)\nabla u_n$, giving $\|\nabla(T\circ u_n)-\nabla(T\
circ u)\|_{L^2}\to 0$. Thus $T\circ u\in W^{1,2}_0(\Omega)$. $\square$

**Step 1: $0$ belongs to the essential range of $u$.**

Suppose not. Then there exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that $|u(x)|\ge \varepsilon$
 for a.e. $x\in\Omega$. Define the Lipschitz map
$$T(t)=\max\bigl(0,\,\min(t,\varepsilon)\bigr),\qquad T(0)=0.$$
By the tool, $T\circ u\in W^{1,2}_0(\Omega)$. But since $u\ge \varepsilon$ a.e.
(consider $u\ge\varepsilon$; the case $u\le -\varepsilon$ is handled by $-u$), w
e have $T(u(x))=\min(u(x),\varepsilon)=\varepsilon$ a.e., i.e. $T\circ u$ is the
 constant function $\varepsilon$.

A constant function $c$ lies in $W^{1,2}_0(\Omega)$ only if $c=0$, because its t
race on $\partial\Omega$ is $c$, while every $W^{1,2}_0$ function has zero trace
. This forces $\varepsilon=0$, contradicting $\varepsilon>0$.

Hence $0$ is in the essential range of $u$: for every $\delta>0$,
$$\bigl|\{x\in\Omega : |u(x)|<\delta\}\bigr|>0.$$

**Step 2: $F$ vanishes on the essential range of $u$.**

Let $y$ be in the essential range of $u$. If $F(y)\neq 0$, then by continuity of
 $F$ there is a neighborhood $U$ of $y$ on which $F\neq 0$. By definition of ess
ential range, $u^{-1}(U)$ has positive measure, and on this set $F(u(x))\neq 0$,
 contradicting $F(u(x))=0$ a.e. Thus $F\equiv 0$ on the essential range of $u$.

**Conclusion.** Since $0$ lies in the essential range of $u$ (Step 1) and $F$ va
nishes on the essential range (Step 2), we obtain
$$F(0)=0.$$
