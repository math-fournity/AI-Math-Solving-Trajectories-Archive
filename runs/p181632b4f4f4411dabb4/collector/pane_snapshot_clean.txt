## Proof

The point $(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ is uniform on the simplex $\{0 < x_1 < \cdots < x_n
< 1\}$, which has volume $1/n!$, so the joint density is $n!$. Equivalently, $(x
_1,\dots,x_n)$ are the order statistics of $n$ i.i.d. $\operatorname{Uniform}[0,
1]$ random variables.

By linearity of expectation:
$$E[S] = \sum_{i=0}^{n} E\bigl[(x_{i+1}-x_i)\,f(x_{i+1})\bigr].$$

**The $i=n$ term vanishes.** Since $x_{n+1}=1$ and $f(1)=0$:
$$(x_{n+1}-x_n)\,f(x_{n+1}) = (1-x_n)\,f(1) = 0.$$

**The $i=0$ term.** Here $x_{i+1}=x_1$ and $x_0=0$, so the gap is $x_1$. The fir
st order statistic $x_1$ has density $p_1(t) = n(1-t)^{n-1}$ on $[0,1]$. Thus:
$$E[x_1\,f(x_1)] = \int_0^1 t\,f(t)\cdot n(1-t)^{n-1}\,dt = \int_0^1 f(t)\cdot \
frac{n!}{1!\,(n-1)!}\,t\,(1-t)^{n-1}\,dt.$$

**The terms $i=1,\dots,n-1$.** Here $x_{i+1}$ is the $(i+1)$-th order statistic
and $x_i$ is the $i$-th. The joint density of the $i$-th and $(i+1)$-th order st
atistics of $n$ uniform variables is:
$$p_{i,\,i+1}(s,t) = \frac{n!}{(i-1)!\,(n-i-1)!}\,s^{i-1}\,(1-t)^{n-i-1}, \qquad
 0 < s < t < 1,$$
since the factor $(t-s)^{k-j-1}$ with $k-j-1=0$ equals $1$. Therefore:
$$E\bigl[(x_{i+1}-x_i)\,f(x_{i+1})\bigr] = \int_0^1 f(t)\cdot\frac{n!}{(i-1)!\,(
n-i-1)!}\,(1-t)^{n-i-1}\left(\int_0^t (t-s)\,s^{i-1}\,ds\right)dt.$$

The inner integral evaluates to:
$$\int_0^t (t-s)\,s^{i-1}\,ds = \frac{t^{i+1}}{i} - \frac{t^{i+1}}{i+1} = \frac{
t^{i+1}}{i(i+1)}.$$

Since $(i-1)!\cdot i\cdot(i+1) = (i+1)!$, we obtain:
$$E\bigl[(x_{i+1}-x_i)\,f(x_{i+1})\bigr] = \int_0^1 f(t)\cdot\frac{n!}{(i+1)!\,(
n-i-1)!}\,t^{i+1}\,(1-t)^{n-i-1}\,dt.$$

**Combining all terms.** Setting $w_i(t) = \frac{n!}{(i+1)!\,(n-i-1)!}\,t^{i+1}\
,(1-t)^{n-i-1}$, the $i=0$ term also fits this formula ($w_0(t) = \frac{n!}{1!(n
-1)!}\,t\,(1-t)^{n-1} = n\,t\,(1-t)^{n-1}$). Hence:
$$E[S] = \int_0^1 f(t)\,P(t)\,dt, \qquad P(t) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \frac{n!}{(i+1)
!\,(n-i-1)!}\,t^{i+1}\,(1-t)^{n-i-1}.$$

Substituting $j = i+1$ (so $j$ ranges from $1$ to $n$):
$$P(t) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} \frac{n!}{j!\,(n-j)!}\,t^{j}\,(1-t)^{n-j} = \sum_{j=1}^{
n} \binom{n}{j}\,t^{j}\,(1-t)^{n-j}.$$

By the **binomial theorem**, $\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}t^j(1-t)^{n-j} = \bigl(t
+(1-t)\bigr)^n = 1$. The $j=0$ term is $(1-t)^n$, so:
$$\boxed{P(t) = 1 - (1-t)^n.}$$

**Verification of the required properties:**

1. **Polynomial of degree $n$, independent of $f$:** $P(t)=1-(1-t)^n$ is a polyn
omial of degree $n$ depending only on $n$. ✓

2. **$0 \le P(t) \le 1$ for $t\in[0,1]$:** We have $P(0)=0$, $P(1)=1$, and $P'(t
)=n(1-t)^{n-1}\ge 0$ on $[0,1]$, so $P$ is non-decreasing, giving $0\le P(t)\le
1$. ✓

Thus the expected value of the Riemann sum is $\displaystyle\int_0^1 f(t)\,P(t)\
,dt$ where $P(t)=1-(1-t)^n$ is a polynomial of degree $n$, independent of $f$, s
atisfying $0\le P(t)\le 1$ on $[0,1]$. $\blacksquare$
