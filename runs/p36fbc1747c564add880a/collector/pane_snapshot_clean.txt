## Solution

**Setup.** Let $(X_n, Y_n) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$ denote the position of the simple s
ymmetric random walk after $n$ steps, with $(X_0, Y_0) = (0,0)$. At each step th
e walk moves to one of the four lattice neighbors with probability $1/4$. Define
 the sum process
$$S_n := X_n + Y_n, \qquad S_0 = 0.$$

**Reduction to a 1D walk.** At each step:
- moving right $(+1,0)$ or up $(0,+1)$: $S_n \mapsto S_n + 1$, total probability
 $\tfrac{1}{2}$;
- moving left $(-1,0)$ or down $(0,-1)$: $S_n \mapsto S_n - 1$, total probabilit
y $\tfrac{1}{2}$.

Hence $(S_n)$ is a one-dimensional simple symmetric random walk on $\mathbb{Z}$
starting at $0$. The line $y = 1 - x$ is exactly the set $\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}^2
 : x+y = 1\}$, so the first time the 2D walk reaches this line coincides with th
e first passage time
$$\tau := \inf\{n \ge 1 : S_n = 1\}.$$

**Computing $\mathbb{E}[\tau]$.** Let $e_0 := \mathbb{E}[\tau]$ be the expected
time to first reach $+1$ starting from $0$. By translation invariance, the expec
ted time to go from any integer $k$ to $k+1$ is the same value $e_0$.

Apply first-step analysis. From $0$, after one step the walk is at $+1$ (probabi
lity $\tfrac12$, done) or at $-1$ (probability $\tfrac12$). From $-1$, to reach
$+1$ it must first reach $0$ and then reach $+1$; by the strong Markov property
and translation invariance these two legs are independent with expectations $e_0
$ and $e_0$. Therefore
$$e_0 = 1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 0 + \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(e_0 + e_0\bigr) = 1 + e_0.$$

This yields the contradiction $0 = 1$, which is consistent only if
$$e_0 = +\infty.$$

**Recurrence check (the walk does hit the line almost surely).** The 1D symmetri
c walk is recurrent, so $\mathbb{P}(\tau < \infty) = 1$; equivalently the 2D wal
k hits $x+y=1$ with probability $1$. Thus the hitting time is almost surely fini
te yet has infinite expectation — the divergence is genuine, not an artifact of
a transient target.

**Conclusion.** The average waiting time for the 2D lattice random walk starting
 at the origin to first reach the line $y = 1 - x$ is

$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}[\tau] = \infty.}$$
