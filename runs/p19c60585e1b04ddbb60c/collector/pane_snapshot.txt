## Proof

Consider two congruent cubes, one placed on the table (the *lower cube*) and one
 placed directly on top of it, aligned (the *upper cube*). A cube has 6 square f
aces. We count the **distinct square faces visible** when viewing from the four
lateral sides and from above (the bottom rests on the table and is not visible).

**Lower cube.** Its 6 faces are:
- Top face: covered by the upper cube → not visible.
- Bottom face: resting on the table → not visible.
- Four lateral faces (front, back, left, right): all exposed → **4 visible squar
es**.

**Upper cube.** Its 6 faces are:
- Bottom face: resting on the lower cube → not visible.
- Top face: exposed to the sky → visible.
- Four lateral faces (front, back, left, right): all exposed → visible.
- So **5 visible squares** (4 lateral + 1 top).

**Verification by viewing direction.**
- From each of the 4 lateral sides, the viewer sees the lateral face of the lowe
r cube and the lateral face of the upper cube behind/above it: 2 squares per sid
e → 4 × 2 = 8.
- From above, the viewer sees only the top face of the upper cube: 1 square.
- Total = 8 + 1 = 9.

Both methods agree. The total number of squares visible from all sides and from
above is

$$4 + 5 = 9.$$
