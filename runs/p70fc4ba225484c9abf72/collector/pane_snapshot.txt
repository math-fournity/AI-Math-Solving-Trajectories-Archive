## Proof

Since $f$ and $g$ are analytic and never zero on the open unit disc $U$, the log
arithmic derivatives

$$
\frac{f'}{f}, \qquad \frac{g'}{g}
$$

are well-defined and analytic on all of $U$ (the denominator never vanishes, and
 $f',g'$ are analytic as derivatives of analytic functions).

By hypothesis,

$$
\frac{f'}{f}\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right) = \frac{g'}{g}\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)
\quad \text{for every integer } n \ge 5.
$$

The set $S = \left\{\frac{1}{n} : n \ge 5\right\}$ is an infinite subset of $U$,
 and its accumulation point is $0$, which lies in $U$. Since $\frac{f'}{f}$ and
$\frac{g'}{g}$ are two analytic functions on the connected open set $U$ that agr
ee on $S$, and $S$ has a limit point in $U$, the **Identity Theorem** yields

$$
\frac{f'}{f}(z) = \frac{g'}{g}(z) \quad \text{for all } z \in U.
$$

Now consider the quotient $h = \frac{f}{g}$. Because $g$ never vanishes on $U$,
$h$ is analytic on $U$, and since $f$ never vanishes, $h$ is never zero on $U$.
Its derivative is

$$
h'(z) = \frac{f'(z)g(z) - f(z)g'(z)}{g(z)^2} = \frac{f(z)}{g(z)}\left(\frac{f'(z
)}{f(z)} - \frac{g'(z)}{g(z)}\right) = h(z)\cdot 0 = 0
$$

for every $z \in U$. Hence $h$ is constant on the connected open set $U$:

$$
\frac{f(z)}{g(z)} = c \quad \text{for all } z \in U,
$$

for some constant $c \in \mathbb{C}$. Since $f$ is never zero on $U$, $c \neq 0$
.

Conversely, any pair $f = cg$ with $c \in \mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$ and $g$ anal
ytic and nonzero on $U$ satisfies the given condition, so this is the precise re
lationship.

**Conclusion.** There exists a nonzero constant $c \in \mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$
 such that

$$
\boxed{\,f(z) = c\, g(z) \quad \text{for all } z \in U.\,}
$$
