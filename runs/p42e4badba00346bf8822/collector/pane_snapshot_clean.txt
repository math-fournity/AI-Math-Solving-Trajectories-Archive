## Proof

**Claim:** Such groups $G, H, I$ and injective homomorphisms $h, i$ exist.

**Construction.** Let
- $G = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$,
- $H = \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$,
- $I = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ (the Klein four-gro
up).

Define $h : G \to H$ by sending the generator $\bar{1} \in \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z
}$ to $\bar{2} \in \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$.

Define $i : G \to I$ by sending the generator $\bar{1} \in \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z
}$ to $(\bar{1}, \bar{0}) \in \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{
Z}$.

**Verification of the required properties.**

**(1) $h$ and $i$ are injective.** The element $\bar{2} \in \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{
Z}$ has order $2$, so $h$ maps the generator of $G$ to an element of the same or
der, hence is injective. Likewise, $(\bar{1}, \bar{0})$ has order $2$ in $I$, so
 $i$ is injective.

**(2) $\mathrm{img}(h) \trianglelefteq H$ and $\mathrm{img}(i) \trianglelefteq I
$.** Both $H$ and $I$ are abelian, so every subgroup is normal. Explicitly, $\ma
thrm{img}(h) = \{\bar{0}, \bar{2}\} \leq \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathrm{im
g}(i) = \langle (\bar{1}, \bar{0}) \rangle = \{(\bar{0},\bar{0}), (\bar{1},\bar{
0})\} \leq \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, both normal.

**(3) $H/\mathrm{img}(h) \cong I/\mathrm{img}(i)$.** We have
$$H/\mathrm{img}(h) = \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\,/\,\{\bar{0}, \bar{2}\} \cong \mat
hbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},$$
since the quotient of a cyclic group of order $4$ by its unique subgroup of orde

$$I/\mathrm{img}(i) = (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\,/\

with the isomorphism induced by projection onto the second factor. Hence $H/\mat
hrm{img}(h) \cong I/\mathrm{img}(i) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.

**(4) $H \not\cong I$.** The group $H = \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ is cyclic (it ha
s an element of order $4$), whereas $I = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z
}/2\mathbb{Z}$ has no element of order $4$ (every non-identity element has order

**Conclusion.** All four conditions are satisfied: $h$ and $i$ are injective, bo
th images are normal, the two quotients are isomorphic, yet $H$ and $I$ are not
isomorphic. Therefore such groups and homomorphisms **do exist**. $\blacksquare$
