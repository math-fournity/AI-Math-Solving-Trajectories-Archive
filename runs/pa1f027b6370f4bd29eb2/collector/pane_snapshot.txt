The sequence $\{u_n\}$ defined by $u_0=0$, $u_{2n}=u_n$, $u_{2n+1}=1-u_n$ is the
 **Thue–Morse sequence**. A standard characterization is:

$$u_n = \operatorname{popcount}(n) \pmod 2,$$

i.e., $u_n$ equals the parity of the number of $1$s in the binary expansion of $
n$.

**Verification of the characterization.** Let $s_2(n)$ be the digit sum of $n$ i
n base $2$. Then $s_2(2n)=s_2(n)$ (appending a $0$) and $s_2(2n+1)=s_2(n)+1$ (ap
pending a $1$). Defining $v_n = s_2(n)\bmod 2$ gives $v_0=0$, $v_{2n}=v_n$, $v_{
2n+1}=1-v_n$, identical to the recurrence for $u_n$. Hence $u_n=v_n$ for all $n$
.

---

**Computing $u_{1998}$.** Write $1998$ in binary:
$$1998 = 1024+512+256+128+64+8+4+2 = (11111001110)_2.$$
The number of $1$s is $8$, which is even. Therefore
$$u_{1998} = 0.$$

---

**Computing $u_{(2^{11}-1)^2}$.** First,
$$(2^{11}-1)^2 = 2047^2 = (2048-1)^2 = 2^{22}-2^{12}+1 = 4190209.$$

Now express $4190209$ in binary. Since
$$4190209 = 2^{22} - 2^{12} + 1,$$
we have
$$2^{22}-2^{12} = 2^{12}(2^{10}-1) = 2^{12}\cdot 1023,$$
which in binary is ten $1$s occupying bit positions $12$ through $21$, with bits
 $0$–$11$ equal to $0$. Adding $1$ sets bit $0$. Thus the binary representation
has
$$10 + 1 = 11$$
ones. Since $11$ is odd,
$$u_{(2^{11}-1)^2} = 1.$$

---

**Sum:**
$$u_{1998} + u_{(2^{11}-1)^2} = 0 + 1 = \boxed{1}.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
