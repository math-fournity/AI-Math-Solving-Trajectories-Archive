## Solution

**Setting up the integral equations.**

From the problem statement:
- $x(t) = 1 + \int_0^t e^{-2(t-s)}\, x(s)\, ds$
- $y(t) = \int_0^t e^{-2(t-s)}\, \bigl(2x(s)+3y(s)\bigr)\, ds$

Both are continuous Volterra-type integral equations. I convert each to an ODE b
y differentiating and using Leibniz's rule.

---

**Solving for $x(t)$.**

Differentiating:
$$x'(t) = -2\int_0^t e^{-2(t-s)}x(s)\,ds + x(t).$$

Since the integral equals $x(t)-1$:
$$x'(t) = -2(x(t)-1) + x(t) = -x(t)+2.$$

With the initial condition $x(0)=1$ (from $t=0$ in the integral equation), this
linear ODE gives:
$$x(t) = 2 - e^{-t}.$$

*Verification:* $x(0)=2-1=1$ ✓; $x'=e^{-t}$ and $-x+2=e^{-t}$ ✓.

---

**Solving for $y(t)$.**

Differentiating:
$$y'(t) = -2\int_0^t e^{-2(t-s)}(2x(s)+3y(s))\,ds + \bigl(2x(t)+3y(t)\bigr).$$

The integral equals $y(t)$, so:
$$y'(t) = -2y(t) + 2x(t) + 3y(t) = 2x(t) + y(t).$$

Substituting $x(t)=2-e^{-t}$:
$$y' - y = 4 - 2e^{-t}, \qquad y(0)=0.$$

Using the integrating factor $e^{-t}$:
$$\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(e^{-t}y\bigr) = 4e^{-t} - 2e^{-2t}.$$

Integrating from $0$ to $t$ (with $y(0)=0$):
$$e^{-t}y(t) = 4(1-e^{-t}) - (1-e^{-2t}) = 3 - 4e^{-t} + e^{-2t}.$$

Therefore:
$$y(t) = 3e^{t} - 4 + e^{-t}.$$

*Verification:* $y(0)=3-4+1=0$ ✓; $y'=3e^t - e^{-t}$ and $2x+y = 2(2-e^{-t})+(3e
^t-4+e^{-t})=3e^t-e^{-t}$ ✓.

---

**Evaluating at $t=\ln 2$.**

Here $e^t = 2$, $e^{-t} = \tfrac{1}{2}$.

$$x(\ln 2) = 2 - \tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{3}{2}.$$
$$y(\ln 2) = 3\cdot 2 - 4 + \tfrac{1}{2} = 6 - 4 + \tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{5}{2}.$
$

---

**Final result.**

$$x(\ln 2) + y(\ln 2) = \tfrac{3}{2} + \tfrac{5}{2} = \boxed{4}.$$
