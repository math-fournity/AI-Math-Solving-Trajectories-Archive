## Solution

**Setup.** We work on $\mathbb{P}^2$ with homogeneous coordinates $[X:Y:Z]$. The
 affine chart $U_Z = \{Z \neq 0\}$ is $\mathbb{C}^2$ with affine coordinates $x
= X/Z,\; y = Y/Z$. The given form
$$\omega = dx \wedge dy$$
is a rational 2-form on $\mathbb{P}^2$, regular and nonzero on $U_Z$. The canoni
cal divisor $K_{\mathbb{P}^2}$ is the divisor $(\omega)$ of this form. We comput
e its divisor by examining how $\omega$ transforms in the other affine charts.

**Change of coordinates.** Consider the chart $U_Y = \{Y \neq 0\}$ with coordina
tes
$$u = \frac{X}{Y} = \frac{x}{y}, \qquad v = \frac{Z}{Y} = \frac{1}{y}.$$
Inverting: $x = u/v$, $y = 1/v$. Computing the differentials:
$$dx = \frac{v\,du - u\,dv}{v^2}, \qquad dy = -\frac{dv}{v^2}.$$
Therefore
$$dx \wedge dy = \frac{v\,du - u\,dv}{v^2} \wedge \left(-\frac{dv}{v^2}\right) =
 -\frac{v\,du \wedge dv}{v^4} = -\frac{du \wedge dv}{v^3}.$$

**Reading off the divisor.** In the chart $U_Y$, the form $\omega = -\dfrac{du \
wedge dv}{v^3}$ has:
- a pole of order $3$ along $\{v = 0\}$, which is the line $\{Z = 0\} = L_\infty
$ (the line at infinity),
- no other zeros or poles (the factor $-1$ is a nonzero constant, and $du \wedge
 dv$ is the standard regular nonzero form on $U_Y$).

On the overlap $U_Z \cap U_Y$ the form is regular and nonzero, and a symmetric c
heck in the chart $U_X$ gives the same conclusion: the only singularity is the o
rder-3 pole along $L_\infty$.

Hence the divisor of $\omega$ is
$$(\omega) = -3\,L_\infty.$$

**Computing the degree.** The line at infinity $L_\infty = \{Z = 0\}$ is a hyper
plane (line) in $\mathbb{P}^2$, so $\deg(L_\infty) = 1$. Therefore
$$\deg K_{\mathbb{P}^2} = \deg(\omega) = -3 \cdot \deg(L_\infty) = -3 \cdot 1 =
-3.$$

This agrees with the general formula $K_{\mathbb{P}^n} = -(n+1)H$, giving $K_{\m
athbb{P}^2} = -3H$ and $\deg K_{\mathbb{P}^2} = -3$.
