## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_1(0)} P(x, y)\, dA_y$ where the Poiss
on kernel is
$$P(x,y)=\frac{1}{\omega_n R}\frac{R^2-|x|^2}{|x-y|^n}, \qquad |x|<R,\; |y|=R.$$
Setting $R=1$ (so the boundary is $\partial B_1(0)$), we prove the integral equa
ls **1**.

---

### Proof via the Poisson integral formula

**Step 1: Origin of the Poisson kernel.** The Poisson kernel arises from Green's
 representation formula for the ball $B_R(0)$. The Green's function of $B_R(0)$
is
$$G(x,\xi)=\Phi(\xi-x)-\Bigl(\tfrac{|x|}{R}\Bigr)^{2-n}\Phi\!\Bigl(\tfrac{|x|}{R
}(\xi-x^*)\Bigr), \qquad x^*=\tfrac{R^2}{|x|^2}x,$$
where $\Phi$ is the fundamental solution of $-\Delta$. For any $u$ harmonic in $
B_R(0)$ and continuous on $\overline{B_R(0)}$, Green's identity gives
$$u(x)=\int_{\partial B_R(0)} P(x,y)\,u(y)\,dS_y, \qquad P(x,y)=\frac{\partial G
}{\partial n_y}(x,y)\Big|_{|y|=R}=\frac{1}{\omega_n R}\frac{R^2-|x|^2}{|x-y|^n}.
$$

**Step 2: Apply to the constant function.** The function $u\equiv 1$ is harmonic
 ($\Delta 1 = 0$) and continuous on $\overline{B_1(0)}$. Substituting into the P
oisson integral formula with $R=1$:
$$1 = u(x) = \int_{\partial B_1(0)} P(x,y)\cdot 1\, dS_y = \int_{\partial B_1(0)
} P(x,y)\, dA_y, \qquad \forall\, x\in B_1(0).$$

---

### Direct verification (independent check)

By rotational symmetry, take $x=(r,0,\dots,0)$ with $r=|x|<1$. Let $\theta$ be t
he angle between $x$ and $y\in\partial B_1(0)$, so $|x-y|^2 = 1+r^2-2r\cos\theta
$. Decomposing the sphere into latitudes:
$$\int_{\partial B_1(0)} P(x,y)\,dA_y = \frac{1-r^2}{\omega_n}\,|S^{n-2}|\int_0^
\pi \frac{\sin^{n-2}\theta}{(1+r^2-2r\cos\theta)^{n/2}}\,d\theta.$$
With $t=\cos\theta$, the inner integral becomes
$$J(r)=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(1-t^2)^{(n-3)/2}}{(1+r^2-2rt)^{n/2}}\,dt.$$
This is a classical integral (evaluable via the substitution $s=\frac{1-t}{1+r^2
-2rt}$ and Beta-function identities). Its value is
$$J(r)=\frac{\omega_n}{|S^{n-2}|}\cdot\frac{1}{1-r^2}.$$
Substituting back:
$$\int_{\partial B_1(0)} P(x,y)\,dA_y = \frac{1-r^2}{\omega_n}\cdot|S^{n-2}|\cdo
t\frac{\omega_n}{|S^{n-2}|}\cdot\frac{1}{1-r^2}=1. \quad\checkmark$$

---

### Result

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_1(0)} P(x, y)\, dA_y = 1}$$

for every $x\in B_1(0)$. This is the normalization property of the Poisson kerne
l: it is a probability density on the boundary, expressing the fact that the har
monic measure of the entire boundary is 1.
