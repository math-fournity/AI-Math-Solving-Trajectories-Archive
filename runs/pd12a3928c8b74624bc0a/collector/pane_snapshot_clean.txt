## Step 1: Simplify the sign term

The exponent T(n) = n(n+1)/2 is the nth triangular number. Its parity:
- T(n) even ⟺ n ≡ 0, 3 (mod 4) → s(n) = +1
- T(n) odd ⟺ n ≡ 1, 2 (mod 4) → s(n) = −1

Checking against the **last two bits** of n:
- 00 → +1, 01 → −1, 10 → −1, 11 → +1

So **s(n) = (−1)^(b₀ ⊕ b₁)** where b₀, b₁ are the two least significant bits. Eq
uivalently, s(n) = +1 if the last two bits are equal, −1 if they differ.

## Step 2: Express a(n) via binary representation

The recurrence unfolds along the chain n → ⌊n/2⌋ → ⌊n/4⌋ → ··· → 1, summing s at

If n has binary form (1 b_{k−1} ··· b₁ b₀)₂ with k+1 bits, then ⌊n/2^j⌋ has last
 two bits equal to bits j and j+1 of n. Therefore:

**a(n) = Σ_{j=0}^{k−1} (−1)^(b_j ⊕ b_{j+1})**

Each term is +1 if consecutive bits are equal, −1 if they differ. If **t** = num
ber of bit transitions among the k consecutive pairs:

**a(n) = (k − t) − t = k − 2t**

where k = ⌊log₂(n)⌋ and t = number of transitions in n's binary representation.

**Verification** (n=1..16): All computed values match. E.g., n=10 = 1010₂: k=3,
