The formula $\dim \mathscr{M}_g = 3g-3$ is derived from deformation theory, and
the number $3g-3$ is in fact $-\chi(C, T_C) = h^1(C,T_C) - h^0(C,T_C)$, the dime
nsion of the **moduli stack** at a generic curve $C$, not automatically the dime
nsion of the coarse moduli space. The two agree only when the generic curve has
no infinitesimal automorphisms, i.e. $h^0(C,T_C)=0$, which fails exactly for $g=
0,1$.

## The deformation-theoretic count

For a smooth curve $C$ of genus $g$, the tangent space to deformations is $H^1(C
, T_C)$ and the infinitesimal automorphisms are $H^0(C, T_C)$. By Riemann–Roch (
with $\deg T_C = 2-2g$, $\operatorname{rk}=1$),
$$\chi(C, T_C) = \deg T_C + (1-g) = (2-2g) + (1-g) = 3 - 3g,$$
so
$$h^1(C,T_C) - h^0(C,T_C) = 3g - 3 \qquad \text{for every } g.$$
This is the dimension of the moduli **stack** $\mathfrak{M}_g$ at $C$.

The coarse moduli space $\mathscr{M}_g$ has the same dimension as the stack at $
C$ **only when $h^0(C,T_C)=0$**, because then the automorphism group is discrete
 (finite) and the stack is locally a quotient by a finite group, which does not
change the dimension. When $h^0(C,T_C)>0$, the connected automorphism group $G =
 \operatorname{Aut}^0(C)$ acts on the deformation space, and the coarse quotient
 has dimension
$$\dim \mathscr{M}_g = h^1(C,T_C) - \dim(\text{orbit of } G \text{ in } H^1(C,T_
C)).$$
The stack formula subtracts the *full* $\dim G = h^0(C,T_C)$, but the coarse spa
ce subtracts only the dimension of the *orbit*, which can be strictly smaller (e
ven $0$) when $G$ acts trivially on deformations.

## When $h^0(C,T_C)=0$: $g \ge 2$

Here $\deg T_C = 2-2g < 0$, so a generic curve has $H^0(C, T_C) = 0$ (no holomor
phic vector fields, hence no infinitesimal automorphisms). Thus
$$\dim \mathscr{M}_g = h^1(C,T_C) = 3g-3, \qquad g \ge 2.$$
Stack and coarse space agree in dimension at the generic point.

## Genus 0: $\deg T_C = 2 > 0$

For $C = \mathbb{P}^1$, $T_C \cong \mathcal{O}(2)$, so
$$h^0(\mathbb{P}^1, T) = 3 \quad (\operatorname{Aut}\mathbb{P}^1 = \mathrm{PGL}_
2), \qquad h^1(\mathbb{P}^1, T) = 0.$$
The curve is rigid: there are no deformations, and every genus $0$ curve is isom
orphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$, so $\mathscr{M}_0$ is a single point, $\dim \mathscr{M
}_0 = 0$.

The stack is $[\mathrm{pt}/\mathrm{PGL}_2]$, of dimension $0 - 3 = -3 = 3g-3$. T
he formula gives the *stack* dimension $-3$; the *coarse* space has dimension $0
$ because $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ acts trivially on the (zero-dimensional) deformation
space, so quotienting by it does not reduce any deformation parameter — there we
re none to begin with. The negative number $-3$ reflects that the $3$-dimensiona
l automorphism group is "larger than the deformation space," which the coarse qu
otient ignores.

## Genus 1: $\deg T_C = 0$

For an elliptic curve $C$, the canonical bundle is trivial, so $T_C \cong \mathc
al{O}_C$. Hence
$$h^0(C, T) = 1 \quad (\text{translations } C \xrightarrow{\sim} \operatorname{A
ut}^0 C), \qquad h^1(C, T) = 1,$$
and $\chi = 0 = 3g-3$, the stack dimension.

The $1$-dimensional deformation space $H^1(C,T_C)$ parametrizes the $j$-invarian
t. Crucially, the translation group $\operatorname{Aut}^0(C) \cong C$ acts **tri
vially** on $H^1(C,T_C)$: translating the origin does not change the complex str
ucture of the curve. Therefore the orbit of $G$ in $H^1(C,T_C)$ is $0$-dimension
al, and the coarse quotient retains the full deformation dimension:
$$\dim \mathscr{M}_1 = h^1(C,T_C) - 0 = 1 \quad (\text{the } j\text{-line } \mat
hbb{A}^1_j).$$
The stack formula $3g-3 = 0$ subtracts the full $1$-dimensional automorphism gro
up, but since that group acts trivially on moduli, the coarse space does not los
e that dimension — giving $\dim \mathscr{M}_1 = 1 \ne 0$.

## Summary

The identity $3g-3 = -\chi(C,T_C) = h^1(C,T_C) - h^0(C,T_C)$ holds for **all** $
g$; it is the dimension of the moduli **stack**. It equals the dimension of the
**coarse** moduli space $\mathscr{M}_g$ precisely when the generic curve has no
infinitesimal automorphisms, i.e. $h^0(C,T_C) = 0$, which is equivalent to $\deg
 T_C = 2-2g < 0$, i.e. $g \ge 2$.

- **$g=0$:** $\deg T_C = 2 > 0$, so $h^0 = 3$, $h^1 = 0$. The curve is rigid and
 $\mathscr{M}_0 = \mathrm{pt}$ ($\dim 0$), while the stack has dimension $-3$. T
he $3$-dimensional automorphism group $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ acts trivially on the emp
ty deformation space, so the coarse quotient keeps dimension $0$.
- **$g=1$:** $\deg T_C = 0$, so $h^0 = 1$, $h^1 = 1$. The $1$-dimensional transl
ation group acts trivially on the $1$-dimensional deformation space (the $j$-lin
e), so the coarse quotient keeps dimension $1$, while the stack has dimension $0
$.

In both cases the failure of $3g-3$ to give $\dim \mathscr{M}_g$ is exactly the
failure of $\deg T_C < 0$: positive-dimensional automorphism groups act with orb
its of dimension smaller than the group itself (trivially, in these two cases),
so the coarse moduli space does not subtract the full automorphism dimension tha
t the stack formula does.
