## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** For every convex closed subset $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$, ther
e exists a convex function $g: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ such that $A = \{x :
 g(x) \leq 0\}$.

---

### Necessity (the conditions on $A$ are forced)

If $g: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ is convex, then:

- **Convexity of the sublevel set.** For $x, y$ with $g(x), g(y) \leq 0$ and $\l
ambda \in [0,1]$:
  $$g(\lambda x + (1-\lambda)y) \leq \lambda g(x) + (1-\lambda) g(y) \leq 0,$$
  so $\{g \leq 0\}$ is convex.

- **Closedness of the sublevel set.** A convex function $g: \mathbb{R}^n \to \ma
thbb{R}$ that is finite everywhere is continuous (a standard result: convexity o
n an open domain implies local boundedness, hence continuity). Therefore $\{g \l
eq 0\} = g^{-1}((-\infty, 0])$ is closed.

So any set representable as $\{g \leq 0\}$ for a convex $g: \mathbb{R}^n \to \ma
thbb{R}$ must be **convex and closed**. The hypotheses on $A$ are exactly the ne
cessary ones.

---

### Sufficiency (construction)

We treat two cases.

**Case 1: $A = \emptyset$.** Take the constant function $g(x) = 1$. It is convex
, and $\{x : g(x) \leq 0\} = \emptyset = A$.

**Case 2: $A \neq \emptyset$.** Define the distance function
$$g(x) := d(x, A) = \inf_{a \in A} \|x - a\|.$$

We verify the three required properties.

**(a) $g$ is finite and $\mathbb{R}$-valued.** Since $A \neq \emptyset$, the inf
imum is over a nonempty set of nonneg reals, so $0 \leq g(x) < \infty$ for every
 $x$.

**(b) $g$ is convex.** Let $x_1, x_2 \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and $\lambda \in [0,1]$.
For any $a_1, a_2 \in A$, by the triangle inequality and homogeneity of the norm
,
$$\|\lambda x_1 + (1-\lambda)x_2 - (\lambda a_1 + (1-\lambda)a_2)\| \leq \lambda
\|x_1 - a_1\| + (1-\lambda)\|x_2 - a_2\|.$$
Since $A$ is convex, $\lambda a_1 + (1-\lambda)a_2 \in A$, so the left side is $
\geq d(\lambda x_1 + (1-\lambda)x_2,\, A)$. Taking the infimum over $a_1 \in A$
on the right and then over $a_2 \in A$,
$$d(\lambda x_1 + (1-\lambda)x_2,\, A) \leq \lambda\, d(x_1, A) + (1-\lambda)\,
d(x_2, A),$$
which is convexity of $g$.

**(c) $A = \{x : g(x) \leq 0\}$.** Since $g \geq 0$, we have $\{g \leq 0\} = \{g
 = 0\}$. It remains to show $\{x : d(x,A) = 0\} = A$:

- If $x \in A$, then $d(x,A) = 0$ (taking $a = x$).
- If $d(x,A) = 0$, there exists a sequence $(a_k) \subset A$ with $\|x - a_k\| \
to 0$, i.e., $a_k \to x$. Since $A$ is **closed**, $x \in A$.

Thus $\{x : g(x) \leq 0\} = A$.

---

### Conclusion

The convex closed subsets of $\mathbb{R}^n$ are *exactly* the sublevel sets $\{g
 \leq 0\}$ of convex functions $g: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$. Necessity follo
ws from convexity + continuity of finite convex functions; sufficiency follows f
rom the distance function $g(x) = d(x,A)$ (with the trivial constant $g \equiv 1
$ for $A = \emptyset$).
