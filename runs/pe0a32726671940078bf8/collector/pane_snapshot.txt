Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there exists $a > 1$ such t
hat $\lfloor a^n \rfloor$ is composite for all $n \geq 1$.

**Answer: Yes, such $a$ exists.**

## Proof

We construct a nested sequence of closed intervals $I_0 \supsetneq I_1 \supsetne
q I_2 \supsetneq \cdots$ in $(1,\infty)$ with $|I_n| \to 0$, such that for every
 $a \in I_n$, $\lfloor a^k \rfloor$ is composite for all $1 \leq k \leq n$. The
nested interval theorem then yields a point $a^* \in \bigcap_n I_n$ with the des
ired property.

**Setup.** Set $I_0 = [4, 8]$, so $u_0 = 4 \geq 4$ and $v_0 - u_0 = 4 > 3$.

**Inductive step.** Suppose $I_{n-1} = [u, v]$ with $u \geq 4$ and $v^n - u^n >
3$. Consider the open interval $(u^n,\, v^n - 1)$, which has length $(v^n - u^n)
 - 1 > 2$. Any open interval of length greater than $2$ contains at least two co
nsecutive integers, hence at least one even integer. Since $u^n \geq 4^n \geq 4$
, this even integer exceeds $4$, so it is at least $6$ and therefore composite.
Choose such an even integer and call it $c_n$.

Since $c_n > u^n$ and $c_n + 1 < v^n$ (both strict, by the open-interval choice)
, the interval
$$I_n := \bigl[c_n^{1/n},\; (c_n+1)^{1/n}\bigr]$$
is **strictly** contained in $[u, v] = I_{n-1}$ at both endpoints. For every $a
\in I_n$, we have $c_n \leq a^n \leq c_n + 1$.

**Verifying the inductive hypotheses for the next step.** We need $u_n := c_n^{1
/n} \geq 4$ and $v_n^{n+1} - u_n^{n+1} > 3$.

- **Lower bound:** Since $c_n > u^n \geq 4^n$, we get $u_n = c_n^{1/n} > 4$. ✓

- **Width condition:** By the Mean Value Theorem applied to $f(t) = t^{(n+1)/n}$
 on $[c_n,\, c_n+1]$:
$$v_n^{n+1} - u_n^{n+1} = (c_n+1)^{(n+1)/n} - c_n^{(n+1)/n} = \frac{n+1}{n}\,\xi
^{1/n}$$
for some $\xi \in (c_n,\, c_n+1)$. Since $\xi > c_n > 4^n$, we have $\xi^{1/n} >
 4$, so:
$$v_n^{n+1} - u_n^{n+1} > \frac{n+1}{n} \cdot 4 \geq 4 > 3. \quad\checkmark$$

**The intervals shrink to a point.** By the MVT applied to $g(t) = t^{1/n}$ on $
[c_n, c_n+1]$:
$$|I_n| = (c_n+1)^{1/n} - c_n^{1/n} = \frac{1}{n}\,\eta^{1/n - 1}$$
for some $\eta \in (c_n, c_n+1)$. For $n \geq 2$, the exponent $1/n - 1 < 0$, an
d $\eta > c_n > 4^n$, so:
$$|I_n| < \frac{1}{n}\,(4^n)^{1/n - 1} = \frac{4^{1-n}}{n} \;\longrightarrow\; 0
.$$

**Applying the nested interval theorem.** The intervals $I_n$ are closed, bounde
d, nested ($I_n \subsetneq I_{n-1}$), and $|I_n| \to 0$. Therefore $\bigcap_{n=0
}^{\infty} I_n = \{a^*\}$ for a unique real number $a^*$.

Since $u_n$ is strictly increasing and $v_n$ is strictly decreasing (both verifi
ed above), and $a^* = \lim u_n = \lim v_n$, we have the **strict** inequalities:
$$u_n < a^* < v_n \quad \text{for all } n \geq 0.$$

Raising to the $n$-th power:
$$c_n = u_n^n < (a^*)^n < v_n^n = c_n + 1 \quad \text{for all } n \geq 1.$$

Therefore $\lfloor (a^*)^n \rfloor = c_n$ for every $n \geq 1$, and each $c_n$ i
s an even integer $\geq 6$, hence composite.

Finally, $a^* \geq u_0 = 4 > 1$, so $a^*$ is a real number greater than $1$ whos
e sequence $\lfloor (a^*)^n \rfloor$ consists entirely of composite numbers. $\b
lacksquare$
