## Solution

We solve the Volterra integral equation of the first kind
$$\int_0^x J_0(x-t)\,y(t)\,dt = \sin(x)$$
using the Laplace transform.

### Step 1. Recognize the convolution structure

The left-hand side is the convolution $(J_0 * y)(x) = \int_0^x J_0(x-t)\,y(t)\,d
t$. The Laplace transform converts convolution into multiplication:
$$\mathcal{L}\{f * g\}(s) = F(s)\,G(s).$$

Applying the Laplace transform to both sides gives
$$\mathcal{L}\{J_0\}(s)\cdot Y(s) = \mathcal{L}\{\sin x\}(s) = \frac{1}{s^2+1}.$
$

### Step 2. Compute the Laplace transform of $J_0$

Using the series $J_0(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k}{(k!)^2}\left(\frac{x}{
2}\right)^{2k}$ and $\mathcal{L}\{x^{2k}\}=\frac{(2k)!}{s^{2k+1}}$:

$$\mathcal{L}\{J_0\}(s)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k}{(k!)^2\,2^{2k}}\cdot\fr
ac{(2k)!}{s^{2k+1}}=\frac{1}{s}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{2k}{k}\left(-\frac{1}{4
s^2}\right)^k.$$

The generating function identity $\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{2k}{k}x^k=\frac{1}{\
sqrt{1-4x}}$ with $x=-\frac{1}{4s^2}$ yields

$$\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{2k}{k}\left(-\frac{1}{4s^2}\right)^k=\frac{1}{\sqrt{
1+1/s^2}}=\frac{s}{\sqrt{s^2+1}}.$$

Therefore
$$\mathcal{L}\{J_0\}(s)=\frac{1}{s}\cdot\frac{s}{\sqrt{s^2+1}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^
2+1}}.$$

### Step 3. Solve for $Y(s)$

Substituting back:
$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^2+1}}\cdot Y(s)=\frac{1}{s^2+1},$$
so
$$Y(s)=\frac{\sqrt{s^2+1}}{s^2+1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^2+1}}.$$

### Step 4. Invert the Laplace transform

Since $Y(s)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^2+1}}=\mathcal{L}\{J_0\}(s)$, by uniqueness of the
Laplace transform:

$$\boxed{y(x)=J_0(x).}$$

### Verification

With $y(t)=J_0(t)$, the left side becomes $(J_0*J_0)(x)$, whose Laplace transfor
m is $\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^2+1}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^2+1}}=\frac{1}{s^2+1}=\mathcal
{L}\{\sin x\}$, confirming $\int_0^x J_0(x-t)\,J_0(t)\,dt=\sin x$. $\blacksquare
$

### PROOF COMPLETE
