## Proof

We show that $M$ satisfies the universal property of the free group on $X$: for
any group $G$ and any map $f: X \to G$, there exists a unique group homomorphism
 $\varphi: M \to G$ extending $f$.

### Setup

Let $X' = \{a, a^{-1}, b, b^{-1}, \dots\}$ and let $X'^*$ denote the free monoid
 on $X'$ (i.e., the set of all words over $X'$ with concatenation, including the
 empty word $\varepsilon$). Let $\sim$ be the congruence on $X'^*$ generated by
the relations $xx^{-1} \sim \varepsilon$ and $x^{-1}x \sim \varepsilon$ for all
$x \in X$. Then $M = X'^* / \sim$.

As noted in the problem, $M$ is a group: every generator $x \in X' \subseteq M$
has an inverse (the symbol $x^{-1}$, and vice versa), so every word $x_1^{\varep
silon_1}\cdots x_n^{\varepsilon_n}$ has inverse $x_n^{-\varepsilon_n}\cdots x_1^
{-\varepsilon_1}$.

### Existence of the extension

Given a group $G$ and a map $f: X \to G$, define a monoid homomorphism
$$\psi: X'^* \to G$$
by setting, on generators:
$$\psi(x) = f(x), \qquad \psi(x^{-1}) = f(x)^{-1} \quad \text{for each } x \in X
.$$
This is well-defined because $X'^*$ is the free monoid on $X'$, so any assignmen
t on generators extends uniquely to a monoid homomorphism.

We verify that $\psi$ respects the defining relations of $M$. For each $x \in X$
:
$$\psi(xx^{-1}) = \psi(x)\,\psi(x^{-1}) = f(x)\,f(x)^{-1} = e_G = \psi(\varepsil
on),$$
$$\psi(x^{-1}x) = \psi(x^{-1})\,\psi(x) = f(x)^{-1}\,f(x) = e_G = \psi(\varepsil
on).$$

Since $\psi$ maps both sides of every defining relation to the same element, and
 $\sim$ is the congruence *generated* by these relations, $\psi$ is constant on
each $\sim$-equivalence class. Therefore $\psi$ descends to a well-defined monoi
d homomorphism
$$\varphi: M = X'^*/{\sim} \;\longrightarrow\; G.$$

Since $M$ is a group and $G$ is a group, the monoid homomorphism $\varphi$ is in
 fact a group homomorphism (it preserves the identity and multiplication; preser
vation of inverses follows automatically). By construction, $\varphi|_X = f$, so
 $\varphi$ extends $f$.

### Uniqueness of the extension

Suppose $\varphi': M \to G$ is any group homomorphism with $\varphi'|_X = f$. Fo
r each $x \in X$, the element $x^{-1} \in M$ is the group-theoretic inverse of $
x \in M$, so:
$$\varphi'(x^{-1}) = \varphi'(x)^{-1} = f(x)^{-1}.$$
Thus $\varphi'$ agrees with $\varphi$ on every generator in $X'$. Since $M$ is g
enerated (as a monoid, hence as a group) by $X'$, and both $\varphi$ and $\varph
i'$ are monoid homomorphisms agreeing on all generators, we conclude $\varphi' =
 \varphi$.

### Conclusion

We have shown that for every group $G$ and every map $f: X \to G$, there exists
a unique group homomorphism $\varphi: M \to G$ extending $f$. This is precisely
the universal property characterizing the free group on $X$. Therefore $M$ is th
e free group over $X$. $\blacksquare$
