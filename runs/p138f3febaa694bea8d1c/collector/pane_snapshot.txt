## Fourier Dimension of [a, b]

**Definition.** For a compact set $E \subset \mathbb{R}$, the Fourier dimension
is
$$\dim_F(E) = \sup\bigl\{s \in [0,1] : \exists\,\mu \in \mathcal{P}(E),\ \exists
\,C>0,\ |\widehat{\mu}(\xi)| \le C|\xi|^{-s/2}\ \text{for all }\xi\neq 0\bigr\},
$$
where $\widehat{\mu}(\xi)=\int e^{-2\pi i \xi x}\,d\mu(x)$.

---

### Upper bound: $\dim_F([a,b]) \le 1$

A classical theorem (going back to Frostman) states that for every $E\subset\mat
hbb{R}$,
$$\dim_F(E) \le \dim_H(E),$$
where $\dim_H$ is the Hausdorff dimension. Indeed, if $|\widehat{\mu}(\xi)|\le C
|\xi|^{-s/2}$, then the $s$-energy
$$I_s(\mu)=\iint |x-y|^{-s}\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y) = c_s\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{\m
u}(\xi)|^2\,|\xi|^{s-1}\,d\xi \le C'\int |\xi|^{-s}|\xi|^{s-1}\,d\xi$$
is finite (the last integral converges near $|\xi|=\infty$ when $s<1$, and the e
nergy identity $I_s(\mu)=c_s\int|\widehat\mu|^2|\xi|^{s-1}d\xi$ combined with Fr
ostman's lemma gives $\dim_H(E)\ge s$). Since $[a,b]$ is an interval, $\dim_H([a
,b])=1$, giving
$$\dim_F([a,b])\le 1.$$

---

### Lower bound: $\dim_F([a,b]) \ge 1$

Take the **normalized Lebesgue measure** on $[a,b]$:
$$\mu = \frac{1}{b-a}\,\mathbf{1}_{[a,b]}\,dx \;\in\; \mathcal{M}([a,b]).$$
This is a probability measure supported on $[a,b]$. Its Fourier transform is
$$\widehat{\mu}(\xi)=\frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b e^{-2\pi i\xi x}\,dx = \frac{1}{b-a}\
cdot\frac{e^{-2\pi i\xi b}-e^{-2\pi i\xi a}}{-2\pi i\xi}.$$
Factoring out the phase $e^{-2\pi i\xi a}$ and taking the modulus:
$$|\widehat{\mu}(\xi)|=\frac{|e^{-2\pi i\xi(b-a)}-1|}{2\pi|\xi|(b-a)}=\frac{|\si
n\!\bigl(\pi\xi(b-a)\bigr)|}{\pi|\xi|(b-a)}.$$
Using $|\sin t|\le 1$:
$$|\widehat{\mu}(\xi)|\le \frac{1}{\pi(b-a)}\,|\xi|^{-1}=:C\,|\xi|^{-1},\qquad \
xi\neq 0.$$
Since $|\xi|^{-1}\le |\xi|^{-1/2}$ for $|\xi|\ge 1$ and $|\widehat{\mu}(\xi)|\le
 1$ for all $\xi$ (as $\mu$ is a probability measure), there exists $C'>0$ with
$$|\widehat{\mu}(\xi)|\le C'\,|\xi|^{-1/2}\quad\text{for all }\xi\neq 0.$$
This is exactly the decay condition with $s=1$, so $\dim_F([a,b])\ge 1$.

---

### Conclusion

Combining both bounds:
$$\boxed{\dim_F([a,b])=1,}$$
achieved by the normalized Lebesgue measure
$$\mu=\frac{1}{b-a}\,\mathbf{1}_{[a,b]}\,dx\;\in\;\mathcal{M}([a,b]).$$
