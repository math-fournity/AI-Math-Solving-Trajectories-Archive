## Proof

**Step 1: Trisection of a centered ellipsoid is universal.**

Let $E = \{x : x^T Q x \le 1\}$ be an ellipsoid centered at the origin, and let
$\mathbf{n}$ be a unit vector. The half-width of $E$ in direction $\mathbf{n}$ i
s $w = \sqrt{\mathbf{n}^T Q^{-1} \mathbf{n}}$. The cross-section at $\mathbf{n}\
cdot x = t$ (for $|t|\le w$) is an ellipse with area $A(t) = A(0)(1 - t^2/w^2)$.
 The cumulative volume from $-w$ to $t$ is:

$$\frac{V(t)}{V_{\text{total}}} = \frac{2 + 3u - u^3}{4}, \quad u = t/w.$$

Setting this equal to $1/3$ and $2/3$ yields $u^3 - 3u = \pm 2/3$. If $u_0 > 0$
solves $u^3 - 3u = 2/3$, then $-u_0$ solves $u^3 - 3u = -2/3$. So **the trisecti
on planes are always at $\mathbf{n}\cdot x = \pm\, u_0\, w(\mathbf{n})$**, where
 $u_0$ is a universal constant independent of the ellipsoid and direction.

**Step 2: Both ellipsoids are centered at the origin.**

$A$: $x^2/a^2 + y^2 + z^2 \le 1$ — no linear terms, centered at origin. ✓

$B$: $x^2 + 4y^2 + 4z^2 - 2xy \le 1$ — no linear terms, centered at origin. ✓

By central symmetry, the trisection planes for each are symmetric about the orig
in.

**Step 3: Condition for simultaneous trisection.**

Two parallel planes with normal $\mathbf{n}$ trisect $A$ iff they are $\mathbf{n
}\cdot x = \pm u_0\, w_A(\mathbf{n})$, and trisect $B$ iff they are $\mathbf{n}\
cdot x = \pm u_0\, w_B(\mathbf{n})$. The same pair of planes works for both iff
$w_A(\mathbf{n}) = w_B(\mathbf{n})$ for some unit vector $\mathbf{n}$.

**Step 4: Compute the half-widths.**

For $A$: $Q_A = \text{diag}(1/a^2, 1, 1)$, so $Q_A^{-1} = \text{diag}(a^2, 1, 1)
$, giving
$$w_A^2 = a^2 n_1^2 + n_2^2 + n_3^2.$$

For $B$: $Q_B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 \end{pmatr
ix}$, so $Q_B^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 4/3 & 1/3 & 0 \\ 1/3 & 1/3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1
/4 \end{pmatrix}$, giving
$$w_B^2 = \tfrac{4}{3}n_1^2 + \tfrac{2}{3}n_1 n_2 + \tfrac{1}{3}n_2^2 + \tfrac{1
}{4}n_3^2.$$

**Step 5: Reduce to a quadratic form condition.**

Define $g(\mathbf{n}) = w_A^2 - w_B^2$:
$$g(\mathbf{n}) = \left(a^2 - \tfrac{4}{3}\right)n_1^2 - \tfrac{2}{3}n_1 n_2 + \
tfrac{2}{3}n_2^2 + \tfrac{3}{4}n_3^2.$$

This is a quadratic form with matrix
$$M = \begin{pmatrix} a^2 - 4/3 & -1/3 & 0 \\ -1/3 & 2/3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3/4 \end
{pmatrix}.$$

We need $g(\mathbf{n}) = 0$ for some unit $\mathbf{n}$, i.e., $0$ lies in the ra
nge of $g$ on the unit sphere. This holds iff the smallest eigenvalue of $M$ is
$\le 0$ and the largest is $\ge 0$.

**Step 6: Eigenvalue analysis.**

The eigenvalue $3/4$ (from the $z$-block) is always positive, so the largest eig
envalue is always $\ge 3/4 > 0$. ✓

The $2\times 2$ block $\begin{pmatrix} a^2-4/3 & -1/3 \\ -1/3 & 2/3 \end{pmatrix
}$ has:
- Trace $= a^2 - 2/3$
- Determinant $= \frac{2a^2}{3} - 1$
- Discriminant $= (a^2 - 2/3)^2 - 4(\frac{2a^2}{3} - 1) = a^4 - 4a^2 + \frac{40}
{9}$

The discriminant $a^4 - 4a^2 + 40/9$: setting $u = a^2$, $u^2 - 4u + 40/9$ has d
iscriminant $16 - 160/9 = -16/9 < 0$, so it is **always positive**. The eigenval
ues are real:

$$\lambda_{\pm} = \frac{(a^2 - 2/3) \pm \sqrt{a^4 - 4a^2 + 40/9}}{2}.$$

The smallest eigenvalue of $M$ is $\min(3/4,\, \lambda_-)$. Since $3/4 > 0$, we
need $\lambda_- \le 0$:

$$\lambda_- \le 0 \iff a^2 - \frac{2}{3} \le \sqrt{a^4 - 4a^2 + \frac{40}{9}}.$$

- **If $a^2 \le 2/3$**: LHS $\le 0$, RHS $> 0$. Inequality holds. ✓
- **If $a^2 > 2/3$**: Square both sides:
$$(a^2 - 2/3)^2 \le a^4 - 4a^2 + 40/9$$
$$a^4 - \frac{4}{3}a^2 + \frac{4}{9} \le a^4 - 4a^2 + \frac{40}{9}$$
$$\frac{8}{3}a^2 \le 4 \iff a^2 \le \frac{3}{2}.$$

Combining: $\lambda_- \le 0 \iff a^2 \le \dfrac{3}{2}$.

**Step 7: Boundary check.**

At $a^2 = 3/2$: $\lambda_- = \frac{(3/2 - 2/3) - \sqrt{9/4 - 6 + 40/9}}{2} = \fr
ac{5/6 - \sqrt{25/36}}{2} = \frac{5/6 - 5/6}{2} = 0$. The eigenvector $(1, 1/2,
0)$ satisfies $g = 0$, so simultaneous trisection exists. ✓

For $a^2 > 3/2$: all eigenvalues of $M$ are positive, so $g(\mathbf{n}) > 0$ for
 all $\mathbf{n} \ne 0$, meaning $w_A > w_B$ in every direction — no simultaneou
s trisection.

For $0 < a^2 < 3/2$: $\lambda_- < 0$ while the largest eigenvalue $> 0$, so by c
ontinuity $g = 0$ somewhere — simultaneous trisection exists.

**Conclusion.**

Since $a > 0$ (required for $A$ to be an ellipsoid), the values of $a$ for which
 two parallel planes simultaneously trisect both $A$ and $B$ are:

$$\boxed{0 < a \le \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{2}}.$$
