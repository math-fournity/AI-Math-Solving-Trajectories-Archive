Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the Tietze-type extension p
roperty (1) and the deformation retract property (2) are equivalent.

**Answer: No, the two statements are not equivalent.** (2) implies (1), but (1)
does not imply (2).

## Proof

### (2) ⇒ (1): Deformation retract implies extension property

Suppose $E$ is a deformation retract of $X$. Then there exists a continuous homo
topy $H: X \times [0,1] \to X$ with $H(x,0)=x$, $H(x,1)\in E$, and $H(e,t)=e$ fo
r all $e\in E$, $t\in[0,1]$. In particular, the map $r: X \to E$ defined by $r(x
)=H(x,1)$ is a **retraction**: $r$ is continuous and $r|_E = \mathrm{id}_E$.

Given any continuous $f: E \to \mathbb{R}$, set $g = f \circ r: X \to \mathbb{R}
$. Then $g$ is continuous (composition of continuous maps), and for every $e \in
 E$:
$$g(e) = f(r(e)) = f(e).$$
So $g|_E = f$. Hence every continuous real-valued function on $E$ extends to $X$
. ∎

### (1) ⇏ (2): Extension property does not imply deformation retract

**Counterexample.** Let $X = [0,1]$ with the usual topology, and let $E = \{0, 1
\}$.

**Claim 1:** Property (1) holds. Since $[0,1]$ is a compact Hausdorff space, it
is normal. The subset $E=\{0,1\}$ is closed. By the **Tietze Extension Theorem**
, every continuous function $f: E \to \mathbb{R}$ extends to a continuous functi
on $g: [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}$. (Concretely, any continuous $f: \{0,1\}\to\mathbb{
R}$ is just a pair of real values $f(0)=a$, $f(1)=b$, and the affine function $g
(t) = a(1-t)+bt$ extends it.) So statement (1) is satisfied.

**Claim 2:** $E$ is **not** a deformation retract of $X$.

Suppose for contradiction that $\{0,1\}$ were a deformation retract of $[0,1]$.
Then the inclusion $i: \{0,1\} \hookrightarrow [0,1]$ and the retraction $r: [0,
1] \to \{0,1\}$ would be homotopy inverses, making $\{0,1\}$ homotopy equivalent
 to $[0,1]$.

But $[0,1]$ is **contractible** (it deformation retracts to a single point), so
any space homotopy equivalent to $[0,1]$ must also be contractible. However, $\{
0,1\}$ is a **discrete two-point space**, which is **not contractible**: a contr
action would require a continuous map $H: \{0,1\}\times[0,1]\to\{0,1\}$ with $H(
x,0)=x$ and $H(x,1)=p$ for some fixed $p\in\{0,1\}$. By continuity and discreten
ess, $H$ must be constant in $t$ for each $x$, so $H(0,0)=0$ forces $H(0,t)=0$ f
or all $t$, contradicting $H(0,1)=p$ (if $p=1$) or $H(1,1)=p=0$ contradicting $H
(1,0)=1$.

This is a contradiction. Therefore $\{0,1\}$ is not a deformation retract of $[0
,1]$.

### Conclusion

We have shown:
- **(2) ⇒ (1)** is true (a retraction allows extension by composition).
- **(1) ⇒ (2)** is false (the closed subset $\{0,1\}\subset[0,1]$ satisfies the
Tietze extension property but is not a deformation retract, since $[0,1]$ is con
tractible while $\{0,1\}$ is not).

Therefore, statements (1) and (2) are **not equivalent**.
