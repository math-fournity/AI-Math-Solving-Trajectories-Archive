## Solution

We consider the Ricci flow on a compact manifold $M$:
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial t} g_{ij} = -2 R_{ij}.$$

We claim that **statement 2 is true**: $g_{ij}$ is a $C^\infty$ function on the
spacetime $M \times [0, T)$.

### Proof

**Step 1: The Ricci flow is only weakly parabolic.** The Ricci flow equation $\p
artial_t g = -2\operatorname{Ric}(g)$ is invariant under the full diffeomorphism
 group of $M$. Consequently, the linearization of the operator $g \mapsto -2\ope
ratorname{Ric}(g)$ at any metric has a kernel coming from infinitesimal diffeomo
rphisms (the Lie derivative $\mathcal{L}_V g$), so the principal symbol is not s
trictly positive-definite. The system is therefore *degenerate (weakly) paraboli
c*, and standard parabolic regularity theory does not apply directly.

**Step 2: DeTurck's trick removes the degeneracy.** Following DeTurck (1983), on
e fixes a background metric $\tilde g$ and considers the modified flow
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial t} g_{ij} = -2 R_{ij} + \nabla_i W_j + \nabla_j W_i,$
$
where $W = W(g, \tilde g)$ is a fixed vector field depending smoothly on $g$ (co
nstructed from the difference of the two Levi-Civita connections). The added ter
m $\mathcal{L}_W g$ is precisely an infinitesimal diffeomorphism, so solutions o
f the modified flow are pulled back to solutions of the original Ricci flow by a
 time-dependent diffeomorphism.

The crucial point is that the linearization of the modified operator now has a s
trictly positive-definite principal symbol: the system is **strictly parabolic**
. This is because the Lie-derivative term cancels the kernel of the Ricci linear
ization.

**Step 3: Strict parabolicity gives smooth solutions.** For a strictly parabolic
 system on a compact manifold with smooth initial data $g(0) = g_0 \in C^\infty$
, standard parabolic theory (cf. Ladyzhenskaya–Solonnikov–Ural'ceva, or the pseu
dodifferential/heat-kernel approach) yields a unique solution on a maximal inter
val $[0, T)$ that is **smooth jointly in $(x, t)$**:
$$g_{ij} \in C^\infty\bigl(M \times [0, T)\bigr).$$
Parabolic smoothing gives that $g(\cdot, t)$ is smooth in space for each $t > 0$
, and differentiability in time follows by bootstrapping: the equation $\partial
_t g = -2\operatorname{Ric}(g)$ expresses $\partial_t g$ in terms of spatial der
ivatives of $g$, so once spatial smoothness is established to sufficiently high
order, $\partial_t g$ is continuous, then $\partial_t^2 g$ involves spatial deri
vatives of $\operatorname{Ric}(g)$, etc. Iterating, all time derivatives exist a
nd are continuous. Hence $g_{ij}$ is $C^\infty$ in both variables jointly.

**Step 4: Pulling back by the diffeomorphism preserves smoothness.** The solutio
n of the original Ricci flow is obtained from the DeTurck-flow solution $\bar g(
t)$ by
$$g(t) = \varphi(t)^* \bar g(t),$$
where $\varphi(t)$ is a family of diffeomorphisms generated by the vector field
$-W(\bar g(t), \tilde g)$. Since $\bar g(t)$ is smooth in $(x,t)$ and $W$ depend
s smoothly on $\bar g$, the ODE generating $\varphi(t)$ has a smooth right-hand
side, so $\varphi(t)$ is smooth in $(x, t)$. The pullback of a smooth tensor by
a smooth diffeomorphism is smooth. Therefore $g_{ij}(x, t) \in C^\infty(M \times
 [0, T))$.

**Step 5: Ruling out the other statements.**
- Statement 1 ($C^1$ in time, $C^\infty$ in space) is strictly weaker than what
holds. While it is true that $g_{ij}$ is $C^1$ in time and $C^\infty$ in space,
this understates the regularity: the bootstrapping in Step 3 gives smoothness in
 time as well, not merely $C^1$.
- Statement 3 ($C^1$ on the spacetime) is weaker still and likewise not the shar
p conclusion.

The sharp and correct statement is that $g_{ij}$ is **$C^\infty$ on the spacetim
e** $M \times [0, T)$.

### Conclusion

Statement **2** is true: $g_{ij}$ is a $C^\infty$ function on the spacetime.
