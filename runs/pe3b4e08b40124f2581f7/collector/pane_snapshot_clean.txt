## Proof

We compute
$$\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0} \exp(tX)\,A\,\exp(-tX),$$
where $A \in M(n,\mathbb{C})$ and $X \in \mathfrak{su}(n)$.

**Step 1: Differentiate the product.** By the product rule (valid for matrix-val
ued smooth curves),
$$\frac{d}{dt}\Big[\exp(tX)\,A\,\exp(-tX)\Big] = \frac{d}{dt}\exp(tX)\cdot A\cdo
t \exp(-tX) + \exp(tX)\cdot A \cdot \frac{d}{dt}\exp(-tX).$$

**Step 2: Differentiate the exponentials.** For any matrix $Y$,
$$\frac{d}{dt}\exp(tY) = Y\exp(tY) = \exp(tY)\,Y,$$
since $Y$ commutes with its own exponential. Hence
$$\frac{d}{dt}\exp(tX) = X\exp(tX), \qquad \frac{d}{dt}\exp(-tX) = -X\exp(-tX).$
$

**Step 3: Evaluate at $t=0$.** Substituting and using $\exp(0) = I$:
$$\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\exp(tX)\,A\,\exp(-tX) = X\exp(0)\,A\,\exp(0) +
 \exp(0)\,A\,(-X\exp(0)) = XA - AX.$$

**Step 4: Identify the result.** The expression $XA - AX$ is the commutator (Lie
 bracket) of $X$ and $A$:
$$XA - AX = [X, A].$$

**Remark on the role of $X \in \mathfrak{su}(n)$.** The computation above holds
for *any* matrix $X \in M(n,\mathbb{C})$; the hypothesis $X \in \mathfrak{su}(n)
$ (i.e. $X$ skew-Hermitian and traceless) is not needed for the differentiation
itself. Its role is geometric: the curve $t \mapsto \exp(tX)$ lies in $\mathrm{S
U}(n)$, so the map $t \mapsto \exp(tX)\,A\,\exp(-tX)$ is the **adjoint orbit** o
f $A$ through the $\mathrm{SU}(n)$-action by conjugation, and the differential a
t $t=0$ gives the **infinitesimal action** of $X$ on $A$, namely the Lie bracket
 $[X,A]$. This is precisely the tangent vector to the adjoint orbit of $A$ in th
e direction of $X$.

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\exp(tX)\,A\,\exp(-tX) = [X,A] = XA - AX
.}$$
