Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the commuting probability $
p$ for a connected compact group $K$ (with normalized Haar measure) is ever stri
ctly between 0 and 1.

**Answer: No.** For a connected compact group, $p = 1$ if $K$ is abelian and $p
= 0$ if $K$ is non-abelian. The probability is never non-trivial.

---

## Proof

Let $\mu$ denote the normalized Haar measure on $K$. The commuting probability i
s:
$$p = \int_K \int_K \mathbf{1}_{xy=yx}\, d\mu(y)\, d\mu(x) = \int_K \mu(C_K(x))\
, d\mu(x),$$
where $C_K(x) = \{y \in K : xy = yx\}$ is the centralizer of $x$, by Fubini's th
eorem.

### Case 1: $K$ is abelian

Every pair commutes, so $p = 1$.

### Case 2: $K$ is non-abelian

We show $p = 0$ in three steps.

**Step 1: $K$ admits a non-abelian connected compact Lie quotient.**

By the Peter–Weyl theorem, $K$ is the inverse limit of its Lie quotients $K/N$,
where $N$ ranges over closed normal subgroups with $K/N$ a Lie group, and $\bigc
ap N = \{e\}$. Since $K$ is non-abelian, there exist $x, y \in K$ with $xy \neq
yx$. Since $\bigcap N = \{e\}$, there exists some such $N$ for which the images
of $x$ and $y$ in $L := K/N$ still do not commute. Thus $L$ is a **non-abelian c
onnected compact Lie group**. Let $\pi: K \to L$ be the quotient map.

**Step 2: For a non-abelian connected compact Lie group $L$, $p(L) = 0$.**

Let $T$ be a maximal torus of $L$. An element $x \in L$ is **regular** if $C_L(x
)$ is a maximal torus. The set $L_{\mathrm{reg}}$ of regular elements is open an
d dense; its complement $L \setminus L_{\mathrm{reg}}$ (the singular set) is a f
inite union of lower-dimensional closed submanifolds, hence has $\mu$-measure $0
$.

For $x \in L_{\mathrm{reg}}$, the centralizer $C_L(x)$ is a maximal torus of dim
ension $\mathrm{rank}(L)$. Since $L$ is non-abelian, $\mathrm{rank}(L) < \dim(L)
$, so $\mu(C_L(x)) = 0$.

Therefore:
$$p(L) = \int_L \mu(C_L(x))\, d\mu(x) = \int_{L_{\mathrm{reg}}} 0\, d\mu(x) + \i
nt_{L \setminus L_{\mathrm{reg}}} \mu(C_L(x))\, d\mu(x) \leq 0 + \mu(L \setminus
 L_{\mathrm{reg}}) = 0.$$

So $p(L) = 0$.

**Step 3: $p(K) \leq p(L) = 0$, hence $p(K) = 0$.**

If $(x, y) \in K^2$ commute, then $(\pi(x), \pi(y)) \in L^2$ commute. Therefore:
$$\{(x,y) \in K^2 : xy = yx\} \subseteq (\pi \times \pi)^{-1}\left(\{(\bar{x}, \
bar{y}) \in L^2 : \bar{x}\bar{y} = \bar{y}\bar{x}\}\right).$$

Since $\pi$ is a continuous surjective homomorphism of compact groups, it pushes
 the Haar measure on $K$ forward to the Haar measure on $L$. Consequently, for a
ny measurable $A \subseteq L^2$:
$$(\mu_K \otimes \mu_K)\big((\pi \times \pi)^{-1}(A)\big) = (\mu_L \otimes \mu_L
)(A).$$

Applying this with $A$ the set of commuting pairs in $L$:
$$p(K) \leq (\mu_K \otimes \mu_K)\big((\pi \times \pi)^{-1}(A)\big) = (\mu_L \ot
imes \mu_L)(A) = p(L) = 0.$$

Since $p(K) \geq 0$ trivially, we conclude $p(K) = 0$.

---

### Conclusion

For a connected compact group $K$:
- $p = 1$ if $K$ is abelian,
- $p = 0$ if $K$ is non-abelian.

The commuting probability is **never** non-trivial: the condition $0 < p < 1$ ne
ver holds for a connected compact group.
