Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every non-negative lower se
micontinuous function on a locally compact Hausdorff space with countable base c
an be approximated pointwise from below by an increasing sequence from $\mathcal
{L}_+$ (non-negative lsc functions with compact support).

**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

**Step 1: Compact Exhaustion.**

Since $X$ is locally compact Hausdorff with a countable base, there exists an in
creasing sequence of compact sets $(K_n)_{n \geq 1}$ such that $K_n \subset \ope
ratorname{int}(K_{n+1})$ for all $n$ and $\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} K_n = X$.

*Construction.* Let $\{B_1, B_2, \dots\}$ be a countable base of open sets, each
 with compact closure (such a base exists: in a locally compact Hausdorff space,
 the base elements with compact closure still form a base). Set $U_1 = B_1$. Ind
uctively, given $U_n$ with $\overline{U_n}$ compact, cover $\overline{U_n}$ by f
initely many base elements $B_{j_1}, \dots, B_{j_m}$, and set
$$U_{n+1} = U_n \cup B_{j_1} \cup \cdots \cup B_{j_m} \cup B_{n+1}.$$
Then $K_n := \overline{U_n}$ is compact (closed subset of a finite union of comp
act sets), $K_n \subset U_{n+1} \subset \operatorname{int}(K_{n+1})$ (since $U_{
n+1}$ is open and contained in $K_{n+1}$), and $\bigcup K_n = X$ (since $B_{n+1}
 \subset U_{n+1}$ for all $n$).

**Step 2: Construction of the Approximating Sequence.**

Define
$$f_n(x) = \min\!\bigl(f(x),\, n\bigr) \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\operatorname{int}(K_n)
}(x).$$

**Step 3: Each $f_n$ is lower semicontinuous.**

- $\min(f, n)$ is lsc: for $a < n$, $\{\min(f,n) > a\} = \{f > a\}$ (open); for
$a \geq n$, $\{\min(f,n) > a\} = \emptyset$ (open). This holds even when $f$ tak
es the value $+\infty$.
- $\mathbf{1}_{\operatorname{int}(K_n)}$ is lsc, being the indicator of an open
set.
- The product of two non-negative lsc functions is lsc: for $a > 0$,
$$\{g \cdot h > a\} = \bigcup_{\substack{r \in \mathbb{Q} \\ r > 0}} \{g > r\} \
cap \{h > a/r\},$$
which is a union of open sets, hence open. For $a \leq 0$, $\{g \cdot h > a\}$ i
s either $X$ (if $a < 0$) or $\{g > 0\} \cap \{h > 0\}$ (if $a = 0$), both open.

Thus $f_n$ is lsc.

**Step 4: Each $f_n$ has compact support.**

The set $\{x : f_n(x) \neq 0\} \subset \operatorname{int}(K_n) \subset K_n$. Sin
ce $X$ is Hausdorff, $K_n$ is closed, so $\operatorname{supp}(f_n) = \overline{\
{f_n \neq 0\}} \subset K_n$, which is compact. Hence $f_n \in \mathcal{L}_+$.

**Step 5: The sequence is non-decreasing.**

Since $K_n \subset \operatorname{int}(K_{n+1})$, we have $\mathbf{1}_{\operatorn
ame{int}(K_n)} \leq \mathbf{1}_{\operatorname{int}(K_{n+1})}$. Also $\min(f, n)
\leq \min(f, n+1)$. Both factors are non-negative, so $f_n \leq f_{n+1}$ pointwi
se.

**Step 6: Pointwise convergence to $f$.**

For any $x \in X$, there exists $N$ with $x \in \operatorname{int}(K_N)$ (since
$\bigcup \operatorname{int}(K_n) \supset \bigcup K_n = X$). For all $n \geq N$,
$x \in \operatorname{int}(K_n)$, so $f_n(x) = \min(f(x), n)$. As $n \to \infty$,
 $\min(f(x), n) \to f(x)$ — this holds whether $f(x)$ is finite or $+\infty$. Th
erefore $f_n(x) \to f(x)$ for every $x \in X$.

**Conclusion.** The sequence $(f_n)$ is non-decreasing, each $f_n \in \mathcal{L
}_+$, and $f_n \to f$ pointwise. The answer is **yes**. $\blacksquare$
