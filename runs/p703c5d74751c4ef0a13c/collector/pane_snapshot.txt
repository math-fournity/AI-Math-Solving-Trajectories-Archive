## Proof

We compute $I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{dx}{(x^2+1)^3}$ using the residue
theorem.

**Setup.** Consider the meromorphic function
$$f(z) = \frac{1}{(z^2+1)^3} = \frac{1}{(z-i)^3(z+i)^3}.$$
Let $\Gamma_R$ be the closed contour consisting of the segment $[-R, R]$ on the
real axis together with the upper semicircle $C_R = \{Re^{i\theta} : 0 \le \thet
a \le \pi\}$. For $R > 1$, the only singularity of $f$ enclosed by $\Gamma_R$ is
 the pole of order $3$ at $z = i$.

**Estimate on the arc.** On $C_R$, $|f(z)| = \frac{1}{|z^2+1|^3} \le \frac{1}{(R
^2-1)^3}$, so
$$\left|\int_{C_R} f(z)\,dz\right| \le \frac{\pi R}{(R^2-1)^3} \to 0 \quad \text
{as } R \to \infty.$$
Hence $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx = \lim_{R\to\infty}\oint_{\Gamma_R} f(z)
\,dz$.

**Residue at $z = i$.** Since $z = i$ is a pole of order $3$,
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=i} f(z) = \frac{1}{2!}\lim_{z\to i}\frac{d^2}{dz^2}\left
[(z-i)^3 f(z)\right] = \frac{1}{2}\lim_{z\to i}\frac{d^2}{dz^2}\frac{1}{(z+i)^3}
.$$

Compute the derivatives:
$$\frac{d}{dz}\frac{1}{(z+i)^3} = -\frac{3}{(z+i)^4}, \qquad \frac{d^2}{dz^2}\fr
ac{1}{(z+i)^3} = \frac{12}{(z+i)^5}.$$

Evaluating at $z = i$:
$$\frac{12}{(i+i)^5} = \frac{12}{(2i)^5} = \frac{12}{32\,i^5} = \frac{12}{32\,i}
 = \frac{3}{8i} = -\frac{3i}{8}.$$

Therefore
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=i} f(z) = \frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{3i}{8}\right) = -\frac
{3i}{16}.$$

**Applying the residue theorem.**
$$\oint_{\Gamma_R} f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}_{z=i} f(z) = 2\pi
i\left(-\frac{3i}{16}\right) = \frac{6\pi}{16} = \frac{3\pi}{8}.$$

Taking $R \to \infty$ and using that the arc integral vanishes,
$$\boxed{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{dx}{(x^2+1)^3} = \frac{3\pi}{8}.}$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
