## Proof

**Setup.** For a given $n$, let $S$ be a uniformly random $(2n-1)$-subset of $\{
1,\ldots,2000\}$. Define $P(n) = \Pr(\text{failure})$ where failure means $S$ co
ntains $\{k, k+1, \ldots, k+n-1\}$ for some $k$.

**Step 1: Expected number of runs.** For each starting position $i \in \{1, \ldo
ts, 2001-n\}$, let $A_i$ be the event that $\{i, i+1, \ldots, i+n-1\} \subseteq
S$. By linearity of expectation, the expected number of runs of $n$ consecutive
selected units is:

$$E(n) = \sum_{i=1}^{2001-n} \Pr(A_i) = (2001-n)\cdot\frac{\binom{2000-n}{n-1}}{
\binom{2000}{2n-1}}$$

since $\Pr(A_i) = \binom{2000-n}{(2n-1)-n}/\binom{2000}{2n-1} = \binom{2000-n}{n
-1}/\binom{2000}{2n-1}$.

**Step 2: Simplify $E(n)$.** Writing out the binomial coefficients:

$$E(n) = (2001-n)\cdot\frac{(2n-1)!\,(2000-n)!}{(n-1)!\,2000!}$$

**Step 3: Compute the ratio $E(n+1)/E(n)$.**

$$\frac{E(n+1)}{E(n)} = \frac{(2000-n)}{(2001-n)}\cdot\frac{(2n+1)!}{(2n-1)!}\cd
ot\frac{(1999-n)!}{(2000-n)!}\cdot\frac{(n-1)!}{n!}$$

$$= \frac{(2000-n)}{(2001-n)}\cdot(2n)(2n+1)\cdot\frac{1}{(2000-n)}\cdot\frac{1}
{n} = \frac{2(2n+1)}{2001-n}$$

**Step 4: Find where $E(n)$ is minimized.** Setting $E(n+1)/E(n) = 1$:

$$2(2n+1) = 2001 - n \implies 4n + 2 = 2001 - n \implies 5n = 1999 \implies n =
399.8$$

Checking the discrete values:
- At $n = 399$: $E(400)/E(399) = \frac{2 \times 799}{1602} = \frac{1598}{1602} <
 1$ (decreasing).
- At $n = 400$: $E(401)/E(400) = \frac{2 \times 801}{1601} = \frac{1602}{1601} >
 1$ (increasing).

Therefore $E(n)$ is minimized at $n = 400$.

**Step 5: $P(n) \approx E(n)$ near the minimum.** By the union bound, $P(n) \le
E(n)$. By inclusion-exclusion, $P(n) \ge E(n) - \sum_{i<j}\Pr(A_i \cap A_j)$. Fo
r $n$ near 400, $E(n)$ is astronomically small (using Stirling's approximation,
$\ln E(400) \approx -440$, so $E(400) \sim e^{-440}$). The second-order terms $\
Pr(A_i \cap A_j)$ require even more positions to be simultaneously selected and
are $o(E(n))$. Thus $P(n) = E(n)(1 - o(1))$ with relative error far smaller than
 the ratio $E(401)/E(400) = 1602/1601 \approx 1.0006$ between adjacent values. H
ence the minimum of $P(n)$ coincides with the minimum of $E(n)$.

**Step 6: Verify endpoints are not competitive.**
- $n = 1$: Any single-element sample trivially contains 1 consecutive integer, s
o $P(1) = 1$.
- $n = 2$: $P(2) = 1 - \binom{1998}{3}/\binom{2000}{3} = 1 - \frac{1997 \cdot 19
96}{2000 \cdot 1999} \approx 0.003$.
- $n = 999$: $E(999) = 1002 \cdot \frac{1001 \cdot 1000 \cdot 999}{2000 \cdot 19
99 \cdot 1998} \approx 125.5 \gg 1$, so $P(999) \approx 1$.

All are vastly larger than $P(400) \approx e^{-440} \approx 0$.

**Conclusion.** The probability of system failure is minimized at $n = 400$.
