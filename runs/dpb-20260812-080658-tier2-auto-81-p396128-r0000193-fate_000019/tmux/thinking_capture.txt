Proof

Let $F$ be a field, regarded as a ring. We show that the only (two-sided) ideals
of $F$ are $(0)={0}$ and $(1)=F$.

$(0)$ and $(1)$ are ideals. The set ${0}$ is the zero ideal: it is an additive
subgroup and closed under multiplication by arbitrary ring elements. The whole
ring $F=(1)$ is an ideal for the same reason. These are distinct since $F$
contains $1\neq 0$ (a field has $1\neq 0$ by definition).

Every nonzero ideal equals $F$. Let $I\subseteq F$ be an ideal with $I\neq{0}$.
Pick any $a\in I$ with $a\neq 0$. Since $F$ is a field, $a$ is a unit: there
exists $a^{-1}\in F$ with $a^{-1}a=1$. By the ideal property (closure under
multiplication by arbitrary ring elements), $a^{-1}\cdot a = 1 \in I$. Now for
any $r\in F$, $r = r\cdot 1 \in I$ (again by the ideal property, since $1\in
I$). Hence $F\subseteq I$, so $I=F$.

Conclusion. The only ideals of $F$ are ${0}=(0)$ and $F=(1)$. $\blacksquare$
