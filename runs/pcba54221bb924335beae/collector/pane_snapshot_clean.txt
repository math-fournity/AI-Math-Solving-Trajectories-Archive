Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the product of two smooth
projective geometrically integral $k$-schemes (char 0), each having a 0-cycle of
degree 1, itself has a 0-cycle of degree 1.

Answer: Yes, $X \times_k Y$ has a 0-cycle of degree 1.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Proof

Let $Z = \sum_i a_i, [x_i]$ be a 0-cycle on $X$ with $$\deg(Z) = \sum_i a_i,
[k(x_i):k] = 1,$$ and let $W = \sum_j b_j, [y_j]$ be a 0-cycle on $Y$ with $$
\deg(W) = \sum_j b_j, [k(y_j):k] = 1.$$

Here the $x_i$ (resp. $y_j$) are closed points of $X$ (resp. $Y$), and $a_i, b_j
\in \mathbb{Z}$.

The external product 0-cycle

Define the external product 0-cycle on $X \times_k Y$: $$Z \times W ;=;
\sum_{i,j} a_i, b_j, [x_i \times y_j],$$ where $[x_i \times y_j]$ denotes the
fundamental cycle of the closed subscheme $${x_i} \times_k {y_j} ;=;
\operatorname{Spec}!\big(k(x_i) \otimes_k k(y_j)\big) ;\subset; X \times_k Y.$$

This is a 0-dimensional closed subscheme of finite type over $k$, so its
fundamental cycle is a well-defined 0-cycle on $X \times_k Y$.

Degree of the product of two closed points

Key claim. For closed points $x \in X$ and $y \in Y$: $$\deg\big([x \times
y]\big) ;=; [k(x):k] \cdot [k(y):k].$$

Proof of claim. The scheme ${x}\times_k{y} = \operatorname{Spec}(k(x)\otimes_k
k(y_j))$ is 0-dimensional and of finite type over $k$, so $A := k(x)\otimes_k
k(y)$ is a finite-dimensional $k$-algebra. It decomposes as a product of
Artinian local $k$-algebras: $$A ;\cong; \prod_{t} A_t, \qquad A_t \text{ local
with residue field } K_t.$$

The fundamental cycle of $\operatorname{Spec} A$ is $$[\operatorname{Spec}
A] ;=; \sum_t \ell_{A_t}(A_t), [z_t],$$ where $z_t = \operatorname{Spec} K_t$ is
the unique closed point of $\operatorname{Spec} A_t$ and $\ell_{A_t}(A_t)$ is
the length. Its degree is: $$\deg\big([\operatorname{Spec} A]\big) ;=; \sum_t
\ell_{A_t}(A_t),_t : k] ;=; \sum_t \dim_k(A_t) ;=; \dim_k(A).$$

Since $A = k(x) \otimes_k k(y)$ is a tensor product of two finite field
extensions of $k$: $$\dim_k!\big(k(x)\otimes_k k(y)\big) ;=;
[k(x):k]\cdot[k(y):k].$$

This establishes the claim. $\checkmark$

│ Remark. This identity holds in any characteristic — the nilpotent structure of
│ $A$ (when $k(x)/k$ or $k(y)/k$ is inseparable) is fully captured by the length
│ factors $\ell_{A_t}(A_t)$, and the total $k$-dimension is always the product
│ of extension degrees. The characteristic-0 hypothesis ensures all residue
│ field extensions are separable (so $A$ is reduced, a product of fields), but
│ the degree formula itself is unconditional.

Computing the degree of $Z \times W$

Using the claim: $$\deg(Z \times W) ;=; \sum_{i,j} a_i, b_j, \deg\big([x_i
\times y_j]\big) ;=; \sum_{i,j} a_i, b_j, [k(x_i):k],[k(y_j):k].$$

Factoring the double sum: $$\deg(Z \times W) ;=; \Big(\sum_i a_i,
[k(x_i):k]\Big)\Big(\sum_j b_j, [k(y_j):k]\Big) ;=; \deg(Z)\cdot\deg(W) ;=; 1
\cdot 1 ;=; 1.$$

Conclusion

The 0-cycle $Z \times W$ on $X \times_k Y$ has degree $1$. Therefore $X \times_k
Y$ has a 0-cycle of degree 1.

$\blacksquare$
