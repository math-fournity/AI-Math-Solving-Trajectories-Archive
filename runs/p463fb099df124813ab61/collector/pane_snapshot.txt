## Proof

Since $X$ is symmetric positive definite, it admits a factorization $X = P^{T}P$
 with $P$ invertible (for instance, the Cholesky factor or the symmetric square
root). Define

$$B \;=\; P\,Y\,P^{-1}.$$

Because $B$ is similar to $Y$, they share the same eigenvalues, hence

$$\rho(Y) = \rho(B).$$

Now compute $X - Y^{T}XY$ in terms of $B$. Using $Y = P^{-1}BP$ (equivalently $P
Y = BP$),

$$Y^{T}XY \;=\; Y^{T}P^{T}P\,Y \;=\; (PY)^{T}(PY) \;=\; (BP)^{T}(BP) \;=\; P^{T}
B^{T}B\,P.$$

Therefore

$$X - Y^{T}XY \;=\; P^{T}P - P^{T}B^{T}B\,P \;=\; P^{T}\!\bigl(I - B^{T}B\bigr)P
.$$

By hypothesis $X - Y^{T}XY \succ 0$. Since $P$ is invertible, congruence by $P$
preserves positive definiteness, so

$$I - B^{T}B \;\succ\; 0,$$

i.e. $B^{T}B \prec I$. This means every eigenvalue of $B^{T}B$ is strictly less
than $1$, so every singular value of $B$ is strictly less than $1$:

$$\sigma_{\max}(B) < 1.$$

The spectral radius is bounded by any operator norm; in particular

$$\rho(B) \;\le\; \|B\|_{2} \;=\; \sigma_{\max}(B) \;<\; 1.$$

Since $\rho(Y) = \rho(B)$, we conclude

$$\rho(Y) \;=\; \max_{i}\,|\lambda_{i}(Y)| \;<\; 1.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
