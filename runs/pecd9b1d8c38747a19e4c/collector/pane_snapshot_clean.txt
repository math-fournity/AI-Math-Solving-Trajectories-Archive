# Wave Equation with Mixed Boundary Conditions

## Problem Setup

We solve $u_{tt} = c^2 u_{xx}$ on $0 < x < L$ (with $L=10$), with:
- $u(x,0) = f(x) = \begin{cases}1 & 4<x<5\\0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}$, $\;u_
t(x,0)=g(x)=0$
- $u(0,t)=0$ (Dirichlet), $\;u_x(L,t)=0$ (Neumann)

---

## (a) Method of Characteristics

**D'Alembert form on an infinite line.** The general solution is $u(x,t)=F(x-ct)
+G(x+ct)$. The initial data give $F(x)+G(x)=f(x)$ and $-cF'(x)+cG'(x)=g(x)=0$. S
ince $g=0$, $F'=G'$, so $F-G=\text{const}$, which we absorb into the arbitrary f
unctions to obtain $F=G=f/2$. Thus on a free domain:

$$u(x,t)=\tfrac12\bigl[f(x-ct)+f(x+ct)\bigr].$$

**Handling the boundaries by reflection.** The two boundary conditions dictate h
ow the traveling waves reflect:

- **At $x=0$ (Dirichlet $u=0$): odd reflection.** A left-moving pulse reflects w
ith a **sign change**.
- **At $x=L$ (Neumann $u_x=0$): even reflection.** A right-moving pulse reflects
 **without sign change**.

We extend $f$ to all of $\mathbb{R}$ by requiring $f_{\text{ext}}(-x)=-f_{\text{
ext}}(x)$ (odd about $0$) and $f_{\text{ext}}(2L-x)=f_{\text{ext}}(x)$ (even abo
ut $L$). These two reflections compose to give $f_{\text{ext}}(x+2L)=-f_{\text{e
xt}}(x)$, hence $f_{\text{ext}}$ is **$4L$-periodic** (here period $40$).

**Sketch of the solution.** The initial rectangular pulse (height $1$ on $(4,5)$
) splits into two half-amplitude pulses:

1. **Right-traveling pulse** (height $\tfrac12$, on $(4,5)$, moving right at spe
ed $c$):
   - Reaches $x=10$ at $t=5/c$. Reflects **evenly** (no sign change) → becomes a
 right-to-left pulse of height $+\tfrac12$.
   - Reaches $x=0$ at $t=15/c$. Reflects **oddly** (sign flip) → becomes a left-
to-right pulse of height $-\tfrac12$.
   - Reaches $x=10$ at $t=25/c$. Reflects evenly → height $-\tfrac12$ moving lef
t.
   - Reaches $x=0$ at $t=35/c$. Reflects oddly → height $+\tfrac12$ moving right
.
   - At $t=40/c$ the configuration repeats (period $4L/c=40/c$).

2. **Left-traveling pulse** (height $\tfrac12$, on $(4,5)$, moving left at speed
 $c$):
   - Reaches $x=0$ at $t=4/c$. Reflects **oddly** (sign flip) → height $-\tfrac1
2$ moving right.
   - Reaches $x=10$ at $t=14/c$. Reflects evenly → height $-\tfrac12$ moving lef
t.
   - Reaches $x=0$ at $t=24/c$. Reflects oddly → height $+\tfrac12$ moving right
.
   - Reaches $x=10$ at $t=34/c$. Reflects evenly → height $+\tfrac12$ moving lef
t.
   - Period $40/c$.

The full solution is the superposition of these two bouncing pulses, each of hei
ght $\tfrac12$ (with appropriate signs after each reflection), repeating with pe
riod $4L/c = 40/c$.

## (b) Fourier-Series Solution

**Eigenfunctions.** Separation of variables $u=X(x)T(t)$ with $X(0)=0$, $X'(L)=0
$ gives eigenfunctions

$$X_n(x)=\sin\!\left(\frac{(2n+1)\pi x}{2L}\right),\qquad n=0,1,2,\dots$$

with eigenvalues $\lambda_n=\left(\frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2L}\right)^2$ and temporal fr
equencies $\omega_n = c\,\frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2L}$.

(Verification: $X_n(0)=\sin 0=0$ ✓; $\;X_n'(L)=\frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2L}\cos\frac{(2n
+1)\pi}{2}=0$ ✓.)

**General solution:**

$$u(x,t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[A_n\cos(\omega_n t)+B_n\sin(\omega_n t)\right]
\sin\!\left(\frac{(2n+1)\pi x}{2L}\right).$$

**Determining coefficients.** From $u_t(x,0)=0$:

$$u_t(x,0)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} B_n\,\omega_n\,\sin\!\left(\frac{(2n+1)\pi x}{2L}
\right)=0 \implies B_n=0 \;\;\forall\, n.$$

From $u(x,0)=f(x)$, using orthogonality $\int_0^L \sin^2\!\left(\frac{(2n+1)\pi
x}{2L}\right)dx = \frac{L}{2}$:

$$A_n = \frac{2}{L}\int_0^L f(x)\sin\!\left(\frac{(2n+1)\pi x}{2L}\right)dx.$$

With $L=10$ and $f=1$ on $(4,5)$:

$$A_n = \frac{2}{10}\int_4^5 \sin\!\left(\frac{(2n+1)\pi x}{20}\right)dx = \frac
{1}{5}\left[-\frac{20}{(2n+1)\pi}\cos\!\left(\frac{(2n+1)\pi x}{20}\right)\right
]_4^5$$

$$=\frac{-4}{(2n+1)\pi}\left[\cos\!\left(\frac{(2n+1)\pi}{4}\right)-\cos\!\left(
\frac{(2n+1)\pi}{5}\right)\right].$$

**Final Fourier-series solution:**

$$\boxed{u(x,t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{-4}{(2n+1)\pi}\left[\cos\frac{(2n+1)\pi
}{4}-\cos\frac{(2n+1)\pi}{5}\right]\cos\!\left(\frac{c(2n+1)\pi t}{20}\right)\si
n\!\left(\frac{(2n+1)\pi x}{20}\right).}$$

## (c) Solution by Conversion to an Infinite Domain

**Extension of initial data.** We extend $f$ from $[0,L]$ to all of $\mathbb{R}$
 via:

1. **Odd extension about $x=0$:** $\;f_{\text{ext}}(-x) = -f_{\text{ext}}(x)$,
2. **Even extension about $x=L$:** $\;f_{\text{ext}}(2L-x) = f_{\text{ext}}(x)$.

These two symmetries generate a function that is $4L$-periodic (since $f_{\text{
ext}}(x+2L)=-f_{\text{ext}}(x)$, hence $f_{\text{ext}}(x+4L)=f_{\text{ext}}(x)$)
. Since $g\equiv 0$, its extension is also zero.

**Explicit construction of $f_{\text{ext}}$ on $[0,40]$ (one period, $L=10$):**

| Interval | Source | Value |
|---|---|---|
| $(4,5)$ | original $f$ | $+1$ |
| $(15,16)$ | even reflection about $10$: $f(20-x)$, $1$ when $4<20-x<5$ | $+1$
|
| $(24,25)$ | anti-periodicity $f_{\text{ext}}(x)=-f_{\text{ext}}(x-20)$, origin
al $(4,5)$ | $-1$ |
| $(35,36)$ | anti-periodicity applied to $(15,16)$ | $-1$ |

Everywhere else on $[0,40]$: $f_{\text{ext}}=0$. This pattern repeats with perio
d $40$.

**Equivalent infinite-domain problem.** Solve $u_{tt}=c^2 u_{xx}$ on $\mathbb{R}
$ with initial data $u(x,0)=f_{\text{ext}}(x)$, $u_t(x,0)=0$. By d'Alembert's fo
rmula:

$$u(x,t)=\frac12\bigl[f_{\text{ext}}(x-ct)+f_{\text{ext}}(x+ct)\bigr]+\frac{1}{2
c}\int_{x-ct}^{x+ct}0\;ds=\frac12\bigl[f_{\text{ext}}(x-ct)+f_{\text{ext}}(x+ct)
\bigr].$$

**Restriction to $0<x<L$ gives the solution to the original problem.**

**Why the boundary conditions are satisfied:**
- **$u(0,t)=0$:** $u(0,t)=\frac12[f_{\text{ext}}(-ct)+f_{\text{ext}}(ct)]=\frac1
2[-f_{\text{ext}}(ct)+f_{\text{ext}}(ct)]=0$ by odd symmetry. ✓
- **$u_x(L,t)=0$:** $u_x(L,t)=\frac12[f_{\text{ext}}'(L-ct)+f_{\text{ext}}'(L+ct
)]$. By even symmetry about $L$, $f_{\text{ext}}'(L-s)=-f_{\text{ext}}'(L+s)$, s
o the two terms cancel. ✓

**Final solution:**

$$\boxed{u(x,t)=\frac12\bigl[f_{\text{ext}}(x-ct)+f_{\text{ext}}(x+ct)\bigr],\qq
uad 0<x<10,}$$

where $f_{\text{ext}}$ is the $4L$-periodic ($=40$-periodic) extension of $f$ ob
tained by odd reflection about $x=0$ and even reflection about $x=L=10$.

---

**Equivalence of all three parts.** Part (a) is the physical/characteristic inte
rpretation of the same formula in (c). Part (b) is the spectral (eigenfunction)
representation of the same solution. The Fourier series in (b) is the Fourier se
ries of the periodic function $\frac12[f_{\text{ext}}(x-ct)+f_{\text{ext}}(x+ct)
]$, and the eigenfunctions $\sin\frac{(2n+1)\pi x}{2L}$ are precisely the Fourie
r modes compatible with the odd-about-$0$ / even-about-$L$ symmetry. All three m
ethods yield the same unique solution.
