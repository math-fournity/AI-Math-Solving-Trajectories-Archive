Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the open unit ball $B(0,1)
\subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ can be written as a countable disjoint union of half-ope
n cubes (each edge independently open or closed) with all edge lengths strictly
less than 1.

**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

**Step 1: Dyadic decomposition of open sets.**

Consider the collection of all dyadic half-open cubes in $\mathbb{R}^n$:
$$\mathcal{D} = \left\{ \prod_{i=1}^n \left[\frac{m_i}{2^j}, \frac{m_i+1}{2^j}\r
ight) : m_i \in \mathbb{Z},\; j \in \mathbb{Z} \right\}.$$

**Key property (nesting):** Any two dyadic cubes are either nested or disjoint.
This holds because at each fixed level $j$, the cubes $\prod [m_i/2^j, (m_i+1)/2
^j)$ partition $\mathbb{R}^n$, and passing to level $j+1$ subdivides each cube i
nto $2^n$ subcubes. Thus if two dyadic cubes intersect, one must contain the oth
er.

**Every point of $B(0,1)$ lies in some dyadic cube contained in $B(0,1)$.** Give
n $x \in B(0,1)$, since $B(0,1)$ is open, there exists $r > 0$ with $B(x, r) \su
bseteq B(0,1)$. For $j$ sufficiently large, the level-$j$ dyadic cube containing
 $x$ has edge length $2^{-j} < r/\sqrt{n}$, hence diameter $< r$, so it is conta
ined in $B(x,r) \subseteq B(0,1)$.

**Maximal cubes exist and partition $B(0,1)$.** Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the set of
all maximal dyadic cubes contained in $B(0,1)$ (i.e., $Q \in \mathcal{F}$ if $Q
\subseteq B(0,1)$ and no larger dyadic cube containing $Q$ lies in $B(0,1)$). Ma
ximality is achievable because a dyadic cube inside $B(0,1)$ has edge length $<
2/\sqrt{n}$ (its diameter must be $< 2$), so the chain of containing dyadic cube
s is finite.

By the nesting property, cubes in $\mathcal{F}$ are pairwise disjoint. By the ex
istence result above, every point of $B(0,1)$ lies in some dyadic cube $\subsete
q B(0,1)$, hence in some maximal one. Therefore:
$$B(0,1) = \bigcup_{Q \in \mathcal{F}} Q, \quad \text{with } \mathcal{F} \text{
countable and pairwise disjoint.}$$

Each $Q \in \mathcal{F}$ is of the form $\prod [m_i/2^{j_Q}, (m_i+1)/2^{j_Q})$ w
ith edge length $\ell_Q = 2^{-j_Q}$.

**Step 2: Subdivision to ensure edge length $< 1$.**

If $j_Q \geq 1$, then $\ell_Q \leq 1/2 < 1$ — already satisfactory.

If $j_Q \leq 0$ (so $\ell_Q \geq 1$), subdivide $Q$ into $2^n$ dyadic subcubes o
f level $j_Q + 1$. Concretely, each coordinate interval $[a, a+\ell)$ splits int
o $[a, a+\ell/2)$ and $[a+\ell/2, a+\ell)$, which are disjoint half-open interva
ls whose union is $[a, a+\ell)$. Applying this to all $n$ coordinates yields $2^
n$ disjoint half-open subcubes whose union is $Q$.

Repeat until the level reaches $\geq 1$. This requires at most $|j_Q| + 1$ subdi
visions (since $j_Q \leq 0$), producing cubes of edge length $2^{-1} = 1/2 < 1$.

Perform this for every $Q \in \mathcal{F}$ with $\ell_Q \geq 1$. The result is a
 refined collection $\mathcal{F}'$ that is:
- **Countable:** each cube is replaced by finitely many subcubes, and $\mathcal{
F}$ was countable.
- **Pairwise disjoint:** subdivision preserves disjointness (subcubes of distinc
t original cubes remain disjoint; subcubes within one cube are disjoint by const
ruction).
- **Covering $B(0,1)$:** the union is unchanged: $\bigcup_{Q' \in \mathcal{F}'}
Q' = \bigcup_{Q \in \mathcal{F}} Q = B(0,1)$.
- **All edge lengths $< 1$:** every cube has edge length $\leq 1/2$.

Each cube uses the convention $[a, b)$ on every edge (closed on the left, open o
n the right), which is a valid instance of "each edge can be either open or clos
ed."

**Conclusion.** The collection $\mathcal{F}'$ is a countable, disjoint collectio
n of half-open cubes with maximum edge length $\leq 1/2 < 1$ whose union is exac
tly $B(0,1)$. Therefore, **yes**, such a covering exists for every $n \geq 1$.
