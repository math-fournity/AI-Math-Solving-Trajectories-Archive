Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a non-constant entire funct
ion always has curves to infinity along which it is unbounded, even when ∞ is no
t an asymptotic value.

The key distinction is:
- **∞ is an asymptotic value**: there exists a curve γ → ∞ with f(γ(t)) → ∞ (eve
ntually exceeds every bound).
- **f is unbounded along a curve**: sup|f(γ(t))| = ∞ (but f may return to small
values).

The latter is strictly weaker. Here is the proof.

---

**Theorem.** Let f be a non-constant entire function. Then there exists a curve
γ: [0,∞) → ℂ with |γ(t)| → ∞ as t → ∞ such that f is unbounded along γ, i.e., su
p_{t≥0} |f(γ(t))| = ∞. This holds regardless of whether ∞ is an asymptotic value
 of f.

**Proof.**

**Step 1 (f is unbounded).** Since f is entire and non-constant, Liouville's the
orem implies f is unbounded on ℂ.

**Step 2 (Unboundedness occurs at points escaping to infinity).** We construct a
 sequence (z_n) with |z_n| → ∞ and |f(z_n)| → ∞.

For each n ≥ 1, since f is unbounded, pick w_n with |f(w_n)| > n. We claim |w_n|
 → ∞ along a subsequence. Suppose not: then there exist R > 0 and a subsequence
w_{n_k} with |w_{n_k}| ≤ R. Since f is continuous on the compact set D̄(0, R+1),
it is bounded there, say |f| ≤ M. But for n_k > M we have |f(w_{n_k})| > n_k > M
 while w_{n_k} ∈ D̄(0, R+1) — contradiction. Hence, passing to a subsequence, we
obtain z_n with |z_n| > n and |f(z_n)| > n for all n.

**Step 3 (Constructing the curve).** For each n, both z_n and z_{n+1} lie in the
 region {z : |z| > n−1}, which is path-connected. Choose a continuous path γ_n:
[n−1, n] → {z : |z| > n−1} with γ_n(n−1) = z_n and γ_n(n) = z_{n+1}. Concatenati
ng these paths gives a continuous curve γ: [0,∞) → ℂ.

For t ∈ [n−1, n], we have |γ(t)| > n−1, so |γ(t)| → ∞ as t → ∞.

**Step 4 (f is unbounded along γ).** At the points t = n−1, we have γ(n−1) = z_n
, so |f(γ(n−1))| = |f(z_n)| > n. Therefore

sup_{t≥0} |f(γ(t))| ≥ sup_{n} |f(z_n)| = ∞.

Thus f is unbounded along γ. ∎

**Remark on the role of the asymptotic-value condition.** The hypothesis "∞ is n
ot an asymptotic value" means no curve to infinity has f → ∞ *convergently* (i.e
., for every M, |f(γ(t))| > M for all sufficiently large t). Our construction on
ly guarantees *unboundedness* along γ — f may oscillate, returning to small valu
es between the points z_n. This is strictly weaker than convergence to ∞, so the
 construction is fully compatible with ∞ not being an asymptotic value.

**Conclusion.** The answer is **yes**: it is always true. For every non-constant
 entire function, regardless of whether ∞ is an asymptotic value, there exist cu
rves to infinity along which the function is unbounded. The essential reason is
that a non-constant entire function is unbounded (Liouville), this unboundedness
 must occur along points escaping to infinity (continuity on compact sets), and
such points can always be connected by a curve to infinity.
