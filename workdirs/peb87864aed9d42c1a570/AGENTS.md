# Problem

In a remote industrial refinery, 300 automated sensors are installed along a pipeline to monitor chemical purity. There are 25 possible purity grades (labeled 1 through 25) that can be assigned to a sensor. During a system-wide calibration, each sensor is assigned exactly one purity grade. 

A critical system constraint, known to all sensors through their shared programming, is that no two purity grades are assigned to the same number of sensors; the count of sensors for each grade is strictly unique.

During the calibration procedure, each sensor is equipped with an optical scanner that allows it to detect the purity grades of the other 299 sensors, but it cannot detect its own internal grade. Simultaneously, every sensor must transmit a data packet reporting its own assigned purity grade. 

The sensors are able to coordinate their logic protocols and establish a shared algorithm before the calibration begins. Let $N$ be the maximum number of sensors that can be guaranteed to report their own purity grade correctly, regardless of how the grades are distributed among them (as long as the counts per grade remain distinct). 

Find $N$.
