Looking at this problem, I need to analyze the asymptotic behavior of $u_n$ defi
ned by $u_1 > 0$ and $u_{n+1} = \ln\!\left(1 + \frac{u_n}{n}\right)$, then deter
mine for which $\alpha$ the series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty u_n^\alpha$ converges.

## Proof

**Step 1: Positivity.** Since $u_1 > 0$ and $\ln(1+x) > 0$ for $x > 0$, by induc
tion $u_n > 0$ for all $n \geq 1$.

**Step 2: Upper bound via $\ln(1+x) \leq x$.** For all $n \geq 1$:
$$u_{n+1} = \ln\!\left(1 + \frac{u_n}{n}\right) \leq \frac{u_n}{n}.$$

**Step 3: $u_n \to 0$.** For $n \geq 2$, the bound gives $u_{n+1} \leq u_n/2$, s
o $(u_n)_{n\ge 2}$ is decreasing and bounded below by $0$; hence $u_n \to \ell \
geq 0$. Passing to the limit in $u_{n+1} \leq u_n/n$ gives $\ell \leq 0$, so $\e
ll = 0$.

**Step 4: Factorial decay.** Iterating the inequality from Step 2 for $n \geq 2$
:
$$u_n \leq u_2 \cdot \prod_{k=2}^{n-1}\frac{1}{k} = \frac{u_2}{(n-1)!}, \qquad n
 \geq 2.$$

**Step 5: Convergence for $\alpha > 0$.** For $\alpha > 0$ and $n \geq 2$:
$$0 < u_n^\alpha \leq \frac{u_2^\alpha}{\big((n-1)!\big)^\alpha}.$$
The series $\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{1}{((n-1)!)^\alpha}$ converges by the rati
o test (ratio of consecutive terms $= 1/n^\alpha \to 0$). By comparison, $\sum_{
n=1}^{\infty} u_n^\alpha$ converges.

**Step 6: Divergence for $\alpha = 0$.** Since $u_n > 0$ for all $n$, $u_n^0 = 1
$, so $\sum u_n^0 = \sum 1 = +\infty$.

**Step 7: Divergence for $\alpha < 0$.** Since $u_n \to 0$ (Step 3) and $u_n > 0
$, we have $u_n^\alpha \to +\infty$. The general term does not tend to zero, so
$\sum u_n^\alpha$ diverges.

**Conclusion.** The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} u_n^\alpha$ **conve
rges if and only if $\alpha > 0$**, and diverges for $\alpha \leq 0$.

The key mechanism is that the recurrence $u_{n+1} = \ln(1 + u_n/n) \leq u_n/n$ f
orces factorial decay $u_n = O(1/(n-1)!)$, which is fast enough to make $\sum u_
n^\alpha$ converge for every positive $\alpha$ — there is no nontrivial critical
 exponent.
