## Solution

We evaluate $\int_{|z|=4} \tan z\, dz$ using the residue theorem.

**Poles of $\tan z$.** Since $\tan z = \dfrac{\sin z}{\cos z}$, the poles occur
where $\cos z = 0$, i.e. at
$$z_k = \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi, \qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}.$$
Each zero of $\cos z$ is simple (since $\cos' z_k = -\sin z_k \neq 0$), so each
$z_k$ is a **simple pole** of $\tan z$.

**Residue at each pole.** At a simple pole $z_k$,
$$\operatorname{Res}(\tan z,\, z_k) = \frac{\sin z_k}{\cos' z_k} = \frac{\sin z_
k}{-\sin z_k} = -1.$$

**Poles inside $|z|=4$.** Using $\pi/2 \approx 1.5708$ and $\pi \approx 3.14159$
:

| $k$ | $z_k$ | $\|z_k\|$ | inside $\|z\|<4$? |
|-----|-------|----------|------------------|
| $0$ | $\pi/2 \approx 1.571$ | $1.571$ | yes |
| $-1$ | $-\pi/2 \approx -1.571$ | $1.571$ | yes |
| $1$ | $3\pi/2 \approx 4.712$ | $4.712$ | no |
| $-2$ | $-3\pi/2 \approx -4.712$ | $4.712$ | no |
| $|k|\ge 2$ | $\|z_k\| \ge 5\pi/2 \approx 7.85$ | — | no |

Exactly **two** poles lie inside the contour: $z_0 = \pi/2$ and $z_{-1} = -\pi/2
$.

**Applying the residue theorem.**
$$\int_{|z|=4} \tan z\, dz = 2\pi i \sum_{\|z_k\|<4} \operatorname{Res}(\tan z,
z_k) = 2\pi i \bigl[(-1)+(-1)\bigr] = 2\pi i \cdot (-2).$$

$$\boxed{\int_{|z|=4} \tan z\, dz = -4\pi i.}$$
