## Proof

**Claim:** $\lim_{n\to\infty} r(n,k) = \infty$ for every fixed $k \in \mathbb{N}
$.

**Key tool:** The Prime Number Theorem gives $p_n \sim n\log n$, i.e., $\frac{p_
n}{n\log n} \to 1$ as $n\to\infty$.

**Step 1: Asymptotic behavior of each factor.**

For any fixed integer $i \geq 0$, as $n\to\infty$:
$$\frac{p_{n+i}}{n\log n} = \frac{p_{n+i}}{(n+i)\log(n+i)} \cdot \frac{(n+i)\log
(n+i)}{n\log n}.$$

- The first factor $\to 1$ by PNT (since $n+i \to \infty$).
- The second factor $\to 1$ since $(n+i)\log(n+i) = n\log n\cdot(1 + i/n)\cdot\f
rac{\log(n+i)}{\log n} \to n\log n \cdot 1 \cdot 1$.

Therefore, for each fixed $i \geq 0$:
$$\frac{p_{n+i}}{n\log n} \to 1. \tag{$\star$}$$

**Step 2: Compute the limit of $r(n,k)/(n\log n)$.**

Write
$$\frac{r(n,k)}{n\log n} = \frac{\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{k} p_{n+i}}{(n\log n)
\cdot p_{n+k+1}^{\,k}} = \frac{\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{k} \frac{p_{n+i}}{n\log
 n}}{\left(\frac{p_{n+k+1}}{n\log n}\right)^{k}}.$$

The numerator is a product of $k+1$ factors, each of the form $\frac{p_{n+i}}{n\
log n}$ with $i \in \{0,1,\dots,k\}$, all of which $\to 1$ by $(\star)$.

The denominator is $\left(\frac{p_{n+k+1}}{n\log n}\right)^k$, where $\frac{p_{n
+k+1}}{n\log n} \to 1$ by $(\star)$ with $i = k+1$.

Therefore:
$$\frac{r(n,k)}{n\log n} \to \frac{1^{k+1}}{1^k} = 1.$$

**Step 3: Conclude.**

Since $r(n,k) \sim n\log n$ and $n\log n \to \infty$ as $n\to\infty$, we conclud
e:
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} r(n,k) = \infty \quad \text{for every } k \in \mathbb{N}.$$

The limit exists (in the extended sense) and equals $+\infty$, so the secondary
question about $\limsup$ is moot — it is automatically satisfied. $\blacksquare$
