## Answer

**Yes.** A map that preserves the inner product necessarily preserves distance,
and this conclusion does **not** depend on linearity at all.

### Setup

Let $E$ be a prehilbertian space with inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$
and induced norm $\|\cdot\|$. Let $f:E\to E$ be a map (no linearity assumed) tha
t **preserves the inner product**, meaning

$$\langle f(x),\,f(y)\rangle \;=\; \langle x,\,y\rangle \qquad \text{for all } x
,y\in E.$$

We must show that $f$ preserves distance, i.e.

$$\|f(x)-f(y)\| \;=\; \|x-y\| \qquad \text{for all } x,y\in E.$$

### Proof

Fix $x,y\in E$. Using only the definition of the norm through the inner product
and the preservation hypothesis:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|f(x)-f(y)\|^{2}
&= \big\langle f(x)-f(y),\; f(x)-f(y)\big\rangle \\
&= \langle f(x),f(x)\rangle - 2\langle f(x),f(y)\rangle + \langle f(y),f(y)\rang
le \\
&= \langle x,x\rangle - 2\langle x,y\rangle + \langle y,y\rangle \qquad\text{(in
ner-product preservation)}\\
&= \langle x-y,\; x-y\rangle \\
&= \|x-y\|^{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Since both sides are non-negative, taking square roots gives

$$\|f(x)-f(y)\| = \|x-y\|.$$

As $x,y$ were arbitrary, $f$ preserves distance. ∎

### Remark on the "non-linear" hypothesis

The argument above uses **only** the equality of inner products; linearity of $f
$ is never invoked. In fact, if one additionally requires $f(0)=0$, then inner-p
roduct preservation *forces* $f$ to be linear: setting $g=f(x+y)-f(x)-f(y)$ and
expanding $\|g\|^{2}$ using preservation gives $\|g\|^{2}=0$, hence $f(x+y)=f(x)
+f(y)$, and a similar computation gives $f(\lambda x)=\lambda f(x)$. Thus a genu
inely "non-linear" inner-product-preserving map can only be **affine** (a linear
 isometry composed with a translation). Even then, the distance-preservation com
putation above is unaffected, because the constant translation cancels in every
difference $f(x)-f(y)$.

### Conclusion

Inner-product preservation implies distance preservation **purely algebraically*
*, with no linearity required. The distance is recovered from the inner product
via the polarization identity $\|u-v\|^{2}=\langle u,u\rangle-2\langle u,v\rangl
e+\langle v,v\rangle$, and every term on the right is preserved by hypothesis.
