**Answer: No.** The triple does not induce the canonical mixed Hodge structure.
While the Hodge filtration $j_* F^\bullet$ is correct (for a good compactificati

### Setup

Let $X$ be a smooth variety, $j: X \hookrightarrow \bar{X}$ a smooth compactific
ation with $D = \bar{X} \setminus X$ a normal crossings divisor. Let $A_X^*$ be
a suitable resolution of $\mathbb{Q}_X$ so that $j_* A_X^*$ computes $Rj_*\mathb
b{Q}_X$, giving $H^*(X,\mathbb{Q}) \simeq H^*(\bar{X}, j_* A_X^*)$.

### The Hodge filtration is correct

For a normal crossings divisor $D$, the classical extension theorem gives $j_* \
Omega_X^p = \Omega_{\bar{X}}^p(\log D)$. Therefore:
$$j_* F^p \Omega_X^\bullet = j_* \Omega_X^{\geq p} = \Omega_{\bar{X}}^{\geq p}(\
log D),$$
which is precisely the Hodge filtration in Deligne's construction. So the second
 component of the triple is correct.

### The weight filtration is wrong

The weight filtration $W$ on $H^k(X)$ is defined via the Leray spectral sequence
:
$$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(\bar{X}, R^q j_* \mathbb{Q}_X) \Rightarrow H^{p+q}(X, \mathbb{
Q}).$$

**Key fact:** $R^q j_* \mathbb{Q}_X$ has weight $2q$ as a mixed Hodge sheaf. Loc
ally near a point where $r$ components of $D$ meet, $U \cap X \simeq (\Delta^*)^
r \times \Delta^{n-r}$, so $H^q(U \cap X) \cong \bigwedge^q \mathbb{Q}(-1)^r$, w
hich is pure of weight $2q$. Therefore $H^p(\bar{X}, R^q j_* \mathbb{Q}_X)$ has
weight $p + 2q$.

Since $p + q = k$ on $H^k$, the $(p,q)$-piece has weight $p + 2q = k + q$.

**The correct weight filtration** is:
$$W_m H^k(X) = \sum_{\substack{p+q=k \\ p+2q \leq m}} E_\infty^{p,q} = \sum_{q \
leq m-k} E_\infty^{k-q,\, q}.$$

**The naive truncation** $\tau_{\leq m}\, Rj_* \mathbb{Q}_X$ instead gives:
$$W_m^{\tau}\, H^k(X) = \sum_{q \leq m} E_\infty^{k-q,\, q},$$
which corresponds to weight $\leq k + m$, not weight $\leq m$. The relationship
is:
$$W_m^{\tau}\, H^k = W_{k+m}\, H^k \neq W_m\, H^k \quad \text{in general}.$$

The weight filtration requires a **degree-dependent shift**: $W_m H^k = \operato
rname{Im}\!\big(H^k(\bar{X},\, \tau_{\leq(m-k)}\, Rj_* \mathbb{Q}) \to H^k(\bar{
X}, Rj_* \mathbb{Q})\big)$, not $\tau_{\leq m}$. The naive truncation conflates
the cohomological degree $q$ of $R^q j_*$ with the weight, but the actual weight
 is $k + q$, not $q$.

### Counterexample: $X = \mathbb{C}^*$, $\bar{X} = \mathbb{P}^1$, $D = \{0, \inf
ty\}$

Here $H^0(X) = \mathbb{Q}$ (weight 0) and $H^1(X) = \mathbb{Q}(-1)$ (weight 2, H
odge type $(1,1)$).

The complex $Rj_* \mathbb{Q}_X$ has:
- $R^0 j_* \mathbb{Q}_X = \mathbb{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^1}$
- $R^1 j_* \mathbb{Q}_X = \mathbb{Q}_0 \oplus \mathbb{Q}_\infty$ (skyscraper she
aves)

The Leray spectral sequence gives $H^1(X) = E_\infty^{0,1} = \ker(d_2: \mathbb{Q
}^2 \to \mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Q}$, with weight $0 + 2\cdot 1 = 2$.

**With the naive truncation:**
- $W_0^{\tau}\, H^1 = 0$ (from $\tau_{\leq 0}$, which kills $R^1 j_*$)
- $W_1^{\tau}\, H^1 = \mathbb{Q}$ (from $\tau_{\leq 1}$, the full complex)

This assigns **weight 1** to $H^1(X)$, but the correct weight is **2**.

**Moreover, the triple fails to define any valid mixed Hodge structure.** The Ho
dge filtration on $H^1(\mathbb{C}^*)$ is $F^0 = \mathbb{C}$, $F^1 = \mathbb{C}$,
 $F^2 = 0$ (since $dz/z \in \Omega^1_{\mathbb{P}^1}(\log D) \cong \mathcal{O}_{\
mathbb{P}^1}$ generates $H^1$). For $\operatorname{Gr}^W_1 H^1 = \mathbb{Q}$ to
be a pure Hodge structure of weight 1, we would need the Hodge decomposition $H_
\mathbb{C} = H^{1,0} \oplus H^{0,1}$ with $H^{1,0} = F^1 \cap \overline{F^0} = \
mathbb{C}$ and $H^{0,1} = F^0 \cap \overline{F^1} = \mathbb{C}$. But then $H^{1,
0} \cap H^{0,1} = \mathbb{C} \neq 0$, violating the direct sum condition. So the
 pair $(W^\tau, F)$ does not even satisfy the axioms of a mixed Hodge structure.

### Conclusion

The correct weight filtration is the one constructed by Deligne using the filtra
tion by the number of logarithmic poles on $\Omega_{\bar{X}}^\bullet(\log D)$ (e
quivalently, the combinatorial filtration from the strata of the boundary diviso
r), which involves the degree-dependent shift $W_m H^k = \tau_{\leq(m-k)}$. The
naive truncation $\tau^{\leq\bullet}$ ignores this shift and therefore:

1. **Gives wrong weights** (off by $k$ on $H^k$), and
2. **Fails the compatibility axiom** between $W$ and $F$ (the induced filtration
 on $\operatorname{Gr}^W_m$ does not yield a pure Hodge structure of weight $m$)
.

The triple $(j_* A_X^*,\, j_* F^\bullet,\, \tau^{\leq\bullet} j_* A_X^*)$ does *
*not** induce the canonical mixed Hodge structure on $H^*(X)$.
