**Step 1: Algebraic substitution.**

Starting from:
$$ax^4 + 2x^3 - 2(2b-a)x^2 + 2x + a = 0$$

Expanding and regrouping:
$$a(x^4 + 2x^2 + 1) + 2x(x^2+1) - 4bx^2 = 0$$
$$a(x^2+1)^2 + 2x(x^2+1) - 4bx^2 = 0$$

Dividing by $(x^2+1)^2 > 0$:
$$a + \frac{2x}{x^2+1} - \frac{4bx^2}{(x^2+1)^2} = 0$$

Setting $y = \frac{x}{x^2+1}$, so that $\frac{x^2}{(x^2+1)^2} = y^2$, this becom
es:
$$a + 2y - 4by^2 = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a = 4by^2 - 2y$$

**Step 2: Range of $y$.**

The function $y = \frac{x}{x^2+1}$ has derivative $y' = \frac{1-x^2}{(x^2+1)^2}$
, vanishing at $x = \pm 1$. We get $y(1) = \frac{1}{2}$ (max), $y(-1) = -\frac{1
}{2}$ (min), and $y \to 0$ as $x \to \pm\infty$. Thus:

$$y \in \left[-\tfrac{1}{2},\, \tfrac{1}{2}\right]$$

and every value in this interval is attained. So $(a,b)$ is **Resonant** iff $a
= f(y) := 4by^2 - 2y$ for some $y \in [-\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2}]$.

**Step 3: Analyze $f(y) = 4by^2 - 2y$ on $[-\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2}]$.**

Critical point: $f'(y) = 8by - 2 = 0 \implies y = \frac{1}{4b}$ (when $b \neq 0$
).

Endpoint values: $f(-\tfrac{1}{2}) = b + 1$, $\quad f(\tfrac{1}{2}) = b - 1$.

**Case $b > 0$ (upward parabola):**
- If $b \geq \frac{1}{2}$: vertex $y = \frac{1}{4b} \in [-\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{
2}]$. Minimum $= -\frac{1}{4b} < 0 < a$. Maximum $= b+1$ (at $y = -\frac{1}{2}$)

1,\, b+1]$ with $b+1 < \frac{3}{2} < 2 \leq a$. **No solution.**

So for $b > 0$: Resonant $\iff a \leq b + 1 \iff b \geq a - 1$. (Since $a \geq 2

**Case $b \leq 0$:**

- $b < 0$ (downward parabola): The maximum of $f$ is at most $\max(b+1,\, -\frac
{1}{4b}) \leq \max(1,\, \frac{1}{2}) = 1 < 2 \leq a$. **No solution.**

**Step 4: Identify the Resonant region.**

Resonant $\iff$ $b \geq a - 1$, with $a \in [2,6]$, $b \in [-2,2]$.

Since $a - 1 \geq 1 > 0$ and $b \leq 2$:
- For $a \in [2, 3]$: $a - 1 \in [1, 2]$, so $b \in [a-1,\, 2]$ is nonempty.
- For $a \in (3, 6]$: $a - 1 > 2 \geq b$, impossible.

The Resonant region is the triangle:
$$A = \{(a, b) : 2 \leq a \leq 3,\; a - 1 \leq b \leq 2\}$$

with vertices $(2, 1)$, $(2, 2)$, $(3, 2)$.
