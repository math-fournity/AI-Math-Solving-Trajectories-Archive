## Solution

We want $\lim_{n\to\infty} P_{n-t}(n)$, where $t\geq 0$ is fixed and $P_k(n)$ de
notes the number of partitions of $n$ into exactly $k$ parts.

**Key bijection.** A partition of $n$ into $n-t$ parts is a sequence
$$n = \lambda_1 \geq \lambda_2 \geq \cdots \geq \lambda_{n-t} \geq 1,\qquad \sum
 \lambda_i = n.$$
Since there are $n-t$ parts each at least $1$, the minimum possible sum is $n-t$
. The "excess" is
$$n - (n-t) = t.$$
Subtracting $1$ from every part, $\mu_i := \lambda_i - 1 \geq 0$, gives a partit
ion of $t$ into **at most** $n-t$ parts (some $\mu_i$ may be $0$). This map is r
eversible: given a partition of $t$ into at most $n-t$ parts, pad with zeros to
length $n-t$ and add $1$ to each entry. Hence
$$P_{n-t}(n) = \bigl|\{\text{partitions of } t \text{ into at most } n-t \text{
parts}\}\bigr|.$$

**Removing the restriction for large $n$.** Any partition of $t$ uses at most $t
$ parts (since each part is $\geq 1$). Therefore, once $n - t \geq t$, i.e. $n \
geq 2t$, the constraint "at most $n-t$ parts" is automatically satisfied by ever
y partition of $t$. Thus for all $n \geq 2t$,
$$P_{n-t}(n) = p(t),$$
where $p(t)$ is the total number of partitions of $t$ (with the convention $p(0)
=1$).

**Verification on small cases.**
- $t=0$: $P_n(n)=1$ (only $1+\cdots+1$), and $p(0)=1$. ✓
- $t=1$: $P_{n-1}(n)=1$ (one part is $2$, rest are $1$), and $p(1)=1$. ✓
- $t=2$: excess $2$ gives either one part $3$ (rest $1$) or two parts $2$ (rest
$1$), so $2$ partitions, and $p(2)=2$. ✓
- $t=3$: excess $3$ gives $\{4,1,\dots\}$, $\{3,2,1,\dots\}$, $\{2,2,2,1,\dots\}
$, so $3$ partitions, and $p(3)=3$. ✓

Since $P_{n-t}(n) = p(t)$ for every $n \geq 2t$, the sequence is eventually cons
tant, and so

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} P_{n-t}(n) \;=\; p(t),}$$

the number of partitions of the fixed integer $t$.
