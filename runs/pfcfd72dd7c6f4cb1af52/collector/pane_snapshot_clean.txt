# Mayer–Vietoris Sequence for de Rham Cohomology

## Statement

Let $M$ be a smooth manifold and let $U, V \subseteq M$ be open subsets with $M
= U \cup V$. Then there is a **long exact sequence** (the **Mayer–Vietoris seque
nce**)

$$
\cdots \longrightarrow H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(M) \longrightarrow H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k
}(U) \oplus H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(V) \longrightarrow H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(U \cap V)
 \longrightarrow H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k+1}(M) \longrightarrow \cdots
$$

extending in both directions, with the initial segment

$$
0 \to H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{0}(M) \to H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{0}(U)\oplus H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{0
}(V) \to H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{0}(U\cap V) \to H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{1}(M) \to \cdots
$$

## Proof

### Step 1. The short exact sequence of complexes

Let $\Omega^{*}(\cdot)$ denote the de Rham complex. Define two maps of complexes
:

- **Restriction** $r: \Omega^{*}(M) \to \Omega^{*}(U)\oplus\Omega^{*}(V)$, $\qua
d \omega \mapsto (\omega|_U,\;\omega|_V)$.
- **Difference** $\delta: \Omega^{*}(U)\oplus\Omega^{*}(V) \to \Omega^{*}(U\cap
V)$, $\quad (\alpha,\beta)\mapsto \alpha|_{U\cap V} - \beta|_{U\cap V}$.

**Claim.** The sequence
$$
0 \longrightarrow \Omega^{*}(M) \xrightarrow{\;r\;} \Omega^{*}(U)\oplus\Omega^{*
}(V) \xrightarrow{\;\delta\;} \Omega^{*}(U\cap V) \longrightarrow 0
\tag{1}
$$
is exact.

- **$r$ is injective.** If $\omega\in\Omega^{k}(M)$ restricts to $0$ on both $U$
 and $V$, then $\omega$ vanishes on $U\cup V = M$, so $\omega = 0$.

- **$\operatorname{im} r = \ker\delta$.**
  ($\subseteq$): For $\omega\in\Omega^{k}(M)$, $\delta(r(\omega)) = \omega|_{U\c
ap V}-\omega|_{U\cap V}=0$.
  ($\supseteq$): Suppose $(\alpha,\beta)\in\Omega^{k}(U)\oplus\Omega^{k}(V)$ wit
h $\alpha|_{U\cap V}=\beta|_{U\cap V}$. Define $\omega\in\Omega^{k}(M)$ by
  $$
  \omega(p) = \begin{cases}\alpha(p) & p\in U,\\ \beta(p) & p\in V.\end{cases}
  $$
  This is well-defined because $\alpha=\beta$ on $U\cap V$, and smooth because $
U,V$ are open. Then $r(\omega)=(\alpha,\beta)$.

- **$\delta$ is surjective.** This is the **partition-of-unity argument**. Let $
\{\rho_U,\rho_V\}$ be a smooth partition of unity subordinate to $\{U,V\}$: $\op
eratorname{supp}\rho_U\subset U$, $\operatorname{supp}\rho_V\subset V$, $\rho_U+
\rho_V=1$. Given $\eta\in\Omega^{k}(U\cap V)$, set
  $$
  \alpha = \rho_V\,\eta \in \Omega^{k}(U),\qquad \beta = -\rho_U\,\eta \in \Omeg
a^{k}(V),
  $$
  where we extend by zero off $U\cap V$ (legitimate since $\operatorname{supp}\r
ho_V\subset U$ and $\operatorname{supp}\rho_U\subset V$). Then
  $$
  \delta(\alpha,\beta) = \alpha|_{U\cap V}-\beta|_{U\cap V} = \rho_V\eta + \rho_
U\eta = (\rho_U+\rho_V)\eta = \eta.
  $$
  Hence $\delta$ is surjective.

Both $r$ and $\delta$ are **chain maps** (they commute with $d$ since restrictio
n and multiplication by a function do). Thus (1) is a short exact sequence of co
chain complexes.

### Step 2. From short exact sequence of complexes to long exact sequence in coh
omology

This is the standard **zig-zag lemma** of homological algebra. A short exact seq
uence of cochain complexes
$$
0\to A^{*}\to B^{*}\to C^{*}\to 0
$$
induces a long exact sequence in cohomology
$$
\cdots\to H^{k}(A^{*})\to H^{k}(B^{*})\to H^{k}(C^{*})\xrightarrow{\;d^{*}\;} H^
{k+1}(A^{*})\to\cdots
$$

Applying this to (1) with $A^{*}=\Omega^{*}(M)$, $B^{*}=\Omega^{*}(U)\oplus\Omeg
a^{*}(V)$, $C^{*}=\Omega^{*}(U\cap V)$, and using $H^{k}(\Omega^{*}(U)\oplus\Ome
ga^{*}(V))\cong H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(U)\oplus H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(V)$, we obtain
the **Mayer–Vietoris long exact sequence**:

$$
\cdots \longrightarrow H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(M) \xrightarrow{\;r^{*}\;} H_{\mathrm
{dR}}^{k}(U)\oplus H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(V) \xrightarrow{\;\delta^{*}\;} H_{\mathr
m{dR}}^{k}(U\cap V) \xrightarrow{\;d^{*}\;} H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k+1}(M) \longrighta
rrow \cdots
$$

### Step 3. Description of the connecting homomorphism (for completeness)

The connecting map $d^{*}: H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(U\cap V)\to H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k+1}
(M)$ is constructed as follows. Given a closed form $\eta\in\Omega^{k}(U\cap V)$
, by surjectivity of $\delta$ choose $(\alpha,\beta)\in\Omega^{k}(U)\oplus\Omega
^{k}(V)$ with $\delta(\alpha,\beta)=\eta$, i.e. $\alpha-\beta=\eta$ on $U\cap V$
. Since $\eta$ is closed,
$$
\delta(d\alpha,d\beta) = d\alpha - d\beta = d(\alpha-\beta)=d\eta=0,
$$
so $(d\alpha,d\beta)\in\ker\delta=\operatorname{im}r$; there is a unique $\gamma
\in\Omega^{k+1}(M)$ with $r(\gamma)=(d\alpha,d\beta)$, i.e. $\gamma|_U=d\alpha$,
 $\gamma|_V=d\beta$. One checks $\gamma$ is closed, and
$$
d^{*}[\eta] = [\gamma]\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k+1}(M).
$$
Well-definedness (independence of the lift $(\alpha,\beta)$) follows from exactn
ess: changing the lift by an element of $\operatorname{im}r$ changes $\gamma$ by
 an exact form.

### Conclusion

The four cohomology groups are related by the **Mayer–Vietoris long exact sequen
ce**:

$$
\boxed{\cdots \to H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(M) \to H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(U)\oplus H_{\ma
thrm{dR}}^{k}(V) \to H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(U\cap V) \to H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k+1}(M) \
to \cdots}
$$

The maps are: restriction $r^{*}$, signed difference $\delta^{*}([\alpha],[\beta
])=[\alpha|_{U\cap V}-\beta|_{U\cap V}]$, and the connecting homomorphism $d^{*}
$ (the zig-zag map). Exactness at every term follows from the zig-zag lemma appl
ied to the short exact sequence of de Rham complexes, whose exactness in turn re
sts on the existence of a smooth partition of unity subordinate to $\{U,V\}$.
