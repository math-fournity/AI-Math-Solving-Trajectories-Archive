Proof

The answer is yes: for every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists a countable $
\varepsilon$-net for the family of all Lebesgue measurable subsets of $[0,1]$
under the metric $d(A_1, A_2) = \int |1_{A_1} - 1_{A_2}| = m(A_1 \triangle
A_2)$.

Construction. Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the collection of all finite unions of
intervals with rational endpoints contained in $[0,1]$. We claim $\mathcal{F}$
is countable and dense (hence a countable $\varepsilon$-net for every $
\varepsilon$).

Step 1: $\mathcal{F}$ is countable. The set of intervals with rational endpoints
in $[0,1]$ is countable (a subset of $\mathbb{Q}^2$). The set of finite unions
of such intervals is a countable union (over $n$) of finite products of a
countable set, hence countable.

Step 2: Every measurable set is approximable by a finite union of intervals. Let
$A \subseteq [0,1]$ be measurable and $\varepsilon > 0$. By outer regularity of
Lebesgue measure, there exists an open set $U \supseteq A$ with $m(U \setminus
A) < \varepsilon/2$. The set $U \cap [0,1]$ is open in $[0,1]$, hence a
countable union of disjoint intervals $I_1, I_2, \ldots$ with $\sum_k m(I_k) =
m(U \cap [0,1]) \leq 1 + \varepsilon/2 < \infty$. Since the series converges,
there exists $N$ such that $\sum_{k > N} m(I_k) < \varepsilon/2$. Set $V =
\bigcup_{k=1}^N I_k$, a finite union of intervals. Then:

  • $A \setminus V \subseteq (U \cap [0,1]) \setminus V$, so $m(A \setminus V)
  \leq m(U \setminus V) < \varepsilon/2$.
  • $V \setminus A \subseteq U \setminus A$, so $m(V \setminus A) \leq m(U
  \setminus A) < \varepsilon/2$.

Therefore $m(A \triangle V) = m(A \setminus V) + m(V \setminus A) <
\varepsilon$.

Step 3: Every finite union of intervals is approximable by an element of $
\mathcal{F}$. Let $V = \bigcup_{k=1}^N I_k$ be a finite union of intervals and $
\varepsilon > 0$. Write each $I_k = (a_k, b_k)$ (the cases of closed or half-
open intervals differ only by endpoints of measure zero). For each $k$, choose
rationals $q_k, r_k \in [0,1]$ with $|q_k - a_k| < \varepsilon/(4N)$ and $|r_k -
b_k| < \varepsilon/(4N)$. Set $W = \bigcup_{k=1}^N (q_k, r_k) \in \mathcal{F}$.
Then $m(I_k \triangle (q_k, r_k)) \leq |q_k - a_k| + |r_k - b_k| < \varepsilon/
(2N)$ for each $k$, so $$m(V \triangle W) \leq \sum_{k=1}^N m(I_k \triangle
(q_k, r_k)) < N \cdot \frac{\varepsilon}{2N} = \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.$$

Combining. Given any measurable $A$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, by Step 2 there is a
finite union of intervals $V$ with $m(A \triangle V) < \varepsilon/2$, and by
Step 3 there is $W \in \mathcal{F}$ with $m(V \triangle W) < \varepsilon/2$. By
the triangle inequality, $$d(A, W) = m(A \triangle W) \leq m(A \triangle V) +
m(V \triangle W) < \varepsilon.$$

Thus $\mathcal{F}$ is a countable dense subset of the measurable sets under $d$,
i.e., a countable $\varepsilon$-net for every $\varepsilon > 0$. $\blacksquare$
