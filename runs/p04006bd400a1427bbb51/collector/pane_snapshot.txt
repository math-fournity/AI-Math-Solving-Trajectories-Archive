# Commutator of Covariant Derivatives on a Vector Field

## Setup

Let $M$ be a surface (2-dimensional Riemannian manifold) with local coordinates
$x^i$, Christoffel symbols $\Gamma_{ij}^k$, and Levi-Civita connection $\nabla$.
 For a vector field $X^k$, the covariant derivative is

$$\nabla_i X^k = \partial_i X^k + \Gamma_{ij}^k X^j.$$

We compute the commutator $[\nabla_r, \nabla_i]X^k = \nabla_r\nabla_i X^k - \nab
la_i\nabla_r X^k$.

## Step 1: First covariant derivative

$\nabla_i X^k = \partial_i X^k + \Gamma_{ij}^k X^j$ is a $(1,1)$-tensor (one upp
er index $k$, one lower index $i$).

## Step 2: Second covariant derivative

Applying $\nabla_r$ to the $(1,1)$-tensor $\nabla_i X^k$, we must account for bo
th the upper index $k$ (with a $+\Gamma$ term) and the lower index $i$ (with a $
-\Gamma$ term):

$$\nabla_r(\nabla_i X^k) = \partial_r(\nabla_i X^k) + \Gamma_{rm}^k(\nabla_i X^m
) - \Gamma_{ri}^m(\nabla_m X^k).$$

Substituting $\nabla_i X^k = \partial_i X^k + \Gamma_{ij}^k X^j$:

$$\nabla_r\nabla_i X^k = \partial_r\partial_i X^k + (\partial_r\Gamma_{ij}^k)X^j
 + \Gamma_{ij}^k\partial_r X^j + \Gamma_{rm}^k\partial_i X^m + \Gamma_{rm}^k\Gam
ma_{ij}^m X^j - \Gamma_{ri}^m\partial_m X^k - \Gamma_{ri}^m\Gamma_{mj}^k X^j.$$

## Step 3: Swap $r \leftrightarrow i$

By symmetry of the exchange, $\nabla_i\nabla_r X^k$ is obtained by swapping $r$
and $i$:

$$\nabla_i\nabla_r X^k = \partial_i\partial_r X^k + (\partial_i\Gamma_{rj}^k)X^j
 + \Gamma_{rj}^k\partial_i X^j + \Gamma_{im}^k\partial_r X^m + \Gamma_{im}^k\Gam
ma_{rj}^m X^j - \Gamma_{ir}^m\partial_m X^k - \Gamma_{ir}^m\Gamma_{mj}^k X^j.$$

## Step 4: Subtract and simplify

Forming $[\nabla_r, \nabla_i]X^k = \nabla_r\nabla_i X^k - \nabla_i\nabla_r X^k$:

**Second-order derivative terms:** $\partial_r\partial_i X^k - \partial_i\partia
l_r X^k = 0$ (partial derivatives commute).

**Torsion terms:** $-\Gamma_{ri}^m\partial_m X^k + \Gamma_{ir}^m\partial_m X^k =
 0$, since the Levi-Civita connection is torsion-free: $\Gamma_{ri}^m = \Gamma_{
ir}^m$.

**First-order derivative terms:** From $\nabla_r\nabla_i$ we have $\Gamma_{ij}^k
\partial_r X^j + \Gamma_{rm}^k\partial_i X^m$; relabeling $m\to j$ in the second
 gives $\Gamma_{ij}^k\partial_r X^j + \Gamma_{rj}^k\partial_i X^j$. From $\nabla
_i\nabla_r$ we have $\Gamma_{rj}^k\partial_i X^j + \Gamma_{im}^k\partial_r X^m =
 \Gamma_{rj}^k\partial_i X^j + \Gamma_{ij}^k\partial_r X^j$ (relabeling $m\to j$
). These are identical, so they cancel completely.

**Zeroth-order (undifferentiated $X^j$) terms:** What remains is

$$[\nabla_r, \nabla_i]X^k = \Big[(\partial_r\Gamma_{ij}^k - \partial_i\Gamma_{rj
}^k) + (\Gamma_{rm}^k\Gamma_{ij}^m - \Gamma_{im}^k\Gamma_{rj}^m) - (\Gamma_{ri}^
m\Gamma_{mj}^k - \Gamma_{ir}^m\Gamma_{mj}^k)\Big]X^j.$$

The last parenthetical vanishes again by torsion-freeness ($\Gamma_{ri}^m = \Gam
ma_{ir}^m$).

## Step 5: Identify the curvature tensor

The remaining coefficient of $X^j$ is precisely the Riemann curvature tensor:

$$\boxed{R_{rij}^k \;=\; \partial_r\Gamma_{ij}^k - \partial_i\Gamma_{rj}^k + \Ga
mma_{rm}^k\Gamma_{ij}^m - \Gamma_{im}^k\Gamma_{rj}^m.}$$

Therefore the **Ricci identity** for a vector field on the surface is:

$$\boxed{[\nabla_r, \nabla_i]\,X^k \;=\; R_{rij}^k\, X^j.}$$

## Verification of properties

1. **Tensorial character:** Although $\Gamma_{ij}^k$ does not transform as a ten
sor, the combination $\partial_r\Gamma_{ij}^k - \partial_i\Gamma_{rj}^k + \Gamma
_{rm}^k\Gamma_{ij}^m - \Gamma_{im}^k\Gamma_{rj}^m$ does transform as a $(1,3)$-t
ensor. This is confirmed by the fact that all non-tensorial (derivative-of-$X$)
terms canceled in the commutator — the result depends on $X$ only through its va
lue $X^j$, not its derivatives, which is the hallmark of a tensorial multiplier.

2. **Antisymmetry in the derivative indices:** $R_{rij}^k = -R_{irj}^k$, evident
 from the formula (swapping $r \leftrightarrow i$ negates every term). This refl
ects $[\nabla_r, \nabla_i] = -[\nabla_i, \nabla_r]$.

3. **Surface dimension:** On a 2-dimensional surface, the indices $r, i, j$ each
 range over $\{1,2\}$. The independent components of $R_{rij}^k$ reduce to a sin
gle scalar (the Gaussian curvature $K$) via $R_{rij}^k = K(\delta_r^k g_{ij} - \
delta_i^k g_{rj})$, consistent with the fact that the Riemann tensor on a 2-mani
fold has only one independent component.

This completes the derivation.
