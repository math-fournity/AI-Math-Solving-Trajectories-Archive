## Solution

We claim that $SO(n+1)/O(n) \cong \mathbb{RP}^n$, the real projective $n$-space.

### The embedding $O(n) \hookrightarrow SO(n+1)$

Define
$$\varphi: O(n) \longrightarrow SO(n+1), \qquad \varphi(A) = \begin{pmatrix} \de
t A & 0 \\ 0 & A \end{pmatrix}.$$

**Homomorphism.** $\varphi(AB) = \begin{pmatrix}\det(AB) & 0 \\ 0 & AB\end{pmatr
ix} = \begin{pmatrix}\det A\det B & 0\\0&AB\end{pmatrix} = \varphi(A)\varphi(B).
$

**Lands in $SO(n+1)$.** $\det\varphi(A) = \det(A)\cdot\det(A) = (\det A)^2 = 1.$

**Injective.** If $\varphi(A)=I_{n+1}$, then $A=I_n$ (and $\det A=1$). ✓

Hence $O(n)$ is realized as a closed subgroup of $SO(n+1)$ via $\varphi$, and th
e quotient $SO(n+1)/O(n)$ is taken with respect to this embedding.

### The transitive action on $\mathbb{RP}^n$

$SO(n+1)$ acts linearly (hence continuously) on $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$, preserving t
he unit sphere $S^n$. This action descends to an action on $\mathbb{RP}^n = S^n/
\{x\sim -x\}$, since $SO(n+1)$ is linear and so commutes with the antipodal map.

**Transitivity.** $SO(n+1)$ acts transitively on $S^n$ (standard: any unit vecto
r can be rotated to any other). Given any line $\ell \in \mathbb{RP}^n$, pick a
unit vector $u\in\ell$; there exists $g\in SO(n+1)$ with $g\cdot e_0 = u$, so $g
$ sends the line $\mathbb{R}e_0$ to $\ell$. Hence the action on $\mathbb{RP}^n$
is transitive.

### The stabilizer of $[e_0]$

Let $e_0=(1,0,\dots,0)$. The stabilizer of the point $[e_0]=\{e_0,-e_0\}\in\math
bb{RP}^n$ in $SO(n+1)$ is
$$\operatorname{Stab}([e_0])=\{g\in SO(n+1): g\cdot e_0 = \pm e_0\},$$
i.e., those $g$ whose first column is $\pm e_0$.

- **First column $=+e_0$:** then $g=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&A\end{pmatrix}$ with $
A\in O(n)$ and $\det g=\det A=1$, so $A\in SO(n)$. This equals $\varphi(A)$ for
$A\in SO(n)\subset O(n)$.

- **First column $=-e_0$:** then $g=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&A\end{pmatrix}$ with
$A\in O(n)$ and $\det g=-\det A=1$, so $\det A=-1$. This equals $\varphi(A)$ for
 $A\in O(n)$ with $\det A=-1$.

Combining both cases:
$$\operatorname{Stab}([e_0]) = \varphi(O(n)).$$

### Conclusion

By the orbit–stabilizer theorem for topological groups (a transitive continuous
action of a compact group $G$ on a Hausdorff space $X$ with stabilizer $H$ yield
s a $G$-equivariant homeomorphism $G/H \xrightarrow{\sim} X$), we obtain

$$SO(n+1)/O(n) \;\cong\; \mathbb{RP}^n.$$

Here $SO(n+1)$ is compact, $\mathbb{RP}^n$ is Hausdorff, the action is continuou
s and transitive, and the stabilizer is exactly $\varphi(O(n))\cong O(n)$, so al
l hypotheses are satisfied.

$\boxed{SO(n+1)/O(n)\cong\mathbb{RP}^n}$
