## Proof

The PDE is:
$$2u_{x_1x_1}+2u_{x_2x_2}-15u_{x_3x_3}+8u_{x_4x_4}-12u_{x_5x_5}-12u_{x_4x_5}=0$$

**Step 1: Identify the coefficient matrix.**

The symmetric coefficient matrix $A$ is diagonal except in the $(x_4,x_5)$ block
. The mixed term $-12u_{x_4x_5}$ gives $A_{45}=A_{54}=-6$ (since $2A_{45}=-12$).
 Thus:

$$A=\operatorname{diag}(2,\,2,\,-15)\;\oplus\;\begin{pmatrix}8&-6\\-6&-12\end{pm
atrix}$$

The $x_1,x_2,x_3$ part is already diagonal. We only need to orthogonally diagona
lize the $2\times2$ block $B=\begin{pmatrix}8&-6\\-6&-12\end{pmatrix}$.

**Step 2: Find eigenvalues of $B$.**

$$\det(B-\lambda I)=(8-\lambda)(-12-\lambda)-36=\lambda^2+4\lambda-132=0$$

$$\lambda=\frac{-4\pm\sqrt{16+528}}{2}=\frac{-4\pm\sqrt{544}}{2}=\frac{-4\pm4\sq
rt{34}}{2}=-2\pm2\sqrt{34}$$

So $\lambda_4=-2+2\sqrt{34}>0$ and $\lambda_5=-2-2\sqrt{34}<0$.

(Check: trace $=-4=\lambda_4+\lambda_5$ ✓; determinant $=-132=\lambda_4\lambda_5
=4-4\cdot34=-132$ ✓.)

**Step 3: Find orthonormal eigenvectors of $B$.**

For $\lambda_4=-2+2\sqrt{34}$: $(10-2\sqrt{34})\,v_1-6\,v_2=0$, giving eigenvect
or $\mathbf{v}_4=(3,\;5-\sqrt{34})^T$.

For $\lambda_5=-2-2\sqrt{34}$: $(10+2\sqrt{34})\,v_1-6\,v_2=0$, giving eigenvect
or $\mathbf{v}_5=(3,\;5+\sqrt{34})^T$.

**Orthogonality check:** $3\cdot3+(5-\sqrt{34})(5+\sqrt{34})=9+25-34=0$ ✓

**Norms:**
$$\|\mathbf{v}_4\|^2=9+(5-\sqrt{34})^2=9+25-10\sqrt{34}+34=68-10\sqrt{34}$$
$$\|\mathbf{v}_5\|^2=9+(5+\sqrt{34})^2=9+25+10\sqrt{34}+34=68+10\sqrt{34}$$

**Step 4: Orthogonal change of coordinates.**

Set $y=Q^Tx$ where $Q$ has the orthonormal eigenvectors as columns:

$$y_1=x_1,\quad y_2=x_2,\quad y_3=x_3$$

$$y_4=\frac{3\,x_4+(5-\sqrt{34})\,x_5}{\sqrt{68-10\sqrt{34}}},\qquad y_5=\frac{3
\,x_4+(5+\sqrt{34})\,x_5}{\sqrt{68+10\sqrt{34}}}$$

This is an orthogonal transformation ($Q^TQ=I$), and the PDE becomes:

$$2\,u_{y_1y_1}+2\,u_{y_2y_2}-15\,u_{y_3y_3}+(-2+2\sqrt{34})\,u_{y_4y_4}+(-2-2\s
qrt{34})\,u_{y_5y_5}=0$$

All mixed partials are eliminated.

**Step 5: Rescale to canonical form.**

Set $z_i = y_i/\sqrt{|\lambda_i|}$, i.e., $y_i = \sqrt{|\lambda_i|}\,z_i$, so th
at $u_{y_iy_i}=\frac{1}{|\lambda_i|}u_{z_iz_i}$ and each coefficient $\lambda_i\
cdot\frac{1}{|\lambda_i|}=\operatorname{sgn}(\lambda_i)$:

$$z_1=\frac{y_1}{\sqrt{2}},\quad z_2=\frac{y_2}{\sqrt{2}},\quad z_3=\frac{y_3}{\
sqrt{15}},\quad z_4=\frac{y_4}{\sqrt{2\sqrt{34}-2}},\quad z_5=\frac{y_5}{\sqrt{2
\sqrt{34}+2}}$$

The equation becomes:

$$\boxed{u_{z_1z_1}+u_{z_2z_2}+u_{z_4z_4}-u_{z_3z_3}-u_{z_5z_5}=0}$$

This is the **canonical form**: three positive and two negative squared terms, c
onfirming the equation is **ultra-hyperbolic** (hyperbolic type with signature $
(3,2)$).
