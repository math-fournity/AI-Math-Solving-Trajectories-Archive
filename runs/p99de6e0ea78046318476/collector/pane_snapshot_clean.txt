## Proof

Let $a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_n$ be independent and identically distributed random va
riables, with the given condition that for each $i$,
$$\Pr(a_i < 0) < e^{-d},$$
where $d$ is a positive integer. We seek a bound on $\Pr\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} a
_i < 0\right)$.

**Key observation.** If every $a_i \geq 0$, then $\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i \geq 0$. Ta
king the contrapositive:
$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i < 0 \implies \exists\, i \in \{1,\ldots,n\} \text{ such tha
t } a_i < 0.$$

Equivalently, as events,
$$\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i < 0\right\} \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} \{a_i < 0\}.
$$

**Applying the union bound.** By subadditivity of probability,
$$\Pr\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i < 0\right) \leq \Pr\!\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n} \{a
_i < 0\}\right) \leq \sum_{i=1}^{n} \Pr(a_i < 0).$$

**Using the i.i.d. hypothesis.** Since the $a_i$ are identically distributed, ea
ch $\Pr(a_i < 0) < e^{-d}$, so
$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} \Pr(a_i < 0) < \sum_{i=1}^{n} e^{-d} = n\, e^{-d}.$$

Combining,
$$\boxed{\Pr\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i < 0\right) < n\, e^{-d}.}$$

**Remark on tightness of the argument.** The bound uses only the marginal condit
ion $\Pr(a_i<0)<e^{-d}$ and the trivial fact that a sum of nonnegatives is nonne
gative; independence is not actually needed for this argument (the union bound h
olds regardless). The i.i.d. assumption simply makes all the per-variable probab
ilities equal, yielding the clean factor $n$. The bound is sharp in the sense th
at one can construct examples where $\Pr(\sum a_i < 0)$ is arbitrarily close to
$n e^{-d}$ (for $n e^{-d} \leq 1$): take $a_i = -1$ with probability $e^{-d}-\va
repsilon$ and $a_i = M > 0$ with the remaining probability, choosing $M$ large e
nough that whenever at least one $a_i = -1$ the sum can still be negative only w
hen exactly one is $-1$ and the rest are $M$... in fact with $M$ chosen so that
$-(1) + (n-1)M > 0$ the sum is negative only when *all* variables are $-1$, givi
ng $\Pr(\sum a_i<0) \approx e^{-nd}$, which is *smaller* — showing the union bou
nd is not always tight. The union bound $n e^{-d}$ is nevertheless the natural,
correct, and standard bound obtainable from the stated hypothesis, and it is the
 best one can state without further assumptions on the distribution (e.g., on th
e mean or the support of the $a_i$).
