## Proof

Let $X=(x_{ij})$, $P=\det X$, and $D=\det(\partial/\partial x_{ij})$.

### Step 1 — The form of $DP^s$

Both $D$ and $P$ are invariant under the $SL_n\times SL_n$ action $(g,h):X\mapst
o gXh^{-1}$ (since $\det(gXh^{-1})=\det X$ and $D$ transforms by $\det(g)^{-1}\d
et(h)=1$). Thus $DP^s$ is $SL_n\times SL_n$-invariant and homogeneous of degree
$n(s-1)$. By the first fundamental theorem for $SL_n\times SL_n$ acting on $\mat
hrm{Mat}_n$, every homogeneous invariant of degree divisible by $n$ is a power o
f $\det X$. Hence
$$DP^s=b_n(s)\,P^{s-1}$$
for some scalar $b_n(s)$ depending only on $s$ (and $n$).

### Step 2 — Reduction to a coefficient extraction

Evaluating at $X=I$ (where $P(I)=1$) gives $b_n(s)=(DP^s)(I)$. Write $X=I+H$; th
en $\partial/\partial x_{ij}$ at $X=I$ equals $\partial/\partial h_{ij}$ at $H=0
$. Since $D$ has order $n$, only the degree-$n$ homogeneous part $F_n(H)$ of $(\
det(I+H))^s$ contributes.

### Step 3 — Cauchy expansion

For positive integer $s$, with $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n$ the eigenvalues of $H
$,
$$\det(I+H)=\prod_i(1+\lambda_i)=\prod_{i=1}^n\prod_{j=1}^s(1+\lambda_i\cdot 1).
$$
By the dual Cauchy identity $\prod_{i,j}(1+x_i y_j)=\sum_\Lambda s_\Lambda(x)\,s
_{\Lambda'}(y)$ (with $s_{\Lambda'}=0$ when $\Lambda_1>\#y$),
$$(\det(I+H))^s=\sum_{\Lambda:\Lambda_1\le s}s_\Lambda(H)\,s_{\Lambda'}(1^s).$$
The degree-$n$ part is
$$F_n(H)=\sum_{\substack{\Lambda\vdash n\\\Lambda_1\le s}}s_\Lambda(H)\,s_{\Lamb
da'}(1^s).$$

### Step 4 — The functional $T:=D|_{X=I}$ on Schur polynomials

**Claim.** $T(s_\Lambda):=\det(\partial/\partial h_{ij})\,s_\Lambda(H)\big|_{H=0
}=n!\,\delta_{\Lambda,(1^n)}$ for $\Lambda\vdash n$.

The functional $T$ is $SL_n\times SL_n$-invariant. The space $\mathrm{Sym}^n(V\o
times V^*)$ of degree-$n$ homogeneous polynomials in the entries of $H$ decompos
es (Cauchy) as $\bigoplus_{\Lambda\vdash n}V_\Lambda\otimes \mathbb{S}_\Lambda(V
^*)$. A summand contains an invariant vector iff **both** $V_\Lambda$ and $\math
bb{S}_\Lambda(V^*)$ are trivial $SL_n$-representations, which happens iff $\Lamb
da=(1^n)$ — the only partition of $n$ that is a full $1\times n$ rectangle (so $
\mathbb{S}_{(1^n)}(V)\cong\det$ is trivial on $SL_n$, and likewise $\mathbb{S}_{
(1^n)}(V^*)$). Hence the space of $SL_n\times SL_n$-invariant linear functionals
 on $\mathrm{Sym}^n(V\otimes V^*)$ is **one-dimensional**.

A direct computation gives $T(\det H)=T(s_{(1^n)})=n!$:
$$\det(\partial)\det(h)=\sum_{\sigma,\tau}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\mathrm{sgn}(\tau)
\prod_i\partial_{i,\sigma(i)}\prod_j h_{j,\tau(j)}=\sum_\sigma\mathrm{sgn}(\sigm
a)^2=n!.$$
So $T$ is $n!$ times the projection onto the $s_{(1^n)}$-direction, and by Schur
 orthogonality $T(s_\Lambda)=0$ for $\Lambda\ne(1^n)$. ∎

### Step 5 — Compute $b_n(s)$

Combining Steps 2–4,
$$b_n(s)=T(F_n)=\sum_{\substack{\Lambda\vdash n\\\Lambda_1\le s}}s_{\Lambda'}(1^
s)\,T(s_\Lambda)=s_{(1^n)'}(1^s)\cdot n!=s_{(n)}(1^s)\cdot n!,$$
since $(1^n)'=(n)$. The Schur polynomial $s_{(n)}=h_n$ (complete homogeneous), s
o
$$s_{(n)}(1^s)=h_n(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{s})=\binom{s+n-1}{n}.$$
Therefore
$$b_n(s)=n!\binom{s+n-1}{n}=\frac{(s+n-1)!}{(s-1)!}=s(s+1)(s+2)\cdots(s+n-1)=\pr
od_{k=0}^{n-1}(s+k).$$

### Verification

- $n=1$: $b_1(s)=s$, and $\partial_{x_{11}}x_{11}^s=s\,x_{11}^{s-1}=s\,P^{s-1}$.
 ✓
- $n=2$: $b_2(s)=s(s+1)$, matching direct computation of $\det(\partial)(x_{11}x
_{22}-x_{12}x_{21})^s$.
- $s=1$: $b_n(1)=n!$, matching $D(\det X)=n!$. ✓

The identity holds for every positive integer $s$ (where $P^s$ is a polynomial),
 and $b_n(s)=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(s+k)$ is the required polynomial in $s$.

$$\boxed{\,b_n(s)=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(s+k)=s(s+1)(s+2)\cdots(s+n-1)\,}$$
