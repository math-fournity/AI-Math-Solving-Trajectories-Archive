Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a smooth manifold graph imp
lies the function is smooth.

## Answer: **No.**

## Proof

**Counterexample.** Let $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ be the real cube root $f(x)
= x^{1/3}$.

**Step 1: $f$ is not smooth.**

$f$ is continuous on all of $\mathbb{R}$. However, at $x=0$:
$$\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(h)-f(0)}{h} = \lim_{h\to 0}\frac{h^{1/3}}{h} = \lim_{h\to
 0} h^{-2/3} = +\infty.$$
So $f$ is not differentiable at $0$, hence not $C^1$, hence not smooth.

**Step 2: The graph $\Gamma_f$ is a smooth submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^2$.**

Define $\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^2$ by $\varphi(t) = (t^3,\, t)$.

- **Smooth:** $\varphi$ is a polynomial map, hence smooth.
- **Injective:** If $(t_1^3, t_1) = (t_2^3, t_2)$, then $t_1 = t_2$ from the sec
ond coordinate.
- **Immersion:** $\varphi'(t) = (3t^2,\, 1)$, which is never $(0,0)$ since the s
econd component is always $1$.
- **Image equals the graph:** $\{(t^3, t): t\in\mathbb{R}\} = \{(x, x^{1/3}): x\
in\mathbb{R}\} = \Gamma_f$, since $x = t^3 \iff t = x^{1/3}$.
- **Homeomorphism onto image (subspace topology):** The inverse $\varphi^{-1}:\G
amma_f\to\mathbb{R}$ is $(x,y)\mapsto y$, which is the restriction of the contin
uous projection $\pi_2:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$, hence continuous.

Therefore $\varphi$ is a **smooth embedding**, and $\Gamma_f$ is a smooth embedd
ed $1$-dimensional submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^2$—that is, a smooth manifold.

**Step 3: Why the implication fails.**

The projection $\pi_1:\Gamma_f\to\mathbb{R}$, $\pi_1(x,y)=x$, is smooth (restric
tion of a smooth map to a submanifold) and is a homeomorphism with inverse $x\ma
psto (x, x^{1/3})$. However, it is **not a diffeomorphism**: its differential at
 the origin is
$$d\pi_1\big|_{(0,0)}\big(\varphi'(0)\big) = d\pi_1\big|_{(0,0)}(0,1) = 0,$$
which is not invertible. The inverse function theorem does not apply, so the smo
othness of $\pi_1$ does not transfer to its inverse, and thus does not force $f
= \pi_2\circ\pi_1^{-1}$ to be smooth.

**Conclusion.** The graph of $f(x)=x^{1/3}$ is a smooth manifold, yet $f$ is not
 even differentiable at the origin. Hence the graph of $f$ being a smooth manifo
ld does **not** imply that $f$ is smooth. $\blacksquare$
