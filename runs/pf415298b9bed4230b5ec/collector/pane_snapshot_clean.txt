## Proof

**Claim:** The norm of $T$ equals the absolute value of its largest eigenvalue.
The answer is **yes**.

**Step 1: $T$ is finite-rank (hence compact).**

Using the identity $\cos(s+t) = \cos s\cos t - \sin s\sin t$,

$$T(f)(s) = \cos s\int_0^1 \cos t\, f(t)\,dt \;-\; \sin s\int_0^1 \sin t\, f(t)\
,dt.$$

So $\operatorname{ran}(T) \subseteq \operatorname{span}\{\cos s,\; \sin s\}|_{[0
,1]}$, a space of dimension $\le 2$. Thus $T$ has finite rank and is therefore a
 compact operator on $L^2[0,1]$.

**Step 2: $T$ is self-adjoint.**

The kernel is symmetric: $K(s,t)=\cos(s+t)=\cos(t+s)=K(t,s)$. For real-valued $L
^2$ (and by conjugate-symmetry in general), a symmetric kernel gives a self-adjo
int operator:

$$\langle Tf, g\rangle = \iint K(s,t)f(t)g(s)\,dt\,ds = \langle f, Tg\rangle.$$

Hence $T=T^*$.

**Step 3: Apply the spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators.**

For a compact self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space, the spectral theorem giv
es:
- The spectrum consists of real eigenvalues (plus possibly $0$), with $0$ as the
 only possible accumulation point.
- The operator norm satisfies $\|T\| = \sup_{\|f\|=1}|\langle Tf,f\rangle| = \ma
x\{|\lambda| : \lambda \in \sigma_p(T)\}$, where $\sigma_p(T)$ is the point spec
trum (eigenvalues).

Since $T$ is finite-rank, the supremum is attained: there are finitely many nonz
ero eigenvalues, and

$$\|T\| = \max_{\lambda \in \sigma_p(T)} |\lambda| = |\lambda_{\max}|,$$

where $\lambda_{\max}$ is the eigenvalue of largest absolute value.

**Step 4: Verification that nonzero eigenvalues exist (the max is nontrivial).**

The $2\times 2$ matrix of $T$ on $\operatorname{span}\{\cos,\sin\}$ in the basis
 $e_1=\cos$, $e_2=\sin$ is
$$M=\begin{pmatrix} A & B\\ B & -C\end{pmatrix},\quad A=\int_0^1\!\cos^2 t\,dt,\
; B=\int_0^1\!\cos t\sin t\,dt,\; C=\int_0^1\!\sin^2 t\,dt.$$
$M$ is real symmetric, so it has two real eigenvalues. With
$$A=\tfrac12+\tfrac{\sin 2}{4},\quad C=\tfrac12-\tfrac{\sin 2}{4},\quad B=\tfrac
{1-\cos 2}{4},$$
the trace is $A-C=\tfrac{\sin 2}{2}\neq 0$, so at least one eigenvalue is nonzer
o. The two eigenvalues $\lambda_\pm = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl((A-C)\pm\sqrt{(A+C)^2+4B
^2}\bigr)$ are real and nonzero in general, and $\|T\|=\max(|\lambda_+|,|\lambda
_-|)$.

**Conclusion.** Since $T$ is compact and self-adjoint, its operator norm equals
the absolute value of its largest eigenvalue. The answer is **yes**.
