# Proof

**Claim:** Yes. For every $E \in \mathcal{U}$, there exists $D \in \mathcal{U}$
such that every $D$-chain $\{x_i\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}$ satisfies $(x_i, x_j) \in E
$ for all $i, j \in \mathbb{Z}$.

**Key fact:** In a compact Hausdorff totally disconnected uniform space, the ent
ourages admit a basis of equivalence relations (i.e., entourages that are equiva
lence relations on $X$, corresponding to finite partitions into clopen sets).

*Proof of key fact.* Let $E \in \mathcal{U}$. Without loss of generality, assume
 $E$ is symmetric. Since $\mathcal{U}$ is a uniformity, choose a symmetric $E' \
in \mathcal{U}$ with $E' \circ E' \subseteq E$.

Since $X$ is compact Hausdorff and totally disconnected, the clopen subsets form
 a basis for the topology. For each $x \in X$, the set $E'[x] = \{y \in X : (x,y
) \in E'\}$ is a neighborhood of $x$, so there exists a clopen set $U_x$ with $x
 \in U_x \subseteq E'[x]$. By compactness of $X$, finitely many such sets $U_{x_
1}, \dots, U_{x_n}$ cover $X$.

Define a disjoint clopen partition by setting $V_1 = U_{x_1}$ and $V_k = U_{x_k}
 \setminus \bigcup_{j=1}^{k-1} U_{x_j}$ for $k = 2, \dots, n$. Each $V_k$ is clo
pen, the $V_k$ are pairwise disjoint, and $\{V_1, \dots, V_n\}$ covers $X$. More
over, $V_k \subseteq U_{x_k} \subseteq E'[x_k]$ for each $k$.

Define $R = \bigcup_{k=1}^{n} (V_k \times V_k)$. This is an equivalence relation
 on $X$ (the partition into the $V_k$). Since each $V_k$ is clopen, $R$ is a clo
pen subset of $X \times X$ containing the diagonal $\Delta$, so $R \in \mathcal{
U}$.

We claim $R \subseteq E$. Indeed, if $(x, y) \in R$, then $x, y \in V_k$ for som
e $k$, hence $x, y \in E'[x_k]$. This gives $(x_k, x) \in E'$ and $(x_k, y) \in
E'$. By symmetry of $E'$, $(x, x_k) \in E'$, so $(x, y) \in E' \circ E' \subsete
q E$. $\square$

**Main proof.** Given $E \in \mathcal{U}$, by the key fact above, choose an equi
valence relation $R \in \mathcal{U}$ with $R \subseteq E$. Set $D = R$.

Let $\{x_i\}_{i \in \mathbb{Z}}$ be any $D$-chain, meaning $(x_i, x_{i+1}) \in D
 = R$ for all $i \in \mathbb{Z}$. Since $R$ is an equivalence relation, it is tr
ansitive: for any $i < j$, the finite chain of relations $(x_i, x_{i+1}), (x_{i+
1}, x_{i+2}), \dots, (x_{j-1}, x_j) \in R$ implies $(x_i, x_j) \in R$ by inducti
on on $j - i$. Since $R$ is symmetric, $(x_j, x_i) \in R$ as well. Thus $(x_i, x
_j) \in R$ for all $i, j \in \mathbb{Z}$.

Since $R \subseteq E$, we conclude $(x_i, x_j) \in E$ for all $i, j \in \mathbb{
Z}$. $\blacksquare$
