## Proof

### Upper Bound: $k \leq 4218$

Let $k$ be the number of primes in the table.

**Observation 1:** The number 1 cannot appear in the table, since $\gcd(1, b) =
1$ for all $b$, so 1 would violate the condition. Thus every cell contains eithe
r a prime or a composite (with at least one prime divisor).

**Observation 2:** For each prime $p$ in the table, the condition requires some
$b \neq p$ in the same row or column with $\gcd(p, b) > 1$. Since $p$ is prime,
this forces $p \mid b$, so $b$ is composite and $p$ is among its prime divisors.
 Call $b$ a *witness* for $p$. (Two distinct primes are coprime, so a prime cann
ot witness another prime.)

**Observation 3:** A composite $b$ with prime divisors $\{q_1, q_2, q_3\}$ (at m
ost 3) can witness only primes among $\{q_1, q_2, q_3\}$ that lie in its row or
column. Since all table entries are distinct, each $q_i$ appears at most once, s
o $b$ witnesses **at most 3 primes**.

**Counting:** The table has $5625 - k$ composite numbers, each witnessing at mos
t 3 primes. Since every prime requires a witness:

$$k \leq 3(5625 - k) \implies 4k \leq 16875 \implies k \leq 4218.$$

### Construction Achieving 4218

We build a table with **4218 primes** and **1407 composites** ($4218 + 1407 = 56
25$).

**Row structure.** Designate:
- **57 dense rows** (rows 1–57): 19 composites, 56 primes each.
- **18 sparse rows** (rows 58–75): 18 composites, 57 primes each.

Totals: composites $= 57 \times 19 + 18 \times 18 = 1083 + 324 = 1407$; primes $
= 57 \times 56 + 18 \times 57 = 3192 + 1026 = 4218$. ✓

**Row witnessing.** In each row, composites are products of primes from that row

- **Sparse row:** 18 composites × 3 primes each = 54 primes witnessed from the r
ow. The remaining **3 primes per row** need column witnessing. Total: $18 \times
 3 = 54$ primes need column witnessing.
- **Dense row:** 18 composites × 3 primes = 54 witnessed from row. One *spare* c
omposite (product of 2 row primes) witnesses the remaining 2 row primes. All 56
row primes are witnessed, and the spare composite has **1 unit of spare capacity

**Column witnessing.** Place the 54 column-needing primes in 54 distinct columns

d \leq 54$) to the column of the $d$-th such prime, with that prime among the co
mposite's divisors. Since $57 \geq 54$, capacity suffices. The 3 remaining spare
 composites (rows 55–57) simply witness 2 primes from their row (unused spare ca
pacity).

**Choosing numbers.** Select 4218 distinct primes. Each composite is a product o
f 2 or 3 of these primes, with each composite using a distinct set, ensuring all

**Verification of all conditions:**
1. **Distinctness:** All primes distinct; each composite uses a unique subset of

4. **Every composite has a witness:** Each composite is a product of primes in i

### Conclusion

The upper bound gives $k \leq 4218$ and the construction achieves $k = 4218$.

The maximum number of prime numbers in the table is $\boxed{4218}$.
