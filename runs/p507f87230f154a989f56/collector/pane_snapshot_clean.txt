## Proof

**Claim.** The term $\dfrac{\partial^{2} f}{\partial x^{2}}\,d[X]_{t}$ appearing
 in Itô's formula — i.e. the process
$$B_{t} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t} f''(X_{s})\,d[X]_{s}$$
— **is** a finite variation process.

---

### Setup

Let $X = (X_{t})_{t\ge 0}$ be a continuous semimartingale on a filtered probabil
ity space $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t}),\mathbb{P})$, with canonical de
composition $X = M + A$, where $M$ is a continuous local martingale starting at
$0$ and $A$ is a continuous adapted process of finite variation. Let $f\in C^{2}
(\mathbb{R})$. Itô's formula reads

$$f(X_{t}) \;=\; f(X_{0}) \;+\; \int_{0}^{t} f'(X_{s})\,dX_{s} \;+\; \tfrac{1}{2
}\int_{0}^{t} f''(X_{s})\,d[X]_{s}.$$

We analyze the third summand $B_{t}=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t} f''(X_{s})\,d[X]_{s
}$.

---

### Step 1. $[X]$ is an increasing (hence finite-variation) process.

For a continuous semimartingale $X = M + A$:

- The cross-variation $[A,A]_{t}=0$ because $A$ has finite variation (its paths
have zero quadratic variation).
- The cross-variation $[M,A]_{t}=0$ because $A$ is continuous and of finite vari
ation while $M$ is a continuous local martingale (Kunita–Watanabe: $|[M,A]_{t}|\
le [M]_{t}^{1/2}[A]_{t}^{1/2}=0$).

Hence
$$[X]_{t} \;=\; [M+A]_{t} \;=\; [M]_{t} + 2[M,A]_{t} + [A]_{t} \;=\; [M]_{t}.$$

The quadratic variation $[M]$ of a continuous local martingale is a **continuous
, adapted, non-decreasing** process with $[M]_{0}=0$ (this is the Doob–Meyer dec
omposition of the submartingale $M^{2}$). Being non-decreasing, $[M]$ (and thus
$[X]$) has finite variation on every compact interval $[0,T]$:
$$\mathrm{Var}_{[0,T]}([X]) \;=\; [X]_{T} - [X]_{0} \;=\; [X]_{T} \;<\; \infty \
quad\text{a.s.}$$

---

### Step 2. The integrand $f''(X_{s})$ is locally bounded and predictable.

Since $f\in C^{2}$, the map $x\mapsto f''(x)$ is continuous. Since $X$ has conti
nuous paths, $s\mapsto f''(X_{s})$ is continuous (hence predictable) and adapted
. On every compact time interval $[0,T]$, the path $s\mapsto X_{s}(\omega)$ is b
ounded, so $s\mapsto f''(X_{s}(\omega))$ is bounded on $[0,T]$. Thus $f''(X)$ is
 **locally bounded and predictable**.

---

### Step 3. The integral $B_{t}=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t} f''(X_{s})\,d[X]_{s}$ i
s of finite variation.

The integral is a pathwise Lebesgue–Stieltjes integral against the (signed) meas
ure $\mu$ induced by the increasing finite-variation function $s\mapsto [X]_{s}(
\omega)$. For each $\omega$ and each $T>0$, the total variation of $B(\omega)$ o
n $[0,T]$ satisfies

$$\mathrm{Var}_{[0,T]}\!\bigl(B(\omega)\bigr) \;\le\; \tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\b
igl|f''(X_{s}(\omega))\bigr|\,d[X]_{s}(\omega).$$

By Step 2 the integrand is bounded on $[0,T]$ by some constant $K_{T}(\omega)$,
and by Step 1 the measure $\mu$ has total mass $[X]_{T}(\omega)<\infty$. Therefo
re

$$\mathrm{Var}_{[0,T]}\!\bigl(B(\omega)\bigr) \;\le\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,K_{T}(\omega
)\,[X]_{T}(\omega) \;<\; \infty \quad\text{a.s.}$$

So $B$ has finite variation on every compact interval, almost surely. Moreover $
B$ is continuous (integral of a continuous integrand against a continuous integr
ator) and adapted, hence $B$ is a **continuous adapted finite-variation process*
*.

---

### Conclusion

The term $\dfrac{\partial^{2} f}{\partial x^{2}}\,d[X]_{t}$ in Itô's formula cor
responds to the process $B_{t}=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}f''(X_{s})\,d[X]_{s}$, wh
ich is a finite-variation process. The essential reasons are:

1. $[X]=[M]$ is increasing (hence finite variation), and
2. the Lebesgue–Stieltjes integral of a locally bounded predictable process agai
nst an increasing finite-variation integrator is itself of finite variation.

This is precisely why, in the Itô decomposition $f(X_{t}) = f(X_{0}) + \underbra
ce{\int f'\,dX}_{\text{martingale part}} + \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2}\int f''\,d[X
]}_{\text{drift / FV part}}$, the second-derivative term is identified as the **
drift** (finite-variation) component, while the first-derivative term carries th
e martingale part.
