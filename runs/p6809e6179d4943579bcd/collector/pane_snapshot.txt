## Proof

**Setup.** Let $Y_1, Y_2, \dots$ be independent, each exponentially distributed
with rate $\tfrac{1}{z}$ (so $\mathbb{E}[Y_i]=z$). Define the partial sum
$$
S_n = Y_1 + \cdots + Y_n.
$$
Since the sum of $n$ independent exponentials of rate $\tfrac{1}{z}$ is $\text{G
amma}\!\big(n,\tfrac{1}{z}\big)$, the density of $S_n$ is
$$
f_{S_n}(x)=\frac{(1/z)^n\, x^{n-1} e^{-x/z}}{(n-1)!},\qquad x>0.
$$

**Step 1 — Reduce to an expectation.** The Laplace transform of $h$ and its deri
vatives are
$$
L_h(\lambda)=\int_0^\infty h(x)e^{-\lambda x}\,dx,\qquad
L_h^{(n-1)}(\lambda)=\int_0^\infty h(x)(-x)^{n-1}e^{-\lambda x}\,dx
= (-1)^{n-1}\!\int_0^\infty h(x)\,x^{n-1}e^{-\lambda x}\,dx,
$$
where differentiating under the integral is justified because $h$ is bounded (so
 $h(x)x^{n-1}e^{-\lambda x}$ is integrable and dominated uniformly in $\lambda$
on compact sets). Hence
$$
\int_0^\infty h(x)\,x^{n-1}e^{-\lambda x}\,dx = (-1)^{n-1}L_h^{(n-1)}(\lambda).
$$

**Step 2 — Express $\mathbb{E}[h(S_n/n)]$ via $L_h$.** Compute
$$
\mathbb{E}\!\left[h\!\left(\tfrac{S_n}{n}\right)\right]
= \int_0^\infty h\!\left(\tfrac{x}{n}\right)\frac{(1/z)^n\,x^{n-1}e^{-x/z}}{(n-1
)!}\,dx.
$$
Apply the change of variable $u = x/n$ (so $x = nu$, $dx = n\,du$):
$$
= \int_0^\infty h(u)\,\frac{(1/z)^n\,(nu)^{n-1}e^{-nu/z}}{(n-1)!}\,n\,du
= \frac{(n/z)^n}{(n-1)!}\int_0^\infty h(u)\,u^{n-1}e^{-(n/z)u}\,du.
$$
Using the identity from Step 1 with $\lambda = n/z$,
$$
\mathbb{E}\!\left[h\!\left(\tfrac{S_n}{n}\right)\right]
= \frac{(n/z)^n}{(n-1)!}\,(-1)^{n-1}L_h^{(n-1)}\!\left(\tfrac{n}{z}\right)
= (-1)^{n-1}\frac{(n/z)^n\,L_h^{(n-1)}(n/z)}{(n-1)!}.
$$

**Step 3 — Take the limit using the LLN.** Since the $Y_i$ are i.i.d. with $\mat
hbb{E}[Y_i]=z$, the Strong Law of Large Numbers gives
$$
\frac{S_n}{n}\xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} z \quad (n\to\infty).
$$
Because $h$ is continuous, $h(S_n/n)\xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} h(z)$. Because $h$
 is **bounded**, the Bounded Convergence Theorem permits interchange of limit an
d expectation:
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\!\left[h\!\left(\tfrac{S_n}{n}\right)\right]
= \mathbb{E}\!\left[\lim_{n\to\infty}h\!\left(\tfrac{S_n}{n}\right)\right]
= h(z).
$$

**Conclusion.** Combining Steps 2 and 3,
$$
\boxed{\,h(z)=\lim_{n\to\infty}(-1)^{n-1}\frac{(n/z)^n\,L_h^{(n-1)}(n/z)}{(n-1)!
}\,}.
$$

The two ingredients are precisely the Gamma density of $S_n$ (sum of i.i.d. expo
nentials of rate $1/z$) and the Law of Large Numbers $S_n/n\to z$, with boundedn
ess of $h$ justifying the dominated/bounded convergence step.
