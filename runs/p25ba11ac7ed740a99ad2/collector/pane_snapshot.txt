## Solution

**Step 1: Identify the indicator as a product of Heaviside functions.**

Let $H(t) = \mathbb{1}_{t>0}$ be the Heaviside function. Then
$$\mathbb{1}_{x_1>0,\,x_2>0} = H(x_1)\,H(x_2).$$

**Step 2: Compute the mixed distributional derivative.**

In $\mathcal{D}'(\mathbb{R}^2)$ we have $\partial_{x_1} H(x_1) = \delta(x_1)$ an
d $\partial_{x_2} H(x_2) = \delta(x_2)$. Differentiating in $x_2$ first:
$$\partial_{x_2}\bigl[H(x_1)\,H(x_2)\bigr] = H(x_1)\,\delta(x_2),$$
then differentiating in $x_1$:
$$\partial_{x_1 x_2}^2\,\mathbb{1}_{x_1>0,\,x_2>0} = \delta(x_1)\,\delta(x_2) =
\delta_{(0,0)}.$$

**Verification via test functions.** For $\varphi \in \mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^2)$
:
$$\langle \partial_{x_1 x_2}^2\,\mathbb{1}_{x_1>0,x_2>0},\,\varphi\rangle = \int
_0^\infty\!\int_0^\infty \partial_{x_1 x_2}^2\,\varphi\,dx_1\,dx_2.$$
Integrating in $x_1$:
$$\int_0^\infty \partial_{x_1}\bigl[\partial_{x_2}\varphi\bigr]\,dx_1 = \bigl[\p
artial_{x_2}\varphi\bigr]_{0}^{\infty} = -\partial_{x_2}\varphi(0,x_2),$$
(the boundary at $\infty$ vanishes by compact support). Then integrating in $x_2
$:
$$\int_0^\infty -\partial_{x_2}\varphi(0,x_2)\,dx_2 = -\bigl[\varphi(0,x_2)\bigr
]_0^\infty = \varphi(0,0).$$
Hence $\partial_{x_1 x_2}^2\,\mathbb{1}_{x_1>0,x_2>0} = \delta_{(0,0)}$, the Dir
ac mass at the origin.

**Step 3: Multiply by the smooth function $\exp(-|x|^2)$.**

Multiplication of a distribution by a $C^\infty$ function is well-defined. For t
he Dirac delta and any smooth $f$:
$$f\cdot\delta_{(0,0)} = f(0)\cdot\delta_{(0,0)}.$$
Since $\exp(-|x|^2)\big|_{x=0} = e^{0} = 1$, we get
$$\exp(-|x|^2)\,\delta_{(0,0)} = 1\cdot\delta_{(0,0)} = \delta_{(0,0)}.$$

**Verification via test functions.**
$$\langle \exp(-|x|^2)\,\delta_{(0,0)},\,\varphi\rangle = \langle \delta_{(0,0)}
,\,\exp(-|x|^2)\,\varphi\rangle = \exp(0)\,\varphi(0,0) = \varphi(0,0) = \langle
 \delta_{(0,0)},\,\varphi\rangle.$$

## Result

$$\boxed{\exp(-|x|^2)\,\partial_{x_1 x_2}^2\,\mathbb{1}_{x_1>0,\,x_2>0} \;=\; \d
elta_{(0,0)}}$$

The result is the Dirac delta distribution at the origin of $\mathbb{R}^2$.
