is uniformly bounded on $\Omega$, hence locally uniformly bounded. By **Montel's
 theorem**, $\{f_n\}$ is a **normal family**: every subsequence of $\{f_n\}$ adm
its a further subsequence that converges uniformly on compact subsets of $\Omega

The pointwise limit $f$ satisfies $|f(z)| \le M$ for all $z \in \Omega$, since $
|f(z)| = \lim |f_n(z)| \le M$.

Let $K \subset \Omega$ be compact. We show $f_n \to f$ uniformly on $K$.

Suppose for contradiction that the convergence is not uniform on $K$. Then there

uch that
$$|f_{n_j}(z_j) - f(z_j)| \ge \varepsilon \quad \text{for all } j.$$

By Montel's theorem, the subsequence $\{f_{n_j}\}$ has a further subsequence $\{
f_{n_{j_k}}\}$ that converges uniformly on compact subsets of $\Omega$ to a holo
morphic function $g$. Uniform convergence on compact sets implies pointwise conv
ergence, so $g(z) = \lim_{k\to\infty} f_{n_{j_k}}(z)$ for every $z \in \Omega$.
But $\{f_{n_{j_k}}\}$ is also a subsequence of the original pointwise-convergent
 sequence $\{f_n\}$, so $f_{n_{j_k}}(z) \to f(z)$ pointwise. By uniqueness of po
intwise limits, $g = f$ on $\Omega$.

Therefore $f_{n_{j_k}} \to f$ uniformly on $K$:

This contradiction establishes that $f_n \to f$ uniformly on $K$. Since $K$ was
