## Proof

Let $g(x) = 2^x$. The polynomial $f$ of degree $\le 1007$ is the unique polynomi
al interpolating $g$ at the $1008$ points $x = 0, 1, \ldots, 1007$.

**Newton's forward difference formula.** The interpolating polynomial of degree
$\le n$ at the integer points $0, 1, \ldots, n$ is

$$P(x) = \sum_{j=0}^{n} \binom{x}{j}\, \Delta^{j} g(0),$$

where $\Delta$ is the forward difference operator $\Delta h(x) = h(x+1) - h(x)$.

**Computing the differences of $g(x)=2^x$.** We have

$$\Delta g(x) = 2^{x+1} - 2^{x} = 2^{x}(2-1) = 2^{x} = g(x).$$

Since $\Delta g = g$, applying $\Delta$ repeatedly gives $\Delta^{j} g = g$ for
every $j \ge 0$. In particular,

$$\Delta^{j} g(0) = g(0) = 2^{0} = 1 \qquad \text{for all } j \ge 0.$$

**The interpolating polynomial.** With $n = 1007$ and $\Delta^{j} g(0) = 1$,

$$f(x) = \sum_{j=0}^{1007} \binom{x}{j}.$$

**Evaluating at $x = 2015$.** We need

$$f(2015) = \sum_{j=0}^{1007} \binom{2015}{j}.$$

By the binomial theorem,

$$\sum_{j=0}^{2015} \binom{2015}{j} = 2^{2015}.$$

Since $2015$ is odd, the binomial coefficients pair up symmetrically: $\binom{20
15}{j} = \binom{2015}{2015-j}$, and the index $j = 1007$ pairs with $j = 1008$ (
there is no fixed central term). Thus the sum splits into two equal halves:

$$\sum_{j=0}^{1007} \binom{2015}{j} = \sum_{j=1008}^{2015} \binom{2015}{j} = \fr
ac{2^{2015}}{2} = 2^{2014}.$$

Therefore,

$$\boxed{f(2015) = 2^{2014}}.$$
