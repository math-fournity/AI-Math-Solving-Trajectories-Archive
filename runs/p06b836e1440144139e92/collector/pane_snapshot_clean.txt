## Proof

**Claim:** The statement is **true**. If each $X_\alpha$ is simply connected, th
en $\pi_1\!\left(\prod_{\alpha\in\Lambda} X_\alpha\right) = 0$.

The key tool is the following standard fact, which holds for **arbitrary** produ
cts (no countability or local niceness assumptions are needed).

**Lemma.** For any family $\{X_\alpha\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}$ of path-connected sp
aces with chosen basepoints $x_\alpha\in X_\alpha$, there is a natural isomorphi
sm
$$\pi_1\!\left(\prod_{\alpha\in\Lambda} X_\alpha,\, (x_\alpha)\right)\;\cong\;\p
rod_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\pi_1(X_\alpha, x_\alpha).$$

*Proof of Lemma.* Let $X = \prod_{\alpha} X_\alpha$ with basepoint $x_0 = (x_\al
pha)_{\alpha\in\Lambda}$, and let $p_\alpha : X \to X_\alpha$ denote the project
ion. Define
$$\Phi : \pi_1(X, x_0) \longrightarrow \prod_{\alpha}\pi_1(X_\alpha, x_\alpha),
\qquad \Phi\big([\gamma]\big) = \big([p_\alpha\circ\gamma]\big)_{\alpha\in\Lambd
a}.$$

This is well-defined: a homotopy $H:\gamma\simeq\gamma'$ rel endpoints in $X$ pr
ojects to homotopies $p_\alpha\circ H : p_\alpha\circ\gamma \simeq p_\alpha\circ
\gamma'$ rel endpoints in each $X_\alpha$.

**Homomorphism.** Concatenation of loops in $X$ is defined componentwise, so $\P
hi([\gamma]*[\gamma']) = \Phi([\gamma])\cdot\Phi([\gamma'])$.

**Injectivity.** Suppose $\Phi([\gamma])$ is trivial, i.e. for every $\alpha$ th
ere is a homotopy $H_\alpha:[0,1]^2\to X_\alpha$ with $H_\alpha(s,0)=p_\alpha\ci
rc\gamma(s)$, $H_\alpha(s,1)=x_\alpha$, $H_\alpha(0,t)=H_\alpha(1,t)=x_\alpha$.
Define $H:[0,1]^2\to X$ by $p_\alpha\circ H = H_\alpha$. This is well-defined an
d continuous by the **universal property of the product topology** (a map into a
 product is continuous iff every coordinate is continuous). Then $H$ is a homoto
py $\gamma\simeq c_{x_0}$ rel endpoints, so $[\gamma]=0$.

**Surjectivity.** Given loops $\gamma_\alpha$ in each $X_\alpha$ (based at $x_\a
lpha$), define $\gamma:[0,1]\to X$ by $p_\alpha\circ\gamma = \gamma_\alpha$. Aga
in by the universal property of the product topology, $\gamma$ is continuous, an
d it is a loop based at $x_0$. Clearly $\Phi([\gamma]) = ([\gamma_\alpha])_\alph
a$. $\square$

**Main proof.** Since each $X_\alpha$ is simply connected, it is in particular p
ath-connected and $\pi_1(X_\alpha, x_\alpha)=0$ for every choice of basepoint. B
y the Lemma,
$$\pi_1\!\left(\prod_{\alpha\in\Lambda} X_\alpha\right)\;\cong\;\prod_{\alpha\in
\Lambda}\pi_1(X_\alpha)\;=\;\prod_{\alpha\in\Lambda} 0 \;=\; 0.$$

Moreover, the product of path-connected spaces is path-connected (any two points
 $(x_\alpha)$, $(y_\alpha)$ are joined by the product of chosen paths $x_\alpha\
leadsto y_\alpha$, which is continuous again by the universal property of the pr
oduct topology). Hence $\prod_\alpha X_\alpha$ is itself simply connected.

**Remark on the role of the product topology.** The argument never requires the
index set $\Lambda$ to be finite or countable, nor does it require any separatio
n or local connectivity hypotheses on the $X_\alpha$. The only topological input
 is the universal property of the product topology: *a map $f:Z\to\prod_\alpha X
_\alpha$ is continuous if and only if every $p_\alpha\circ f$ is continuous.* Th
is is exactly what makes both the loop $\gamma$ and the homotopy $H$ continuous
when their coordinate maps are, and it is what guarantees that the componentwise
 construction of homotopies (used in injectivity) and of loops (used in surjecti
vity) lands in the product space legitimately.

Therefore the fundamental group of the product is trivial. $\blacksquare$
