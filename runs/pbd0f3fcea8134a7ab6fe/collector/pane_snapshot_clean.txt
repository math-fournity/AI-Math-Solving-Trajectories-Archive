$$\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 + \alpha_3 = 0, \quad \alpha_1\alpha_2 + \alpha_1\alpha_3
+ \alpha_2\alpha_3 = 1, \quad \alpha_1\alpha_2\alpha_3 = -1.$$

Let $\beta = \alpha_1\alpha_2$. From the third relation, $\beta \cdot \alpha_3 =
 -1$, so

$$\alpha_3 = -\frac{1}{\beta}.$$

From the first relation, $\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 = -\alpha_3 = \dfrac{1}{\beta}$.

Substituting into the second Vieta relation:

$$\alpha_1\alpha_2 + \alpha_3(\alpha_1+\alpha_2) = \beta + \left(-\frac{1}{\beta
}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\beta}\right) = \beta - \frac{1}{\beta^2} = 1.$$

Multiplying through by $\beta^2$:

$$\beta^3 - 1 = \beta^2 \implies \beta^3 - \beta^2 - 1 = 0.$$

So $\beta$ is a root of $g(x) = x^3 - x^2 - 1$.

**Irreducibility.** Testing rational roots of $g$: $g(1) = 1-1-1 = -1 \neq 0$ an

is irreducible. Hence $g(x)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, and since $\beta$
 satisfies it, $g$ is the minimal polynomial of $\beta$.
