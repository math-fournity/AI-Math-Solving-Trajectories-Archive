## Solution

Let $f_n(x)$ denote $\sin$ applied $n$ times to $x$, i.e. $f_1(x)=\sin x$ and $f
_{k+1}(x)=\sin(f_k(x))$. We seek
$$L=\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{x-f_n(x)}{x^3}.$$

**Claim.** For every $n\ge 1$,
$$f_n(x)=x-\frac{n}{6}x^3+O(x^5)\quad(x\to 0).$$

**Proof by induction.**

*Base case.* $\sin x = x - \dfrac{x^3}{6}+O(x^5)$, so the claim holds for $n=1$
with coefficient $a_1=-\tfrac16$.

*Inductive step.* Suppose $f_n(x)=x+a_n x^3+O(x^5)$ with $a_n=-\tfrac{n}{6}$. Th
en
$$f_{n+1}(x)=\sin(f_n(x))=f_n(x)-\frac{f_n(x)^3}{6}+O\!\big(f_n(x)^5\big).$$

Since $f_n(x)=x+O(x^3)$, we have $f_n(x)^5=O(x^5)$, and
$$f_n(x)^3=\big(x+a_n x^3+O(x^5)\big)^3=x^3+3a_n x^5+O(x^7)=x^3+O(x^5).$$

Therefore
$$f_{n+1}(x)=\big(x+a_n x^3+O(x^5)\big)-\frac{x^3}{6}+O(x^5)=x+\Big(a_n-\frac16\
Big)x^3+O(x^5).$$

So $a_{n+1}=a_n-\tfrac16=-\tfrac{n}{6}-\tfrac16=-\tfrac{n+1}{6}$, completing the
 induction. $\square$

**Evaluating the limit.** From the claim,
$$x-f_n(x)=x-\Big(x-\frac{n}{6}x^3+O(x^5)\Big)=\frac{n}{6}x^3+O(x^5).$$

Hence
$$\frac{x-f_n(x)}{x^3}=\frac{n}{6}+O(x^2)\xrightarrow{x\to 0}\frac{n}{6}.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{x-\sin(\sin(\cdots(\sin x)\cdots))}{x^3
}=\frac{n}{6}}$$
