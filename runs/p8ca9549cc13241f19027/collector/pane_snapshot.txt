## Proof

Recall that for a holomorphic map $f: U \to \mathbf{C}$ on a Riemann surface, th
e differential $df$ is the $\mathbf{C}$-valued $1$-form defined at each point $x
 \in U$ by linearizing $f$ in local coordinates. A key property of the exterior
derivative is its $\mathbf{C}$-linearity and its vanishing on constants:

$$d(c) = 0 \quad \text{for every constant } c \in \mathbf{C}.$$

Now $z': V \to B(0,1)$ is defined by
$$z'(x) = z(x) - z(Q), \qquad x \in V.$$

Here $Q$ is a **fixed** point, so $z(Q)$ is a fixed complex number — a constant
with respect to the variable $x \in V$. Applying the differential to both sides
and using linearity of $d$:

$$dz'(x) = d\bigl(z(x) - z(Q)\bigr) = dz(x) - d\bigl(z(Q)\bigr).$$

Since $z(Q)$ is constant in $x$, we have $d(z(Q)) = 0$. Therefore

$$dz'(x) = dz(x) \quad \text{for all } x \in V.$$

**Interpretation.** The map $z'$ is obtained from $z|_V$ by composing with the t
ranslation $w \mapsto w - z(Q)$ of the target disc. A translation of the target
has derivative identically equal to $1$, so by the chain rule the differential i
s unchanged. Concretely, in the local coordinate $z$ itself, both $dz$ and $dz'$
 are represented by the same $1$-form $d z$ on $V$; the only difference between
$z$ and $z'$ is that $z'$ re-centers the image so that $z'(Q) = 0$, which is a s
hift of the *values* of the coordinate function, not of its first-order variatio
n.

Hence the differentials coincide on $V$:

$$\boxed{dz' = dz \text{ on } V.}$$
