## Proof

We seek $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ satisfying
$$f(-y^{2})+f(x^{2}+y)-y=(x+y)\,f(x-y)\qquad(\star)$$
for all real $x,y$.

**Step 1: Two key substitutions.**

*Set $x=-y$.* Then $x-y=-2y$, $x+y=0$, $x^{2}+y=y^{2}+y$, so the RHS is $0$ and
$$f(-y^{2})+f(y^{2}+y)-y=0,\quad\text{i.e.}\quad f(-y^{2})+f(y^{2}+y)=y. \tag{1}
$$

*Set $x=y$.* Then $x-y=0$, $x+y=2y$, $x^{2}+y=y^{2}+y$, so
$$f(-y^{2})+f(y^{2}+y)-y=2y\,f(0).$$
Using (1), the LHS equals $0$, hence $2y\,f(0)=0$ for all $y$, giving
$$f(0)=0. \tag{2}$$

**Step 2: Reduce to a homogeneous equation.**

The function $f(x)=x$ satisfies $(\star)$ (direct check: LHS $=-y^{2}+x^{2}+y-y=
x^{2}-y^{2}=(x+y)(x-y)=$ RHS). Let $g(x)=f(x)-x$; then $f(x)=x+g(x)$ and $g(0)=0
$. Substituting into $(\star)$:

$$\bigl(-y^{2}+g(-y^{2})\bigr)+\bigl(x^{2}+y+g(x^{2}+y)\bigr)-y=(x+y)\bigl((x-y)
+g(x-y)\bigr).$$

The $x^{2}-y^{2}$ terms cancel on both sides, leaving
$$g(-y^{2})+g(x^{2}+y)=(x+y)\,g(x-y)\qquad\text{for all }x,y. \tag{3}$$

**Step 3: Determine $g$.**

*Set $y=0$ in (3):* $g(0)+g(x^{2})=x\,g(x)$, so by $g(0)=0$,
$$g(x^{2})=x\,g(x). \tag{4}$$
Replacing $x$ by $-x$ gives $g(x^{2})=-x\,g(-x)$. Comparing with (4), for $x\neq
 0$ we get $g(-x)=-g(x)$; together with $g(0)=0$ this shows **$g$ is odd**.

*Set $x=0$ in (3):* $g(-y^{2})+g(y)=y\,g(-y)$. Using oddness, $g(-y)=-g(y)$, and
 $g(-y^{2})=-g(y^{2})=-y\,g(y)$ by (4). Hence
$$-y\,g(y)+g(y)=-y\,g(y),$$
which forces $g(y)=0$ for every real $y$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Since $g\equiv 0$, we have $f(x)=x$ for all $x\in\mathbb{R}$. This function inde
ed satisfies $(\star)$, and it is the unique solution.
