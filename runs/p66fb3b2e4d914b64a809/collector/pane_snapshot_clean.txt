Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest $K_n$ such that the inequal
ity holds for all $a_1 \geq \cdots \geq a_n > 0$.

**Step 1: Reduction to extreme configurations.**

By homogeneity, set $a_1 = 1$ and $a_n = t \in (0,1]$. The middle terms $a_2, \l
dots, a_{n-1} \in [t, 1]$. Define $S = \sum a_i$, $T = \sum 1/a_i$. I claim that
 for fixed $a_1, a_n$, the product $ST$ is maximized when each middle $a_i$ equa
ls either $a_1$ or $a_n$.

Fix all variables except $a_i$ (for some $2 \leq i \leq n-1$). Write $ST = (A +
a_i)(B + 1/a_i)$ where $A = \sum_{j \neq i} a_j > 0$, $B = \sum_{j \neq i} 1/a_j
$. Then:
$$\frac{\partial^2(ST)}{\partial a_i^2} = \frac{2A}{a_i^3} > 0,$$
so $ST$ is strictly convex in each $a_i$ separately. A separately convex functio
n on a box $[t,1]^{n-2}$ attains its maximum at a vertex (iteratively push each
coordinate to an endpoint without decreasing the value). Thus the maximum of $ST
$ is achieved when every middle $a_i \in \{1, t\}$, and the ordering $a_1 \geq \
cdots \geq a_n$ is preserved by placing all $1$'s before all $t$'s.

**Step 2: Computing the ratio at extreme configurations.**

Suppose $m$ of the $n$ terms equal $1$ and $n - m$ equal $t$, where $m \in \{1,
\ldots, n-1\}$. Then:
$$S = m + (n-m)t, \quad T = m + \frac{n-m}{t}.$$
$$ST = m^2 + (n-m)^2 + m(n-m)\!\left(t + \frac{1}{t}\right).$$
Since $n^2 = m^2 + (n-m)^2 + 2m(n-m)$:
$$ST - n^2 = m(n-m)\!\left(t + \frac{1}{t} - 2\right) = m(n-m)\cdot\frac{(1-t)^2
}{t}.$$
The key ratio is therefore:
$$\frac{ST - n^2}{(a_1 - a_n)^2/(a_1 a_n)} = \frac{m(n-m)\cdot(1-t)^2/t}{(1-t)^2
/t} = m(n-m),$$
which is **independent of $t$**.

**Step 3: Maximizing over $m$.**

We maximize $m(n-m)$ over integers $m \in \{1, \ldots, n-1\}$. This quadratic in
 $m$ is maximized at $m = n/2$:
- If $n$ is even: $m = n/2$ gives $m(n-m) = n^2/4$.
- If $n$ is odd: $m = (n \pm 1)/2$ gives $m(n-m) = (n^2 - 1)/4$.

In both cases, $\max_m m(n-m) = \lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

For any valid configuration, by Step 1 the product $ST$ is bounded by the extrem
e configuration with the same $a_1, a_n$, so:
$$ST - n^2 \leq \lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor \cdot \frac{(a_1 - a_n)^2}{a_1 a_n}.$$
This bound is **sharp**: taking $m = \lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ terms equal to $a_1$ a
nd the rest equal to $a_n$ (with $a_1 > a_n$) achieves the ratio exactly $\lfloo
r n^2/4 \rfloor$ for every $t \in (0,1)$.

Therefore:
$$\boxed{K_n = \left\lfloor \frac{n^2}{4} \right\rfloor}.$$
