## Proof

The error function is
$$\operatorname{erf}(z)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_0^z e^{-t^2}\,dt=\frac{2}{\sqrt
{\pi}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n\,z^{2n+1}}{n!\,(2n+1)},$$
which is entire. We determine its **order of growth**
$$\rho=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log\log M(r)}{\log r},\qquad M(r)=\max_{|z|=r}
|\operatorname{erf}(z)|.$$

---

### Step 1. Coefficient formula gives $\rho=2$

For an entire function $f(z)=\sum_{k\ge 0}a_k z^k$, the order is
$$\rho=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{k\log k}{-\log|a_k|}.$$

Here $a_{2n+1}=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\dfrac{(-1)^n}{n!\,(2n+1)}$ and $a_{2n}=0$.
The zero coefficients contribute $0$ to the limsup, so we use the odd indices $k
=2n+1$:
$$|a_{2n+1}|=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{1}{n!\,(2n+1)},\qquad -\log|a_{2n+1}|=\lo
g\!\left(\tfrac{\sqrt\pi}{2}\right)+\log(n!)+\log(2n+1).$$
By Stirling, $\log(n!)=n\log n-n+O(\log n)$, so
$$-\log|a_{2n+1}|=n\log n+O(n).$$
With $k=2n+1\sim 2n$ and $\log k\sim \log n$:
$$\frac{k\log k}{-\log|a_k|}\sim\frac{2n\log n}{n\log n}=2.$$
Hence $\rho=2$.

---

### Step 2. Confirmation via the maximum modulus

Using $\operatorname{erf}(z)=1-\operatorname{erfc}(z)$ and the standard asymptot
ic $\operatorname{erfc}(z)\sim \dfrac{e^{-z^2}}{z\sqrt\pi}$ (valid in $|\arg z|<
\pi/4$, and by connection formulas in the complementary sectors), the growth of
$\operatorname{erf}$ is governed by $e^{-z^2}$ in the directions where $\operato
rname{Re}(z^2)<0$.

On $|z|=r$, write $z=re^{i\theta}$; then $|e^{-z^2}|=e^{-r^2\cos 2\theta}$, maxi
mized at $\theta=\pi/2$ (i.e. $z=\pm ir$), giving
$$\max_{|z|=r}|e^{-z^2}|=e^{r^2}.$$
Thus $M(r)\asymp \dfrac{e^{r^2}}{r\sqrt\pi}$, so $\log M(r)\sim r^2$ and
$$\frac{\log\log M(r)}{\log r}\sim\frac{\log(r^2)}{\log r}=2.$$
Both methods agree.

---

### Conclusion

The error function, as an entire function, has **order of growth $\boxed{2}$** (
and, incidentally, type $1$, since $\log M(r)\sim r^2$).
