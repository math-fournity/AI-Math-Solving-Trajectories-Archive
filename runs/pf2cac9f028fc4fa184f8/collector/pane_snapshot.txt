## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. The projection map $\pi: H \oplus H \to H$ always preserves open
ness; in particular, it maps every open subspace of $H \oplus H$ to an open subs
pace of $H$.

**Setup.** Let $H$ be a Hilbert space equipped with its norm topology. The direc
t sum $H \oplus H$ is endowed with the product topology (equivalently, the topol
ogy induced by any of the standard equivalent norms such as $\|(x,y)\| = \sqrt{\
|x\|^2+\|y\|^2}$). The projection is
$$\pi: H \oplus H \to H, \qquad \pi(x,y) = x.$$

**Key fact (projections from product spaces are open maps).** Let $U \subseteq H
 \oplus H$ be an arbitrary open set. We show $\pi(U)$ is open in $H$.

Take any $x \in \pi(U)$. By definition there exists $y \in H$ with $(x,y) \in U$
. Since $U$ is open in the product topology, there exist open sets $V \subseteq
H$ and $W \subseteq H$ such that
$$(x,y) \in V \times W \subseteq U.$$
Now observe: for every $x' \in V$, the point $(x', y)$ lies in $V \times W \subs
eteq U$, hence $x' = \pi(x',y) \in \pi(U)$. Therefore
$$x \in V \subseteq \pi(U).$$
Since $V$ is an open neighborhood of $x$ contained in $\pi(U)$, every point of $
\pi(U)$ is an interior point. Thus $\pi(U)$ is open in $H$.

This argument uses only the definition of the product topology and holds for **a
ny** topological spaces, hence certainly for Hilbert spaces of any dimension (fi
nite or infinite).

**Application to subspaces.** A "subspace" of $H \oplus H$ that is open is, in p
articular, an open set. By the result above, its projection is open in $H$. More
over:

- If $S \subseteq H \oplus H$ is a *linear* subspace that is open, then $S = H \
oplus H$ (the only open linear subspace of a normed space is the whole space, si
nce any open ball around $0$ contained in a linear subspace generates the entire
 space under scalar multiplication and addition). Then $\pi(S) = H$, which is op
en. ✓
- More generally, for any open set $U$ (subspace or not), $\pi(U)$ is open by th
e argument above. ✓

**Conclusion.** The projection $\pi: H \oplus H \to H$ is an open map: it sends
every open set—and in particular every open subspace—to an open set in $H$. This
 is a purely topological consequence of the product topology and requires no fin
ite-dimensionality or completeness assumption. Therefore the projection always p
reserves openness of subspaces.
