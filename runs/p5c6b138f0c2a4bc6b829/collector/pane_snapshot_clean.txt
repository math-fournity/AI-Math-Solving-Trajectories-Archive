## Proof

We are given:
- $f(x) = \Theta(x+1) - \Theta(x-1)$, the indicator function of the interval $[-
1, 1]$.
- Its Fourier transform (with convention $\widetilde{f}(k) = \int_{-\infty}^{\in
fty} f(x)\,e^{-ikx}\,dx$) is
$$\widetilde{f}(k) = \frac{2\sin k}{k}.$$

**Goal.** Evaluate $\displaystyle I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^2 x}{x^2
}\,dx$.

### Step 1. Parseval/Plancherel identity

For the Fourier transform convention $\widetilde{f}(k)=\int f(x)e^{-ikx}dx$, the
 Plancherel theorem states
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(x)|^2\,dx \;=\; \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty
}\bigl|\widetilde{f}(k)\bigr|^2\,dk.$$

### Step 2. Left-hand side

Since $f(x)=1$ on $[-1,1]$ and $0$ elsewhere,
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(x)|^2\,dx = \int_{-1}^{1}1\,dx = 2.$$

### Step 3. Right-hand side in terms of the target integral

$$\bigl|\widetilde{f}(k)\bigr|^2 = \frac{4\sin^2 k}{k^2}.$$

Hence
$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigl|\widetilde{f}(k)\bigr|^2\,dk
= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{4\sin^2 k}{k^2}\,dk
= \frac{4}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^2 k}{k^2}\,dk
= \frac{2}{\pi}\,I.$$

### Step 4. Equate and solve

By Plancherel,
$$2 = \frac{2}{\pi}\,I \quad\Longrightarrow\quad I = \pi.$$

### Verification (sanity check via a second route)

Using $\sin^2 x = \tfrac{1-\cos 2x}{2}$ and the distributional identity $\int_{-
\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos(ax)}{x^2}\,dx = \pi|a|$ (obtainable from differenti
ating the sinc-integral $\int\frac{\sin t}{t}dt=\pi$), we get
$$I = \frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos 2x}{x^2}\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\
cdot\pi\cdot 2 = \pi,$$
confirming the result.

### Result

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^2 x}{x^2}\,dx = \pi}$$
