$$\mathcal{G} = \mathbb{R}^{N}, \qquad N = n \cdot \prod_{j=1}^{n} m_j,$$

**Mixed strategies and the equilibrium equations.** Let $\Delta_i = \{p \in \mat
hbb{R}^{m_i}_{\ge 0} : \sum p = 1\}$ be player $i$'s simplex. A mixed-strategy p

$$U_i(a; p_{-i}) - U_i(p_i; p_{-i}) \le 0, \qquad \text{with equality whenever }
 p_i(a) > 0,$$

where $U_i$ is the expected payoff. These are polynomial (in fact multilinear) e

**Regular games.** Following Wilson (1971) and Harsanyi (1973), a Nash equilibri
um $p^*$ of a game $g$ is called *regular* (or *quasi-regular / indexable*) if:
- $p^*$ is isolated, and
- the Jacobian of the equilibrium-defining system (the KKT / complementarity sys
tem restricted to the active support) is non-singular at $p^*$.

A game $g$ is *regular* if every Nash equilibrium of $g$ is regular. The set $\m
athcal{R} \subseteq \mathcal{G}$ of regular games is the complement of a proper
real-algebraic subvariety of $\mathbb{R}^N$: the degeneracy conditions are given
 by the vanishing of certain polynomial resultants / minors of the Jacobian, and

icit games that are regular — e.g., generic games with a unique equilibrium, or
games whose equilibria are all strict). Hence

$$\lambda(\mathcal{G} \setminus \mathcal{R}) = 0, \qquad \text{i.e., } \lambda(\
mathcal{R}) = \lambda(\mathcal{G}) = +\infty.$$

Moreover, every regular game has **finitely many** Nash equilibria (each equilib
rium is isolated and the joint strategy space $\prod_i \Delta_i$ is compact, so
isolated equilibria can only accumulate on the boundary; regularity rules out bo
undary accumulation into a continuum).

**Oddness via the index theorem.** For a regular game $g$, each Nash equilibrium

namely the sign of the determinant of the Jacobian of the equilibrium map (the s
tandard Nash-map / Walrasian-map formulation) at $p^*$.

The fundamental **index theorem** for Nash equilibria (Govindan–Wilson, building
 on Wilson 1971 and the Poincaré–Hopf / topological-degree argument applied to t
he Nash equilibrium correspondence over the space of games) states:

$$\sum_{p^* \in \mathrm{NE}(g)} \operatorname{ind}(p^*) = +1.$$

The sum equals $+1$ because it is the degree of the Nash map, which is homotopic
 (through the convex-combination family of games) to the identity map on the pro
duct of simplices, whose degree is $+1$.

Let $n_+ = \#\{p^* : \operatorname{ind}(p^*) = +1\}$ and $n_- = \#\{p^* : \opera
torname{ind}(p^*) = -1\}$. Then

$$n_+ - n_- = 1.$$

Consequently the total number of Nash equilibria is

$$|\mathrm{NE}(g)| = n_+ + n_- = (n_- + 1) + n_- = 2n_- + 1,$$

which is **finite and odd**.

a. Therefore

$$\mathcal{R} \;\subseteq\; \{\,g \in \mathcal{G} : \mathrm{NE}(g) \text{ is fin

$$\boxed{\lambda(\mathcal{O}) = \lambda(\mathbb{R}^N) = +\infty.}$$

ash equilibria (i.e., games with infinitely many equilibria, or with a finite ev
