## Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory

**Setup.** Let \(L/K\) be a (finite) Galois extension, and let
\[
G = \operatorname{Gal}(L/K) = \{\,K\text{-automorphisms of }L\,\}
\]
denote its **Galois group**. For any intermediate field \(E\) with \(K \subseteq
 E \subseteq L\), define
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(L/E) = \{\,\sigma \in G : \sigma|_E = \mathrm{id}_E\,\},
\]
the subgroup of \(G\) fixing \(E\) pointwise. For any subgroup \(H \le G\), defi
ne
\[
L^H = \operatorname{Fix}(H) = \{\,x \in L : \sigma(x) = x \text{ for all } \sigm
a \in H\,\},
\]
the **fixed field** of \(H\).

---

### The Theorem

The maps
\[
E \;\longmapsto\; \operatorname{Gal}(L/E), \qquad H \;\longmapsto\; L^H
\]
are **mutually inverse order-reversing bijections** between

- the set of **intermediate fields** \(E\) with \(K \subseteq E \subseteq L\), a
nd
- the set of **subgroups** \(H\) of \(G\).

Explicitly:

1. **(Inversion / Galois correspondence.)** For every intermediate field \(E\),
   \[
   L^{\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)} = E,
   \]
   and for every subgroup \(H \le G\),
   \[
   \operatorname{Gal}(L/L^H) = H.
   \]
   Hence the two maps are inverse to each other, giving a bijection
   \[
   \{\text{intermediate fields}\} \;\longleftrightarrow\; \{\text{subgroups of }
G\}.
   \]

2. **(Order reversal.)** The bijection is inclusion-reversing:
   \[
   E_1 \subseteq E_2 \iff \operatorname{Gal}(L/E_1) \supseteq \operatorname{Gal}
(L/E_2).
   \]
   In particular,
   \[
   \operatorname{Gal}(L/K) = G \quad\text{corresponds to}\quad K,
   \]
   \[
   \operatorname{Gal}(L/L) = \{\mathrm{id}\} \quad\text{corresponds to}\quad L.
   \]

3. **(Degree / index duality.)** For every intermediate field \(E\),
   \[
   [L : E] = |\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)| \quad\text{and}\quad [E:K] = [G : \operat
orname{Gal}(L/E)].
   \]
   So the degree of the extension equals the order of the corresponding subgroup
, and the degree of the bottom part equals the index of the subgroup in \(G\).

4. **(Normality correspondence.)** An intermediate field \(E\) is a **Galois (no
rmal) extension of \(K\)** if and only if the corresponding subgroup \(\operator
name{Gal}(L/E)\) is a **normal subgroup** of \(G\). When this holds,
   \[
   \operatorname{Gal}(E/K) \;\cong\; G \big/ \operatorname{Gal}(L/E),
   \]
   and the quotient map \(G \to G/\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)\) is induced by restri
ction of automorphisms: each \(\sigma \in G\) restricts to an automorphism \(\si
gma|_E \in \operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\), with kernel \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)\).

---

### Key Properties of the Galois Group

- **Finiteness.** For a finite Galois extension, \(|G| = [L:K]\).
- **Fixed field of the whole group.** \(L^G = K\). This is in fact *equivalent*
to \(L/K\) being Galois (when \(L/K\) is finite and separable): the extension is
 Galois precisely when the fixed field of the full automorphism group is exactly
 the base field.
- **Action on roots.** If \(L\) is the splitting field of a separable polynomial
 \(f \in K[x]\), then \(G\) acts faithfully on the set of roots of \(f\) in \(L\
), realizing \(G\) as a subgroup of the symmetric group on those roots.
- **Artin's lemma (engine of the theorem).** For any finite subgroup \(H \le \op
eratorname{Aut}(L)\),
  \[
  [L : L^H] = |H|.
  \]
  This is the key counting result that, combined with \(L^G = K\) for Galois ext
ensions, drives the inversion formulas in part (1).

---

### Summary in one line

For a finite Galois extension \(L/K\), there is an inclusion-reversing bijection
 between intermediate fields \(K \subseteq E \subseteq L\) and subgroups \(H \le
 \operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\), given by \(E \mapsto \operatorname{Gal}(L/E)\) and \
(H \mapsto L^H\); under this correspondence \([L:E] = |\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)|\
), \([E:K] = [G:\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)]\), and \(E/K\) is Galois iff \(\operato
rname{Gal}(L/E) \trianglelefteq G\), in which case \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/K) \co
ng G/\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)\).
