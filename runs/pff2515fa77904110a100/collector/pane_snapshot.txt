## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle L = \lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{k}{n^{
2}}\right)^{\!n^{2}/k}$.

**Key observation.** Set $x_k = \dfrac{k}{n^{2}}$, so each summand equals $x_k^{
\,1/x_k}$. Define $g(x)=x^{1/x}=e^{(\ln x)/x}$ for $x>0$.

**Monotonicity of $g$ on the relevant range.** We have
$$
\frac{d}{dx}\ln g(x)=\frac{d}{dx}\frac{\ln x}{x}=\frac{1-\ln x}{x^{2}},
$$
which is positive for $x<e$. Since $x_k = k/n^{2}\le n/n^{2}=1/n\le 1<e$ for all
 $n\ge 1$ and all $1\le k\le n$, the function $g$ is **increasing** on the entir
e range $\{x_1,\dots,x_n\}\subset(0,\,1/n]$.

**Bounding each term.** By monotonicity, the largest summand occurs at $k=n$:
$$
\left(\frac{k}{n^{2}}\right)^{n^{2}/k}=g(x_k)\le g\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)=\le
ft(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\!n}=n^{-n},\qquad 1\le k\le n.
$$

**Squeezing the sum.** There are $n$ non-negative terms, each at most $n^{-n}$,
so
$$
0\;\le\;\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{k}{n^{2}}\right)^{n^{2}/k}\;\le\; n\cdot n^{-n
}=n^{1-n}.
$$

Since $n^{1-n}\to 0$ as $n\to\infty$, the Squeeze Theorem gives

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{k}{n^{2}}\right
)^{n^{2}/k}=0.}$$
