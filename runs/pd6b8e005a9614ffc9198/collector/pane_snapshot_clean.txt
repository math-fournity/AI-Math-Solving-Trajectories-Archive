# Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $C([0,T];\, L^1(\mathbb{R}^N)\cap L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^N))$ is a
complete metric space under the given norm.

---

## Step 1: $X := L^1(\mathbb{R}^N)\cap L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^N)$ with $\|f\|_X = \
|f\|_1 + \|f\|_\infty$ is a Banach space.

Let $(f_n)$ be Cauchy in $X$. Then $(f_n)$ is Cauchy in both $L^1$ and $L^\infty
$ (since each norm is bounded by $\|\cdot\|_X$). Since $L^1$ and $L^\infty$ are
complete, there exist $f\in L^1$ and $g\in L^\infty$ with
$$f_n \to f \text{ in } L^1, \qquad f_n \to g \text{ in } L^\infty.$$

Convergence in $L^1$ gives a subsequence $f_{n_k}\to f$ a.e.; convergence in $L^
\infty$ gives $f_{n_k}\to g$ a.e. Hence $f=g$ a.e., so $f\in L^1\cap L^\infty =
X$. Moreover
$$\|f_n - f\|_X = \|f_n-f\|_1 + \|f_n-f\|_\infty \to 0.$$
Thus $X$ is a Banach space. $\checkmark$

---

## Step 2: The norm $\sup_{0<t<T}\|u(t)\|_X$ equals $\sup_{0\le t\le T}\|u(t)\|_
X$ for $u\in C([0,T];X)$.

For $u\in C([0,T];X)$, the map $t\mapsto \|u(t)\|_X$ is continuous (composition
of continuous $u$ with the continuous norm $\|\cdot\|_X$), hence attains its max
imum on the compact set $[0,T]$.

Clearly $\sup_{(0,T)}\le \sup_{[0,T]}$. For the reverse: if the maximum is attai
ned at $t=0$, take any sequence $t_k\to 0^+$ with $t_k\in(0,T)$; by continuity $
\|u(t_k)\|_X\to\|u(0)\|_X$, so $\sup_{(0,T)}\ge\|u(0)\|_X$. The case $t=T$ is id
entical. Hence
$$\sup_{0<t<T}\|u(t)\|_X = \sup_{0\le t\le T}\|u(t)\|_X.$$

So the given norm is exactly the standard sup-norm on $C([0,T];X)$. $\checkmark$

---

## Step 3: $C([0,T];X)$ with the sup-norm is a Banach space whenever $X$ is a Ba
nach space.

Let $(u_n)$ be Cauchy in $C([0,T];X)$. For each fixed $t\in[0,T]$, $(u_n(t))$ is
 Cauchy in $X$, so by completeness of $X$ there exists $u(t)\in X$ with $u_n(t)\
to u(t)$.

Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose $N_0$ such that $\|u_n-u_m\|_\infty<\varepsilon$ f
or $n,m\ge N_0$. Then for all $t$:
$$\|u_n(t)-u(t)\|_X = \lim_{m\to\infty}\|u_n(t)-u_m(t)\|_X \le \varepsilon.$$
So $\|u_n-u\|_\infty\le\varepsilon$ for $n\ge N_0$; i.e., $u_n\to u$ uniformly.

Uniform convergence preserves continuity: $u_n\to u$ uniformly and each $u_n$ is
 continuous, so $u\in C([0,T];X)$. Hence $C([0,T];X)$ is complete. $\checkmark$

---

## Conclusion

By Step 1, $X=L^1(\mathbb{R}^N)\cap L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^N)$ is a Banach space. B
y Step 2, the given norm coincides with the standard sup-norm on $C([0,T];X)$. B
y Step 3, $C([0,T];X)$ with the sup-norm is a Banach space, hence a **complete m
etric space**.

**Answer: Yes**, the space $C([0,T];\, L^1(\mathbb{R}^N)\cap L^\infty(\mathbb{R}
^N))$ is complete under the norm $\|u\|=\sup_{0<t<T}\bigl(\|u(t)\|_{L^1}+\|u(t)\
|_{L^\infty}\bigr)$.
