## Proof

We are given the zero-mean Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process
$$X_t = \sigma e^{-\alpha t}\int_0^t e^{\alpha s}\,dB_s.$$

**Step 1: Identify the martingale.** Define
$$M_t := \int_0^t e^{\alpha s}\,dB_s.$$
Since the integrand $f(s)=e^{\alpha s}$ is deterministic (hence square-integrabl
e on every finite interval $[0,t]$), $M_t$ is a continuous (local) martingale st
arting at $0$. Its quadratic variation is given by the standard Itô isometry ide
ntity for deterministic integrands:
$$[M,M]_t = \int_0^t e^{2\alpha s}\,ds.$$

**Step 2: Express $X$ as a deterministic factor times a martingale.** We have $X
_t = g(t)\,M_t$ where $g(t) = \sigma e^{-\alpha t}$ is a deterministic $C^1$ fun
ction (hence a continuous finite-variation process).

**Step 3: Compute the quadratic variation of $X$.** Since $g$ is of finite varia
tion and $M$ is a continuous local martingale, applying Itô's formula to $X_t =
g(t)M_t$:
$$dX_t = g(t)\,dM_t + M_t\,g'(t)\,dt.$$
The term $M_t g'(t)\,dt$ is of finite variation, while $g(t)\,dM_t$ is the local
-martingale part.

For a continuous semimartingale $X = X^{\text{fv}} + X^{\text{mart}}$, the quadr
atic variation satisfies:
- $[X^{\text{fv}}, X^{\text{fv}}]_t = 0$ (finite-variation continuous processes
have zero quadratic variation),
- $[X^{\text{fv}}, X^{\text{mart}}]_t = 0$ (the cross-variation between a contin
uous FV process and a continuous local martingale vanishes),
- $[X,X]_t = [X^{\text{mart}}, X^{\text{mart}}]_t$.

The martingale part is $X^{\text{mart}}_t = \int_0^t g(s)\,dM_s$, so by the defi
nition of quadratic variation of an Itô integral with a predictable integrand $g
(s)$:
$$[X,X]_t = \int_0^t g(s)^2\,d[M,M]_s.$$

**Step 4: Substitute and evaluate.** Using $g(s) = \sigma e^{-\alpha s}$ and $d[
M,M]_s = e^{2\alpha s}\,ds$:
$$[X,X]_t = \int_0^t \sigma^2 e^{-2\alpha s}\cdot e^{2\alpha s}\,ds = \int_0^t \
sigma^2\,ds = \sigma^2 t.$$

**Verification via the SDE.** The process $X_t$ satisfies the OU SDE $dX_t = -\a
lpha X_t\,dt + \sigma\,dB_t$, obtained by differentiating the representation via
 Itô's product rule. The diffusion coefficient is the constant $\sigma$, so the
quadratic variation — which for an Itô process $dX_t = \mu_t\,dt + \nu_t\,dB_t$
equals $\int_0^t \nu_s^2\,ds$ — gives $[X,X]_t = \int_0^t \sigma^2\,ds = \sigma^
2 t$, confirming the result.

### Result
