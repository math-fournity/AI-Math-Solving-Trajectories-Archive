## Answer

**Yes, $\lim A_i$ is nonempty.**

I'll prove this for the case of **sets** (the substantive case); the case of **m
odules** is immediate since the zero tuple $(0,0,\dots)$ always lies in the inve
rse limit (every transition map is a module homomorphism, hence sends $0\mapsto
0$), so $\lim A_i\neq\varnothing$ trivially, regardless of ML or countability.

---

### Proof (sets)

Let $(A_i,\varphi_{ji})_{i\in I}$ be a directed inverse system of nonempty sets
over a countable directed set $I$, satisfying the **Mittag-Leffler** condition:
for every $i\in I$, the descending family
$$\varphi_{ji}(A_j)\subseteq A_i,\qquad j\ge i,$$
is eventually constant. Denote its stable value by
$$B_i \;:=\; \bigcap_{j\ge i}\varphi_{ji}(A_j)\;=\;\varphi_{ji}(A_j)\quad\text{f
or all }j\gg i.$$

The proof proceeds in three steps.

---

**Step 1. Reduction to $I=\mathbb{N}$.**

Enumerate $I=\{i_0,i_1,i_2,\dots\}$. Using directedness, define a sequence $j_0,
j_1,\dots$ by $j_0=i_0$ and $j_{n+1}:=$ any element $\ge j_n$ and $\ge i_{n+1}$.
 Then $(j_n)$ is **cofinal** in $I$ (every $i_k\le j_k$). Passing to a cofinal s
ubsequence preserves inverse limits:
$$\lim_{i\in I} A_i \;\cong\; \lim_{n\in\mathbb N} A_{j_n},$$
and it preserves the ML condition (the stable images $B_{j_n}$ are unchanged) an
d nonemptiness of the terms. Hence it suffices to prove the theorem for $I=\math
bb{N}$ with its usual order.

---

**Step 2. The subsystem $(B_n)$ is a surjective inverse system of nonempty sets.
**

Write $\varphi_{mn}:A_m\to A_n$ for $m\ge n$. Set
$$B_n=\bigcap_{k\ge n}\varphi_{kn}(A_k).$$

*Nonemptiness.* By ML, there exists $N(n)\ge n$ such that $\varphi_{kn}(A_k)=B_n
$ for all $k\ge N(n)$. Since $A_k\neq\varnothing$ and $\varphi_{kn}$ is a functi
on, $\varphi_{kn}(A_k)\neq\varnothing$; hence $B_n\neq\varnothing$.

*Compatibility.* For $m\ge n$ and $b_m\in B_m$,
$$\varphi_{mn}(b_m)\in\varphi_{mn}\!\Big(\bigcap_{k\ge m}\varphi_{km}(A_k)\Big)\
subseteq\bigcap_{k\ge m}\varphi_{kn}(A_k)\subseteq\bigcap_{k\ge n}\varphi_{kn}(A
_k)=B_n,$$
so $\varphi_{mn}$ restricts to a map $B_m\to B_n$. Thus $(B_n,\varphi_{mn}|_{B_m
})$ is an inverse subsystem of $(A_n)$.

*Surjectivity of the restricted transitions.* Fix $m\ge n$ and $b_n\in B_n$. Cho
ose $k\ge \max\{N(m),N(n)\}$; then $k\ge m\ge n$ and
$$\varphi_{km}(A_k)=B_m,\qquad \varphi_{kn}(A_k)=B_n.$$
Since $b_n\in B_n=\varphi_{kn}(A_k)$, pick $a_k\in A_k$ with $\varphi_{kn}(a_k)=
b_n$. Set $b_m:=\varphi_{km}(a_k)\in\varphi_{km}(A_k)=B_m$. Then
$$\varphi_{mn}(b_m)=\varphi_{mn}(\varphi_{km}(a_k))=\varphi_{kn}(a_k)=b_n.$$
Hence $\varphi_{mn}|_{B_m}:B_m\twoheadrightarrow B_n$ is **surjective** for ever
y $m\ge n$.

---

**Step 3. Constructing a thread in $\lim B_n$ (hence in $\lim A_n$).**

Since each $B_n\neq\varnothing$ and the transitions $B_{n+1}\to B_n$ are surject
ive, we build a thread inductively (using the axiom of choice, countably many ti
mes):

- Pick $b_0\in B_0$.
- Having chosen $b_n\in B_n$, use surjectivity of $B_{n+1}\to B_n$ to pick $b_{n
+1}\in B_{n+1}$ with $\varphi_{n+1,n}(b_{n+1})=b_n$.

Because $\mathbb N$ is linearly ordered and the system is compatible ($\varphi_{
m,n}=\varphi_{n+1,n}\circ\cdots\circ\varphi_{m,m-1}$), the sequence $(b_n)_{n\ge
 0}$ satisfies $\varphi_{mn}(b_m)=b_n$ for **all** $m\ge n$. Thus $(b_n)\in\lim
B_n$.

Since $B_n\subseteq A_n$ for all $n$ and the inclusion maps are compatible with
the transition maps, $(b_n)\in\lim A_n$ as well. Therefore
$$\lim A_n\;\supseteq\;\lim B_n\;\neq\;\varnothing.\qquad\blacksquare$$

---

### Conclusion

For **sets**: $\lim A_i\neq\varnothing$ by the argument above (ML + countability
 of $I$ + nonemptiness of $A_i$).

For **modules**: $\lim A_i\neq\varnothing$ trivially, since $(0,0,\dots)\in\lim
A_i$ (every transition map is a module homomorphism and sends $0$ to $0$). The s
et-theoretic argument also applies, giving the same conclusion.

**In both cases, $\lim A_i$ is nonempty.** The answer is **yes**.
