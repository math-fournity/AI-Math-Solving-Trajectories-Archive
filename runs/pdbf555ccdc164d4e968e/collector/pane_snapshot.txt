## Proof

An **antisymmetric tensor of order $k$** on $\mathbb{R}^n$ is a multilinear map
$$T : \underbrace{\mathbb{R}^n \times \cdots \times \mathbb{R}^n}_{k} \to \mathb
b{R}$$
whose components $T_{i_1 i_2 \cdots i_k}$ change sign under the interchange of a
ny two indices:
$$T_{\cdots i_p \cdots i_q \cdots} = -T_{\cdots i_q \cdots i_p \cdots}.$$

**Consequence of antisymmetry.** If any two indices among $i_1, \ldots, i_k$ are
 equal, then swapping those two equal indices leaves the component unchanged, wh
ile antisymmetry forces it to equal its own negative:
$$T_{\cdots i \cdots i \cdots} = -T_{\cdots i \cdots i \cdots} \implies T_{\cdot
s i \cdots i \cdots} = 0.$$
Hence a nonzero component must have **all $k$ indices distinct**.

**Reduction to index sets.** Once we fix the *set* of $k$ distinct indices $\{i_
1, \ldots, i_k\} \subseteq \{1, \ldots, n\}$, every ordering of these indices gi
ves a component determined by a single one (say the increasing order) up to the
sign of the permutation that reorders them. Specifically, if $\sigma$ is the per
mutation with $\sigma(1) \ldots \sigma(k)$ being the chosen ordering and $j_1 <
\cdots < j_k$ is the increasing order of the same set, then
$$T_{i_1 \cdots i_k} = \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\, T_{j_1 \cdots j_k}.$$
So each distinct $k$-element subset of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ contributes **exactly on
e** independent component.

**Counting.** The number of independent components equals the number of $k$-elem
ent subsets of an $n$-element set:
$$\binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!\,(n-k)!}.$$

**Applying to the problem.** Here $k = 6$ (order of the tensor) and $n = 7$ (dim
ension of $\mathbb{R}^7$). Therefore the number of independent components is
$$\binom{7}{6} = \frac{7!}{6!\,1!} = 7.$$

**Answer:** The antisymmetric tensor of order $6$ on $\mathbb{R}^7$ has $\boxed{
7}$ independent components.
