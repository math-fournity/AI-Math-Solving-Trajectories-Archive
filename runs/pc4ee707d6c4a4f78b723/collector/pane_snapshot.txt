## Solution

**Step 1: Identify the region of integration.**

For $y \in [0,1]$, $x$ ranges from $1-\sqrt{1-y^2}$ to $1+\sqrt{1-y^2}$. The bou
nds satisfy $(x-1)^2 = 1-y^2$, i.e., $(x-1)^2 + y^2 = 1$. This is the **upper ha
lf of the disk** of radius $1$ centered at $(1,0)$.

**Step 2: Change the order of integration.**

Swapping to $dy\,dx$: $x$ ranges from $0$ to $2$, and for each $x$, $y$ ranges f
rom $0$ to $\sqrt{1-(x-1)^2} = \sqrt{2x-x^2}$:

$$I = \int_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{2x-x^2}} \frac{(x^2+y^2)\,x}{\sqrt{4x^2-(x^2+y
^2)^2}}\,dy\,dx.$$

**Step 3: Convert to polar coordinates.**

Set $x = r\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\theta$. The region $(x-1)^2+y^2\le 1$, $y\ge 0
$ becomes $r \le 2\cos\theta$ with $\theta\in[0,\pi/2]$. The integrand transform
s as:

$$\frac{(x^2+y^2)\,x}{\sqrt{4x^2-(x^2+y^2)^2}} = \frac{r^2\cdot r\cos\theta}{\sq
rt{4r^2\cos^2\theta - r^4}} = \frac{r^2\cos\theta}{\sqrt{4\cos^2\theta - r^2}}.$
$

Including the Jacobian $r$:

$$I = \int_{0}^{\pi/2}\int_{0}^{2\cos\theta} \frac{r^3\cos\theta}{\sqrt{4\cos^2\
theta - r^2}}\,dr\,d\theta.$$

**Step 4: Evaluate the inner integral.**

Substitute $r = 2\cos\theta\sin\phi$, $dr = 2\cos\theta\cos\phi\,d\phi$, with $\
phi: 0\to\pi/2$:

$$\sqrt{4\cos^2\theta - r^2} = 2\cos\theta\cos\phi,\qquad r^3 = 8\cos^3\theta\,\
sin^3\phi.$$

$$\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{8\cos^3\theta\,\sin^3\phi\cdot\cos\theta}{2\cos\theta\co
s\phi}\cdot 2\cos\theta\cos\phi\,d\phi = 8\cos^4\theta\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sin^3\phi
\,d\phi = 8\cos^4\theta\cdot\frac{2}{3} = \frac{16}{3}\cos^4\theta.$$

**Step 5: Evaluate the outer integral.**

$$I = \frac{16}{3}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^4\theta\,d\theta = \frac{16}{3}\cdot\frac
{3\pi}{16} = \pi,$$

using the Wallis integral $\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^4\theta\,d\theta = \frac{3\pi}{1
6}$.

$$\boxed{I = \pi}$$
