We must show that for every $\varepsilon > 0$,
$$
\sup_{\theta}\; P\!\left(\left|\frac{1}{\sigma_n(\theta)} - \frac{1}{\sigma(\the
ta)}\right| > \varepsilon\right) \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.
$$

The two hypotheses are:

(H1) $\sup_\theta P(|\sigma_n(\theta) - \sigma(\theta)| > \eta) \to 0$ for every
 $\eta > 0$ (uniform convergence in probability).

(H2) $\sup_\theta P(\sigma_n(\theta) < \mu) \to 0$ for some $\mu > 0$.

---

**Step 1. The limit $\sigma(\theta)$ is bounded below by $\mu$ a.s., uniformly i
n $\theta$.**

Fix any $c$ with $0 < c < \mu$ and set $\delta = \mu - c > 0$. On the event $\{\
sigma(\theta) < c,\; |\sigma_n(\theta) - \sigma(\theta)| \le \delta\}$ we have
$$
\sigma_n(\theta) \le \sigma(\theta) + \delta < c + \delta = \mu,
$$
so this event is contained in $\{\sigma_n(\theta) < \mu\}$. Hence
$$
P(\sigma(\theta) < c) \le P(\sigma_n(\theta) < \mu) + P(|\sigma_n(\theta) - \sig
ma(\theta)| > \delta).
$$
The left-hand side is independent of $n$, while the right-hand side tends to $0$
 uniformly in $\theta$ by (H1) and (H2). Therefore $P(\sigma(\theta) < c) = 0$ f
or every $\theta$. Since $c < \mu$ was arbitrary,
$$
\sigma(\theta) \ge \mu \quad \text{a.s., for every } \theta. \tag{A}
$$

---

**Step 2. Decompose the event of interest.**

Using $\left|\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{b}\right| = \frac{|a-b|}{|ab|}$,
$$
\left|\frac{1}{\sigma_n(\theta)} - \frac{1}{\sigma(\theta)}\right| = \frac{|\sig
ma_n(\theta) - \sigma(\theta)|}{\sigma_n(\theta)\,\sigma(\theta)}.
$$

Define the "good" event $G_n(\theta) := \{\sigma_n(\theta) \ge \mu\}$. On $G_n(\
theta)$, by (A) we have $\sigma(\theta) \ge \mu$ a.s., so $\sigma_n(\theta)\,\si
gma(\theta) \ge \mu^2$ a.s., and consequently
$$
\left|\frac{1}{\sigma_n(\theta)} - \frac{1}{\sigma(\theta)}\right| \le \frac{|\s
igma_n(\theta) - \sigma(\theta)|}{\mu^2} \quad \text{on } G_n(\theta).
$$

Therefore, for any $\varepsilon > 0$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
P\!\left(\left|\frac{1}{\sigma_n(\theta)} - \frac{1}{\sigma(\theta)}\right| > \v
arepsilon\right)
&\le P\!\left(\left|\frac{1}{\sigma_n(\theta)} - \frac{1}{\sigma(\theta)}\right|
 > \varepsilon,\; G_n(\theta)\right) + P(G_n(\theta)^c) \\
&\le P\!\left(\frac{|\sigma_n(\theta) - \sigma(\theta)|}{\mu^2} > \varepsilon\ri
ght) + P(\sigma_n(\theta) < \mu) \\
&= P\!\left(|\sigma_n(\theta) - \sigma(\theta)| > \varepsilon\mu^2\right) + P(\s
igma_n(\theta) < \mu).
\end{aligned}
$$

---

**Step 3. Take the supremum and pass to the limit.**

Taking $\sup_\theta$ on both sides,
$$
\sup_\theta P\!\left(\left|\frac{1}{\sigma_n(\theta)} - \frac{1}{\sigma(\theta)}
\right| > \varepsilon\right)
\le \sup_\theta P\!\left(|\sigma_n(\theta) - \sigma(\theta)| > \varepsilon\mu^2\
right) + \sup_\theta P(\sigma_n(\theta) < \mu).
$$

The first term on the right tends to $0$ by (H1) applied with $\eta = \varepsilo
n\mu^2 > 0$, and the second tends to $0$ by (H2). Hence
$$
\sup_\theta P\!\left(\left|\frac{1}{\sigma_n(\theta)} - \frac{1}{\sigma(\theta)}
\right| > \varepsilon\right) \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.
$$

Since $\varepsilon > 0$ was arbitrary, $1/\sigma_n(\theta) \to 1/\sigma(\theta)$
 uniformly in probability over $\theta$. $\blacksquare$
