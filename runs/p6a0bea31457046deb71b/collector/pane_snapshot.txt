Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether, for any given positive rea
l $l$, there exists a closed hyperbolic surface with injectivity radius $\geq l$
.

**Answer: Yes.** For every $l > 0$, such a surface exists.

---

## Proof

### Preliminaries

**Injectivity radius and systole.** For a closed hyperbolic surface $X = \mathbb
{H}^2/\Gamma$ (where $\Gamma$ is a torsion-free cocompact Fuchsian group), the i
njectivity radius at a point $p \in X$ is:

$$\operatorname{inj}(p) = \frac{1}{2}\min_{\gamma \in \Gamma \setminus\{\mathrm{
id}\}} d(\tilde{p},\, \gamma\cdot\tilde{p}),$$

where $\tilde{p}$ is any lift of $p$ to $\mathbb{H}^2$. This holds because the e
xponential map $\exp_{\tilde{p}}$ is a diffeomorphism on $B(0,r) \subset T_{\til
de p}\mathbb{H}^2$ projecting injectively to $X$ if and only if $B(\tilde{p}, r)
$ is disjoint from all its translates $\gamma \cdot B(\tilde{p}, r)$, which requ
ires $r \leq \frac{1}{2}d(\tilde{p}, \gamma \tilde{p})$ for every $\gamma \neq \
mathrm{id}$.

Taking the infimum over $p$ and interchanging $\inf_p$ and $\min_\gamma$ (justif
ied since only finitely many conjugacy classes contribute to small displacements
):

$$\operatorname{inj}(X) = \frac{1}{2}\min_{\gamma \in \Gamma\setminus\{\mathrm{i
d}\}} \ell(\gamma) = \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{sys}(X),$$

where $\ell(\gamma) = \inf_{p \in \mathbb{H}^2} d(p, \gamma p)$ is the **transla
tion length** of $\gamma$ (the length of the corresponding closed geodesic), and
 $\operatorname{sys}(X)$ is the **systole** (shortest closed geodesic). The infi
mum $\inf_p d(p,\gamma p) = \ell(\gamma)$ is achieved on the axis of the hyperbo
lic isometry $\gamma$.

Thus, $\operatorname{inj}(X) \geq l$ is equivalent to $\operatorname{sys}(X) \ge
q 2l$, i.e., every non-identity element of $\Gamma$ has translation length $\geq
 2l$.

### Two key facts

**Fact 1 (Finiteness of short geodesics).** On a closed hyperbolic surface, for
any $L > 0$, there are only finitely many closed geodesics of length $\leq L$.

*Justification:* The closed geodesics of length $\leq L$ correspond to conjugacy
 classes in $\Gamma$ with translation length $\leq L$. Since $\Gamma$ is finitel
y generated and acts cocompactly on $\mathbb{H}^2$, the length spectrum is discr
ete: only finitely many conjugacy classes have bounded translation length. (Conc
retely, any $\gamma$ with $\ell(\gamma) \leq L$ satisfies $d(p_0, \gamma p_0) \l
eq L + 2\operatorname{diam}(F)$ for a fixed $p_0$ and fundamental domain $F$; by
 proper discontinuity, only finitely many $\gamma$ satisfy this, hence finitely
many conjugacy classes.)

**Fact 2 (Residual finiteness of surface groups).** The fundamental group $\Gamm
a = \pi_1(S)$ of a closed surface of genus $g \geq 2$ is **residually finite**:
for every non-identity $\gamma \in \Gamma$, there exists a homomorphism $\phi: \
Gamma \to F$ to a finite group $F$ with $\phi(\gamma) \neq 1$. Equivalently, the
re exists a **normal** finite-index subgroup $N \trianglelefteq \Gamma$ with $\g
amma \notin N$.

*Justification:* $\Gamma$ is a finitely generated linear group (it embeds in $PS
L(2,\mathbb{R})$). By **Mal'cev's theorem** (1940), every finitely generated lin
ear group is residually finite.

### Construction

Fix $l > 0$.

1. **Start with a base surface.** Let $S$ be any closed hyperbolic surface of ge
nus $g \geq 2$ (e.g., the regular octagon with standard side identifications giv
es genus 2). Let $\Gamma = \pi_1(S)$, a torsion-free cocompact Fuchsian group.

2. **Enumerate short conjugacy classes.** By Fact 1, there are finitely many con
jugacy classes in $\Gamma$ with translation length $\leq 2l$. Let $\gamma_1, \ga
mma_2, \ldots, \gamma_N$ be one representative from each such conjugacy class. (
If there are no such classes, skip to step 4 with $\Gamma' = \Gamma$.)

3. **Find a normal subgroup excluding all representatives.** By Fact 2 (residual
 finiteness), for each $i$, there exists a normal finite-index subgroup $N_i \tr
ianglelefteq \Gamma$ with $\gamma_i \notin N_i$. Set:

$$\Gamma' = \bigcap_{i=1}^{N} N_i.$$

   Then $\Gamma'$ is a **normal** finite-index subgroup of $\Gamma$ (finite inte
rsection of normal finite-index subgroups). Since $\Gamma' \subseteq N_i$ and $\
gamma_i \notin N_i$, we have $\gamma_i \notin \Gamma'$ for every $i$.

4. **Normality excludes entire conjugacy classes.** Since $\Gamma'$ is normal in
 $\Gamma$, it is a union of conjugacy classes: if $\gamma \notin \Gamma'$, then
$\delta\gamma\delta^{-1} \notin \Gamma'$ for all $\delta \in \Gamma$ (otherwise
$\gamma = \delta^{-1}(\delta\gamma\delta^{-1})\delta \in \Gamma'$ by normality,
contradiction). Therefore, $\Gamma'$ excludes **every** element in the conjugacy
 class of each $\gamma_i$—that is, $\Gamma'$ excludes **all** non-identity eleme
nts of $\Gamma$ with translation length $\leq 2l$.

5. **Define the surface.** Set $X = \mathbb{H}^2 / \Gamma'$. Since $\Gamma'$ is
a finite-index subgroup of the torsion-free cocompact group $\Gamma$:
   - $\Gamma'$ is torsion-free (subgroup of a torsion-free group), so $X$ is a m
anifold.
   - $\Gamma'$ is cocompact (finite-index subgroup of a cocompact group), so $X$
 is **compact**.
   - $X$ has no boundary and inherits the hyperbolic metric (curvature $-1$).

   Thus $X$ is a **closed hyperbolic surface** (a finite cover of $S$).

6. **Verify the injectivity radius.** Every non-identity element of $\Gamma'$ is
 a non-identity element of $\Gamma$ with translation length $> 2l$ (since all el
ements with translation length $\leq 2l$ were excluded in step 4). Therefore:

$$\operatorname{sys}(X) = \min_{\gamma \in \Gamma'\setminus\{\mathrm{id}\}} \ell
(\gamma) > 2l,$$

and consequently:

$$\operatorname{inj}(X) = \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{sys}(X) > l \geq l.$$

### Conclusion

For every positive real number $l$, there exists a closed hyperbolic surface $X$
 with injectivity radius $\operatorname{inj}(X) \geq l$. The surface is construc
ted as a finite cover of any fixed closed hyperbolic surface of genus $\geq 2$,
where the covering group is chosen (via residual finiteness of the surface group
) to exclude all deck transformations of translation length $\leq 2l$.
