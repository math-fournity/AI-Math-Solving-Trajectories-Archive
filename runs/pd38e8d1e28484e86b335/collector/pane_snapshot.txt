## Proof

Let $T^2 = S^1 \times S^1$. Its homology groups are
$$H_1(T^2;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2, \qquad H_2(T^2;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \math
bb{Z},$$
where $H_1$ is freely generated by the two circle factors, which we denote $x$ a
nd $y$, and $H_2$ is generated by the fundamental class $[T^2]$, represented by
the cross product $x \times y$ (the orientation class).

The induced map $f_*: H_1(T^2) \to H_1(T^2)$ is specified by $f(x+y) = mx + ny$.
 Interpreting this as the diagonal action on the generators,
$$f_*(x) = mx, \qquad f_*(y) = ny,$$
the matrix of $f_*$ with respect to the basis $\{x, y\}$ is
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} m & 0 \\ 0 & n \end{pmatrix}.$$

**Degree via $H_2$.** The degree of $f: T^2 \to T^2$ is the unique integer $\deg
(f)$ satisfying
$$f_*([T^2]) = \deg(f)\,[T^2] \quad \text{on } H_2(T^2;\mathbb{Z}).$$

By naturality of the cross product (Künneth), the induced map on $H_2$ acts on t
he generator $[T^2] = x \times y$ as
$$f_*(x \times y) = f_*(x) \times f_*(y) = (mx) \times (ny) = mn\,(x \times y) =
 mn\,[T^2].$$

Therefore
$$\deg(f) = mn.$$

**Equivalent determinant formulation.** More generally, for any continuous map $
f: T^2 \to T^2$, the degree equals the determinant of the induced map on $H_1$:
$$\deg(f) = \det\!\big(f_*: H_1(T^2) \to H_1(T^2)\big) = \det\begin{pmatrix} m &
 0 \\ 0 & n \end{pmatrix} = mn.$$

This agrees with the $H_2$ computation above, confirming the result.

$$\boxed{\deg(f) = mn}$$
