## Solution

The surface is given by $(x^2+y^2)z = 1$, i.e., $z = \dfrac{1}{x^2+y^2}$. Settin
g $r^2 = x^2+y^2$, the surface is the graph $z = 1/r^2$ over the annulus $t^2 \l
e r^2 \le 1$, i.e., $t \le r \le 1$.

**Surface area element.** With $f(x,y) = \frac{1}{x^2+y^2}$:

$$f_x = \frac{-2x}{(x^2+y^2)^2}, \quad f_y = \frac{-2y}{(x^2+y^2)^2},$$

$$f_x^2 + f_y^2 = \frac{4(x^2+y^2)}{(x^2+y^2)^4} = \frac{4}{r^6}.$$

Hence

$$dS = \sqrt{1 + \frac{4}{r^6}}\, dx\, dy = \frac{\sqrt{r^6+4}}{r^3}\, dx\, dy.$
$

Converting to polar coordinates ($dx\,dy = r\,dr\,d\theta$):

$$A_t = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_t^1 \frac{\sqrt{r^6+4}}{r^3}\cdot r\, dr\, d\theta =
 2\pi\int_t^1 \frac{\sqrt{r^6+4}}{r^2}\, dr.$$

**Behavior as $t \to 0^+$.** Near $r = 0$, the integrand satisfies

$$\frac{\sqrt{r^6+4}}{r^2} \ge \frac{\sqrt{4}}{r^2} = \frac{2}{r^2}.$$

Since $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \frac{2}{r^2}\, dr = 2\left[-\frac{1}{r}\right]_0^1
 = +\infty$ diverges, by comparison the improper integral $\displaystyle\int_0^1
 \frac{\sqrt{r^6+4}}{r^2}\, dr$ diverges to $+\infty$.

Therefore

$$\lim_{t \to 0^+} A_t = 2\pi \int_0^1 \frac{\sqrt{r^6+4}}{r^2}\, dr = +\infty.$
$

**Conclusion.** The limit $\lim_{t\to 0^+} A_t$ does **not** exist as a finite r
eal number; the surface area diverges to $+\infty$.
