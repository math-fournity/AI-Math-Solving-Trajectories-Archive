## Proof

Let $k = n/m$ (an integer since $m \mid n$), and set $c = b^m \in K$.

**Step 1: $[F(b):F] = n$.**
Since $x^n - a$ is irreducible over $F$ and $b$ is a root, $x^n - a$ is the mini
mal polynomial of $b$ over $F$. Hence
$$[F(b):F] = n.$$

**Step 2: Upper bound on $[F(c):F]$.**
Since $b^n = a$ and $n = mk$, we have
$$c^k = (b^m)^k = b^{mk} = b^n = a.$$
Thus $c$ is a root of $x^k - a \in F[x]$, so the minimal polynomial of $c$ over
$F$ divides $x^k - a$. Therefore
$$[F(c):F] \leq k = \frac{n}{m}.$$

**Step 3: Lower bound on $[F(c):F]$.**
We have the tower $F \subseteq F(c) \subseteq F(b)$, so
$$[F(b):F] = [F(b):F(c)]\cdot [F(c):F].$$
Over $F(c)$, the element $b$ satisfies $b^m = c$, i.e. $b$ is a root of $x^m - c
 \in F(c)[x]$. Hence the minimal polynomial of $b$ over $F(c)$ divides $x^m - c$
, giving
$$[F(b):F(c)] \leq m.$$
Combining with $[F(b):F] = n$:
$$[F(c):F] = \frac{n}{[F(b):F(c)]} \geq \frac{n}{m} = k.$$

**Step 4: Conclusion.**
From Steps 2 and 3,
$$\frac{n}{m} \leq [F(c):F] \leq \frac{n}{m},$$
so
$$[F(c):F] = \frac{n}{m}.$$

Since $[F(c):F]$ equals the degree of the minimal polynomial of $c = b^m$ over $
F$, the degree of the minimal polynomial of $b^m$ over $F$ is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{n}{m}}.$$
