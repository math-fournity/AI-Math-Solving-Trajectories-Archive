**Answer: False**

**Proof.**

I construct an explicit counterexample in an incomplete normed space.

**The space.** Let $X = c_{00}$, the space of finitely supported real sequences,
 equipped with the $\ell^2$ norm $\|x\|_2 = \left(\sum_{k=1}^\infty |x_k|^2\righ
t)^{1/2}$. This is a normed space. It is **not complete**: the partial sums $s_N
 = \sum_{k=1}^N \frac{1}{k} e_k$ form a Cauchy sequence in $c_{00}$, but converg
e to $(1/k)_{k=1}^\infty \in \ell^2 \setminus c_{00}$, which is not in $X$.

**The series.** Define:
$$x_{2k-1} = \frac{1}{k}\, e_k, \qquad x_{2k} = -\frac{1}{k}\, e_k, \qquad k = 1
, 2, 3, \ldots$$
where $e_k$ is the $k$-th standard basis vector. Each $x_n \in c_{00}$.

**Not absolutely convergent.** We have $\|x_{2k-1}\|_2 = \|x_{2k}\|_2 = \frac{1}
{k}$, so
$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \|x_n\|_2 = 2\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{1}{k} = \infty.$$

**Converges to 0 in $X$.** The partial sums satisfy:
$$s_{2K} = \sum_{n=1}^{2K} x_n = \sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\tfrac{1}{k}e_k - \tfrac{1}
{k}e_k\right) = 0,$$
$$s_{2K-1} = s_{2K-2} + x_{2K-1} = \tfrac{1}{K}\, e_K, \qquad \|s_{2K-1}\|_2 = \
tfrac{1}{K} \to 0.$$
Both subsequences converge to $0 \in c_{00}$, so $\sum x_n = 0$ in $X$.

**Unconditionally convergent.** I verify the unconditional Cauchy criterion: for
 every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists $N_0$ such that for every finite $F \subs
et \{N_0, N_0+1, \ldots\}$, $\|\sum_{n \in F} x_n\|_2 < \varepsilon$.

For any finite $F$, the $k$-th coordinate of $\sum_{n \in F} x_n$ is:
- $\frac{1}{k}$ if $2k{-}1 \in F$ and $2k \notin F$,
- $-\frac{1}{k}$ if $2k \in F$ and $2k{-}1 \notin F$,
- $0$ otherwise.

If $F \subset \{N_0, N_0{+}1, \ldots\}$, then a nonzero $k$-th coordinate requir
es $2k{-}1 \geq N_0$ or $2k \geq N_0$, hence $k \geq \lceil N_0/2 \rceil$. There
fore:

$$\left\|\sum_{n \in F} x_n\right\|_2^2 = \sum_{k \geq \lceil N_0/2\rceil} \frac
{c_k^2}{k^2} \leq \sum_{k \geq \lceil N_0/2\rceil} \frac{1}{k^2},$$

where $c_k \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$. Since $\sum_{k=N}^\infty \frac{1}{k^2} \to 0$ as $
N \to \infty$, we can choose $N_0$ so that this is less than $\varepsilon^2$. Th
is confirms the unconditional Cauchy criterion, and since the net of finite part
ial sums converges to $0$ (as shown above), the series is **unconditionally conv
ergent** in $X$.

**Conclusion.** In the incomplete normed space $X = (c_{00}, \|\cdot\|_2)$, the
series $\sum x_n$ is unconditionally convergent but not absolutely convergent. T
herefore, it is **not true** that every unconditionally convergent series in an
incomplete normed space is absolutely convergent.
