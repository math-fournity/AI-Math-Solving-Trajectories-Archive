**Answer: Yes, M is necessarily orientable.**

**Proof.**

Every manifold $M$ admits an **orientation double cover** $p:\widetilde{M}\to M$
, a two-sheeted covering space. The key facts are:

1. $\widetilde{M}$ is connected if and only if $M$ is non-orientable. (If $M$ is
 orientable, $\widetilde{M}$ is two disjoint copies of $M$.)

2. This cover is classified by the **orientation character** (first Stiefel–Whit
ney class)
$$w_1:\pi_1(M)\longrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},$$
defined as follows: a loop $\gamma$ in $M$ lifts to a path in $\widetilde{M}$; t
he lift is closed iff $w_1(\gamma)=0$, and connects the two sheets iff $w_1(\gam
ma)=1$.

3. $M$ is orientable if and only if $w_1$ is the **trivial** homomorphism (equiv
alently, $\widetilde{M}$ is disconnected).

Now suppose $\pi_1(M)=0$. Then the only homomorphism from $\pi_1(M)$ to $\mathbb
{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is the trivial one. In particular $w_1$ must be trivial, so $M$
 is orientable.

(Equivalently, via cohomology: $w_1(M)\in H^1(M;\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})$. By Hur
ewicz, $\pi_1(M)=0\Rightarrow H_1(M;\mathbb{Z})=0$. Since $H_0(M;\mathbb{Z})\con
g\mathbb{Z}$ is free, the universal coefficient theorem gives $H^1(M;\mathbb{Z}/
2\mathbb{Z})\cong\operatorname{Hom}(H_1(M;\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})=0$
. Hence $w_1=0$ and $M$ is orientable.)

Therefore, a compact $n$-manifold with trivial fundamental group is necessarily
orientable.
