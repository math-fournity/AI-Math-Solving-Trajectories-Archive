## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle I = \int_C y^2\,dx + z^2\,dy + x^2\,dz$ where $C$ is
the intersection of the sphere $x^2+y^2+z^2=a^2$ and the paraboloid $x^2+y^2=az$
 (with $a>0$, $z\ge 0$), oriented anticlockwise when viewed from the origin.

### Step 1. Compute the curl

With $\vec{F} = (P,Q,R) = (y^2,\, z^2,\, x^2)$:

$$\nabla\times\vec{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ \partial_
x & \partial_y & \partial_z \\ y^2 & z^2 & x^2 \end{vmatrix} = \bigl(\partial_y(
x^2)-\partial_z(z^2),\;\; \partial_z(y^2)-\partial_x(x^2),\;\; \partial_x(z^2)-\
partial_y(y^2)\bigr) = (-2z,\,-2x,\,-2y).$$

### Step 2. Choose the surface and parameterize

Take $S$ to be the cap of the **paraboloid** $z = \dfrac{x^2+y^2}{a}$ bounded by
 $C$. Parameterize with polar coordinates $(r,\theta)$:

$$\vec{r}(r,\theta) = \bigl(r\cos\theta,\; r\sin\theta,\; r^2/a\bigr), \qquad 0\
le r\le r_0,\; 0\le\theta\le 2\pi.$$

**Finding $r_0$ (the boundary):** On $C$, both $x^2+y^2+z^2=a^2$ and $x^2+y^2=az
$ hold. Substituting $r^2 = az$ and $z = r^2/a$ into the sphere equation:

$$az + z^2 = a^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; z^2 + az - a^2 = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; z =
\frac{a(\sqrt{5}-1)}{2},$$

so $r_0^2 = az = \dfrac{a^2(\sqrt{5}-1)}{2}$. (The exact value of $r_0$ won't ma
tter.)

### Step 3. Compute the normal vector

$$\vec{r}_r = \bigl(\cos\theta,\,\sin\theta,\,2r/a\bigr),\qquad \vec{r}_\theta =
 \bigl(-r\sin\theta,\,r\cos\theta,\,0\bigr).$$

$$\vec{r}_r\times\vec{r}_\theta = \bigl(-2r^2\cos\theta/a,\;\; -2r^2\sin\theta/a
,\;\; r\bigr).$$

This has positive $z$-component ($r>0$), i.e. it points **away** from the origin
 (upward). Since $C$ is oriented **anticlockwise viewed from the origin** (which
 lies below the surface), the right-hand rule requires the normal to point **tow
ard** the origin (downward). Hence we take the opposite orientation:

$$\vec{n}\,dS = \vec{r}_\theta\times\vec{r}_r\,dr\,d\theta = \bigl(2r^2\cos\thet
a/a,\;\; 2r^2\sin\theta/a,\;\; -r\bigr)\,dr\,d\theta.$$

### Step 4. Evaluate the surface integral

On $S$: $z = r^2/a$, $x = r\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\theta$, so

$$\nabla\times\vec{F} = \bigl(-2r^2/a,\;\; -2r\cos\theta,\;\; -2r\sin\theta\bigr
).$$

The dot product:

$$(\nabla\times\vec{F})\cdot(\vec{r}_\theta\times\vec{r}_r) = \left(-\frac{2r^2}
{a}\right)\!\left(\frac{2r^2\cos\theta}{a}\right) + (-2r\cos\theta)\!\left(\frac
{2r^2\sin\theta}{a}\right) + (-2r\sin\theta)(-r)$$

$$= -\frac{4r^4\cos\theta}{a^2} \;-\; \frac{4r^3\cos\theta\sin\theta}{a} \;+\; 2
r^2\sin\theta.$$

### Step 5. Integrate

$$I = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{r_0}\left[-\frac{4r^4\cos\theta}{a^2} - \frac{4r^
3\cos\theta\sin\theta}{a} + 2r^2\sin\theta\right]dr\,d\theta.$$

Each term contains a trigonometric factor in $\theta$ alone:
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\theta\,d\theta = 0$,
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\theta\sin\theta\,d\theta = \tfrac{1}{2}\int_0^
{2\pi}\sin 2\theta\,d\theta = 0$,
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\sin\theta\,d\theta = 0$.

Therefore every term vanishes and

$$I = 0.$$

### Verification (symmetry argument)

The surface $S$ (paraboloid cap) is rotationally symmetric about the $z$-axis. T
he curl $(-2z,-2x,-2y)$ has its horizontal components $(-2x,-2y)$ odd under the
reflection $(x,y)\mapsto(-x,-y)$, while the normal's horizontal components are a
lso odd — their dot product pairs with the $z$-component contribution $(-2y)\,n_
z$ (odd in $y$). All contributions cancel by rotational/reflection symmetry, con
firming $I=0$. A parallel computation using the **spherical cap** as the surface
 yields the same vanishing result.
