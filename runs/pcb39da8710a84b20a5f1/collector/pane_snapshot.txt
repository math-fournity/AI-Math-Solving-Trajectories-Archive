## Proof

Let $(M,\omega,G,\mu)$ be a Hamiltonian $G$-space with $G$ a compact Lie group.
Thus $\mu:M\to\mathfrak g^*$ is a moment map that is **equivariant** with respec
t to the coadjoint action:
$$\mu(g\cdot x)=\operatorname{Ad}^*_g\,\mu(x),\qquad g\in G,\ x\in M.$$

We seek necessary and sufficient conditions for the level set $\mu^{-1}(\xi)$ to
 be **$G$-invariant** (preserved setwise by the $G$-action), and then spell out
the consequences for symplectic reduction at $\xi$.

---

### 1. Derivation of the necessary and sufficient condition

Fix $x\in\mu^{-1}(\xi)$, so $\mu(x)=\xi$. For $g\in G$,
$$g\cdot x\in\mu^{-1}(\xi)\iff \mu(g\cdot x)=\xi\iff \operatorname{Ad}^*_g\,\xi=
\xi,$$
using equivariance in the second equivalence. Hence
$$g\cdot\mu^{-1}(\xi)\subseteq\mu^{-1}(\xi)\iff g\in G_\xi:=\{h\in G:\operatorna
me{Ad}^*_h\xi=\xi\},$$
the **coadjoint stabilizer** of $\xi$. Since $G_\xi$ is a subgroup, $g\cdot\mu^{
-1}(\xi)\subseteq\mu^{-1}(\xi)$ for all $g$ is equivalent to $G\subseteq G_\xi$,
 i.e. $G_\xi=G$.

Therefore:

> **Necessary and sufficient condition.** $\mu^{-1}(\xi)$ is $G$-invariant if an
d only if $\xi$ is a **fixed point of the coadjoint representation**:
> $$\operatorname{Ad}^*_g\xi=\xi\quad\forall\,g\in G,\qquad\text{equivalently}\q
quad \xi\in(\mathfrak g^*)^G.$$

*Necessity:* If $\mu^{-1}(\xi)$ is $G$-invariant, pick any $x\in\mu^{-1}(\xi)$ (
assuming the level is nonempty; if empty, invariance is vacuous and the conditio
n is imposed as the structural requirement). Then $g\cdot x\in\mu^{-1}(\xi)$, so
 $\operatorname{Ad}^*_g\xi=\mu(g\cdot x)=\xi$.

*Sufficiency:* If $\operatorname{Ad}^*_g\xi=\xi$ for all $g$, then for $x\in\mu^
{-1}(\xi)$, $\mu(g\cdot x)=\operatorname{Ad}^*_g\xi=\xi$, so $g\cdot x\in\mu^{-1
}(\xi)$.

---

### 2. Characterization of $(\mathfrak g^*)^G$

Differentiating $\operatorname{Ad}^*_{\exp(tX)}\xi=\xi$ at $t=0$ gives the infin
itesimal condition
$$\operatorname{ad}^*_X\xi=0\quad\forall X\in\mathfrak g\iff \xi([X,Y])=0\quad\f
orall X,Y\in\mathfrak g,$$
since $\langle\operatorname{ad}^*_X\xi,Y\rangle=\langle\xi,[Y,X]\rangle=-\langle
\xi,[X,Y]\rangle$. Thus
$$(\mathfrak g^*)^G=\bigl[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g\bigr]^\circ=\bigl(\mathfrak g/
[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]\bigr)^*,$$
the annihilator of the commutator ideal, i.e. the dual of the abelianization of
$\mathfrak g$. For compact (hence reductive) $G$, $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak z(\math
frak g)\oplus[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]$, so $(\mathfrak g^*)^G\cong\mathfrak z(\
mathfrak g)^*$: **$\xi$ must be supported only on the center of $\mathfrak g$**.
 In particular $\xi=0$ always satisfies the condition.

---

### 3. Implications for symplectic reduction at $\xi$

The Marsden–Weinstein–Meyer reduction at a regular value $\xi$ is defined as
$$M_\xi=\mu^{-1}(\xi)/G_\xi,$$
where $G_\xi$ is precisely the subgroup that preserves $\mu^{-1}(\xi)$. The anal
ysis above shows that the **full** group $G$ preserves the level set **iff $G_\x
i=G$**, i.e. iff $\xi\in(\mathfrak g^*)^G$. The consequences are:

**(a) Reduction by the full group.** When $\xi\in(\mathfrak g^*)^G$, the reduced
 space is
$$M_\xi=\mu^{-1}(\xi)/G,$$
a quotient by the **entire** compact group, not by a proper stabilizer subgroup.
 Since $G$ is compact, the action on $\mu^{-1}(\xi)$ is automatically **proper**
; if $G$ acts **freely** on $\mu^{-1}(\xi)$, then $M_\xi$ is a smooth manifold a
nd carries the unique reduced symplectic form $\omega_\xi$ satisfying $\iota^*\o
mega=\pi^*\omega_\xi$ ($\iota:\mu^{-1}(\xi)\hookrightarrow M$, $\pi:\mu^{-1}(\xi
)\to M_\xi$). Without freeness one obtains an orbifold (or a singular stratified
 symplectic space via the Sjamaar–Lerman theory).

**(b) Dimension formula.** For regular $\xi$ and free $G$-action on $\mu^{-1}(\x
i)$,
$$\dim M_\xi=\dim M-\dim G-\dim G_\xi.$$
When $\xi\in(\mathfrak g^*)^G$ so $G_\xi=G$, this specializes to
$$\boxed{\dim M_\xi=\dim M-2\dim G.}$$
This is the maximal reduction in dimension achievable at a single level: the ful
l group contributes its dimension twice (once from the constraint $\mu=\xi$, onc
e from the quotient). For non-fixed $\xi$ one has $\dim G_\xi<\dim G$ and the re
duction is "less effective": $\dim M_\xi=\dim M-\dim G-\dim G_\xi>\dim M-2\dim G
$.

**(c) The level $\xi=0$ is always admissible.** Since $0\in(\mathfrak g^*)^G$, r
eduction at the zero level is **always** performed by the full group $G$; this i
s the canonical "reduction at zero" and is the case used in most applications (e
.g. constructing coadjoint orbits, toric varieties, moduli spaces).

**(d) Contrast: non-fixed $\xi$.** If $\xi\notin(\mathfrak g^*)^G$, then $G_\xi\
subsetneq G$ and $\mu^{-1}(\xi)$ is **not** $G$-invariant: generic elements of $
G$ move the level set to $\mu^{-1}(\operatorname{Ad}^*_g\xi)$, a different coadj
oint orbit. Reduction must then be performed with $G_\xi$, and the resulting $M_
\xi=\mu^{-1}(\xi)/G_\xi$ is a symplectic manifold of the larger dimension $\dim
M-\dim G-\dim G_\xi$. The family $\{M_{\xi'}\}_{\xi'\in\mathcal O_\xi}$ over the
 coadjoint orbit $\mathcal O_\xi$ is related by the "shifting trick" (guillemin–
sternberg): $M_\xi\cong(\mu^{-1}(\mathcal O_\xi)\times\mathcal O_\xi^-)/G$, whic
h uses the full $G$ but at the cost of an auxiliary factor.

**(e) Equivariance is essential.** The entire argument hinges on $\mu$ being $\o
peratorname{Ad}^*$-equivariant. If $\mu$ were only infinitesimally equivariant (
a "non-equivariant" moment map with cocycle $\sigma:\mathfrak g\wedge\mathfrak g
\to\mathbb R$), the condition would read $\operatorname{Ad}^*_g\xi+\sigma(g)=\xi
$, modifying the fixed-point condition by the cocycle; for compact $G$ one can a
lways average to obtain an equivariant moment map, so the equivariant formulatio
n is the natural one.

### Summary

The level set $\mu^{-1}(\xi)$ is preserved by the full $G$-action **if and only
if $\xi$ is a fixed point of the coadjoint representation**, $\xi\in(\mathfrak g
^*)^G=[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]^\circ\cong\mathfrak z(\mathfrak g)^*$, i.e. $\xi
$ vanishes on all commutators (equivalently $\operatorname{Ad}^*_g\xi=\xi$ for a
ll $g$). In that case $G_\xi=G$ and symplectic reduction is performed by the **e
ntire** group, yielding $M_\xi=\mu^{-1}(\xi)/G$ of dimension $\dim M-2\dim G$ (f
or regular $\xi$ and free action). The zero level $\xi=0$ always satisfies the c
ondition, giving the canonical full-group reduction. For non-fixed $\xi$ the lev
el is only $G_\xi$-invariant and reduction must use the proper stabilizer $G_\xi
$, producing a space of larger dimension; the coadjoint-orbit shifting trick rec
overs a full-$G$ description at the cost of an auxiliary orbit factor.
