## Proof

### Setup and indexing convention

The 3-qubit computational basis is ordered as
$$|000\rangle, |001\rangle, |010\rangle, |011\rangle, |100\rangle, |101\rangle,
|110\rangle, |111\rangle.$$

We decompose each basis label $|ijk\rangle$ into a **two-qubit part** $|ij\rangl
e$ (qubits 1 and 2) and a **one-qubit part** $|k\rangle$ (qubit 3). Introduce th
e combined index
$$p = 2i + j \in \{0,1,2,3\}, \qquad k \in \{0,1\}.$$
With $k$ running fastest, the 8-dimensional state vector is indexed by the pair
$(p,k)$, and the original coefficients satisfy
$$c_{ijk} = c_{p,k} = a_i\, b_{jk}.$$

### What the transformation does

The unitary $U = (u_{nm})_{n,m=0}^{3}$ acts on the first two qubits (the $|ij\ra
ngle$ register), and **nothing** acts on the third qubit. Acting on the third qu
bit with "nothing" is the same as acting with the $2\times 2$ identity $I_2$.

Therefore the overall operator on the full 8-dimensional Hilbert space is the te
nsor (Kronecker) product
$$\boxed{M \;=\; U \otimes I_2}$$
where $U$ acts on the $(i,j)$ register and $I_2$ acts on the $k$ register.

### Deriving the action on coefficients

Let $c'_{p,k}$ denote the coefficients after the transformation. Since $U$ mixes
 only the $p$ index and $I_2$ leaves $k$ fixed,
$$c'_{p,k} \;=\; \sum_{q=0}^{3} u_{pq}\, c_{q,k} \;=\; \sum_{q=0}^{3} u_{pq}\, c
_{q,k}\,\delta_{k,\,k}.$$
In the original $(i,j,k)$ notation this reads
$$c'_{ijk} \;=\; \sum_{i',j'} u_{(ij),(i'j')}\, c_{i'j'k} \;=\; \sum_{i',j'} u_{
(ij),(i'j')}\, a_{i'}\, b_{j'k},$$
which is precisely the matrix–vector product $M\mathbf{c}$ with $M = U\otimes I_
2$.

### Explicit 8×8 form

The Kronecker product $U\otimes I_2$ is the $4\times 4$ block matrix whose $(n,m
)$ block is the $2\times 2$ scalar matrix $u_{nm} I_2$:

$$U\otimes I_2 \;=\; \begin{pmatrix}
u_{00}I_2 & u_{01}I_2 & u_{02}I_2 & u_{03}I_2 \\
u_{10}I_2 & u_{11}I_2 & u_{12}I_2 & u_{13}I_2 \\
u_{20}I_2 & u_{21}I_2 & u_{22}I_2 & u_{23}I_2 \\
u_{30}I_2 & u_{31}I_2 & u_{32}I_2 & u_{33}I_2
\end{pmatrix}.$$

Expanding each $u_{nm}I_2 = \begin{pmatrix} u_{nm} & 0 \\ 0 & u_{nm}\end{pmatrix
}$ gives the full $8\times 8$ matrix (rows/columns in the basis order above):

$$M \;=\; \begin{pmatrix}
u_{00} & 0 & u_{01} & 0 & u_{02} & 0 & u_{03} & 0 \\
0 & u_{00} & 0 & u_{01} & 0 & u_{02} & 0 & u_{03} \\
u_{10} & 0 & u_{11} & 0 & u_{12} & 0 & u_{13} & 0 \\
0 & u_{10} & 0 & u_{11} & 0 & u_{12} & 0 & u_{13} \\
u_{20} & 0 & u_{21} & 0 & u_{22} & 0 & u_{23} & 0 \\
0 & u_{20} & 0 & u_{21} & 0 & u_{22} & 0 & u_{23} \\
u_{30} & 0 & u_{31} & 0 & u_{32} & 0 & u_{33} & 0 \\
0 & u_{30} & 0 & u_{31} & 0 & u_{32} & 0 & u_{33}
\end{pmatrix}.$$

### Verification of unitarity

Since $U$ is unitary ($U^\dagger U = I_4$) and $I_2$ is unitary,
$$(U\otimes I_2)^\dagger (U\otimes I_2) \;=\; (U^\dagger\otimes I_2)(U\otimes I_
2) \;=\; (U^\dagger U)\otimes I_2 \;=\; I_4\otimes I_2 \;=\; I_8,$$
so $M$ is genuinely an $8\times 8$ unitary, as required for a valid quantum oper
ation on the 3-qubit system.

### Structural interpretation

The block structure reflects the partial separability: because the third qubit i
s untouched, every $2\times 2$ block on the block-diagonal-of-blocks is a scalar
 multiple of $I_2$. Equivalently, $M$ acts identically on the two subspaces span
ned by $\{|ij0\rangle\}$ and $\{|ij1\rangle\}$ — it never couples $k=0$ with $k=
1$. The entanglement between qubits 2 and 3 (encoded in the $b_{jk}$) is preserv
ed in structure and merely "rotated" within the first-two-qubit register by $U$.

---

**Conclusion.** The overall $8\times 8$ matrix representing the action on the en
tire 3-qubit system is the Kronecker product
$$M \;=\; U \otimes I_2,$$
i.e. the $4\times 4$ array of $2\times 2$ blocks $u_{nm}I_2$ shown explicitly ab
ove. The third qubit contributes the identity factor $I_2$, so $U$ is "lifted" t
o the full space by replacing each entry $u_{nm}$ with the $2\times 2$ scalar ma
trix $u_{nm}I_2$.
