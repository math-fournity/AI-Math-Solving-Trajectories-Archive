## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. If $U$ is an ultrafilter on $X$ and $\{x(i)\} \in U$, then $U =
U(x(i))$, the principal ultrafilter at $x(i)$.

**Proof.**

Recall that $U(x(i)) = \{F \subseteq X \mid x(i) \in F\}$ is the principal ultra
filter concentrated at the point $x(i)$. We prove the two inclusions.

**($\subseteq$ direction: $U(x(i)) \subseteq U$.)**

Since $\{x(i)\} \in U$ and a filter is upward closed under supersets, every set
$F$ with $\{x(i)\} \subseteq F$ belongs to $U$. But $\{x(i)\} \subseteq F$ is ex
actly the condition $x(i) \in F$, i.e. $F \in U(x(i))$. Hence $U(x(i)) \subseteq
 U$.

**($\supseteq$ direction: $U \subseteq U(x(i))$.)**

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists $F \in U$ with $F \notin U(x(i))$,
 i.e. $x(i) \notin F$. Since $U$ is a filter, it is closed under finite intersec
tions, so
$$F \cap \{x(i)\} \in U.$$
But $x(i) \notin F$ means $F \cap \{x(i)\} = \emptyset$. This forces $\emptyset
\in U$, contradicting the defining property of a (proper) filter that $\emptyset
 \notin U$.

Therefore no such $F$ exists: every $F \in U$ satisfies $x(i) \in F$, giving $U
\subseteq U(x(i))$.

**Conclusion.** Combining both inclusions, $U = U(x(i))$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** Note that the ultrafilter property (maximality) was not actually nee
ded — the argument uses only that $U$ is a *proper filter* containing the single
ton $\{x(i)\}$. The ultrafilter hypothesis is automatic in the sense that any pr
oper filter containing a singleton is already principal (and hence already an ul
trafilter). Conversely, an ultrafilter that contains no singleton is a *free* (n
on-principal) ultrafilter, which exists only on infinite sets (assuming a weak f
orm of choice).
