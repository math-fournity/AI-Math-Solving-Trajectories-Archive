## Solution

We evaluate

$$I = \int_0^\infty \frac{\ln x}{1+x^2}\,dx.$$

### Step 1. Convergence

The integral is improper at both endpoints, so we first verify convergence.

- **Near $x = 0$:** $\dfrac{\ln x}{1+x^2} \sim \ln x$ as $x \to 0^+$, and $\disp
laystyle\int_0^1 \ln x\,dx = \bigl[x\ln x - x\bigr]_0^1 = -1$ converges.
- **Near $x = \infty$:** $\dfrac{\ln x}{1+x^2} \sim \dfrac{\ln x}{x^2}$ as $x \t
o \infty$, and $\displaystyle\int_1^\infty \frac{\ln x}{x^2}\,dx$ converges (e.g
. by comparison, since $\ln x = o(x^{1/2})$ gives $\frac{\ln x}{x^2} = o(x^{-3/2
})$, which is integrable).

Hence $I$ converges, and we may manipulate it via substitutions that preserve co
nvergence.

### Step 2. The involution $x \mapsto 1/x$

Apply the substitution $x = \dfrac{1}{t}$, so $dx = -\dfrac{1}{t^2}\,dt$. The li
mits swap: $x \to 0^+$ corresponds to $t \to +\infty$, and $x \to +\infty$ corre
sponds to $t \to 0^+$.

$$
I = \int_{\infty}^{0} \frac{\ln(1/t)}{1 + 1/t^2}\cdot\left(-\frac{1}{t^2}\right)
dt
  = \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{-\ln t}{\,\dfrac{t^2+1}{t^2}\,}\cdot\frac{1}{t^2}\,d
t
  = \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{-\ln t}{1+t^2}\,dt
  = -I.
$$

### Step 3. Conclude

We have shown $I = -I$, i.e. $2I = 0$, so

$$I = 0.$$

### Verification (optional sanity check)

Splitting at $x=1$ and substituting $x = 1/u$ on $(0,1)$:

$$\int_0^1 \frac{\ln x}{1+x^2}\,dx = \int_1^\infty \frac{-\ln u}{1+u^2}\,du,$$

so the two halves cancel exactly, confirming $I = 0$.

---

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_0^\infty \frac{\ln x}{1+x^2}\,dx = 0}$$
