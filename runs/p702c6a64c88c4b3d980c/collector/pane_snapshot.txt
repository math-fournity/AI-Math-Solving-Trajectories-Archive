Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum $L$ such that the intersecti
on area of $I = \{(x,y) \mid x > (y^4/9 + 2015)^{1/4}\}$ with the disk $x^2+y^2
\le r^2$ is less than $Lr^2$ for all $r>0$.

## Proof

**Setup and scaling.** Define the intersection area:
$$A(r) = \text{Area}\bigl(\{(x,y): x^2+y^2 \le r^2,\; x > (y^4/9+2015)^{1/4}\}\b
igr).$$

We seek $L = \sup_{r>0} \frac{A(r)}{r^2}$, the smallest constant dominating $A(r
)/r^2$ for all $r>0$.

**Key substitution.** Set $x = rX,\; y = rY$, so $dx\,dy = r^2\,dX\,dY$ and the
disk becomes $X^2+Y^2 \le 1$. The inequality $x > (y^4/9+2015)^{1/4}$ becomes:
$$rX > (r^4 Y^4/9 + 2015)^{1/4}.$$
Since the right side is positive, $X > 0$ is forced. Raising both sides to the 4
th power:
$$r^4 X^4 > \frac{r^4 Y^4}{9} + 2015 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad X^4 - \frac{Y
^4}{9} > \frac{2015}{r^4}.$$

Setting $c = 2015/r^4 > 0$, we obtain:
$$\frac{A(r)}{r^2} = g(c), \qquad g(c) := \text{Area}\bigl(\{(X,Y): X^2+Y^2\le 1
,\; X>0,\; X^4 - Y^4/9 > c\}\bigr).$$

**Monotonicity.** As $c$ increases, the constraint $X^4 - Y^4/9 > c$ becomes str
icter, so $g(c)$ is **strictly decreasing**. Since $c = 2015/r^4$ is strictly de
creasing in $r$, the composition $g(2015/r^4) = A(r)/r^2$ is **strictly increasi
ng** in $r$.

**Limit as $r \to \infty$ (i.e., $c \to 0^+$).** The region approaches:
$$\{X^2+Y^2 \le 1,\; X > 0,\; X^4 > Y^4/9\} = \{X^2+Y^2 \le 1,\; X > |Y|/\sqrt{3
}\}.$$

This is the sector of the unit disk where $|Y| < \sqrt{3}\,X$ and $X > 0$. In po
lar coordinates ($X = \rho\cos\theta,\, Y=\rho\sin\theta$), the condition is $|\
tan\theta| < \sqrt{3}$, i.e., $|\theta| < \pi/3$. The sector spans angles $(-\pi
/3, \pi/3)$, with total angle $2\pi/3$, giving area:
$$g(0^+) = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 1^2 \cdot \tfrac{2\pi}{3} = \frac{\pi}{3}.$$

**Limit as $r \to 0^+$ (i.e., $c \to \infty$).** The constraint $X^4 - Y^4/9 > c
$ with $X^2+Y^2 \le 1$ becomes impossible for large $c$ (since $X^4 \le 1$ on th
e disk), so $g(c) \to 0$.

**Conclusion.** Since $A(r)/r^2 = g(2015/r^4)$ is strictly increasing in $r$ wit
h:
$$\lim_{r \to 0^+} \frac{A(r)}{r^2} = 0, \qquad \lim_{r\to\infty} \frac{A(r)}{r^
2} = \frac{\pi}{3},$$
we have $A(r)/r^2 < \pi/3$ for every $r > 0$ (strict inequality since $c = 2015/
r^4 > 0$ always, so the region is strictly smaller than the limiting sector), ye
t $A(r)/r^2$ can be made arbitrarily close to $\pi/3$ by taking $r$ sufficiently
 large.

Therefore:
$$\sup_{r>0} \frac{A(r)}{r^2} = \frac{\pi}{3},$$
and this supremum is never attained. The minimum $L$ satisfying $A(r) < Lr^2$ fo
r all $r > 0$ is:

$$\boxed{L = \dfrac{\pi}{3}}.$$
