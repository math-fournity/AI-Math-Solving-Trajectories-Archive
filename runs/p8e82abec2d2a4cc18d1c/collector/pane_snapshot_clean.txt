## Proof

**Yes, $f$ is necessarily an isomorphism.**

Let $K = \ker f$. Since $f$ is surjective, $G_2 \cong G_1/K$. I will show $K = \
{1\}$.

**Step 1: A standard fact on the lower central series of a quotient.**

For any group $G$ and normal subgroup $K \trianglelefteq G$,
$$\gamma_k(G/K) = \gamma_k(G)\cdot K\,/\,K \quad\text{for all } k \geq 1.$$

*Proof by induction on $k$.* The base case $k=1$ is immediate: $\gamma_1(G/K)=G/
K = G\cdot K/K = \gamma_1(G)\cdot K/K$.

For the inductive step, assume $\gamma_k(G/K)=\gamma_k(G)\cdot K/K$. Then
$$\gamma_{k+1}(G/K)=\bigl[\gamma_k(G/K),\, G/K\bigr]=\bigl[\gamma_k(G)\cdot K/K,
\; G/K\bigr].$$

For normal subgroups $A,B\supseteq K$, one has $[A/K, B/K]=[A,B]\cdot K/K$. Taki
ng $A=\gamma_k(G)\cdot K$ and $B=G$ (both contain $K$):
$$\bigl[\gamma_k(G)\cdot K/K,\; G/K\bigr]=\bigl[\gamma_k(G)\cdot K,\; G\bigr]\cd
ot K/K.$$

Since $K\trianglelefteq G$, we have $,G]\subseteq K$, so
$$\bigl[\gamma_k(G)\cdot K,\; G\bigr]=[\gamma_k(G),G]\cdot,G]\subseteq \gamma_
{k+1}(G)\cdot K,$$
and conversely $\gamma_{k+1}(G)=[\gamma_k(G),G]\subseteq[\gamma_k(G)\cdot K, G]$
. Hence
$$\bigl[\gamma_k(G)\cdot K,\; G\bigr]\cdot K = \gamma_{k+1}(G)\cdot K,$$
giving $\gamma_{k+1}(G/K)=\gamma_{k+1}(G)\cdot K/K$. ∎

**Step 2: Translating the hypothesis.**

Because $f$ is surjective, $f(\gamma_k(G_1))=\gamma_k(G_2)$. The induced map
$$\bar f_k : G_1/\gamma_k(G_1) \longrightarrow G_2/\gamma_k(G_2),\qquad g\,\gamm
a_k(G_1)\mapsto f(g)\,\gamma_k(G_2)$$
is well-defined and surjective. Its kernel is
$$\ker \bar f_k = f^{-1}\bigl(\gamma_k(G_2)\bigr)\,/\,\gamma_k(G_1)=\bigl(\gamma
_k(G_1)\cdot K\bigr)\,/\,\gamma_k(G_1),$$
using $f^{-1}(\gamma_k(G_2))=\gamma_k(G_1)\cdot K$ (which follows from Step 1 ap
plied to $G_1/K\cong G_2$).

The hypothesis that $\bar f_k$ is an isomorphism for every $k\geq 1$ means $\ker
\bar f_k$ is trivial for every $k$, i.e.,
$$\gamma_k(G_1)\cdot K = \gamma_k(G_1)\quad\text{for all }k\geq 1,$$
equivalently,
$$K\subseteq \gamma_k(G_1)\quad\text{for all }k\geq 1.$$

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

Taking the intersection over all $k$:
$$K \subseteq \bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty} \gamma_k(G_1).$$

By hypothesis $\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma_k(G_1)=\{1\}$. Therefore $K=\{1\}$,
so $f$ is injective. Being both injective and surjective, $f$ is an isomorphism.
 $\blacksquare$
