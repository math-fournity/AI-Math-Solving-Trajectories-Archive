## Proof

Let $K = F_p(X^p, Y^p)$ and $L = F_p(X, Y)$, where $X, Y$ are algebraically inde
pendent transcendentals over $F_p$. We compute $[L : K]$ via the intermediate fi
eld $M = F_p(X, Y^p)$:

$$K = F_p(X^p, Y^p) \;\subset\; M = F_p(X, Y^p) \;\subset\; L = F_p(X, Y).$$

**Step 1: $[M : K] = p$.**

The element $X$ satisfies $T^p - X^p \in K[T]$. In characteristic $p$,
$$T^p - X^p = (T - X)^p,$$
so $X$ is purely inseparable over $K$, and its minimal polynomial over $K$ is $(
T-X)^m$ for some $m \mid p$, i.e., $m \in \{1, p\}$.

If $m = 1$, then $X \in K = F_p(X^p, Y^p)$. But $X$ and $Y$ are algebraically in
dependent, so $X$ cannot be expressed as a rational function of $X^p$ and $Y^p$
(any such rational function, raised to the $p$-th power, lands in $F_p(X^{p^2},
Y^{p^2}) \subsetneq F_p(X^p, Y^p)$, and more directly, $F_p(X^p,Y^p) \subset F_p
(X,Y)^p$ consists of $p$-th powers, while $X$ is not a $p$-th power in $F_p(X,Y)
$). Hence $X \notin K$, so $m = p$ and $[M:K] = p$.

**Step 2: $[L : M] = p$.**

The element $Y$ satisfies $T^p - Y^p \in M[T]$, and again $T^p - Y^p = (T-Y)^p$.
 The minimal polynomial of $Y$ over $M = F_p(X, Y^p)$ is $(T-Y)^m$ with $m \in \
{1, p\}$.

If $m = 1$, then $Y \in F_p(X, Y^p)$. Since $Y$ is transcendental over $F_p(X)$
and $Y^p$ is transcendental over $F_p(X)$ (by algebraic independence of $X, Y$),
 the extension $F_p(X, Y)/F_p(X, Y^p)$ is purely inseparable of exponent $1$. Th
e element $Y \notin F_p(X, Y^p)$ because $F_p(X, Y^p) \subset F_p(X,Y)^p \cdot F
_p(X)$, and $Y$ is not a $p$-th power in $F_p(X,Y)$. Hence $m = p$ and $[L:M] =
p$.

**Step 3: Multiply.**

By the tower law,
$$[L : K] = [L : M] \cdot [M : K] = p \cdot p = p^2.$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{p^2}$
