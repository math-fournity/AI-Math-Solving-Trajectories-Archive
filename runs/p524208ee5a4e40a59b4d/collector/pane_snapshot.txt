**Setup.** Let $f$ be the degree-42 polynomial satisfying, for each $i=0,1,\dots
,42$,

$$\sum_{k=0}^{46} f(43k+i)=(-2)^i. \tag{1}$$

Define the polynomial

$$P(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{46} f(x+43k).$$

Since $f$ has degree $42$ and there are $47$ summands each contributing the same
 leading term $a x^{42}$, $P$ is a degree-$42$ polynomial (with leading coeffici
ent $47a\neq 0$). Equation (1) says

$$P(i)=(-2)^i,\qquad i=0,1,\dots,42. \tag{2}$$

So $P$ is the **unique** polynomial of degree $\le 42$ interpolating $h(x):=(-2)
^x$ at $x=0,1,\dots,42$.

---

**Relating $f(2021)-f(0)$ to $P$.** Note $2021=43\cdot 47$. Shift the argument o
f $P$ by $43$:

$$P(x+43)=\sum_{k=0}^{46}f\bigl(x+43(k+1)\bigr)=\sum_{k=1}^{47}f(x+43k).$$

Subtracting,

$$P(x+43)-P(x)=f(x+43\cdot 47)-f(x)=f(x+2021)-f(x).$$

Hence

$$f(x+2021)=P(x+43)-P(x)+f(x).$$

Setting $x=0$ and using $P(0)=(-2)^0=1$:

$$\boxed{f(2021)-f(0)=P(43)-P(0)=P(43)-1.} \tag{3}$$

So it remains to compute $P(43)$, the value at $x=43=n+1$ (with $n=42$) of the d
egree-$n$ interpolant of $h(x)=(-2)^x$ at $0,1,\dots,n$.

---

**Evaluating $P(43)$ via Newton forward differences.** For $h(x)=(-2)^x$,

$$\Delta h(x)=h(x+1)-h(x)=(-2)^{x+1}-(-2)^x=(-2)^x(-2-1)=-3\,h(x).$$

Iterating, $\Delta^{j}h(x)=(-3)^{j}h(x)$, so $\Delta^{j}h(0)=(-3)^{j}$.

The Newton forward-difference expansion of the interpolating polynomial of degre
e $n=42$ is

$$P(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{x}{j}\Delta^{j}h(0)=\sum_{j=0}^{42}\binom{x}{j}(-3)^
{j}.$$

Evaluating at $x=43=n+1$:

$$P(43)=\sum_{j=0}^{42}\binom{43}{j}(-3)^{j}.$$

For the actual function, since $43$ is a non-negative integer the binomial theor
em gives the **finite** expansion

$$h(43)=(-2)^{43}=\sum_{j=0}^{43}\binom{43}{j}(-3)^{j}.$$

Therefore the truncation error is exactly the last term:

$$P(43)=h(43)-\binom{43}{43}(-3)^{43}=(-2)^{43}-(-3)^{43}=3^{43}-2^{43}.$$

---

**Final computation.** Substituting into (3):

$$f(2021)-f(0)=P(43)-1=\bigl(3^{43}-2^{43}\bigr)-1.$$

$$\boxed{\,f(2021)-f(0)=3^{43}-2^{43}-1\,}.$$
