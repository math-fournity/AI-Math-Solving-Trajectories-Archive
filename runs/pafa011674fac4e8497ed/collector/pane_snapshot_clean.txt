# Expected Length of the Longest Increasing Subsequence

## Answer

Let $L_n$ denote the length of the longest increasing subsequence (LIS) of a uni
formly random permutation of $\{1,2,\dots,n\}$. Then

$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}[L_n] = 2\sqrt{n} + o(\sqrt{n}) \quad\text{as } n\to\infty.}$
$

More precisely (Baik–Deift–Johansson, 1999),

$$\frac{L_n - 2\sqrt{n}}{n^{1/6}} \xrightarrow{d} F_2,$$

the Tracy–Widom GUE distribution, so $\mathbb{E}[L_n] = 2\sqrt{n} + \mu_{\mathrm
{TW}}\, n^{1/6} + o(n^{1/6})$ with $\mu_{\mathrm{TW}}=\int x\,dF_2(x)\approx -1.
771$.

Below is the proof of the leading term $2\sqrt n$, which is the content of the V
ershik–Kerov / Logan–Shepp theorem (1977).

---

## Proof

### Step 1. Reduction to Plancherel measure via RSK

The **Robinson–Schensted–Knuth correspondence** is a bijection between permutati
ons $\sigma\in S_n$ and pairs $(P,Q)$ of standard Young tableaux (SYT) of a comm
on shape $\lambda\vdash n$. Its key property is:

> The length of the longest increasing subsequence of $\sigma$ equals $\lambda_1
$, the length of the first row of $\lambda$.

Since the correspondence is a bijection and the permutation is uniform, the indu
ced distribution on shapes is

$$\Pr(\lambda) = \frac{(\dim \lambda)^2}{n!},$$

where $\dim\lambda$ is the number of SYT of shape $\lambda$. This is **Planchere
l measure** on partitions of $n$. Consequently

$$\mathbb{E}[L_n] = \sum_{\lambda\vdash n} \lambda_1 \cdot \frac{(\dim\lambda)^2
}{n!} = \mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{Plancherel}}[\lambda_1].$$

So the problem reduces to understanding the first row of a Plancherel-random par
tition.

### Step 2. The hook-length formula and the variational principle

By the hook-length formula,

$$\dim\lambda = \frac{n!}{\prod_{c\in\lambda} h(c)},$$

where $h(c)$ is the hook length of cell $c$. Hence

$$\Pr(\lambda) = \frac{n!}{\bigl(\prod_{c\in\lambda} h(c)\bigr)^2}.$$

Using Stirling's approximation $\log n! = n\log n - n + O(\log n)$ and approxima
ting $\sum \log h(c)$ by an integral, one obtains the following **variational ch
aracterization** (Vershik–Kerov): the rescaled Young diagram concentrates on the
 unique maximizer of a certain entropy functional on continuous Young diagrams.

Concretely, identify a Young diagram with its boundary in the **Russian conventi
on** (rotate by $45^\circ$), rescaled by $1/\sqrt n$ in both directions. The fun
ctional to be maximized is

$$\mathcal{S}[\omega] = \int\!\!\int \log|x-y|\,d\omega(x)\,d\omega(y) \;-\; \in
t x^2\,d\omega(x),$$

over the class of admissible curves $\omega$ (the boundary of a Young diagram of
 total area $1$ in rescaled units). The first term is the "hook-length entropy"
and the second is the area constraint. This is precisely the functional whose Eu
ler–Lagrange equation is a singular integral equation.

### Step 3. The limit shape and its equation

The unique maximizer is the curve (Vershik–Kerov; independently Logan–Shepp)

$$\Omega(x) = \frac{2}{\pi}\Bigl(x\arcsin\frac{x}{2} + \sqrt{4-x^2}\Bigr), \qqua
d x\in[-2,2].$$

**Verification that it is the maximizer.** The Euler–Lagrange equation for the f
unctional reduces, after differentiating, to

$$\operatorname{p.v.}\!\int_{-2}^{2}\frac{\Omega'(t)}{x-t}\,dt = x, \qquad x\in(
-2,2).$$

This is a singular integral equation whose unique solution (with the boundary co
nditions $\Omega(\pm 2)=\pm 2$ and the area constraint $\int_{-2}^{2}\Omega = 1$
) is exactly the curve above. One checks directly: differentiating $\Omega$ give
s $\Omega'(x) = \frac{2}{\pi}\arcsin(x/2)$, and the Hilbert transform of $\arcsi
n(x/2)$ on $[-2,2]$ is $\frac{\pi}{2}x$, confirming the equation. The strict con
cavity of the functional then guarantees uniqueness.

**Concentration.** A large-deviation estimate (Vershik–Kerov) shows that the Pla
ncherel measure of the set of partitions whose rescaled boundary deviates from $
\Omega$ by more than $\varepsilon$ is exponentially small in $n$. Therefore the
rescaled Young diagram converges in probability (and in $L^1$) to $\Omega$.

### Step 4. Reading off $\lambda_1$

In the Russian convention, the first row $\lambda_1$ corresponds to the **rightm
ost** point of the boundary. From the formula,

$$\Omega(2) = \frac{2}{\pi}\bigl(2\cdot \tfrac{\pi}{2} + 0\bigr) = 2, \qquad \Om
ega(-2) = -2.$$

Since the diagram is rescaled by $1/\sqrt n$, the rightmost point in original co
ordinates is $\lambda_1 \approx \sqrt n \cdot 2$. The concentration result gives

$$\frac{\lambda_1}{\sqrt n} \xrightarrow{p} 2.$$

By symmetry (conjugation of partitions), $\lambda_1'/\sqrt n\to 2$ as well, and
the limit shape is symmetric about the vertical axis in Russian convention, cons
istent with $\Omega$.

### Step 5. From convergence in probability to expectation

Since $0\le \lambda_1\le n$, the random variables $\lambda_1/\sqrt n$ are bounde
d in $L^p$ for every fixed $p$ (in fact $\lambda_1\le n$). Convergence in probab
ility plus uniform integrability (which follows from the exponential tail / larg
e-deviation bound of Step 3, or more simply from the a priori bound $\Pr(\lambda
_1 > m)\le \binom{n}{m}/m!$ giving Gaussian-type tails) implies convergence of m
oments:

$$\frac{\mathbb{E}[\lambda_1]}{\sqrt n} \to 2.$$

Recalling $\mathbb{E}[L_n]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{Plancherel}}[\lambda_1]$, we conc
lude

$$\mathbb{E}[L_n] = 2\sqrt n + o(\sqrt n).$$

### Step 6. Finer asymptotics (BDJ)

The Baik–Deift–Johansson theorem refines this: using the connection between Plan
cherel measure and the unitary group (via the Gessel integral / Toeplitz determi
nant representation of $\sum_\lambda (\dim\lambda)^2 \mathbf{1}_{\lambda_1\le k}
$), and the Deift–Zhou steepest-descent analysis of the associated Riemann–Hilbe
rt problem (Painlevé II), one obtains

$$\Pr\!\Bigl(\frac{L_n-2\sqrt n}{n^{1/6}}\le s\Bigr) \to F_2(s) = \exp\!\Bigl(-\
int_s^\infty (x-s)\,q(x)^2\,dx\Bigr),$$

where $q$ is the Hastings–McLeod solution of Painlevé II. This identifies the $o
(\sqrt n)$ correction as of order $n^{1/6}$, with mean $\mu_{\mathrm{TW}}\approx
 -1.771$, giving

$$\mathbb{E}[L_n] = 2\sqrt n + \mu_{\mathrm{TW}}\, n^{1/6} + o(n^{1/6}).$$

---

**Summary.** Via RSK the LIS length equals the first row of a Plancherel-random
partition. The Vershik–Kerov / Logan–Shepp limit shape, derived as the unique ma
ximizer of the hook-length entropy functional, has rightmost extent $2$ in $\sqr
t n$-rescaled coordinates, yielding $\mathbb{E}[L_n]/\sqrt n\to 2$. The BDJ theo
rem supplies the Tracy–Widom fluctuation correction of order $n^{1/6}$.

$$\mathbb{E}[L_n] = 2\sqrt n + o(\sqrt n).$$
