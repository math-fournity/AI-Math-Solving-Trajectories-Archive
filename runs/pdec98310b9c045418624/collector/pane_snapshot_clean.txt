## Solution

**Constraint:** $x + y + z = 18$, with $x, y, z > 0$ (dimensions of a rectangula
r prism).

### Part 1: Maximum of $V = xyz$

By the AM-GM inequality, for positive $x, y, z$ with fixed sum:

$$\frac{x+y+z}{3} \ge \sqrt[3]{xyz}$$

$$\sqrt[3]{xyz} \le \frac{18}{3} = 6 \implies xyz \le 216$$

Equality holds when $x = y = z = 6$.

$$\boxed{V_{\max} = 6 \cdot 6 \cdot 6 = 216}$$

### Part 2: Maximum of $S = 2(xy + yz + zx)$

From the identity $(x+y+z)^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + 2(xy + yz + zx)$:

$$324 = (x^2 + y^2 + z^2) + 2(xy + yz + zx)$$

$$S = 2(xy + yz + zx) = 324 - (x^2 + y^2 + z^2)$$

To maximize $S$, we minimize $x^2 + y^2 + z^2$. By the QM-AM inequality (or the
Cauchy-Schwarz / convexity argument):

$$x^2 + y^2 + z^2 \ge \frac{(x+y+z)^2}{3} = \frac{324}{3} = 108$$

with equality when $x = y = z = 6$.

Therefore:

$$S_{\max} = 324 - 108 = 216$$

**Boundary check:** If one dimension approaches $0$, say $z \to 0$ with $x + y =
 18$, then $S = 2xy \le 2 \cdot 9^2 = 162 < 216$. So the interior critical point
 indeed gives the global maximum.

$$\boxed{S_{\max} = 216}$$

### Final Answer

$$V_{\max} + S_{\max} = 216 + 216 = \boxed{432}$$

Both maxima are achieved at the cube configuration $x = y = z = 6$ meters.
