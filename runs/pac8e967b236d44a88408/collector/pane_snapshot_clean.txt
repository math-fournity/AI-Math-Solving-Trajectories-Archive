Looking at this problem, I need to find $b_n$: the least positive integer base $
b$ such that for every integer $N$ and every $2 \leq m \leq n$, if $m$ divides t
he base-$b$ digit sum $S_b(N)$, then $m$ divides $N$.

## Key Observation

In base $b$, we have $N \equiv S_b(N) \pmod{b-1}$, since $b \equiv 1 \pmod{b-1}$
 implies $b^k \equiv 1 \pmod{b-1}$ for all $k$.

## Necessity: $b > n$

Suppose $b \leq n$. Take $m = b$ and $N = 2b - 1 = 1 \cdot b + (b-1)$. Then $S_b
(N) = 1 + (b-1) = b$, so $b \mid S_b(N)$. But $N = 2b - 1 \equiv -1 \pmod{b}$, s
o $b \nmid N$. **Contradiction.** Thus $b > n$.

## Necessity: $\operatorname{lcm}(2,\ldots,n) \mid (b-1)$

For $b > n$, fix any $2 \leq m \leq n$. Since $m \leq n < b$, the value $m - 1$
is a valid digit. Take $N = b + (m-1)$, so $S_b(N) = 1 + (m-1) = m$, giving $m \
mid S_b(N)$. The condition forces $m \mid N = b + m - 1$, i.e., $m \mid (b-1)$.

Since this holds for **every** $2 \leq m \leq n$, we get $\operatorname{lcm}(2,\
ldots,n) \mid (b-1)$.

## Sufficiency

If $\operatorname{lcm}(2,\ldots,n) \mid (b-1)$, then for each $2 \leq m \leq n$,
 we have $m \mid (b-1)$, so $b \equiv 1 \pmod{m}$, hence $b^k \equiv 1 \pmod{m}$
 for all $k$. Writing $N = \sum a_i b^i$:

$$N \equiv \sum a_i = S_b(N) \pmod{m}$$

So $m \mid N \iff m \mid S_b(N)$, and the condition is satisfied.

## Conclusion: $b_n = \operatorname{lcm}(2,3,\ldots,n) + 1$

This is the smallest $b > n$ with $\operatorname{lcm}(2,\ldots,n) \mid (b-1)$.

## Computing the Ratio

**Highest prime powers up to 25:** $2^4=16,\; 3^2=9,\; 5^2=25,\; 7,\; 11,\; 13,\
; 17,\; 19,\; 23$

$$\operatorname{lcm}(2,\ldots,25) = 16 \cdot 9 \cdot 25 \cdot 7 \cdot 11 \cdot 1
3 \cdot 17 \cdot 19 \cdot 23 = 26{,}771{,}144{,}400$$

**Highest prime powers up to 36:** $2^5=32,\; 3^3=27,\; 5^2=25,\; 7,\; 11,\; 13,
\; 17,\; 19,\; 23,\; 29,\; 31$

The ratio of the two lcms is:

$$\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(2,\ldots,36)}{\operatorname{lcm}(2,\ldots,25)} = \fra
c{32}{16}\cdot\frac{27}{9}\cdot 29 \cdot 31 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 29 \cdot 31 = 5394
$$

Setting $L = \operatorname{lcm}(2,\ldots,25) = 26{,}771{,}144{,}400$:

$$\frac{b_{36}}{b_{25}} = \frac{5394\,L + 1}{L + 1} = 5394 - \frac{5393}{L+1} =
5394 - \frac{5393}{26{,}771{,}144{,}401}$$

Since $\frac{5393}{26{,}771{,}144{,}401} \approx 2 \times 10^{-7}$, the ratio is
 extremely close to $5394$.

## Answer

The integer nearest to $b_{36}/b_{25}$ is $\boxed{5394}$.
