Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a line in the special fiber
 of a smooth quadric over $\mathcal{O}_K$ lifts to a line over $\mathcal{O}_K$.

**Answer: Yes.** The key tool is Hensel's lemma applied to the Fano variety of l
ines, which is smooth over $\mathcal{O}_K$.

---

## Proof

Let $Q \subset \mathbb{P}^n_{\mathcal{O}_K}$ be a projective quadric, smooth ove
r $\mathcal{O}_K$, defined by a quadratic form $q$ on $\mathcal{O}_K^{n+1}$. Let
 $\bar{Q} \subset \mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{F}}$ be the special fiber, defined by th
e reduction $\bar{q}$. By hypothesis, $\bar{Q}$ is smooth over $\mathbb{F}$ and
contains a line $\bar{\ell} \cong \mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{F}}$ defined over $\math
bb{F}$.

### Step 1: The Fano variety of lines

Let $\mathrm{Gr} = \mathrm{Gr}(1, n)_{\mathcal{O}_K}$ be the Grassmannian of lin
es in $\mathbb{P}^n_{\mathcal{O}_K}$, which is smooth over $\mathcal{O}_K$ of re
lative dimension $2(n-1)$. There is a tautological rank-2 subbundle $\mathcal{S}
$ on $\mathrm{Gr}$, and the quadratic form $q$ restricts to a section
$$s \in H^0\!\bigl(\mathrm{Gr},\, \mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathcal{S}^\vee)\bigr)$$
of a rank-3 vector bundle $E := \mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathcal{S}^\vee)$. The **Fano va
riety of lines** is the zero scheme:
$$F_1(Q) := Z(s) \subset \mathrm{Gr}.$$
An $\mathcal{O}_K$-point of $F_1(Q)$ corresponds to a rank-2 free subspace of $\
mathcal{O}_K^{n+1}$ on which $q$ vanishes identically, i.e., a line $\mathbb{P}^
1_{\mathcal{O}_K} \hookrightarrow Q$. The special fiber of $F_1(Q)$ is $F_1(\bar
{Q}) = Z(\bar{s})$, where $\bar{s}$ is the reduction of $s$.

### Step 2: $F_1(\bar{Q})$ is smooth

We show $F_1(\bar{Q})$ is smooth at every point $[\bar{\ell}]$. For a line $\bar
{\ell} \subset \bar{Q}$, the normal bundle $N_{\bar{\ell}/\bar{Q}}$ fits in the
exact sequence
$$0 \to N_{\bar{\ell}/\bar{Q}} \to N_{\bar{\ell}/\mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{F}}} \to
N_{\bar{Q}/\mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{F}}}|_{\bar{\ell}} \to 0.$$
We have $N_{\bar{\ell}/\mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{F}}} \cong \mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus(n
-1)}$ and $N_{\bar{Q}/\mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{F}}}|_{\bar{\ell}} \cong \mathcal{O}
(2)$. Since $\bar{Q}$ is smooth along $\bar{\ell}$, the map $\mathcal{O}(1)^{\op
lus(n-1)} \to \mathcal{O}(2)$ is surjective. Tensoring by $\mathcal{O}(-1)$, thi
s is a surjection $\mathcal{O}^{\oplus(n-1)} \twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{O}(1)$,
 whose kernel is $\mathcal{O}(-1) \oplus \mathcal{O}^{\oplus(n-3)}$ (any surject
ion $\mathcal{O}^{\oplus(n-1)} \to \mathcal{O}(1)$ is equivalent to the standard
 one up to $\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}$). Therefore
$$N_{\bar{\ell}/\bar{Q}} \cong \mathcal{O} \oplus \mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus(n-3)}.$
$
The tangent space to $F_1(\bar{Q})$ at $[\bar{\ell}]$ is $H^0(\bar{\ell}, N_{\ba
r{\ell}/\bar{Q}})$ and the obstruction space is $H^1(\bar{\ell}, N_{\bar{\ell}/\
bar{Q}})$. Since every summand has degree $\geq 0$,
$$H^1(\bar{\ell}, N_{\bar{\ell}/\bar{Q}}) = 0.$$
Thus $F_1(\bar{Q})$ is smooth at $[\bar{\ell}]$, of dimension $h^0(N_{\bar{\ell}
/\bar{Q}}) = 1 + 2(n-3) = 2n - 5 = 2(n-1) - 3$, which is the expected codimensio
n-3 in $\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{F}}$.

### Step 3: $F_1(Q)$ is smooth over $\mathcal{O}_K$

The tangent space to $F_1(\bar{Q})$ at $[\bar{\ell}]$ is $\ker(d\bar{s}_{[\bar{\
ell}]})$, where $d\bar{s}_{[\bar{\ell}]}: T_{[\bar{\ell}]}\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb{F
}} \to E_{[\bar{\ell}]}$ is the derivative of $\bar{s}$. Since $F_1(\bar{Q})$ is
 smooth of dimension $2(n-1)-3$ and $\mathrm{rank}(E) = 3$,
$$\dim \ker(d\bar{s}_{[\bar{\ell}]}) = 2(n-1) - 3 = \dim T_{[\bar{\ell}]}\mathrm
{Gr}_{\mathbb{F}} - \mathrm{rank}(E),$$
so $d\bar{s}_{[\bar{\ell}]}$ is **surjective**.

By the local criterion for smoothness (EGA IV, 17.11.1): since $\mathrm{Gr}$ is
smooth over $\mathcal{O}_K$ and the derivative of the defining section $s$ is su
rjective modulo the maximal ideal, the zero scheme $F_1(Q) = Z(s)$ is smooth ove
r $\mathcal{O}_K$ at every point lying over $[\bar{\ell}]$.

### Step 4: Hensel's lemma

The given line $\bar{\ell} \cong \mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{F}}$ defined over $\mathb
b{F}$ gives an $\mathbb{F}$-rational point $[\bar{\ell}] \in F_1(\bar{Q})(\mathb
b{F})$. Since $F_1(Q)$ is smooth over $\mathcal{O}_K$ (a complete DVR), Hensel's
 lemma applies: the $\mathbb{F}$-point $[\bar{\ell}]$ lifts to an $\mathcal{O}_K
$-point $[\ell] \in F_1(Q)(\mathcal{O}_K)$.

This $\mathcal{O}_K$-point corresponds to a rank-2 free submodule of $\mathcal{O
}_K^{n+1}$ on which $q$ vanishes identically, yielding a closed immersion
$$\ell \cong \mathbb{P}^1_{\mathcal{O}_K} \hookrightarrow Q$$
whose special fiber is $\bar{\ell}$. $\square$

---

**Summary.** The Fano variety of lines $F_1(Q)$ is smooth over $\mathcal{O}_K$ b
ecause (1) the Fano variety of lines on any smooth quadric over a field is smoot
h (the normal bundle of any line splits as $\mathcal{O} \oplus \mathcal{O}(1)^{\
oplus(n-3)}$, giving vanishing $H^1$ and hence no obstructions), and (2) smoothn
ess of the special fiber $F_1(\bar{Q})$ at the expected dimension forces the der
ivative of the defining equations to be surjective, which lifts smoothness to $\
mathcal{O}_K$. Hensel's lemma then lifts the $\mathbb{F}$-rational line to an $\
mathcal{O}_K$-rational line.
