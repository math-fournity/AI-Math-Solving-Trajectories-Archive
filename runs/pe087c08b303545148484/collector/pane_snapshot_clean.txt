**Problem.** Is the unit ball in $TC(\mathcal{H})$ compact in the trace norm top
ology?

**Answer.** It depends on $\dim\mathcal{H}$:
- If $\dim\mathcal{H}<\infty$: **yes**.
- If $\dim\mathcal{H}=\infty$: **no**.

Below is the complete proof for both cases, with the infinite-dimensional case b
eing the substantive one.

---

## Preliminaries

$TC(\mathcal{H})$ is the Banach space of trace-class operators on a Hilbert spac
e $\mathcal{H}$, equipped with the trace norm
$$\|T\|_1=\operatorname{tr}|T|=\sum_{k}\langle|T|e_k,e_k\rangle,$$
where $\{e_k\}$ is any orthonormal basis (the sum is independent of the basis).
The unit ball is
$$B_1=\{T\in TC(\mathcal{H}):\|T\|_1\le 1\}.$$

We use two standard facts:

**(F1) Riesz's theorem.** The closed unit ball of a normed space is norm-compact
 iff the space is finite-dimensional.

**(F2) Heine–Borel in finite dimensions.** The closed unit ball of a finite-dime
nsional normed space over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ is compact.

---

## Case 1: $\dim\mathcal{H}<\infty$ — the unit ball **is** compact

If $\dim\mathcal{H}=n<\infty$, every bounded operator is trace-class, so $TC(\ma
thcal{H})=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\cong M_n(\mathbb{C})$, a finite-dimensional v
ector space (real dimension $2n^2$). All norms on a finite-dimensional space are
 equivalent, so the trace-norm topology coincides with the usual Euclidean topol
ogy on $\mathbb{C}^{n^2}$. By (F2), the closed unit ball $B_1$ is compact. $\squ
are$

## Case 2: $\dim\mathcal{H}=\infty$ — the unit ball is **not** compact

We give a direct sequential argument (in metric spaces, compactness $\iff$ seque
ntial compactness, so exhibiting a sequence in $B_1$ with no norm-convergent sub
sequence suffices).

**Construction.** Since $\mathcal{H}$ is infinite-dimensional, it contains an in
finite orthonormal sequence $\{e_n\}_{n\ge 1}$. Define the rank-one projections
$$T_n := e_n\otimes e_n,\qquad T_n(x)=\langle x,e_n\rangle\,e_n.$$

**Each $T_n$ lies in $B_1$.** $T_n$ is positive and rank-one with sole nonzero e
igenvalue $1$, so
$$\|T_n\|_1=\operatorname{tr}(T_n)=\langle T_n e_n,e_n\rangle=1.$$

**Pairwise distance is $2$.** For $n\neq m$, the operator $T_n-T_m$ is self-adjo
int and acts as
$$(T_n-T_m)e_n=e_n,\quad (T_n-T_m)e_m=-e_m,\quad (T_n-T_m)e_k=0\;(k\neq n,m).$$
Hence the spectral decomposition of $T_n-T_m$ on $\operatorname{span}\{e_n,e_m\}
$ has eigenvalues $+1$ and $-1$, and $|T_n-T_m|$ has eigenvalues $1,1$ (and $0$
elsewhere). Therefore
$$\|T_n-T_m\|_1=\operatorname{tr}|T_n-T_m|=1+1=2,\qquad n\neq m.$$

**No convergent subsequence.** Any subsequence $\{T_{n_j}\}$ still satisfies $\|
T_{n_j}-T_{n_k}\|_1=2$ for $j\neq k$, so it is not Cauchy and hence cannot conve
rge in the trace norm. Thus $B_1$ is not sequentially compact, hence not compact
.

**Alternative (quick) argument via (F1).** $TC(\mathcal{H})$ is infinite-dimensi
onal whenever $\mathcal{H}$ is: the operators $\{T_n=e_n\otimes e_n\}$ are linea
rly independent and all belong to $TC(\mathcal{H})$. By Riesz's theorem (F1), th
e closed unit ball of an infinite-dimensional normed space is never norm-compact
. $\square$

## Summary

| $\dim\mathcal{H}$ | $TC(\mathcal{H})$ dimension | Unit ball trace-norm compact
? |
|---|---|---|
| finite ($n$) | $2n^2$ (real) | **Yes** (Heine–Borel) |
| infinite | infinite | **No** (Riesz / explicit $\{e_n\otimes e_n\}$ sequence)
|

In the standard setting where $\mathcal{H}$ is an infinite-dimensional separable
 Hilbert space — the context in which trace-class operators form a proper, disti
nct ideal — **the unit ball of $TC(\mathcal{H})$ is not compact in the trace nor
m topology.**

The key structural point: $TC(\mathcal{H})=K(\mathcal{H})^*$ (dual of compact op
erators), so by Banach–Alaoglu its unit ball *is* compact in the weak-* topology
 $\sigma(TC,K)$. But weak-* compactness is strictly weaker than norm compactness
, and the gap is exactly captured by Riesz's theorem: norm-compactness of the ba
ll forces finite-dimensionality. The explicit sequence $\{e_n\otimes e_n\}$ with
 constant pairwise trace-norm distance $2$ makes this gap concrete — the ball "l
eaks mass to infinity" through rank-one pieces supported on ever-higher basis ve
ctors, with no norm-convergent way to recombine them.
