## Proof

Consider a regular 11-gon inscribed in a circle. Label its vertices $0, 1, \dots
, 10$ around the circle. Choose any triple of vertices; going clockwise around t
he circle, let the three arcs (measured in number of sides) be $a, b, c$, where
$a, b, c \geq 1$ and $a + b + c = 11$.

**Acute condition.** An inscribed angle equals half the central angle subtending
 the same arc. The angle at the vertex between arcs $c$ and $a$ subtends arc $b$
, so it is acute iff the arc $b < 180°$, i.e., $b < 11/2 = 5.5$, i.e., $b \leq 5
$. Applying this to all three vertices, the triangle is acute iff

$$a, b, c \leq 5.$$

**Counting ordered arc triples.** Set $a' = a-1,\; b' = b-1,\; c' = c-1$, so $a'
, b', c' \geq 0$ and $a'+b'+c' = 8$ with $a', b', c' \leq 4$.

Total nonnegative solutions to $a'+b'+c'=8$: $\binom{10}{2} = 45$.

Subtract solutions where some variable $\geq 5$. If $a' \geq 5$, set $a'' = a'-5
$; then $a''+b'+c' = 3$, giving $\binom{5}{2} = 10$ solutions. By symmetry over
3 variables: $3 \times 10 = 30$.

Add back solutions where two variables $\geq 5$: then their sum is $\geq 10 > 8$
, impossible, so $0$.

By inclusion–exclusion: $45 - 30 = 15$ ordered triples $(a, b, c)$.

**From arc triples to triangles.** Each ordered arc triple together with a choic
e of starting vertex (11 choices) determines a triangle, but each triangle is co
unted 3 times (once for each of its 3 vertices as the starting point). Hence

$$\text{number of acute triangles} = \frac{11 \times 15}{3} = 55.$$

**Verification of the counting factor.** The same formula applied to all triangl
es gives $\frac{11 \times 45}{3} = 165 = \binom{11}{3}$, confirming the factor i
s correct.
