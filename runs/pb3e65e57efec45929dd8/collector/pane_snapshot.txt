## Evaluation of $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx$

### Step 1 — Identify the singularities

The integrand $f(x)=\dfrac{\cos x}{x}$ has:
- a singularity at $x=0$ (where $\cos x/x \sim 1/x$), and
- oscillatory tails as $x\to\pm\infty$.

So the integral is improper at **both** $x=0$ and at $\pm\infty$.

### Step 2 — Behavior at infinity (convergent)

For $x\ge 1$, $|\cos x|\le 1$ is bounded and $g(x)=1/x$ decreases monotonically
to $0$. By the **Dirichlet test**,
$$\int_1^{\infty}\frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx \quad\text{converges (conditionally)},$$
and by the same argument $\int_{-\infty}^{-1}\frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx$ converges. So
 the tails are not the problem.

### Step 3 — Behavior near $x=0$ (divergent)

Near the origin, $\cos x = 1 - \frac{x^2}{2}+\cdots$, so
$$\frac{\cos x}{x} = \frac{1}{x} - \frac{x}{2} + O(x^3).$$

For $x\in(0,1]$, we have $\cos x \ge \cos 1 > 0$, hence
$$\frac{\cos x}{x}\ge \frac{\cos 1}{x}.$$

Since $\displaystyle\int_0^{1}\frac{\cos 1}{x}\,dx = \cos 1\cdot\lim_{\epsilon\t
o 0^+}\ln\frac{1}{\epsilon}=+\infty,$

by comparison,
$$\int_0^{1}\frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx = +\infty.$$

By the substitution $x\mapsto -x$ (using that $\cos x/x$ is **odd**),
$$\int_{-1}^{0}\frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx = -\infty.$$

### Step 4 — Conclusion for the improper integral

In the standard (Lebesgue / improper Riemann) sense, the integral is split at th
e singularity:
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx = \int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{\cos x}{
x}\,dx + \int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx.$$

Each piece must converge independently. Since
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx \;\ge\; \int_{0}^{1}\frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx
 \;=\; +\infty,$$
the right half diverges to $+\infty$, and the left half diverges to $-\infty$. T
herefore

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx \text{ diverge
s (does not exist as an improper integral).}}$$

### Step 5 — Cauchy principal value (for completeness)

Although the improper integral diverges, the **Cauchy principal value** is well
defined. Since $\cos x/x$ is an **odd** function ($\cos(-x)/(-x)=-\cos x/x$), fo
r every $\epsilon>0$ and $R>\epsilon$,
$$\int_{-R}^{-\epsilon}\frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx \;=\; -\int_{\epsilon}^{R}\frac{\cos
 x}{x}\,dx,$$
so the symmetric sum vanishes identically:
$$\operatorname{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx
= \lim_{\substack{\epsilon\to 0^+\\ R\to\infty}}\left(\int_{-R}^{-\epsilon}\frac
{\cos x}{x}\,dx+\int_{\epsilon}^{R}\frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx\right)=0.$$

### Final answer

- **As an improper integral:** the integral **diverges** (the singularity at $x=
0$ is non-integrable, since $\cos x/x\sim 1/x$).
- **As a Cauchy principal value:** $\displaystyle\operatorname{PV}\!\int_{-\inft
y}^{\infty}\frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx = 0$, by the oddness of the integrand.
