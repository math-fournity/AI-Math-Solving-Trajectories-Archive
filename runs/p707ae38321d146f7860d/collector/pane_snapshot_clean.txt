## Proof

**Claim:** The statement is **true**. Let $A$ be a unital complex Banach algebra
, $x \in A$, $\sigma(x)$ its spectrum, and $\rho(x) = \mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma(
x)$ the resolvent set. Let $U \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ be open with $\sigma(x) \sub
seteq U$. Then
$$\inf\big\{\,\|(x-\lambda 1_A)^{-1}\|^{-1} : \lambda \in \mathbb{C}\setminus U\
,\big\} > 0.$$

---

**Step 1: Setup and continuity.** Since $\sigma(x) \subseteq U$, we have $\mathb
b{C}\setminus U \subseteq \rho(x)$, so $(x - \lambda 1_A)^{-1}$ exists for every
 $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}\setminus U$. Define
$$f(\lambda) := \|(x-\lambda 1_A)^{-1}\|^{-1}, \qquad \lambda \in \rho(x).$$
The resolvent $\lambda \mapsto (x-\lambda 1_A)^{-1}$ is analytic on $\rho(x)$ (a
 standard result in Banach algebra theory), hence continuous. Since the resolven
t is invertible (hence nonzero) at every point of $\rho(x)$, its norm is strictl
y positive, and $f$ is a continuous, strictly positive function on $\rho(x)$.

**Step 2: Growth at infinity.** For $|\lambda| > \|x\|$, the Neumann series give
s
$$(x - \lambda 1_A)^{-1} = -\lambda^{-1}\!\left(1_A - \frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{
-1} = -\lambda^{-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^n,$$
so
$$\|(x-\lambda 1_A)^{-1}\| \leq \frac{1}{|\lambda|}\cdot\frac{1}{1 - \|x\|/|\lam
bda|} = \frac{1}{|\lambda| - \|x\|}.$$
Therefore
$$f(\lambda) = \|(x-\lambda 1_A)^{-1}\|^{-1} \geq |\lambda| - \|x\| \xrightarrow
{|\lambda|\to\infty} \infty.$$

**Step 3: Compact reduction.** Choose $R > \|x\| + 1$ sufficiently large that $f
(\lambda) \geq 1$ for all $|\lambda| \geq R$ (possible by Step 2). Set
$$K := (\mathbb{C}\setminus U) \cap \overline{B(0, R)}.$$
Since $U$ is open, $\mathbb{C}\setminus U$ is closed; $\overline{B(0,R)}$ is com
pact; hence $K$ is a closed subset of a compact set, thus compact. Moreover $K \
subseteq \mathbb{C}\setminus U \subseteq \rho(x)$, so $f$ is defined, continuous
, and strictly positive on $K$.

**Step 4: Bounding the infimum.** We consider two cases.

*Case 1: $K \neq \emptyset$.* By compactness and continuity, $f$ attains its min
imum on $K$:
$$m := \min_{\lambda \in K} f(\lambda) > 0,$$
where the strict positivity holds because $f$ is strictly positive on $K$. Now f
or any $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}\setminus U$:
- If $|\lambda| \leq R$, then $\lambda \in K$, so $f(\lambda) \geq m$.
- If $|\lambda| > R$, then by the choice of $R$, $f(\lambda) \geq 1$.

Hence
$$\inf_{\lambda \in \mathbb{C}\setminus U} f(\lambda) \geq \min(m,\, 1) > 0.$$

*Case 2: $K = \emptyset$.* Then $\mathbb{C}\setminus U \subseteq \{|\lambda| > R
\}$, so $f(\lambda) \geq 1$ for all $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}\setminus U$, giving
$\inf \geq 1 > 0$.

**Conclusion.** In both cases,
$$\inf\big\{\,\|(x-\lambda 1_A)^{-1}\|^{-1} : \lambda \in \mathbb{C}\setminus U\
,\big\} > 0.$$

The key ingredients are: (i) the resolvent is analytic (hence $f$ is continuous)
 on $\rho(x)$; (ii) $f(\lambda) \to \infty$ as $|\lambda|\to\infty$ (Neumann ser
ies), which reduces the problem to a bounded region; (iii) the openness of $U$ m
akes $\mathbb{C}\setminus U$ closed, so its intersection with a closed ball is c
ompact; and (iv) a continuous strictly positive function on a compact set has a
strictly positive minimum. $\blacksquare$
