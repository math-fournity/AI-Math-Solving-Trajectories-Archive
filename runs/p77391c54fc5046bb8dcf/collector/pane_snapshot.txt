## Solution

**Answer: Yes.** In fact, the entire sequence $(u_n)$ converges to some $u \in W
^{2,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^N)$, and the convergence is strong on every compact subs
et (not merely weak, and not merely along a subsequence).

### Construction of the limit $u$

From property (2), $u_n = u_{n+1}$ in $B(0,n)$. By induction, for all $k \geq 0$
 and all $n \geq m$:
$$u_n = u_{n+k} \quad \text{in } B(0, m), \quad \text{whenever } n \geq m.$$
Indeed, $B(0,m) \subset B(0,n)$ when $n \geq m$, and property (2) gives $u_n = u
_{n+1}$ on $B(0,n) \supset B(0,m)$; chaining this forward yields the claim.

Define $u: \mathbb{R}^N \to \mathbb{R}$ by setting, on each ball $B(0,m)$:
$$u\big|_{B(0,m)} := u_n\big|_{B(0,m)} \quad \text{for any } n \geq m.$$
This is well-defined: if $n, n' \geq m$, then both $u_n$ and $u_{n'}$ agree with
 $u_{\max(n,n')}$ on $B(0,m) \subset B(0,\min(n,n'))$, so they agree with each o
ther. Consistency across different $m$ is immediate since the definition on $B(0
,m) \subset B(0,m')$ (for $m < m'$) is compatible.

### $u$ belongs to $W^{2,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^N)$

Fix any $m$. For $n \geq m$, $u = u_n$ on $B(0,m)$, so by property (4):
$$\|u\|_{W^{2,p}(B(0,m))} = \|u_n\|_{W^{2,p}(B(0,m))} \leq K(m) < \infty.$$
Since $u_n \in C^2_0(B(0,n)) \subset W^{2,p}(B(0,m))$ (by property (3)), we have
 $u\big|_{B(0,m)} \in W^{2,p}(B(0,m))$ with a uniform bound. As $m$ is arbitrary
, $u \in W^{2,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^N)$.

### Convergence (strong, hence weak)

Fix any $m$ and any $n \geq m$. Then $u_n = u$ on $B(0,m)$, so:
$$\|u_n - u\|_{W^{2,p}(B(0,m))} = 0.$$
Thus $u_n \to u$ in $W^{2,p}(B(0,m))$ for every $m$; in particular $u_n \rightha
rpoonup u$ weakly in $W^{2,p}(B(0,m))$ for every $m$, which is precisely weak co
nvergence in $W^{2,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^N)$.

### Conclusion

The entire sequence $(u_n)$ converges strongly (and hence weakly) in $W^{2,p}_{l
oc}(\mathbb{R}^N)$ to the function $u$ constructed above. Therefore a weakly con
vergent subsequence certainly exists — namely the full sequence itself — with li
mit $u \in W^{2,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^N)$.

The key mechanism is property (2): it makes the sequence *eventually constant on
 each fixed ball*, which is far stronger than what a compactness argument (Banac
h–Alaoglu + reflexivity of $W^{2,p}$, $1 < p < \infty$) would yield. The bound i
n property (4) guarantees the limit retains finite $W^{2,p}$-norm on each compac
t, placing it in $W^{2,p}_{loc}$.
