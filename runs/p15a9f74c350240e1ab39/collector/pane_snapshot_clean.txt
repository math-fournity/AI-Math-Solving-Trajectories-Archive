Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $H^2(G, -)$ and $H^2(G^{\te
xt{ab}}, -)$ are **always** different for non-abelian $G$. I'll show they are no
t always different by providing an explicit counterexample.

## Proof

**Claim:** The statement is **false**. There exists a non-abelian group $G$ such
 that $H^2(G, \mathbb{Z}) \cong H^2(G^{\text{ab}}, \mathbb{Z})$ (with trivial ac
tion).

**Counterexample:** Let $G = S_3$, the symmetric group on 3 letters. This is non
-abelian (order 6). Its abelianization is
$$G^{\text{ab}} = S_3 / [S_3, S_3] \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},$$
since the commutator subgroup $[S_3, S_3] = A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$.

**Step 1: Compute $H^2(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z})$.**

For a cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ with trivial action on $\mathbb{Z}$,
 the standard resolution gives
$$H^2(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}.$$
Thus $H^2(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.

**Step 2: Compute $H^2(S_3, \mathbb{Z})$ via the universal coefficient theorem.*
*

For group cohomology with trivial $\mathbb{Z}$-coefficients, the universal coeff
icient theorem gives:
$$H^2(G, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(H_2(G, \mathbb{Z}),\, \mathbb{Z})
\oplus \operatorname{Ext}^1(H_1(G, \mathbb{Z}),\, \mathbb{Z}).$$

We need the homology groups of $S_3$:
- $H_1(S_3, \mathbb{Z}) = S_3^{\text{ab}} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.
- $H_2(S_3, \mathbb{Z}) = M(S_3)$, the Schur multiplier of $S_3$.

Since $S_3 \cong D_6$ (the dihedral group of order 6, i.e., $D_{2 \cdot 3}$), an
d the Schur multiplier of the dihedral group $D_{2n}$ is trivial when $n$ is odd
 (and $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ when $n$ is even), we get:
$$H_2(S_3, \mathbb{Z}) = M(S_3) = 0 \quad (\text{since } n = 3 \text{ is odd}).$
$

**Step 3: Assemble the result.**

Substituting into the universal coefficient theorem:
$$H^2(S_3, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(0, \mathbb{Z}) \oplus \operatorn
ame{Ext}^1(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}) \cong 0 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2\mathb
b{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.$$

**Step 4: Compare.**

$$H^2(S_3, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \cong H^2(S_3^{\text{ab}}, \
mathbb{Z}).$$

The two second cohomology groups are **isomorphic** (both $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z
}$), even though $S_3$ is non-abelian.

**Remark (additional counterexample with $\mathbb{C}^*$ coefficients):** The sam
e phenomenon appears with other coefficient modules. Taking coefficients in $\ma
thbb{C}^*$ (trivial action), the Schur multiplier interpretation gives $H^2(S_3,
 \mathbb{C}^*) = M(S_3) = 0$, while $H^2(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{C}^*) =
 0$ (since $\mathbb{C}^*$ is divisible). Again both are equal (trivial).

**Conclusion:** Since $S_3$ is a non-abelian group whose second cohomology group
 (with trivial $\mathbb{Z}$-coefficients) is isomorphic to that of its abelianiz
ation, the second cohomology groups of a non-abelian group and its abelianizatio
n are **not** always different.

The answer is **false**.
