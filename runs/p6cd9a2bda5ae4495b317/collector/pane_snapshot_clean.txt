## Answer

**No.** Not every localization of a reduced Noetherian commutative ring at a max
imal ideal is a field.

### Counterexample

Let $R = k[x]$, the polynomial ring in one variable over a field $k$.

**$R$ is commutative Noetherian with no nonzero nilpotents.**
- $R$ is commutative by definition.
- $R$ is Noetherian by the Hilbert basis theorem (a PID, in fact).
- $R$ is an integral domain, so its nilradical is $\{0\}$; hence it has no nonze
ro nilpotents.

**Take the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m} = (x)$.** This is maximal since $R/(x) \c
ong k$ is a field.

**The localization $R_{\mathfrak{m}} = k[x]_{(x)}$ is not a field.**

The localization $R_{\mathfrak{m}}$ is the local ring of the affine line at the
origin. Its unique maximal ideal is $\mathfrak{m}R_{\mathfrak{m}} = (x)R_{\mathf
rak{m}}$, which consists of all fractions $f/g$ with $f \in (x)$ and $g \notin (
x)$ (i.e., $g(0) \neq 0$).

This maximal ideal is **nonzero**: the element $\dfrac{x}{1} \in R_{\mathfrak{m}
}$ is nonzero (since $x \neq 0$ in $R$ and $1 \notin \mathfrak{m}$), and it lies
 in $\mathfrak{m}R_{\mathfrak{m}}$.

Indeed, $\dfrac{x}{1}$ is not a unit in $R_{\mathfrak{m}}$: if it were a unit, t
here would exist $h/s \in R_{\mathfrak{m}}$ (with $s \notin \mathfrak{m}$, i.e.,
 $s(0)\neq 0$) such that $\dfrac{x}{1}\cdot\dfrac{h}{s} = \dfrac{1}{1}$, i.e., $
xh = s$ in $R$. But the left side vanishes at $x=0$ while $s(0)\neq 0$, a contra
diction.

Since $R_{\mathfrak{m}}$ contains a nonzero non-unit (namely $\frac{x}{1}$), it
is **not a field**. (Equivalently, $R_{\mathfrak{m}}$ is a discrete valuation ri
ng with uniformizer $x$.)

### General criterion

$R_{\mathfrak{m}}$ is a field if and only if $\mathfrak{m}R_{\mathfrak{m}} = 0$,
 which happens if and only if $\mathfrak{m}$ is a **minimal prime** of $R$ (in a
ddition to being maximal). In a reduced Noetherian ring, minimal primes exist an
d the maximal ideals that are also minimal correspond to the isolated (clopen) p
oints of $\operatorname{Spec} R$. For rings like $k[x]$ whose spectrum is irredu
cible, the only minimal prime is $(0)$, which is not maximal, so **no** localiza
tion at a maximal ideal is a field.

### Conclusion

The statement is **false** in general. A reduced Noetherian commutative ring nee
d not have the property that every localization at a maximal ideal is a field; $
R = k[x]$ with $\mathfrak{m}=(x)$ provides an explicit counterexample.
