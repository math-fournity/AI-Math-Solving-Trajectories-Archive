**Answer: Yes, such a family exists.**

**Construction.** Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is countable, fix a bijection $\varphi: \ma
thbb{N} \to \mathbb{Q}$. For each real number $r \in \mathbb{R}$, define
$$A_r \;=\; \bigl\{\, n \in \mathbb{N} : \varphi(n) < r \,\bigr\} \;\subseteq\;
\mathbb{N}.$$
Set $\mathcal{A} = \{ A_r : r \in \mathbb{R} \}$.

**$\mathcal{A}$ is totally ordered by inclusion.** Let $r, s \in \mathbb{R}$ wit
h $r \le s$. If $\varphi(n) < r$ then $\varphi(n) < s$, so $A_r \subseteq A_s$.
Hence any two members of $\mathcal{A}$ are comparable under $\subseteq$.

**The ordering is strict (so the map $r \mapsto A_r$ is injective).** If $r < s$
, by density of $\mathbb{Q}$ in $\mathbb{R}$ there exists $q \in \mathbb{Q}$ wit
h $r < q < s$. Writing $q = \varphi(n)$ for some $n \in \mathbb{N}$, we have $n
\in A_s$ but $n \notin A_r$, so $A_r \subsetneq A_s$. Thus $r \ne s \Rightarrow
A_r \ne A_s$, and the map $r \mapsto A_r$ is injective.

**Cardinality.** The injection $\mathbb{R} \to \mathcal{A}$, $r \mapsto A_r$, gi
ves $|\mathcal{A}| \ge |\mathbb{R}| = \mathfrak{c}$. Since $\mathcal{A} \subsete
q \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})$ and $|\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})| = \mathfrak{c}$, we ge
t $|\mathcal{A}| = \mathfrak{c}$.

Therefore $\mathcal{A}$ is a chain in $(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N}), \subseteq)$ of
cardinality $\mathfrak{c}$, as required. $\blacksquare$
