**IMO 1989, Problem 5.** Prove that for each positive integer $n$ there exist $n
$ consecutive positive integers, none of which is an integral power of a prime n
umber.

---

**Proof.**

Recall that an *integral power of a prime* is a number of the form $p^k$ where $
p$ is prime and $k\geq 1$. Such a number is divisible by exactly one prime, name
ly $p$. Consequently, any integer divisible by **two distinct primes** is automa
tically not a prime power.

We use this observation together with the Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT).

**Setup.** Let $n$ be a fixed positive integer. Choose $2n$ distinct primes
$$p_1,q_1,\;p_2,q_2,\;\ldots,\;p_n,q_n.$$
(For instance, take the first $2n$ primes and pair them up.) Since they are all
distinct, the moduli $p_1,q_1,\ldots,p_n,q_n$ are pairwise coprime.

**Applying CRT.** Consider the system of $2n$ congruences
$$N \equiv -i \pmod{p_i}, \qquad N \equiv -i \pmod{q_i}, \qquad i=1,2,\ldots,n.$
$
By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, since the moduli are pairwise coprime, this sy
stem has a solution modulo
$$M = \prod_{i=1}^{n} p_i\, q_i.$$
Let $N_0$ be any integer solution. Then every integer of the form $N = N_0 + mM$
 ($m\in\mathbb{Z}$) is also a solution.

**Choosing $N$ large enough.** Pick $m$ sufficiently large so that $N = N_0 + mM
$ satisfies $N + i \geq 1$ for all $i=1,\ldots,n$ (in fact we can make $N$ as la
rge as we like). Set
$$a_i = N + i, \qquad i = 1,2,\ldots,n.$$
Then $a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_n$ are $n$ consecutive positive integers.

**None is a prime power.** For each $i$, the congruences $N\equiv -i\pmod{p_i}$
and $N\equiv -i\pmod{q_i}$ give
$$a_i = N+i \equiv 0 \pmod{p_i}, \qquad a_i = N+i \equiv 0 \pmod{q_i}.$$
Thus $a_i$ is divisible by both $p_i$ and $q_i$, which are **distinct** primes.
Therefore $a_i$ has at least two distinct prime divisors, so $a_i$ cannot be of
the form $p^k$ for any single prime $p$ and any $k\geq 1$. Hence $a_i$ is not an
 integral power of a prime.

**Conclusion.** The $n$ consecutive positive integers $N+1, N+2, \ldots, N+n$ ar
e each divisible by two distinct primes, and therefore none of them is an integr
al power of a prime number. Since $n$ was arbitrary, this holds for every positi
ve integer $n$. $\blacksquare$
