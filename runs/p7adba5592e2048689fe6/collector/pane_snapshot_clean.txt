Looking at this problem, I need to count integer solutions $(a,b)$ to $a^2 + 2b^
2 = p^{11}q^{13}$ where $p,q$ are distinct odd primes (the different letters and
 exponents indicate distinctness), given at least one solution exists.

## Proof

**Setup: arithmetic of $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-2}]$.**

The ring $R = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-2}]$ is a UFD (class number 1) with norm $N(a+b\
sqrt{-2}) = a^2 + 2b^2$ and units $\{\pm 1\}$. Each solution $(a,b)$ corresponds
 bijectively to an element $\alpha = a + b\sqrt{-2} \in R$ with $N(\alpha) = n$.
 Thus $r(n) = \#\{\alpha \in R : N(\alpha) = n\}$.

**Splitting of odd primes.** For an odd prime $\ell$, the Legendre symbol $\left
(\frac{-2}{\ell}\right)$ determines the splitting:
- $\left(\frac{-2}{\ell}\right) = 1$ (i.e., $\ell \equiv 1, 3 \pmod{8}$): $\ell$
 **splits** as $\ell = \pi_\ell \bar{\pi}_\ell$ with $N(\pi_\ell) = \ell$.
- $\left(\frac{-2}{\ell}\right) = -1$ (i.e., $\ell \equiv 5, 7 \pmod{8}$): $\ell
$ is **inert**, remaining prime in $R$ with $N(\ell) = \ell^2$.

Since $p \neq q$ are odd primes, the prime factors $\pi_p, \bar{\pi}_p, \pi_q, \
bar{\pi}_q$ (when they exist) are pairwise non-associate.

**Case analysis for $n = p^{11}q^{13}$.**

*Case 1: Both $p$ and $q$ split.* Write $p = \pi_p \bar\pi_p$, $q = \pi_q \bar\p
i_q$. By unique factorization, every $\alpha$ with $N(\alpha) = n$ has the form:
$$\alpha = u \cdot \pi_p^{a}\,\bar\pi_p^{\,11-a}\,\pi_q^{b}\,\bar\pi_q^{\,13-b}$
$
where $u \in \{+1, -1\}$, $0 \le a \le 11$, $0 \le b \le 13$. These are all dist
inct (UFD + pairwise non-associate primes). The count is:
$$r(n) = 2 \times 12 \times 14 = 336.$$

*Case 2: $p$ splits, $q$ inert.* Since $q$ is prime in $R$ with $N(q)=q^2$, any
$\alpha$ with $q \mid N(\alpha)$ must satisfy $q \mid \alpha$ (as $q \mid \alpha
\bar\alpha$ and $q$ prime forces $q \mid \alpha$; then also $q \mid \bar\alpha$)
. Writing $\alpha = q^k \beta$ with $q \nmid \beta$, we get $N(\alpha) = q^{2k}N
(\beta)$. For $q^{13} \mid N(\alpha)$ we need $2k \ge 13$, i.e., $k \ge 7$, but
then $N(\beta) = p^{11}q^{13-2k}$ has $q \mid N(\beta)$ while $q \nmid \beta$ —
contradiction since $q \nmid \beta \Rightarrow q \nmid \bar\beta \Rightarrow q \
nmid N(\beta)$. So **no solutions**.

*Case 3: $p$ inert, $q$ splits.* By the same argument with $p$: $p^{11} \mid N(\
alpha)$ requires $k \ge 6$ (since $2k \ge 11$), but $k \le 5$ (since $11 - 2k \g
e 1$ gives $p \mid N(\beta)$ with $p \nmid \beta$) — contradiction. **No solutio
ns**.

*Case 4: Both inert.* Combining both arguments: **no solutions**.

**Conclusion.** The hypothesis "at least one solution exists" forces Case 1: bot
h $p \equiv 1,3 \pmod{8}$ and $q \equiv 1,3 \pmod{8}$. The total number of integ
er solutions is:

$$r(n) = 2 \times 12 \times 14 = \boxed{336}$$
