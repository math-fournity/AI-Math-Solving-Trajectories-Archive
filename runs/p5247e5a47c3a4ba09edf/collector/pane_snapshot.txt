## Solution

Let $\mu(M)$ denote the minimum number of generators of $M$. We claim the answer
 is

$$\boxed{\mu(M)}.$$

---

### Proof

Let $R$ be the underlying ring. We are given a short exact sequence
$$0 \to K \to N \to M \to 0$$
with $N$ a free $R$-module and $M$ finitely presented. We must determine the min
imum number of generators of $N$ (i.e. the rank of the free module $N$) for whic
h the sequence is **not forced** to split.

---

**Step 1 — A lower bound: $\operatorname{rank}(N) \ge \mu(M)$.**

The map $N \to M$ is surjective. If $N$ is free on $n$ generators, then $M$ is g
enerated by the $n$ images of those generators. Hence $M$ is generated by $n$ el
ements, so
$$n \ge \mu(M).$$
Thus no free module with fewer than $\mu(M)$ generators can surject onto $M$, an
d the question is vacuous below this bound.

---

**Step 2 — The bound is attained.**

By definition of $\mu(M)$, there exists a surjection $\pi : N_0 \twoheadrightarr
ow M$ with $N_0$ free of rank $\mu(M)$. Setting $K_0 = \ker\pi$ yields a short e
xact sequence
$$0 \to K_0 \to N_0 \to M \to 0.$$
So a free module with exactly $\mu(M)$ generators does occur in such a sequence.

---

**Step 3 — The kernel is finitely generated (this is where finite presentation i
s used).**

Since $M$ is finitely presented, there exists a finite presentation
$$0 \to K_1 \to F_1 \to M \to 0$$
with $F_1$ free of finite rank and $K_1$ finitely generated. Applying **Schanuel
's lemma** to the two short exact sequences with projective middle terms $N_0$ a
nd $F_1$,
$$K_0 \oplus F_1 \cong K_1 \oplus N_0.$$
The right-hand side $K_1 \oplus N_0$ is finitely generated (both $K_1$ and $N_0$
 are). Hence $K_0 \oplus F_1$ is finitely generated, and so its direct summand $
K_0$ is finitely generated. Thus the sequence $0 \to K_0 \to N_0 \to M \to 0$ is
 itself a finite presentation of $M$. This is precisely the content of the hypot
hesis "$M$ finitely presented": it guarantees that the kernel of *any* surjectio
n from a finitely generated free module onto $M$ is finitely generated.

---

**Step 4 — The sequence does not necessarily split.**

A short exact sequence $0 \to K \to N \to M \to 0$ with $N$ free splits if and o
nly if $M$ is a direct summand of the free module $N$, i.e. if and only if $M$ i
s projective. Equivalently:

- If $M$ is projective, every such sequence splits (by the defining lifting prop
erty of projective modules).
- If $M$ is **not** projective, then $M$ cannot be a direct summand of a free mo
dule, so **no** such sequence splits — regardless of the rank of $N$.

The problem assumes only that $M$ is finitely presented, **not** that $M$ is pro
jective. Hence, in general, the sequence $0 \to K_0 \to N_0 \to M \to 0$ constru
cted in Step 2 does **not** split. The splitting is not forced; it occurs only i
n the special (excluded) case $M$ projective.

---

**Step 5 — Minimality.**

Combining Steps 1, 2, and 4:

- For $n < \mu(M)$: no surjection $N \twoheadrightarrow M$ exists, so the questi
on does not arise.
- For $n = \mu(M)$: a surjection exists (Step 2), the kernel is finitely generat
ed (Step 3, using finite presentation), and the sequence is not forced to split
(Step 4, since $M$ is not assumed projective).

Therefore the minimum number of generators of $N$ such that the sequence does no
t necessarily split is exactly

$$\mu(M),$$

the minimum number of generators of $M$.

---

**Remark.** The hypothesis that $M$ is *finitely presented* (rather than merely
finitely generated) is essential: it is exactly the condition that, by Schanuel'
s lemma, the kernel $K$ in any free presentation $0 \to K \to N \to M \to 0$ wit
h $N$ finitely generated free is itself finitely generated. The splitting behavi
or, however, is governed solely by whether $M$ is projective — a property indepe
ndent of the rank of $N$ — so the rank of $N$ enters only through the constraint
 that $N$ must surject onto $M$, giving the sharp lower bound $\mu(M)$.
