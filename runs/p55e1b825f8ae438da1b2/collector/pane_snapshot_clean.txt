## Proof

Let $C \subset [0,1]$ be the Cantor set. Recall that $C$ is constructed by itera
tively removing open middle-thirds, and its Lebesgue measure is

$$
m(C) = \lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^n = 0.
$$

The function $f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}$ is defined by $f(x)=0$ on $C$ and $f(x)=x$ o
n $[0,1]\setminus C$.

**Step 1: Measurability.** The Cantor set $C$ is closed (it is an intersection o
f closed sets), hence Borel measurable, so $[0,1]\setminus C$ is Borel measurabl
e. The functions $0$ and $x$ are continuous (hence measurable), and $f$ is defin
ed piecewise on the measurable partition $\{C,\,[0,1]\setminus C\}$ using measur
able functions. Therefore $f$ is Lebesgue measurable.

**Step 2: Split the integral.** Since $\{C,\,[0,1]\setminus C\}$ is a measurable
 partition of $[0,1]$,

$$
\int_{[0,1]} f\,du = \int_C f\,du + \int_{[0,1]\setminus C} f\,du.
$$

**Step 3: Integral over $C$.** On $C$, $f=0$, so $f\equiv 0$ on $C$ and

$$
\int_C f\,du = 0.
$$

(Equivalently, $m(C)=0$ forces this integral to vanish regardless.)

**Step 4: Integral over $[0,1]\setminus C$.** On $[0,1]\setminus C$, $f(x)=x$. S
ince $m(C)=0$, the set $[0,1]\setminus C$ has measure $1$, and modifying the int
egrand $x$ on the null set $C$ does not change the Lebesgue integral. Hence

$$
\int_{[0,1]\setminus C} x\,du = \int_{[0,1]} x\,du.
$$

The function $x\mapsto x$ is continuous on $[0,1]$, so its Lebesgue integral equ
als its Riemann integral:

$$
\int_{[0,1]} x\,du = \int_0^1 x\,dx = \frac{1}{2}.
$$

**Step 5: Combine.**

$$
\int_{[0,1]} f\,du = 0 + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2}.
$$
