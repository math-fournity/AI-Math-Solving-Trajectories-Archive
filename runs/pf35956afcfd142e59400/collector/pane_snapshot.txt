## Proof

Let $X \subset \mathbb{P}^3_\mathbb{C}$ be a smooth surface of degree $d$, and l
et $L \subset X$ be a line. We compute $L^2$ via the **adjunction formula**.

**Step 1: Adjunction.** Since $L \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ is a smooth rational curve,
 its genus is $g = 0$. The adjunction formula on $X$ gives
$$2g - 2 = L^2 + L \cdot K_X,$$
where $K_X$ is the canonical divisor of $X$. With $g = 0$:
$$-2 = L^2 + L \cdot K_X. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2: The canonical class of $X$.** By the adjunction formula for $X \subset
 \mathbb{P}^3$ (or equivalently the conormal exact sequence), the canonical divi
sor of a smooth degree-$d$ surface is
$$K_X = (d - 4)\,H,$$
where $H$ is the hyperplane class on $X$, i.e. the restriction of $\mathcal{O}_{
\mathbb{P}^3}(1)$ to $X$.

**Step 3: Compute $L \cdot K_X$.** Since $L$ is a line, a generic hyperplane mee
ts $L$ in exactly one point, so
$$L \cdot H = 1.$$
Therefore
$$L \cdot K_X = (d - 4)(L \cdot H) = (d - 4) \cdot 1 = d - 4. \tag{2}$$

**Step 4: Solve for $L^2$.** Substituting (2) into (1):
$$-2 = L^2 + (d - 4),$$
$$L^2 = -2 - (d - 4) = 2 - d.$$

**Verification against known cases:**
- $d = 1$ (plane $\mathbb{P}^2$): $L^2 = 1$. A line in $\mathbb{P}^2$ has self-i
ntersection $1$. ✓
- $d = 2$ (smooth quadric $\cong \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$): $L^2 = 0$.
Each ruling class has self-intersection $0$. ✓
- $d = 3$ (smooth cubic surface): $L^2 = -1$. The 27 lines are exceptional ($(-1
)$)-curves. ✓

All cases agree with classical surface theory.

$$\boxed{L^2 = 2 - d}$$
