## Proof

**Setup.** Let $G$ be a graph on 10 vertices (cities). We require that for every
 5-element subset $S \subseteq V(G)$, the induced subgraph $G[S]$ is connected.
(Note: if every 5-set is connected, then automatically both halves of any 5-5 pa
rtition are connected, since each half is a 5-set.) We seek the minimum number o
f edges.

Let $H = \overline{G}$ be the complement of $G$, so $|E(G)| = \binom{10}{2} - |E
(H)| = 45 - |E(H)|$. Minimizing $|E(G)|$ is equivalent to maximizing $|E(H)|$.

---

### Lower Bound: $|E(G)| \geq 30$

**Claim:** Every vertex of $G$ has degree at least 6, i.e., $\delta(G) \geq 6$.

*Proof of claim.* Suppose vertex $v$ has degree $d \leq 5$ in $G$. Then $v$ has
$9 - d \geq 4$ non-neighbors in $G$. Choose any 4 of them: $w_1, w_2, w_3, w_4$,
 and set $S = \{v, w_1, w_2, w_3, w_4\}$. In $G[S]$, vertex $v$ has no edges to
any other vertex (they are all non-neighbors), so $v$ is isolated and $G[S]$ is
disconnected. This contradicts our requirement. Hence $d \geq 6$ for every verte
x. $\square$

By the handshaking lemma:
$$|E(G)| = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{v} \deg(v) \geq \frac{1}{2} \cdot 10 \cdot 6 = 30.$$

---

### Upper Bound: Construction with 30 edges

**Construction.** Let $H$ be the **Petersen graph**, which is 3-regular on 10 ve
rtices (15 edges) and is *strongly regular* with parameters $(10, 3, 0, 1)$. Thi
s means:
- Every vertex has degree 3 (so $\Delta(H) = 3$).
- Any two **adjacent** vertices share $\lambda = 0$ common neighbors.
- Any two **non-adjacent** vertices share $\mu = 1$ common neighbor.

In particular, **any two vertices of $H$ share at most 1 common neighbor.**

Let $G = \overline{H}$, the complement of the Petersen graph. Then $G$ is 6-regu
lar on 10 vertices with $|E(G)| = 45 - 15 = 30$.

**Claim:** Every 5-element subset $S$ of $V(G)$ induces a connected subgraph.

*Proof.* Suppose for contradiction that $G[S]$ is disconnected. Then $S$ can be
partitioned into nonempty sets $A$ and $B$ with no edges of $G$ between them. Th
is means **every** edge between $A$ and $B$ is present in $H$; equivalently, $H$
 contains the complete bipartite graph $K_{|A|,\,|B|}$ on the vertex set $S = A
\cup B$.

Since $|A| + |B| = 5$ with $|A|, |B| \geq 1$, we have $\{|A|, |B|\} \in \bigl\{\
{1,4\},\; \{2,3\}\bigr\}$.

- **Case $\{1, 4\}$:** $H$ contains $K_{1,4}$, meaning some vertex of $H$ has de
gree $\geq 4$. This contradicts $\Delta(H) = 3$.

- **Case $\{2, 3\}$:** $H$ contains $K_{2,3}$, meaning two vertices of $H$ each
share 3 common neighbors. This contradicts the fact that any two vertices in the
 Petersen graph share **at most 1** common neighbor.

Both cases yield contradictions, so $G[S]$ must be connected. $\square$

Since every 5-set is connected in $G$, every 5-5 partition produces two connecte
d induced subgraphs. This construction uses exactly 30 edges.
