## Proof

The equivalence relation states $A \equiv B$ iff there exists $r \in \mathbb{Z}$
 such that $B = A + r := \{a + r : a \in A\}$ (as subsets of $\mathbb{Z}^+$). In
deed, the two conditions $\forall x \in A,\, x+r \in B$ and $\forall y \in B,\,
y-r \in A$ are equivalent to $A+r \subseteq B$ and $B \subseteq A+r$, hence $B =
 A+r$.

We determine the equivalence class $[\mathbb{Z}^+]_\equiv$, i.e., all sets $B \s
ubseteq \mathbb{Z}^+$ of the form $B = \mathbb{Z}^+ + r$ for some $r \in \mathbb
{Z}$.

**Step 1: Which $r$ are admissible?** Since $B = \{1+r,\, 2+r,\, 3+r, \ldots\}$
must satisfy $B \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^+ = \{1, 2, 3, \ldots\}$, every element $1+
r$ must be a positive integer. The smallest element of $B$ is $1+r$, so we need
$1 + r \geq 1$, i.e., $r \geq 0$.

- If $r \geq 0$: $B = \{1+r, 2+r, \ldots\} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^+$, and the reve
rse condition $\forall y \in B,\, y - r \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ holds since $y - r \ge
q (1+r) - r = 1$. ✓
- If $r < 0$, say $r = -k$ with $k \geq 1$: $B = \{1-k, 2-k, \ldots\}$ contains
$1 - k \leq 0 \notin \mathbb{Z}^+$, so $B \not\subseteq \mathbb{Z}^+$. ✗

Hence the admissible translations are exactly $r \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots\}$.

**Step 2: Distinctness.** For $r_1 \neq r_2$ with $r_1, r_2 \geq 0$, the sets $B
_{r_i} = \{1+r_i,\, 2+r_i, \ldots\}$ have distinct least elements $1+r_1$ and $1
+r_2$, so $B_{r_1} \neq B_{r_2}$.

**Step 3: Cardinality.** The map $r \mapsto \mathbb{Z}^+ + r$ is a bijection fro
m $\{0, 1, 2, \ldots\}$ onto $[\mathbb{Z}^+]_\equiv$. Therefore:

$$|[\mathbb{Z}^+]_\equiv| = |\{0, 1, 2, \ldots\}| = \aleph_0.$$
