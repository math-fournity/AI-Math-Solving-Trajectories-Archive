## Proof

**Yes, $E[X] = \infty$.**

**Setup.** Since $X_n \searrow X$ (decreasing convergence) and all variables are
 non-negative, we have $X_n \geq X \geq 0$ pointwise for every $n$. Therefore $X
_n - X \geq 0$, so the condition $|X_n - X| \leq c_n$ becomes

$$0 \leq X_n - X \leq c_n, \quad\text{i.e.,}\quad X_n \leq X + c_n. \tag{1}$$

**Key step.** Both $X_n \geq 0$ and $X + c_n \geq 0$ are non-negative random var
iables. For non-negative random variables, expectation is always well-defined (i
n $[0, \infty]$) and is **monotone**: if $0 \leq Y \leq Z$ pointwise, then $E[Y]
 \leq E[Z]$. Applying this to (1):

$$E[X_n] \leq E[X + c_n]. \tag{2}$$

By linearity of expectation for non-negative random variables (which holds in th
e extended sense, including the case $E[X] = \infty$):

$$E[X + c_n] = E[X] + c_n. \tag{3}$$

Combining (2) and (3):

$$\infty = E[X_n] \leq E[X] + c_n.$$

Since $c_n$ is a finite real number, the only way $E[X] + c_n \geq \infty$ is if
 $E[X] = \infty$.

**Conclusion.** $E[X] = \infty$.

**Remark.** The hypothesis $c_n \to 0$ is not needed for the conclusion; any fin
ite bound $c_n$ suffices. The uniform (pointwise) bound $X_n \leq X + c_n$ toget
her with $E[X_n] = \infty$ already forces $E[X] = \infty$ by monotonicity and li
nearity of expectation for non-negative random variables. The condition $c_n \to
 0$ merely confirms that $X_n \to X$ uniformly, which is consistent with—but str
onger than—the decreasing convergence already assumed.
