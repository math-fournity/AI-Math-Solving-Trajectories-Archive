## Proof

Let $p_n$ denote the $n$-th prime number. The notation $(n_p)!$ denotes the prod
uct of the first $n$ primes, i.e., the primorial
$$p_n\# \;=\; \prod_{k=1}^{n} p_k \;=\; 2\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdots p_n.$$

We must evaluate
$$L \;=\; \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{n^2}{p_n\#}.$$

**Key tool — Chebyshev's theta function.** Define
$$\theta(x) \;=\; \sum_{p\le x} \ln p,$$
the sum running over primes $p\le x$. A classical equivalent of the Prime Number
 Theorem (Hadamard–de la Vallée Poussin, 1896) states
$$\theta(x)\sim x \qquad (x\to\infty).$$

**Step 1 — Logarithm of the primorial.** Since the primes not exceeding $p_n$ ar
e exactly $p_1,\dots,p_n$,
$$\ln(p_n\#) \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{n}\ln p_k \;=\; \theta(p_n).$$

**Step 2 — Asymptotic size of $\ln(p_n\#)$.** By the PNT equivalent above,
$$\ln(p_n\#)\;=\;\theta(p_n)\;\sim\;p_n.$$
A second standard consequence of the PNT is $p_n\sim n\ln n$. Hence
$$\ln(p_n\#)\;\sim\;n\ln n\;\longrightarrow\;+\infty.$$

**Step 3 — Logarithm of the ratio.** Consider
$$\ln\!\left(\frac{n^2}{p_n\#}\right) \;=\; 2\ln n-\ln(p_n\#) \;=\; 2\ln n-\thet
a(p_n).$$
Since $\theta(p_n)\sim n\ln n$ while $2\ln n=o(n\ln n)$, we obtain
$$\ln\!\left(\frac{n^2}{p_n\#}\right) \;=\; 2\ln n-\theta(p_n)\;\longrightarrow\
;-\infty.$$

**Step 4 — Conclusion.** A sequence whose logarithm tends to $-\infty$ tends to
$0$. Therefore
$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n^2}{(n_p)!}\;=\;0.}$$
