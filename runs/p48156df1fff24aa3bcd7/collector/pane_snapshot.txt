## Proof

We prove the inequality holds for the Lebesgue integral.

**Notation.** Let $(X, \mathcal{M}, \mu)$ be a measure space, $A \in \mathcal{M}
$ a measurable set, and $f : A \to \mathbb{R}$ (or $\mathbb{C}$) a $\mu$-integra
ble function. Note that $\int_A d\mu(x) = \mu(A)$, so $\left|\int_A d\mu(x)\righ
t| = \mu(A)$ (measures are non-negative). Set $M := \sup_{x \in A} |f(x)|$.

The claimed inequality is
$$\left|\int_A f\,d\mu\right| \leq M \cdot \mu(A).$$

**Step 1 (Pointwise bound).** By definition of the supremum, for every $x \in A$
,
$$|f(x)| \leq M.$$

**Step 2 (Integrate the bound — monotonicity).** The function $|f|$ is non-negat
ive and measurable, and $|f| \leq M \cdot \mathbf{1}_A$ pointwise on $A$. By mon
otonicity of the Lebesgue integral for non-negative measurable functions,
$$\int_A |f|\,d\mu \;\leq\; \int_A M\,d\mu \;=\; M \int_A d\mu \;=\; M\cdot\mu(A
).$$

**Step 3 (Triangle inequality for integrals).** For any integrable $f$,
$$\left|\int_A f\,d\mu\right| \leq \int_A |f|\,d\mu.$$
This follows from writing $\int_A f\,d\mu = r e^{i\theta}$ (for the complex case
; the real case is similar via $f = f^+ - f^-$), so
$$\left|\int_A f\,d\mu\right| = \int_A \operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\theta}f)\,d\mu \l
eq \int_A |f|\,d\mu,$$
since $\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\theta}f) \leq |f|$ pointwise.

**Step 4 (Combine).** Chaining Steps 3 and 2:
$$\left|\int_A f\,d\mu\right| \leq \int_A |f|\,d\mu \leq M \cdot \mu(A) = \left(
\sup_{x \in A}|f(x)|\right)\cdot\left|\int_A d\mu(x)\right|.$$

**Remark on edge cases.** If $M = \infty$ (i.e., $f$ is unbounded on $A$), the r
ight-hand side is $+\infty$ (when $\mu(A)>0$) and the inequality is trivially sa
tisfied; if $\mu(A)=0$, then $\int_A f\,d\mu = 0$ by the convention that integra
ls over null sets vanish, and both sides are $0$. If $f$ is not integrable, the
left-hand side is undefined and the statement is vacuous. In all well-defined ca
ses the inequality holds. $\blacksquare$
