## Proof

**Yes**, such a $\delta > 0$ exists.

**Step 1: $\overline{NR(N)}$ is compact.**

Since $N$ is a bounded normal operator, the spectral theorem gives $\overline{NR
(N)} = \operatorname{conv}(\sigma(N))$, the convex hull of the spectrum. Since $
N \in \mathcal{B}(H)$, the spectrum $\sigma(N)$ is a compact subset of $\mathbb{
C}$. The convex hull of a compact set in $\mathbb{R}^2 \cong \mathbb{C}$ is comp
act. Hence $\overline{NR(N)}$ is compact.

**Step 2: Positive distance from $\overline{NR(N)}$ to $\partial\Gamma^+$.**

By hypothesis $\overline{NR(N)} \subseteq \Gamma^+ = \{z : \operatorname{Re}(z)
> 0\}$, which is open. The boundary is $\partial\Gamma^+ = \{z : \operatorname{R
e}(z) = 0\}$. Since $\overline{NR(N)}$ is compact and $\partial\Gamma^+$ is clos
ed, and they are disjoint, the distance
$$d := \operatorname{dist}\bigl(\overline{NR(N)},\, \partial\Gamma^+\bigr) > 0.$
$
Equivalently, $\operatorname{Re}(z) \geq d$ for every $z \in \overline{NR(N)}$,
so in particular $\operatorname{Re}\langle Nx, x\rangle \geq d$ for every unit v
ector $x$.

**Step 3: Lipschitz continuity of the numerical range.**

For any $E \in \mathcal{B}(H)$ and any unit vector $x \in H$:
$$|\langle Ex, x\rangle - \langle Nx, x\rangle| = |\langle (E-N)x, x\rangle| \le
q \|E - N\|.$$
Hence if $\|E - N\| < \delta$, then for every unit vector $x$:
$$\operatorname{Re}\langle Ex, x\rangle \geq \operatorname{Re}\langle Nx, x\rang
le - |\langle Ex,x\rangle - \langle Nx,x\rangle| \geq d - \delta.$$

Set $\delta := d/2 > 0$. Then for every $E \in B(N, \delta)$ and every unit vect

$$\operatorname{Re}\langle Ex, x\rangle \geq d - \frac{d}{2} = \frac{d}{2} > 0.$

$$\overline{NR(E)} \subseteq \{z : \operatorname{Re}(z) \geq d/2\} \subseteq \Ga
