## Proof (Key Idea and Construction of μ_f)

The theorem asserts that every monotone increasing, right-continuous function $f
:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ induces a unique Radon measure $\mu_f$ satisfying $\mu
_f((a,b])=f(b)-f(a)$. The proof proceeds by **constructing a pre-measure on the
ring generated by half-open intervals and then extending it via the Carathéodory
 extension theorem**, with right-continuity of $f$ supplying exactly the propert
y needed for countable additivity, and uniqueness following from the $\pi$-$\lam
bda$ theorem.

---

### Step 1 — Define $\mu$ on the semiring of half-open intervals

Let $\mathcal{S}=\{(a,b]:a\le b\}\cup\{\varnothing\}$. This is a semiring of sub
sets of $\mathbb{R}$. Define

$$
\mu\bigl((a,b]\bigr)=f(b)-f(a),\qquad \mu(\varnothing)=0.
$$

Since $f$ is monotone increasing, $\mu\ge 0$. Finite additivity on $\mathcal{S}$
 holds: if $(a,b]=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}(a_i,b_i]$, then ordering the endpoints sho
ws $f(b)-f(a)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(f(b_i)-f(a_i)\bigr)$.

### Step 2 — Extend to the ring $\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{S})$

The ring $\mathcal{R}$ generated by $\mathcal{S}$ consists of all finite disjoin
t unions of half-open intervals. Define $\mu$ on $\mathcal{R}$ by additivity:

$$
\mu\!\left(\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}(a_i,b_i]\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(f(b_i)-f(a_i)
\bigr).
$$

Finite additivity on $\mathcal{S}$ guarantees this is well defined (independent
of the particular disjoint decomposition). Thus $(\mathcal{R},\mu)$ is a **finit
ely additive measure on a ring**.

### Step 3 — Countable additivity on $\mathcal{R}$ (the crux; uses right-continu
ity)

We must show: if $(a,b]=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{\infty}(a_i,b_i]$, then

$$
f(b)-f(a)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\bigl(f(b_i)-f(a_i)\bigr). \tag{$\star$}
$$

**Inequality "$\ge$" (finite sub-additivity, no right-continuity needed).** For
any $N$, the finite disjoint union $\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{N}(a_i,b_i]\subseteq(a,b]$,
 so by finite additivity and monotonicity of $f$,

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(f(b_i)-f(a_i)\bigr)=\mu\!\left(\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{N}(a_i,b_i]\
right)\le f(b)-f(a).
$$

Letting $N\to\infty$ gives $\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\bigl(f(b_i)-f(a_i)\bigr)\le f(b)
-f(a)$.

**Inequality "$\le$" (requires right-continuity).** Fix $\varepsilon>0$.

- *Right-continuity of $f$ at $a$*: since $f(a+)=f(a)$ and $f$ is increasing, th
ere exists $a'>a$ with $f(a')-f(a)<\varepsilon$.
- *Right-continuity of $f$ at each $b_i$*: for each $i$, choose $b_i'>b_i$ with
$f(b_i')-f(b_i)<\varepsilon/2^{i}$.

Now $[a',b]$ is compact and

$$
[a',b]\subseteq(a,b]\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}(a_i,b_i').
$$

By compactness, finitely many open intervals $(a_i,b_i')$ cover $[a',b]$, say fo
r $i\in F\subseteq\{1,\dots,\infty\}$ finite. Then

$$
f(b)-f(a)=\bigl(f(b)-f(a')\bigr)+\bigl(f(a')-f(a)\bigr)\le\bigl(f(b)-f(a')\bigr)
+\varepsilon.
$$

Since $[a',b]\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in F}(a_i,b_i')$ and the intervals $(a_i,b_i')$
 cover $[a',b]$, a standard covering argument (ordering the intervals along $[a'
,b]$) yields

$$
f(b)-f(a')\le\sum_{i\in F}\bigl(f(b_i')-f(a_i)\bigr)\le\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\bigl(
f(b_i)-f(a_i)\bigr)+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{i}}.
$$

Combining,

$$
f(b)-f(a)\le\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\bigl(f(b_i)-f(a_i)\bigr)+2\varepsilon.
$$

Since $\varepsilon>0$ is arbitrary, ($\star$) follows. Hence $\mu$ is **countabl
y additive on $\mathcal{R}$**, i.e. a **pre-measure**.

> **Key idea summarized:** right-continuity of $f$ lets us enlarge each $(a_i,b_
i]$ slightly to the right (changing $\mu$ by an arbitrarily small amount) so tha
t the enlarged *open* intervals cover a compact subinterval $[a',b]$, where fini
te sub-cover and compactness close the gap. Monotonicity alone gives the easy in
equality; right-continuity gives the hard one.

### Step 4 — Carathéodory extension

Since $\mu$ is a $\sigma$-finite pre-measure on the ring $\mathcal{R}$ (note $\m
athbb{R}=\bigcup_{n}(-n,n]$ with $\mu((-n,n])=f(n)-f(-n)<\infty$ since $f$ is re
al-valued), the **Carathéodory extension theorem** extends $\mu$ to a complete m
easure on a $\sigma$-algebra containing $\sigma(\mathcal{R})=\mathcal{B}(\mathbb
{R})$. Restricting to $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$ gives the desired Borel measure
$\mu_f$ with $\mu_f((a,b])=f(b)-f(a)$.

### Step 5 — $\mu_f$ is a Radon measure

Because $f$ is real-valued, $\mu_f((a,b])=f(b)-f(a)<\infty$ for every bounded in
terval, so $\mu_f$ is **locally finite** (finite on compact sets). On $\mathbb{R
}$ (a locally compact, $\sigma$-compact Polish space), every Borel measure that
is finite on compact sets is **regular** (inner regular on Borel sets by compact
 subsets, outer regular by open sets). Hence $\mu_f$ is a **Radon measure**.

### Step 6 — Uniqueness (the $\pi$-$\lambda$ theorem)

The half-open intervals $\{(a,b]:a<b\}$ form a **$\pi$-system** generating $\mat
hcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$. If $\mu_f$ and $\nu$ are two Radon measures agreeing on al
l $(a,b]$, then the class $\{A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}):\mu_f(A)=\nu(A)\}$ is a
 **$\lambda$-system** containing this $\pi$-system. By **Dynkin's $\pi$-$\lambda
$ theorem**, it contains $\sigma(\mathcal{S})=\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$. The agre
ement is legitimate because both measures are **$\sigma$-finite** on the $\pi$-s
ystem ($\mathbb{R}=\bigcup_n(-n,n]$, each of finite measure). Therefore $\mu_f=\
nu$ on all of $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$, establishing **uniqueness**.

### Summary of the key idea

The construction is a **Carathéodory extension** in three layers:

1. **Define** $\mu$ on the semiring of half-open intervals by the increment $f(b
)-f(a)$ of $f$.
2. **Verify countable additivity** on the generated ring — the essential step —
where **right-continuity** of $f$ is used to pass from a countable disjoint cove
r of $(a,b]$ to a finite open cover of a compact subinterval, closing the estima
te via compactness.
3. **Extend** by Carathéodory's theorem to $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$, obtaining
a locally finite (hence Radon) measure; **uniqueness** follows from the $\pi$-$\
lambda$ theorem because half-open intervals are a $\pi$-system generating the Bo
rel $\sigma$-algebra and the measure is $\sigma$-finite.

Right-continuity is precisely the regularity condition on $f$ that makes the pre
-measure countably additive; monotonicity ensures non-negativity and the easy su
b-additivity direction. Together they are exactly what is needed for the extensi
on to go through.
