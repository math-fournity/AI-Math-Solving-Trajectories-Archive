## Proof

**Interpreted statement.** For a prime $p$, there exists a prime $q$ such that t
he congruence
$$n^{p}\equiv p\pmod q$$
has no integer solution $n$.

---

Let
$$N=\Phi_{p}(p)=1+p+p^{2}+\cdots+p^{p-1}=\frac{p^{p}-1}{p-1}.$$

**Step 1. Basic facts about $N$.** Since $p\ge 2$, we have $N\ge 3$, so $N$ has
at least one prime divisor. Also
$$N=1+p+p^{2}+\cdots+p^{p-1}\equiv 1\pmod p,$$
so $p\nmid N$; every prime divisor $q$ of $N$ satisfies $q\neq p$.

**Step 2. Every prime divisor $q$ of $N$ satisfies $q\equiv 1\pmod p$ and $\oper
atorname{ord}_{q}(p)=p$.** Let $q\mid N$. Then $q\mid p^{p}-1$, so $\operatornam
e{ord}_{q}(p)\mid p$. Since $p$ is prime, $\operatorname{ord}_{q}(p)\in\{1,p\}$.
 If $\operatorname{ord}_{q}(p)=1$, then $q\mid p-1$; but also $q\mid N$, and
$$N=1+p+\cdots+p^{p-1}\equiv 1+1+\cdots+1=p\equiv 1\pmod{p-1},$$
so $\gcd(N,p-1)=1$, contradicting $q\mid\gcd(N,p-1)$. Hence $\operatorname{ord}_
{q}(p)=p$, and consequently $p\mid q-1$, i.e. $q\equiv 1\pmod p$.

**Step 3. Reducing the problem to a congruence condition on $q$.** For a prime $
q\equiv 1\pmod p$ with $q\neq p$, the nonzero $p$-th powers in $\mathbb F_{q}^{\
,*}$ form the unique subgroup $H$ of index $p$ (of order $(q-1)/p$). An element
$a\in\mathbb F_{q}^{\,*}$ lies in $H$ iff $a^{(q-1)/p}\equiv 1\pmod q$, i.e. iff
 $\operatorname{ord}_{q}(a)\mid (q-1)/p$.

Applying this to $a=p$ and using $\operatorname{ord}_{q}(p)=p$ from Step 2:
$$p\in H\iff p\mid (q-1)/p\iff p^{2}\mid (q-1).$$
Also $n=0$ gives $0\not\equiv p\pmod q$ (since $q\nmid p$). Therefore
$$n^{p}\equiv p\pmod q\text{ has a solution}\iff p^{2}\mid (q-1).$$

**Step 4. $N$ has a prime divisor $q$ with $q\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}$.** Comput
e $N$ modulo $p^{2}$:
$$N=1+p+p^{2}+\cdots+p^{p-1}\equiv 1+p\pmod{p^{2}},$$
so $N\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}$ (since $p\ge 2$ gives $1+p\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}
}$). If every prime divisor $q$ of $N$ satisfied $q\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}$, then t
heir product $N$ would also satisfy $N\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}$, a contradiction. He
nce some prime divisor $q$ of $N$ has $q\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}$, i.e. $p^{2}\n
mid (q-1)$.

**Step 5. Conclusion.** Let $q$ be the prime divisor of $N$ furnished by Step 4.
 By Step 2, $q\equiv 1\pmod p$ and $q\neq p$; by Step 4, $p^{2}\nmid(q-1)$. By t
he equivalence of Step 3, the congruence $n^{p}\equiv p\pmod q$ has **no** solut
ion in $\mathbb Z$.

Thus for every prime $p$ there exists a prime $q$ (namely any prime divisor of $
\Phi_{p}(p)$ that is not $\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}$) such that $n^{p}\not\equiv p\pm
od q$ for all $n\in\mathbb Z$. $\blacksquare$
