I'll solve Laplace's equation in the cylinder $0\le r\le a$, $0\le\theta<2\pi$,
$0\le z\le H$ for each case using separation of variables.

## General Framework

Laplace's equation in cylindrical coordinates:
$$\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left(r\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\r
ight)+\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial\theta^2}+\frac{\partial^2 u}{\pa
rtial z^2}=0.$$

Separating $u=R(r)\Theta(\theta)Z(z)$:

- **Angular:** $\Theta''+n^2\Theta=0\;\Rightarrow\;\Theta=\cos n\theta,\;\sin n\
theta$, $n=0,1,2,\dots$ (periodicity).
- **Radial:** $r^2R''+rR'+(\lambda^2 r^2-n^2)R=0\;\Rightarrow\;R=J_n(\lambda r)$
 (regularity at $r=0$ rejects $Y_n$).
- **Axial:** $Z''-\lambda^2 Z=0$ (choosing the sign so that $Z$ is hyperbolic, c
onsistent with bounded/finite data on a finite cylinder).

**Notation.** Let $j_{n,m}$ denote the $m$-th positive zero of $J_n$ (Dirichlet
radial BC), and $j'_{n,m}$ the $m$-th positive zero of $J_n'$ (Neumann radial BC
). For $n=0$ we also have the trivial zero $j'_{0,0}=0$ giving the constant radi
al mode $J_0(0)=1$.

**Normalization integrals:**
$$\int_0^a r\,J_n^2\!\left(\tfrac{j_{n,m}}{a}r\right)dr=\frac{a^2}{2}\,J_{n+1}^2
(j_{n,m}),\qquad \int_0^a r\,J_n^2\!\left(\tfrac{j'_{n,m}}{a}r\right)dr=\frac{a^
2}{2}\!\left(1-\frac{n^2}{j_{n,m}^{\prime\,2}}\right)J_n^2(j'_{n,m}).$$

## (a) Dirichlet: $u(r,\theta,0)=\alpha(r,\theta)$, $u(r,\theta,H)=0$, $u(a,\the
ta,z)=0$

**Radial BC** $R(a)=0$: $\lambda=j_{n,m}/a$. **Axial BC** $Z(H)=0$: $Z=\sinh\!\b
ig(\tfrac{j_{n,m}}{a}(H-z)\big)$.

$$\boxed{u=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}J_n\!\left(\tfrac{j_{n,m}}{a}r\
right)\sinh\!\left(\tfrac{j_{n,m}}{a}(H-z)\right)\Big[A_{n,m}\cos n\theta+B_{n,m
}\sin n\theta\Big]}$$

Setting $z=0$ and using Fourier–Bessel orthogonality ($\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^2 n\the
ta\,d\theta=\pi$ for $n\ge1$, $=2\pi$ for $n=0$):

**For $n\ge 1$:**
$$A_{n,m}=\frac{2\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^a \alpha(r,\theta)\,J_n\!\
left(\tfrac{j_{n,m}}{a}r\right)\cos n\theta\;r\,dr\,d\theta}{\pi a^2\,J_{n+1}^2(
j_{n,m})\,\sinh\!\left(\tfrac{j_{n,m}H}{a}\right)},\quad B_{n,m}=\frac{2\display
style\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^a \alpha(r,\theta)\,J_n\!\left(\tfrac{j_{n,m}}{a}r\
right)\sin n\theta\;r\,dr\,d\theta}{\pi a^2\,J_{n+1}^2(j_{n,m})\,\sinh\!\left(\t
frac{j_{n,m}H}{a}\right)}.$$

**For $n=0$** (only cosine/constant mode, factor $2\pi$):
$$A_{0,m}=\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^a \alpha(r,\theta)\,J_0\!\l
eft(\tfrac{j_{0,m}}{a}r\right)\,r\,dr\,d\theta}{\pi a^2\,J_1^2(j_{0,m})\,\sinh\!
\left(\tfrac{j_{0,m}H}{a}\right)},\qquad B_{0,m}=0.$$

A unique solution always exists (pure Dirichlet problem).

## (b) Dirichlet with single angular mode: $u(r,\theta,0)=\alpha(r)\sin 7\theta$
, $u(r,\theta,H)=0$, $u(a,\theta,z)=0$

Only the $n=7$ sine mode is excited. The general solution of (a) collapses to:

$$\boxed{u=\sin 7\theta\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}B_{7,m}\,J_7\!\left(\tfrac{j_{7,m}}{a}
r\right)\sinh\!\left(\tfrac{j_{7,m}}{a}(H-z)\right)}$$

At $z=0$: $\alpha(r)=\sum_m B_{7,m}\sinh\!\big(\tfrac{j_{7,m}H}{a}\big)J_7\!\big
(\tfrac{j_{7,m}}{a}r\big)$, giving

$$\boxed{B_{7,m}=\frac{2\displaystyle\int_0^a \alpha(r)\,J_7\!\left(\tfrac{j_{7,
m}}{a}r\right)r\,dr}{a^2\,J_8^2(j_{7,m})\,\sinh\!\left(\tfrac{j_{7,m}H}{a}\right
)}.}$$

Unique solution always exists.

## (c) Mixed Dirichlet–Neumann: $u(r,\theta,0)=0$, $u(r,\theta,H)=\beta(r)\cos 3
\theta$, $\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}(a,\theta,z)=0$

**Radial BC** $R'(a)=0$: $\lambda=j'_{n,m}/a$ (zeros of $J_n'$). **Axial BC** $Z
(0)=0$: $Z=\sinh\!\big(\tfrac{j'_{n,m}}{a}z\big)$.

Only $n=3$, cosine mode survives. (Note: $j'_{3,0}=0$ gives $J_3(0)=0$, a trivia
l mode, so we sum over $m\ge 1$.)

$$\boxed{u=\cos 3\theta\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}A_{3,m}\,J_3\!\left(\tfrac{j'_{3,m}}{a
}r\right)\sinh\!\left(\tfrac{j'_{3,m}}{a}z\right)}$$

At $z=H$: $\beta(r)=\sum_m A_{3,m}\sinh\!\big(\tfrac{j'_{3,m}H}{a}\big)J_3\!\big
(\tfrac{j'_{3,m}}{a}r\big)$. Using the Neumann normalization:

$$\boxed{A_{3,m}=\frac{2\displaystyle\int_0^a \beta(r)\,J_3\!\left(\tfrac{j'_{3,
m}}{a}r\right)r\,dr}{a^2\!\left(1-\dfrac{9}{j_{3,m}^{\prime\,2}}\right)J_3^2(j'_
{3,m})\,\sinh\!\left(\tfrac{j'_{3,m}H}{a}\right)}.}$$

Unique solution always exists (Dirichlet data on top/bottom fixes the solution;
Neumann on the side is compatible).

## (d) Neumann in $z$ and $r$: $\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}(r,\theta,0)=\alpha
(r)\sin 3\theta$, $\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}(r,\theta,H)=0$, $\frac{\partial
 u}{\partial r}(a,\theta,z)=0$

**Radial BC** $R'(a)=0$: $\lambda=j'_{3,m}/a$. **Axial BC** $Z'(H)=0$: choose $Z
=\cosh\!\big(\tfrac{j'_{3,m}}{a}(H-z)\big)$, so $Z'(z)=-\tfrac{j'_{3,m}}{a}\sinh
\!\big(\tfrac{j'_{3,m}}{a}(H-z)\big)$ and $Z'(H)=0$. ✓

$$\boxed{u=\sin 3\theta\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}C_{3,m}\,J_3\!\left(\tfrac{j'_{3,m}}{a
}r\right)\cosh\!\left(\tfrac{j'_{3,m}}{a}(H-z)\right)}$$

Compute $\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}\big|_{z=0}$:
$$\alpha(r)\sin 3\theta=-\sin 3\theta\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}C_{3,m}\,\frac{j'_{3,m}}
{a}\,J_3\!\left(\tfrac{j'_{3,m}}{a}r\right)\sinh\!\left(\tfrac{j'_{3,m}H}{a}\rig
ht).$$

Expanding $\alpha(r)$ in the Neumann–Bessel basis $J_3(j'_{3,m}r/a)$:

$$\boxed{C_{3,m}=\frac{-\,2a\displaystyle\int_0^a \alpha(r)\,J_3\!\left(\tfrac{j
'_{3,m}}{a}r\right)r\,dr}{j'_{3,m}\,a^2\!\left(1-\dfrac{9}{j_{3,m}^{\prime\,2}}\
right)J_3^2(j'_{3,m})\,\sinh\!\left(\tfrac{j'_{3,m}H}{a}\right)}.}$$

**Solvability:** This is a pure Neumann problem (all boundaries have Neumann con
ditions). The compatibility condition $\oint_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial u}{\
partial n}\,dS=0$ requires
$$-\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^a \alpha(r)\sin 3\theta\;r\,dr\,d\theta + 0 + 0 = 0.$
$
Since $\int_0^{2\pi}\sin 3\theta\,d\theta=0$, this is **automatically satisfied*
*. The solution is unique up to an additive constant.

## (e) General pure Neumann: $\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}(r,\theta,0)=\alpha(r
,\theta)$, $\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}(r,\theta,H)=0$, $\frac{\partial u}{\pa
rtial r}(a,\theta,z)=0$

All angular modes $n\ge 0$ are present. Radial eigenfunctions $J_n(j'_{n,m}r/a)$
; axial $Z=\cosh\!\big(\tfrac{j'_{n,m}}{a}(H-z)\big)$.

$$\boxed{u=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}J_n\!\left(\tfrac{j'_{n,m}}{a}r
\right)\cosh\!\left(\tfrac{j'_{n,m}}{a}(H-z)\right)\Big[A_{n,m}\cos n\theta+B_{n
,m}\sin n\theta\Big]}$$

(The $n=0,m=0$ constant mode $J_0(0)=1$ with $Z''=0$, $Z'(H)=0\Rightarrow Z=\tex
t{const}$, $Z'=0$ is excluded from the sum since it contributes nothing to $\par
tial u/\partial z$; it represents the undetermined additive constant.)

From $\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}\big|_{z=0}=-\sum_{n,m}\frac{j'_{n,m}}{a}\sin
h\!\big(\tfrac{j'_{n,m}H}{a}\big)J_n\!\big(\tfrac{j'_{n,m}}{a}r\big)[A_{n,m}\cos
 n\theta+B_{n,m}\sin n\theta]=\alpha(r,\theta)$:

**For $n\ge 1$:**
$$A_{n,m}=\frac{-\,2a\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^a \alpha(r,\theta)\,J_
n\!\left(\tfrac{j'_{n,m}}{a}r\right)\cos n\theta\;r\,dr\,d\theta}{\pi a^2\,j'_{n
,m}\!\left(1-\dfrac{n^2}{j_{n,m}^{\prime\,2}}\right)J_n^2(j'_{n,m})\,\sinh\!\lef
t(\tfrac{j'_{n,m}H}{a}\right)},$$

$$B_{n,m}=\frac{-\,2a\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^a \alpha(r,\theta)\,J_
n\!\left(\tfrac{j'_{n,m}}{a}r\right)\sin n\theta\;r\,dr\,d\theta}{\pi a^2\,j'_{n
,m}\!\left(1-\dfrac{n^2}{j_{n,m}^{\prime\,2}}\right)J_n^2(j'_{n,m})\,\sinh\!\lef
t(\tfrac{j'_{n,m}H}{a}\right)}.$$

**For $n=0$:**
$$A_{0,m}=\frac{-\,a\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^a \alpha(r,\theta)\,J_0
\!\left(\tfrac{j'_{0,m}}{a}r\right)\,r\,dr\,d\theta}{\pi a^2\,j'_{0,m}\,J_0^2(j'
_{0,m})\,\sinh\!\left(\tfrac{j'_{0,m}H}{a}\right)},\qquad m\ge 1.$$

## Solvability Condition

Cases **(a)**, **(b)**, **(c)** involve Dirichlet data on at least one $z$-face,
 so solutions **always exist and are unique** — no condition required.

Cases **(d)** and **(e)** are **pure Neumann problems** (Neumann on all three bo
undary surfaces: $z=0$, $z=H$, $r=a$). By the divergence theorem applied to $\na
bla^2 u=0$:
$$\oint_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\,dS=\int_{\Omega}\nabla^2
u\,dV=0.$$

Evaluating each face (outward normals: bottom $-z$, top $+z$, side $+r$):
- **Bottom** ($z=0$): $\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=-\alpha$,
- **Top** ($z=H$): $\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=0$,
- **Side** ($r=a$): $\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=0$.

Hence the **solvability condition** is:

$$\boxed{\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^a \alpha(r,\theta)\;r\,dr\,d\theta=0.}$$

**For (d):** $\alpha(r)\sin 3\theta$ satisfies this automatically since $\int_0^
{2\pi}\sin 3\theta\,d\theta=0$. Solution exists, unique up to additive constant.

**For (e):** The condition $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^a \alpha(r,\the
ta)\,r\,dr\,d\theta=0$ (i.e., the zero-mean / net-flux condition) must be impose
d. Equivalently, the $n=0$, $m=0$ (constant) Fourier–Bessel coefficient of $\alp
ha$ must vanish, since that mode cannot be represented by any $z$-derivative of
a harmonic function. When satisfied, the solution exists and is **unique up to a
n additive constant** (the $n=0,m=0$ null mode).
