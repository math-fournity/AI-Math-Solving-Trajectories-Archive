## Proof

**Key observation:** $f$ has degree 2014 with leading coefficient 2014, and $g$
has degree 2013 with leading coefficient 2014. The coefficients $c_i$ of $g$ are
 arbitrary — the answer will be independent of them.

**Partial fraction decomposition.** Since $\deg g = 2013 < 2014 = \deg f$, the r
ational function $\frac{g(x)}{f(x)}$ admits the partial fraction decomposition

$$\frac{g(x)}{f(x)} = \sum_{k=1}^{2014} \frac{g(a_k)}{f'(a_k)} \cdot \frac{1}{x
- a_k},$$

where we used $f(a_k) = 0$ and the standard residue formula: the coefficient of
$\frac{1}{x-a_k}$ is $\frac{g(a_k)}{f'(a_k)}$.

**Extracting the sum via asymptotics.** Multiply both sides by $x$ and take $x \
to \infty$.

**Left side:**
$$x \cdot \frac{g(x)}{f(x)} = x \cdot \frac{2014\,x^{2013} + \text{(lower order)
}}{2014\,x^{2014} + \text{(lower order)}} \;\longrightarrow\; x \cdot \frac{1}{x
} = 1.$$

**Right side:**
$$\sum_{k=1}^{2014} \frac{g(a_k)}{f'(a_k)} \cdot \frac{x}{x - a_k} \;\longrighta
rrow\; \sum_{k=1}^{2014} \frac{g(a_k)}{f'(a_k)} \cdot 1 = \sum_{k=1}^{2014} \fra
c{g(a_k)}{f'(a_k)},$$

since $\frac{x}{x-a_k} \to 1$ for each fixed $k$ as $x \to \infty$.

**Equating both limits:**
$$\sum_{n=1}^{2014} \frac{g(a_n)}{f'(a_n)} = 1.$$

**Verification with a small case.** Let $f(x) = 2(x-1)(x-2) = 2x^2 - 6x + 4$ (de
gree 2, leading coeff 2) and $g(x) = 2x + c_1$ (degree 1, leading coeff 2). Then
 $f'(x) = 4x - 6$, so $f'(1) = -2$, $f'(2) = 2$, $g(1) = 2 + c_1$, $g(2) = 4 + c
_1$. The sum is
$$\frac{2+c_1}{-2} + \frac{4+c_1}{2} = -1 - \tfrac{c_1}{2} + 2 + \tfrac{c_1}{2}
= 1,$$
confirming the result is independent of $c_1$. ✓
