# Problem

A city planning commission is developing a square district of size 1 by 1. Within this district, exactly $n$ specific coordinates have been designated as "Protected Landmarks." These $n$ landmarks are all located strictly inside the district's boundaries.

The commission intends to cover every single point within the district that is not a landmark with a set of $k$ rectangular parks. To ensure the project is valid, the following regulations must be met:
1. Every park must be rectangular, and its sides must be perfectly aligned with the North-South and East-West boundaries of the district.
2. No landmark can be located in the interior of any park (though a landmark may sit exactly on a park's boundary).
3. Every point within the interior of the district that is not one of the $n$ landmarks must be contained within the interior of at least one of the $k$ parks.

Determine the smallest positive integer $k$, expressed in terms of $n$, that guarantees such a set of parks can be constructed regardless of where the $n$ landmarks are positioned.
