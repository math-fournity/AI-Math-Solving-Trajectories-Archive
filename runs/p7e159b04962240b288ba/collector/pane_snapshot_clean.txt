## Solution

**Answer:** $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_n^{k+1}}{n^k}=\left(\frac{k+1
}{k}\right)^k$.

### Proof

**Step 1: $a_n\to\infty$.**

Since $a_{n+1}-a_n=a_n^{-1/k}>0$, the sequence $(a_n)$ is strictly increasing. S
uppose for contradiction that it is bounded, hence convergent to some $L>0$. The
n $a_{n+1}-a_n\to L^{-1/k}>0$, which contradicts the fact that the differences o
f a convergent sequence tend to $0$. Therefore $a_n\to\infty$.

**Step 2: Set up the transformed sequence.**

Define $b_n = a_n^{(k+1)/k}$, so $b_n\to\infty$. We compute the increment of $b_
n$. From
$$a_{n+1}=a_n+a_n^{-1/k}=a_n\bigl(1+a_n^{-(k+1)/k}\bigr),$$
raising to the power $(k+1)/k$ gives
$$b_{n+1}=b_n\bigl(1+x_n\bigr)^{(k+1)/k},\qquad x_n:=a_n^{-(k+1)/k}=\frac{1}{b_n
}.$$
Hence
$$b_{n+1}-b_n=b_n\Bigl[(1+x_n)^{(k+1)/k}-1\Bigr].$$

**Step 3: Asymptotics of the increment.**

Since $x_n=1/b_n\to 0$, we use the Taylor expansion $(1+x)^{(k+1)/k}=1+\frac{k+1
}{k}x+O(x^2)$ as $x\to 0$. Substituting,
$$b_{n+1}-b_n=b_n\left[\frac{k+1}{k}\,x_n+O(x_n^2)\right]=\frac{k+1}{k}\,b_n x_n
+O(b_n x_n^2)=\frac{k+1}{k}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{b_n}\right).$$
Because $b_n\to\infty$, the error term vanishes, and
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}(b_{n+1}-b_n)=\frac{k+1}{k}.$$

**Step 4: Apply Stolz–Cesàro.**

Since $b_n\to\infty$ and $b_{n+1}-b_n\to\frac{k+1}{k}$, the Stolz–Cesàro theorem
 yields
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{b_n}{n}=\frac{k+1}{k}.$$

**Step 5: Conclude.**

Since $a_n^{k+1}=b_n^{\,k}$, we have
$$\frac{a_n^{k+1}}{n^k}=\left(\frac{b_n}{n}\right)^k\longrightarrow\left(\frac{k
+1}{k}\right)^k.$$

Therefore
$$\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_n^{k+1}}{n^k}=\left(\frac{k+1}{k}
\right)^k.}$$
