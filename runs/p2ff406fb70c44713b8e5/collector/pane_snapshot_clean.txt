Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a single finite-dimensional
 Lie algebra can be the Lie algebra of infinitely many non-isomorphic compact co
nnected Lie groups.

**Answer: No.** For every finite-dimensional Lie algebra $L$, there are only fin
itely many non-isomorphic compact connected Lie groups with Lie algebra isomorph
ic to $L$.

---

## Proof

### Step 1: Reduction to compact Lie algebras

A compact Lie group admits a bi-invariant Haar measure, which yields an $\mathrm
{Ad}$-invariant inner product on its Lie algebra. This forces the Killing form t
o be negative semidefinite, i.e., the Lie algebra is a **compact Lie algebra**.
Hence, if $L$ is not compact, there are *zero* compact connected Lie groups with
 Lie algebra $L$. We may assume $L$ is compact.

### Step 2: Structure of the simply connected cover

Every compact Lie algebra decomposes as a direct sum of ideals:
$$L = \mathfrak{z} \oplus \mathfrak{s}, \qquad \mathfrak{z} = Z(L), \quad \mathf
rak{s} = [L, L],$$
where $\mathfrak{z} \cong \mathbb{R}^k$ is abelian ($k = \dim \mathfrak{z}$) and
 $\mathfrak{s}$ is compact semisimple. Let $\widetilde{G}$ be the simply connect
ed Lie group with Lie algebra $L$, and $\widetilde{S}$ the simply connected comp
act group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{s}$. Then:
$$\widetilde{G} = \mathbb{R}^k \times \widetilde{S}, \qquad Z(\widetilde{G}) = \
mathbb{R}^k \times F, \quad F := Z(\widetilde{S}).$$
Since $\widetilde{S}$ is compact and simply connected semisimple, its center $F$
 is **finite**.

### Step 3: Classification of connected Lie groups with Lie algebra $L$

Every connected Lie group with Lie algebra $L$ is isomorphic to $\widetilde{G}/\
Gamma$, where $\Gamma$ is a discrete subgroup of $Z(\widetilde{G})$. Two such qu
otients $\widetilde{G}/\Gamma_1$ and $\widetilde{G}/\Gamma_2$ are isomorphic (as
 Lie groups) if and only if there exists $\varphi \in \mathrm{Aut}(\widetilde{G}
)$ with $\varphi(\Gamma_1) = \Gamma_2$.

### Step 4: Compactness condition

Since $\widetilde{S}$ is already compact, $\widetilde{G}/\Gamma$ is compact if a
nd only if the projection of $\Gamma$ onto $\mathbb{R}^k$ is a **full lattice**
$\Lambda \cong \mathbb{Z}^k$ in $\mathbb{R}^k$.

Let $\Gamma_0 = \Gamma \cap (\{0\} \times F) = \ker(\pi_1|_\Gamma)$, a subgroup
of the finite group $F$. The projection $\pi_1: \Gamma \to \Lambda \cong \mathbb
{Z}^k$ is surjective with finite kernel $\Gamma_0$. Since $\mathbb{Z}^k$ is free
, this extension **splits**: choosing a section $s: \Lambda \to \Gamma$ with $s(
\lambda) = (\lambda, \sigma(\lambda))$ for a homomorphism $\sigma: \Lambda \to F
$, we get:
$$\Gamma = \{(\lambda,\, \sigma(\lambda)\,\gamma_0) : \lambda \in \Lambda,\; \ga
mma_0 \in \Gamma_0\}.$$
Thus $\Gamma$ is completely determined by the triple $(\Lambda, \Gamma_0, \sigma
)$ where $\Gamma_0 \leq F$ and $\sigma \in \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^k, F)$.

### Step 5: Finiteness of isomorphism classes

The automorphism group of $\widetilde{G}$ is:
$$\mathrm{Aut}(\widetilde{G}) \cong \mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{R}^k) \times \mathrm{Aut
}(\widetilde{S}),$$
since $L = \mathfrak{z} \oplus \mathfrak{s}$ is a direct sum of ideals. An eleme
nt $(A, \alpha)$ acts on a triple $(\Lambda, \Gamma_0, \sigma)$ by:
$$(A, \alpha) \cdot (\Lambda, \Gamma_0, \sigma) = (A\Lambda,\; \bar\alpha(\Gamma
_0),\; \bar\alpha \circ \sigma \circ A^{-1}),$$
where $\bar\alpha$ is the induced action of $\alpha$ on $F = Z(\widetilde{S})$.

Since $\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{R}^k)$ acts **transitively** on full lattices in $\ma
thbb{R}^k$, we may fix $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}^k$. The stabilizer is $\mathrm{GL}(
k, \mathbb{Z})$. The isomorphism classes are then in bijection with orbits of:
$$\mathrm{GL}(k, \mathbb{Z}) \times \mathrm{Aut}(\widetilde{S})$$
acting on the set:
$$\mathcal{D} = \{(\Gamma_0, \sigma) : \Gamma_0 \leq F,\; \sigma \in \mathrm{Hom
}(\mathbb{Z}^k, F)\}.$$

Now we count:
- **Subgroups of $F$**: $F$ is finite, so there are finitely many subgroups $\Ga
mma_0 \leq F$.
- **Homomorphisms** $\sigma: \mathbb{Z}^k \to F$: determined by $(\sigma(e_1), \
ldots, \sigma(e_k)) \in F^k$, so $|\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^k, F)| = |F|^k$, whic
h is **finite**.

Therefore $|\mathcal{D}|$ is finite, and a finite group acting on a finite set p
roduces **finitely many orbits**.

### Conclusion

For any finite-dimensional Lie algebra $L$:
- If $L$ is not compact: zero compact connected Lie groups.
- If $L$ is compact: finitely many isomorphism classes of compact connected Lie
groups (by Step 5).

In particular, there is **no** finite-dimensional Lie algebra $L$ for which infi
nitely many non-isomorphic compact connected Lie groups have Lie algebra isomorp
hic to $L$. $\blacksquare$
