## Proof

**Setup.** Three nodes A, B, C form a complete graph (triangle). A packet starts
 at A. At each of 6 steps it moves from its current node to one of the two adjac
ent nodes, each chosen with probability 1/2.

**State reduction.** By symmetry, only two states matter: being at A, or being a
t "not-A" (B or C). Let $p_n$ = P(at A after $n$ steps), $q_n$ = P(not at A afte
r $n$ steps) = $1 - p_n$.

**Transition rule.**
- From A: both neighbors are not-A, so the next state is not-A with probability
1.
- From not-A: one of the two neighbors is A (probability 1/2), the other is not-
A (probability 1/2).

Hence:
$$p_{n+1} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,q_n = \tfrac{1}{2}(1 - p_n), \qquad p_0 = 1.$$

**Solving the recurrence.** The fixed point satisfies $p = \tfrac{1}{2}(1-p)$, g
iving $p^* = \tfrac{1}{3}$. Writing $p_n = p^* + r_n$:
$$r_{n+1} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\,r_n, \qquad r_0 = 1 - \tfrac{1}{3} = \tfrac{2}{3}.$$
So $r_n = \tfrac{2}{3}\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^n$, and
$$p_n = \tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{2}{3}\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^n.$$

**Substituting $n = 6$:**
$$p_6 = \tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{2}{3}\cdot\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^6 = \tfrac{1}
{3} + \tfrac{2}{3}\cdot\tfrac{1}{64} = \tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{2}{192} = \tfrac{1}
{3} + \tfrac{1}{96} = \tfrac{32}{96} + \tfrac{1}{96} = \tfrac{33}{96} = \tfrac{1
1}{32}.$$

**Verification by direct iteration:**

| $n$ | $p_n$ | $q_n$ |
|-----|-------|-------|
| 0 | 1 | 0 |
| 1 | 0 | 1 |
| 2 | 1/2 | 1/2 |
| 3 | 1/4 | 3/4 |
| 4 | 3/8 | 5/8 |
| 5 | 5/16 | 11/16 |
| 6 | 11/32 | 21/32 |

Each row satisfies $p_{n+1} = \tfrac{1}{2}q_n$ and $q_{n+1} = p_n + \tfrac{1}{2}
q_n$, and $p_n + q_n = 1$ throughout. At $n=6$: $p_6 = \tfrac{11}{32}$, $q_6 = \
tfrac{21}{32}$, and $\tfrac{11}{32}+\tfrac{21}{32}=1$. ✓

**Conclusion.** The probability that the packet is back at Node A after the sixt
h transfer is $\boxed{\dfrac{11}{32}}$.
