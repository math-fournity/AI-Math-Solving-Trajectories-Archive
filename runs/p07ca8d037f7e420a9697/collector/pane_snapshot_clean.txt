## Proof

**Construction.** Define \(f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\) by
\[
f(x)=\begin{cases} 1, & x\in\mathbb{Q},\\ -1, & x\notin\mathbb{Q}. \end{cases}
\]

**Image of \(f\).** Since \(f\) takes only the values \(1\) and \(-1\), and both
 are attained (e.g. \(f(0)=1\), \(f(\sqrt2)=-1\)),
\[
\operatorname{Im}(f)=\{-1,1\}.
\]

**Verification of property \(P\).** Let \(x,y\in\mathbb{R}\), \(x\neq y\). We sh
ow \(\dfrac{f(x)-f(y)}{x-y}\notin\{-1,1\}\).

*Case 1: \(x,y\) are both rational or both irrational.* Then \(f(x)=f(y)\), so
\[
\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{x-y}=0\notin\{-1,1\}.
\]

*Case 2: \(x,y\) are of different types* (one rational, one irrational). Then \(
|f(x)-f(y)|=2\), and \(x-y\) is irrational (a rational minus an irrational, or v
ice versa, is irrational), hence in particular \(x-y\neq 0\). The slope is
\[
\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{x-y}=\frac{\pm 2}{x-y}.
\]
This equals \(1\) only if \(x-y=\pm 2\), and equals \(-1\) only if \(x-y=\mp 2\)
; but \(x-y\) is irrational, so \(x-y\neq \pm 2\). Therefore
\[
\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{x-y}\notin\{-1,1\}=\operatorname{Im}(f).
\]

Thus property \(P\) holds for all \(x\neq y\).

**Discontinuity at every point.** Let \(a\in\mathbb{R}\) and let \(\delta>0\). E
very interval \((a-\delta,a+\delta)\) contains both rational and irrational numb
ers, so within any neighborhood of \(a\) the function takes both values \(1\) an
d \(-1\). Hence \(f\) cannot be continuous at \(a\) (the limit cannot equal any
single value, in particular cannot equal \(f(a)\)). Since \(a\) was arbitrary, \
(f\) is discontinuous at every point of \(\mathbb{R}\).

**Conclusion.** The function \(f(x)=1\) for \(x\in\mathbb{Q}\) and \(f(x)=-1\) f
or \(x\notin\mathbb{Q}\) satisfies property \(P\) and is discontinuous at every
point of \(\mathbb{R}\).
