## Construction of the Covariant Derivative from Parallel Transport

### The core difficulty

On a curved manifold (or even in curvilinear coordinates on flat space), the vec
tors $V^i(q)$ and $V^i(q+dq)$ live in **different tangent spaces** $T_qM$ and $T
_{q+dq}M$. The naive difference $V^i(q+dq) - V^i(q)$ is therefore not a geometri
cally meaningful object — one cannot subtract vectors attached to different poin
ts. Parallel transport is precisely the tool that maps a vector from one tangent
 space to a neighboring one, making the subtraction legitimate.

### Step 1 — The parallel-transport equation

A vector $V^i$ is *parallel transported* along a curve $q^i(\lambda)$ when it sa
tisfies
$$
\frac{DV^i}{D\lambda} \;=\; \frac{dV^i}{d\lambda} + \Gamma^i_{\;jk}\,V^k\,\frac{
dq^j}{d\lambda} \;=\; 0,
$$
where $\Gamma^i_{\;jk}$ are the connection coefficients. This is the defining eq
uation of parallel transport: the vector "does not change" relative to the conne
ction.

### Step 2 — Transport along a single coordinate direction

To define $V^i_{\;;j}$ we transport along the coordinate line in the $q^j$-direc
tion only, i.e. $q^j$ varies by $dq^j$ while all other $q^k$ ($k\neq j$) are hel
d fixed. Using $dq^j$ itself as the curve parameter, $dq^k/d\lambda = \delta^k_{
\;j}$, the transport equation reduces to
$$
\frac{dV^i}{dq^j} \;=\; -\,\Gamma^i_{\;jk}\,V^k .
$$

### Step 3 — First-order integration of the transport

Starting from the value $V^i(q)$ at the base point and integrating to first orde
r in $dq^j$ gives the **parallel-transported value at the neighboring point**:
$$
\widetilde{V}^{\,i}(q+dq^j) \;=\; V^i(q) \;-\; \Gamma^i_{\;jk}(q)\,V^k(q)\,dq^j
\;+\; O\!\big((dq^j)^2\big).
$$
The tilde distinguishes this transported vector from the field's actual value at
 $q+dq^j$. By construction $\widetilde{V}^{\,i}(q+dq^j)\in T_{q+dq}M$.

### Step 4 — The actual field value at the neighboring point

Taylor-expanding the vector field itself,
$$
V^i(q+dq^j) \;=\; V^i(q) \;+\; \partial_j V^i(q)\,dq^j \;+\; O\!\big((dq^j)^2\bi
g),
$$
and this too lies in $T_{q+dq}M$.

### Step 5 — The well-defined difference

Both $V^i(q+dq^j)$ and $\widetilde{V}^{\,i}(q+dq^j)$ now reside in the *same* ta
ngent space $T_{q+dq}M$, so their difference is a genuine vector. Subtracting:
$$
V^i(q+dq^j) - \widetilde{V}^{\,i}(q+dq^j)
\;=\; \Big[\partial_j V^i(q) + \Gamma^i_{\;jk}(q)\,V^k(q)\Big]\,dq^j \;+\; O\!\b
ig((dq^j)^2\big).
$$
The $V^i(q)$ pieces cancel: the parallel-transport contribution $-\Gamma^i_{\;jk
}V^k\,dq^j$ removes the "spurious" change of components that is merely due to th
e twisting of the basis, leaving only the *intrinsic* change of the vector field
.

### Step 6 — The limiting procedure

Dividing by $dq^j$ and taking the limit defines the **covariant derivative**:
$$
\boxed{\;V^i_{\;;j} \;\equiv\; \lim_{dq^j\to 0}\frac{V^i(q+dq^j)\;-\;\widetilde{
V}^{\,i}(q+dq^j)}{dq^j}
\;=\; \partial_j V^i \;+\; \Gamma^i_{\;jk}\,V^k\; } .
$$

### Interpretation of the problem's notation

The problem writes the limit as $\big[V^i(q_j+dq_j)-V^i(q_j)\big]/dq^j$ with "$V
^i(q_j+dq_j)$ obtained by parallel transporting $V^i(q_j)$." Read literally this
 would yield only the transport term $-\Gamma^i_{\;jk}V^k$. The geometrically me
aningful construction — and the one that produces the covariant derivative — is
the comparison just derived: **the actual field value at the new point minus the
 parallel-transported old value**, both now in the same tangent space. The role
of parallel transport in the formula is to *bring $V^i(q)$ to $q+dq^j$* so that
the subtraction is well defined; the residual, non-transport change is precisely
 what the covariant derivative measures.

### Verification of tensor character

The result $V^i_{\;;j}=\partial_j V^i+\Gamma^i_{\;jk}V^k$ transforms as a $(1,1)
$-tensor even though neither $\partial_j V^i$ nor $\Gamma^i_{\;jk}V^k$ does so i
ndividually. This is guaranteed by the construction: the limit was built from th
e coordinate-invariant operation "compare field with its parallel-transported se
lf," so its components must transform tensorially. The inhomogeneous terms in th
e transformation of $\partial_j V^i$ and of $\Gamma^i_{\;jk}$ cancel exactly — w
hich is the whole point of introducing the connection.

### Summary

Parallel transport supplies the canonical identification of neighboring tangent
spaces. Using it to carry $V^i(q)$ to $q+dq^j$, subtracting from the field's act
ual value there, dividing by $dq^j$, and taking the limit yields
$$
V^i_{\;;j} \;=\; \frac{\partial V^i}{\partial q^j} \;+\; \Gamma^i_{\;jk}\,V^k,
$$
the covariant derivative of $V^i$ along $q^j$.
