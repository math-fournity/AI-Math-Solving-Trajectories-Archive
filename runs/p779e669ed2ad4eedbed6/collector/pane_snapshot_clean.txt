## Proof

We compute $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cts}}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}, \mathbb{Z})$.

**Key facts.**
- $\hat{\mathbb{Z}} = \varprojlim_n \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is a **compact** (pr
ofinite) topological group.
- $\mathbb{Z}$ carries the **discrete** topology.

**Argument.** Let $f \colon \hat{\mathbb{Z}} \to \mathbb{Z}$ be a continuous hom
omorphism.

Since $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is compact and $f$ is continuous, the image
$$f(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}) \subseteq \mathbb{Z}$$
is a **compact** subset of $\mathbb{Z}$. But $\mathbb{Z}$ is discrete, so every
compact subset of $\mathbb{Z}$ is **finite**. Moreover, $f(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})$ is
 a subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}$ (being the image of a group homomorphism). The only
finite subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}$ is the trivial subgroup $\{0\}$.

Therefore $f(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}) = \{0\}$, i.e. $f$ is the zero homomorphism.

**Conclusion.** The only continuous homomorphism $\hat{\mathbb{Z}} \to \mathbb{Z
}$ is the trivial one, so

$$\boxed{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cts}}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}},\, \mathbb{Z}) = \{0\}.}$$

(Equivalently: a compact group admits no nontrivial continuous homomorphism into
 a torsion-free discrete group, since the image must be both compact-hence-finit
e and a subgroup of a torsion-free group.)
