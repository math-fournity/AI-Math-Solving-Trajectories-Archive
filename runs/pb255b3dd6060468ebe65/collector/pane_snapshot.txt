I'll solve this ODE by reducing it to the Airy equation via a standard transform
ation.

## Problem

Solve \(y'' - 2xy' + (x+1)^2 y = 0\), on \(4 \le x \le 19\).

## Step 1: Remove the first-derivative term

Write the equation in standard form
\[
y'' + P(x)y' + Q(x)y = 0,\qquad P(x)=-2x,\quad Q(x)=(x+1)^2.
\]

Use the substitution \(y = u\,\exp\!\bigl(-\tfrac12\!\int P\,dx\bigr)\). Since
\[
-\tfrac12\int P\,dx = -\tfrac12\int(-2x)\,dx = \tfrac{x^{2}}{2},
\]
set
\[
\boxed{\,y(x)=e^{x^{2}/2}\,u(x)\,}.
\]

The reduced (normal) form is \(u'' + \widetilde Q(x)\,u = 0\) with
\[
\widetilde Q = Q - \tfrac{P'}{2} - \tfrac{P^{2}}{4}.
\]
Here \(P'=-2\), \(P^{2}/4 = x^{2}\), \(Q=(x+1)^{2}=x^{2}+2x+1\), so
\[
\widetilde Q = (x^{2}+2x+1) - \tfrac{-2}{2} - x^{2} = x^{2}+2x+1+1-x^{2}=2(x+1).
\]

**Verification of the reduction.** With \(y=e^{x^{2}/2}u\):
\[
y'=e^{x^{2}/2}(u'+xu),\qquad
y''=e^{x^{2}/2}\bigl(u''+2xu'+(1+x^{2})u\bigr).
\]
Substituting,
\[
y''-2xy'+(x+1)^{2}y
= e^{x^{2}/2}\bigl[u''+2xu'+(1+x^{2})u-2x(u'+xu)+(x+1)^{2}u\bigr]
\]
\[
= e^{x^{2}/2}\bigl[u''+\bigl(1+x^{2}-2x^{2}+x^{2}+2x+1\bigr)u\bigr]
= e^{x^{2}/2}\bigl[u''+2(x+1)u\bigr].
\]
Hence the original equation is equivalent to
\[
\boxed{\,u'' + 2(x+1)\,u = 0\,}. \tag{1}
\]

## Step 2: Reduce (1) to the Airy equation

Let \(t = x+1\) (so \(dt/dx=1\)); equation (1) becomes
\[
\frac{d^{2}u}{dt^{2}} + 2t\,u = 0. \tag{2}
\]

Introduce the scaled variable
\[
\boxed{\,z = -\,2^{1/3}\,t = -\,2^{1/3}(x+1)\,}.
\]
Then \(t = -z\,2^{-1/3}\), and by the chain rule
\[
\frac{du}{dt} = -2^{1/3}\frac{du}{dz},\qquad
\frac{d^{2}u}{dt^{2}} = 2^{2/3}\frac{d^{2}u}{dz^{2}}.
\]
Substituting into (2):
\[
2^{2/3}\,u_{zz} + 2\bigl(-z\,2^{-1/3}\bigr)u = 0
\;\Longrightarrow\;
2^{2/3}\bigl(u_{zz} - z\,u\bigr)=0,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{\,\frac{d^{2}u}{dz^{2}} - z\,u = 0\,}. \tag{3}
\]

Equation (3) is precisely the **Airy equation**, whose two linearly independent
solutions are the Airy functions \(\operatorname{Ai}(z)\) and \(\operatorname{Bi
}(z)\). Therefore
\[
u(z) = C_{1}\,\operatorname{Ai}(z) + C_{2}\,\operatorname{Bi}(z),
\qquad z = -2^{1/3}(x+1).
\]

## Step 3: General solution

Reverting the substitutions \(u\mapsto y\) via \(y=e^{x^{2}/2}u\) and \(z=-2^{1/
3}(x+1)\):

\[
\boxed{\;
y(x) = e^{x^{2}/2}\Bigl[\,C_{1}\,\operatorname{Ai}\!\bigl(-2^{1/3}(x+1)\bigr)
+ C_{2}\,\operatorname{Bi}\!\bigl(-2^{1/3}(x+1)\bigr)\Bigr],\quad C_{1},C_{2}\in
\mathbb{R}.
\;}
\]

## Verification

The Airy functions satisfy \(\operatorname{Ai}''(z)=z\operatorname{Ai}(z)\) and
\(\operatorname{Bi}''(z)=z\operatorname{Bi}(z)\), so \(u_{zz}-zu=0\) is satisfie
d by both. The chain of equivalent transformations

\[
y''-2xy'+(x+1)^{2}y=0
\;\xleftrightarrow{\;y=e^{x^{2}/2}u\;}\;
u''+2(x+1)u=0
\;\xleftrightarrow{\;z=-2^{1/3}(x+1)\;}\;
u_{zz}-zu=0
\]

is invertible (the exponential factor \(e^{x^{2}/2}\) never vanishes, and the sc
aling \(z=-2^{1/3}(x+1)\) is a nonzero affine change of variable). Hence the dis
played two-parameter family is the **complete general solution** of the given OD
E on every interval, in particular on \(4\le x\le 19\).

Since no boundary or initial conditions are specified, the solution is determine
d up to the two arbitrary constants \(C_{1},C_{2}\), as given above.
