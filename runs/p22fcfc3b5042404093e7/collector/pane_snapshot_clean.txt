$$\rho(f)=\inf\bigl\{\alpha>0:\exists\,A,B>0\text{ with }|f(z)|\le A\,e^{B|z|^{\

The assumed inequality shows $\rho(f)\le p<1$.

## Proof (final, clean version)

**Claim.** No. There is no entire function $f\not\equiv 0$ whose zero set is exa
ctly $\{-1,-2,-3,\dots\}$ (each a zero of finite multiplicity $\ge 1$) satisfyin
g $|f(z)|\le C_1 e^{C_2|z|^p}$ with $p<1$.

**Proof.** Suppose such $f$ exists. The inequality $|f(z)|\le C_1 e^{C_2|z|^p}$
means $f$ has **order** $\rho(f)\le p<1$.

Let $\{a_n\}_{n\ge 1}$ be the zeros of $f$ listed with multiplicity in order of
increasing modulus. Since every negative integer $-k$ ($k\ge 1$) is a zero, amon
g the first $N$ zeros (counted with multiplicity) the moduli are at most $N$ — m
ore precisely, the $n$-th zero satisfies $|a_n|\le n$, because the integers $-1,
-2,\dots,-n$ already provide $n$ zeros each of modulus $\le n$, so the $n$-th sm
allest modulus is $\le n$.

Therefore
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|a_n|^{\alpha}}\ge\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\
alpha}},$$
which **diverges** for every $\alpha\le 1$. Hence the **exponent of convergence*
* of the zero sequence satisfies
$$\rho_1:=\inf\Bigl\{\alpha>0:\sum_n\frac{1}{|a_n|^{\alpha}}<\infty\Bigr\}\ge 1.
$$

By **Hadamard's factorization theorem**, an entire function of finite order $\rh
o$ satisfies $\rho\ge\rho_1$, where $\rho_1$ is the exponent of convergence of i
ts zeros. Thus
$$\rho(f)\ge\rho_1\ge 1.$$

But we also have $\rho(f)\le p<1$. This is a contradiction.

Hence no such entire function exists. $\blacksquare$
