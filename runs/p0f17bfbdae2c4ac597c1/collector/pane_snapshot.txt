## Proof

We solve the Fredholm integral equation of the second kind
\[
y(x)=\sin(x+\alpha)+\lambda\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin(x+z)\,y(z)\,dz,
\qquad K(x,z)=\sin(x+z),
\]
by the Schmidt–Hilbert (eigenfunction expansion) method.

---

### 1. Verification of the spectral data

Since \(\sin(x+z)=\sin x\cos z+\cos x\sin z\), the operator
\[
(Ty)(x)=\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin(x+z)\,y(z)\,dz
=\sin x\!\int_{0}^{\pi}\!\cos z\,y(z)\,dz+\cos x\!\int_{0}^{\pi}\!\sin z\,y(z)\,
dz
\]
has range contained in \(\operatorname{span}\{\sin x,\cos x\}\). Writing \(y=a\s
in x+b\cos x\),
\[
\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos z\,y(z)\,dz=\frac{\pi b}{2},\qquad
\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin z\,y(z)\,dz=\frac{\pi a}{2},
\]
so \(Ty=\frac{\pi}{2}(b\sin x+a\cos x)\). The eigenvalue equation \(Ty=\mu y\) (
\(\mu=1/\lambda\)) gives \(\frac{\pi}{2}b=\mu a,\ \frac{\pi}{2}a=\mu b\), hence
\(\mu^{2}=\pi^{2}/4\), i.e. \(\lambda=\pm 2/\pi\), with normalized eigenfunction
s
\[
y_{1}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt\pi}(\sin x+\cos x),\quad \lambda_{1}=\tfrac{2}{\pi};\qquad
y_{2}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt\pi}(\sin x-\cos x),\quad \lambda_{2}=-\tfrac{2}{\pi},
\]
confirming the given data. The kernel is real and symmetric, so \(\{y_{1},y_{2}\
}\) is a complete orthonormal basis of the range of \(T\); all higher eigenfunct
ions have eigenvalue \(0\) (orthogonal to \(\operatorname{span}\{\sin x,\cos x\}
\)).

---

### 2. Fourier coefficients of the free term

Expand the free term \(f(x)=\sin(x+\alpha)=\sin x\cos\alpha+\cos x\sin\alpha\):
\[
f_{n}=\int_{0}^{\pi}f(x)\,y_{n}(x)\,dx.
\]
Using \(\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{2}x\,dx=\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos^{2}x\,dx=\frac{\pi}{2}\)
and \(\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin x\cos x\,dx=0\):
\[
f_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt\pi}\Big(\cos\alpha\cdot\tfrac{\pi}{2}+\sin\alpha\cdot\tfrac
{\pi}{2}\Big)=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}(\cos\alpha+\sin\alpha),
\]
\[
f_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt\pi}\Big(\cos\alpha\cdot\tfrac{\pi}{2}-\sin\alpha\cdot\tfrac
{\pi}{2}\Big)=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}(\cos\alpha-\sin\alpha).
\]

---

### 3. General case \(\lambda\neq \lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\) (i.e. \(\lambda\neq\
pm 2/\pi\))

Writing \(y=\sum_{n}c_{n}y_{n}\) and using \(Ty_{n}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}y_{n}\)
, the equation \((I-\lambda T)y=f\) yields
\[
c_{n}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{n}}\Big)=f_{n}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
c_{n}=\frac{f_{n}\,\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda}.
\]
Thus
\[
c_{1}=\frac{\sqrt\pi(\cos\alpha+\sin\alpha)}{2-\pi\lambda},\qquad
c_{2}=\frac{\sqrt\pi(\cos\alpha-\sin\alpha)}{2+\pi\lambda}.
\]
Reconstructing \(y=\frac{1}{\sqrt\pi}\big[(c_{1}+c_{2})\sin x+(c_{1}-c_{2})\cos
x\big]\):
\[
c_{1}+c_{2}=\frac{2\sqrt\pi\,(2\cos\alpha+\pi\lambda\sin\alpha)}{4-\pi^{2}\lambd
a^{2}},\qquad
c_{1}-c_{2}=\frac{2\sqrt\pi\,(\pi\lambda\cos\alpha+2\sin\alpha)}{4-\pi^{2}\lambd
a^{2}}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\;y(x)=\frac{2\big[\,2\sin(x+\alpha)+\pi\lambda\cos(x-\alpha)\,\big]}{4-\
pi^{2}\lambda^{2}}\;},\qquad \lambda\neq\pm\frac{2}{\pi}.
\]
(Consistency check: at \(\lambda=0\) this reduces to \(y(x)=\sin(x+\alpha)=f(x)\
).)

---

### 4. Resonant cases (Fredholm alternative)

**Case \(\lambda=\lambda_{1}=2/\pi\).** The coefficient of \(y_{1}\) becomes sin
gular; a solution exists iff \(f_{1}=0\), i.e.
\[
\cos\alpha+\sin\alpha=0\iff \alpha=-\frac{\pi}{4}+k\pi.
\]
When this holds, \(c_{2}=\dfrac{f_{2}\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}=\dfra
c{f_{2}}{2}=-\dfrac{\sqrt\pi\sin\alpha}{2}\), while \(c_{1}\) is arbitrary. Henc
e
\[
y(x)=\frac{C}{\sqrt\pi}(\sin x+\cos x)-\frac{\sin\alpha}{2}(\sin x-\cos x),\quad
 C\in\mathbb{R}\text{ arbitrary},\quad \alpha=-\tfrac{\pi}{4}+k\pi.
\]
If \(\cos\alpha+\sin\alpha\neq0\), **no solution exists**.

**Case \(\lambda=\lambda_{2}=-2/\pi\).** A solution exists iff \(f_{2}=0\), i.e.
\[
\cos\alpha-\sin\alpha=0\iff \alpha=\frac{\pi}{4}+k\pi.
\]
When this holds, \(c_{1}=\dfrac{f_{1}\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}=\dfra
c{f_{1}}{2}=\dfrac{\sqrt\pi\sin\alpha}{2}\), while \(c_{2}\) is arbitrary. Hence
\[
y(x)=\frac{\sin\alpha}{2}(\sin x+\cos x)+\frac{C}{\sqrt\pi}(\sin x-\cos x),\quad
 C\in\mathbb{R}\text{ arbitrary},\quad \alpha=\tfrac{\pi}{4}+k\pi.
\]
If \(\cos\alpha-\sin\alpha\neq0\), **no solution exists**.

---

### 5. Summary

- **Non-resonant** (\(\lambda\neq\pm 2/\pi\)): unique solution
  \[
  y(x)=\frac{2\,[\,2\sin(x+\alpha)+\pi\lambda\cos(x-\alpha)\,]}{4-\pi^{2}\lambda
^{2}}.
  \]
- **\(\lambda=2/\pi\)**: solvable iff \(\cos\alpha+\sin\alpha=0\); then infinite
ly many solutions differing by a multiple of \(y_{1}\). Otherwise no solution.
- **\(\lambda=-2/\pi\)**: solvable iff \(\cos\alpha-\sin\alpha=0\); then infinit
ely many solutions differing by a multiple of \(y_{2}\). Otherwise no solution.

This is exactly the Fredholm alternative as predicted by Schmidt–Hilbert theory
for a real symmetric (Hermitian) kernel with a discrete, finite-rank non-zero sp
ectrum.
