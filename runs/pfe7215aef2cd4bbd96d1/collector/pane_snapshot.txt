## Proof

Let $q_n$ denote the $n$th nice prime and $p_n$ the $n$th prime. We determine th
e asymptotic behavior of $q_n / p_n$ as $n \to \infty$.

**Step 1. Counting nice primes.** Define
$$N(x) := \#\{p \le x : p \text{ nice}\} = \#\{p \le x : p \equiv 1 \pmod{4}\} +
 \mathbf{1}_{x \ge 2}.$$
By the Prime Number Theorem for arithmetic progressions (de la Vallée Poussin),
for $\gcd(a,m)=1$,
$$\pi(x; m, a) \sim \frac{1}{\phi(m)} \cdot \frac{x}{\ln x}.$$
With $m = 4$, $a = 1$, $\phi(4) = 2$:
$$\pi(x; 4, 1) \sim \frac{x}{2 \ln x}.$$
The single extra prime $p = 2$ contributes $O(1)$, which is negligible. Hence
$$N(x) \sim \frac{x}{2 \ln x}. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2. Asymptotic for the $n$th nice prime.** By definition $N(q_n) = n$. Sub
stituting into (1):
$$n \sim \frac{q_n}{2 \ln q_n}, \qquad \text{i.e.} \qquad q_n \sim 2 n \ln q_n.
\tag{2}$$
Taking logarithms of (2): $\ln q_n = \ln n + \ln\ln q_n + O(1)$, so $\ln q_n \si
m \ln n$. Feeding this back into (2):
$$q_n \sim 2 n \ln n. \tag{3}$$

**Step 3. Asymptotic for the $n$th prime.** By the classical Prime Number Theore
m,
$$p_n \sim n \ln n. \tag{4}$$

**Step 4. The ratio.** Combining (3) and (4):
$$\frac{q_n}{p_n} \sim \frac{2 n \ln n}{n \ln n} = 2.$$

Therefore the ratio of the $n$th nice prime to the $n$th prime tends to $2$:
$$\boxed{\displaystyle \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{q_n}{p_n} = 2.}$$

**Remark on the structure.** More generally, the argument shows that for any fix
ed admissible residue class $a \pmod{m}$ (with $\gcd(a,m)=1$), the $n$th prime i
n that class is asymptotic to $\phi(m)\, n \ln n$, so its ratio to $p_n$ tends t
o $\phi(m)$. Here $\phi(4) = 2$, giving the factor $2$. The key inputs are (i) F
ermat's two-squares theorem identifying nice primes with the class $1 \pmod 4$,
and (ii) the PNT in arithmetic progressions, which supplies the equidistribution
 of primes among reduced residue classes and the density $1/\phi(4) = 1/2$ of ni
