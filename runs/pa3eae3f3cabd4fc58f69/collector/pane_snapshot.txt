## Proof

**Claim:** No. If two functions $f, g: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ have the same
limit at every point (i.e., for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$, both $\lim_{t \to x} f
(t)$ and $\lim_{t \to x} g(t)$ exist and are equal), then the set $\{x : f(x) \n
eq g(x)\}$ is at most countable.

**Proof.**

Define $h = f - g$. Since limits are linear, for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$:

$$\lim_{t \to x} h(t) = \lim_{t \to x} f(t) - \lim_{t \to x} g(t) = 0.$$

We show that $S := \{x \in \mathbb{R} : h(x) \neq 0\}$ is countable.

For each positive integer $n$, define:

$$B_n := \left\{x \in \mathbb{R} : |h(x)| \geq \frac{1}{n}\right\}.$$

Then $S = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n$, so it suffices to show each $B_n$ is coun
table.

**Key step: each $B_n$ has no limit points.**

Suppose for contradiction that $x_0$ is a limit point of $B_n$. Then there exist
s a sequence $(x_k)$ in $B_n \setminus \{x_0\}$ with $x_k \to x_0$. Since each $
x_k \in B_n$, we have $|h(x_k)| \geq 1/n$ for all $k$.

But $x_k \to x_0$ with $x_k \neq x_0$, and $\lim_{t \to x_0} h(t) = 0$, so we mu
st have $h(x_k) \to 0$. This contradicts $|h(x_k)| \geq 1/n$ for all $k$. $\quad
\Rightarrow\!\Leftarrow$

Therefore $B_n$ has no limit points.

**A subset of $\mathbb{R}$ with no limit points is countable.**

For each $N \in \mathbb{N}$, the set $B_n \cap [-N, N]$ is bounded. If it were i
nfinite, then by the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem it would have a limit point in
$[-N, N]$, contradicting that $B_n$ has no limit points. Hence $B_n \cap [-N, N]
$ is finite. Therefore:

$$B_n = \bigcup_{N=1}^{\infty} (B_n \cap [-N, N])$$

is a countable union of finite sets, hence countable.

**Conclusion.**

$$S = \{x : f(x) \neq g(x)\} = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n$$

is a countable union of countable sets, hence countable.

Therefore, two functions that share the same limit at every point can differ at
only countably many points — **not** at an uncountable number of points. $\black
square$
