Looking at the problem: find the largest radius of a circle (2D disk) contained
in a 4D hypercube of side length 1.

## Proof

**Setup.** Place the hypercube as $C = [-\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{2}]^4 \subset \
mathbb{R}^4$, centered at the origin.

**Step 1: The optimal disk is centered at the origin and lies in a plane through
 the origin.**

The cube $C$ is centrally symmetric ($C = -C$) and convex. For any 2-dimensional
 affine plane $P$, the section $C \cap P$ is a centrally symmetric convex polygo
n whose inradius is maximized when $P$ passes through the center of symmetry (tr
anslating $P$ toward the center only enlarges the section by symmetry and convex
ity). When $P$ passes through the origin, the largest inscribed disk in $C \cap
P$ is centered at the origin. Hence we may restrict to 2D linear subspaces throu
gh the origin, with the disk centered at the origin.

**Step 2: Reduction to an optimization over orthonormal frames.**

Let the plane be spanned by orthonormal vectors $u, v \in \mathbb{R}^4$. A point
 of the disk of radius $r$ is $\alpha u + \beta v$ with $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 \le
r^2$. Its $i$-th coordinate is $\alpha u_i + \beta v_i$, so the constraint $|\al
pha u_i + \beta v_i| \le \tfrac{1}{2}$ for all $i$ is equivalent to
$$r\,\sqrt{u_i^2 + v_i^2} \le \tfrac{1}{2}, \quad i = 1,2,3,4.$$
Setting $s_i := u_i^2 + v_i^2$, the largest feasible radius for this plane is
$$r = \frac{1}{2\,\max_i \sqrt{s_i}}.$$

**Step 3: The constraint on the $s_i$.**

Since $u, v$ are orthonormal,
$$\sum_{i=1}^{4} s_i = \sum_i u_i^2 + \sum_i v_i^2 = \|u\|^2 + \|v\|^2 = 2.$$

**Step 4: Minimizing $\max_i s_i$.**

By the pigeonhole principle, $\max_i s_i \ge \tfrac{1}{4}\sum s_i = \tfrac{1}{2}
$. This lower bound is **achieved** when $s_i = \tfrac{1}{2}$ for all $i$, via t
he explicit orthonormal pair
$$u = \bigl(\tfrac{1}{2},\,\tfrac{1}{2},\,\tfrac{1}{2},\,\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr), \qq
uad v = \bigl(\tfrac{1}{2},\,\tfrac{1}{2},\,-\tfrac{1}{2},\,-\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr).
$$
Indeed $\|u\|^2 = \|v\|^2 = 1$, $u \cdot v = 0$, and $u_i^2 + v_i^2 = \tfrac{1}{
4} + \tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{1}{2}$ for every $i$.

**Step 5: Computing the radius.**

With $\max_i s_i = \tfrac{1}{2}$,
$$r = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{1/2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.$$

**Verification.** For any point $\alpha u + \beta v$ with $\alpha^2+\beta^2 \le
\tfrac{1}{2}$, the $i$-th coordinate satisfies
$$|\alpha u_i + \beta v_i| \le \sqrt{\alpha^2+\beta^2}\,\sqrt{u_i^2+v_i^2} \le \
sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}} \cdot \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}} = \tfrac{1}{2},$$
so the entire disk of radius $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ lies in $C$. The bound is tig
ht since equality is attained (e.g., along the direction maximizing a coordinate
).

**Conclusion.** The largest possible radius is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}}.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
