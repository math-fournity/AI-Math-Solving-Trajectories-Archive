## Proof

We model the situation as a complete graph $K_{17}$ on 17 vertices (the people),
 whose edges are colored with 3 colors (the three topics). We must show that som
e triangle has all three edges of the same color.

**Step 1: Reduction from 3 colors to 2 colors.**

Pick any person $P$. Person $P$ corresponds with the other 16 people, and each o
f these 16 edges is colored with one of 3 topics. By the pigeonhole principle, a
t least $\lceil 16/3 \rceil = 6$ of these edges share the same color; call this
color (topic) $T$, and call these 6 people $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_6$.

Now consider the edges among $A_1, \dots, A_6$:
- If some edge $A_iA_j$ is also colored $T$, then $P, A_i, A_j$ form a triangle
all of whose edges are color $T$, and we are done.
- Otherwise, **every** edge among $A_1, \dots, A_6$ is colored with one of the *
*remaining 2** topics. So we have a complete graph $K_6$ whose edges are 2-color

**Step 2: A 2-colored $K_6$ contains a monochromatic triangle.**

Among the 6 people $A_1, \dots, A_6$, pick any one, say $A_1$. Person $A_1$ is j

Now examine the edges among $B_1, B_2, B_3$:
- If some edge $B_iB_j$ is colored $T'$, then $A_1, B_i, B_j$ form a triangle al

**Conclusion.**

Combining both steps: either we found a monochromatic triangle already in Step 1
