**Answer: Yes, $L_g f$ is always right uniformly continuous.**

## Proof

**Definition (right uniform continuity).** A function $f: G \to \mathbb{C}$ on a
 topological group $G$ is *right uniformly continuous* if for every $\varepsilon
 > 0$ there exists a neighborhood $V$ of the identity $e$ such that for all $x,
y \in G$,
$$x^{-1}y \in V \implies |f(x) - f(y)| < \varepsilon.$$
(Equivalently, $f$ is uniformly continuous for the *right uniform structure* on
$G$, whose basic entourages are $U_V = \{(x,y) : x^{-1}y \in V\}$, $V$ ranging o
ver neighborhoods of $e$.)

**Setup.** Fix $g \in G$ and define the left-translation map
$$\phi_g : G \to G, \qquad \phi_g(x) = g^{-1}x.$$
Note that $(L_g f)(x) = f(g^{-1}x) = (f \circ \phi_g)(x)$, so $L_g f = f \circ \
phi_g$.

**Key observation: $\phi_g$ is an isometry of the right uniform structure.** For
 any $x, y \in G$,
$$(\phi_g(x))^{-1}\,\phi_g(y) = (g^{-1}x)^{-1}(g^{-1}y) = x^{-1}g\,g^{-1}y = x^{
-1}y.$$
Hence
$$x^{-1}y \in V \iff (\phi_g(x))^{-1}\phi_g(y) \in V,$$
i.e. $(x,y) \in U_V \iff (\phi_g(x), \phi_g(y)) \in U_V$. So $\phi_g$ maps each
entourage $U_V$ onto itself; in particular $\phi_g$ is uniformly continuous (for
 the right uniform structure) with the *same* neighborhood $V$ at every scale.

**Composition.** Let $\varepsilon > 0$. Since $f$ is right uniformly continuous,
 there is a neighborhood $V$ of $e$ such that for all $u, v \in G$,
$$u^{-1}v \in V \implies |f(u) - f(v)| < \varepsilon.$$
Now take any $x, y \in G$ with $x^{-1}y \in V$. Setting $u = \phi_g(x) = g^{-1}x
$ and $v = \phi_g(y) = g^{-1}y$, the key observation gives $u^{-1}v = x^{-1}y \i
n V$, so
$$|(L_g f)(x) - (L_g f)(y)| = |f(g^{-1}x) - f(g^{-1}y)| = |f(u) - f(v)| < \varep
silon.$$
Thus the *same* neighborhood $V$ witnesses right uniform continuity of $L_g f$.

**Conclusion.** For every $g \in G$, the left translate $L_g f$ is right uniform
ly continuous (with the same modulus of continuity as $f$). The boundedness hypo
thesis is not even needed for this argument; it holds for every right uniformly
continuous $f$.

The underlying reason is structural: the right uniform structure on a topologica
l group is invariant under left translations (since $(gx)^{-1}(gy) = x^{-1}y$),
so left translations act as uniform isometries of $(G, \text{right uniformity})$
, and composing a uniformly continuous function with a uniformly continuous map
preserves uniform continuity.
