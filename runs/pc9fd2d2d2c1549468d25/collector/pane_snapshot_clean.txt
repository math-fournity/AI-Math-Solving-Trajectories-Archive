## Proof

Since $f$ is univalent (injective holomorphic) on $\mathbb{D}$ with $f(0)=0$ and
 $f'(0)=1$, we may write the Taylor expansion
$$f(z) = z + a_2 z^2 + a_3 z^3 + \cdots = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n z^n, \quad a_1
 = 1.$$

**Area formula.** The area of $f(\mathbb{D})$ is given by
$$\text{Area}(f(\mathbb{D})) = \iint_{\mathbb{D}} |f'(z)|^2 \, dA(z).$$

This formula is valid because $f$ is univalent: $f$ is a bijection from $\mathbb
{D}$ onto $f(\mathbb{D})$, so the change of variables $w = f(z)$ gives $dA(w) =
|f'(z)|^2 dA(z)$, and integrating over $\mathbb{D}$ yields the area of the image
.

**Computing the integral.** We have $f'(z) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n\, a_n\, z^{n-
1}$. By Parseval's identity on the circle $|z| = r$:
$$\int_0^{2\pi} |f'(re^{i\theta})|^2\, d\theta = 2\pi \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^2 |a
_n|^2\, r^{2(n-1)}.$$

Therefore:
$$\text{Area}(f(\mathbb{D})) = \int_0^1 \left(\int_0^{2\pi} |f'(re^{i\theta})|^2
\, d\theta\right) r\, dr = 2\pi \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^2 |a_n|^2 \int_0^1 r^{2n-1
}\, dr.$$

Since $\int_0^1 r^{2n-1}\, dr = \frac{1}{2n}$, we obtain:
$$\text{Area}(f(\mathbb{D})) = 2\pi \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^2 |a_n|^2 \cdot \frac{
1}{2n} = \pi \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n\, |a_n|^2.$$

**Lower bound.** Since $a_1 = 1$ and every term $n|a_n|^2 \geq 0$:
$$\text{Area}(f(\mathbb{D})) = \pi \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n\, |a_n|^2 \geq \pi \cdo
t 1 \cdot |a_1|^2 = \pi.$$

**Equality is achieved.** The function $f(z) = z$ is univalent on $\mathbb{D}$,
satisfies $f(0) = 0$ and $f'(0) = 1$, and has $a_n = 0$ for all $n \geq 2$. For
this function:
$$\text{Area}(f(\mathbb{D})) = \pi \cdot 1 \cdot |1|^2 = \pi.$$

The image $f(\mathbb{D}) = \mathbb{D}$ is the unit disk, which indeed has area $
\pi$.

**Conclusion.** The minimum area of $f(\mathbb{D})$ is $\boxed{\pi}$.
