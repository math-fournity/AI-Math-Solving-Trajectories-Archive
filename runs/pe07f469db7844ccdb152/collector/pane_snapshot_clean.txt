## Solution

**Setup.** Place the regular hexagon with center at the origin and side length $
s$, with vertices:
$$A=(s,0),\ B=\left(\tfrac{s}{2},\tfrac{s\sqrt3}{2}\right),\ C=\left(-\tfrac{s}{
2},\tfrac{s\sqrt3}{2}\right),\ D=(-s,0),\ E=\left(-\tfrac{s}{2},-\tfrac{s\sqrt3}
{2}\right),\ F=\left(\tfrac{s}{2},-\tfrac{s\sqrt3}{2}\right).$$

**Finding $P$ (intersection of lines $BC$ and $DE$).** Line $BC$ is horizontal:
$y=\tfrac{s\sqrt3}{2}$. Line $DE$ through $D=(-s,0)$ with direction $(\tfrac{s}{
2},-\tfrac{s\sqrt3}{2})$ is parametrized as $(-s+\tfrac{s}{2}t,\,-\tfrac{s\sqrt3
}{2}t)$. Setting $y=\tfrac{s\sqrt3}{2}$ gives $t=-1$, so $x=-s-\tfrac{s}{2}=-\tf
rac{3s}{2}$.

$$P=\left(-\tfrac{3s}{2},\,\tfrac{s\sqrt3}{2}\right).$$

**Finding $Q$ (intersection of lines $AP$ and $CD$).** Line $AP$: from $A=(s,0)$
 toward $P$, direction $(-\tfrac{5s}{2},\tfrac{s\sqrt3}{2})$, parametrized as $(
s-\tfrac{5s}{2}t,\;\tfrac{s\sqrt3}{2}t)$. Line $CD$: from $C=(-\tfrac{s}{2},\tfr
ac{s\sqrt3}{2})$ toward $D=(-s,0)$, parametrized as $(-\tfrac{s}{2}-\tfrac{s}{2}
u,\;\tfrac{s\sqrt3}{2}-\tfrac{s\sqrt3}{2}u)$.

Equating $y$-coordinates: $\tfrac{s\sqrt3}{2}t=\tfrac{s\sqrt3}{2}(1-u)$, so $t=1
-u$. Equating $x$-coordinates:
$$s-\tfrac{5s}{2}t=-\tfrac{s}{2}-\tfrac{s}{2}(1-t)=-s+\tfrac{s}{2}t.$$
$$2s=3st \implies t=\tfrac{2}{3}.$$

$$Q=\left(s-\tfrac{5s}{3},\;\tfrac{s\sqrt3}{3}\right)=\left(-\tfrac{2s}{3},\;\tf
rac{s\sqrt3}{3}\right).$$

**Area of $\triangle QEP$.** Using the shoelace formula with $Q=(-\tfrac{2s}{3},
\tfrac{s\sqrt3}{3})$, $E=(-\tfrac{s}{2},-\tfrac{s\sqrt3}{2})$, $P=(-\tfrac{3s}{2
},\tfrac{s\sqrt3}{2})$:

$$[\triangle QEP]=\tfrac{1}{2}\left|x_Q(y_E-y_P)+x_E(y_P-y_Q)+x_P(y_Q-y_E)\right
|.$$

Computing each term:
- $y_E-y_P=-s\sqrt3$, so $x_Q(y_E-y_P)=\tfrac{2s^2\sqrt3}{3}=\tfrac{8s^2\sqrt3}{
12}$.
- $y_P-y_Q=\tfrac{s\sqrt3}{6}$, so $x_E(y_P-y_Q)=-\tfrac{s^2\sqrt3}{12}$.
- $y_Q-y_E=\tfrac{5s\sqrt3}{6}$, so $x_P(y_Q-y_E)=-\tfrac{15s^2\sqrt3}{12}$.

$$\text{Sum}=\frac{(8-1-15)s^2\sqrt3}{12}=\frac{-8s^2\sqrt3}{12}=\frac{-2s^2\sqr
t3}{3}.$$

$$[\triangle QEP]=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2s^2\sqrt3}{3}=\frac{s^2\sqrt3}{3}.$$

**Solving for the hexagon area.** Setting $\frac{s^2\sqrt3}{3}=72$ gives $s^2=72
\sqrt3$.

The area of a regular hexagon with side $s$ is $\frac{3\sqrt3}{2}s^2$:

$$[ABCDEF]=\frac{3\sqrt3}{2}\cdot 72\sqrt3=\frac{3\cdot 72\cdot 3}{2}=\frac{648}
{2}=\boxed{324}.$$
