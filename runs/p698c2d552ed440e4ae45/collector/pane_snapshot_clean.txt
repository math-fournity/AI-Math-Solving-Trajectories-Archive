# Solution

**Answer:** The quotient space $(S^1 \times S^1)/C_n$ is always homeomorphic (in
deed, diffeomorphic) to $S^1 \times S^1$, regardless of the choice of rotations
and whether they are faithful.

---

## Setup

Identify $S^1 = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$, so $T^2 = S^1 \times S^1 = \mathbb{R}^2/
\mathbb{Z}^2$. A rotation of $S^1$ is a translation $x \mapsto x + \theta \pmod{
1}$. Since $C_n = \langle g \mid g^n = 1\rangle$ acts on each factor by rotation
, the action on $T^2$ is determined by two integers $a, b \in \{0, 1, \dots, n-1
\}$:

$$g \cdot (x, y) = \left(x + \tfrac{a}{n},\; y + \tfrac{b}{n}\right) \pmod{\math
bb{Z}^2}.$$

(Any homomorphism $C_n \to S^1$ sends $g$ to $e^{2\pi i a/n}$ for a unique $a \b
mod n$; the action may be non-faithful when $a$ or $b$ shares a factor with $n$,
 and the two actions may be distinct when $a \neq b$.)

## The quotient as a lattice quotient

The $C_n$-action on $\mathbb{R}^2/\mathbb{Z}^2$ lifts to the action of the subgr
oup $H = \left\{\left(\frac{ka}{n}, \frac{kb}{n}\right) : k \in \mathbb{Z}\right
\}$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$ by translations, modulo $\mathbb{Z}^2$. The quotient is:

$$(S^1 \times S^1)/C_n \;=\; \mathbb{R}^2 \big/ \Lambda, \qquad \Lambda := \math
bb{Z}^2 + \mathbb{Z}\!\left\langle\left(\tfrac{a}{n},\, \tfrac{b}{n}\right)\righ
t\rangle.$$

## $\Lambda$ is a rank-2 lattice

- **$\Lambda$ is discrete:** Since $\Lambda \subseteq \frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}^2$,
which is discrete in $\mathbb{R}^2$, so is $\Lambda$.
- **$\Lambda$ has rank 2:** $\Lambda$ contains $\mathbb{Z}^2$, which already spa
ns $\mathbb{R}^2$ over $\mathbb{R}$, so $\operatorname{rank}(\Lambda) = 2$.
- **$\Lambda$ is a lattice (full-rank discrete subgroup):** By the two points ab
ove, $\Lambda$ is a discrete subgroup of $\mathbb{R}^2$ of full rank, hence a la
ttice.

(Equivalently, $\mathbb{Z}^2 \subseteq \Lambda \subseteq \frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}^2
$, so $[\Lambda : \mathbb{Z}^2] \leq n^2 < \infty$, confirming $\Lambda$ is a fi
nite-index superlattice of $\mathbb{Z}^2$.)

## Conclusion

Any rank-2 lattice $\Lambda$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ yields $\mathbb{R}^2/\Lambda \con
g S^1 \times S^1$ as a smooth manifold (via a linear change of coordinates sendi
ng $\Lambda$ to $\mathbb{Z}^2$). Therefore:

$$\boxed{(S^1 \times S^1)\,/\,C_n \;\cong\; S^1 \times S^1.}$$

**Remark on edge cases.** If $a = b = 0$, the action is trivial and $\Lambda = \
mathbb{Z}^2$, giving $T^2$ itself. If only one of $a, b$ is zero, say $a = 0$, t
hen $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z} \times \frac{1}{n'}\mathbb{Z}$ (where $n' = n/\gcd(b,n
)$), which is still a rank-2 lattice, yielding $S^1 \times S^1$. The argument is
 uniform and covers all cases—faithful or not, distinct or not.
