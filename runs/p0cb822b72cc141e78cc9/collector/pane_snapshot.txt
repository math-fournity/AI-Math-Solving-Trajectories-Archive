## Proof

**Yes, $M$ is necessarily a quotient of $L$.**

**Definitions.** To say "$M$ is a quotient of $N$" means there exists a surjecti
ve $\mathfrak{g}$-module homomorphism $\varphi: N \twoheadrightarrow M$ (equival
ently, $M \cong N/K$ for some submodule $K \subseteq N$). Likewise, "$N$ is a qu
otient of $L$" means there exists a surjective $\mathfrak{g}$-module homomorphis
m $\psi: L \twoheadrightarrow N$.

**Argument.** Suppose $\varphi: N \twoheadrightarrow M$ and $\psi: L \twoheadrig
htarrow N$ are surjective $\mathfrak{g}$-module homomorphisms. Consider the comp
osition
$$\varphi \circ \psi : L \longrightarrow M.$$

We verify the two required properties:

1. **It is a $\mathfrak{g}$-module homomorphism.** Both $\varphi$ and $\psi$ are
 $\mathfrak{g}$-equivariant, i.e., $\varphi(x \cdot n) = x \cdot \varphi(n)$ and
 $\psi(x \cdot \ell) = x \cdot \psi(\ell)$ for all $x \in \mathfrak{g}$, $n \in
N$, $\ell \in L$. Then for any $x \in \mathfrak{g}$ and $\ell \in L$:
$$(\varphi \circ \psi)(x \cdot \ell) = \varphi\bigl(\psi(x \cdot \ell)\bigr) = \
varphi\bigl(x \cdot \psi(\ell)\bigr) = x \cdot \varphi\bigl(\psi(\ell)\bigr) = x
 \cdot (\varphi \circ \psi)(\ell).$$
So $\varphi \circ \psi$ is $\mathfrak{g}$-equivariant. It is also $\mathbb{C}$-l
inear (composition of linear maps), hence a $\mathfrak{g}$-module homomorphism.

2. **It is surjective.** Let $m \in M$. Since $\varphi$ is surjective, there exi
sts $n \in N$ with $\varphi(n) = m$. Since $\psi$ is surjective, there exists $\
ell \in L$ with $\psi(\ell) = n$. Then $(\varphi \circ \psi)(\ell) = \varphi(n)
= m$. Hence $\varphi \circ \psi$ is surjective.

Since $\varphi \circ \psi: L \twoheadrightarrow M$ is a surjective $\mathfrak{g}
$-module homomorphism, $M$ is a quotient of $L$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** This argument uses only that the composition of surjective module ho
momorphisms is a surjective module homomorphism — a fact valid for modules over
any associative algebra (or any ring). The hypotheses that $\mathfrak{g}$ is com
plex reductive and that the modules are finite-dimensional are not needed; the c
onclusion holds in full generality.
