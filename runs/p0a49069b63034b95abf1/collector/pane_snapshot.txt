## Proof

**Setup.** Place the square in the coordinate plane with
$$A=(0,0),\quad B=(1,0),\quad C=(1,1),\quad D=(0,1),$$
and let $P=(x,y)$ with $0\le x\le 1,\;0\le y\le 1$. We minimize
$$f(x,y)=\sqrt{2}\,AP+BP+CP=\sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{x^2+y^2}+\sqrt{(x-1)^2+y^2}+\sqrt{(x
-1)^2+(y-1)^2}.$$

**Convexity.** Each term is a positive scalar times a Euclidean norm, hence conv
ex; their sum $f$ is convex on the square. Therefore any interior critical point
 is the global minimum.

**Equilibrium (weighted Fermat) condition.** At an interior minimizer the gradie
nt vanishes, i.e. the three force vectors balance:
$$\sqrt{2}\,\frac{P-A}{|P-A|}+\frac{P-B}{|P-B|}+\frac{P-C}{|P-C|}=0.$$
The three force magnitudes are $\sqrt{2},\,1,\,1$. Since $1^2+1^2=(\sqrt{2})^2$,
 the force triangle is a **right isosceles triangle**: the side $\sqrt{2}$ is op
posite a right angle, and each side $1$ is opposite a $45^\circ$ angle.

By the standard force-balance angle relation, the angle at $P$ between two verti
ces equals $\pi$ minus the opposite angle in the force triangle:
$$\angle BPC = 180^\circ-90^\circ=90^\circ,\qquad \angle APB=\angle APC=180^\cir
c-45^\circ=135^\circ.$$

**Locating $P$ from $\angle BPC=90^\circ$.** The locus of points subtending $90^
\circ$ on segment $BC$ (from $(1,0)$ to $(1,1)$) is the circle with diameter $BC
$:
$$(x-1)^2+(y-\tfrac12)^2=\tfrac14. \tag{1}$$

**Locating $P$ from $\angle APB=135^\circ$.** For chord $AB$ of length $1$ with
inscribed angle $135^\circ$, the circumradius is $R=1/(2\sin135^\circ)=1/\sqrt2$
. Since the angle is obtuse, the center lies on the opposite side of $AB$ from $
P$ (which is above the $x$-axis), giving center $(\tfrac12,-\tfrac12)$:
$$(x-\tfrac12)^2+(y+\tfrac12)^2=\tfrac12. \tag{2}$$

**Solving the system.** Expanding (1): $x^2+y^2-2x-y+1=0$. Expanding (2): $x^2+y
^2-x+y=0$. Subtracting gives $-x-2y+1=0$, i.e. $x=1-2y$. Substituting into (2):
$$(1-2y)^2+y^2-(1-2y)+y=0\;\Longrightarrow\;5y^2-y=0\;\Longrightarrow\;y=0\text{
 or }y=\tfrac15.$$
The root $y=0$ gives $P=B$ (degenerate); the valid root is $y=\tfrac15$, $x=\tfr
ac35$, so
$$P=\Bigl(\tfrac35,\tfrac15\Bigr).$$

**Verifying $\angle APC=135^\circ$.** With $P=(\tfrac35,\tfrac15)$:
$$\overrightarrow{PA}=(-\tfrac35,-\tfrac15),\quad \overrightarrow{PC}=(\tfrac25,
\tfrac45),$$
$$\overrightarrow{PA}\cdot\overrightarrow{PC}=-\tfrac{6}{25}-\tfrac{4}{25}=-\tfr
ac{10}{25}=-\tfrac25,$$
$$|PA|=\tfrac{\sqrt{10}}{5},\quad |PC|=\tfrac{2\sqrt5}{5},$$
$$\cos\angle APC=\frac{-2/5}{(\sqrt{10}/5)(2\sqrt5/5)}=\frac{-2/5}{2\sqrt{50}/25
}=\frac{-2/5}{10\sqrt2/25}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt2},$$
so $\angle APC=135^\circ$. ✓ All three angle conditions are satisfied, confirmin

**Computing the minimum cost.**
$$AP=\frac{\sqrt{10}}{5},\quad BP=\sqrt{\Bigl(\tfrac35-1\Bigr)^2+\Bigl(\tfrac15\

$$f_{\min}=\sqrt2\cdot\frac{\sqrt{10}}{5}+\frac{\sqrt5}{5}+\frac{2\sqrt5}{5}=\fr

**Conclusion.** The point $P=(\tfrac35,\tfrac15)$ lies in the interior of the sq
