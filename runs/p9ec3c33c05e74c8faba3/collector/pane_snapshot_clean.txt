We solve \( y'(t) = \sin(t) + \int_0^t y(x)\cos(t-x)\,dx \), \( y(0)=0 \), via t
he Laplace transform.

### Taking the Laplace transform

Let \( Y(s) = \mathcal{L}\{y(t)\} \).

**Left-hand side.** Using the derivative rule and \( y(0)=0 \):
$$\mathcal{L}\{y'(t)\} = sY(s) - y(0) = sY(s).$$

**Right-hand side.** The first term is standard:
$$\mathcal{L}\{\sin(t)\} = \frac{1}{s^2+1}.$$

The integral \( \int_0^t y(x)\cos(t-x)\,dx \) is the **convolution** \( (y * \co
s)(t) \). By the convolution theorem:
$$\mathcal{L}\{y * \cos\} = Y(s)\cdot\mathcal{L}\{\cos(t)\} = Y(s)\cdot\frac{s}{
s^2+1}.$$

### Solving for \( Y(s) \)

The transformed equation is:
$$sY(s) = \frac{1}{s^2+1} + Y(s)\cdot\frac{s}{s^2+1}.$$

Collecting the \( Y(s) \) terms:
$$Y(s)\left(s - \frac{s}{s^2+1}\right) = \frac{1}{s^2+1}.$$

Factor the bracket:
$$s - \frac{s}{s^2+1} = s\left(1 - \frac{1}{s^2+1}\right) = s\cdot\frac{s^2}{s^2
+1} = \frac{s^3}{s^2+1}.$$

Hence:
$$Y(s)\cdot\frac{s^3}{s^2+1} = \frac{1}{s^2+1},$$
$$Y(s) = \frac{1}{s^2+1}\cdot\frac{s^2+1}{s^3} = \frac{1}{s^3}.$$

### Inverse Laplace transform

Using \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\{1/s^{n+1}\} = t^n/n! \) with \( n=2 \):
$$y(t) = \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s^3}\right\} = \frac{t^2}{2}.$$

### Verification

- **Initial condition:** \( y(0) = 0 \). ✓
- **Derivative:** \( y'(t) = t \).
- **Convolution term:** Compute \( \int_0^t \frac{x^2}{2}\cos(t-x)\,dx \). Subst
ituting \( u = t-x \):
$$\int_0^t \frac{x^2}{2}\cos(t-x)\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^t (t-u)^2\cos(u)\,du$$
$$= \frac{1}{2}\left[t^2\sin t - 2t\bigl(t\sin t + \cos t - 1\bigr) + \bigl(t^2\
sin t + 2t\cos t - 2\sin t\bigr)\right]$$
$$= \frac{1}{2}\bigl[2t - 2\sin t\bigr] = t - \sin t.$$
- **Right-hand side:** \( \sin t + (t - \sin t) = t = y'(t) \). ✓

The solution satisfies both the differential equation and the initial condition.

### Result

$$\boxed{y(t) = \dfrac{t^2}{2}}$$
