## Proof

Let $r = |v| = (v_1^2+v_2^2+v_3^2)^{1/2}$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$.

**Key observation.** Compute $\partial_{v_j} r = v_j/r$, and then
$$
\partial_{v_i}\partial_{v_j} r \;=\; \partial_{v_i}\!\left(\frac{v_j}{r}\right)
\;=\; \frac{\delta_{ij}}{r} - \frac{v_i v_j}{r^3} \;=\; \Phi^{ij}(v).
$$
Hence $\Phi^{ij}(v) = \partial_{v_i}\partial_{v_j} r$.

---

### Part 1: $\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^3 \partial_{v_j}\Phi^{ij}(v)$

Using $\Phi^{ij}=\partial_{v_i}\partial_{v_j} r$ and swapping the (smooth away f
rom $0$) derivatives,
$$
\sum_{j=1}^3 \partial_{v_j}\Phi^{ij} \;=\; \sum_{j=1}^3 \partial_{v_j}\partial_{
v_i}\partial_{v_j} r \;=\; \partial_{v_i}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^3 \partial_{v_j}^2 r
\right) \;=\; \partial_{v_i}(\Delta r),
$$
where $\Delta = \sum_j \partial_{v_j}^2$ is the Laplacian.

In $\mathbb{R}^3$, for $r=|v|$:
$$
\Delta r \;=\; \frac{n-1}{r}\Big|_{n=3} \;=\; \frac{2}{r}\qquad (v\neq 0).
$$
Therefore
$$
\sum_{j=1}^3 \partial_{v_j}\Phi^{ij}(v) \;=\; \partial_{v_i}\!\left(\frac{2}{r}\
right) \;=\; -\,\frac{2\,v_i}{r^3} \;=\; -\,\frac{2\,v_i}{|v|^3}\qquad (v\neq 0)
.
$$

---

### Part 2: $\displaystyle \partial_{v_i,v_j}\Phi^{ij}(v) = \chi\,\delta(v)$

The double divergence (summed over both indices) is
$$
\partial_{v_i,v_j}\Phi^{ij} \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^3 \partial_{v_i}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^
3 \partial_{v_j}\Phi^{ij}\right) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^3 \partial_{v_i}\!\left(-\frac
{2v_i}{r^3}\right) \;=\; -2\,\nabla_v\!\cdot\!\left(\frac{v}{r^3}\right).
$$

For $v\neq 0$, $\nabla\!\cdot\!(v/r^3)=0$ (direct calculation: $3r^{-3}-3r^{-5}|
v|^2=0$). So the double divergence vanishes pointwise off the origin, which is c
onsistent with a pure delta distribution.

Distributionally, since $\dfrac{v}{r^3} = -\nabla_v\!\left(\dfrac{1}{r}\right)$,
 we use the classical identity $\Delta(1/r) = -4\pi\,\delta(v)$ in $\mathbb{R}^3
$:
$$
\nabla\!\cdot\!\left(\frac{v}{r^3}\right) \;=\; -\Delta\!\left(\frac{1}{r}\right
) \;=\; 4\pi\,\delta(v).
$$

Hence
$$
\partial_{v_i,v_j}\Phi^{ij}(v) \;=\; -2\cdot 4\pi\,\delta(v) \;=\; -8\pi\,\delta
(v).
$$

**Cross-check via the biharmonic identity.** Since $\Phi^{ij}=\partial_{v_i}\par
tial_{v_j} r$,
$$
\partial_{v_i,v_j}\Phi^{ij} \;=\; \partial_{v_i}\partial_{v_j}\partial_{v_i}\par
tial_{v_j} r \;=\; \Delta^2 r \;=\; \Delta\!\left(\frac{2}{r}\right) \;=\; 2\,\D
elta\!\left(\frac{1}{r}\right) \;=\; 2(-4\pi\delta(v)) \;=\; -8\pi\,\delta(v),
$$
confirming the result independently.

---

### Conclusion

$$
\boxed{\sum_{j=1}^3 \partial_{v_j}\Phi^{ij}(v) = -\frac{2\,v_i}{|v|^3}},\qquad \
boxed{\partial_{v_i,v_j}\Phi^{ij}(v) = -8\pi\,\delta(v)},\qquad \boxed{\chi = -8
\pi.}
$$
