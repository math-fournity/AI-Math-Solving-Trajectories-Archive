## Solution

We are given the linear system arising in the first-order Rosenbrock scheme for
the nonautonomous ODE $\dot{x} = f(x,t)$:

$$
(I - h\gamma J)\, k_1 \;=\; f(x_k, t) + h\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\bigg|_{
(x_k,\,t)}.
$$

Denote the right-hand side as

$$
b \;=\; f(x_k, t) + h\, f_t(x_k, t),
$$

and the coefficient matrix as

$$
A \;=\; I - h\gamma J,
$$

where $J = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\big|_{(x_k,t)}$ is the Jacobian. The sy
stem is the standard linear-algebraic problem

$$
A\, k_1 = b.
$$

### Existence and uniqueness

The system admits a unique solution if and only if $A = I - h\gamma J$ is nonsin
gular, i.e.

$$
\det(I - h\gamma J) \neq 0.
$$

Equivalently, $h\gamma$ must not be the reciprocal of any eigenvalue $\lambda_i$
 of $J$:

$$
h\gamma \,\lambda_i \neq 1 \quad \text{for all eigenvalues } \lambda_i \text{ of
 } J.
$$

In the standard Rosenbrock setting $\gamma$ is a fixed method parameter (e.g. $\
gamma = 1 - 1/\sqrt{2}$ for certain second-order variants, or $\gamma = 1$ for t
he simplest first-order scheme), and $h$ is the step size; for a stable ODE (eig
envalues of $J$ in the left half-plane) and the usual positive $\gamma$, the mat
rix $I - h\gamma J$ is automatically nonsingular for all $h > 0$, since $1 - h\g
amma\lambda_i \neq 0$ when $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_i) \le 0$ and $h\gamma > 0
$.

### Formal solution

When $A$ is nonsingular, the unique solution is obtained by left-multiplication
with $A^{-1}$:

$$
\boxed{\,k_1 \;=\; (I - h\gamma J)^{-1}\!\left[\, f(x_k,\, t) \;+\; h\,\frac{\pa
rtial f}{\partial t}\bigg|_{(x_k,\,t)}\,\right]\,}.
$$

### Practical factorization (LU form)

In computation one does not form the inverse explicitly. Instead one performs an
 LU decomposition (with partial pivoting) of the coefficient matrix:

$$
I - h\gamma J = P\,L\,U,
$$

where $P$ is a permutation matrix, $L$ unit lower-triangular, and $U$ upper-tria
ngular. The solution is then obtained in two triangular solves:

1. **Forward substitution** — solve $L\, y = P\,b$ for the intermediate vector $
y$.
2. **Back substitution** — solve $U\, k_1 = y$ for $k_1$.

This gives

$$
k_1 = U^{-1} L^{-1} P\, b = (I - h\gamma J)^{-1} b,
$$

which is exactly the formal solution above.

### Role in the Rosenbrock step

Once $k_1$ is computed, the first-order accurate Rosenbrock update for the nonau
tonomous system is

$$
x_{k+1} = x_k + k_1,
$$

yielding a scheme that is linearly implicit (only one linear solve per step, no
iteration) and first-order accurate in $h$. The term $h\, f_t$ on the right-hand
 side is precisely the nonautonomous correction: it accounts for the explicit ti
me dependence of $f$, which is absent in the autonomous formulation. Without it
(i.e. for an autonomous ODE where $f$ has no explicit $t$-dependence), the right
-hand side reduces to $f(x_k, t)$ and one recovers the autonomous Rosenbrock sta
ge equation.

### Verification

Substituting the computed $k_1$ back into the left-hand side:

$$
(I - h\gamma J)\, k_1 = (I - h\gamma J)(I - h\gamma J)^{-1} b = I\, b = b = f(x_
k, t) + h\, f_t(x_k, t),
$$

which equals the prescribed right-hand side. Hence the expression satisfies the
original system identically, confirming correctness. $\blacksquare$
