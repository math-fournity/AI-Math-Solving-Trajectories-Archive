## Proof

A frog starts at 0 and must reach 7. Its steps are $+1$, $+2$, and **exactly one
** $-1$ (the "turnaround"). The turnaround occurs before the frog first arrives
at 7, and no step lands on a negative number.

**Key observations.**

1. **The $-1$ step is the only non-positive step.** Split any valid path into th
ree parts: the segment *before* the $-1$ (using only $+1,+2$), the $-1$ step its
elf, and the segment *after* the $-1$ (using only $+1,+2$).

2. **The before-segment is strictly increasing** (both $+1$ and $+2$ increase po
sition), so it never exceeds its endpoint $p$. Since the frog must not arrive at
 7 before the turnaround, we need $p \le 6$ (if $p=7$ the frog would have alread
y arrived at 7). Also $p \ge 1$ so that $p-1 \ge 0$.

3. **The after-segment starts at $p-1$ and ends at 7** using only $+1,+2$. Since
 these steps are positive, the frog approaches 7 from below and lands on 7 only
at the final step — so 7 is reached exactly once, at the end. The condition "7 n
ot visited before the end" is automatically satisfied.

4. **Non-negativity is automatic:** the before-segment starts at 0 and only incr
eases; the $-1$ lands at $p-1 \ge 0$; the after-segment only increases.

So every choice of $p \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$ gives valid paths, with no further re
strictions.

**Counting.** Let $g(n)$ = number of paths from $0$ to $n$ using steps $+1,+2$.
This satisfies $g(n)=g(n-1)+g(n-2)$ with $g(0)=g(1)=1$, i.e. $g(n) = F_{n+1}$ (F
ibonacci):

$$g(0),g(1),\ldots,g(8) = 1,1,2,3,5,8,13,21,34.$$

For a fixed turnaround position $p$ (jumping from $p$ to $p-1$):
- before-segment: $g(p)$ paths from $0$ to $p$,
- after-segment: $g(7-(p-1)) = g(8-p)$ paths from $p-1$ to $7$.

Total:
$$\sum_{p=1}^{6} g(p)\,g(8-p) = g(1)g(7)+g(2)g(6)+g(3)g(5)+g(4)g(4)+g(5)g(3)+g(6
)g(2).$$

Computing each term:
$$1\cdot21 + 2\cdot13 + 3\cdot8 + 5\cdot5 + 8\cdot3 + 13\cdot2 = 21+26+24+25+24+
26 = 146.$$

**The frog can get to seven in $\boxed{146}$ ways.**
