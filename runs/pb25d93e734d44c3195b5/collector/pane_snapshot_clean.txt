## Solution

Let $\alpha = 1 + \sqrt{3}$ and $\beta = 1 - \sqrt{3}$. Then:
- $\alpha + \beta = 2$, $\alpha\beta = 1 - 3 = -2$,
- so $\alpha, \beta$ are roots of $x^2 - 2x - 2 = 0$.

**Step 1: $\alpha^n + \beta^n$ is an integer.**

Define $a_n = \alpha^n + \beta^n$. Then $a_0 = 2$, $a_1 = 2$, and from the minim
al polynomial,
$$a_n = 2a_{n-1} + 2a_{n-2},$$
so by induction $a_n \in \mathbb{Z}$ for all $n \ge 0$.

**Step 2: Locate the fractional part of $\alpha^{1500}$.**

Since $1500$ is even, $\beta^{1500} = (\sqrt{3}-1)^{1500} > 0$. Also $|\beta| =
\sqrt{3}-1 \approx 0.732 < 1$, so
$$0 < \beta^{1500} < 1.$$

Now
$$\alpha^{1500} = a_{1500} - \beta^{1500} = (a_{1500} - 1) + \bigl(1 - \beta^{15
00}\bigr).$$

Since $0 < \beta^{1500} < 1$, the integer part of $\alpha^{1500}$ is $a_{1500}-1
$ and the **fractional part** is
$$\{\alpha^{1500}\} = 1 - (\sqrt{3}-1)^{1500}.$$

**Step 3: Bound $(\sqrt{3}-1)^{1500}$.**

We have $\sqrt{3}-1 < 0.74$ (since $\sqrt{3} < 1.74$). Therefore
$$(\sqrt{3}-1)^{1500} < 0.74^{1500}.$$

Taking $\log_{10}$:
$$\log_{10}(0.74^{1500}) = 1500 \cdot \log_{10}(0.74) = 1500 \cdot (-0.1308\ldot
s) \approx -196.2.$$

So $(\sqrt{3}-1)^{1500} < 10^{-196}$, i.e. it is smaller than $10^{-50}$ (in fac
t vastly smaller).

**Step 4: Read off the 50th digit.**

The fractional part is
$$1 - \epsilon, \qquad 0 < \epsilon < 10^{-196}.$$

A number of the form $1 - \epsilon$ with $0 < \epsilon < 10^{-196}$ has decimal
expansion
$$0.\underbrace{999\ldots 9}_{\text{at least 196 nines}}\ldots$$

In particular, every digit from the 1st through the 196th position after the dec
imal point is $9$. Since $50 \le 196$, the **50th digit after the decimal point
is $9$**.

### PROOF COMPLETE
