## Proof

Define $h_n(x) = \dfrac{2\,f_n(x)\,g_n(x)}{1 + f_n(x)^2 + g_n(x)^2}$.

**Step 1: Pointwise convergence to 0.**

Since $f_n \to 0$ and $g_n \to 0$ almost everywhere, for a.e. $x$ we have $f_n(x
) \to 0$ and $g_n(x) \to 0$. The function $(u,v) \mapsto \dfrac{2uv}{1+u^2+v^2}$
 is continuous on $[0,\infty)^2$, so by continuity

$$h_n(x) = \frac{2\,f_n(x)\,g_n(x)}{1 + f_n(x)^2 + g_n(x)^2} \longrightarrow \fr
ac{0}{1+0+0} = 0 \quad \text{a.e.}$$

**Step 2: Uniform domination by an integrable function.**

Using AM–GM, $2\,f_n\,g_n \le f_n^2 + g_n^2$, hence

$$0 \le h_n(x) = \frac{2\,f_n\,g_n}{1 + f_n^2 + g_n^2} \le \frac{f_n^2 + g_n^2}{
1 + f_n^2 + g_n^2} \le 1.$$

Thus $|h_n| \le \mathbf{1}_A$ on $A$. Since $A$ has finite Lebesgue measure, $\m
athbf{1}_A \in L^1(\mathbb{R})$.

**Step 3: Apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem.**

The sequence $(h_n)$ satisfies:
- $h_n \to 0$ a.e. on $A$,
- $|h_n| \le \mathbf{1}_A \in L^1(A)$.

By the Dominated Convergence Theorem,

$$\int_A \frac{2\,f_n\,g_n}{1 + f_n^2 + g_n^2}\,dx = \int_A h_n\,dx \longrightar
row \int_A 0\,dx = 0.$$

$\blacksquare$
