## Solution

**Setup.** There are $\binom{10}{2}=45$ segments total. We choose 4 distinct seg
ments uniformly at random, so the sample space has size $\binom{45}{4}$.

Since no three of the ten points are collinear, every triple of points determine
s a genuine (nondegenerate) triangle. Thus "some three of the chosen segments fo
rm a triangle with vertices among the ten points" is equivalent to: **the 4-edge
 simple graph on the 10 labeled vertices contains a 3-cycle**.

**Total outcomes:**
$$\binom{45}{4}=\frac{45\cdot44\cdot43\cdot42}{24}=148995.$$

**Counting outcomes with at least one triangle.** A triangle is determined by ch
oosing its 3 vertices: $\binom{10}{3}=120$ triangles. For each fixed triangle (3
 specific edges), the 4th edge can be any of the remaining $45-3=42$ edges, givi
ng $120\cdot42=5040$ pairs (triangle, 4th edge).

**No double counting.** We check that a 4-edge graph cannot contain two distinct
 triangles. Two distinct triangles:
- sharing 3 vertices are the same triangle;
- sharing 2 vertices share the edge between them, giving $3+3-1=5$ edges;
- sharing $\le 1$ vertex give $6$ edges.

So two distinct triangles require $\ge 5$ edges. Hence every 4-edge graph contai
ns **at most one** triangle, and the count $5040$ is exact with no overcounting.

**Probability:**
$$P=\frac{5040}{148995}.$$

**Simplification.** Factor:
- $5040 = 2^4\cdot 3^2\cdot 5\cdot 7$,
- $148995 = 5\cdot 29799 = 5\cdot 3^2\cdot 3311 = 3^2\cdot 5\cdot 7\cdot 11\cdot
 43$ (since $3311=7\cdot473=7\cdot 11\cdot 43$).

Common factor: $3^2\cdot 5\cdot 7 = 315$.
$$\frac{5040}{315}=16,\qquad \frac{148995}{315}=473.$$
Since $473=11\cdot 43$ is odd, $\gcd(16,473)=1$, so
$$P=\frac{16}{473},\qquad m=16,\ n=473.$$

**Answer:**
$$m+n = 16+473 = \boxed{489}.$$
