**Answer: Yes.** If two real matrices are conjugated over ℂ, then they are conju
gated over ℝ.

**Proof.**

Let $A, B \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$, and suppose they are conjugated over $\mathbb{C}
$. Then there exists $P \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})$ with
$$P^{-1}AP = B, \quad\text{i.e.,}\quad AP = PB.$$

Write $P = R + iS$ where $R, S \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$ are the real and imaginary p
arts. Substituting into $AP = PB$:
$$A(R + iS) = (R + iS)B \implies AR + i\,AS = RB + i\,SB.$$
Equating real and imaginary parts (valid since $A, B, R, S$ are real):
$$AR = RB \quad\text{and}\quad AS = SB.$$

Therefore, for **every** real $t$, the real matrix $Q_t := R + tS$ satisfies
$$AQ_t = A(R + tS) = AR + t\,AS = RB + t\,SB = (R + tS)B = Q_t B.$$

It remains to show that some $Q_t$ is invertible. Consider the polynomial
$$p(t) := \det(R + tS) \in \mathbb{R}[t].$$

This is a polynomial in $t$ of degree at most $n$. It is **not the zero polynomi
al**, because evaluating at $t = i$:
$$p(i) = \det(R + iS) = \det(P) \neq 0,$$
since $P \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})$.

A nonzero polynomial over $\mathbb{R}$ has only finitely many real roots. Hence
there exists some $t_0 \in \mathbb{R}$ with $p(t_0) \neq 0$, i.e., $Q_{t_0} = R
+ t_0 S$ is invertible over $\mathbb{R}$.

Setting $Q := Q_{t_0} \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{R})$, we have $AQ = QB$, and the
refore
$$Q^{-1}AQ = B,$$
with $Q$ a real invertible matrix. $\blacksquare$
