**Answer: No, we cannot conclude that $\lim_{k\to\infty}\langle u_k,w\rangle=0$
for each $w$.**

**Counterexample.**

Let $H=\ell^2(\mathbb N)$ with the standard orthonormal basis $e_n=(0,\dots,0,1,
0,\dots)$ (the $1$ in position $n$). Define
$$
u_k := k\, e_k, \qquad k\in\mathbb N.
$$

*Step 1: The coordinate condition holds.* For each fixed $n\in\mathbb N$,
$$
\langle u_k, e_n\rangle = k\langle e_k,e_n\rangle = k\,\delta_{kn}.
$$
For all $k>n$ this equals $0$, so $\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\langle u_k,e_n
\rangle = 0$ for every $n$.

*Step 2: The norm lower bound holds.* $\|u_k\| = k\ge 1$ for every $k$, so the h
ypothesis is satisfied with $C=1$.

*Step 3: The conclusion fails for some $w\in\ell^2$.* Take $w=(w_n)_{n=1}^\infty
$ with $w_n = \tfrac{1}{n}$. Since $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \tfrac{1}{n^2}<\infty$, we
 have $w\in\ell^2$. Then
$$
\langle u_k, w\rangle = k\, w_k = k\cdot\frac{1}{k}=1 \quad\text{for every }k.
$$
Hence $\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\langle u_k,w\rangle = 1 \neq 0$.

**Why the hypothesis is insufficient.** The standard result guaranteeing weak co
nvergence to $0$ from coordinate-wise convergence requires an *upper* bound $\|u
_k\|\le M$ (boundedness of the sequence). With such an upper bound, one approxim
ates any $w$ by its finite basis expansion $w^{(N)}=\sum_{n=1}^N\langle w,e_n\ra
ngle e_n$ and writes
$$
|\langle u_k,w\rangle|\le \underbrace{|\langle u_k,w^{(N)}\rangle|}_{\to 0\text{
 as }k\to\infty} + \|u_k\|\,\|w-w^{(N)}\|\le |\langle u_k,w^{(N)}\rangle|+M\,\|w
-w^{(N)}\|,
$$
then lets $N\to\infty$. The hypothesis in the problem gives only a *lower* bound
 $\|u_k\|\ge C$, which permits $\|u_k\|\to\infty$; the tail term $\|u_k\|\,\|w-w
^{(N)}\|$ is then uncontrollable, and the counterexample above exploits exactly
this by making $\|u_k\|=k$ grow while concentrating the mass of $u_k$ on the coo
rdinate $e_k$ where $w$ has mass $\sim 1/k$.

Therefore the proposed conclusion does **not** follow from the stated hypotheses
.
