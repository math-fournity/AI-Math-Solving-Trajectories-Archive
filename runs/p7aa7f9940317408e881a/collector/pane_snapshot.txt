## Proof

**Step 1: $f(0)=0$.**

Suppose $f(0)\neq 0$. If $f(z_0)=0$ for some $z_0\neq 0$, then $0=|f(z_0)|\geq |
z_0|^2/(1+|z_0|)>0$, a contradiction. Hence $f$ is zero-free, so $1/f$ is entire
. For $z\neq 0$,
$$|1/f(z)|\le \frac{1+|z|}{|z|^2}.$$
For $|z|\geq 1$ this is $\le 2/|z|\le 2$; for $|z|\le 1$, $1/f$ is continuous on
 the closed unit disk, hence bounded. Thus $1/f$ is a bounded entire function, s
o constant by Liouville's theorem. Then $f$ is constant, say $f\equiv c$, but $|
c|\geq |z|^2/(1+|z|)\to\infty$ as $|z|\to\infty$, impossible. Therefore $f(0)=0$
.

**Step 2: Factorization.**

Write $f(z)=z^k g(z)$ with $k\geq 1$, $g$ entire, $g(0)\neq 0$. The inequality g
ives, for $z\neq 0$,
$$|g(z)|\ge \frac{|z|^{2-k}}{1+|z|}.\tag{$\ast$}$$

Since the right-hand side is strictly positive for $z\neq 0$, $g$ has no zeros o
utside $0$; combined with $g(0)\neq 0$, $g$ is zero-free, so $1/g$ is entire.

**Step 3: Ruling out $k\geq 3$.**

From ($\ast$), $|1/g(z)|\le |z|^{k-2}(1+|z|)$, so $1/g$ has polynomial growth of
 degree $\le k-1$. Hence $1/g$ is a polynomial $P$ of degree $\le k-1$. But $g=1
/P$ is entire only if $P$ has no zeros, forcing $P$ (and thus $g$) to be constan
t. So $f(z)=cz^k$. Then ($\ast$) requires $|c|\ge |z|^{2-k}/(1+|z|)$, which tend
s to $+\infty$ as $z\to 0$ when $k\geq 3$ — impossible. So $k\le 2$.

**Step 4: Case $k=2$.**

Here $|1/g(z)|\le 1+|z|$, so $1/g$ is a polynomial of degree $\le 1$: $1/g(z)=az
+b$. For $g=1/(az+b)$ to be entire, $az+b$ must never vanish, which forces $a=0$
 (otherwise the linear polynomial has a root). Thus $g$ is constant, $f(z)=cz^2$
. The inequality becomes $|c|\ge 1/(1+|z|)$ for all $z$, whose supremum is $1$,
so $|c|\ge 1$.

**Step 5: Case $k=1$.**

Here $|1/g(z)|\le (1+|z|)/|z|$. For $|z|\ge 1$ this is $\le 2$; on $|z|\le 1$, $
1/g$ is continuous, hence bounded. So $1/g$ is bounded entire, hence constant. T
hus $g$ is constant, $f(z)=cz$. The inequality becomes $|c|\ge |z|/(1+|z|)$ for
all $z\neq 0$, whose supremum is $1$, so $|c|\ge 1$.

**Step 6: Verification.**

If $f(z)=cz$ with $|c|\ge 1$: $|cz|=|c||z|\ge |z|\ge |z|^2/(1+|z|)$ since $|z|\l
e 1+|z|$. ✓

If $f(z)=cz^2$ with $|c|\ge 1$: $|cz^2|=|c||z|^2\ge |z|^2\ge |z|^2/(1+|z|)$. ✓

**Conclusion.** The entire functions satisfying the inequality are exactly
$$\boxed{f(z)=az\ \text{with}\ |a|\ge 1,\quad\text{and}\quad f(z)=az^2\ \text{wi
th}\ |a|\ge 1.}$$
