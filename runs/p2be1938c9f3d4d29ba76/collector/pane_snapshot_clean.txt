## Solution

**Reformulation.** Rewrite the left-hand side. Note that $8\left(\ln 2 + \frac{a
x}{2}\right) = 8\ln 2 + 4ax = \ln 4 + 4\cdot\frac{ax+\ln 4}{1}\cdot\frac{1}{...}
$; more directly:

$$8e^{ax}\!\left(\ln 2 + \frac{ax}{2}\right) = 4e^{ax}(ax + 2\ln 2) = 4e^{ax}(ax
 + \ln 4).$$

Since $4 = e^{\ln 4}$, setting $u = ax + \ln 4$ gives

$$4e^{ax}(ax+\ln 4) = e^{ax+\ln 4}(ax+\ln 4) = u\,e^{u}.$$

So the inequality becomes

$$u\,e^{u} \geq x\ln x, \qquad u = ax + \ln 4,\quad x\in[1/e,\,+\infty). \tag{$\
star$}$$

---

**Key observation.** The function $\varphi(v)=ve^{v}$ is strictly increasing on
$[-1,+\infty)$ (since $\varphi'(v)=(1+v)e^{v}\geq 0$). Likewise $\psi(x)=x\ln x$
 is strictly increasing on $[1/e,+\infty)$ (since $\psi'(x)=1+\ln x\geq 0$), wit
h $\psi(1/e)=-1/e$ and $\psi(1)=0$.

---

**The candidate $a=\dfrac{1}{4e}$.** Then $u=\dfrac{x}{4e}+\ln 4$.

*Case 1: $x\in[1/e,1]$.* Here $x\ln x\leq 0$ while $u>0$ so $ue^{u}>0$. Hence $(
\star)$ holds.

*Case 2: $x\geq 1$.* Both $u>0$ and $\ln x\geq 0$, so it suffices (by monotonici
ty of $\varphi$ on $[0,\infty)$) to show $u\geq \ln x$:

$$u\geq \ln x \iff \frac{x}{4e}+\ln 4\geq \ln x \iff \frac{x}{4e}\geq \ln\!\frac
{x}{4}.$$

Let $t=x/4>0$; the claim is $\dfrac{t}{e}\geq \ln t$. Define $\Phi(t)=\dfrac{t}{
e}-\ln t$. Then

$$\Phi'(t)=\frac{1}{e}-\frac{1}{t},\qquad \Phi'(t)=0\iff t=e,\qquad \Phi''(t)=\f
rac{1}{t^{2}}>0.$$

So $t=e$ is the global minimum, and $\Phi(e)=1-1=0$. Thus $\Phi(t)\geq 0$ for al
l $t>0$, i.e. $u\geq\ln x$ for all $x>0$, with equality exactly at $t=e$, i.e. $
x=4e$.

Therefore $u\,e^{u}\geq (\ln x)\,e^{\ln x}=x\ln x$ for all $x\geq 1$, and $(\sta
r)$ holds on the whole interval. ✓

---

**Minimality: any $a<\dfrac{1}{4e}$ fails.** Take $x_{0}=4e$. With $a<\frac{1}{4
e}$,

$$u_{0}=ax_{0}+\ln 4 < \frac{4e}{4e}+\ln 4 = 1+\ln 4 = \ln(4e)=\ln x_{0}.$$

Both $u_{0}>0$ and $\ln x_{0}>0$, and $\varphi$ is strictly increasing on $[0,\i
nfty)$, so

$$u_{0}e^{u_{0}}<(\ln x_{0})\,e^{\ln x_{0}}=x_{0}\ln x_{0},$$

violating $(\star)$ at $x=x_{0}$. Hence no smaller $a$ works.

---

**Conclusion.** The minimum value of $a$ is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{4e}}.$$
