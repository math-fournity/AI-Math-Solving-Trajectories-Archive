Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest natural $k$ such that there
 exist positive rationals $a, b$ (each with minimal decimal period 30) where $a-
b$ has minimal period 15 and $a+kb$ also has minimal period 15.

## Key Framework

The minimal period of a rational $r = p/q$ (lowest terms, $q = 2^\alpha 5^\beta
m$, $\gcd(m,10)=1$) equals $\operatorname{ord}_m(10)$, the multiplicative order
of 10 mod $m$. I call $m$ the **repeating denominator**.

**Critical observation:** $a + kb = (a-b) + (k+1)b$. Since sums/differences of r
ationals with period dividing $d$ also have period dividing $d$:

$$\operatorname{per}(a+kb) \mid 15 \iff \operatorname{per}((k+1)b) \mid 15$$

(given $\operatorname{per}(a-b) \mid 15$). This is because $(k+1)b = (a+kb)-(a-b
)$, and both directions follow from closure of "period divides 15" under additio
n.

## Lower Bound: $k \geq 6$

Let $b = p_2/q_2$ with repeating denominator $m_2$, $\operatorname{ord}_{m_2}(10
)=30$. Then $(k+1)b$ has repeating denominator $m_2/\gcd(k+1, m_2)$. For $\opera
torname{per}((k+1)b) \mid 15$, we need:

$$\frac{m_2}{\gcd(k+1, m_2)} \;\Big|\; 10^{15}-1.$$

Decompose $m_2 = m_2' \cdot m_2''$ where $m_2' = \gcd(m_2, 10^{15}-1)$ and $m_2'
' = m_2/m_2'$. The condition becomes $m_2'' \mid (k+1)$.

Since $\operatorname{ord}_{m_2}(10) = 30 \nmid 15$, we have $m_2 \nmid 10^{15}-1
$, so $m_2'' > 1$. Every prime factor $p$ of $m_2''$ satisfies $p \nmid 10^{15}-
1$, meaning $\operatorname{ord}_p(10) \nmid 15$.

**Finding the smallest such prime** (coprime to 10, with order not dividing 15):
- $p=3$: $\operatorname{ord}_3(10)=1$, and $1\mid 15$. So $3\mid 10^{15}-1$. ✗
- $p=7$: $\operatorname{ord}_7(10)=6$, and $6\nmid 15$. So $7\nmid 10^{15}-1$. ✓

Since 7 is the smallest prime coprime to 10 with $\operatorname{ord}_p(10)\nmid
15$, we get $m_2'' \geq 7$, hence $k+1 \geq 7$, i.e., **$k \geq 6$**.

## Construction: $k = 6$ is achievable

Set $m_2' = \Phi_{15}(10) = 10^8 - 10^7 + 10^5 - 10^4 + 10^3 - 10 + 1 = 90090991
$.

**Verification that $\operatorname{ord}_{90090991}(10) = 15$:** $\Phi_{15}(10) \
mid 10^{15}-1$ by construction. For any proper divisor $d$ of 15 (i.e., $d \in \
{1,3,5\}$), $\gcd(\Phi_{15}(10), 10^d-1)$ divides $\gcd(\Phi_{15}(10), \Phi_1(10
)\Phi_3(10)\Phi_5(10))$, which can only contain primes dividing $\gcd(15,1)\cdot
\gcd(15,3)\cdot\gcd(15,5) = 3\cdot 5$. But $90090991$ is divisible by neither 3
(digit sum 37) nor 5 (ends in 1). So $\operatorname{ord}_{90090991}(10) = 15$. ✓

Let $m_2 = 7 \times 90090991 = 630636937$. Then:
$$\operatorname{ord}_{m_2}(10) = \operatorname{lcm}(\operatorname{ord}_7(10), \o

Choose $b = \frac{1}{630636937}$ and $a = \frac{8}{630636937}$.

**Check all conditions:**
1. $\operatorname{per}(a) = \operatorname{ord}_{630636937}(10) = 30$ ✓ (since $\
gcd(8, 630636937)=1$)
2. $\operatorname{per}(b) = \operatorname{ord}_{630636937}(10) = 30$ ✓
3. $a - b = \frac{7}{630636937} = \frac{1}{90090991}$, so $\operatorname{per}(a-

4. $a + 6b = \frac{8+6}{630636937} = \frac{14}{630636937} = \frac{2}{90090991}$,

Both $a$ and $b$ are positive, and $7b = \frac{7}{630636937} = \frac{1}{90090991
}$ has period 15 (dividing 15), confirming the mechanism: the factor of 7 in $k+
1=7$ cancels the order-6 component from $m_2$, leaving only the order-15 compone

## Conclusion

The lower bound $k \geq 6$ holds for all valid $(a,b)$, and $k = 6$ is achieved
by the explicit construction above.

$$\boxed{k = 6}$$
