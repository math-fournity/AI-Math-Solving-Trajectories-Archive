## Proof

**Yes**, such a subsequence can always be extracted.

**Key observation.** Each eigenvalue $\lambda_n$ satisfies $|\lambda_n| = 1$, so
 the entire sequence $(\lambda_n)_{n=1}^{\infty}$ lies on the unit circle
$$S^1 = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| = 1\}.$$

**Compactness of the unit circle.** The set $S^1$ is closed and bounded in $\mat
hbb{C} \cong \mathbb{R}^2$, hence compact by the Heine–Borel theorem.

**Application of Bolzano–Weierstrass.** Since $S^1$ is a compact subset of $\mat
hbb{R}^2$, every sequence in $S^1$ admits a convergent subsequence with limit in
 $S^1$ (Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem for compact sets). Applying this to $(\lambd
a_n) \subset S^1$, there exist indices $n_1 < n_2 < n_3 < \cdots$ and a point $\
lambda \in S^1$ such that
$$\lambda_{n_k} \to \lambda \quad \text{as } k \to \infty.$$

**Conclusion.** The subsequence $(A_{n_k})_{k=1}^{\infty}$ is the desired subseq
uence of operators. The corresponding eigenvalues $(\lambda_{n_k})$ converge to
$\lambda \in S^1$, so $\lambda$ is a limit point of the eigenvalue sequence asso
ciated to this subsequence.

**Remark.** The argument uses only the compactness of the unit circle and requir
es no hypothesis on the operators $A_n$ beyond the existence of the eigenvalues
$\lambda_n$ on $S^1$. In particular, no convergence assumption on the operators
themselves (in norm, strong, or weak topology) is needed—the extraction of the s
ubsequence is driven entirely by the geometry of the eigenvalues.
