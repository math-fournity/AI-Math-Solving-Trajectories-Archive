# Epimorphisms in the Category of Stone Spaces Are Always Surjective

**Claim.** In the category **Stone** (Stone spaces = compact, Hausdorff, totally
 disconnected spaces; morphisms = continuous maps), every epimorphism is surject
ive.

## Proof

Let $f: X \to Y$ be an epimorphism in **Stone**. We show $f$ is surjective by co
ntradiction.

**Step 1. The image $f(X)$ is closed in $Y$.**

$X$ is compact, the continuous image of a compact space is compact, and a compac
t subset of a Hausdorff space is closed. Since every Stone space is compact Haus
dorff, $f(X)$ is closed in $Y$.

**Step 2. Assume $f$ is not surjective and separate a missed point from the imag
e.**

Suppose $f(X) \subsetneq Y$, and pick $y_0 \in Y \setminus f(X)$. Then $f(X)$ is
 a closed set not containing $y_0$. In a Stone space the clopen subsets form a b
ase for the topology, so clopen sets separate points from closed sets: there exi
sts a clopen set $U \subseteq Y$ with
$$y_0 \in U \subseteq Y \setminus f(X).$$
In particular $U \cap f(X) = \emptyset$ and $U \neq \emptyset$.

**Step 3. Construct two morphisms that agree on $f(X)$ but differ at $y_0$.**

Let $Z = \{0, 1\}$ with the discrete topology; this is a finite, hence compact,
Hausdorff, totally disconnected space, so $Z$ is a Stone space. Define two conti
nuous maps $g, h: Y \to Z$:

- $g(y) = \begin{cases} 1 & y \in U \\ 0 & y \notin U \end{cases}$ — this is con
tinuous because $U$ is clopen (it is the characteristic function of a clopen set
).
- $h(y) = 0$ for all $y \in Y$ — the constant map, which is continuous.

**Step 4. Verify $g \circ f = h \circ f$ but $g \neq h$.**

For every $x \in X$, we have $f(x) \in f(X)$, and since $U \cap f(X) = \emptyset
$, we get $f(x) \notin U$, hence
$$g(f(x)) = 0 = h(f(x)).$$
Thus $g \circ f = h \circ f$.

However, $y_0 \in U$, so $g(y_0) = 1 \neq 0 = h(y_0)$, giving $g \neq h$.

**Step 5. Contradiction with $f$ being epi.**

We have produced two distinct morphisms $g, h: Y \to Z$ in **Stone** with $g \ci
rc f = h \circ f$. This contradicts the assumption that $f$ is an epimorphism. T
herefore $f$ must be surjective. $\blacksquare$

**Remark (via Stone duality).** The same conclusion follows elegantly from duali
ty. Stone duality is a contravariant equivalence $\mathbf{Stone} \simeq \mathbf{
BA}^{\mathrm{op}}$, so epimorphisms in **Stone** correspond to monomorphisms in
**BA** (Boolean algebras). Since **BA** is a variety, monomorphisms are exactly
the injective homomorphisms. Under duality, $f: X \to Y$ corresponds to the pull
back $f^{*}: \mathrm{Clop}(Y) \to \mathrm{Clop}(X)$ on clopen algebras; $f^{*}$
is injective iff $f$ is surjective (a non-surjective $f$ leaves a point $y_0$ ou
tside the closed image $f(X)$, and a clopen neighbourhood $U$ of $y_0$ disjoint
from $f(X)$ satisfies $f^{*}(U) = \emptyset = f^{*}(\emptyset)$ with $U \neq \em
ptyset$, violating injectivity). Hence epi in **Stone** $\Leftrightarrow$ mono i
n **BA** $\Leftrightarrow$ injective pullback $\Leftrightarrow$ surjective.
