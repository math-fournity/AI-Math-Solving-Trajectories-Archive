$$I = \int_0^{\pi/4} \frac{\ln(1+\tan\theta)}{\sec^2\theta}\,\sec^2\theta\,d\the

pi/4]$) gives
$$I = \int_0^{\pi/4} \ln\!\bigl(1+\tan(\tfrac{\pi}{4}-\theta)\bigr)\,d\theta.$$

$$\tan\!\Bigl(\frac{\pi}{4}-\theta\Bigr) = \frac{1-\tan\theta}{1+\tan\theta},$$

$$1 + \tan\!\Bigl(\frac{\pi}{4}-\theta\Bigr) = 1 + \frac{1-\tan\theta}{1+\tan\th
eta} = \frac{(1+\tan\theta)+(1-\tan\theta)}{1+\tan\theta} = \frac{2}{1+\tan\thet

Therefore
$$\ln\!\Bigl(1+\tan\!\Bigl(\frac{\pi}{4}-\theta\Bigr)\Bigr) = \ln 2 - \ln(1+\tan
\theta).$$

$$I = \int_0^{\pi/4} \ln(1+\tan\theta)\,d\theta, \qquad I = \int_0^{\pi/4}\bigl[
