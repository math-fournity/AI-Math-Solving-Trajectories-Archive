## Proof

Let $H = L^2(\Omega)$ equipped with the inner product $(\cdot,\cdot)_\Omega$ and
 the induced norm $\|\cdot\|$. Let $f \in H$ and define the linear functional $l
 : H \to \mathbb{R}$ by
$$l(v) := (f, v)_\Omega.$$

The operator norm of $l$ is
$$\|l\| = \sup_{v \in H,\; v \neq 0} \frac{|l(v)|}{\|v\|} = \sup_{v \neq 0} \fra
c{|(f,v)_\Omega|}{\|v\|}.$$

### Part 1 — Prove $\|l\| \le \|f\|$

By the **Cauchy–Schwarz inequality** on $L^2(\Omega)$,
$$|(f, v)_\Omega| \le \|f\|\,\|v\| \qquad \text{for all } v \in H.$$

Dividing both sides by $\|v\| > 0$,
$$\frac{|(f,v)_\Omega|}{\|v\|} \le \|f\| \qquad \text{for all } v \neq 0.$$

Taking the supremum over all $v \neq 0$ yields
$$\boxed{\|l\| \le \|f\|.}$$

### Part 2 — Is $\|l\| = \|f\|$ possible?

**Yes**, equality is achievable. In fact, on $L^2(\Omega)$ equality **always** h
olds.

- **Trivial case** ($f = 0$): Then $l \equiv 0$, so $\|l\| = 0 = \|f\|$.

- **Nontrivial case** ($f \neq 0$): Choose the test function $v = f \in H$. Then
$$l(f) = (f, f)_\Omega = \|f\|^2,$$
and therefore
$$\frac{|l(f)|}{\|f\|} = \frac{\|f\|^2}{\|f\|} = \|f\|.$$

Since the supremum defining $\|l\|$ is at least as large as the value attained a
t any particular $v$, we get $\|l\| \ge \|f\|$. Combined with Part 1,
$$\boxed{\|l\| = \|f\|.}$$

The supremum is **attained** at $v = f$, so equality is not merely possible but
guaranteed.

### Remark

This is exactly the content of the **Riesz Representation Theorem**: on a Hilber
t space $H$, every bounded linear functional $l$ can be uniquely represented as
$l(v) = (f, v)$ for some $f \in H$, and the isometric identity $\|l\| = \|f\|$ h
olds. The upper bound $\|l\| \le \|f\|$ is Cauchy–Schwarz; the reverse inequalit
y $\|l\| \ge \|f\|$ is witnessed by the Riesz representer $f$ itself.
