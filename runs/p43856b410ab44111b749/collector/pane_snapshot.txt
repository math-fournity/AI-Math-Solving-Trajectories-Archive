## Solution

We want $S = \sum_{k=1}^{9\,999\,999\,999} 2^{f(k)}$, where $f(k)$ counts the ze
ros in the decimal representation of $k$.

**Key idea.** Group numbers by their digit length $d$. The upper limit $9\,999\,
999\,999$ is exactly the largest 10-digit number, so we sum over all $d$-digit n
umbers for $d = 1, 2, \dots, 10$.

**Counting contribution of $d$-digit numbers.** A $d$-digit number has its first
 digit in $\{1,\dots,9\}$ (9 choices, never zero) and each of the remaining $d-1
$ digits in $\{0,1,\dots,9\}$. Since $f(k)$ counts zeros, and the first digit is
 never zero, zeros arise only from the last $d-1$ positions.

For a single trailing position, the digit is either $0$ (contributing one zero,
i.e. a multiplicative factor of $2$ to $2^{f(k)}$) or in $\{1,\dots,9\}$ (contri
buting no zero, i.e. a factor of $1$). Summing over the 10 possible digits, each
 trailing position contributes a total multiplicative weight
$$\underbrace{2}_{\text{digit }0} + \underbrace{9\cdot 1}_{\text{digits }1\text{
–}9} = 11.$$

The leading digit contributes a factor of $9$ (choices $1$–$9$, each adding no z
ero). By independence across positions, the sum of $2^{f(k)}$ over all $d$-digit
 numbers is
$$9 \cdot 11^{d-1}.$$

**Summing over all digit lengths.**
$$S = \sum_{d=1}^{10} 9\cdot 11^{d-1} = 9\sum_{j=0}^{9} 11^{j} = 9\cdot\frac{11^
{10}-1}{11-1} = \frac{9}{10}\bigl(11^{10}-1\bigr).$$

**Computing $11^{10}$.** We have $11^5 = 161051$, so
$$11^{10} = 161051^2 = 25\,937\,424\,601.$$

Therefore
$$11^{10}-1 = 25\,937\,424\,600,\qquad \frac{11^{10}-1}{10} = 2\,593\,742\,460,$
$
$$S = 9 \times 2\,593\,742\,460 = 23\,343\,682\,140.$$

**Verification of the grouping.** The numbers $1$ to $9\,999\,999\,999$ partitio
n exactly into the sets of 1-digit through 10-digit numbers (1-digit: $1$–$9$; 1
0-digit: $10^{9}$–$10^{10}-1 = 9\,999\,999\,999$). No number is missed or double
-counted, and every number in this range has at most 10 digits, so the partition
 is complete.

**Answer:**
$$\boxed{S = 23\,343\,682\,140}$$
