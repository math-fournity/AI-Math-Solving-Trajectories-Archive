**Claim: True.** If $R$ is a commutative Noetherian ring with unity and $I \tria
nglelefteq R$ satisfies $R/I \cong R$, then $I = (0)$.

**Key Lemma (Noetherian rings are Hopfian).** *Every surjective ring endomorphis
m $f: R \to R$ of a Noetherian ring $R$ is injective.*

*Proof of Lemma.* Let $f: R \to R$ be a surjective ring homomorphism. Consider t
he ascending chain of ideals
$$\ker f \;\subseteq\; \ker f^{2} \;\subseteq\; \ker f^{3} \;\subseteq\; \cdots$
$
Each $\ker f^{n}$ is an ideal (being the kernel of the ring homomorphism $f^{n}
= f \circ f \circ \cdots \circ f$), and the inclusions hold since $f^{n}(x) = 0
\Rightarrow f^{n+1}(x) = f(0) = 0$.

Since $R$ is Noetherian, this chain stabilizes: there exists $n \geq 1$ with $\k
er f^{n} = \ker f^{n+1}$.

Now take any $x \in \ker f$. Because $f$ is surjective, every iterate $f^{k}$ is
 surjective; in particular $f^{n}$ is surjective, so there exists $y \in R$ with
 $f^{n}(y) = x$. Then
$$f^{n+1}(y) = f\bigl(f^{n}(y)\bigr) = f(x) = 0,$$
so $y \in \ker f^{n+1} = \ker f^{n}$. Therefore
$$x = f^{n}(y) = 0.$$
Since $x \in \ker f$ was arbitrary, $\ker f = (0)$, i.e. $f$ is injective. $\squ
are$

**Main Proof.** Let $\pi: R \to R/I$ be the canonical quotient map (a surjective
 ring homomorphism with $\ker \pi = I$), and let $\varphi: R/I \xrightarrow{\;\s
im\;} R$ be the given ring isomorphism. Define
$$f := \varphi \circ \pi : R \longrightarrow R.$$
Then $f$ is a surjective ring endomorphism of $R$ (composition of two surjection
s). By the Lemma, $f$ is injective.

Since $f = \varphi \circ \pi$ is injective and $\varphi$ is an isomorphism (henc
e injective), it follows that $\pi$ is injective: indeed, if $\pi(a) = \pi(b)$,
then $f(a) = f(b)$, so $a = b$ by injectivity of $f$.

Therefore
$$I = \ker \pi = (0). \qquad \blacksquare$$

---

**Remark.** The Noetherian hypothesis is essential. Without it, non-Hopfian ring
s exist: e.g. the ring of polynomials in countably many variables $R = k[x_1, x_
2, x_3, \dots]$ admits the surjective endomorphism $x_1 \mapsto 0,\; x_{i} \maps
to x_{i-1}$ for $i \geq 2$, whose kernel $(x_1)$ is nonzero, yielding $R/(x_1) \
cong R$ with $(x_1) \neq (0)$. The ascending chain condition is precisely what p
revents such behavior.
