## Proof

ian.

**Setup.** Let $G$ be a finite group with $(ab)^n = a^n b^n$ for all $a,b \in G$
, where $n$ is a positive integer and $\gcd(|G|,\, n(n-1)) = 1$. In particular
$$\gcd(|G|, n) = 1 \quad\text{and}\quad \gcd(|G|, n-1) = 1.$$

**Step 1: A derived identity.** For all $a, b \in G$, using $(ab)^n = a(ba)^{n-1
}b$ together with the hypothesis $(ab)^n = a^n b^n$:
$$a^n b^n = a\,(ba)^{n-1}\,b.$$
Multiplying on the left by $a^{-1}$ and on the right by $b^{-1}$:
$$(ba)^{n-1} = a^{n-1} b^{n-1}. \tag{$\star$}$$

Swapping the roles of $a$ and $b$ in $(\star)$:
$$(ab)^{n-1} = b^{n-1} a^{n-1}. \tag{$\star\star$}$$

**Step 2: A commutation relation.** Compute $(ab)^n$ in two ways. On one hand, $
(ab)^n = a^n b^n$ by hypothesis. On the other hand, using $(\star\star)$:
$$(ab)^n = (ab)^{n-1}(ab) = b^{n-1} a^{n-1}\, a\, b = b^{n-1} a^n b.$$
Equating:
$$a^n b^n = b^{n-1} a^n b.$$
Multiplying on the right by $b^{-1}$:
$$a^n\, b^{n-1} = b^{n-1}\, a^n \quad\text{for all } a, b \in G. \tag{$\dagger$}
$$

**Step 3: The power maps are surjective.**

- Since $\gcd(|G|, n) = 1$, for every $g \in G$ we have $\gcd(\operatorname{ord}
(g), n) = 1$ (as $\operatorname{ord}(g) \mid |G|$). Hence there exists an intege
r $m$ with $mn \equiv 1 \pmod{\operatorname{ord}(g)}$, giving $g = (g^m)^n$. So
every element of $G$ is an $n$-th power:
$$\{a^n : a \in G\} = G.$$

- Likewise, since $\gcd(|G|, n-1) = 1$, the same argument gives
$$\{b^{n-1} : b \in G\} = G.$$

**Step 4: Conclusion.** Let $x, y \in G$ be arbitrary. By Step 3, $x = a^n$ for
some $a \in G$ and $y = b^{n-1}$ for some $b \in G$. By $(\dagger)$:
$$xy = a^n\, b^{n-1} = b^{n-1}\, a^n = yx.$$
Since $x$ and $y$ were arbitrary, $G$ is commutative. $\blacksquare$
