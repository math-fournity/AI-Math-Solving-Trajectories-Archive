## Step 1: $p_1$ can never be stable

$p_1 = x_1 - \frac{1}{x_1} = \frac{x_1^2 - 1}{x_1}$. Since $\gcd(x_1, x_1^2-1) =
 \gcd(x_1,1) = 1$, we need $x_1 \mid 1$, so $x_1 = 1$. But then $p_1 = 0$, which
 is not a **positive** integer. For $x_1 \geq 2$, $x_1 \nmid (x_1^2-1)$, so $p_1
$ is not an integer.

**Therefore the maximum is at most $2023$** (only $p_2, \ldots, p_{2024}$ can po
tentially be stable).

## Step 2: Construction achieving 2023

Take **consecutive integers** starting from 2: $x_k = k+1$ for $k = 1, 2, \ldots

**Claim:** $p_i$ is a positive integer for every $i \geq 2$.

**Proof of claim.** Compute:
$$p_i = \prod_{k=1}^{i}\frac{x_k^2-1}{x_k} = \frac{\prod_{k=1}^{i}(x_k-1)(x_k+1)
}{\prod_{k=1}^{i}x_k}.$$

With $x_k = k+1$, the numerator is:
$$\prod_{k=1}^{i}(k)(k+2) = \left(\prod_{k=1}^{i} k\right)\left(\prod_{k=1}^{i}(
k+2)\right) = i! \cdot \frac{(i+2)!}{2!} = i! \cdot \frac{(i+2)!}{2}.$$

The denominator is:
$$\prod_{k=1}^{i}(k+1) = \frac{(i+1)!}{1!} = (i+1)!$$

So:
$$p_i = \frac{i! \cdot (i+2)!/2}{(i+1)!} = \frac{i! \cdot (i+2)!}{2 \cdot (i+1)!
} = \frac{i! \cdot (i+2)}{2}.$$

For $i \geq 2$: $i!$ contains the factor $2$ (since $i \geq 2$), so $\frac{i!}{2
