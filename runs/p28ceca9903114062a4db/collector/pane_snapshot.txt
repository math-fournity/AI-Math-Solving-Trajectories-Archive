**Claim.** $Y_n \xrightarrow{d} N(0,1)$ as $n \to \infty$.

**Proof.** Write

$$Y_n = \frac{\sqrt{n}\,X_0}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i^2}} = \frac{X_0}{\sqrt{\tf
rac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i^2}}.$$

The random variables $X_1, X_2, \dots$ are i.i.d. with $E[X_1^2] = \operatorname
{Var}(X_1) = 1 < \infty$. By the Strong Law of Large Numbers,

$$\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i^2 \xrightarrow{a.s.} E[X_1^2] = 1.$$

Since the square-root function is continuous on $(0,\infty)$ and the limit is $1
 > 0$, by the continuous mapping theorem

$$\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i^2} \xrightarrow{a.s.} 1.$$

Crucially, $X_0$ is independent of $\{X_1, \dots, X_n\}$ for every $n$, hence in
dependent of $\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i^2$. Therefore the pair

$$\left(X_0,\; \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i^2}\right) \xrightarrow{d} (X
_0,\, 1),$$

where the first component is the constant-in-$n$ random variable $X_0 \sim N(0,1
)$. Applying the continuous mapping $g(a,b) = a/b$ (continuous at every point wi
th $b \neq 0$, and the limit has $b = 1$),

$$Y_n = \frac{X_0}{\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i^2}} \xrightarrow{d} \fra
c{X_0}{1} = X_0 \sim N(0,1).$$

**Remark (exact distribution).** In fact $Y_n$ has exactly a Student-$t$ distrib
ution with $n$ degrees of freedom: since $\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i^2 \sim \chi^2_n$ is
 independent of $X_0 \sim N(0,1)$,

$$Y_n = \frac{X_0}{\sqrt{\chi^2_n / n}} \sim t_n,$$

and the well-known limit $t_n \xrightarrow{d} N(0,1)$ as $n \to \infty$ recovers
 the same conclusion — confirming the result above.

---

**Conclusion.** The limiting distribution function of $Y_n$ as $n \to \infty$ is
 the standard normal cumulative distribution function

$$F(y) = \Phi(y) = \int_{-\infty}^{y} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-t^2/2}\,dt, \qqu
ad y \in \mathbb{R}.$$
