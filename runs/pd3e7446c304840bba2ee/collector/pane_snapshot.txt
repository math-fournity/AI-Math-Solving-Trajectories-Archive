# Proof: Complete Regularity Implies Regularity

## Definitions

Recall the standard separation axioms (with the T1 convention):

- A topological space $X$ is **regular** (i.e. $T_3$) if $X$ is $T_1$ and for ev
ery closed set $F \subseteq X$ and every point $x \in X \setminus F$, there exis
t disjoint open sets $U, V \subseteq X$ with $x \in U$ and $F \subseteq V$.

- A topological space $X$ is **completely regular** (i.e. $T_{3\frac{1}{2}}$, Ty
chonoff) if $X$ is $T_1$ and for every closed set $F \subseteq X$ and every poin
t $x \in X \setminus F$, there exists a continuous function $f : X \to [0,1]$ su
ch that $f(x) = 0$ and $f(F) \subseteq \{1\}$.

## Claim

**Every completely regular space is regular.** That is, complete regularity impl
ies regularity.

## Proof

Let $X$ be a completely regular space. We verify the two conditions for regulari
ty.

**(1) $X$ is $T_1$.**
This is immediate: complete regularity includes $T_1$ as part of its definition.

**(2) Point–closed-set separation by disjoint open sets.**

Let $F \subseteq X$ be closed and let $x \in X \setminus F$. By complete regular
ity, there exists a continuous function
$$f : X \to [0,1]$$
such that $f(x) = 0$ and $f(y) = 1$ for every $y \in F$.

Define
$$U := f^{-1}\bigl([0, \tfrac{1}{2})\bigr), \qquad V := f^{-1}\bigl((\tfrac{1}{2
}, 1]\bigr).$$

Since $f$ is continuous and $[0, \tfrac{1}{2})$ and $(\tfrac{1}{2}, 1]$ are open
 in the subspace topology of $[0,1]$ (note $[0,\tfrac{1}{2}) = [0,1] \cap (-\inf
ty, \tfrac{1}{2})$ and $(\tfrac{1}{2},1] = [0,1] \cap (\tfrac{1}{2}, \infty)$, b
oth being intersections of $[0,1]$ with open subsets of $\mathbb{R}$), the preim
ages $U$ and $V$ are open in $X$.

**Disjointness:** The intervals $[0, \tfrac{1}{2})$ and $(\tfrac{1}{2}, 1]$ are
disjoint (they meet only at the excluded point $\tfrac{1}{2}$), so their preimag
es are disjoint:
$$U \cap V = f^{-1}\bigl([0,\tfrac{1}{2})\bigr) \cap f^{-1}\bigl((\tfrac{1}{2},1
]\bigr) = f^{-1}\bigl([0,\tfrac{1}{2}) \cap (\tfrac{1}{2},1]\bigr) = f^{-1}(\var
nothing) = \varnothing.$$

**$x \in U$:** Since $f(x) = 0 \in [0, \tfrac{1}{2})$, we have $x \in U$.

**$F \subseteq V$:** For every $y \in F$, $f(y) = 1 \in (\tfrac{1}{2}, 1]$, so $
y \in V$.

Thus $U$ and $V$ are disjoint open neighborhoods of $x$ and $F$ respectively.

## Conclusion

Since $X$ is $T_1$ and every point can be separated from every closed set not co
ntaining it by disjoint open sets, $X$ is regular. Therefore **complete regulari
ty implies regularity**. $\blacksquare$
