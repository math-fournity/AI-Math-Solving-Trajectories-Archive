# Problem

An elite logistics firm is evaluating the efficiency of three cargo transport routes with lengths $a$, $b$, and $c$. These routes are configured such that they form a closed triangular circuit. To optimize their fleet, the engineers analyze the "frequency of passage" for each route, defined as the reciprocal of each length: $1/a$, $1/b$, and $1/c$. Interestingly, these three frequency values are also capable of forming a closed triangular configuration.

The firm uses a specific "Congestion Index" to measure the network's performance, calculated by multiplying the sum of the route lengths $(a+b+c)$ by the sum of their frequencies $(1/a + 1/b + 1/c)$. 

It is mathematically guaranteed that the minimum possible value for this Congestion Index is $9$. Based on the constraint that both the lengths and their reciprocals must satisfy the triangle inequality, find the maximum possible value $M$ that this Congestion Index can reach.
