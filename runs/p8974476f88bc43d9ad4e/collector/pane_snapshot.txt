Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every linear functional on
an algebra must vanish on some finite-codimension ideal.

**Answer: No.**

**Counterexample.** Let $\mathbb{k} = \mathbb{Q}$ and $A = \mathbb{Q}[x]$, the p
olynomial algebra (commutative, associative, unital). Define the $\mathbb{Q}$-li
near functional $f: A \to \mathbb{Q}$ by setting
$$f(x^n) = n! \quad \text{for all } n \geq 0,$$
and extending linearly.

**Claim.** There is no ideal $I \trianglelefteq A$ with $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(A/I)
< \infty$ and $f|_I = 0$.

**Proof of claim.** Since $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ is a PID, every nonzero ideal is princ
ipal: $I = (g)$ for some nonzero $g \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$. The quotient $\mathbb{Q}
[x]/(g)$ has dimension $\deg(g)$, so ideals of finite codimension are exactly th
e nonzero principal ideals $(g)$ with $g \neq 0$ (and $\{0\}$, which has infinit
e codimension since $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}[x] = \aleph_0$).

Suppose for contradiction that $f|_{(g)} = 0$ for some nonzero $g = \sum_{i=0}^{
d} b_i x^i$ with $b_d \neq 0$. Then $f(g \cdot h) = 0$ for every $h \in \mathbb{
Q}[x]$. Taking $h = x^n$ for each $n \geq 0$:
$$0 = f(g \cdot x^n) = f\!\left(\sum_{i=0}^{d} b_i\, x^{n+i}\right) = \sum_{i=0}
^{d} b_i\,(n+i)!\,.$$

Dividing both sides by $n!$ (which is nonzero in $\mathbb{Q}$):
$$0 = \sum_{i=0}^{d} b_i \prod_{j=1}^{i}(n+j) \quad \text{for all } n \geq 0. \t
ag{$\ast$}$$

The right-hand side of $(\ast)$ is a polynomial in $n$. Its leading term (highes
t degree in $n$) comes from $i = d$:
$$b_d \prod_{j=1}^{d}(n+j),$$
which is a polynomial of degree $d$ with leading coefficient $b_d \neq 0$. Since
 $(\ast)$ holds for all $n \geq 0$, this polynomial must be identically zero, fo
rcing $b_d = 0$—a contradiction.

(More explicitly: once $b_d = 0$, the remaining sum has degree $d-1$ with leadin
g coefficient $b_{d-1}$, forcing $b_{d-1} = 0$, and by induction all $b_i = 0$,
contradicting $g \neq 0$.)

Therefore no nonzero ideal is contained in $\ker(f)$. The only ideal contained i
n $\ker(f)$ is $\{0\}$, which has infinite codimension. Hence $f$ is not zero on
 any ideal of finite codimension. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The argument reveals the underlying structure: for $A = \mathbb{k}[x
]$, an ideal of finite codimension is $(g)$ with $\deg g = d < \infty$, and $f|_
{(g)} = 0$ is equivalent to the sequence $(f(x^n))_{n \geq 0}$ satisfying the li
near recurrence determined by $g$. The sequence $a_n = n!$ satisfies no linear r
ecurrence (as the polynomial argument above shows), giving the counterexample.
