## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. If $f: \mathbb{X} \times \mathbb{Y} \to [0, +\infty]$ is $\maths
cr{A}$-measurable, then $f(x, y_1) = f(x, y_2)$ for all $x \in \mathbb{X}$ and a
ll $y_1, y_2 \in \mathbb{Y}$.

**Step 1: $\mathscr{A}$ is a $\sigma$-algebra on $\mathbb{X} \times \mathbb{Y}$.
**

- $\mathbb{X} \times \mathbb{Y} \in \mathscr{A}$ (take $X = \mathbb{X}$), and $\
emptyset = \emptyset \times \mathbb{Y} \in \mathscr{A}$.
- If $X \times \mathbb{Y} \in \mathscr{A}$, then its complement in $\mathbb{X} \
times \mathbb{Y}$ is $(\mathbb{X} \setminus X) \times \mathbb{Y} \in \mathscr{A}
$.
- If $\{X_n \times \mathbb{Y}\}_{n \geq 1} \subseteq \mathscr{A}$, then $\bigcup
_n (X_n \times \mathbb{Y}) = \left(\bigcup_n X_n\right) \times \mathbb{Y} \in \m
athscr{A}$.

**Step 2: Structure of $\mathscr{A}$-measurable preimages.**

Since $f$ is $\mathscr{A}$-measurable, for every Borel set $B \in \mathscr{B}([0
,+\infty])$, the preimage $f^{-1}(B) \in \mathscr{A}$. By definition of $\mathsc
r{A}$, there exists $X_B \in \mathscr{X}$ such that

$$f^{-1}(B) = X_B \times \mathbb{Y}.$$

The crucial property of such a set is: **membership depends only on the first co
ordinate.** Specifically, for any $x \in \mathbb{X}$ and any $y, y' \in \mathbb{
Y}$:

$$(x, y) \in X_B \times \mathbb{Y} \iff x \in X_B \iff (x, y') \in X_B \times \m
athbb{Y}.$$

**Step 3: $f(x, \cdot)$ is constant on $\mathbb{Y}$ for each fixed $x$.**

Fix $x \in \mathbb{X}$ and suppose for contradiction that there exist $y_1, y_2
\in \mathbb{Y}$ with $f(x, y_1) \neq f(x, y_2)$. Set $a = f(x, y_1)$, $b = f(x,
y_2)$, with $a \neq b$.

**Case $a < b$:** Let $c = (a+b)/2$ and $B = [0, c] \cap [0,+\infty]$, which is
a Borel set in $[0,+\infty]$. Then $a \in B$ but $b \notin B$. So:
- $(x, y_1) \in f^{-1}(B) = X_B \times \mathbb{Y}$, hence $x \in X_B$.
- But then $(x, y_2) \in X_B \times \mathbb{Y} = f^{-1}(B)$, so $f(x, y_2) \in B
$, i.e., $b \in B$. **Contradiction** with $b \notin B$.

**Case $a > b$:** Same argument with $y_1$ and $y_2$ swapped (or using $B = [0,
(a+b)/2]$, which contains $b$ but not $a$, leading to the same contradiction).

In both cases we reach a contradiction. Therefore $f(x, y_1) = f(x, y_2)$.

**Conclusion:** Since $x \in \mathbb{X}$ and $y_1, y_2 \in \mathbb{Y}$ were arbi
trary, $f$ is constant in its second argument. Equivalently, $f$ factors through
 the projection $\pi_{\mathbb{X}}: \mathbb{X} \times \mathbb{Y} \to \mathbb{X}$;
 there exists an $\mathscr{X}$-measurable function $g: \mathbb{X} \to [0,+\infty
]$ such that $f(x, y) = g(x)$ for all $(x, y) \in \mathbb{X} \times \mathbb{Y}$.

### PROOF COMPLETE
