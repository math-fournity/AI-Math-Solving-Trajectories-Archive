## Proof

**The statement is TRUE.**

Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ be a bounded domain with smooth boundary. Reca
ll:
- $H^1(\Omega) = \{u \in L^2(\Omega) : \partial_i u \in L^2(\Omega),\; i=1,\dots
,n\}$, with norm $\|u\|_{H^1}^2 = \|u\|_{L^2}^2 + \|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^2$.
- $H^{-1}(\Omega) = \big(H^1_0(\Omega)\big)^*$, the dual of $H^1_0(\Omega)$ (the
 closure of $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ in the $H^1$-norm).

We must show: for every $u \in H^1(\Omega)$, the distributional Laplacian $\Delt
a u$ defines a bounded linear functional on $H^1_0(\Omega)$, hence belongs to $H
^{-1}(\Omega)$.

**Step 1: Define the distributional Laplacian.** For $u \in H^1(\Omega)$ and $\v
arphi \in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$, set
$$\langle \Delta u, \varphi \rangle := \int_\Omega u\,\Delta\varphi\,dx.$$
Since $\varphi$ has compact support in $\Omega$, integration by parts produces *
*no boundary term**:
$$\int_\Omega u\,\Delta\varphi\,dx = -\int_\Omega \nabla u \cdot \nabla\varphi\,
dx.$$

**Step 2: Establish the bound.** By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,
$$\big|\langle \Delta u, \varphi\rangle\big| = \left|\int_\Omega \nabla u \cdot
\nabla\varphi\,dx\right| \le \|\nabla u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\,\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^2(
\Omega)} \le \|u\|_{H^1(\Omega)}\,\|\varphi\|_{H^1_0(\Omega)}.$$

**Step 3: Extend by density.** $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ is dense in $H^1_0(\Omega)$
by definition. The estimate above shows that the linear map
$$\varphi \mapsto \langle \Delta u, \varphi\rangle$$
is bounded on $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ with respect to the $H^1_0$-norm, with operat
or norm $\le \|u\|_{H^1}$. By the BLT (bounded linear transformation) theorem, i
t extends uniquely to a bounded linear functional on all of $H^1_0(\Omega)$, sti
ll satisfying
$$\big|\langle \Delta u, \varphi\rangle\big| \le \|u\|_{H^1}\,\|\varphi\|_{H^1_0
}, \qquad \forall\,\varphi\in H^1_0(\Omega).$$

**Step 4: Conclude.** This extension is precisely an element of $\big(H^1_0(\Ome
ga)\big)^* = H^{-1}(\Omega)$, with
$$\|\Delta u\|_{H^{-1}} \le \|u\|_{H^1}.$$

Therefore the map $u \mapsto \Delta u$ sends $H^1(\Omega)$ continuously into $H^
{-1}(\Omega)$. In particular, for every $u\in H^1(\Omega)$, $\Delta u \in H^{-1}
(\Omega)$.

**Remark.** The argument uses only that $\nabla u \in L^2(\Omega)$, which is exa
ctly the content of $u\in H^1(\Omega)$. The boundedness and smoothness of $\part
ial\Omega$ ensure the standard Sobolev framework is in force, though the estimat
e itself is interior (no boundary trace of $u$ is needed) and would in fact hold
 for any open set $\Omega$.
