## Proof

**Claim.** $P(T = Y) = 1$.

**Setup and key fact.** Recall that almost sure convergence implies convergence
in probability: if $X_n \to Y$ almost surely, then for every $\epsilon > 0$,
$$P(|X_n - Y| > \epsilon) \;\longrightarrow\; 0.$$
Indeed, $X_n \to Y$ a.s. means $X_n \to Y$ pointwise on an event $\Omega_0$ with
 $P(\Omega_0)=1$; on $\Omega_0$ the indicator $\mathbf{1}_{\{|X_n - Y|>\epsilon\
}} \to 0$, so by dominated convergence $P(|X_n - Y| > \epsilon) = E[\mathbf{1}_{
\{|X_n-Y|>\epsilon\}}] \to 0$.

Thus from the two hypotheses we have **both**
$$X_n \xrightarrow{P} T \qquad\text{and}\qquad X_n \xrightarrow{P} Y.$$

**Uniqueness of limits in probability.** Fix $\epsilon > 0$. By the triangle ine
quality, whenever $|T - Y| > \epsilon$ we must have either $|T - X_n| > \epsilon
/2$ or $|X_n - Y| > \epsilon/2$. Hence by a union bound,
$$P(|T - Y| > \epsilon) \;\le\; P\!\bigl(|T - X_n| > \epsilon/2\bigr) \;+\; P\!\
bigl(|X_n - Y| > \epsilon/2\bigr).$$

Both terms on the right tend to $0$ as $n \to \infty$ (the first by $X_n \xright
arrow{P} T$, the second by $X_n \xrightarrow{P} Y$, which we derived from a.s. c
onvergence). Therefore
$$P(|T - Y| > \epsilon) = 0 \qquad\text{for every } \epsilon > 0.$$

**Conclusion.** Apply this with $\epsilon = 1/k$ for $k = 1, 2, \dots$ Since $\{
|T - Y| > 0\} = \bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty} \{|T - Y| > 1/k\}$, a countable union,
$$P(T \neq Y) \;=\; P(|T - Y| > 0) \;\le\; \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} P\!\bigl(|T - Y|
> 1/k\bigr) \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} 0 \;=\; 0.$$

Hence $P(T = Y) = 1$. $\blacksquare$
