# Proof: The Completion of a Topological Group is Complete

**Answer: Yes.** The completion $\hat{G}$ of a topological group $G$ is complete
: every Cauchy filter (and hence every Cauchy sequence) in $\hat{G}$ converges.

---

## Setup and Construction of $\hat{G}$

Let $G$ be a topological group with identity $e$, and let $\mathfrak{N}$ denote
the set of open neighborhoods of $e$.

**Cauchy filters.** A filter $\mathcal{F}$ on $G$ is *Cauchy* if for every $U \i
n \mathfrak{N}$, there exists $A \in \mathcal{F}$ with $A^{-1}A \subset U$. A Ca
uchy filter is *minimal* if it contains no proper Cauchy subfilter.

**The completion.** Define $\hat{G}$ as the set of all minimal Cauchy filters on
 $G$. The group structure is inherited from $G$: for $\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G} \
in \hat{G}$, the product $\mathcal{F}\mathcal{G}$ is the minimal Cauchy filter g
enerated by $\{AB : A \in \mathcal{F}, B \in \mathcal{G}\}$, and $\mathcal{F}^{-
1}$ is generated by $\{A^{-1} : A \in \mathcal{F}\}$.

**Topology on $\hat{G}$.** For each $U \in \mathfrak{N}$, define
$$\hat{U} = \{\mathcal{F} \in \hat{G} : U \in \mathcal{F}\}.$$
The collection $\{\hat{U} : U \in \mathfrak{N}\}$ forms a neighborhood basis at
the identity $\hat{e}$ (the minimal Cauchy filter of all sets containing $e$), a
nd translations give the full topology.

**Embedding.** The map $\iota: G \to \hat{G}$, $g \mapsto \mathcal{F}_g$ (the pr
incipal ultrafilter at $g$), is a continuous injective homomorphism.

**Key facts used below** (each verified directly from definitions):

- **(F1)** $G \cap \hat{V} = V$ for all $V \in \mathfrak{N}$: $\mathcal{F}_g \in
 \hat{V} \iff V \in \mathcal{F}_g \iff g \in V$.
- **(F2)** $\hat{U}\hat{W} \subset \widehat{UW}$: if $\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{G}\
mathcal{H}$ with $U \in \mathcal{G}, W \in \mathcal{H}$, then $UW \in \mathcal{F
}$.
- **(F3)** $\hat{U}^{-1} = \widehat{U^{-1}}$: $\mathcal{F} \in \hat{U}^{-1} \iff
 U \in \mathcal{F}^{-1} \iff U^{-1} \in \mathcal{F}$.
- **(F4)** $G$ is dense in $\hat{G}$: for any $\hat{x} \in \hat{G}$ and $U \in \
mathfrak{N}$, since $\hat{x}$ is Cauchy, pick $A \in \hat{x}$ with $A^{-1}A \sub
set U$ and $a \in A$; then $A \subset aU$ so $aU \in \hat{x}$, giving $\mathcal{
F}_a \in \hat{x}\hat{U}^{-1} \cap G \neq \emptyset$.

---

## Proof that $\hat{G}$ is Complete

Let $\Phi$ be a Cauchy filter on $\hat{G}$. We construct a limit point $\hat{g}_
0 \in \hat{G}$ and show $\Phi \to \hat{g}_0$.

### Step 1: Construct a Cauchy filter $\Psi$ on $G$

For each $A \in \Phi$ (a subset of $\hat{G}$) and each $U \in \mathfrak{N}$, def
ine
$$\widetilde{A}_U = \{g \in G : g\hat{U} \cap A \neq \emptyset\} = \bigcup_{\hat
{x} \in A} (\hat{x}\hat{U}^{-1} \cap G).$$

By density **(F4)**, each $\widetilde{A}_U$ is nonempty. The collection $\{\wide
tilde{A}_U : A \in \Phi,\, U \in \mathfrak{N}\}$ is a filter base: for $A_1, A_2
 \in \Phi$ and $U_1, U_2 \in \mathfrak{N}$, we have $A_1 \cap A_2 \in \Phi$ and
$$\widetilde{A_1 \cap A_2}_{\,U_1 \cap U_2} \subset \widetilde{A_1}_{U_1} \cap \
widetilde{A_2}_{U_2}.$$
Let $\Psi$ be the filter on $G$ generated by this base.

**Claim: $\Psi$ is Cauchy.** Given $V \in \mathfrak{N}$, choose symmetric $V' \i
n \mathfrak{N}$ with $(V')^3 \subset V$ (possible by continuity of multiplicatio
n). Since $\Phi$ is Cauchy on $\hat{G}$, there exists $A \in \Phi$ with $A^{-1}A
 \subset \hat{V'}$, meaning $\hat{x}^{-1}\hat{y} \in \hat{V'}$ for all $\hat{x},
 \hat{y} \in A$.

We show $\widetilde{A}_{V'}^{-1}\widetilde{A}_{V'} \subset V$. Let $g, h \in \wi
detilde{A}_{V'}$. Then $g \in \hat{x}\widehat{V'}^{-1} \cap G$ and $h \in \hat{y
}\widehat{V'}^{-1} \cap G$ for some $\hat{x}, \hat{y} \in A$, so $g^{-1}\hat{x}
\in \hat{V'}$ and $h^{-1}\hat{y} \in \hat{V'}$. Then
$$g^{-1}h = (g^{-1}\hat{x})(\hat{x}^{-1}\hat{y})(\hat{y}^{-1}h) \in \hat{V'} \cd
ot \hat{V'} \cdot \widehat{V'}^{-1} \subset \widehat{(V')^3} \subset \hat{V},$$
using **(F2)**, **(F3)**, and symmetry of $V'$. Since $g^{-1}h \in G$, **(F1)**
gives $g^{-1}h \in V$. $\checkmark$

### Step 2: Obtain the limit point

Let $\mathcal{F}_0$ be the minimal Cauchy filter on $G$ containing $\Psi$ (obtai
ned by intersecting all Cauchy filters containing $\Psi$, or equivalently taking
 the filter generated by all Cauchy subfamilies). Set $\hat{g}_0 := \mathcal{F}_
0 \in \hat{G}$.

### Step 3: $\Phi$ converges to $\hat{g}_0$

Let $\hat{W}$ be a basic neighborhood of $\hat{e}$, corresponding to $W \in \mat
hfrak{N}$. We must show $\hat{g}_0\hat{W} \in \Phi$.

Choose symmetric $W_1, W_2 \in \mathfrak{N}$ with $W_2^2 \subset W_1$ and $W_1^2
 \subset W$.

Since $\Phi$ is Cauchy, pick $A_0 \in \Phi$ with $A_0^{-1}A_0 \subset \hat{W_2}$
.

**Sub-claim:** $A_0 \subset \hat{g}_0\hat{W}$.

*Proof of sub-claim.* Let $\hat{y} \in A_0$. We show $\hat{y} \in \hat{g}_0\hat{
W}$, i.e., $\hat{g}_0^{-1}\hat{y} \in \hat{W}$, i.e., $W \in \mathcal{F}_0^{-1}\
hat{y}$. This requires finding $B_0 \in \mathcal{F}_0$ and $C_0 \in \hat{y}$ wit
h $B_0^{-1}C_0 \subset W$.

Since $\widetilde{A_0}_{W_2} \in \Psi \subset \mathcal{F}_0$ and $\mathcal{F}_0$
 is Cauchy, there exists $B_0 \in \mathcal{F}_0$ with $B_0 \subset \widetilde{A_
0}_{W_2}$ and $B_0^{-1}B_0 \subset W_2$. Pick $g_0 \in B_0$.

Since $g_0 \in \widetilde{A_0}_{W_2}$, there exists $\hat{z} \in A_0$ with $g_0^
{-1}\hat{z} \in \hat{W_2}$, so $W_2 \in g_0^{-1}\hat{z}$: there exists $D \in \h
at{z}$ with $g_0^{-1}D \subset W_2$, i.e., $D \subset g_0 W_2$.

Since $\hat{y}, \hat{z} \in A_0$ and $A_0^{-1}A_0 \subset \hat{W_2}$, we have $\
hat{z}^{-1}\hat{y} \in \hat{W_2}$, so $W_2 \in \hat{z}^{-1}\hat{y}$: there exist
 $E \in \hat{z}$, $F \in \hat{y}$ with $E^{-1}F \subset W_2$.

Now $D, E \in \hat{z}$, so $D \cap E \in \hat{z}$ and is nonempty. Pick $d \in D
 \cap E$. Then:
- $d \in D \subset g_0 W_2$, so $g_0^{-1}d \in W_2$.
- $d \in E$ and $E^{-1}F \subset W_2$, so $d^{-1}F \subset W_2$, i.e., $F \subse
t d\, W_2$.

Combining: $g_0^{-1}F \subset g_0^{-1}d\, W_2 \subset W_2^2 \subset W_1$, so $F
\subset g_0 W_1$.

Set $C_0 := F \in \hat{y}$. Then:
$$B_0^{-1}C_0 = B_0^{-1}F \subset B_0^{-1}(g_0 W_1).$$
Since $g_0 \in B_0$ and $B_0^{-1}B_0 \subset W_2$, we have $B_0^{-1}g_0 \subset
W_2$, so
$$B_0^{-1}C_0 \subset W_2 \cdot W_1 \subset W_1 \cdot W_1 = W_1^2 \subset W.$$

Thus $W \in \mathcal{F}_0^{-1}\hat{y}$, proving $\hat{y} \in \hat{g}_0\hat{W}$.
Since $\hat{y} \in A_0$ was arbitrary, $A_0 \subset \hat{g}_0\hat{W}$. $\checkma
rk$

Since $A_0 \in \Phi$ and $A_0 \subset \hat{g}_0\hat{W}$, the filter property giv
es $\hat{g}_0\hat{W} \in \Phi$. As $\hat{W}$ was an arbitrary basic neighborhood
, $\Phi$ converges to $\hat{g}_0$.

### Conclusion

Every Cauchy filter on $\hat{G}$ converges. In particular, when $G$ (and hence $
\hat{G}$) is first-countable, every Cauchy sequence in $\hat{G}$ converges. Ther
efore $\hat{G}$ is complete. $\blacksquare$
