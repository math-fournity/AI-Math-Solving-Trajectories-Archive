## Claim

Let $(B_t)_{t \geq 0}$ be a standard Brownian motion on a probability space $(\O
mega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$, and let $(\mathcal{F}_t)_{t \geq 0}$ be its nat
ural filtration. Then the process $X_t := B_t^2$ is a Markov process (with respe
ct to its own natural filtration $\mathcal{G}_t := \sigma(X_s : s \leq t)$).

## Proof

We must show that for every $0 \leq s \leq t$ and every bounded Borel function $
f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$,

$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(X_t) \mid \mathcal{G}_s\bigr] = \mathbb{E}\bigl[f(X_t) \mid
X_s\bigr] \quad \text{a.s.}$$

The proof proceeds in two steps.

### Step 1. Conditional expectation given the Brownian filtration

Since $B_t = B_s + (B_t - B_s)$ and the increment $Z := B_t - B_s$ is independen
t of $\mathcal{F}_s$ with distribution $N(0,\, t-s)$, we have

$$X_t = B_t^2 = (B_s + Z)^2.$$

Because $Z$ is independent of $\mathcal{F}_s$, conditioning on $\mathcal{F}_s$ r
educes to conditioning on $B_s$:

$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(B_t^2) \mid \mathcal{F}_s\bigr] = \mathbb{E}\bigl[f\bigl((B_
s + Z)^2\bigr) \mid \mathcal{F}_s\bigr] = g(B_s), \tag{1}$$

where we define the deterministic function

$$g(x) := \mathbb{E}\bigl[f\bigl((x + Z)^2\bigr)\bigr] = \int_{\mathbb{R}} f\big
l((x+z)^2\bigr)\, \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t-s)}} e^{-z^2/(2(t-s))}\, dz.$$

### Step 2. $g$ is an even function, so $g(B_s)$ is $\sigma(B_s^2)$-measurable

The Gaussian distribution is symmetric: $Z \stackrel{d}{=} -Z$. Therefore, for e
very $x \in \mathbb{R}$,

$$g(x) = \mathbb{E}\bigl[f\bigl((x+Z)^2\bigr)\bigr] = \mathbb{E}\bigl[f\bigl((x-
Z)^2\bigr)\bigr] = \mathbb{E}\bigl(f\bigl((-x+Z)^2\bigr)\bigr) = g(-x).$$

Thus $g$ is **even**: $g(x) = g(-x)$ for all $x$. An even Borel function of $B_s
$ is a Borel function of $B_s^2$; explicitly, there exists a Borel function $h :
 [0,\infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ such that $g(x) = h(x^2)$. Consequently

$$g(B_s) = h(B_s^2) = h(X_s), \tag{2}$$

which is $\sigma(X_s)$-measurable (and in particular $\mathcal{G}_s$-measurable)
.

### Step 3. Projection onto the natural filtration of $X$

Since $\mathcal{G}_s = \sigma(B_u^2 : u \leq s) \subseteq \mathcal{F}_s$, we app
ly the tower property of conditional expectation together with (1) and (2):

$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(X_t) \mid \mathcal{G}_s\bigr] = \mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathbb{E}\b
igl[f(X_t) \mid \mathcal{F}_s\bigr] \;\Big|\; \mathcal{G}_s\Bigr] = \mathbb{E}\b
igl[g(B_s) \mid \mathcal{G}_s\bigr] = \mathbb{E}\bigl[h(X_s) \mid \mathcal{G}_s\
bigr] = h(X_s).$$

Since $h(X_s)$ is $\sigma(X_s)$-measurable, this yields

$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(X_t) \mid \mathcal{G}_s\bigr] = h(X_s) = \mathbb{E}\bigl[f(X
_t) \mid X_s\bigr].$$

This is precisely the Markov property. $\blacksquare$

### Remark (transition semigroup)

The transition kernel of $X_t = B_t^2$ is explicitly

$$P_{s,t}(x, A) = \mathbb{P}\bigl((\sqrt{x} + Z)^2 \in A\bigr), \qquad Z \sim N(
0,\, t-s),\; x \geq 0,$$

which depends only on $x = B_s^2$ and the elapsed time $t - s$ (time-homogeneous
). The process $X_t = B_t^2$ is in fact the **squared Bessel process of dimensio
n 1** (BESQ$^{(1)}$), satisfying the SDE

$$dX_t = 1\, dt + 2\sqrt{X_t}\, dW_t, \qquad X_0 = 0,$$

for a suitable Brownian motion $W$, which gives an independent confirmation of t
he Markov property via the theory of Itô diffusions.
