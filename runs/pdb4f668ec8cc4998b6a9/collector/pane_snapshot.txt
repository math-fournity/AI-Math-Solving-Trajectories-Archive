**Step 1: Recognize the polynomial.** By Kronecker's theorem, a monic integer po
lynomial whose roots all lie on the unit circle must be a product of cyclotomic
polynomials. Testing candidates of degree 8, recall that for odd $n$, $\Phi_{2n}
(x) = \Phi_n(-x)$. The 15th cyclotomic polynomial is

$$\Phi_{15}(x) = x^8 - x^7 + x^5 - x^4 + x^3 - x + 1.$$

Substituting $x \mapsto -x$:

$$\Phi_{30}(x) = \Phi_{15}(-x) = x^8 + x^7 - x^5 - x^4 - x^3 + x + 1 = f(x).$$

So $f(x) = \Phi_{30}(x)$.

**Step 2: Identify the roots.** The roots of $\Phi_{30}$ are the primitive 30th
roots of unity $e^{2\pi i k/30}$ with $\gcd(k,30)=1$ and $1 \le k < 30$:

$$k \in \{1, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23, 29\}, \qquad \varphi(30) = 8. \checkmark$$

**Step 3: Apply the constraints.**
- $r \in [0,1)$: satisfied by $r = k/30$.
- $e^{2\pi i r}$ is a root: satisfied.
- Positive imaginary part: $e^{2\pi i k/30}$ has positive imaginary part iff $0
< k < 15$.

From the coprime set, the values with $0 < k < 15$ are $k \in \{1, 7, 11, 13\}$.

**Step 4: Compute the sum.**

$$S = \frac{1+7+11+13}{30} = \frac{32}{30} = \frac{16}{15}.$$

With $p = 16$, $q = 15$ (coprime), we get

$$p + q = 16 + 15 = \boxed{31}.$$
