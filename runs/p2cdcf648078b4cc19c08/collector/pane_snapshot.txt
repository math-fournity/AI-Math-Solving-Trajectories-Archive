## Solution

We solve
$$\frac{(y'')^{2}-y'\,y'''}{(y')^{2}}=\frac{1}{x^{2}}.$$

### 1. A total-derivative observation

Compute
$$\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(\frac{y''}{y'}\right)=\frac{y'''\,y'-(y'')^{2}}{(y')^{2}}=
-\,\frac{(y'')^{2}-y'\,y'''}{(y')^{2}}.$$

Hence the left-hand side of the equation is exactly $-\dfrac{d}{dx}\!\left(\dfra
c{y''}{y'}\right)$.

Since $\dfrac{y''}{y'}=\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln|y'|$, the equation becomes
$$-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\ln|y'|=\frac{1}{x^{2}},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \bigl(\ln
|y'|\bigr)''=-\frac{1}{x^{2}}.$$

### 2. Two integrations

Integrating once with respect to $x$:
$$\bigl(\ln|y'|\bigr)'=\frac{1}{x}+\lambda,\qquad \lambda\in\mathbb{R}.$$

Integrating again:
$$\ln|y'|=\ln|x|+\lambda x+\mu,\qquad \mu\in\mathbb{R}.$$

Exponentiating (and absorbing the sign of $|x|$ and $e^{\mu}$ into a single real
 constant $\nu$):
$$y'=\nu\,x\,e^{\lambda x},\qquad \nu\in\mathbb{R}.$$

### 3. A third integration — two cases

**Case $\lambda\neq 0$.** Integrating by parts,
$$y=\nu\!\int x\,e^{\lambda x}\,dx=\nu\,\frac{e^{\lambda x}(\lambda x-1)}{\lambd
a^{2}}+B.$$
Renaming $\nu/\lambda^{2}=C_{1}$, $B=C_{2}$:
$$\boxed{\,y=C_{1}\,e^{\lambda x}(\lambda x-1)+C_{2}\,},\qquad \lambda\neq 0.$$

**Case $\lambda=0$.** Then $y'=\nu x$, so
$$\boxed{\,y=C_{1}\,x^{2}+C_{2}\,}.$$

### 4. Verification

**General case.** Let $y=C_{1}e^{\lambda x}(\lambda x-1)+C_{2}$. Then
$$y'=C_{1}\,x\,\lambda\,e^{\lambda x}\cdot\lambda^{-1}\cdot\lambda\;\;$$ — let u
s recompute directly. With $y'=\nu\,x\,e^{\lambda x}$:
$$y''=\nu\,e^{\lambda x}(1+\lambda x),\qquad y'''=\nu\,e^{\lambda x}\,\lambda(2+
\lambda x).$$
Therefore
$$(y'')^{2}-y'\,y'''=\nu^{2}e^{2\lambda x}\Big[(1+\lambda x)^{2}-\lambda x(2+\la
mbda x)\Big]$$
$$=\nu^{2}e^{2\lambda x}\Big[1+2\lambda x+\lambda^{2}x^{2}-2\lambda x-\lambda^{2
}x^{2}\Big]=\nu^{2}e^{2\lambda x},$$
and $(y')^{2}=\nu^{2}x^{2}e^{2\lambda x}$. Hence
$$\frac{(y'')^{2}-y'\,y'''}{(y')^{2}}=\frac{\nu^{2}e^{2\lambda x}}{\nu^{2}x^{2}e
^{2\lambda x}}=\frac{1}{x^{2}}.\;\checkmark$$

**Special case $\lambda=0$.** $y=C_{1}x^{2}+C_{2}$ gives $y'=2C_{1}x$, $y''=2C_{
1}$, $y'''=0$, so
$$\frac{(y'')^{2}-y'\,y'''}{(y')^{2}}=\frac{4C_{1}^{2}}{4C_{1}^{2}x^{2}}=\frac{1
}{x^{2}}.\;\checkmark$$

### 5. Counting constants

The original equation involves $y'''$, so three arbitrary constants are expected
. Each family carries $(\lambda,C_{1},C_{2})$ (resp. $(C_{1},C_{2})$ with $\lamb
da=0$ fixed) — three constants total, as required. The additive constant $C_{2}$
 reflects that $y$ itself never appears, only its derivatives.

---

### General solution

$$\boxed{\,y=C_{1}\,e^{\lambda x}(\lambda x-1)+C_{2}\quad(\lambda\neq 0),\qquad
y=C_{1}\,x^{2}+C_{2}\quad(\lambda=0)\,}$$

where $C_{1},C_{2},\lambda$ are arbitrary real constants (with $y'\neq 0$ on the
 interval considered, i.e. $x\neq 0$).
