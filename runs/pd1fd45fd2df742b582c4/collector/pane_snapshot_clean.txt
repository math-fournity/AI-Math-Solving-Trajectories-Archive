Let $G$ be an algebraic group over a field $k$, with coordinate bialgebra $A = k
[G] = \mathcal{O}(G)$ (the ring of regular functions on $G$). Let $V$ be a $k$-v
ector space. I will establish a natural bijection between linear representations
 $\rho: G \to \mathrm{GL}(V)$ and $A$-comodule structures on $V$.

## Setup and the two structures

**Representations.** A linear representation of $G$ on $V$ is a morphism of alge
braic groups
$$\rho: G \to \mathrm{GL}(V),$$
i.e., a morphism of algebraic varieties that is a group homomorphism. Equivalent
ly, it is a regular (algebraic) map
$$\rho: G \to \mathrm{GL}(V) \hookrightarrow \mathrm{End}(V) \cup \{*\}$$
valued in the open subset $\mathrm{GL}(V) \subset \mathrm{End}(V)$ of invertible
 endomorphisms, satisfying $\rho(e) = \mathrm{id}_V$ and $\rho(gh) = \rho(g)\rho
(h)$.

**Comodules.** An $A$-comodule structure on $V$ is a $k$-linear map
$$\Delta_V: V \to V \otimes_k A$$
satisfying the coassociativity axiom $(\Delta_V \otimes \mathrm{id}_A)\circ \Del
ta_V = (\mathrm{id}_V \otimes \Delta)\circ \Delta_V$ and the counitality axiom $
(\mathrm{id}_V \otimes \varepsilon)\circ \Delta_V = \mathrm{id}_V$, where $\Delt
a: A \to A\otimes A$ and $\varepsilon: A \to k$ are the comultiplication and cou
nit of the bialgebra $A = k[G]$.

## The bijection

**From a representation to a comodule.** Given $\rho: G \to \mathrm{GL}(V)$, reg
ard it as a regular map $G \to \mathrm{End}(V)$. The algebra of regular function
s on $\mathrm{End}(V)$ is the symmetric algebra on $V \otimes V^*$; in particula
r, for each $v \in V$ and each $\lambda \in V^*$, the function $T \mapsto \lambd
a(Tv)$ is a regular function on $\mathrm{End}(V)$. Pulling back along $\rho$ giv
es a regular function $\rho^*(\mathrm{ev}_\lambda) \in A = k[G]$.

Define $\Delta_V: V \to V \otimes A$ as follows. For $v \in V$, the element $\De
lta_V(v) \in V \otimes A$ is characterized by
$$\lambda\bigl(\Delta_V(v)\bigr) = \rho^*(\mathrm{ev}_\lambda) \in A \quad \text
{for all } \lambda \in V^*,$$
where we extend $\lambda$ linearly to $V \otimes A \to A$. Concretely, choosing
a basis $\{e_i\}$ of $V$ with dual basis $\{e_i^*\}$, write $\rho(g)(e_j) = \sum
_i a_{ij}(g)\, e_i$ with $a_{ij} \in A$. Then
$$\Delta_V(e_j) = \sum_i e_i \otimes a_{ij}.$$

*Coassociativity.* The identity $\rho(gh) = \rho(g)\rho(h)$ translates, at the l
evel of coordinate functions, into
$$\Delta(a_{ij}) = \sum_k a_{ik} \otimes a_{kj},$$
since $a_{ij}(gh) = \sum_k a_{ik}(g)\,a_{kj}(h)$. Then
$$(\Delta_V \otimes \mathrm{id})(\Delta_V(e_j)) = \sum_{i,k} e_i \otimes a_{ik}
\otimes a_{kj} = (\mathrm{id}\otimes \Delta)(\Delta_V(e_j)),$$
which is coassociativity of $\Delta_V$.

*Counitality.* The identity $\rho(e) = \mathrm{id}_V$ gives $a_{ij}(e) = \delta_
{ij}$, i.e., $\varepsilon(a_{ij}) = \delta_{ij}$. Hence
$$(\mathrm{id}\otimes \varepsilon)(\Delta_V(e_j)) = \sum_i e_i\, \varepsilon(a_{
ij}) = e_j,$$
which is counitality.

**From a comodule to a representation.** Conversely, given $\Delta_V: V \to V\ot
imes A$ satisfying the comodule axioms, choose a basis $\{e_i\}$ and write $\Del
ta_V(e_j) = \sum_i e_i \otimes a_{ij}$ with $a_{ij} \in A$. Define, for each $k$
-point $g \in G$ (or more generally each $R$-point, for a $k$-algebra $R$),
$$\rho(g)(e_j) = \sum_i a_{ij}(g)\, e_i,$$
i.e., $\rho(g)$ is the matrix $(a_{ij}(g))$. Coassociativity of $\Delta_V$ yield
s
$$\sum_k a_{ik}\otimes a_{kj} = \Delta(a_{ij}) = \sum_k a_{ik}\otimes a_{kj},$$
and evaluating at $(g,h) \in G\times G$ gives $\rho(gh) = \rho(g)\rho(h)$. Couni
tality gives $\rho(e) = \mathrm{id}_V$. Finally, the matrix $(a_{ij})$ is invert
ible: the antipode $S$ of the Hopf algebra $A = k[G]$ (which exists because $G$
is a group, so $A$ is a Hopf algebra) satisfies $\sum_k a_{ik}\, S(a_{kj}) = \de
lta_{ij} = \sum_k S(a_{ik})\, a_{kj}$, so $S(a_{ij})$ is the inverse matrix. Thu
s $\rho(g) \in \mathrm{GL}(V)$ for all $g$, and $\rho$ is a morphism of algebrai
c groups $G \to \mathrm{GL}(V)$.

## The two constructions are inverse

Starting from $\rho$, forming $\Delta_V$ via $\Delta_V(e_j) = \sum_i e_i \otimes
 a_{ij}$, and then reconstructing the representation by evaluating $a_{ij}$ at $
g$, recovers $\rho(g)(e_j) = \sum_i a_{ij}(g) e_i = \rho(g)(e_j)$. Conversely, s
tarting from $\Delta_V$ with structure functions $a_{ij}$, building $\rho$ and p
ulling back recovers exactly the same $a_{ij} \in A$. Hence the two construction
s are mutually inverse.

## Functoriality / naturality

The bijection is natural in $V$: a $G$-equivariant linear map $f: V \to W$ is th
e same as an $A$-comodule morphism (a linear map $f$ with $\Delta_W \circ f = (f
 \otimes \mathrm{id})\circ \Delta_V$). This follows directly from the constructi
on, since $f$ intertwining the representations means $f(\rho_V(g)v) = \rho_W(g)(
f(v))$, which at the coordinate level is exactly the comodule-morphism condition
. Thus the category of linear representations of $G$ is equivalent (in fact isom
orphic) to the category of $A$-comodules.

## Conclusion

The equivalence rests on a single mechanism: **regular functions on $G$ are the
elements of $A$, and the group law on $G$ is encoded dually by the comultiplicat
ion $\Delta$ on $A$.** A representation $\rho: G \to \mathrm{GL}(V)$ is a regula
r map, hence is determined by the pullbacks of the coordinate functions on $\mat
hrm{End}(V)$ — these are the "matrix coefficients" $a_{ij} \in A$. The homomorph

ly the coassociativity of the comodule map $\Delta_V(e_j) = \sum_i e_i \otimes a
_{ij}$. The unit condition $\rho(e)=\mathrm{id}$ is the counit axiom, and invert

> **Giving a linear representation of $G$ on $V$ is equivalent to giving an $A$-
