**Theorem.** If $\theta \in \mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}$, then $\{(2n+1)\theta
 \bmod 1 : n \in \mathbb{N}\}$ is dense in $[0,1]$.

**Key Lemma (Kronecker density).** *If $\alpha$ is irrational, then $\{n\alpha \
bmod 1 : n \geq 0\}$ is dense in $[0,1]$.*

*Proof of Lemma.* Fix $N\geq 1$. Partition $[0,1)$ into $N$ subintervals $I_k=[k
/N,(k+1)/N)$, $k=0,\dots,N-1$. Consider the $N+1$ points $0,\alpha,2\alpha,\dots
,N\alpha$ (all taken mod $1$). By the pigeonhole principle, two of them, say $i\
alpha$ and $j\alpha$ with $0\le i<j\le N$, fall in the same subinterval. Set $q=
j-i$ ($1\le q\le N$); then $\|q\alpha\|<1/N$, where $\|\cdot\|$ denotes distance
 to the nearest integer. Since $\alpha$ is irrational, $q\alpha\notin\mathbb{Z}$
, so $\beta:=q\alpha\bmod 1$ is a nonzero element of $(0,1)$ with $\beta<1/N$ or
 $\beta>1-1/N$.

The orbit $\{0,\beta,2\beta,\dots\}\bmod 1$ steps around the circle by $\beta$ (
or equivalently by $1-\beta$ in the other direction), with step $<1/N$. Consecut
ive points are within $1/N$ of each other, so every point of $[0,1)$ is within $
1/N$ of some $mq\alpha\bmod 1$. Since $mq\alpha\bmod 1\in\{n\alpha\bmod 1:n\ge 0
\}$, the set $\{n\alpha\bmod 1\}$ is $1/N$-dense. As $N$ was arbitrary, it is de
nse. $\square$

**Proof of Theorem.** Since $\theta$ is irrational, $2\theta$ is irrational. By
the Lemma, the set
$$S:=\{2n\theta \bmod 1 : n\ge 0\}$$
is dense in $[0,1]$.

Observe that $(2n+1)\theta = \theta + 2n\theta$, so
$$\{(2n+1)\theta\bmod 1 : n\in\mathbb{N}\} = \{(\theta + 2n\theta)\bmod 1 : n\in
\mathbb{N}\} = (S+\theta)\bmod 1,$$
i.e., it is the image of $S$ under the circle rotation $R_\theta:x\mapsto (x+\th
eta)\bmod 1$.

The map $R_\theta:\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ is a homeomorph
ism (its inverse is $R_{-\theta}$). Homeomorphisms preserve density: if $S$ is d
ense and $U$ is any nonempty open set, then $R_\theta^{-1}(U)$ is nonempty and o
pen, so $S\cap R_\theta^{-1}(U)\neq\varnothing$, giving $R_\theta(S)\cap U\neq\v
arnothing$.

Therefore $R_\theta(S)=\{(2n+1)\theta\bmod 1:n\in\mathbb{N}\}$ is dense in $[0,1
