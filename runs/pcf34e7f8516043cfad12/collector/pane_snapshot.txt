**Claim:** Yes. The Haar measure on a compact group is always completely positiv
e: every nonempty open set has positive measure.

**Proof.**

Let $G$ be a compact group and let $\mu$ be its (left) Haar measure, normalized
so that $\mu(G)=1$ (finiteness is guaranteed by compactness).

Let $U \subseteq G$ be a nonempty open set. We must show $\mu(U)>0$.

**Step 1: Reduce to a neighborhood of the identity.**

Pick any $x \in U$. Since $U$ is open and the map $g \mapsto xg$ is continuous (
group multiplication is continuous), the preimage $x^{-1}U$ is an open set conta
ining the identity $e$. Set $W = x^{-1}U$; this is an open neighborhood of $e$,
and by left-invariance $\mu(W) = \mu(x^{-1}U) = \mu(U)$.

Thus it suffices to show that every open neighborhood $W$ of $e$ satisfies $\mu(
W) > 0$.

**Step 2: Show $\mu(W) > 0$ by contradiction.**

Suppose $\mu(W) = 0$. For each $g \in G$, the left translate $gW$ is open (left
translation is a homeomorphism) and, by left-invariance,
$$\mu(gW) = \mu(W) = 0.$$

The family $\{gW : g \in G\}$ is an open cover of $G$: every point $g \in G$ lie
s in $gW$ (since $e \in W$ implies $g = ge \in gW$).

Since $G$ is compact, there exist finitely many elements $g_1, \dots, g_n \in G$
 such that
$$G \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} g_i W.$$

By subadditivity and left-invariance,
$$\mu(G) \leq \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mu(g_i W) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mu(W) = 0.$$

But $\mu(G) = 1$, a contradiction. Therefore $\mu(W) > 0$.

**Step 3: Conclude.**

Since $\mu(U) = \mu(W) > 0$, every nonempty open subset of $G$ has positive Haar
 measure. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The argument uses three essential ingredients: (1) compactness, to p
ass from the open cover $\{gW\}$ to a finite subcover; (2) left-invariance, to e
quate $\mu(gW) = \mu(W)$ for all $g$; and (3) finiteness of $\mu(G)$ (guaranteed
 by compactness), so that $\mu(G)=1 \neq 0$ yields the contradiction. The same p
roof works for right Haar measure by symmetry.
