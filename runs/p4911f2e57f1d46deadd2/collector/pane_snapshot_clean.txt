## Solution

**Setup.** Since $f$ is holomorphic in $\Omega = D(0,2)\setminus\{\tfrac{1}{2}\}
$ with a simple pole at $z=\tfrac{1}{2}$ of residue $1$, we decompose

$$f(z) = \frac{1}{z - \tfrac{1}{2}} + g(z),$$

where $g$ is holomorphic in all of $D(0,2)$ (the principal part at the simple po
le is exactly $\frac{1}{z-1/2}$, and the remainder extends holomorphically acros
s the puncture).

**Taylor coefficients.** Let $f(z) = \sum_{n\ge 0} a_n z^n$ be the Taylor expans
ion of $f$ about $0$, so that $a_n = \frac{f^{(n)}(0)}{n!}$. The quantity we see
k is

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{f^{(n)}(0)}{2^n\, n!} = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{a_n}{2
^n}.$$

**Principal part contribution.** Expanding $\frac{1}{z-1/2}$ about $0$ (radius o
f convergence $1/2$):

$$\frac{1}{z-\tfrac{1}{2}} = \frac{-2}{1-2z} = -2\sum_{n\ge 0}(2z)^n = -\sum_{n\
ge 0} 2^{n+1}\, z^n.$$

So the $n$-th Taylor coefficient of $\frac{1}{z-1/2}$ is $-2^{n+1}$.

**Remainder contribution.** Since $g$ is holomorphic in $D(0,2)$, its Taylor ser
ies $\sum b_n z^n$ has radius of convergence $R \ge 2$. By the Cauchy–Hadamard t
heorem, $\limsup |b_n|^{1/n} = 1/R \le \tfrac{1}{2}$. Hence for every $\varepsil
on>0$, eventually $|b_n| \le (\tfrac{1}{2}+\varepsilon)^n$, giving

$$\left|\frac{b_n}{2^n}\right| \le \left(\frac{1/2+\varepsilon}{2}\right)^n \to
0.$$

Thus $\frac{b_n}{2^n}\to 0$.

**Combining.** We have $a_n = -2^{n+1} + b_n$, so

$$\frac{a_n}{2^n} = \frac{-2^{n+1}+b_n}{2^n} = -2 + \frac{b_n}{2^n} \xrightarrow
{n\to\infty} -2 + 0 = -2.$$

Therefore,

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{f^{(n)}(0)}{2^n\, n!} = -2}.$$
