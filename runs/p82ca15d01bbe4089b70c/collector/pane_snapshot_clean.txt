## Proof

We show that in any normed vector space $X$, for a sequence $\{x_n\}\subset X$,

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} x_n = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \lim_{n\to\infty}\|x_n
\|=0.$$

Recall the two defining properties of a norm that we use:

- (Positive definiteness) $\|x\|\ge 0$ for all $x$, and $\|x\|=0 \iff x=0$.
- (Triangle inequality) $\|x-y\|\le \|x\|+\|y\|$, which gives the reverse triang
le inequality $|\,\|x\|-\|y\|\,|\le \|x-y\|$. In particular the norm map $x\maps
to\|x\|$ is Lipschitz (hence continuous).

---

**($\Longrightarrow$) Suppose $x_n\to 0$ in $X$. We show $\|x_n\|\to 0$.**

Let $\varepsilon>0$. Since $x_n\to 0$, there exists $N$ such that for all $n\ge
N$,
$$\|x_n - 0\| < \varepsilon.$$
But $\|x_n-0\|=\|x_n\|$, so for all $n\ge N$,
$$\|x_n\|<\varepsilon.$$
This is exactly the definition of $\|x_n\|\to 0$ in $\mathbb{R}$.

(Equivalently: the norm is continuous, so $\|x_n\|\to\|0\|=0$.)

---

**($\Longleftarrow$) Suppose $\|x_n\|\to 0$. We show $x_n\to 0$ in $X$.**

Let $\varepsilon>0$. Since $\|x_n\|\to 0$, there exists $N$ such that for all $n
\ge N$,
$$\|x_n\|<\varepsilon.$$
But $\|x_n\|=\|x_n-0\|$, so for all $n\ge N$,
$$\|x_n-0\|<\varepsilon.$$
This is exactly the definition of $x_n\to 0$ in the norm topology of $X$.

---

**Conclusion.** Both directions hold, using only the identity $\|x_n-0\|=\|x_n\|
$ (i.e., positive definiteness with $\|0\|=0$). No special property of $X$ beyon
d being a normed space is required. Therefore

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} x_n = 0 \iff \lim_{n\to\infty}\|x_n\|=0}$$

holds for every sequence in every normed vector space.
