## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. For an oriented, compact, connected $n$-dimensional smooth manif
old $M$ with boundary $\partial M$, the relative homology group $H_n(M,\partial
M)$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}$.

---

### Step 1. Poincaré–Lefschetz duality

Since $M$ is an oriented, compact $n$-dimensional manifold with boundary, the **
Poincaré–Lefschetz duality theorem** applies. It gives a natural isomorphism, fo
r every $k$,

$$
D : H^k(M) \xrightarrow{\;\cong\;} H_{n-k}(M,\partial M),
$$

induced by capping with the relative fundamental class $[M,\partial M]\in H_n(M,
\partial M)$. This is the standard generalization of Poincaré duality to manifol
ds with boundary (see, e.g., Hatcher, *Algebraic Topology*, Theorem 3.43, or Bre
don, *Topology and Geometry*, VI.9).

### Step 2. Apply duality at $k=0$

Setting $k=0$, we obtain

$$
H_n(M,\partial M)\;\cong\; H^0(M).
$$

### Step 3. Compute $H^0(M)$

The group $H^0(M)$ is the group of locally constant (hence constant, since $M$ i
s a manifold and thus locally path-connected) $\mathbb{Z}$-valued functions on $
M$, modulo the coboundary relation. Equivalently,

$$
H^0(M)\;\cong\;\mathbb{Z}^{\pi_0(M)},
$$

where $\pi_0(M)$ is the set of path-components of $M$. By hypothesis $M$ is **co
nnected**, so $|\pi_0(M)|=1$, and therefore

$$
H^0(M)\;\cong\;\mathbb{Z}.
$$

### Step 4. Conclude

Combining Steps 2 and 3,

$$
H_n(M,\partial M)\;\cong\; H^0(M)\;\cong\;\mathbb{Z}.
$$

---

### Remarks on the hypotheses (each is necessary)

- **Orientability** is essential: without it, Poincaré–Lefschetz duality with $\
mathbb{Z}$ coefficients fails; one only obtains duality with $\mathbb{Z}/2$ coef
ficients, giving $H_n(M,\partial M;\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2$ but no $\math
bb{Z}$-isomorphism in general (e.g. the Möbius band has $H_2(M,\partial M)=0$).
- **Compactness** ensures the fundamental class $[M,\partial M]$ lives in $H_n(M
,\partial M)$ (finite chains). For non-compact manifolds the analogous statement
 uses locally finite (Borel–Moore) homology.
- **Connectedness** gives $H^0(M)\cong\mathbb{Z}$ rather than $\mathbb{Z}^r$ for
 $r>1$ components.
- **Smoothness** is used only to guarantee a clean orientation/fundamental-class
 theory; the result holds for any oriented, compact, connected topological $n$-m
anifold with boundary.

---

Therefore $H_n(M,\partial M)\cong\mathbb{Z}$, and the answer to the question is
**yes**.
