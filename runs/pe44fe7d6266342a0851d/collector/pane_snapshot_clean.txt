**Claim:** Yes, $(\Gamma, \geq)$ is a totally ordered abelian group.

Let $v: K^* \to \Gamma = K^*/A^*$ be the canonical quotient homomorphism. Note t
hat $\ker v = A^*$ (the units of $A$), and the image of $A\setminus\{0\}$ under
$v$ is precisely $v(A\setminus\{0\}) = \{v(a) : a \in A,\, a \neq 0\}$.

We verify each axiom.

### $\Gamma$ is an abelian group

$\Gamma = K^*/A^*$ is a quotient of the abelian group $(K^*, \cdot)$ by the subg
roup $A^*$, hence is an abelian group (written additively, so the operation is $
v(x) + v(y) = v(xy)$ and the identity is $0 = v(1)$).

### Reflexivity

For any $\gamma \in \Gamma$, we have $\gamma - \gamma = 0 = v(1)$, and $1 \in A\
setminus\{0\}$. Hence $\gamma \geq \gamma$. ✓

### Antisymmetry

Suppose $\gamma \geq \gamma'$ and $\gamma' \geq \gamma$. Then there exist $a, b
\in A\setminus\{0\}$ with
$$\gamma - \gamma' = v(a), \qquad \gamma' - \gamma = v(b).$$
Adding gives $0 = v(a) + v(b) = v(ab)$, so $ab \in \ker v = A^*$, i.e., $ab$ is
a unit of $A$.

Since $A$ is a valuation ring, for any $x \in K^*$, either $x \in A$ or $x^{-1}
\in A$. Apply this to $b \in K^*$: either $b \in A$ (which we know) or $b^{-1} \
in A$. We show $b^{-1} \in A$: indeed $b^{-1} = (ab)^{-1} \cdot a = u^{-1} a$ wh
ere $u = ab \in A^*$. Since $u^{-1} \in A$ (units have inverses in $A$) and $a \
in A$, we get $b^{-1} \in A$. Therefore $b \in A^*$, so $v(b) = 0$, giving $\gam
ma' - \gamma = 0$, i.e., $\gamma = \gamma'$. ✓

### Transitivity

Suppose $\gamma \geq \gamma'$ and $\gamma' \geq \gamma''$. Then $\gamma - \gamma
' = v(a)$ and $\gamma' - \gamma'' = v(b)$ for some $a, b \in A\setminus\{0\}$. S
ince $A$ is a subring, $ab \in A\setminus\{0\}$, and
$$\gamma - \gamma'' = v(a) + v(b) = v(ab), \qquad ab \in A\setminus\{0\}.$$
Hence $\gamma \geq \gamma''$. ✓

### Totality

Let $\gamma, \gamma' \in \Gamma$ and pick $x \in K^*$ with $v(x) = \gamma - \gam
ma'$. Since $A$ is a valuation ring, either $x \in A$ or $x^{-1} \in A$.

- If $x \in A\setminus\{0\}$: then $\gamma - \gamma' = v(x) \in v(A\setminus\{0\
})$, so $\gamma \geq \gamma'$.
- If $x^{-1} \in A\setminus\{0\}$: then $\gamma' - \gamma = v(x^{-1}) = -v(x) \i
n v(A\setminus\{0\})$, so $\gamma' \geq \gamma$.

Thus for every pair, at least one of $\gamma \geq \gamma'$ or $\gamma' \geq \gam
ma$ holds. ✓

### Translation invariance (compatibility with the group law)

Suppose $\gamma \geq \gamma'$, so $\gamma - \gamma' = v(a)$ for some $a \in A\se
tminus\{0\}$. For any $\delta \in \Gamma$,
$$(\gamma + \delta) - (\gamma' + \delta) = \gamma - \gamma' = v(a),$$
so $\gamma + \delta \geq \gamma' + \delta$. ✓

### Conclusion

$(\Gamma, +)$ is an abelian group, $\geq$ is reflexive, antisymmetric, transitiv
e, total, and translation-invariant. Therefore $(\Gamma, \geq)$ is a totally ord
ered abelian group.
