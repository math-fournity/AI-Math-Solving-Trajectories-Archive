## Solution

**Interpretation note.** The integrand $\frac{e^{-z}}{\sqrt{z}}$ requires $z>0$
for $\sqrt{z}$ to be real-valued, and $e^{-z}$ diverges as $z\to -\infty$. Hence
 the only convergent real interpretation is the integral over $[0,\infty)$, i.e.

$$I=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-z}}{\sqrt{z}}\,dz.$$

(The notation $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}$ is understood via the change of variable
s below, where the dummy variable ranges over the whole real line.)

**Substitution.** Set $z=x^{2}$ with $x\ge 0$. Then
$$dz=2x\,dx,\qquad \sqrt{z}=\sqrt{x^{2}}=x.$$
As $z:0\to\infty$, we have $x:0\to\infty$. Therefore

$$I=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-x^{2}}}{x}\,(2x\,dx)=2\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}}
\,dx.$$

**Reduce to the Gaussian.** The standard Gaussian integral is

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}}\,dx=\sqrt{\pi}.$$

Since $e^{-x^{2}}$ is even,

$$\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}}\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}}\,
dx=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$I=2\cdot\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}=\sqrt{\pi}.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-z}}{\sqrt{z}}\,dz=\sqrt{\pi}}.$
$
