## Proof

**Setup.** Let $X(u,v)$ be an orthogonal parametrization of $M$, so $F = \langle
 X_u, X_v \rangle = 0$. Set $E = \langle X_u, X_u\rangle$ and $G = \langle X_v,
X_v\rangle$.

**Christoffel symbols with $F=0$.** With $g_{11}=E,\; g_{12}=0,\; g_{22}=G$ and
inverse $g^{11}=1/E,\; g^{22}=1/G$, the relevant Christoffel symbols are:

$$\Gamma^1_{22} = \tfrac{1}{2}g^{11}(\partial_v g_{21}+\partial_v g_{12}-\partia
l_u g_{22}) = -\frac{G_u}{2E}$$

$$\Gamma^2_{11} = \tfrac{1}{2}g^{22}(\partial_u g_{12}+\partial_u g_{21}-\partia
l_v g_{11}) = -\frac{E_v}{2G}$$

**Condition from $v$-parameter curves being unparametrized geodesics.** The curv
e $X(u_0,v)$ has velocity $X_v$ and
$$\nabla_{X_v}X_v = \Gamma^1_{22}\,X_u + \Gamma^2_{22}\,X_v.$$
Since $F=0$, the components $X_u$ and $X_v$ are orthogonal. For the curve to be
a geodesic *up to reparametrization*, the component of $\nabla_{X_v}X_v$ perpend
icular to the velocity $X_v$ must vanish. The perpendicular component is $\Gamma
^1_{22}\,X_u$, so we need
$$\Gamma^1_{22}=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad G_u = 0.$$

**Condition from $u$-parameter curves being unparametrized geodesics.** The curv
e $X(u,v_0)$ has velocity $X_u$ and
$$\nabla_{X_u}X_u = \Gamma^1_{11}\,X_u + \Gamma^2_{11}\,X_v.$$
The perpendicular component is $\Gamma^2_{11}\,X_v$, so we need
$$\Gamma^2_{11}=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad E_v = 0.$$

**Computing the Gauss curvature.** For an orthogonal parametrization ($F=0$), th
e Gauss curvature is given by:

$$K = -\frac{1}{2\sqrt{EG}}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\!\left(\frac{E_v}{\
sqrt{EG}}\right) + \frac{\partial}{\partial u}\!\left(\frac{G_u}{\sqrt{EG}}\righ
t)\right].$$

Since the parameter curves in *both* families are geodesics for every value of $
u_0$ and $v_0$, the conditions $G_u = 0$ and $E_v = 0$ hold **everywhere** on $M
$. Substituting:

$$K = -\frac{1}{2\sqrt{EG}}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial v}(0) + \frac{\partial
}{\partial u}(0)\right] = 0.$$

**Conclusion.** The Gauss curvature of $M$ vanishes identically: $K \equiv 0$. T
he surface is developable (locally isometric to the plane).
