## Solution

**Yes, $a(x)$ and $b(x)$ must be constant.**

### Proof

Consider the affine plane curve
$$E:\; y^2 = x^3 + 1.$$
Its projective closure $\widetilde{E}: Y^2 Z = X^3 + Z^3$ is smooth: the partial
 derivatives are $\frac{\partial F}{\partial X} = -3X^2$, $\frac{\partial F}{\pa
rtial Y} = 2YZ$, $\frac{\partial F}{\partial Z} = Y^2 - 3Z^2$, and one checks th
ere is no common zero with $F=0$ (the only point with $X=0$ is $[0:1:0]$, where
$Y^2-3Z^2 = 1 \neq 0$). Hence $\widetilde{E}$ is a smooth projective plane cubic
, so it has **genus $1$** (an elliptic curve).

The pair $(a(x), b(x))$ of rational functions defines a rational map
$$\varphi: \mathbb{P}^1 \dashrightarrow \widetilde{E}, \qquad x \mapsto [a(x) :
b(x) : 1].$$

Since $\mathbb{P}^1$ is a **smooth curve**, every rational map from $\mathbb{P}^
1$ to a projective variety extends uniquely to a **morphism** (a rational map fr
om a smooth curve to a projective variety has no indeterminacy). Thus $\varphi:
\mathbb{P}^1 \to \widetilde{E}$ is a genuine morphism.

Suppose, for contradiction, that $a$ and $b$ are not both constant. Then $\varph
i$ is non-constant. A non-constant morphism between smooth projective curves is
**finite and surjective**; let $d = \deg \varphi \geq 1$.

Apply the **Riemann–Hurwitz formula**:
$$2\,g(\mathbb{P}^1) - 2 \;=\; d\bigl(2\,g(\widetilde{E}) - 2\bigr) + \sum_{P \i
n \mathbb{P}^1}(e_P - 1),$$
where $e_P \geq 1$ is the ramification index at $P$, so $\sum (e_P - 1) \geq 0$.

Substituting $g(\mathbb{P}^1) = 0$ and $g(\widetilde{E}) = 1$:
$$-2 \;=\; d \cdot (2 \cdot 1 - 2) + \sum_{P}(e_P - 1) \;=\; 0 + \sum_{P}(e_P -
1) \;\geq\; 0.$$

This is a contradiction ($-2 \geq 0$). Therefore $\varphi$ must be **constant**,
 which means $a(x)$ and $b(x)$ are both constant rational functions.

### Remark

The constant solutions are exactly the $\mathbb{C}$-points of $E$: for each $a_0
 \in \mathbb{C}$ with $a_0^3 + 1$ a perfect square, $b_0 = \pm\sqrt{a_0^3+1}$ gi
ves a constant solution. The point at infinity $[0:1:0]$ corresponds to the "tri
vial" solution at infinity. The essential point is that **no non-constant family
 exists**, because a genus-$0$ curve ($\mathbb{P}^1$, the source of the rational
 functions) cannot map non-trivially onto a genus-$1$ curve — the topological ob
struction encoded in Riemann–Hurwitz forbids it.
