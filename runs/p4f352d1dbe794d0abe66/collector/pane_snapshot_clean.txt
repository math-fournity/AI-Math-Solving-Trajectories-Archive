## Proof

The integrand is a product of four terms. Recognizing the sigmoid $\sigma(x) = \
frac{e^x}{1+e^x}$, we note that $\sigma(x)(1-\sigma(x)) = \frac{e^x}{(1+e^x)^2}
= \sigma'(x)$. The integrand becomes:

$$\sigma'(\gamma+0.2\delta)\cdot\sigma'(\gamma+0.1\delta).$$

**Coordinate transformation.** Set
$$u = \gamma + 0.2\delta, \qquad v = \gamma + 0.1\delta.$$
The Jacobian is
$$\frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(\gamma,\delta)} = \begin{vmatrix}1 & 0.2\\ 1 & 0
.1\end{vmatrix} = 0.1 - 0.2 = -0.1,$$
so $|J|=0.1$ and $d\gamma\,d\delta = \frac{1}{0.1}\,du\,dv = 10\,du\,dv$. Since
the transformation is linear with nonzero determinant, as $(\gamma,\delta)$ rang
e over $\mathbb{R}^2$, so do $(u,v)$.

**Separation.** The integral becomes
$$I = 10\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\sigma'(u)\,\sigma'(v)\,du
\,dv = 10\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\sigma'(u)\,du\right)\left(\int_{-\infty}^
{\infty}\sigma'(v)\,dv\right).$$

**Evaluating the one-dimensional integral.** With $t=e^x$ (so $dx = dt/t$):
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^x}{(1+e^x)^2}\,dx = \int_0^{\infty}\frac{t}{(1+
t)^2}\cdot\frac{dt}{t} = \int_0^{\infty}\frac{dt}{(1+t)^2} = \left[-\frac{1}{1+t
}\right]_0^{\infty} = 1.$$

**Result.** Therefore
$$I = 10 \cdot 1 \cdot 1 = \boxed{10}.$$
