## Solution

**Claim.** A non-zero harmonic function $u$ on the plane vanishing on $\ell_1\cu
p\ell_2$ exists **if and only if the angle between the two rays is a rational mu
ltiple of $\pi$**.

---

### Setup

Let the two rays issue from the origin at polar angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$, wit
h $\delta:=\beta-\alpha\in(0,2\pi)$ (the case of coincident rays is trivial and
excluded). Since the origin belongs to $\ell_1\cup\ell_2$, any such $u$ satisfie
s $u(0)=0$.

The plane is simply connected, so every harmonic function on it admits a global
harmonic conjugate: $u=\operatorname{Re} F$ for an entire function $F$. Writing
$F(z)=\sum_{n\ge 0}c_n z^n$ (convergent for all $z$) and $z=re^{i\theta}$,

$$
u(r,\theta)=\sum_{n\ge 0} r^n\,\operatorname{Re}\!\bigl(c_n e^{in\theta}\bigr)
          =\sum_{n\ge 0} r^n\, g_n(\theta),
\qquad g_n(\theta):=A_n\cos n\theta+B_n\sin n\theta,
$$

with $g_0\equiv A_0=u(0)=0$. The series converges for every $r\ge 0$.

### Necessity

The condition $u\equiv 0$ on the ray $\theta=\alpha$ reads
$$
u(r,\alpha)=\sum_{n\ge 1} r^n\, g_n(\alpha)=0\quad\text{for all }r>0.
$$
This is a power series in $r$ vanishing on $(0,\infty)$, hence identically zero,
 so $g_n(\alpha)=0$ for every $n\ge 1$. Likewise $g_n(\beta)=0$ for every $n\ge
1$.

Since $u\not\equiv 0$, some $g_N$ is not identically zero. A non-zero function $
g_N(\theta)=A_N\cos N\theta+B_N\sin N\theta=C\cos(N\theta-\varphi)$ is a sinusoi
d of period $2\pi/N$; it vanishes at $\alpha$ and $\beta$ only when its two zero
s are separated by an integer multiple of its half-period $\pi/N$:
$$
N(\beta-\alpha)\equiv 0 \pmod{\pi}.
$$
Thus $\delta=\beta-\alpha = \dfrac{k\pi}{N}$ for some integers $k,N$ with $N\ge
1$, i.e.
$$
\boxed{\;\frac{\delta}{\pi}\in\mathbb{Q}\;}.
$$

### Sufficiency

Conversely, suppose $\delta/\pi = p/q$ in lowest terms ($p,q\in\mathbb{Z}$, $q\g
e 1$). Set
$$
u(r,\theta)=r^{q}\sin\bigl(q(\theta-\alpha)\bigr).
$$
This is the imaginary part of $z\mapsto e^{-iq\alpha}z^{q}$, hence harmonic on t
he whole plane, and non-zero. On the two rays:
- $\theta=\alpha$: $\sin 0 = 0$;
- $\theta=\beta$: $\sin\bigl(q(\beta-\alpha)\bigr)=\sin(q\cdot p\pi/q)=\sin(p\pi
)=0$.

So $u$ vanishes on $\ell_1\cup\ell_2$.

### Conclusion

The two angles determined by the rays are $\delta$ and $2\pi-\delta$; since $\de
lta/\pi\in\mathbb{Q}\iff (2\pi-\delta)/\pi\in\mathbb{Q}$, the characterization i
s unambiguous:

> **The pair $(\ell_1,\ell_2)$ admits a non-zero plane-harmonic function vanishi
ng on $\ell_1\cup\ell_2$ if and only if the angle between the two rays is a rati
onal multiple of $\pi$.**

Equivalently, writing the angle as $\frac{p}{q}\pi$ with $p,q$ coprime, the extr
emal example is $u(r,\theta)=r^{q}\sin\!\bigl(q(\theta-\alpha)\bigr)$.
