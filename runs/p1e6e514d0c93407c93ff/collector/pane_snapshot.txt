### Step 2: Determine the Möbius Transformation $T: H \to \mathbb{D}$

We need $T: H \to \mathbb{D}$ (unit disk) satisfying:
| $z$ | $\zeta = \sqrt{z}$ | Required $w$ |
|-----|---------------------|---------------|
| $0$ | $0$ | $i$ |
| $-1+0i$ | $i$ | $+1$ |
| $-1-0i$ | $-i$ | $-1$ |

A Möbius transformation is uniquely determined by three points and their images.
 Using the **cross-ratio formula** with $(z_1, z_2, z_3) = (0, i, -i)$ and $(w_1
, w_2, w_3) = (i, 1, -1)$:

$$\frac{(w - i)(1 - (-1))}{(w - (-1))(1 - i)} = \frac{(\zeta - 0)(i - (-i))}{(\z
eta - (-i))(i - 0)}$$

$$\frac{2(w - i)}{(w + 1)(1 - i)} = \frac{2i\,\zeta}{i(\zeta + i)} = \frac{2\zet
a}{\zeta + i}$$

Cross-multiplying:

$$(w - i)(\zeta + i) = \zeta(w + 1)(1 - i)$$

$$w\zeta + wi - i\zeta + 1 = (1-i)w\zeta + (1-i)\zeta$$

Collecting $w$-terms:

$$w\bigl[\zeta + i - (1-i)\zeta\bigr] = (1-i)\zeta + i\zeta - 1$$

$$w\bigl[i\zeta + i\bigr] = \zeta - 1$$

$$\boxed{\;w = T(\zeta) = \frac{-i(\zeta - 1)}{\zeta + 1}\;}$$

Substituting $\zeta = \sqrt{z}$:

$$\boxed{\;w = \frac{-i(\sqrt{z} - 1)}{\sqrt{z} + 1}\;}$$

### Step 4: Images of Circles Centered at 0

$$|z| = r \implies |\zeta|^2 = r \implies |\zeta| = \sqrt{r} =: \rho$$

Now apply $T$. The inverse of $T$ is $\zeta = \frac{i - w}{w + i}$. The conditio

$$|i - w|^2 = \rho^2\,|w + i|^2$$

With $w = u + iv$:

$$u^2 + (v-1)^2 = \rho^2\bigl(u^2 + (v+1)^2\bigr)$$

$$(1 - \rho^2)(u^2 + v^2) - 2(1 + \rho^2)v + (1 - \rho^2) = 0$$

**Case $r \neq 1$ (i.e., $\rho \neq 1$):** Dividing by $(1 - \rho^2)$ and comple

$$u^2 + \left(v - \frac{1 + \rho^2}{1 - \rho^2}\right)^2 = \frac{4\rho^2}{(1 - \
rho^2)^2}$$

- **Center:** $\displaystyle\left(0,\; \frac{1 + r}{1 - r}\right)$ (on the imagi

**Case $r = 1$ (i.e., $\rho = 1$):** The equation reduces to $-4v = 0$, i.e., $v

**Orthogonality:** The distance from the origin to the center of $C_r$ is $d = \

So **each $C_r$ is orthogonal to the unit circle**.

$$w\big|_{\zeta = i\rho} = \frac{2\sqrt{r}}{1+r} + i\,\frac{1-r}{1+r}, \qquad w\

These are symmetric about the imaginary axis and lie on the unit circle (one can
