## Proof

Let $A_n = \{X_n = Y_n\}$ for each $n \in \mathbb{N}$. By hypothesis, $P(A_n) =
1$ for every $n$, equivalently $P(A_n^c) = 0$.

We want to determine $P\!\left(\bigcap_{n \in \mathbb{N}} A_n\right)$, i.e. the
probability that $X_n = Y_n$ holds simultaneously for all $n$.

**Claim:** $P\!\left(\bigcap_{n \in \mathbb{N}} A_n\right) = 1$.

**Proof of claim.** Using De Morgan's law and countable subadditivity,

$$
P\!\left(\Big(\bigcap_{n \in \mathbb{N}} A_n\Big)^{\!c}\right)
= P\!\left(\bigcup_{n \in \mathbb{N}} A_n^c\right)
\le \sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}} P(A_n^c)
= \sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}} 0
= 0.
$$

Hence $P\!\left(\bigcap_{n \in \mathbb{N}} A_n\right) = 1 - 0 = 1$.

The crucial ingredient is that $\mathbb{N}$ is **countable**: countable subaddit
ivity lets us bound the union of the null sets $A_n^c$ by a sum of zeros. (For a
n uncountable index set this argument would fail, and indeed the conclusion can
fail in that case — a countable intersection of full-measure sets need not exten
d to an uncountable one.)

Therefore, **yes**, $P(X_n = Y_n \ \forall\, n \in \mathbb{N}) = 1$.
