## Proof

**Yes**, we can conclude that $g_n' \to h \cdot g$ uniformly on $[c,d]$.

**Setup and boundedness.** Since each $g_n$ is differentiable on $[c,d]$, each $
g_n$ is continuous. The uniform limit $g$ of continuous functions is continuous,
 hence bounded on the compact interval $[c,d]$; write $\|g\|_\infty = M$. By uni
form convergence, for all sufficiently large $n$ we have $\|g_n - g\|_\infty \le
 1$, so
$$\|g_n\|_\infty \le \|g\|_\infty + \|g_n - g\|_\infty \le M + 1.$$
Thus the sequence $(\|g_n\|_\infty)$ is eventually bounded; let $B$ be a uniform
 bound valid for all large $n$. Also, $h$ is a uniform limit of (continuous) fun
ctions $g_n'/g_n$, so $h$ is continuous and bounded; let $H = \|h\|_\infty$.

**Key decomposition.** For every $n$ (large enough that $g_n \neq 0$ on $[c,d]$,
 which is guaranteed since $g_n'/g_n$ is defined),
$$g_n' = \frac{g_n'}{g_n}\cdot g_n.$$
Therefore, pointwise on $[c,d]$,
$$g_n' - h\,g \;=\; \frac{g_n'}{g_n}\,g_n - h\,g \;=\; \Bigl(\frac{g_n'}{g_n} -
h\Bigr)g_n \;+\; h\,(g_n - g).$$

**Uniform estimate.** Taking suprema and applying the triangle inequality,
$$\|g_n' - h\,g\|_\infty \;\le\; \Bigl\|\frac{g_n'}{g_n} - h\Bigr\|_\infty\cdot\
|g_n\|_\infty \;+\; \|h\|_\infty\cdot\|g_n - g\|_\infty.$$
Using the bounds above, for all large $n$,
$$\|g_n' - h\,g\|_\infty \;\le\; B\,\Bigl\|\frac{g_n'}{g_n} - h\Bigr\|_\infty \;
+\; H\,\|g_n - g\|_\infty.$$

**Conclusion.** By hypothesis both $\bigl\|\frac{g_n'}{g_n} - h\bigr\|_\infty \t
o 0$ and $\|g_n - g\|_\infty \to 0$. Since $B$ and $H$ are fixed constants, the
right-hand side tends to $0$. Hence
$$\|g_n' - h\,g\|_\infty \to 0,$$
i.e. $g_n'$ converges uniformly to $h\cdot g$ on $[c,d]$.
