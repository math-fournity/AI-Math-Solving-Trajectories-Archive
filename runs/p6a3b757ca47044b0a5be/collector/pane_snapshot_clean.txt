**Step 1: Find the roots.** Setting $y=x^2$, the polynomial becomes $y^2-4y+5=0$
, giving $y=2\pm i$. Hence the four roots are
$$\pm\alpha,\quad \pm\beta, \qquad \alpha:=\sqrt{2+i},\;\; \beta:=\sqrt{2-i}.$$
Note $\alpha\beta=\sqrt{(2+i)(2-i)}=\sqrt{5}$, so $\beta=\sqrt{5}/\alpha$.

tion $(x^2+ax+b)(x^2-ax+c)$: we need $bc=5$, $a(c-b)=0$, $b+c-a^2=-4$. If $a=0$:
 $b+c=-4, bc=5$ gives discriminant $16-20<0$, no rational $b,c$. If $b=c$: $b^2=
5$, no rational. So $f$ is irreducible, $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}]=4$.
