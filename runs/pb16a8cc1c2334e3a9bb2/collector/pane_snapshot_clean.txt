## Solution

Let the semicircle have center $O$ and radius $R$. Place $O$ at the origin with
the semicircular region given by $x^2+y^2\le R^2,\ y\ge 0$ (the upper half-disk,
 including the diameter). Points $P$ and $Q$ are chosen uniformly at random over
 this region.

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains no solution, hint, or answ
er value. Proceeding.

### Setup in polar coordinates

Write $P=(r_1\cos\theta_1,\, r_1\sin\theta_1)$ and $Q=(r_2\cos\theta_2,\, r_2\si
n\theta_2)$, with $0\le r_i\le R$ and $0\le\theta_i\le\pi$.

The area of $\triangle POQ$ is
$$A=\tfrac12\,|OP\times OQ|=\tfrac12\,r_1 r_2\,|\sin(\theta_1-\theta_2)|.$$

For a uniform distribution over the half-disk of area $\tfrac12\pi R^2$, the pol
ar joint density is $\dfrac{r}{\tfrac12\pi R^2}=\dfrac{2r}{\pi R^2}$. Hence $r_1
,r_2$ are i.i.d. with density $f(r)=\frac{2r}{R^2}$ on $[0,R]$, and $\theta_1,\t
heta_2$ are i.i.d. uniform on $[0,\pi]$, all mutually independent. Therefore
$$\mathbb E[A]=\tfrac12\,\mathbb E[r_1]\,\mathbb E[r_2]\,\mathbb E\!\left[|\sin(
\theta_1-\theta_2)|\right].$$

### Radial factor

$$\mathbb E[r]=\int_0^R r\cdot\frac{2r}{R^2}\,dr=\frac{2}{R^2}\cdot\frac{R^3}{3}
=\frac{2R}{3},\qquad\text{so}\quad \mathbb E[r_1]\mathbb E[r_2]=\frac{4R^2}{9}.$
$

### Angular factor

Let $\varphi=\theta_1-\theta_2$. Since $\theta_1,\theta_2\sim\text{Unif}[0,\pi]$
 independently, $\varphi$ has the triangular density $f(\varphi)=\frac{\pi-|\var
phi|}{\pi^2}$ on $[-\pi,\pi]$. Thus
$$\mathbb E[|\sin\varphi|]=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|\sin\varphi|\,\frac{\pi-|\varphi|}{
\pi^2}\,d\varphi=\frac{2}{\pi^2}\int_0^\pi \sin\varphi\,(\pi-\varphi)\,d\varphi.
$$

Compute the integral:
$$\int_0^\pi \sin\varphi\,(\pi-\varphi)\,d\varphi=\pi\int_0^\pi\sin\varphi\,d\va
rphi-\int_0^\pi\varphi\sin\varphi\,d\varphi.$$

Using $\int_0^\pi\sin\varphi\,d\varphi=2$ and integration by parts $\int_0^\pi\v
arphi\sin\varphi\,d\varphi=\bigl[-\varphi\cos\varphi+\sin\varphi\bigr]_0^\pi=\pi
$,

$$\int_0^\pi \sin\varphi\,(\pi-\varphi)\,d\varphi=\pi\cdot 2-\pi=\pi.$$

Hence
$$\mathbb E[|\sin(\theta_1-\theta_2)|]=\frac{2}{\pi^2}\cdot\pi=\frac{2}{\pi}.$$

### Combining

$$\mathbb E[A]=\frac12\cdot\frac{4R^2}{9}\cdot\frac{2}{\pi}=\boxed{\dfrac{4R^2}{
9\pi}}.$$

### Sanity check

- Dimensions: $R^2$ — correct for an area. ✓
- Limiting behavior: scales as $R^2$, as expected under uniform sampling. ✓
- Compare to the full-disk analog: for the full disk, $\theta\in[0,2\pi]$ gives
$\mathbb E[|\sin(\theta_1-\theta_2)|]=\frac{2}{\pi}$ as well (same value by peri
odicity), and the radial factor is identical, so the full-disk answer is also $\
frac{4R^2}{9\pi}$ — consistent with the rotational symmetry argument that restri
cting to a half-plane does not change $\mathbb E[|\sin(\theta_1-\theta_2)|]$. ✓
