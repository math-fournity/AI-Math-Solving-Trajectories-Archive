Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether "every prime ideal of $A$ i
s contracted from $B$" implies $f': \text{Spec}(B) \to \text{Spec}(A)$ is surjec
tive.

**Answer: Yes, the converse holds.**

## Proof

**Setup and notation.** Let $f: A \to B$ be a ring homomorphism. For an ideal $I
 \subseteq A$, the extension is $I^e = f(I)B \subseteq B$; for an ideal $J \subs
eteq B$, the contraction is $J^c = f^{-1}(J) \subseteq A$. A prime $\mathfrak{p}
 \subset A$ is **contracted** if $\mathfrak{p} = \mathfrak{p}^{ec} = f^{-1}(f(\m
athfrak{p})B)$.

**Key characterization via the fiber.** For a prime $\mathfrak{p} \in \text{Spec
}(A)$, the **fiber** of $f'$ over $\mathfrak{p}$ is
$$f'^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}) \cong \text{Spec}\!\bigl(B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p}
)\bigr),$$
where $\kappa(\mathfrak{p}) = \text{Frac}(A/\mathfrak{p})$. This is a standard f
act: primes of $B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p})$ correspond bijectively to prim
es $\mathfrak{q} \in \text{Spec}(B)$ with $f^{-1}(\mathfrak{q}) = \mathfrak{p}$.

**Lemma.** *For $\mathfrak{p} \in \text{Spec}(A)$, the following are equivalent:
*
1. *$\mathfrak{p} \in \text{Im}(f')$ (i.e., $f'$ is surjective onto $\mathfrak{p
}$),*
2. *$B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p}) \neq 0$,*
3. *$\mathfrak{p} = \mathfrak{p}^{ec}$ (i.e., $\mathfrak{p}$ is contracted).*

**Proof of Lemma.**

**(1) $\Leftrightarrow$ (2):** By the fiber description, $\mathfrak{p} \in \text
{Im}(f')$ iff $B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p})$ has a prime ideal. Every nonzer
o (unital) ring has a prime ideal (by Zorn's Lemma), and the zero ring has none.
 Hence $B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p})$ has a prime iff it is nonzero.

**(2) $\Leftrightarrow$ (3):** We compute $B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p}) \con
g B_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}\,B_{\mathfrak{p}}$, where $B_{\mathfrak{p}} = S^
{-1}B$ with $S = f(A \setminus \mathfrak{p})$. This is the zero ring iff $\mathf
rak{p}\,B_{\mathfrak{p}} = B_{\mathfrak{p}}$, iff $1 \in \mathfrak{p}\,B_{\mathf
rak{p}}$.

The condition $1 \in \mathfrak{p}\,B_{\mathfrak{p}} = \mathfrak{p}^e\,B_{\mathfr
ak{p}}$ means: there exist $p_i \in \mathfrak{p}$, $b_i \in B$, and $s_i \in S$
with
$$1 = \sum_i \frac{f(p_i)\,b_i}{s_i} \quad \text{in } B_{\mathfrak{p}}.$$
Clearing denominators (take $u = \prod s_i \in S$), this is equivalent to: there
 exists $u \in S$ (i.e., $u = f(a)$ for some $a \in A \setminus \mathfrak{p}$) w
ith $u \in \mathfrak{p}^e = f(\mathfrak{p})B$. Applying $f^{-1}$, this is equiva
lent to: there exists $a \in A \setminus \mathfrak{p}$ with $a \in \mathfrak{p}^
{ec}$, i.e., $\mathfrak{p}^{ec} \not\subseteq \mathfrak{p}$.

Since $\mathfrak{p} \subseteq \mathfrak{p}^{ec}$ always holds (for $x \in \mathf
rak{p}$, $f(x) \in f(\mathfrak{p}) \subseteq \mathfrak{p}^e$, so $x \in \mathfra
k{p}^{ec}$), we have $\mathfrak{p}^{ec} \not\subseteq \mathfrak{p}$ iff $\mathfr
ak{p}^{ec} \supsetneq \mathfrak{p}$.

Therefore:
$$B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p}) = 0 \iff \mathfrak{p}^{ec} \supsetneq \mathfr
ak{p},$$
$$B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p}) \neq 0 \iff \mathfrak{p}^{ec} = \mathfrak{p}.
$$

This establishes (2) $\Leftrightarrow$ (3). $\square$

**Conclusion.** By the Lemma, for each $\mathfrak{p} \in \text{Spec}(A)$:
$$\mathfrak{p} \in \text{Im}(f') \iff \mathfrak{p} \text{ is contracted.}$$
Therefore $f'$ is surjective **if and only if** every prime ideal of $A$ is cont
racted from $B$. The converse holds. $\blacksquare$
