## Proof

Let $K$ be a compact (Hausdorff) space and let $x \neq y$ be distinct points of
$K$. The unit ball of $C(K)$ is
$$B = \{f \in C(K) : \|f\|_\infty \le 1\}.$$

**Upper bound.** For any $f \in B$ and any $x, y \in K$,
$$|f(x) - f(y)| \le |f(x)| + |f(y)| \le \|f\|_\infty + \|f\|_\infty \le 1 + 1 =
2.$$
Hence $\sup_{f \in B} |f(x)-f(y)| \le 2$.

**Lower bound (attainment).** Since $K$ is compact Hausdorff, it is normal. The
set $\{x, y\}$ is closed in $K$. Define a continuous function $g : \{x,y\} \to \
mathbb{R}$ by
$$g(x) = 1, \qquad g(y) = -1,$$
so that $\|g\|_\infty = 1$. By the Tietze extension theorem (applicable since $K
$ is normal), $g$ extends to a continuous function $f : K \to \mathbb{R}$ with $
\|f\|_\infty \le \|g\|_\infty = 1$. Thus $f \in B$, and
$$|f(x) - f(y)| = |g(x) - g(y)| = |1 - (-1)| = 2.$$

Therefore the upper bound $2$ is attained, and
$$\sup_{f \in B,\, x \neq y} |f(x) - f(y)| = 2.$$
