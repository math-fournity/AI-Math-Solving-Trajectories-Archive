Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether convergence in distribution
 (the CLT) implies convergence of the second moments for the sample median.

## Proof

**Notation.** Let $Z_n = \sqrt{n}(Y_n - m)$. The CLT gives $Z_n \xrightarrow{d}
Z \sim N(0, [2f(m)]^{-2})$. The question is whether $E[Z_n^2] = nE[(Y_n - m)^2]
\to E[Z^2] = [2f(m)]^{-2}$.

---

### Step 1: Convergence in distribution does not imply convergence of moments

By the portmanteau theorem, $Z_n \xrightarrow{d} Z$ implies $E[g(Z_n)] \to E[g(Z
)]$ for all **bounded** continuous $g$. The map $g(x) = x^2$ is unbounded, so th
is implication does not apply directly.

The standard theorem (Billingsley, *Convergence of Probability Measures*, Theore
m 3.5) states:

> If $Z_n \xrightarrow{d} Z$ and $\{Z_n^2\}$ is uniformly integrable, then $E[Z_
n^2] \to E[Z^2]$. Conversely, if $Z_n \xrightarrow{d} Z$ and $E[Z_n^2] \to E[Z^2
] < \infty$, then $\{Z_n^2\}$ is uniformly integrable.

Therefore $nE[(Y_n - m)^2] \to [2f(m)]^{-2}$ holds **if and only if** $\{n(Y_n -
 m)^2\}_{n \geq 1}$ is uniformly integrable. The CLT alone is insufficient.

---

### Step 2: Counterexample — CLT holds but all second moments are infinite

We construct a distribution where $f$ is continuous and positive at the median (
so the CLT holds) but the tails are so heavy that $E[(Y_n - m)^2] = \infty$ for
every $n$.

**Define the CDF $F$** (with median $m = 0$):

- For $|t| \leq 1$: $\;F(t) = \tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{t}{4}$, so $f(t) = \tfrac{1}
{4}$ on $[-1,1]$.
- For $t > 1$: $\;F(t) = 1 - \dfrac{\log 2}{4\log(t+1)}$.
- For $t < -1$: $\;F(t) = \dfrac{\log 2}{4\log(|t|+1)}$ (by symmetry).

**$F$ is a valid CDF:** At $t = 1$, both pieces give $F(1) = 3/4$; at $t = -1$,
both give $F(-1) = 1/4$. $F$ is continuous, strictly increasing, with $F(t) \to
1$ as $t \to +\infty$ and $F(t) \to 0$ as $t \to -\infty$.

**CLT conditions are satisfied:**
- $F(0) = 1/2$, so $m = 0$ is the population median. ✓
- $f(0) = 1/4 > 0$. ✓
- $f$ is continuous at $m = 0$ (constant on $(-1,1)$). ✓

By the standard CLT for the sample median (van der Vaart, *Asymptotic Statistics
*, Theorem 5.15 — a **local** result depending only on $f$ near $m$):

$$\sqrt{n}\, Y_n \xrightarrow{d} N\!\left(0,\; [2f(0)]^{-2}\right) = N(0, 4).$$

**Second moments are infinite for all $n$:**

For odd $n = 2k - 1$, using the quantile representation $U_n = F(Y_n) \sim \math
rm{Beta}(k,k)$:

$$E[Y_n^2] = \int_0^1 \bigl(F^{-1}(u)\bigr)^2 \,\mathrm{Beta}(u;\, k,k)\, du.$$

For $u$ near $1$, solving $1 - F(t) = \frac{\log 2}{4\log(t+1)} = 1 - u$ gives $
F^{-1}(u) = 2^{1/(4(1-u))} - 1 \sim 2^{1/(4(1-u))}$ as $u \to 1^-$.

Near $u = 1$, the Beta density satisfies $\mathrm{Beta}(u; k,k) \sim \frac{(1-u)
^{k-1}}{B(k,k)}$. Setting $v = 1 - u$, the integrand behaves as

$$\frac{2^{1/(2v)}\, v^{k-1}}{B(k,k)} = \frac{e^{(\log 2)/(2v)}\, v^{k-1}}{B(k,k
)}.$$

Since $e^{(\log 2)/(2v)}$ grows faster than any power of $1/v$ as $v \to 0^+$,

$$\int_0^{\delta} e^{(\log 2)/(2v)}\, v^{k-1}\, dv = \infty \quad \text{for ever
y } k \text{ and every } \delta > 0.$$

By symmetry the same holds near $u = 0$. Therefore $E[Y_n^2] = \infty$ for every
 $n$, and

$$n\,E[Y_n^2] = \infty \quad \text{for all } n, \qquad \text{yet} \quad \sqrt{n}
\,Y_n \xrightarrow{d} N(0,4).$$

The convergence $nE[(Y_n - m)^2] \to [2f(m)]^{-2}$ **fails**.

---

### Step 3: When the convergence does hold

The convergence holds if and only if $\{n(Y_n - m)^2\}$ is uniformly integrable.
 Sufficient conditions include:

- **Bounded support of $F$:** Then $(F^{-1}(u) - m)^2$ is bounded, and dominated
 convergence applies directly to the integral $\int_0^1 (F^{-1}(u) - m)^2 \mathr
m{Beta}(u;k,k)\,du$.
- **Polynomial tail decay:** If there exist $C, \alpha > 0$ with $1 - F(t) \leq
Ct^{-\alpha}$ for large $t$ (and similarly for the left tail), then $F^{-1}(u)$
grows at most polynomially in $1/(1-u)$, while the Beta density decays exponenti
ally (like $(1/2 - \varepsilon)^{k}$) away from $u = 1/2$. One can show the tail
 contributions $n\int_{|u - 1/2| > \varepsilon} (F^{-1}(u) - m)^2 \mathrm{Beta}(
u;k,k)\,du \to 0$, and the central contribution gives the limit $[2f(m)]^{-2}$ b
y the local linear approximation $F^{-1}(u) - m \approx (u - 1/2)/f(m)$.

Under any such condition, UI holds, and $nE[(Y_n - m)^2] \to [2f(m)]^{-2}$ follo
ws from the CLT plus UI.

---

### Conclusion

The convergence $nE[(Y_n - m)^2] \to [2f(m)]^{-2}$ **does not follow from the CL
T alone**. Convergence in distribution does not imply convergence of second mome
nts; uniform integrability of $\{n(Y_n - m)^2\}$ is the necessary and sufficient
 additional condition. The counterexample above — with $f$ continuous and positi
ve at $m$ (so the CLT holds) but tails so heavy that $1 - F(t) \sim c/\log t$ —
yields $E[(Y_n - m)^2] = \infty$ for every $n$, so $nE[(Y_n - m)^2] \equiv \inft
y \not\to [2f(m)]^{-2} < \infty$.
