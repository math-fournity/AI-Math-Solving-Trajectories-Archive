**Answer: Yes, it is possible.**

**Proof.**

Let $p$ be a prime, and consider the field $\mathbb{F}_p(t)$ of rational functio
ns in one indeterminate $t$ over the finite field $\mathbb{F}_p$. This is the fr
action field of the polynomial ring $\mathbb{F}_p[t]$.

**The field is infinite.** The elements $1, t, t^2, t^3, \dots$ are all distinct
 elements of $\mathbb{F}_p(t)$, so the field is infinite.

**Every element has finite additive order.** The field $\mathbb{F}_p(t)$ has cha
racteristic $p$, which means $p \cdot 1 = 0$ in the field. For any element $a \i
n \mathbb{F}_p(t)$, we compute:
$$p \cdot a = \underbrace{a + a + \cdots + a}_{p \text{ times}} = a \cdot \under
brace{(1 + 1 + \cdots + 1)}_{p \text{ times}} = a \cdot (p \cdot 1) = a \cdot 0
= 0.$$
Thus every element $a$ satisfies $p \cdot a = 0$, so the additive order of every
 element divides $p$ and is therefore finite (it is $1$ for $a = 0$, and exactly
 $p$ for $a \neq 0$, since no smaller positive multiple of a nonzero element can
 vanish in characteristic $p$).

Hence $\mathbb{F}_p(t)$ is an infinite field in which every element has finite a
dditive order. $\blacksquare$
