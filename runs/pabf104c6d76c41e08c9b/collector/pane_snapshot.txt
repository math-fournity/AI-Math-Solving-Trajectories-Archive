## Proof

Let $X_1, X_2, \dots$ be i.i.d. with $\mathbb{P}(X_i = 1) = \mathbb{P}(X_i = -1)
 = \tfrac{1}{2}$, and $S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$. Define the reverse filtration
$$\mathcal{G}_{n+1} = \sigma(S_{n+1}, S_{n+2}, \dots).$$

**Step 1. Reduce $\mathcal{G}_{n+1}$ to $\sigma(S_{n+1})$.**

For $k \ge 2$,
$$S_{n+k} = S_{n+1} + X_{n+2} + \cdots + X_{n+k},$$
so $X_{n+k} = S_{n+k} - S_{n+k-1}$ is $\mathcal{G}_{n+1}$-measurable, and conver
sely $S_{n+k}$ is determined by $S_{n+1}$ and $X_{n+2},\dots,X_{n+k}$. Hence
$$\mathcal{G}_{n+1} = \sigma(S_{n+1},\, X_{n+2}, X_{n+3}, \dots).$$

The family $\{X_{n+2}, X_{n+3}, \dots\}$ is independent of $\sigma(X_1, \dots, X
_{n+1})$, and therefore independent of $\sigma(X_i, S_{n+1})$ for every $1 \le i
 \le n+1$. By the standard independence-conditional-expectation lemma (if $Y$ is
 independent of $\sigma(Z, W)$ then $\mathbb{E}[Z \mid Y, W] = \mathbb{E}[Z \mid
 W]$), we obtain
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[X_i \,\big|\, \mathcal{G}_{n+1}\right] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[X
_i \,\big|\, S_{n+1}\right], \qquad 1 \le i \le n.$$

**Step 2. Compute $\mathbb{E}[X_i \mid S_{n+1}]$ by exchangeability.**

Since $X_1, \dots, X_{n+1}$ are i.i.d., the conditional law of $(X_1, \dots, X_{
n+1})$ given $S_{n+1} = s$ is exchangeable: for any permutation $\pi$ of $\{1,\d
ots,n+1\}$,
$$(X_1,\dots,X_{n+1}) \mid S_{n+1}=s \;\stackrel{d}{=}\; (X_{\pi(1)},\dots,X_{\p
i(n+1)}) \mid S_{n+1}=s.$$
Consequently $\mathbb{E}[X_i \mid S_{n+1}]$ is the same constant for all $1 \le
i \le n+1$. Summing over $i$,
$$\sum_{i=1}^{n+1} \mathbb{E}[X_i \mid S_{n+1}] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^{
n+1} X_i \,\bigg|\, S_{n+1}\right] = \mathbb{E}[S_{n+1} \mid S_{n+1}] = S_{n+1}.
$$
With all $n+1$ terms equal, each equals
$$\mathbb{E}[X_i \mid S_{n+1}] = \frac{S_{n+1}}{n+1}.$$

**Step 3. Combine.**

For every $1 \le i \le n$,
$$\boxed{\;\mathbb{E}\!\left[X_i \,\big|\, \mathcal{G}_{n+1}\right] = \frac{S_{n
+1}}{n+1}.\;}$$

**Consistency check.** Summing the result over $i = 1, \dots, n$:
$$\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}[X_i \mid \mathcal{G}_{n+1}] = \frac{n}{n+1} S_{n+1}.$$
On the other hand $S_n = S_{n+1} - X_{n+1}$, so $\mathbb{E}[S_n \mid \mathcal{G}
_{n+1}] = S_{n+1} - \mathbb{E}[X_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{G}_{n+1}] = S_{n+1} - \tfrac{
S_{n+1}}{n+1} = \tfrac{n}{n+1}S_{n+1}$, which agrees — confirming that $S_n$ is
a reverse martingale with respect to $(\mathcal{G}_n)$, consistent with the stat
ed martingale assumption.
