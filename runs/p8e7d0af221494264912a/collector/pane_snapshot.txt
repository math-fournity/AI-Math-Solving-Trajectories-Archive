## Proof

**Claim is TRUE.** We prove $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\gamma(n+1,n)}{
\Gamma(n+1)}=\frac12$.

**Step 1: Probabilistic interpretation.**

Recall that for $X\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(\alpha,1)$ (shape $\alpha$, rate $1$), the
density is $f(t)=\frac{t^{\alpha-1}e^{-t}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}$ for $t>0$, and its C
DF is
$$P(X\le x)=\frac{\gamma(\alpha,x)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}.$$
Taking $\alpha=n+1$ and $x=n$,
$$\frac{\gamma(n+1,n)}{\Gamma(n+1)}=P(X_n\le n),\qquad X_n\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(n+1
,1).$$
A $\mathrm{Gamma}(n+1,1)$ variable is the sum of $n+1$ i.i.d. $\mathrm{Exp}(1)$
variables $E_1,\dots,E_{n+1}$, each with mean $1$ and variance $1$. Hence
$$X_n=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}E_i,\qquad \mathbb E[X_n]=n+1,\quad \mathrm{Var}(X_n)=n+1.
$$

**Step 2: Apply the CLT.**

Define $Z_n=\dfrac{X_n-(n+1)}{\sqrt{n+1}}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n+1}}\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(
E_i-1)$. Since the $E_i-1$ are i.i.d. with mean $0$ and variance $1$, the classi
cal CLT gives
$$Z_n\xrightarrow{d}Z\sim N(0,1).$$
Equivalently, $F_{Z_n}(x)\to\Phi(x)$ for every $x\in\mathbb R$.

**Step 3: Evaluate the limit of $P(X_n\le n)$.**

We rewrite
$$P(X_n\le n)=P\!\left(Z_n\le \frac{n-(n+1)}{\sqrt{n+1}}\right)=P\!\left(Z_n\le
a_n\right)=F_{Z_n}(a_n),\qquad a_n=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n+1}}.$$
Note $a_n\to 0$. We show $F_{Z_n}(a_n)\to\Phi(0)=\tfrac12$.

Fix $\varepsilon>0$. For all sufficiently large $n$, $|a_n|<\varepsilon$, hence
$-\varepsilon<a_n<\varepsilon$ and by monotonicity of CDFs,
$$F_{Z_n}(-\varepsilon)\le F_{Z_n}(a_n)\le F_{Z_n}(\varepsilon).$$
Taking $\liminf$ and $\limsup$ and using $F_{Z_n}(x)\to\Phi(x)$ at the continuit
y points $\pm\varepsilon$,
$$\Phi(-\varepsilon)\le\liminf_{n\to\infty}F_{Z_n}(a_n)\le\limsup_{n\to\infty}F_
{Z_n}(a_n)\le\Phi(\varepsilon).$$
Letting $\varepsilon\to 0^+$, both bounds tend to $\Phi(0)=\tfrac12$. Therefore
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{Z_n}(a_n)=\frac12.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\gamma(n+1,n)}{\Gamma(n+1)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}P(X_n\le n
)=\frac12.$$

The claim holds. $\blacksquare$
