## Proof of the Finite-Dimensional Banach–Mazur Rotation Theorem

**Theorem.** Let $(E, \|\cdot\|)$ be a finite-dimensional real normed space. If
the group $G$ of linear isometric equivalences $E \to E$ acts transitively on th
e unit sphere $S = \{x \in E : \|x\| = 1\}$, then the norm $\|\cdot\|$ is induce
d by an inner product (i.e., $E$ is Euclidean / `InnerProductSpaceable E`).

### Step 1 — The isometry group $G$ is compact.

Since $E$ is finite-dimensional, choose any auxiliary inner product $\langle\cdo
t,\cdot\rangle_0$ on $E$, identifying $E \cong \mathbb{R}^n$ and $G$ with a subg
roup of $GL(n,\mathbb{R})$.

- **$G$ is closed:** If $T_k \in G$ and $T_k \to T$ in $GL(n,\mathbb{R})$, then
for every $x \in E$, $\|T_k x\| = \|x\|$; by continuity of the norm, $\|Tx\| = \
|x\|$, so $T \in G$.

- **$G$ is bounded:** The unit ball $B = \{x : \|x\| \le 1\}$ is compact in $\ma
thbb{R}^n$ (closed and bounded). Every $T \in G$ satisfies $T(B) = B$. Since all
 norms on $\mathbb{R}^n$ are equivalent, the operator norm $\|T\|_{\mathrm{op},0
}$ (measured against $\|\cdot\|_0$) is bounded over $T \in G$: indeed $T$ maps t
he compact set $B$ onto itself, and $B$ contains a $\|\cdot\|_0$-ball of some ra
dius $r > 0$, so $\|T\|_{\mathrm{op},0} \le 1/r$.

A closed and bounded subgroup of $GL(n,\mathbb{R})$ is compact. Hence $G$ is a *
*compact topological group**.

### Step 2 — Construct a $G$-invariant inner product by Haar averaging.

Since $G$ is compact, it carries a bi-invariant (left Haar) probability measure
$\mu$. Define:

$$\langle x, y \rangle \;:=\; \int_G \langle Tx,\, Ty\rangle_0 \, d\mu(T).$$

**Well-definedness and inner-product properties:**
- *Bilinearity* follows from linearity of each $T$ and bilinearity of $\langle\c
dot,\cdot\rangle_0$.
- *Symmetry:* $\langle x,y\rangle = \int_G \langle Tx,Ty\rangle_0\,d\mu = \int_G
 \langle Ty,Tx\rangle_0\,d\mu = \langle y,x\rangle$.
- *Positive definiteness:* For $x \neq 0$, every $T \in G$ is invertible so $Tx
\neq 0$, giving $\langle Tx,Tx\rangle_0 = \|Tx\|_0^2 > 0$. Integrating a strictl
y positive continuous function over a compact group yields $\langle x,x\rangle >
 0$.

**$G$-invariance:** For any $U \in G$,

$$\langle Ux, Uy\rangle = \int_G \langle TUx, TUy\rangle_0\,d\mu(T) = \int_G \la
ngle Sx, Sy\rangle_0\,d\mu(S) = \langle x,y\rangle,$$

using the substitution $S = TU$ and left-invariance of $\mu$.

### Step 3 — The $G$-invariant norm is a scalar multiple of $\|\cdot\|$.

Let $\|x\|_G := \sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle}$. By $G$-invariance, $\|Tx\|_G = \|x\|
_G$ for all $T \in G$.

By the transitivity hypothesis, for any $x, y \in S$ there exists $T \in G$ with
 $Tx = y$. Therefore:

$$\|y\|_G = \|Tx\|_G = \|x\|_G.$$

So $\|x\|_G$ is **constant** on $S$; call this constant $c$. Since $\|\cdot\|_G$
 is a genuine norm and $S$ excludes the origin, $c > 0$.
