# Proof

We seek all values of $k$ for a strongly regular Moore graph of diameter $2$ wit
h $\lambda=0$ and $\mu=1$.

## Step 1: Parameter relations

A strongly regular graph $\mathrm{srg}(n,k,\lambda,\mu)$ satisfies
$$(n-k-1)\mu = k(k-1-\lambda).$$
With $\lambda=0, \mu=1$:
$$n-k-1 = k(k-1) \implies n = k^2+1.$$
This is precisely the **Moore bound** for degree $k$ and diameter $2$. Hence any
 $\mathrm{srg}(k^2+1, k, 0, 1)$ is a Moore graph of diameter $2$ (equivalently,
a $k$-regular graph of girth $5$), and conversely every Moore graph of diameter
$2$ is strongly regular with $\lambda=0, \mu=1$.

## Step 2: Eigenvalues and multiplicities

The non-principal eigenvalues of an $\mathrm{srg}(n,k,\lambda,\mu)$ are the root
s of
$$x^2 - (\lambda-\mu)x - (k-\mu)=0.$$
With $\lambda=0,\mu=1$:
$$x^2 + x - (k-1) = 0 \implies x = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{4k-3}}{2}.$$
Set $s = \sqrt{4k-3}$, so the eigenvalues are $r = \frac{-1+s}{2}$ and $\ell = \
frac{-1-s}{2}$, with multiplicities $f$ and $g$.

Using $\operatorname{tr}(A)=0$: $k + fr + g\ell = 0$, and $f+g = n-1 = k^2$. Sol
ving:
$$f-g = \frac{k^2-2k}{s}.$$

## Step 3: Integrality constraint

**Case A: $s$ irrational.** Then $f-g$ is rational only if $k^2-2k=0$, giving $k
=2$ (excluding $k=0$). This yields $n=5$: the $5$-cycle $C_5 = \mathrm{srg}(5,2,
0,1)$, which has diameter $2$ and girth $5$. ✓

**Case B: $s$ a positive integer.** Since $4k-3$ is odd, $s$ is odd, and $k = \f
rac{s^2+3}{4}$ is an integer. The multiplicities $f,g$ are integers iff $s \mid
(k^2-2k)$.

Compute:
$$k^2 - 2k = \frac{(s^2+3)(s^2-5)}{16}.$$
Since $s$ is odd, $\gcd(s,16)=1$, so we need $s \mid (s^2+3)(s^2-5)$. Reducing m
od $s$:
$$(s^2+3)(s^2-5) \equiv 3\cdot(-5) = -15 \pmod{s}.$$
Hence $s \mid 15$.

## Step 4: Enumerate

The positive odd divisors of $15$ are $s \in \{1, 3, 5, 15\}$:

| $s$ | $k=\frac{s^2+3}{4}$ | $n=k^2+1$ | Graph |
|-----|---------------------|-----------|-------|
| $1$ | $1$ | $2$ | $K_2$ — diameter $1$, **excluded** |
| $3$ | $3$ | $10$ | Petersen graph ✓ |
| $5$ | $7$ | $50$ | Hoffman–Singleton graph ✓ |
| $15$ | $57$ | $3250$ | Existence open, but parameters are feasible ✓ |

The case $s=1$ ($k=1$) gives $K_2$ with diameter $1 \neq 2$, so it is excluded.

## Conclusion

The possible values of $k$ for a strongly regular Moore graph of diameter $2$ wi
th $\lambda=0$ and $\mu=1$ are:

$$\boxed{k \in \{2,\, 3,\, 7,\, 57\}}.$$

The cases $k=2,3,7$ are realized by $C_5$, the Petersen graph, and the Hoffman–S
ingleton graph respectively; the case $k=57$ (with $n=3250$) remains a famous op
en existence problem, but it passes all necessary algebraic conditions.
