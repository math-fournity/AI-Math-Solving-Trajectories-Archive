**Proof.**

The matrix $A$ satisfies $A^3 = A + I$, i.e., $A^3 - A - I = 0$. Hence the minim
al polynomial $m_A(x)$ of $A$ divides
$$p(x) = x^3 - x - 1.$$

**Step 1: Factor $p(x)$ over $\mathbb{R}$.**

The discriminant of $x^3 + px + q$ is $\Delta = -4p^3 - 27q^2$. Here $p = -1$, $
q = -1$, so
$$\Delta = -4(-1)^3 - 27(-1)^2 = 4 - 27 = -23 < 0.$$
Since $\Delta < 0$, the polynomial $p(x)$ has **one real root** $\alpha$ and **t
wo complex conjugate roots** $\beta, \overline{\beta}$.

We locate the real root: $p(0) = -1 < 0$ and $p(2) = 5 > 0$, so $\alpha \in (0,
2)$, in particular $\alpha > 0$.

By Vieta's formulas (for $x^3 - x - 1$, the product of roots equals $-(-1) = 1$)
:
$$\alpha \cdot \beta \cdot \overline{\beta} = 1.$$
Since $\alpha > 0$, we get $\beta\overline{\beta} = |\beta|^2 = 1/\alpha > 0$.

**Step 2: Eigenvalues of $A$.**

Since $m_A(x) \mid p(x)$, every eigenvalue of $A$ (over $\mathbb{C}$) is a root
of $p(x)$, hence belongs to $\{\alpha, \beta, \overline{\beta}\}$.

Since $A$ is a real matrix, non-real eigenvalues occur in conjugate pairs with e
qual algebraic multiplicity. Therefore, if $\beta$ appears with algebraic multip
licity $b \geq 0$, then $\overline{\beta}$ also appears with multiplicity $b$, a
nd $\alpha$ appears with multiplicity $a = n - 2b \geq 0$.

**Step 3: Compute $\det A$.**

The determinant is the product of all eigenvalues (with algebraic multiplicity):
$$\det A = \alpha^{a} \cdot \beta^{b} \cdot \overline{\beta}^{\,b} = \alpha^{a}
\cdot (\beta\overline{\beta})^{b} = \alpha^{a} \cdot |\beta|^{2b}.$$

Since $\alpha > 0$ and $|\beta|^2 = 1/\alpha > 0$, every factor is strictly posi
tive:
$$\det A = \alpha^{a} \cdot \left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)^{b} = \alpha^{a - b} >
 0.$$

In either form, $\det A$ is a product of strictly positive real numbers, so $\de
t A > 0$. $\blacksquare$
