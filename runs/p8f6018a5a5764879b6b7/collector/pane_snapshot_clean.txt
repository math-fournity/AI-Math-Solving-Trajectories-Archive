## Analysis

The candidate delta sequence is:
$$f_A(x-t) = A\, e^{-A(x-t)}\, \theta(x-t)$$

where $\theta$ is the Heaviside step function. Setting $s = x - t$, this becomes
 $f_A(s) = A\, e^{-As}\,\theta(s)$, which is the probability density function of
 an exponential distribution with rate parameter $A$.

To be a valid representation of $\delta(x-t)$, the family $\{f_A\}_{A>0}$ must s
atisfy the two defining properties of a delta sequence:

1. **Normalization**: $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_A(s)\,ds = 1$ for all $A > 0$.
2. **Concentration**: For every $\varepsilon > 0$, $\int_{|s|>\varepsilon} f_A(s
)\,ds \to 0$ as $A \to \infty$.

Together these imply the **sifting property**: $\lim_{A\to\infty}\int_{-\infty}^
{\infty} f_A(s)\,g(s)\,ds = g(0)$ for every continuous bounded test function $g$
.

## Proof

**Property 1 (Normalization).** For every $A > 0$:
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} A\, e^{-As}\,\theta(s)\,ds = \int_{0}^{\infty} A\, e^{
-As}\,ds = A\cdot\frac{1}{A} = 1. \quad\checkmark$$

**Property 2 (Concentration).** Let $\varepsilon > 0$. Since $\theta(s)=0$ for $
s<0$, the only contribution outside $(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$ comes from $s \
geq \varepsilon$:
$$\int_{|s|>\varepsilon} f_A(s)\,ds = \int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty} A\, e^{-As}\,d
s = e^{-A\varepsilon}.$$
As $A \to \infty$, $e^{-A\varepsilon} \to 0$. $\quad\checkmark$

**Property 3 (Sifting — derived from 1 and 2).** Let $g$ be any continuous funct
ion (bounded on $[0,\infty)$ suffices). Then:
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_A(s)\,g(s)\,ds = \int_{0}^{\infty} A\, e^{-As}\,g(s)
\,ds.$$
Substituting $u = As$ (so $s = u/A$, $ds = du/A$):
$$= \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-u}\,g\!\left(\frac{u}{A}\right)du.$$
For each fixed $u \geq 0$, as $A \to \infty$ we have $g(u/A) \to g(0)$ by contin
uity of $g$ at $0$. Since $|e^{-u} g(u/A)| \leq M\, e^{-u}$ where $M = \sup_{s\g
eq 0}|g(s)|$, and $M e^{-u}$ is integrable on $[0,\infty)$, the **Dominated Conv
ergence Theorem** gives:
$$\lim_{A\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-u}\,g\!\left(\frac{u}{A}\right)du = g(0
)\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-u}\,du = g(0)\cdot 1 = g(0).$$

Restoring $s = x - t$, this yields:
$$\lim_{A\to\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} A\, e^{-A(x-t)}\,\theta(x-t)\,g(x)\,d
x = g(t),$$
which is precisely the sifting property of $\delta(x-t)$.

**Remark on the positivity constraint.** The condition that $A, x, t > 0$ does n
ot affect validity. Positivity of $A$ is required for the exponential decay and
normalization. The positivity of $x$ and $t$ merely restricts the domain of inte
rest; the delta sequence localizes near $x = t$ from the right ($x > t$) side, a
nd all three properties hold on any domain containing a neighborhood of $x = t$.

## Conclusion

The expression satisfies all defining properties of a Dirac delta sequence: unit
 normalization for every $A$, vanishing mass outside any neighborhood of $x = t$
 as $A \to \infty$, and the sifting property against continuous test functions.
It is a **valid** (one-sided) representation of the Dirac delta function $\delta
(x-t)$.
