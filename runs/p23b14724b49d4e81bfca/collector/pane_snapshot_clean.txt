$$\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(1+z^{2^k}\right)=\frac{1-z^{2^n}}{1-z}.$$
This follows from repeatedly factoring differences of squares:
$$1-z^{2^n}=(1-z)(1+z)(1+z^2)(1+z^4)\cdots\left(1+z^{2^{n-1}}\right).$$

**Apply to our product.** Set $z=\dfrac{1+i}{2}$. Our product runs from $k=0$ to
 $k=2001$, i.e., $n=2002$ terms:
$$P=\prod_{k=0}^{2001}\left(1+z^{2^k}\right)=\frac{1-z^{2^{2002}}}{1-z}.$$
