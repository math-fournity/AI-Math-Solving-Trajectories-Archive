## Proof

**Theorem (Baire Category Theorem).** Let $(X, d)$ be a complete metric space, a
nd let $\{U_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ be a countable collection of dense, open subsets
 of $X$. Then $\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} U_n$ is dense in $X$.

**Proof.** We must show that $\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} U_n$ is dense in $X$, i.e.,
 every nonempty open subset of $X$ meets $\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} U_n$.

Let $W \subseteq X$ be any nonempty open set. We will produce a point $x \in W \
cap \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} U_n$.

**Step 1 (Base case).** Since $U_1$ is dense and open, $W \cap U_1$ is a nonempt
y open set. Pick any point $x_1 \in W \cap U_1$. Since $W \cap U_1$ is open, the
re exists $r_1 > 0$ with $r_1 < 1$ such that the closed ball
$$\overline{B}(x_1, r_1) = \{x \in X : d(x, x_1) \le r_1\} \subseteq W \cap U_1.
$$
(We use the closed ball to ensure compactness-like closure properties; we can al
ways shrink the radius so that the closed ball of radius $r_1/2$ fits inside the
 open set, then relabel.)

**Step 2 (Inductive construction).** Suppose we have constructed $x_n \in X$ and
 $r_n > 0$ with $r_n < 1/n$ such that
$$\overline{B}(x_n, r_n) \subseteq U_1 \cap U_2 \cap \cdots \cap U_n \cap W.$$
Since $U_{n+1}$ is dense and open, the open set $B(x_n, r_n) \cap U_{n+1}$ is no
nempty. Choose $x_{n+1} \in B(x_n, r_n) \cap U_{n+1}$. Since this set is open, t
here exists $r_{n+1} > 0$ with $r_{n+1} < 1/(n+1)$ and $r_{n+1} < r_n - d(x_n, x
_{n+1})$ such that
$$\overline{B}(x_{n+1}, r_{n+1}) \subseteq B(x_n, r_n) \cap U_{n+1} \subseteq \o
verline{B}(x_n, r_n) \cap U_{n+1}.$$
Thus
$$\overline{B}(x_{n+1}, r_{n+1}) \subseteq U_1 \cap \cdots \cap U_{n+1} \cap W,$
$
completing the induction.

**Step 3 (The sequence is Cauchy).** By construction, for $m > n$:
$$x_m \in \overline{B}(x_{n+1}, r_{n+1}) \subseteq B(x_n, r_n),$$
so $d(x_m, x_n) < r_n < 1/n$. Hence for any $\varepsilon > 0$, choosing $N > 1/\
varepsilon$ gives $d(x_m, x_n) < \varepsilon$ for all $m, n \ge N$. The sequence
 $(x_n)$ is Cauchy.

**Step 4 (Convergence and conclusion).** Since $X$ is complete, $x_n \to x$ for
some $x \in X$. For each fixed $n$ and all $m > n$, we have $x_m \in \overline{B
}(x_n, r_n)$, so by taking the limit, $x \in \overline{B}(x_n, r_n) \subseteq U_
n$. This holds for every $n$, so $x \in \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} U_n$. Also $\over
line{B}(x_1, r_1) \subseteq W$, so $x \in W$.

Therefore $x \in W \cap \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} U_n$, proving this intersection i
s nonempty. Since $W$ was an arbitrary nonempty open set, $\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty
} U_n$ is dense in $X$. $\blacksquare$

**Answer: Yes**, the intersection of a countable collection of dense, open subse
ts of a complete metric space is dense in the space.
