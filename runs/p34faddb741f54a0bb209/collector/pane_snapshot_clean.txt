## Proof

View $F_{q^2}$ as a 2-dimensional vector space over $F_q$ (using any $F_q$-basis
, e.g. $\{1, \beta\}$ where $\beta$ is a root of an irreducible quadratic over $
F_q$). For each $\alpha \in F_{q^2}^*$, the map $x \mapsto \alpha x$ is $F_q$-li
near and invertible (its inverse is multiplication by $\alpha^{-1}$), so it defi
nes a matrix $M_\alpha \in GL_2(F_q)$. Thus $\varphi: F_{q^2}^* \to GL_2(F_q)$ i
s well-defined.

**$\varphi$ is a group homomorphism.** For $\alpha, \gamma \in F_{q^2}^*$, multi
plication by $\alpha\gamma$ equals the composition of multiplication by $\gamma$
 followed by multiplication by $\alpha$, so $M_{\alpha\gamma} = M_\alpha M_\gamm
a$.

**The kernel is trivial.** Suppose $M_\alpha = I$, i.e. multiplication by $\alph
a$ is the identity map on $F_{q^2}$. Then in particular $\alpha \cdot 1 = 1$, gi
ving $\alpha = 1$. Hence $\ker\varphi = \{1\}$.

Since $\varphi$ is injective,

$$|\operatorname{im}\varphi| = |F_{q^2}^*| = q^2 - 1.$$
