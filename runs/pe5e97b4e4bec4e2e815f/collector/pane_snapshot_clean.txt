Yes. The one-point compactification of a locally compact Hausdorff space is uniq
ue up to homeomorphism, and this uniqueness is natural (functorial) with respect
 to homeomorphisms of the underlying spaces. Hence homeomorphic locally compact
Hausdorff spaces have homeomorphic one-point compactifications.

**Proof.**

Let $X$ and $Y$ be locally compact Hausdorff spaces, and let $h\colon X \to Y$ b
e a homeomorphism.

**Step 1 (Construction of the one-point compactification).** For a locally compa
ct Hausdorff space $X$, its one-point compactification is the set $\alpha X := X
 \sqcup \{\infty_X\}$ endowed with the topology
$$\tau_{\alpha X} \;=\; \mathcal{T}_X \;\cup\; \{\,\alpha X \setminus K : K \sub
seteq X \text{ compact}\,\},$$
where $\mathcal{T}_X$ is the original topology on $X$. The point $\infty_X$ is t
he unique point added. It is a standard theorem that $\alpha X$ is a compact Hau
sdorff space, that $X$ embeds as an open dense subspace, and that $\alpha X \set
minus X = \{\infty_X\}$.

**Step 2 (Extending $h$ to a bijection $\alpha h\colon \alpha X \to \alpha Y$).*
* Define
$$\alpha h(x) = h(x) \;\text{ for } x\in X, \qquad \alpha h(\infty_X) = \infty_Y
.$$
Since $h$ is a bijection $X\to Y$ and $\infty_X,\infty_Y$ are single distinguish
ed points, $\alpha h$ is a bijection $\alpha X \to \alpha Y$.

**Step 3 ($\alpha h$ is continuous).** We check continuity at every point.

- For $x\in X$: any open neighborhood of $h(x)=\alpha h(x)$ in $Y$ is of the for
m $V\subseteq Y$ open in $Y$ (since $Y$ is open in $\alpha Y$). Then $U := h^{-1
}(V)$ is open in $X$, hence open in $\alpha X$, contains $x$, and $\alpha h[U] =
 V$. So $\alpha h$ is continuous at $x$.

- At $\infty_X$: a basic open neighborhood of $\infty_Y$ in $\alpha Y$ is $\alph
a Y \setminus K$ for some compact $K\subseteq Y$. Since $h$ is a homeomorphism,
$h^{-1}(K)$ is compact in $X$. Then
$$\alpha X \setminus h^{-1}(K)$$
is an open neighborhood of $\infty_X$ in $\alpha X$, and
$$\alpha h\bigl[\alpha X \setminus h^{-1}(K)\bigr] \;=\; \alpha Y \setminus K.$$
So $\alpha h$ is continuous at $\infty_X$.

**Step 4 ($\alpha h$ is a homeomorphism).** The same argument with $h^{-1}$ in p
lace of $h$ shows that $\alpha(h^{-1})\colon \alpha Y \to \alpha X$ is a continu
ous two-sided inverse of $\alpha h$. Equivalently, since $\alpha X$ is compact a
nd $\alpha Y$ is Hausdorff, a continuous bijection from $\alpha X$ to $\alpha Y$
 is automatically a homeomorphism. Either way, $\alpha h$ is a homeomorphism.

**Step 5 (Conclusion).** We have constructed, from an arbitrary homeomorphism $h
\colon X\to Y$, a homeomorphism $\alpha h\colon \alpha X \to \alpha Y$ between t
he one-point compactifications. Therefore the one-point compactifications of hom
eomorphic locally compact Hausdorff spaces are homeomorphic. $\blacksquare$
