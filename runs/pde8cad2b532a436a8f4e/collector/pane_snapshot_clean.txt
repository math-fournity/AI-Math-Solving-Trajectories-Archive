**Proof.**

Let $R = \mathbb{C}[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ and $I = (P_1, \dots, P_t) \subseteq R$ be
 the ideal generated by the given polynomials. The variety $V = V(I) \subseteq \
mathbb{A}^n_{\mathbb{C}}$ is non-empty by hypothesis, so $I$ is a proper ideal o
f $R$.

We use **Krull's Height Theorem** (the generalized principal ideal theorem): *In
 a Noetherian ring, if an ideal $I$ is generated by $t$ elements, then every min
imal prime $\mathfrak{p}$ over $I$ satisfies $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) \l
e t$.*

Since $R = \mathbb{C}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ is Noetherian, every minimal prime $\mathf
rak{p}$ over $I$ has
$$\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) \le t.$$

The ring $R$ is a finitely generated algebra over a field, hence catenary, and i
n particular for every prime $\mathfrak{p}$,
$$\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) + \dim(R/\mathfrak{p}) = \dim R = n.$$

Therefore
$$\dim(R/\mathfrak{p}) = n - \operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) \ge n - t.$$

The dimension of the variety $V = V(I)$ is the supremum of $\dim(R/\mathfrak{p})
$ over all minimal primes $\mathfrak{p}$ of $I$:
$$\dim V = \max_{\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Min}(I)} \dim(R/\mathfrak{p}) \g
e n - t.$$

(The maximum is attained because $R$ is Noetherian, so $I$ has finitely many min
imal primes.) Since $V$ is non-empty, $I \ne R$, so $\operatorname{Min}(I)$ is n
on-empty and the dimension is well-defined (not $-\infty$).

Hence $\dim V \ge n - t$. $\blacksquare$
