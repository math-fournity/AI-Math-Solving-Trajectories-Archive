## Proof

We must show
$$
I_n:=\int_n^\infty\left(1+\frac{x}{n}\right)^n e^{-x}\,dx \xrightarrow[n\to\inft
y]{}0.
$$

**Step 1. Shift the integration variable.** Set $x=n+t$, so $dx=dt$, $x=n\iff t=
0$, $x\to\infty\iff t\to\infty$. Then
$$
I_n=\int_0^\infty\left(1+\frac{n+t}{n}\right)^n e^{-(n+t)}\,dt
    =e^{-n}\int_0^\infty\left(2+\frac{t}{n}\right)^n e^{-t}\,dt.
$$

Factor out the $2$:
$$
\left(2+\frac{t}{n}\right)^n=2^n\left(1+\frac{t}{2n}\right)^n,
$$
hence
$$
I_n=\left(\frac{2}{e}\right)^n\int_0^\infty\left(1+\frac{t}{2n}\right)^n e^{-t}\
,dt. \tag{1}
$$

**Step 2. Bound the remaining factor.** Using the standard inequality $\ln(1+u)\
le u$ for $u>-1$, with $u=\frac{t}{2n}\ge 0$:
$$
\left(1+\frac{t}{2n}\right)^n=\exp\!\left(n\ln\!\left(1+\frac{t}{2n}\right)\righ
t)\le\exp\!\left(n\cdot\frac{t}{2n}\right)=e^{t/2}.
$$

Substituting into (1):
$$
0\le I_n\le\left(\frac{2}{e}\right)^n\int_0^\infty e^{t/2}e^{-t}\,dt
      =\left(\frac{2}{e}\right)^n\int_0^\infty e^{-t/2}\,dt
      =\left(\frac{2}{e}\right)^n\cdot 2. \tag{2}
$$

(The lower bound $0$ holds because the original integrand is non-negative.)

**Step 3. Take the limit.** Since $e>2$, we have $\frac{2}{e}<1$, so
$$
\left(\frac{2}{e}\right)^n\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.
$$
By (2) and the squeeze theorem,
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}I_n=0.
$$

$\blacksquare$
