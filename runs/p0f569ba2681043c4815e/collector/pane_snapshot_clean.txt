## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\sin x}{x - i}\,dx$
.

**Step 1: Decompose $\sin x$.** Using $\sin x = \dfrac{e^{ix} - e^{-ix}}{2i}$,

$$I = \frac{1}{2i}\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{ix}}{x-i}\,dx}_{I_
1} \;-\; \frac{1}{2i}\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-ix}}{x-i}\,dx}
_{I_2}.$$

**Step 2: Evaluate $I_1$ by contour integration.** Consider $f_1(z) = \dfrac{e^{
iz}}{z - i}$, integrated over the contour consisting of $[-R,R]$ plus the upper
semicircle $\Gamma_R$. Since $|e^{iz}| = e^{-\operatorname{Im} z} \to 0$ as $\op
eratorname{Im} z \to +\infty$, Jordan's lemma gives $\int_{\Gamma_R} f_1 \to 0$
as $R\to\infty$. The only singularity inside the contour is the simple pole at $
z = i$, with residue

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=i} f_1(z) = \lim_{z\to i}(z-i)\frac{e^{iz}}{z-i} = e^{i\
cdot i} = e^{-1}.$$

By the residue theorem, $I_1 = 2\pi i \cdot e^{-1} = \dfrac{2\pi i}{e}$.

**Step 3: Evaluate $I_2$ by contour integration.** Consider $f_2(z) = \dfrac{e^{
-iz}}{z - i}$, integrated over $[-R,R]$ plus the **lower** semicircle (since $|e
^{-iz}| = e^{\operatorname{Im} z} \to 0$ as $\operatorname{Im} z \to -\infty$).
The pole at $z = i$ lies in the **upper** half-plane, so it is **not** enclosed.
 Jordan's lemma again kills the arc contribution, giving

$$I_2 = 0.$$

**Step 4: Combine.**

$$I = \frac{1}{2i}\,I_1 - \frac{1}{2i}\,I_2 = \frac{1}{2i}\cdot\frac{2\pi i}{e}
- \frac{1}{2i}\cdot 0 = \frac{\pi}{e}.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x-i}\,dx = \frac{\pi
}{e}.}$$
