## Proof

Let $B$ be a unital $C^*$-algebra and $y \in B$ with $y^* = y$ and $y^2 = y^4$.

**Step 1: The spectrum is real.** Since $y$ is self-adjoint, $\sigma(y) \subsete
q \mathbb{R}$.

**Step 2: The polynomial relation forces the spectrum into $\{-1,0,1\}$.** The r
elation $y^2 = y^4$ gives $p(y) = 0$ where $p(t) = t^4 - t^2 = t^2(t-1)(t+1)$. B
y the spectral mapping theorem, for every $\lambda \in \sigma(y)$,
$$0 = p(\lambda) = \lambda^2(\lambda - 1)(\lambda + 1).$$
Hence $\lambda \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$, so
$$\sigma(y) \subseteq \{-1, 0, 1\}.$$

**Step 3: The spectrum is nonempty.** In a unital $C^*$-algebra the spectrum of
any element is nonempty, so $\sigma(y) \neq \emptyset$.

**Step 4: Every nonempty subset of $\{-1,0,1\}$ is realized.** We verify each ca
se:

- $\{0\}$: $y = 0$.
- $\{1\}$: $y = 1$ (the unit).
- $\{-1\}$: $y = -1$.
- $\{0,1\}$: any nonzero projection $p \neq 1$ (self-adjoint idempotent); then $
p^2 = p$, so $p^4 = p^2$, and $\sigma(p) = \{0,1\}$.
- $\{-1,0\}$: $y = -p$ for a projection $p$ with $0 \neq p \neq 1$; then $y^2 =
p = y^4$ and $\sigma(y) = -\sigma(p) = \{-1,0\}$.
- $\{-1,1\}$: any self-adjoint unitary $u$ with $u \neq \pm 1$; then $u^2 = 1 =
u^4$ and $\sigma(u) \subseteq \{-1,1\}$, nonempty and not a singleton, so $\sigm
a(u) = \{-1,1\}$.
- $\{-1,0,1\}$: in $B = \mathbb{C}^3$, take $y = (-1, 0, 1)$; then $y^* = y$, $y
^2 = (1,0,1) = y^4$, and $\sigma(y) = \{-1,0,1\}$.

**Conclusion.** The spectrum of $y$ is an arbitrary nonempty subset of $\{-1, 0,
 1\}$:
$$\boxed{\sigma(y) \subseteq \{-1,\, 0,\, 1\},\quad \sigma(y) \neq \emptyset,}$$
and conversely every nonempty subset of $\{-1,0,1\}$ occurs as the spectrum of s
ome such $y$.
