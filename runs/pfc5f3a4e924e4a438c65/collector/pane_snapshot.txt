## Proof

**Claim.** $T_n = n! + 2^n$ for all $n \geq 0$.

Since the recurrence is **linear**, it suffices to show that both $f(n) = n!$ an

$$x_n = (n+4)\,x_{n-1} - 4n\,x_{n-2} + (4n-8)\,x_{n-3}, \qquad n \geq 3, \tag{$\
star$}$$
and that $f+g$ matches the initial values. By linearity, $f+g$ then satisfies $(
\star)$, and uniqueness of the solution to a third-order linear recurrence with
three initial conditions gives the result.

### Step 1: $g(n) = 2^n$ satisfies $(\star)$.

Substituting $x_k = 2^k$ into the right-hand side and factoring out $2^{n-3}$:

$$(n+4)\cdot 2^{n-1} - 4n\cdot 2^{n-2} + (4n-8)\cdot 2^{n-3} = 2^{n-3}\bigl[4(n+
4) - 8n + (4n-8)\bigr].$$

The bracket simplifies to $4n + 16 - 8n + 4n - 8 = 8$, so the RHS equals $2^{n-3
}\cdot 8 = 2^n$, which equals the LHS. ✓

### Step 2: $f(n) = n!$ satisfies $(\star)$.

Substituting $x_k = k!$ into the right-hand side and factoring out $(n-3)!$:

$$(n+4)(n-1)! - 4n(n-2)! + (4n-8)(n-3)! = (n-3)!\bigl[(n+4)(n-1)(n-2) - 4n(n-2)
+ 4(n-2)\bigr].$$

Factor $(n-2)$ from the bracket:

$$(n-3)!\,(n-2)\bigl[(n+4)(n-1) - 4n + 4\bigr].$$

The inner bracket is $(n^2+3n-4) - 4n + 4 = n^2 - n = n(n-1)$. Therefore the RHS

$$(n-3)!\,(n-2)\cdot n(n-1) = n!,$$

which equals the LHS. ✓
