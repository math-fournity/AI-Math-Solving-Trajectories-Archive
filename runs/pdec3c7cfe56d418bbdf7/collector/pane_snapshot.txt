Looking at this problem, I need to determine the codimension of the singular loc
us of the quotient variety $V/G$, where $G \subset GL(V)$ is a finite group acti
ng linearly on a finite-dimensional complex vector space $V$.

**Answer: The codimension of the singular locus in $V/G$ is at least $2$.**

## Proof

Let $\pi: V \to V/G$ be the quotient map, and let $n = \dim V = \dim(V/G)$ (the
map $\pi$ is finite, hence dimension-preserving).

### Step 1: Characterization of the singular locus

For $v \in V$, let $G_v = \operatorname{Stab}(v) = \{g \in G : gv = v\}$.

**Claim:** $\pi(v)$ is a smooth point of $V/G$ if and only if $G_v$ is generated
 by pseudo-reflections.

*A pseudo-reflection* is an element $g \in GL(V)$ that fixes a hyperplane pointw
ise (i.e., $\operatorname{codim}(V^g) = 1$, where $V^g = \{v \in V : gv = v\}$).

*Proof of Claim.* Since $G_v$ fixes $v$, after translating $v$ to the origin, $G
_v$ acts linearly on $V$ with $0$ as a fixed point. By Luna's étale slice theore
m (which for finite groups simplifies to a direct argument), the quotient map $V
/G_v \to V/G$ is étale at the image of $v$. Since étale morphisms preserve the s
mooth/singular distinction:

$$\pi(v) \text{ smooth in } V/G \iff \bar{v} \text{ smooth in } V/G_v,$$

where $\bar{v}$ is the image of $v$ in $V/G_v$.

The local ring of $V/G_v$ at $\bar{v}$ has completion $\mathbb{C}[[x_1,\dots,x_n
]]^{G_v}$. By the Chevalley–Shephard–Todd theorem (in its complete/local form),
$\mathbb{C}[[x_1,\dots,x_n]]^{G_v}$ is a regular local ring (i.e., a power serie
s ring) if and only if $G_v$ is generated by pseudo-reflections. $\square$

### Step 2: Description of the singular locus

From the claim:

$$\operatorname{Sing}(V/G) = \pi\!\left(\bigcup_{\substack{g \in G \setminus \{\
mathrm{id}\} \\ g \text{ not a pseudo-reflection}}} V^g\right).$$

**Justification:** $G_v$ fails to be generated by pseudo-reflections if and only
 if $G_v$ contains some element $g \neq \mathrm{id}$ that is not a pseudo-reflec
tion (the trivial group $\{\mathrm{id}\}$ is vacuously generated by pseudo-refle
ctions, giving smooth points). The set of $v$ with such a $g$ in $G_v$ is exactl
y $\bigcup_{g} V^g$ over all non-identity, non-pseudo-reflection elements $g$.

### Step 3: Codimension estimate

For each $g \in G \setminus \{\mathrm{id}\}$ that is **not** a pseudo-reflection
:
- $g \neq \mathrm{id}$, so $V^g \neq V$, meaning $\operatorname{codim}(V^g) \geq
 1$.
- $g$ is not a pseudo-reflection, so $V^g$ is not a hyperplane, meaning $\operat
orname{codim}(V^g) \geq 2$.

Thus each $V^g$ is a **proper linear subspace of codimension $\geq 2$** in $V$.

The finite union $S = \bigcup_{g} V^g$ therefore satisfies:
$$\dim S \leq n - 2, \quad \text{i.e.,} \quad \operatorname{codim}_V(S) \geq 2.$
$

### Step 4: Image under the finite quotient map

The quotient map $\pi: V \to V/G$ is **finite** (since $G$ is finite). Finite mo
rphisms preserve dimensions of closed subvarieties: for any closed $X \subseteq
V$,

$$\dim \pi(X) = \dim X.$$

Therefore:
$$\dim \pi(S) = \dim S \leq n - 2 = \dim(V/G) - 2.$$

This gives:
$$\operatorname{codim}_{V/G}(\operatorname{Sing}(V/G)) = \operatorname{codim}_{V
/G}(\pi(S)) \geq 2.$$

### Step 5: Sharpness

The bound is achieved: take $G = \{\pm I\}$ acting on $V = \mathbb{C}^2$. The el
ement $-I$ is not a pseudo-reflection (it fixes only $\{0\}$, codimension $2$).
The quotient $\mathbb{C}^2/\{\pm 1\}$ has an isolated $A_1$ singularity at the o
rigin, so $\operatorname{Sing}(V/G) = \{\text{point}\}$ has codimension exactly
$2$.

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{\operatorname{codim}_{V/G}(\operatorname{Sing}(V/G)) \geq 2.}$$

Equivalently, $V/G$ is **regular in codimension $1$** (the $R_1$ property): ever
y codimension-$1$ point of $V/G$ is smooth. This is a fundamental property of qu
otient singularities by finite groups.
