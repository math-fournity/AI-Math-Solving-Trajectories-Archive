**Answer: TRUE** (for a non-zero positive Radon measure; the condition $0<\mu(U_
n)$ forces $\mu\neq 0$, which is the meaningful reading of "positive" here).

**Proof.**

Let $(X,d)$ be locally compact and separable (hence second countable, since a se
parable metric space is second countable), and let $\mu$ be a non-zero positive
Radon measure on $X$.

**Step 1. A compact exhaustion with interior nesting.**
Since $X$ is locally compact, Hausdorff, and second countable, there exists a se
quence of open sets $(G_n)_{n\ge 1}$ such that
- $\overline{G_n}$ is compact,
- $\overline{G_n}\subset G_{n+1}$ for all $n$,
- $\bigcup_{n} G_n = X$.

(Standard construction: take a countable base $\{B_k\}$ of precompact open sets
— which exists because local compactness + second countability gives a base of p
recompact open sets — and build $(G_n)$ inductively so that $\overline{G_n}\subs
et G_{n+1}$ and the $G_n$'s cover $X$.) Set $K_n:=\overline{G_n}$. Then each $K_
n$ is compact, $K_n\subset G_{n+1}\subset\operatorname{int}K_{n+1}$, and $\bigcu
p_n K_n\supset\bigcup_n G_n=X$.

Because $\mu$ is Radon, it is finite on compact sets, so $\mu(K_n)<\infty$ for e
very $n$.

**Step 2. An open set of positive finite measure.**
Since $\mu\neq 0$ and $\mu$ is Radon (inner regular on Borel sets), there exists
 a compact set $C\subset X$ with $\mu(C)>0$. Cover $C$ by finitely many precompa
ct open neighborhoods $W_{x_1},\dots,W_{x_m}$ (one around each point of $C$, usi
ng local compactness; extract a finite subcover by compactness of $C$). Set
$$V:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m} W_{x_i}.$$
Then $V$ is open, $\overline{V}\subset\bigcup_i\overline{W_{x_i}}$ is compact (f
inite union of compact sets), hence $\mu(V)\le\mu(\overline{V})<\infty$, and $\m
u(V)\ge\mu(C)>0$. Thus
$$0<\mu(V)<\infty,\qquad V\text{ open, }\overline{V}\text{ compact}.$$

**Step 3. The desired sequence.**
Define, for $n\ge 1$,
$$U_n:=\operatorname{int}K_{n+1}\;\cup\; V \;=\; G_{n+1}\;\cup\;V.$$
(We used $G_{n+1}=\operatorname{int}K_{n+1}$, which holds because $K_n\subset\op
eratorname{int}K_{n+1}=G_{n+1}$ gives $G_{n+1}\subset\operatorname{int}K_{n+1}\s
ubset K_{n+1}$... more directly, since $\overline{G_{n+1}}=K_{n+1}$ and $G_{n+1}
$ is open with $G_{n+1}\subset\operatorname{int}\overline{G_{n+1}}$; in fact $G_
{n+1}\subset\operatorname{int}K_{n+1}$, and we may simply use $G_{n+1}$ in place
 of $\operatorname{int}K_{n+1}$.)

Let me use the cleaner definition $U_n:=G_{n+1}\cup V$. Then:

- **Open:** $G_{n+1}$ and $V$ are open, so $U_n$ is open.
- **Increasing:** $G_{n+1}\subset G_{n+2}$ (since $\overline{G_{n+1}}\subset G_{
n+2}$), hence $U_n=G_{n+1}\cup V\subset G_{n+2}\cup V=U_{n+1}$.
- **Finite measure:** $U_n\subset K_{n+1}\cup\overline{V}$, which is compact (un
ion of two compact sets), so $\mu(U_n)\le\mu(K_{n+1}\cup\overline{V})<\infty$.
- **Positive measure:** $V\subset U_n$, so $\mu(U_n)\ge\mu(V)>0$.
- **Covering:** $\bigcup_{n\ge 1}U_n\supset\bigcup_{n\ge 1}G_{n+1}=X$.

Therefore $(U_n)_{n\ge 1}$ is an increasing sequence of open subsets of $X$ with
 $0<\mu(U_n)<\infty$ for every $n$ and $\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}U_n=X$.

**Remark on the zero-measure edge case.** If "positive Radon measure" were inter
preted to include the identically zero measure, the statement would be trivially
 false (no set can satisfy $0<\mu(U_n)$). The condition $0<\mu(U_n)<\infty$ in t
he statement itself forces $\mu$ to be non-zero, so "positive" is naturally read
 as "non-trivially positive" (a non-negative, non-zero Radon measure). Under thi
s reading the statement is **true**, as constructed above. $\blacksquare$
