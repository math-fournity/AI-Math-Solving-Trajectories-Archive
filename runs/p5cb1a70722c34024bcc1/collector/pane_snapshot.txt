## Laplace's Equation in a Circular Annulus

**Setup.** In polar coordinates, Laplace's equation on the annulus $\Omega=\{(r,
\theta):a<r<b\}$ reads

$$\nabla^2 u = u_{rr}+\frac{1}{r}u_r+\frac{1}{r^2}u_{\theta\theta}=0,\qquad u\te
xt{ }2\pi\text{-periodic in }\theta.$$

**Separation of variables.** With $u=R(r)\Theta(\theta)$, periodicity forces $\T
heta''+n^2\Theta=0$ for $n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}$, giving $\cos n\theta,\sin n\the
ta$ ($n\ge1$) and a constant ($n=0$). The radial equation is the Euler equation
$r^2R''+rR'-n^2R=0$, whose independent solutions are $\{1,\ln r\}$ for $n=0$ and
 $\{r^n,r^{-n}\}$ for $n\ge1$. Hence every regular harmonic function on the annu
lus has the form

$$u(r,\theta)=A_0+B_0\ln r+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl[(A_n r^n+B_n r^{-n})\cos n\t
heta+(C_n r^n+D_n r^{-n})\sin n\theta\bigr].\tag{1}$$

The radial derivative is

$$u_r(r,\theta)=\frac{B_0}{r}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\bigl[(A_n r^{n-1}-B_n r^{-n-1
})\cos n\theta+(C_n r^{n-1}-D_n r^{-n-1})\sin n\theta\bigr].\tag{2}$$

**Fourier notation.** For a $2\pi$-periodic boundary datum $h(\theta)$ write

$$h(\theta)=\tfrac12 c_0(h)+\sum_{n\ge1}\bigl[c_n(h)\cos n\theta+s_n(h)\sin n\th
eta\bigr],\quad c_n(h)=\tfrac1\pi\!\int_0^{2\pi}\!h\cos n\theta\,d\theta,\ s_n(h
)=\tfrac1\pi\!\int h\sin n\theta\,d\theta.$$

Note $\tfrac12 c_0(h)=\tfrac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}h\,d\theta$ is the mean. Uniquen
ess of Fourier coefficients lets us match modes term-by-term.

---

### (a) Dirichlet/Dirichlet: $u(a,\theta)=f(\theta),\ u(b,\theta)=g(\theta)$

Matching (1) at $r=a$ and $r=b$ mode by mode gives, for each mode, a $2\times2$
system.

**$n=0$ (mean):**
$$A_0+B_0\ln a=\tfrac12 c_0(f),\qquad A_0+B_0\ln b=\tfrac12 c_0(g),$$
so
$$B_0=\frac{c_0(g)-c_0(f)}{2\ln(b/a)},\qquad A_0=\tfrac12 c_0(f)-B_0\ln a.$$

**$n\ge1$, cosine modes** (replace $c_n\!\mapsto\! s_n$ for sine, with $C_n,D_n$
 in place of $A_n,B_n$):
$$A_n a^n+B_n a^{-n}=c_n(f),\qquad A_n b^n+B_n b^{-n}=c_n(g).$$
The determinant is $\Delta_n:=a^n b^{-n}-b^n a^{-n}=\dfrac{a^{2n}-b^{2n}}{(ab)^n
}\ne0$, so
$$A_n=\frac{c_n(f)\,b^{-n}-c_n(g)\,a^{-n}}{\Delta_n},\qquad B_n=\frac{c_n(g)\,a^
{n}-c_n(f)\,b^{n}}{\Delta_n},$$
and analogously
$$C_n=\frac{s_n(f)\,b^{-n}-s_n(g)\,a^{-n}}{\Delta_n},\qquad D_n=\frac{s_n(g)\,a^
{n}-s_n(f)\,b^{n}}{\Delta_n}.$$

**Solvability:** none — Dirichlet/Dirichlet always has a unique solution (the de
terminant $\Delta_n\ne0$ for $a<b$, and the $n=0$ system is solvable since $\ln(
b/a)\ne0$).

---

### (b) Neumann (homogeneous)/Dirichlet: $u_r(a,\theta)=0,\ u(b,\theta)=g(\theta
)$

Match (2) at $r=a$ to $0$ and (1) at $r=b$ to $g$.

**$n=0$:** $B_0/a=0\Rightarrow B_0=0$; then $A_0=\tfrac12 c_0(g)$.

**$n\ge1$, cosine:** $A_n a^{n-1}-B_n a^{-n-1}=0\Rightarrow B_n=A_n a^{2n}$, and
 $A_n b^n+B_n b^{-n}=c_n(g)$, i.e. $A_n\bigl(b^n+a^{2n}b^{-n}\bigr)=c_n(g)$. Sin
ce $b^n+a^{2n}b^{-n}=(b^{2n}+a^{2n})/b^n>0$,
$$A_n=\frac{b^n\,c_n(g)}{a^{2n}+b^{2n}},\qquad B_n=\frac{a^{2n}b^n\,c_n(g)}{a^{2
n}+b^{2n}},$$
and identically for the sine modes with $c_n(g)\mapsto s_n(g)$:
$$C_n=\frac{b^n\,s_n(g)}{a^{2n}+b^{2n}},\qquad D_n=\frac{a^{2n}b^n\,s_n(g)}{a^{2
n}+b^{2n}}.$$

**Solvability:** none. The homogeneous Neumann datum $0$ has zero mean, which is
 automatically compatible with $B_0=0$; every higher mode is uniquely determined
 because $a^{2n}+b^{2n}\ne0$. (A non-zero constant Neumann datum at $r=a$ would
*not* be compatible with a pure Dirichlet condition at $r=b$ unless that constan
t were zero, since the $n=0$ Neumann equation $B_0/a=\text{const}$ would force $
B_0\ne0$, while Dirichlet at $b$ only fixes $A_0+B_0\ln b$ — actually still solv
able; the genuine obstruction only appears for *two* Neumann conditions, as in p
art (c).)

---

### (c) Neumann/Neumann: $u_r(a,\theta)=f(\theta),\ u_r(b,\theta)=g(\theta)$

Match (2) at $r=a$ to $f$ and at $r=b$ to $g$.

**$n=0$ (mean mode):**
$$\frac{B_0}{a}=\tfrac12 c_0(f),\qquad \frac{B_0}{b}=\tfrac12 c_0(g).$$
These two equations for the single unknown $B_0$ are compatible iff
$$\boxed{\,a\,c_0(f)=b\,c_0(g)\,}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\int_0^{2\pi}f(\
theta)\,d\theta=b\int_0^{2\pi}g(\theta)\,d\theta.\tag{SC}$$

This is the **solvability condition**. When it holds, $B_0=\tfrac{a}{2}c_0(f)=\t
frac{b}{2}c_0(g)$, while $A_0$ is **undetermined** — reflecting the fact that a
harmonic function is defined only up to an additive constant under pure Neumann
data. (One fixes $A_0$ by an auxiliary normalization, e.g. $\int_0^{2\pi}u(a,\th
eta)\,d\theta=0$.)

**$n\ge1$, cosine:** the system
$$A_n a^{n-1}-B_n a^{-n-1}=\frac{c_n(f)}{n},\qquad A_n b^{n-1}-B_n b^{-n-1}=\fra
c{c_n(g)}{n}$$
has determinant
$$\widetilde\Delta_n:=a^{n-1}(-b^{-n-1})-b^{n-1}(-a^{-n-1})=b^{n-1}a^{-n-1}-a^{n
-1}b^{-n-1}=\frac{b^{2n}-a^{2n}}{(ab)^{n+1}}\ne0,$$
so
$$A_n=\frac{-\,c_n(f)\,b^{-n-1}+c_n(g)\,a^{-n-1}}{n\,\widetilde\Delta_n}=\frac{a
^{n+1}c_n(g)-b^{n+1}c_n(f)}{n\,(b^{2n}-a^{2n})}\,(ab)^{?}$$
More cleanly, multiplying numerator and denominator by $(ab)^{n+1}$:
$$A_n=\frac{a^{n+1}c_n(g)-b^{n+1}c_n(f)}{n\,(b^{2n}-a^{2n})},\qquad B_n=\frac{a^
{n-1}c_n(g)-b^{n-1}c_n(f)}{n\,\widetilde\Delta_n}\cdot(\text{sign})\;\Rightarrow
\; B_n=\frac{a^{n+1}b^{2n}c_n(g)-b^{n+1}a^{2n}c_n(f)}{n\,(b^{2n}-a^{2n})}.$$

Let me re-derive $B_n$ explicitly: $B_n=\dfrac{a^{n-1}\,\frac{c_n(g)}{n}-b^{n-1}
\,\frac{c_n(f)}{n}}{\widetilde\Delta_n}=\dfrac{a^{n-1}c_n(g)-b^{n-1}c_n(f)}{n\,\
widetilde\Delta_n}$. Multiplying by $(ab)^{n+1}/(ab)^{n+1}$ and using $\widetild
e\Delta_n\,(ab)^{n+1}=b^{2n}-a^{2n}$:
$$B_n=\frac{(ab)^{n+1}\bigl(a^{n-1}c_n(g)-b^{n-1}c_n(f)\bigr)}{n\,(b^{2n}-a^{2n}
)}=\frac{a^{2n}b^{n+1}c_n(g)-a^{n+1}b^{2n}c_n(f)}{n\,(b^{2n}-a^{2n})}.$$

The sine modes are identical with $c_n\!\mapsto\! s_n$ and $(A_n,B_n)\!\mapsto\!
(C_n,D_n)$:
$$C_n=\frac{a^{n+1}s_n(g)-b^{n+1}s_n(f)}{n\,(b^{2n}-a^{2n})},\qquad D_n=\frac{a^
{2n}b^{n+1}s_n(g)-a^{n+1}b^{2n}s_n(f)}{n\,(b^{2n}-a^{2n})}.$$

**Solvability condition (SC) and its physical meaning.** For a harmonic $u$, the
 divergence theorem gives $\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial u/\partial n\,ds=0$. On
 the annulus the outer normal is $+\hat r$ on $r=b$ (arc length $b\,d\theta$) an
d $-\hat r$ on $r=a$ (arc length $a\,d\theta$), so

$$\int_0^{2\pi}u_r(b,\theta)\,b\,d\theta-\int_0^{2\pi}u_r(a,\theta)\,a\,d\theta=
0 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; b\int_0^{2\pi}g\,d\theta=a\int_0^{2\pi}f\,d\theta,$$

which is exactly (SC). Physically: the net heat flux (or electric flux, or fluid
 mass flux) entering through the inner circle must equal the net flux leaving th
rough the outer circle; otherwise a steady-state harmonic solution cannot exist
(sources/sinks would accumulate in the annulus, violating $\nabla^2 u=0$). When
(SC) holds, the solution is unique up to the additive constant $A_0$, precisely
the kernel of the pure Neumann problem.

---

**Summary.**
- **(a)** Unique solution for arbitrary $f,g$; coefficients as above, no solvabi
lity condition.
- **(b)** Unique solution for arbitrary $g$ (the homogeneous Neumann datum $0$ i
s automatically mean-zero); coefficients as above, no solvability condition.
- **(c)** Solution exists **iff** $a\int f\,d\theta=b\int g\,d\theta$ (flux bala
nce); when it holds the solution is unique up to an additive constant, with coef
ficients as above.
