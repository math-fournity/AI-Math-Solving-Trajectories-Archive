## Proof

**Setup.** Let $f(x,y) = \mathbf{1}_{y>0} = H(y)$, the Heaviside function in $y$
, constant in $x$. Let $\phi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^2)$ with $\phi(0,0) \neq
0$, and let $V$ be a conic neighborhood of $\xi_0 = (0,1)$.

**Computing the Fourier transform.** We have
$$\widehat{\phi f}(\xi_1, \xi_2) = \int_0^\infty \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \phi(x,
y)\, e^{-i(x\xi_1 + y\xi_2)}\, dx\, dy = \int_0^\infty g(y;\xi_1)\, e^{-iy\xi_2}
\, dy,$$
where $g(y;\xi_1) = \int \phi(x,y)\, e^{-ix\xi_1}\, dx$ is the partial Fourier t
ransform of $\phi$ in $x$. Note $g(\cdot;\xi_1)$ is smooth and compactly support
ed for each $\xi_1$.

**Asymptotic expansion via integration by parts.** Since $g(y;\xi_1)\, H(y)$ has
 a jump discontinuity at $y=0$ of magnitude $g(0;\xi_1)$, repeated integration b
y parts gives:
$$\widehat{\phi f}(\xi_1, \xi_2) = \frac{g(0;\xi_1)}{i\xi_2} + \frac{g'(0;\xi_1)
}{(i\xi_2)^2} + \cdots + \frac{1}{(i\xi_2)^N}\int_0^\infty g^{(N)}(y;\xi_1)\, e^
{-iy\xi_2}\, dy.$$

The leading term is
$$\frac{g(0;\xi_1)}{i\xi_2} = \frac{1}{i\xi_2}\int \phi(x,0)\, e^{-ix\xi_1}\, dx
 = \frac{\widehat{\phi(\cdot,0)}(\xi_1)}{i\xi_2}.$$

Since $\phi(\cdot,0) \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ and $\phi(0,0)\neq 0$ (so $\phi
(\cdot,0)$ is not identically zero), its Fourier transform $\widehat{\phi(\cdot,
0)}(\xi_1)$ is a Schwartz function of $\xi_1$ that is **not identically zero**.
Being the Fourier transform of a compactly supported smooth function, it is enti
re (analytic), hence nonzero on an open dense set. In particular, there exists $
\delta > 0$ such that $|\widehat{\phi(\cdot,0)}(\xi_1)| \geq c_0 > 0$ for $|\xi_
1| \leq \delta$.

**Convergence for $s < 1/2$.** In the conic neighborhood $V$ of $(0,1)$, there e
xists $\epsilon > 0$ with $\xi_2 \geq \epsilon|\xi|$, so $|\xi_1| \leq C\xi_2$ a
nd $|\xi|^2 \asymp \xi_2^2$. Using the full asymptotic expansion, $|\widehat{\ph
i f}(\xi_1,\xi_2)| \leq C_N(1+|\xi_1|)^{-N}\xi_2^{-1}$ for any $N$. Then:
$$\int_V |\widehat{\phi f}|^2 (1+|\xi|^2)^s\, d\xi \lesssim \int_1^\infty \xi_2^
{2s-2}\!\!\int_{|\xi_1|\leq C\xi_2}\!\! (1+|\xi_1|)^{-2N}\, d\xi_1\, d\xi_2.$$
For $N > 1/2$, the inner integral is bounded by a constant, so the whole express
ion is $\lesssim \int_1^\infty \xi_2^{2s-2}\, d\xi_2$, which converges iff $2s -
 2 < -1$, i.e., **$s < 1/2$**.

**Divergence for $s \geq 1/2$.** For any $\phi$ with $\phi(0,0)\neq 0$ and any c
onic $V$ containing $(0,1)$: for $\xi_2$ sufficiently large, the slice $\{(\xi_1
,\xi_2)\in V\}$ contains $\xi_1 = 0$, hence contains $\{|\xi_1|\leq \delta\}$ fo
r $\xi_2$ large enough. On this region, $|\widehat{\phi f}(\xi_1,\xi_2)| \geq \f
rac{c_0}{2\xi_2}$ for $\xi_2$ large (the leading term dominates). Therefore:
$$\int_V |\widehat{\phi f}|^2 (1+|\xi|^2)^s\, d\xi \geq c \int_{R}^{\infty} \xi_
2^{2s-2}\!\!\int_{|\xi_1|\leq\delta}\!\! d\xi_1\, d\xi_2 = c'\int_R^\infty \xi_2
^{2s-2}\, d\xi_2 = \infty$$
when $2s - 2 \geq -1$, i.e., **$s \geq 1/2$**.

**Conclusion.** The integral $\int_V |\widehat{\phi f}(\xi)|^2(1+|\xi|^2)^s\, d\
xi$ is finite for $s < 1/2$ and infinite for $s \geq 1/2$, regardless of the cho
ice of $\phi$ (with $\phi(0,0)\neq 0$) and conic neighborhood $V$ of $(0,1)$. Th
us $(x_0,\xi_0)\notin WF^s(f)$ precisely for $s < 1/2$, and the critical thresho

$$\boxed{s = \dfrac{1}{2}}$$

This is consistent with the well-known fact that the Heaviside function $H(y)$ b
