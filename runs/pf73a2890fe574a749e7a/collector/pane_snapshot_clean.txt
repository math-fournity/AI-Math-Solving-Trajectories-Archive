## Proof

**Yes, $\dim \pi(K) < n$.**

**Step 1: $\dim K < n$.**

Since $X$ is an irreducible affine variety with $\dim X = n$, every proper close
d subset of $X$ has dimension strictly less than $n$. As $K \subsetneq X$ is a p
roper closed subset, we have
$$\dim K \le n - 1 < n.$$

**Step 2: Finite morphisms are closed, and restrict to finite morphisms on close
d subsets.**

A finite morphism is proper (it is integral, hence universally closed and of fin
ite type), so $\pi$ is a closed map. In particular $\pi(K)$ is closed in $\mathb
b{A}^n$. Moreover, the restriction of a finite morphism to a closed subset is ag
ain finite: if $K \hookrightarrow X$ is a closed immersion (which is finite) and
 $\pi: X \to \mathbb{A}^n$ is finite, then the composition $\pi|_K : K \to \math
bb{A}^n$ is finite. The induced map $K \to \pi(K)$ is therefore finite and surje
ctive.

**Step 3: Finite surjective morphisms preserve dimension.**

Let $K = K_1 \cup \cdots \cup K_m$ be the decomposition into irreducible compone
nts, so $\dim K = \max_i \dim K_i$. For each $i$, the restriction
$$\pi|_{K_i} : K_i \longrightarrow \pi(K_i)$$
is finite and surjective (the image $\pi(K_i)$ is closed since $\pi$ is a closed
 map, and $K_i \to \pi(K_i)$ is finite as argued above). At the level of coordin
ate rings, this corresponds to an integral extension
$$\overline{\pi(K_i)} \hookrightarrow \overline{K_i},$$
i.e. an integral ring extension. By the going-up theorem (integral extensions pr
eserve Krull dimension), we have
$$\dim K_i = \dim \pi(K_i) \quad \text{for each } i.$$

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Since $\pi(K) = \bigcup_{i=1}^m \pi(K_i)$ and each $\pi(K_i)$ is closed and irre
ducible (the image of an irreducible set is irreducible),
$$\dim \pi(K) = \max_{1 \le i \le m} \dim \pi(K_i) = \max_{1 \le i \le m} \dim K
_i = \dim K < n.$$

Therefore
$$\boxed{\dim \pi(K) < n.}$$
