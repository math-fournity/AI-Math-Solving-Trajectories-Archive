## Solution

**Claim.** The function $f(z) = e^{z}$ is entire and bounded on the imaginary ax
is.

**Proof.**

The exponential function $f(z)=e^{z}$ is entire: it is given by the everywhere-c
onvergent power series

$$
e^{z}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n!},
$$

which has infinite radius of convergence, so $f$ is holomorphic on all of $\math
bb{C}$.

Now consider the imaginary axis, i.e. points of the form $z=iy$ with $y\in\mathb
b{R}$. Using the defining property $e^{a+b}=e^{a}e^{b}$ together with $e^{iy}=\c
os y+i\sin y$ (which follows from the power series, comparing with those of $\co
s$ and $\sin$), we obtain

$$
f(iy)=e^{iy}=\cos y+i\sin y.
$$

Hence

$$
|f(iy)|=|e^{iy}|=\sqrt{\cos^{2}y+\sin^{2}y}=1
$$

for every $y\in\mathbb{R}$.

Therefore $|f(z)|=1$ for all $z$ on the imaginary axis, so $f$ is bounded (by $1
$) on the imaginary axis. $\blacksquare$
