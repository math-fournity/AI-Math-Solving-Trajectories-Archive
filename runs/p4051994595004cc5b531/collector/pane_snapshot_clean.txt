## Proof

Let $d = \dim V$ and let $\chi = \chi_V$ be the character of $V$. The multiplici
ty of the trivial representation $\mathbb{1}$ in $V^{\otimes n}$ is

$$m_n = \langle \chi^n, \mathbb{1} \rangle = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g \in G} \chi(g)
^n,$$

which is always a non-negative integer. We show that $m_n \ge 1$ for some positi
ve integer $n$.

**Setup.** Let $e$ be the exponent of $G$ (the lcm of the orders of all elements
). For any $g \in G$, every eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $\rho(g)$ acting on $V$ is a
 root of unity satisfying $\lambda^{\mathrm{ord}(g)} = 1$, hence $\lambda^e = 1$
.

Define
$$S = \{g \in G : \rho(g) \text{ is a scalar multiple of } \mathrm{Id}_V\}.$$
For $g \in S$, write $\rho(g) = \lambda_g \cdot \mathrm{Id}_V$, so $\chi(g) = d\
,\lambda_g$ where $\lambda_g$ is a root of unity with $\lambda_g^e = 1$. Note $e
 \in S$ (with $\lambda_e = 1$), so $|S| \ge 1$.

For $g \notin S$: $\chi(g) = \sum_{i=1}^d \lambda_i$ is a sum of $d$ roots of un
ity. By the equality case of the triangle inequality, $|\chi(g)| = d$ iff all $\
lambda_i$ are equal, i.e., iff $g \in S$. Therefore
$$d' := \max_{g \notin S} |\chi(g)| < d.$$

**Estimate.** Take $n = ke$ for a positive integer $k$.

- For $g \in S$: $\chi(g)^n = (d\,\lambda_g)^n = d^n \lambda_g^n = d^n(\lambda_g
^e)^k = d^n$.
- For $g \notin S$: $|\chi(g)^n| = |\chi(g)|^n \le (d')^n$.

Hence
$$m_n = \frac{1}{|G|}\left(|S|\,d^n + \sum_{g \notin S}\chi(g)^n\right) \ge \fra
c{1}{|G|}\left(|S|\,d^n - (|G|-|S|)(d')^n\right).$$

Since $|S| \ge 1$ and $d > d'$, the term $d^n$ dominates $(d')^n$ as $n \to \inf
ty$, so the right-hand side tends to $+\infty$. In particular, for all sufficien
tly large $k$,

$$m_{ke} \ge 1.$$

Since $m_{ke}$ is a non-negative integer, this means $\mathbb{1} \le V^{\otimes
ke}$.

**Conclusion.** Yes — for every finite group $G$ and every irreducible complex r
epresentation $V$, there exists a positive integer $n$ (for instance, $n = ke$ f
or sufficiently large $k$, where $e$ is the exponent of $G$) such that $\mathbb{
1} \le V^{\otimes n}$.
