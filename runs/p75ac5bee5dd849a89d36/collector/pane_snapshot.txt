## Proof

**Notation.** Let $g(x) = (x-a)^2$. We seek continuous $f$ with $f \circ f = g$.

### Step 1. Range constraint

Since $\operatorname{range}(g) = [0,\infty)$ and $\operatorname{range}(f\circ f)
 = f(\operatorname{range}(f)) \subseteq \operatorname{range}(f)$, we get $[0,\in
fty) \subseteq \operatorname{range}(f)$.

### Step 2. Local-injectivity analysis

A continuous $h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ is *locally injective at* $x_0$ if $h$
is injective on some neighborhood of $x_0$ (equivalently, strictly monotone near
 $x_0$).

**Claim:** For continuous $f$, the set of points where $f\circ f$ is **not** loc
ally injective equals $S \cup f^{-1}(S)$, where $S = \{x : f \text{ is not local
ly injective at } x\}$.

*Proof of claim.* Three lemmas:

- If $f$ is not locally injective at $x_0$: for every $\delta>0$ there exist $u\
neq v$ near $x_0$ with $f(u)=f(v)$, hence $f(f(u))=f(f(v))$. So $f\circ f$ is no
t locally injective at $x_0$.

- If $f$ is locally injective at $x_0$ but not at $f(x_0)$: $f$ is strictly mono
tone near $x_0$, mapping a neighborhood $U$ of $x_0$ bijectively onto an interva
l $I$ containing $f(x_0)$. Since $f$ is not locally injective at $f(x_0)$, for p
oints $p\neq q$ near $f(x_0)$ (inside $I$) with $f(p)=f(q)$, their preimages $u\
neq v$ in $U$ satisfy $f(f(u))=f(f(v))$. So $f\circ f$ is not locally injective
at $x_0$.

- If $f$ is locally injective at both $x_0$ and $f(x_0)$: $f$ is strictly monoto
ne near $x_0$ and near $f(x_0)$; by continuity the image of a small neighborhood
 of $x_0$ lies where $f$ is strictly monotone, so $f\circ f$ is strictly monoton
e near $x_0$. ∎

### Step 3. Determining $S$

For $g(x)=(x-a)^2$, the unique point where $g$ is not locally injective is $x=a$
 (strict local minimum; strictly monotone on each side). So:

$$S \cup f^{-1}(S) = \{a\}.$$

This gives $S \subseteq \{a\}$ and $f^{-1}(S) \subseteq \{a\}$.

**$S\neq\emptyset$:** If $S=\emptyset$, then $f$ is locally injective everywhere
, hence strictly monotone on $\mathbb{R}$ (a continuous, everywhere locally inje
ctive function on $\mathbb{R}$ is strictly monotone). Then $f\circ f$ is strictl
y monotone — but $g$ is not. Contradiction.

Therefore $S = \{a\}$, and $f^{-1}(\{a\}) \subseteq \{a\}$, meaning: **the only
possible preimage of $a$ under $f$ is $a$ itself.**

### Step 4. Ruling out $a > 0$

For $a > 0$: by Step 1, $[0,\infty)\subseteq\operatorname{range}(f)$, so $a \in
\operatorname{range}(f)$. There exists $x$ with $f(x)=a$; by $f^{-1}(\{a\})\subs
eteq\{a\}$, we get $x=a$, i.e., $f(a)=a$. Then:

$$0 = g(a) = f(f(a)) = f(a) = a > 0,$$

a contradiction. **No continuous $f$ exists for $a>0$.**

### Step 5. Construction for $a = 0$

Define $f(x) = |x|^{\sqrt{2}}$.

- **Continuity:** $|x|^\alpha$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$ for $\alpha > 0$; h
ere $\alpha = \sqrt{2} > 0$. ✓
- **Functional equation:** $f(f(x)) = \bigl|f(x)\bigr|^{\sqrt{2}} = \bigl(|x|^{\
sqrt{2}}\bigr)^{\sqrt{2}} = |x|^{2} = x^{2} = (x-0)^{2}$. ✓

So for $a=0$ a continuous solution exists.

### Conclusion

The only value is $a = 0$.

$$\boxed{a = 0}$$
