Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum area of a square whose four
vertices (as complex numbers) are exactly the roots of a monic quartic with inte
ger coefficients.

## Setup

A square with center $c_0 \in \mathbb{C}$ and half-diagonal vector $w \in \mathb
b{C}\setminus\{0\}$ has vertices:
$$z_1 = c_0 + w,\quad z_2 = c_0 + iw,\quad z_3 = c_0 - w,\quad z_4 = c_0 - iw.$$

The side length is $|z_1 - z_2| = |w - iw| = |w|\,|1-i| = |w|\sqrt{2}$, so the *
*area** is $2|w|^2$.

## The polynomial

The four values $w, iw, -w, -iw$ are precisely the four fourth-roots of $w^4$. S
etting $y = x - c_0$, the monic polynomial with these roots is:
$$\prod_{k=0}^{3}(y - i^k w) = y^4 - w^4.$$

So the polynomial is:
$$(x - c_0)^4 - w^4 = x^4 - 4c_0\, x^3 + 6c_0^2\, x^2 - 4c_0^3\, x + (c_0^4 - w^
4).$$

## Integer coefficient constraints

By Vieta's formulas, we need:
- $p = -4c_0 \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $q = 6c_0^2 \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $r = -4c_0^3 \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $s = c_0^4 - w^4 \in \mathbb{Z}$

From $p = -4c_0 \in \mathbb{Z}$: write $c_0 = -p/4$ with $p \in \mathbb{Z}$.

From $q = 6c_0^2 = 6p^2/16 = 3p^2/8 \in \mathbb{Z}$: since $\gcd(3,8)=1$, we nee
d $8 \mid p^2$, hence $4 \mid p$.

Set $p = 4m$, $m \in \mathbb{Z}$, so $c_0 = -m \in \mathbb{Z}$. Then automatical
ly:
- $q = 6m^2 \in \mathbb{Z}$ ✓
- $r = 4m^3 \in \mathbb{Z}$ ✓

The final constraint is $s = m^4 - w^4 \in \mathbb{Z}$, i.e., **$w^4 \in \mathbb
{Z}$**.

## Minimizing the area

The area is $2|w|^2$. Since $w^4 \in \mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$ (nonzero, else th
e square is degenerate), we have:
$$|w|^4 = |w^4| \geq 1 \implies |w|^2 \geq 1 \implies \text{area} = 2|w|^2 \geq
2.$$

## Achieving the minimum

Take $w^4 = 1$, so $w = 1$ (a fourth-root of unity), giving $|w| = 1$ and area $
= 2$.

With $m = 0$ (i.e., $c_0 = 0$), the polynomial is:
$$x^4 - 1 = 0,$$
with roots $1, i, -1, -i$ — the four vertices of a square of side $\sqrt{2}$ and
 area $2$. All coefficients $(p,q,r,s) = (0,0,0,-1)$ are integers. ✓

More generally, for any $m \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $w^4 = 1$:
$$(x+m)^4 - 1 = x^4 + 4mx^3 + 6m^2x^2 + 4m^3x + (m^4-1)$$
has all integer coefficients and roots forming a square of area $2$.

## Conclusion

The minimum square area is $\boxed{2}$.
