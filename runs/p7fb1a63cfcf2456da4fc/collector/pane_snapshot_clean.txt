The hypothesis $K[X]/I \cong K[X]/J$ **as $K$-algebras** yields, in particular,
a $K$-vector space isomorphism (a $K$-algebra isomorphism fixes $K$ and is $K$-l
inear). Therefore
$$\dim_K K[X]/I \;=\; \dim_K K[X]/J. \qquad (\ast)$$

**Case 1: $I = (0)$.** Then $K[X]/I \cong K[X]$, which is infinite-dimensional o
ver $K$. By $(\ast)$, $K[X]/J$ is infinite-dimensional over $K$, which forces $J
 = (0)$ (for any nonzero $g$, the quotient $K[X]/(g)$ has finite dimension $\deg
 g$). Hence $I = J = (0)$.

**Case 2: $I \neq (0)$, so $f \neq 0$.** Then $\dim_K K[X]/(f) = \deg f$, since
the residue classes of $1, X, \dots, X^{\deg f - 1}$ form a $K$-basis (by the di

\ast)$ because $\deg f < \infty$. Thus $J \neq (0)$, so $g \neq 0$, and likewise
 $\dim_K K[X]/(g) = \deg g$. By $(\ast)$,
$$\deg f = \deg g.$$

The inclusion $I \subseteq J$ reads $(f) \subseteq (g)$, i.e. $g \mid f$. So $f

$$\deg f = \deg g + \deg h.$$

it. Therefore $(f) = (g)$, that is, $I = J$.
