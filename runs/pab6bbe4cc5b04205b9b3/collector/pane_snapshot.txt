## Proof

The answer is **no**: pointwise (strong) convergence of the net $(T_\alpha)_{\al
pha \in \mathcal{A}}$ does **not** imply convergence in the norm (operator) topo
logy. We provide an explicit counterexample.

### Setup

Let $I$ be an uncountable set. Take $E = F = \ell^2(I)$, the Hilbert space of sq
uare-summable families $(x_i)_{i \in I}$ with $\|x\|^2 = \sum_{i \in I} |x_i|^2$
. Note $E$ is a normed space and $F$ is a Banach space (indeed a Hilbert space).
 Let $(e_i)_{i \in I}$ denote the standard orthonormal basis.

For each finite subset $\alpha \in \mathcal{A} = \operatorname{Fin}(I)$, define
$T_\alpha := P_\alpha$, the orthogonal projection onto the finite-dimensional su
bspace $\operatorname{span}\{e_i : i \in \alpha\}$:

$$(P_\alpha x)_i = \begin{cases} x_i & \text{if } i \in \alpha, \\ 0 & \text{if
} i \notin \alpha. \end{cases}$$

Each $P_\alpha$ is a bounded linear operator with $\|P_\alpha\| = 1$ (for nonemp
ty $\alpha$; for $\alpha = \emptyset$, $P_\alpha = 0$).

### Claim 1: $P_\alpha x \to x$ for every $x \in \ell^2(I)$ (strong convergence
to $T = I$)

Fix $x \in \ell^2(I)$ and $\varepsilon > 0$. The support $S := \{i \in I : x_i \
neq 0\}$ is at most countable (a standard fact for $\ell^2$). Since $\sum_{i \in
 S} |x_i|^2 < \infty$, there exists a finite set $\alpha_0 \subseteq S$ such tha
t

$$\sum_{i \in S \setminus \alpha_0} |x_i|^2 < \varepsilon^2.$$

Now for any $\alpha \in \mathcal{A}$ with $\alpha \supseteq \alpha_0$ (i.e., $\a
lpha \geq \alpha_0$ in the inclusion order):

$$\|P_\alpha x - x\|^2 = \sum_{i \notin \alpha} |x_i|^2 = \sum_{i \in S \setminu
s \alpha} |x_i|^2 \leq \sum_{i \in S \setminus \alpha_0} |x_i|^2 < \varepsilon^2
.$$

Hence $\|P_\alpha x - x\| < \varepsilon$ for all $\alpha \supseteq \alpha_0$. Th
is proves $P_\alpha x \to x = Ix$ for every $x \in E$, with the limit operator $
T = I$ (the identity).

### Claim 2: $P_\alpha \not\to I$ in the norm topology

For norm convergence we would need: for every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists $\
alpha_0 \in \mathcal{A}$ such that for all $\alpha \supseteq \alpha_0$, $\|P_\al
pha - I\| < \varepsilon$.

We show this fails for $\varepsilon = 1$. Fix any $\alpha_0 \in \mathcal{A}$; $\
alpha_0$ is a finite subset of $I$. Since $I$ is uncountable (in particular, inf
inite), there exists $j \in I \setminus \alpha_0$. Let $\alpha = \alpha_0 \cup \
{j\}$, so $\alpha \supseteq \alpha_0$. Then:

$$(P_\alpha - I)e_j = P_\alpha e_j - e_j = e_j - e_j = 0?$$

Wait — $j \in \alpha$, so $P_\alpha e_j = e_j$, giving $(P_\alpha - I)e_j = 0$.
That's not useful. Instead, pick $j \in I \setminus \alpha$ (possible since $\al
pha$ is finite and $I$ is infinite). Then $P_\alpha e_j = 0$, so $(I - P_\alpha)
e_j = e_j$, giving $\|I - P_\alpha\| \geq \|e_j\| = 1$.

More directly: for **any** finite $\alpha \subsetneq I$, pick any $j \in I \setm
inus \alpha$. Then $(I - P_\alpha)e_j = e_j$, so

$$\|I - P_\alpha\| \geq \|(I - P_\alpha)e_j\| = \|e_j\| = 1.$$

Since $I - P_\alpha$ is itself an orthogonal projection, $\|I - P_\alpha\| \leq
1$, hence $\|I - P_\alpha\| = 1$ for every finite $\alpha \subsetneq I$.

Therefore $\|P_\alpha - I\| = 1$ for all $\alpha \in \mathcal{A}$ with $\alpha \
neq I$ (which is all $\alpha$, since every element of $\mathcal{A}$ is finite an
d $I$ is uncountable). The net $(P_\alpha)$ stays at distance exactly $1$ from $
I$ and thus **cannot** converge to $I$ in norm.

### Conclusion

We have exhibited a net $(P_\alpha)_{\alpha \in \operatorname{Fin}(I)}$ of bound
ed linear operators on $\ell^2(I)$ (with $I$ uncountable) such that:
- $P_\alpha x \to Ix$ for every $x \in \ell^2(I)$ (strong/pointwise convergence)
, yet
- $\|P_\alpha - I\| = 1$ for all $\alpha$, so $(P_\alpha)$ does **not** converge
 to $I$ in the norm topology.

Hence pointwise convergence of the net does **not** imply norm convergence. $\bl
acksquare$
