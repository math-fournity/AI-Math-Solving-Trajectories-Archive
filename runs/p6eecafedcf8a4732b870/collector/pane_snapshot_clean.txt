## Proof

We connect the Galois group $S_n$ of the general degree-$n$ polynomial to solvab
ility by radicals via two pillars: (1) the Galois solvability criterion, and (2)
 the group-theoretic fact that $S_n$ is solvable if and only if $n \le 4$.

### 1. The Galois Solvability Criterion

**Theorem (Galois).** Let $f \in F[x]$ with $\operatorname{char} F = 0$, and let
 $G = \operatorname{Gal}(f/F)$ be its Galois group (the Galois group of its spli
tting field over $F$). Then $f$ is solvable by radicals if and only if $G$ is a
**solvable group**.

*Why this holds.*

($\Rightarrow$) If $f$ is solvable by radicals, each root lies in a radical exte
nsion $E/F$, i.e., a tower
$$F = E_0 \subseteq E_1 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq E_m = E, \qquad E_i = E_{i-1}
(\alpha_i),\ \ \alpha_i^{p_i} \in E_{i-1}.$$
Adjoining a primitive $p_i$-th root of unity makes each step cyclic (Kummer theo
ry). After passing to the normal closure, the Galois group of the resulting exte
nsion is built up as successive quotients of cyclic groups — hence is solvable.
The splitting field of $f$ is a subfield, so $G$ is a quotient of a solvable gro
up, hence solvable.

($\Leftarrow$) If $G$ is solvable with composition series
$$G = G_0 \triangleright G_1 \triangleright \cdots \triangleright G_r = \{e\}, \
qquad G_{i-1}/G_i \text{ cyclic of prime order},$$
the Galois correspondence gives a tower of fields where each step has cyclic Gal
ois group of prime order. After adjoining the relevant roots of unity (a radical
 step itself), each cyclic extension is a Kummer extension, hence radical. Thus
the splitting field sits inside a radical extension, so $f$ is solvable by radic
als. $\square$

### 2. Solvability of $S_n$

**Claim.** $S_n$ is solvable $\iff$ $n \le 4$.

*Small cases.*

- **$n = 1$:** $S_1 = \{e\}$, trivially solvable.
- **$n = 2$:** $S_2 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$, abelian, solvable.
- **$n = 3$:** The derived subgroup is $[S_3, S_3] = A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3$, wh
ich is abelian. The derived series $S_3 \triangleright A_3 \triangleright \{e\}$
 terminates, so $S_3$ is solvable.
- **$n = 4$:** The derived series is
$$S_4 \triangleright A_4 \triangleright V_4 \triangleright \{e\},$$
  where $V_4 = \{e,\,(12)(34),\,(13)(24),\,(14)(23)\}$ is the Klein four-group.
Each quotient is abelian: $S_4/A_4 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$, $A_4/V_4 \cong \mathbb{Z
}/3$, $V_4 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2$. So $S_4$ is solvable.

*Large case ($n \ge 5$).*

For $n \ge 5$, the alternating group $A_n$ is **simple and non-abelian** (a clas
sical result: the conjugacy class of a 3-cycle in $A_n$ generates $A_n$, and any
 nontrivial normal subgroup must contain all 3-cycles).

Now suppose for contradiction that $S_n$ were solvable. Then every subgroup of a
 solvable group is solvable, so $A_n \le S_n$ would be solvable. But $A_n$ is si
mple and non-abelian. A solvable simple group must be cyclic of prime order (hen
ce abelian): indeed, a nontrivial solvable group has a nontrivial abelian normal
 subgroup (the last nontrivial term in its derived series), which for a simple g
roup must be the whole group — forcing the group to be abelian. Since $A_n$ is n
on-abelian, it cannot be solvable. Contradiction.

Therefore $S_n$ is **not solvable** for $n \ge 5$. $\square$

### 3. Conclusion

By hypothesis, the Galois group of the general polynomial of degree $n$ is $S_n$
. Applying the Galois solvability criterion:

- **$n \le 4$:** $S_n$ is solvable $\Rightarrow$ the general degree-$n$ polynomi
al **is solvable by radicals**. This recovers the classical solution formulas: t
he linear formula ($n=1$), the quadratic formula ($n=2$), Cardano's formula for
cubics ($n=3$), and Ferrari's formula for quartics ($n=4$).

- **$n \ge 5$:** $S_n$ is not solvable $\Rightarrow$ the general degree-$n$ poly
nomial **is not solvable by radicals**. This is precisely the **Abel–Ruffini the
orem**: there exists no general formula in radicals for the roots of a polynomia
l of degree 5 or higher.

Thus the fact that the Galois group of the general degree-$n$ polynomial is $S_n
$ provides a complete group-theoretic explanation for the boundary between solva
ble and unsolvable-by-radicals polynomial equations: **solvability by radicals h
olds exactly for $n \le 4$ and fails for all $n \ge 5$**, with the transition go
verned by the non-solvability of $S_n$ (equivalently, the simplicity of $A_n$) a
t $n = 5$.
