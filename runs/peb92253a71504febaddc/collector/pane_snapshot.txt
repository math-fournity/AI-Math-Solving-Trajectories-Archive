## Proof

**Key Observation.** Any continuous function $Q:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}$ that is non-
decreasing (i.e., $Q'(x)\geq 0$) with $Q(0)=0$ and $Q(1)=1$ must satisfy $0 \leq
 Q(x) \leq 1$ for all $x\in[0,1]$. Indeed, $Q(0)=0$ and non-decreasing gives $Q(
x)\geq 0$; $Q(1)=1$ and non-decreasing gives $Q(x)\leq 1$. Therefore:

$$0 \;\leq\; \int_0^1 P(x)\,Q(x)\,dx \;\leq\; \int_0^1 P(x)\,dx = 1.$$

So the integral always lies in $[0,1]$. We will show that **every** value in the
 open interval $(0,1)$ is achievable, which immediately implies $0.7$ is achieva
ble.

**Two parametric families.** Define, for $a \geq 1$:

$$F_a(x) = x^a, \qquad G_a(x) = 1 - (1-x)^a.$$

Both are continuously differentiable on $[0,1]$, satisfy $F_a(0)=G_a(0)=0$, $F_a
(1)=G_a(1)=1$, and have non-negative derivatives:
- $F_a'(x) = a\,x^{a-1} \geq 0$,
- $G_a'(x) = a\,(1-x)^{a-1} \geq 0$.

So both families satisfy all four conditions (with condition 4 to be tuned).

**Family $F_a$ covers $(0,\, \tfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}]$.** Let

$$I(a) = \int_0^1 P(x)\,x^a\,dx = \mathbb{E}[X^a], \quad X\sim\mathrm{Beta}(\alp
ha,\beta).$$

- At $a=1$: $I(1) = \mathbb{E}[X] = \frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}$.
- As $a\to\infty$: $x^a \to 0$ for every $x\in[0,1)$ and $x^a=1$ only at $x=1$ (
measure zero). Since $0\leq x^a\leq 1$ and $P$ is integrable, by the Dominated C
onvergence Theorem, $I(a)\to 0$.
- $I(a)$ is continuous in $a$ (again by DCT, since $|x^a|\leq 1$).

By the Intermediate Value Theorem, $I$ attains every value in $\bigl(0,\;\tfrac{
\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}\bigr]$.

**Family $G_a$ covers $[\tfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta},\, 1)$.** Let

$$J(a) = \int_0^1 P(x)\,\bigl(1-(1-x)^a\bigr)\,dx = 1 - \mathbb{E}[(1-X)^a].$$

- At $a=1$: $J(1) = 1 - \mathbb{E}[1-X] = 1 - \frac{\beta}{\alpha+\beta} = \frac
{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}$.
- As $a\to\infty$: $(1-x)^a \to 0$ for every $x\in(0,1]$, so by DCT, $\mathbb{E}
[(1-X)^a]\to 0$, hence $J(a)\to 1$.
- $J(a)$ is continuous in $a$.

By the Intermediate Value Theorem, $J$ attains every value in $\bigl[\tfrac{\alp
ha}{\alpha+\beta},\;1\bigr)$.

**Union covers $(0,1)$.** The two ranges share the endpoint $\tfrac{\alpha}{\alp
ha+\beta}$, so together:

$$\bigl(0,\,\tfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}\bigr] \;\cup\; \bigl[\tfrac{\alpha}{\al
pha+\beta},\,1\bigr) \;=\; (0,\,1).$$

Since $0.7 \in (0,1)$, there exists $a\geq 1$ and a choice of family ($F_a$ or $
G_a$) such that the integral equals $0.7$.

**Explicit example (for $\alpha=\beta=1$, i.e., uniform distribution).** If $P(x

$$Q(x) = 1 - (1-x)^{7/3}.$$
