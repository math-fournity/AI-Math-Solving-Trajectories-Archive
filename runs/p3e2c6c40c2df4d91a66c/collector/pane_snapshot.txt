# Proof

**Setup.** Let $a = BC$, $b = CA$, $c = AB$. By the angle bisector theorem, $A'$
 on $BC$ satisfies
$$BA' = \frac{ac}{b+c}, \qquad A'C = \frac{ab}{b+c}.$$

**Coordinate computation for $A''$.** Place $B=(0,0)$, $C=(a,0)$. Then $A' = \bi
gl(\frac{ac}{b+c},\,0\bigr)$, and $A = (x_A,y_A)$ with
$$x_A = \frac{c^2+a^2-b^2}{2a}, \qquad x_A^2+y_A^2 = c^2.$$

Since $A''=(t,0)$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AA'$, we have $A''A^2 =
 A''A'^2$:
$$(t-x_A)^2+y_A^2 = \Bigl(t-\frac{ac}{b+c}\Bigr)^2.$$
Expanding and using $x_A^2+y_A^2=c^2$:
$$-2t\,x_A + c^2 = -\frac{2t\,ac}{b+c} + \frac{a^2c^2}{(b+c)^2},$$
$$2t\Bigl(\frac{ac}{b+c}-x_A\Bigr) = c^2\cdot\frac{a^2-(b+c)^2}{(b+c)^2}.$$

A direct expansion gives
$$\frac{ac}{b+c}-x_A = \frac{(c-b)(a-b-c)(a+b+c)}{2a(b+c)},$$
so (cancelling the non-zero factor $(a-b-c)(a+b+c)$):
$$t = \frac{ac^2}{(b+c)(c-b)} = \frac{ac^2}{c^2-b^2}.$$

Thus
$$BA'' = \frac{ac^2}{c^2-b^2}, \qquad A''C = a - BA'' = \frac{ab^2}{b^2-c^2},$$
and therefore
$$\frac{BA''}{A''C} = -\frac{c^2}{b^2}.$$

**Cyclic permutation.** Applying the same argument at vertices $B$ and $C$:
$$\frac{CB''}{B''A} = -\frac{a^2}{c^2}, \qquad \frac{AC''}{C''B} = -\frac{b^2}{a
^2}.$$

**Menelaus.** Multiplying the three signed ratios:
$$\frac{BA''}{A''C}\cdot\frac{CB''}{B''A}\cdot\frac{AC''}{C''B} = \Bigl(-\frac{c
^2}{b^2}\Bigr)\Bigl(-\frac{a^2}{c^2}\Bigr)\Bigl(-\frac{b^2}{a^2}\Bigr) = -1.$$

Since $A''\in BC$, $B''\in CA$, $C''\in AB$ (each possibly on an extension, whic
h is exactly the signed-ratio setting) and the product of signed ratios equals $
-1$, **Menelaus' theorem** implies that $A''$, $B''$, $C''$ are collinear. $\bla
cksquare$
