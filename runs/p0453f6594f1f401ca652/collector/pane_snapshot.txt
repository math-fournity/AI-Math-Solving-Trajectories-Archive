## Proof

We are given two **totally finite** measure spaces $(\Omega_1,\Sigma_1,\mu_1)$ a
nd $(\Omega_2,\Sigma_2,\mu_2)$, and a set $A\subseteq \Omega_1\times\Omega_2$ sa
tisfying the measurability conditions required for the slicing theorem — namely
$A\in\Sigma_1\otimes\Sigma_2$ (the product $\sigma$-algebra), so that every sect
ion
$$A_x=\{y\in\Omega_2:(x,y)\in A\},\qquad A^y=\{x\in\Omega_1:(x,y)\in A\}$$
is measurable ($A_x\in\Sigma_2$, $A^y\in\Sigma_1$), and the maps $x\mapsto\mu_2(
A_x)$, $y\mapsto\mu_1(A^y)$ are measurable.

Since both measure spaces are **totally finite**, they are in particular $\sigma
$-finite. Hence **Tonelli's theorem** applies to the non-negative measurable fun
ction $\mathbf{1}_A$:

$$
(\mu_1\times\mu_2)(A)=\int_{\Omega_1\times\Omega_2}\mathbf{1}_A\,d(\mu_1\times\m
u_2)
=\int_{\Omega_1}\!\left(\int_{\Omega_2}\mathbf{1}_A(x,y)\,d\mu_2(y)\right)d\mu_1
(x)
=\int_{\Omega_1}\mu_2(A_x)\,d\mu_1(x).
$$

By hypothesis the right-hand side equals $0$. Therefore

$$
(\mu_1\times\mu_2)(A)=\int_{\Omega_1}\mu_2(A_x)\,d\mu_1=0.
$$

(The second hypothesis $\int_{\Omega_2}\mu_1(A^y)\,d\mu_2=0$ is consistent and g
ives the same value, as Tonelli also yields $(\mu_1\times\mu_2)(A)=\int_{\Omega_
2}\mu_1(A^y)\,d\mu_2$.)

**Conclusion.** Yes. Under the stated measurability hypotheses and the total fin
iteness of both measures (which guarantees $\sigma$-finiteness, the standing ass
umption of Tonelli's theorem), the identity
$$(\mu_1\times\mu_2)(A)=\int_{\Omega_1}\mu_2(A_x)\,d\mu_1=\int_{\Omega_2}\mu_1(A
^y)\,d\mu_2$$
holds, so either iterated integral being $0$ forces $(\mu_1\times\mu_2)(A)=0$. I
n particular, the given condition that **both** iterated integrals vanish trivia
lly yields

$$\boxed{\,(\mu_1\times\mu_2)(A)=0\,}.$$
