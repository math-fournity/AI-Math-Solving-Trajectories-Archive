## Proof

We compute
$$I = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \phi(x,1)\,|x|^2\,dx, \qquad \phi(x,1)=(4\pi)^{-n/2}e^
{-|x|^2/4}.$$

**Step 1: Reduce to one-dimensional integrals.**

Write $|x|^2 = x_1^2 + \cdots + x_n^2$. By Fubini and the product structure of t
he Gaussian,

$$I = (4\pi)^{-n/2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} x_i^2\, e^{-(x_1^2+\cdots+x
_n^2)/4}\,dx_1\cdots dx_n.$$

For each $i$, the integrand factors: the $x_i$-part contributes $\int x_i^2 e^{-
x_i^2/4}\,dx_i$, and every $x_j$ with $j\neq i$ contributes $\int e^{-x_j^2/4}\,
dx_j$. By symmetry each summand is identical, so

$$I = n\,(4\pi)^{-n/2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}} t^2 e^{-t^2/4}\,dt\right)\left(\in
t_{\mathbb{R}} e^{-t^2/4}\,dt\right)^{n-1}.$$

**Step 2: Evaluate the two one-dimensional integrals.**

For $a>0$, the standard Gaussian identities give
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} e^{-at^2}\,dt = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}, \qquad \int_{\mathbb{R
}} t^2 e^{-at^2}\,dt = \frac{1}{2a}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}.$$

Setting $a = \tfrac{1}{4}$:

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} e^{-t^2/4}\,dt = \sqrt{4\pi} = 2\sqrt{\pi},$$

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} t^2 e^{-t^2/4}\,dt = \frac{1}{2\cdot(1/4)}\sqrt{4\pi} = 2\cd
ot 2\sqrt{\pi} = 4\sqrt{\pi}.$$

**Step 3: Substitute.**

$$I = n\,(4\pi)^{-n/2}\cdot 4\sqrt{\pi}\cdot(2\sqrt{\pi})^{n-1}.$$

Since $(4\pi)^{n/2} = 2^n\pi^{n/2}$, we have $(4\pi)^{-n/2} = 2^{-n}\pi^{-n/2}$,
 and $(2\sqrt{\pi})^{n-1} = 2^{n-1}\pi^{(n-1)/2}$. Therefore

$$I = n\cdot 2^{-n}\pi^{-n/2}\cdot 4\sqrt{\pi}\cdot 2^{n-1}\pi^{(n-1)/2}.$$

Collecting powers of $2$:
$$2^{-n}\cdot 2^2\cdot 2^{n-1} = 2^{-n+2+n-1} = 2^{1} = 2.$$

Collecting powers of $\pi$:
$$\pi^{-n/2}\cdot\pi^{1/2}\cdot\pi^{(n-1)/2} = \pi^{-n/2 + 1/2 + (n-1)/2} = \pi^
{0} = 1.$$

Hence

$$I = n\cdot 2\cdot 1 = 2n.$$

**Result.**

$$\boxed{\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\phi(x,1)\,|x|^2\,dx = 2n.}$$

This is consistent with the interpretation of $\phi(\cdot,1)$ as the heat kernel
 at $t=1$, which is a Gaussian of variance $2t=2$ in each coordinate; the expect
ed value of $|x|^2$ under an $n$-dimensional Gaussian of per-coordinate variance
 $\sigma^2=2$ is $n\sigma^2 = 2n$.
