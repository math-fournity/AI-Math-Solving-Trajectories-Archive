## Proof

Let $Q(x) = P(x)^2 - 1 = (P(x)-1)(P(x)+1)$, which has degree 200.

**Step 1: All roots of $Q$ are real.**

$P(x)-1$ has degree 100 with 65 distinct real roots whose multiplicities sum to
100 (the degree), so all roots of $P-1$ are real. Similarly, $P(x)+1$ has degree
 100 with 85 distinct real roots whose multiplicities sum to 100, so all roots o
f $P+1$ are real. Therefore $Q = (P-1)(P+1)$ has all 200 roots real — specifical
ly, 150 distinct real roots $r_1 < r_2 < \cdots < r_{150}$ with multiplicities s
umming to 200.

**Step 2: All roots of $P$ and $P'$ are real.**

Since $Q$ has all real roots, its derivative $Q' = 2PP'$ has all 199 roots real
(a standard result: the derivative of a polynomial with all real roots has all r
eal roots, by interlacing/Rolle's theorem).

If $P$ had a non-real root $z$, then $Q'(z) = 2P(z)P'(z) = 0$, making $z$ a non-
real root of $Q'$ — contradiction. So all 100 roots of $P$ are real. The same ar
gument applied to $P'$ (using $Q'(w) = 2P(w)P'(w) = 0$ when $P'(w)=0$) shows all
 99 roots of $P'$ are real.

**Step 3: $P$ has no multiple roots.**

Let $m_i$ be the multiplicity of $r_i$ in $Q$. Since all roots of $Q$ are real,
all roots of $Q'$ lie in $[r_1, r_{150}]$ (by interlacing: if $s_1 \le \cdots \l
e s_{200}$ are $Q$'s roots and $t_1 \le \cdots \le t_{199}$ are $Q'$'s roots, th
en $s_i \le t_i \le s_{i+1}$, so $t_1 \ge r_1$ and $t_{199} \le r_{150}$).

At each $r_i$ with $m_i \ge 2$, $Q'$ has $r_i$ as a root with multiplicity $m_i
- 1$. The total multiplicity at such points is $\sum(m_i - 1) = 200 - 150 = 50$.
 The remaining $199 - 50 = 149$ roots of $Q'$ lie strictly between consecutive d
istinct roots of $Q$ (one per gap by Rolle's theorem, and 149 gaps with exactly
149 roots means **exactly one simple root per gap**).

Now suppose for contradiction that $\alpha$ is a multiple root of $P$ with multi
plicity $m \ge 2$. Then:
- $P(\alpha) = 0$, so $Q(\alpha) = -1 \neq 0$, meaning $\alpha$ is **not** a roo
t of $Q$.
- Since $\alpha$ is a root of $Q'$ (as $Q'(\alpha) = 2P(\alpha)P'(\alpha) = 0$)
and all roots of $Q'$ lie in $[r_1, r_{150}]$ with none at the $r_i$'s (where $P
 = \pm 1 \neq 0$), we have $\alpha \in (r_i, r_{i+1})$ for some $i$.
- In this gap, $Q'$ has exactly one root, which is **simple** (multiplicity 1).
- But $\alpha$ is a root of $P$ with multiplicity $m$ and of $P'$ with multiplic
ity $m-1$, so its multiplicity in $Q' = 2PP'$ is $m + (m-1) = 2m - 1 \ge 3$.

This contradicts the simplicity of the gap root. Therefore $P$ has **no multiple
 roots**.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

$P$ has degree 100, all roots real (Step 2), and all roots simple (Step 3). Ther
efore $P$ has exactly **100 distinct real roots**. This holds for every polynomi
al satisfying the given conditions, so the minimal possible number of roots is:

$$\boxed{100}$$
