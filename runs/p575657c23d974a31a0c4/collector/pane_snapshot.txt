## Proof

**Claim:** No. For a finite-rank operator $T$ on an infinite-dimensional Banach
space $V$, the spectrum $\sigma(T)$ is a **finite** set, so $0$ is an isolated p
oint of the spectrum, not an accumulation point.

---

**Step 1. $T$ is compact, hence $0\in\sigma(T)$.**

Since $T$ has finite rank, it maps the closed unit ball $\overline{B_V}$ into th
e finite-dimensional subspace $R:=T(V)$. The set $T(\overline{B_V})$ is bounded
in $R$ (as $T$ is bounded), hence precompact in $R$ and therefore precompact in
$V$. So $T$ is a compact operator.

If $0\notin\sigma(T)$, then $T$ would be invertible with bounded inverse $T^{-1}
\in\mathcal{B}(V)$, and the identity $I = T^{-1}T$ would be compact (composition
 of a bounded and a compact operator). But in an infinite-dimensional Banach spa
ce the closed unit ball is not compact, so $I$ is not compact — contradiction. T
hus

$$0\in\sigma(T).$$

**Step 2. Every nonzero spectral value is an eigenvalue, and its eigenvectors li
e in $R=T(V)$.**

Let $\lambda\in\sigma(T)$, $\lambda\neq 0$. By the Riesz–Schauder theory for com
pact operators, every nonzero point of the spectrum is an eigenvalue. Concretely
: if $\lambda\neq 0$ is an eigenvalue with eigenvector $x$, then
$$x = \frac{1}{\lambda}Tx \in T(V) = R.$$
So every eigenvector associated to a nonzero eigenvalue lies in the finite-dimen
sional subspace $R$.

**Step 3. There are at most $\dim R = \operatorname{rank}(T)$ nonzero eigenvalue
s.**

The nonzero eigenvalues of $T$ are exactly the nonzero eigenvalues of the finite
-dimensional operator $T|_R : R\to R$ (every eigenvector for a nonzero eigenvalu
e already lies in $R$, and $R$ is $T$-invariant since $T(R)\subseteq R$). A line
ar operator on a finite-dimensional space of dimension $r=\operatorname{rank}(T)
$ has at most $r$ eigenvalues. Hence $T$ has at most $r$ nonzero eigenvalues.

**Step 4. The spectrum is finite.**

Combining Steps 1–3:
$$\sigma(T) \;=\; \{0\}\;\cup\;\{\text{nonzero eigenvalues of }T\},$$
and the second set has at most $\operatorname{rank}(T)<\infty$ elements. Therefo
re $\sigma(T)$ is a **finite** set.

**Step 5. $0$ is not an accumulation point.**

A finite subset of $\mathbb{C}$ has no accumulation points at all. In particular
, $0\in\sigma(T)$ but there exists $\varepsilon>0$ (e.g. smaller than the minimu
m modulus of the finitely many nonzero spectral values, or any $\varepsilon>0$ i
f there are no nonzero eigenvalues) such that
$$\sigma(T)\cap\bigl(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}\bigr)\cap B(0,\varepsilon)=\varnot
hing.$$
So $0$ is an **isolated** point of $\sigma(T)$, not an accumulation point.

---

**Conclusion.** The spectrum of a finite-rank operator on an infinite-dimensiona
l Banach space is the finite set $\{0\}\cup\{\text{finitely many nonzero eigenva
lues}\}$. Consequently $0$, although always belonging to the spectrum, is **not*
* an accumulation point of it.
