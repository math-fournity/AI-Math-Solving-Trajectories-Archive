## Proof

Let $w = b_1 b_2 \cdots b_k$ be a fixed binary string of length $k \geq 1$. For
$x \in (0,1]$, write its binary expansion $x = 0.d_1 d_2 d_3 \cdots$ (choosing t
he non-terminating expansion for dyadic rationals to make it unique; the set of
dyadic rationals is countable and hence has measure zero, so this convention doe
s not affect the measure-theoretic conclusion).

**Definition.** Say $w$ *occurs at position* $n$ if $d_n d_{n+1} \cdots d_{n+k-1
} = b_1 b_2 \cdots b_k$.

**Goal.** Show that the set
$$E = \{x \in (0,1] : w \text{ occurs at only finitely many positions}\}$$
has Lebesgue measure $0$.

---

### Step 1. Independence structure at non-overlapping positions

Consider the positions $n_j = 1 + (j-1)k$ for $j = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ These positi
ons are spaced $k$ apart, so the blocks
$$B_j = d_{n_j}\, d_{n_j+1} \cdots d_{n_j+k-1}$$
involve *disjoint* sets of binary digits of $x$.

Under the Lebesgue measure on $(0,1]$, the digits $d_1, d_2, d_3, \ldots$ are in
dependent and identically distributed Bernoulli($1/2$) random variables. (This i
s the standard fact that Lebesgue measure corresponds to fair coin flips.) There
fore the blocks $B_1, B_2, B_3, \ldots$ are independent, each uniformly distribu
ted over $\{0,1\}^k$.

Define the events $A_j = \{B_j = w\}$. Then:
- $\Pr(A_j) = 2^{-k}$ for every $j$,
- $A_1, A_2, A_3, \ldots$ are mutually independent.

### Step 2. Apply the second Borel–Cantelli lemma

Since $\sum_{j=1}^{\infty} \Pr(A_j) = \sum_{j=1}^{\infty} 2^{-k} = \infty$ and t
he events are independent, the **second Borel–Cantelli lemma** gives:

$$\Pr\!\left(\limsup_{j\to\infty} A_j\right) = 1,$$

i.e., with probability $1$, infinitely many of the $A_j$ occur. In other words,
for almost every $x \in (0,1]$, the string $w$ appears at infinitely many of the
 non-overlapping positions $n_j$.

### Step 3. Conclude the measure of $E$

If $w$ appears infinitely often among the positions $n_j$, then $w$ appears infi
nitely often in the full binary expansion. Therefore

$$\Pr(E) \;\leq\; \Pr\!\left(\text{$w$ appears only finitely often among } \{n_j
\}\right) \;=\; 0.$$

Since $E$ has non-negative measure, we conclude $m(E) = 0$.

---

### Remark on the dyadic-rational convention

The set of dyadic rationals in $(0,1]$ (numbers of the form $m/2^n$) is countabl
e, hence has measure $0$. Whatever convention is used for their (two possible) b
inary expansions, the measure of $E$ is unaffected.

---

**The measure of the set of points in $(0,1]$ for which a fixed binary string ap
pears only finitely many times in their binary expansion is $\boxed{0}$.**
