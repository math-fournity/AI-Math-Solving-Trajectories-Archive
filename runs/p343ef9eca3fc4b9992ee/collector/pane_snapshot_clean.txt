## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** When the domain is the one-dimensional space $\mathbb{R}$, Gâte
aux differentiability and Fréchet differentiability coincide.

Let $f:\mathbb{R}\to X$ be Gâteaux differentiable at a point $x\in\mathbb{R}$. B
y definition there exists a bounded linear map $A:\mathbb{R}\to X$ such that for
 every direction $h\in\mathbb{R}$,

$$\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{f(x+th)-f(x)}{t}=A(h),$$

where the limit is taken in the norm topology of $X$ (and $t\to 0$ two-sided, $t
\neq 0$).

**Step 1: Identify the derivative.** Take $h=1$ and set $v:=A(1)\in X$. Then

$$\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{f(x+t)-f(x)}{t}=v.$$

Since $A$ is linear and $\mathbb{R}$ is one-dimensional, every $h\in\mathbb{R}$
satisfies $A(h)=A(h\cdot 1)=h\,A(1)=h\,v$. Moreover $A$ is automatically bounded
 (continuous), since $\|A(h)\|=\|h\,v\|=|h|\,\|v\|$, so $\|A\|=\|v\|<\infty$.

**Step 2: Verify the Fréchet condition.** Fréchet differentiability at $x$ with
derivative $A$ requires

$$\lim_{\substack{h\to 0\\ h\neq 0}}\frac{\|f(x+h)-f(x)-A(h)\|}{\|h\|}=0.$$

Since the domain is $\mathbb{R}$, we have $\|h\|=|h|$ and $A(h)=h\,v$, so the co
ndition becomes

$$\lim_{\substack{h\to 0\\ h\neq 0}}\frac{\|f(x+h)-f(x)-h\,v\|}{|h|}=0.$$

But

$$\frac{\|f(x+h)-f(x)-h\,v\|}{|h|}=\left\|\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}-v\right\|,$$

and this tends to $0$ precisely by the limit established in Step 1 (which is two
-sided, covering both $h\to 0^{+}$ and $h\to 0^{-}$). Hence the Fréchet conditio
n holds.

**Why one-dimensionality is essential.** In dimensions $\geq 2$, Gâteaux differe
ntiability only requires the directional limit to hold *pointwise in each direct
ion* $h$, with no uniformity across directions as $t\to 0$. Fréchet differentiab
ility demands *uniform* convergence over the unit sphere of directions. That gap
 permits standard counterexamples in dimension $\geq 2$. Here, however, the unit
 sphere of $\mathbb{R}$ consists of just two points $\{\pm 1\}$, both covered by
 the single two-sided limit along $h=1$; there is no family of directions over w
hich uniformity could fail. Thus the pointwise Gâteaux condition *is* the Fréche
t condition.

Since $x\in\mathbb{R}$ was arbitrary, $f$ is Fréchet differentiable everywhere,
with $Df(x)=A$ at every point.
