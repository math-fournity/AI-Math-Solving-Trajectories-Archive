## Solution

We compute the length of the line segment $y = x$ inside the open unit disk $A =
 \{x^2 + y^2 < 1\}$ under the Poincaré metric
$$ds^2 = \frac{4\,dx^2 + 4\,dy^2}{(1 - x^2 - y^2)^2}.$$

### Parametrization

The line $y = x$ passes through the origin, so it is a diameter of the disk. We
parametrize it by
$$x = \frac{t}{\sqrt{2}}, \qquad y = \frac{t}{\sqrt{2}}, \qquad t \in (-1, 1).$$

The constraint $x^2 + y^2 < 1$ becomes $t^2 < 1$, confirming the parameter range
 $t \in (-1, 1)$. The endpoints $t = \pm 1$ correspond to the boundary points $(
\pm 1/\sqrt{2}, \pm 1/\sqrt{2})$, which lie on the unit circle and are **not** i
n the open disk $A$.

### Computing the metric along the curve

Along the curve:
$$dx = \frac{dt}{\sqrt{2}}, \quad dy = \frac{dt}{\sqrt{2}} \implies dx^2 + dy^2
= dt^2,$$
$$x^2 + y^2 = t^2 \implies 1 - x^2 - y^2 = 1 - t^2.$$

Substituting into the metric:
$$ds^2 = \frac{4\,dt^2}{(1 - t^2)^2} \implies ds = \frac{2\,dt}{1 - t^2}.$$

### Length integral

The hyperbolic length is
$$L = \int_{-1}^{1} \frac{2\,dt}{1 - t^2} = 2\int_{-1}^{1} \frac{dt}{1 - t^2}.$$

Using the standard antiderivative
$$\int \frac{dt}{1 - t^2} = \operatorname{artanh}(t) = \frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{1+t}{
1-t},$$

we obtain
$$L = 2\Big[\operatorname{artanh}(t)\Big]_{-1}^{1}.$$

### Divergence

As $t \to 1^-$, $\operatorname{artanh}(t) = \frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{1+t}{1-t} \to +\
infty$.

As $t \to -1^+$, $\operatorname{artanh}(t) \to -\infty$.

Therefore
$$L = 2\Big(\lim_{t \to 1^-}\operatorname{artanh}(t) - \lim_{t \to -1^+}\operato
rname{artanh}(t)\Big) = 2(+\infty - (-\infty)) = +\infty.$$

### Geometric interpretation

The line $y = x$ passes through the origin, making it a **geodesic diameter** of
 the Poincaré disk. In hyperbolic geometry (curvature $-1$), the boundary circle
 is at infinite hyperbolic distance from any interior point. Since the segment $
y = x \cap A$ is an open geodesic whose endpoints approach the ideal boundary, i
ts hyperbolic length diverges.

### Result

$$\boxed{L = +\infty}$$

The length of the line segment $y = x$ within the unit disk under the Poincaré m
etric is **infinite**.
