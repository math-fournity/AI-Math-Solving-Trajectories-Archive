## Proof

Let $Z(t)$ be a standard Brownian motion with $Z(0) = 0$. We wish to compute
$$P\bigl(Z(3) > Z(2),\; Z(2) > 0\bigr).$$

**Step 1: Decompose using independent increments.**

By the defining properties of Brownian motion, the increments are independent an
d normally distributed. Set
$$X = Z(2), \qquad Y = Z(3) - Z(2).$$
Then:
- $X \sim N(0, 2)$,
- $Y \sim N(0, 1)$,
- $X$ and $Y$ are **independent** (increments over disjoint intervals $[0,2]$ an
d $(2,3]$).

Note that $Z(3) = Z(2) + \bigl(Z(3)-Z(2)\bigr) = X + Y$.

**Step 2: Rewrite the event.**

The event $\{Z(3) > Z(2),\; Z(2) > 0\}$ becomes
$$\{X + Y > X,\; X > 0\} = \{Y > 0,\; X > 0\}.$$

**Step 3: Factor using independence.**

Since $X$ and $Y$ are independent,
$$P(Y > 0,\; X > 0) = P(Y > 0)\cdot P(X > 0).$$

**Step 4: Evaluate each factor.**

Both $X \sim N(0,2)$ and $Y \sim N(0,1)$ are centered (mean-zero) normal random
variables, so each is symmetric about $0$:
$$P(X > 0) = \frac{1}{2}, \qquad P(Y > 0) = \frac{1}{2}.$$

**Step 5: Conclude.**

$$P\bigl(Z(3) > Z(2),\; Z(2) > 0\bigr) = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2} = \boxed{\f
rac{1}{4}}.$$
