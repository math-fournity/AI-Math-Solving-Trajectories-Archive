# Wave Equation on a Disk with Neumann Boundary Condition

## Setup: Eigenvalue Problem

The wave equation $u_{tt} = c^2 \nabla^2 u$ in polar coordinates on the disk $0
\le r \le a$:

$$u_{tt} = c^2\left(u_{rr} + \frac{1}{r}u_r + \frac{1}{r^2}u_{\theta\theta}\righ
t), \qquad u_r(a,\theta,t)=0,$$

with $u$ regular at $r=0$. Separate variables $u = R(r)\Theta(\theta)T(t)$:

- **Angular:** $\Theta'' + n^2\Theta = 0 \Rightarrow \Theta \in \{1,\;\cos n\the
ta,\;\sin n\theta\}$, $n = 0,1,2,\dots$
- **Radial (Bessel of order $n$):** $R'' + \frac{1}{r}R' + \left(\lambda^2 - \fr
ac{n^2}{r^2}\right)R = 0$. Regularity at $r=0$ excludes $Y_n$, so $R = J_n(\lamb
da r)$. The Neumann condition $R'(a)=0$ gives

$$J_n'(\lambda a) = 0.$$

Denote by $j'_{n,k}$ the $k$-th positive zero of $J_n'(x)$, $k=1,2,\dots$. Then
$\lambda_{n,k} = j'_{n,k}/a$. For $n=0$, since $J_0'(x)=-J_1(x)$, the zeros $j'_
{0,k}$ are the zeros $j_{1,k}$ of $J_1$. Additionally, $J_0'(0)=0$ yields the **
constant mode** $\lambda=0$ (only for $n=0$).

- **Temporal:** $T'' + c^2\lambda^2 T = 0$. For $\lambda > 0$: $T = A\cos(c\lamb
da t) + B\sin(c\lambda t)$. For $\lambda = 0$: $T = A + Bt$.

**Orthogonality / norms.** With weight $r$ on $[0,a]$, and using $J_n'(\lambda_{
n,k}a)=0$:

$$N_{n,k} := \int_0^a J_n^2(\lambda_{n,k} r)\,r\,dr = \frac{a^2}{2}\left(1 - \fr
ac{n^2}{j_{n,k}^{\prime\,2}}\right)J_n^2(j'_{n,k}).$$

For $n=0$: $N_{0,k} = \tfrac{a^2}{2}J_0^2(j_{1,k})$. The constant eigenfunction
$1$ has norm $\int_0^a r\,dr = a^2/2$, and $\int_0^a J_0(\lambda_{0,k}r)\,r\,dr
= \frac{a}{\lambda}J_1(\lambda a)=0$, confirming orthogonality to the constant m
ode.

## (a) $u(r,\theta,0)=0$, $\;u_t(r,\theta,0)=\beta(r)\cos 5\theta$

The initial velocity is purely $\cos 5\theta$ in angle, so only the $n=5$ cosine
-mode is excited. The condition $u(r,\theta,0)=0$ forces all cosine-in-time coef
ficients to vanish. Thus

$$\boxed{u(r,\theta,t) = \cos 5\theta\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} B_{5,k}\,\sin(c\lambda_
{5,k}t)\,J_5(\lambda_{5,k}r),}$$

where $\lambda_{5,k}=j'_{5,k}/a$ and

$$B_{5,k} = \frac{1}{c\,\lambda_{5,k}}\cdot\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^a \beta(r)\
,J_5(\lambda_{5,k}r)\,r\,dr}{\displaystyle N_{5,k}} = \frac{\displaystyle\int_0^
a \beta(r)\,J_5(\lambda_{5,k}r)\,r\,dr}{\,c\,\lambda_{5,k}\,N_{5,k}\,}.$$

*Verification.* At $t=0$: $u=0$ ✓. Differentiating: $u_t(r,\theta,0)=\cos 5\thet
a\sum_k c\lambda_{5,k}B_{5,k}J_5(\lambda_{5,k}r) = \cos 5\theta\,\beta(r)$ by th
e Fourier–Bessel expansion of $\beta$ in $\{J_5(\lambda_{5,k}r)\}$ (complete ort
hogonal system under Neumann BC). ✓

## (b) $u(r,\theta,0)=0$, $\;u_t(r,\theta,0)=\beta(r)$ (radially symmetric)

Only $n=0$ is excited. The $n=0$ spectrum includes the constant mode ($\lambda=0
$, giving a linear-in-$t$ term) plus $\{J_0(\lambda_{0,k}r)\}_{k\ge 1}$ with $\l
ambda_{0,k}=j_{1,k}/a$.

$$\boxed{u(r,t) = B_{0,0}\,t \;+\; \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} B_{0,k}\,\sin(c\lambda_{0
,k}t)\,J_0(\lambda_{0,k}r),}$$

with

$$B_{0,0} = \frac{\displaystyle\int_0^a \beta(r)\,r\,dr}{\displaystyle\int_0^a r
\,dr} = \frac{2}{a^2}\int_0^a \beta(r)\,r\,dr, \qquad B_{0,k} = \frac{\displayst
yle\int_0^a \beta(r)\,J_0(\lambda_{0,k}r)\,r\,dr}{\,c\,\lambda_{0,k}\,N_{0,k}\,}
.$$

*Verification.* $u(r,0)=0$ ✓. $u_t(r,0) = B_{0,0} + \sum_k c\lambda_{0,k}B_{0,k}
J_0(\lambda_{0,k}r) = \beta(r)$ by the completeness of $\{1, J_0(\lambda_{0,k}r)
\}$ under the Neumann Sturm–Liouville problem. ✓ The linear term $B_{0,0}t$ refl
ects the zero-frequency (constant) mode: with insulated boundary, a nonzero mean
 initial velocity produces secular growth.

## (c) $u(r,\theta,0)=\alpha(r,\theta)$, $\;u_t(r,\theta,0)=0$

The condition $u_t(r,\theta,0)=0$ forces all sine-in-time coefficients to vanish
; only cosine-in-time (and the constant) terms remain. Expand $\alpha$ in a Four
ier series in $\theta$:

$$\alpha(r,\theta) = \frac{a_0(r)}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl[a_n(r)\cos n\th
eta + b_n(r)\sin n\theta\bigr],$$

where $a_n(r)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\alpha(r,\phi)\cos n\phi\,d\phi$, $\;b_n
(r)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\alpha(r,\phi)\sin n\phi\,d\phi$.

Then expand each radial profile in the appropriate Bessel basis. The result:

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned}
u(r,\theta,t) =\;& A_{0,0} \;+\; \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} A_{0,k}\,\cos(c\lambda_{0,k
}t)\,J_0(\lambda_{0,k}r) \\
&+ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Bigl[A_{n,k}^{(c)}\cos n\theta + A_{n,
k}^{(s)}\sin n\theta\Bigr]\cos(c\lambda_{n,k}t)\,J_n(\lambda_{n,k}r),
\end{aligned}}$$

where $\lambda_{n,k}=j'_{n,k}/a$ and

$$A_{0,0} = \frac{1}{a^2}\int_0^a a_0(r)\,r\,dr, \qquad A_{0,k} = \frac{\display
style\int_0^a a_0(r)\,J_0(\lambda_{0,k}r)\,r\,dr}{\,2\,N_{0,k}\,},$$

$$A_{n,k}^{(c)} = \frac{\displaystyle\int_0^a a_n(r)\,J_n(\lambda_{n,k}r)\,r\,dr
}{\,N_{n,k}\,}, \qquad A_{n,k}^{(s)} = \frac{\displaystyle\int_0^a b_n(r)\,J_n(\
lambda_{n,k}r)\,r\,dr}{\,N_{n,k}\,}, \quad n\ge 1.$$

(The factor $1/2$ in $A_{0,k}$ comes from the $a_0(r)/2$ convention.) *Verificat
ion.* At $t=0$: cosines $=1$, reproducing the Fourier–Bessel expansion of $\alph
a$. Also $u_t(r,\theta,0)=0$ since every term's time-derivative vanishes at $t=0
$. ✓

## (d) $u(r,\theta,0)=0$, $\;u_t(r,\theta,0)=\beta(r,\theta)$

This is the general case combining (a) and (b). The condition $u(r,\theta,0)=0$
kills all cosine-in-time coefficients. Expand $\beta(r,\theta)$ in $\theta$:

$$\beta(r,\theta) = \frac{p_0(r)}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl[p_n(r)\cos n\the

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned}

0,k}t)\,J_0(\lambda_{0,k}r) \\

k}^{(s)}\sin n\theta\Bigr]\sin(c\lambda_{n,k}t)\,J_n(\lambda_{n,k}r),
\end{aligned}}$$

where $\lambda_{n,k}=j'_{n,k}/a$ and

$$B_{0,0} = \frac{1}{a^2}\int_0^a p_0(r)\,r\,dr, \qquad B_{0,k} = \frac{\display

*Verification.* $u(r,\theta,0)=0$ ✓. Differentiating in $t$ and setting $t=0$: t
