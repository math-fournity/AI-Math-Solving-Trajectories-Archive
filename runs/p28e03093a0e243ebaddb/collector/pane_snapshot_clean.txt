## Proof

**Setup.** $R = K[x_1,x_2,x_3]$ and $S = K[y_1,y_2]$ are standard graded polynom
ial rings ($r=3$, $s=2$). The Segre product is
$$\overline{R}\,\Theta_K\, S \;=\; \bigoplus_{n\ge 0}\, \overline{R}_n \otimes_K
 S_n,$$
a standard graded $K$-algebra whose Poincaré series is
$$P(t) \;=\; \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\, \dim_K \overline{R}_n \cdot \dim_K S_n\;\cdot
\, t^n.$$

---

**Step 1 — Hilbert series of $\overline{R}$.**

The polynomial $f = x_1^3+x_2^3+x_3^3$ is homogeneous of degree $3$ and is a non
-zero-divisor in the domain $R$. Hence
$$H_{\overline{R}}(t) \;=\; \frac{1-t^3}{(1-t)^3} \;=\; \frac{(1-t)(1+t+t^2)}{(1
-t)^3} \;=\; \frac{1+t+t^2}{(1-t)^2}.$$

---

**Step 2 — Graded dimensions of $\overline{R}$.**

Expanding $(1+t+t^2)\sum_{n\ge 0}(n+1)\,t^n$:

| $n$ | $\dim_K \overline{R}_n$ |
|-----|------------------------|
| $0$ | $1$ |
| $1$ | $2+1 = 3$ |
| $\ge 2$ | $(n+1)+n+(n-1) = 3n$ |

So $\dim_K \overline{R}_0 = 1$ and $\dim_K \overline{R}_n = 3n$ for $n \ge 1$.

*Check:* $\dim \overline{R}_3 = \binom{5}{2}-1 = 9 = 3\cdot 3$. ✓

---

**Step 3 — Graded dimensions of $S$.**

$H_S(t) = \frac{1}{(1-t)^2}$, so $\dim_K S_n = n+1$.

---

**Step 4 — Poincaré series of the Segre product.**

$$P(t) \;=\; \underbrace{1\cdot 1}_{n=0} \;+\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 3n\,(n+1)\;t^
n \;=\; 1 \;+\; 3\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n(n+1)\,t^n.$$

Using the standard identity
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n(n+1)\,t^n \;=\; \frac{t(1+t)}{(1-t)^3} + \frac{t}{(1-t)^
2} \;=\; \frac{t(1+t)+t(1-t)}{(1-t)^3} \;=\; \frac{2t}{(1-t)^3},$$

we obtain
$$P(t) \;=\; 1 + \frac{6t}{(1-t)^3} \;=\; \frac{(1-t)^3 + 6t}{(1-t)^3} \;=\; \bo
xed{\dfrac{1 + 3t + 3t^2 - t^3}{(1-t)^3}}.$$

---

**Verification.**

- **Dimension:** The pole at $t=1$ has order $3$, giving $\dim(\overline{R}\,\Th
eta_K\,S) = \dim\overline{R}+\dim S - 1 = 2+2-1 = 3$. ✓

- **Multiplicity (degree):** $h(1) = 1+3+3-1 = 6$. Geometrically, $\overline{R}$
 defines a plane cubic curve (degree $3$, dimension $1$) and $S$ defines $\mathb
b{P}^1$ (degree $1$, dimension $1$). The Segre product has degree $3\cdot 1\cdot
\binom{2}{1}=6$. ✓

- **First coefficients:** Expanding $\frac{1+3t+3t^2-t^3}{(1-t)^3}$ via $(1-t)^{
-3}=\sum\binom{n+2}{2}t^n$:

  | $n$ | coeff | $\dim\overline{R}_n\cdot\dim S_n$ |
  |-----|-------|-----------------------------------|
  | $0$ | $1$ | $1\cdot 1 = 1$ ✓ |
  | $1$ | $6$ | $3\cdot 2 = 6$ ✓ |
  | $2$ | $18$ | $6\cdot 3 = 18$ ✓ |
  | $3$ | $36$ | $9\cdot 4 = 36$ ✓ |
  | $4$ | $60$ | $12\cdot 5 = 60$ ✓ |

All checks pass.
