# Problem

In a specialized botanical laboratory, Dr. Aris is conducting an experiment to synthesize a rare bioluminescent protein. The process involves a sequence of chemical catalysts added one by one. Each time a catalyst is added, there is a chance the protein will undergo a "Brilliant Mutation."

The stability of the reaction depends on the number of mutations that have already occurred. If the protein has already experienced exactly $n$ mutations, the probability that the next catalyst causes a new mutation is $\frac{1}{n+2}$. Conversely, the probability that the next catalyst fails to cause a mutation is $\frac{n+1}{n+2}$.

Dr. Aris plans to continue adding catalysts indefinitely to observe the long-term behavior of the protein. Let $p$ be the probability that, at some point during this infinite sequence of trials, the protein undergoes a Brilliant Mutation for three consecutive catalyst additions.

Compute $\lfloor 180 p\rfloor$.
