Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether, for any measurable $A \sub
set \mathbb{R}$ with $\lambda(A)>0$, there exist measurable sets $K_1, K$ with $
K_1 \subset A$, $K_1 \subset K$, $K$ compact, and $\lambda(K_1)=\lambda(K)>0$.

## Answer: Yes.

**Proof.**

Since $\lambda(A) > 0$ and $A = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}(A \cap [-n,n])$, there ex
ists $n \in \mathbb{N}$ with $\lambda(A \cap [-n,n]) > 0$ (otherwise $\lambda(A)
=0$ by countable subadditivity, a contradiction).

Set $B := A \cap [-n,n]$. Then $B$ is measurable, $B \subset A$, and $0 < \lambd
a(B) \le 2n < \infty$.

By **inner regularity of Lebesgue measure** (which holds for every measurable se
t of finite measure): for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists a compact set $K
\subset B$ with $\lambda(K) > \lambda(B) - \varepsilon$.

Choose $\varepsilon = \lambda(B)/2 > 0$. Then there exists compact $K \subset B
\subset A$ with
$$\lambda(K) > \lambda(B) - \frac{\lambda(B)}{2} = \frac{\lambda(B)}{2} > 0.$$

Now set $K_1 := K$. We verify all the required conditions:

1. **$K_1 \subset A$**: $K_1 = K \subset B \subset A$. ✓
2. **$K \subset \mathbb{R}$**: $K$ is a compact subset of $\mathbb{R}$. ✓
3. **$K_1 \subset K$**: $K_1 = K$, so $K_1 \subset K$ (as subsets, equality is p
ermitted). ✓
4. **$K$ is compact**: by construction. ✓
5. **$\lambda(K_1) = \lambda(K) > 0$**: since $K_1 = K$ and $\lambda(K) > \lambd
a(B)/2 > 0$. ✓

All conditions are satisfied. The existence of such $K_1$ and $K$ is therefore g
uaranteed for every measurable set $A$ with positive Lebesgue measure, using onl
y the inner regularity of Lebesgue measure (which ensures that any measurable se
t of positive finite measure contains a compact subset of positive measure).
