## Solution

We evaluate
$$I_n = \int_0^{2n\pi}\frac{(n+x)\sin(x/n)}{x(1+x)^2}\,dx.$$

**Step 1: Pointwise limit of the integrand.**

For each fixed $x > 0$, as $n\to\infty$ we have $x/n \to 0$, so $\sin(x/n)/(x/n)
\to 1$. Rewriting:
$$\frac{(n+x)\sin(x/n)}{x(1+x)^2} = \frac{(1+x/n)}{(1+x)^2}\cdot\frac{\sin(x/n)}
{x/n} \;\longrightarrow\; \frac{1}{(1+x)^2}.$$

At $x=0$ the integrand is understood by continuity; its limit is $1$ there as we
ll.

**Step 2: Extend to $[0,\infty)$ and dominate.**

Set $g_n(x) = \dfrac{(n+x)\sin(x/n)}{x(1+x)^2}\,\mathbf{1}_{[0,\,2n\pi]}(x)$, so
 $I_n = \int_0^\infty g_n(x)\,dx$. For every fixed $x\ge 0$, eventually $x < 2n\
pi$, hence $g_n(x)\to \dfrac{1}{(1+x)^2}$ pointwise on $[0,\infty)$.

For a dominating function: using $|\sin t|\le |t|$,
$$|g_n(x)| \le \mathbf{1}_{[0,2n\pi]}(x)\cdot\frac{(n+x)\cdot(x/n)}{x(1+x)^2} =
\mathbf{1}_{[0,2n\pi]}(x)\cdot\frac{1+x/n}{(1+x)^2}.$$
On $[0,2n\pi]$ we have $x/n\le 2\pi$, so $1+x/n\le 1+2\pi$. Therefore
$$|g_n(x)|\le \frac{1+2\pi}{(1+x)^2} \;=:\; G(x),$$
and $G\in L^1([0,\infty))$ since $\int_0^\infty \frac{dx}{(1+x)^2}=1<\infty$.

**Step 3: Apply Dominated Convergence.**

By the DCT,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} I_n = \int_0^\infty \lim_{n\to\infty} g_n(x)\,dx = \int_0^\i
nfty \frac{dx}{(1+x)^2} = \left[-\frac{1}{1+x}\right]_0^\infty = 0-(-1)=1.$$

**Verification via an alternative substitution.** Setting $u=x/n$ then $t=1/(1+n
u)$ transforms $I_n$ into $\int_{1/(1+2n\pi)}^{1}\frac{(1+u)\sin u}{u}\,dt$ with
 $u=(1-t)/(nt)\to 0$ for each fixed $t\in(0,1]$. The integrand (bounded on $u\in
[0,2\pi]$) tends to $1$ pointwise, and DCT on $[0,1]$ yields $\int_0^1 1\,dt=1$,
 confirming the result.

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^{2n\pi}\frac{(n+x)\sin(x/n)}{x(1+x)^2}\,dx = 1}
$$
