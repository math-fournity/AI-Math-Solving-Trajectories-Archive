## Solution

We evaluate the integral using the residue theorem (which is the general form of
 the Cauchy integral formula).

**Poles inside the contour.** The integrand $f(z) = \dfrac{1}{z\cos z}$ has sing
ularities where $z = 0$ or $\cos z = 0$, i.e., at $z = 0$ and $z = \dfrac{(2n+1)
\pi}{2}$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

The contour is $|z| = 4$. We check which poles lie inside:
- $z = 0$: $|0| = 0 < 4$ ✓
- $z = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{2}$: $\left|\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right| \approx 1.571 < 4$ ✓
- $z = \pm\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$: $\left|\dfrac{3\pi}{2}\right| \approx 4.712 > 4$ ✗

So exactly three poles lie inside $|z|=4$: $z = 0$ and $z = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

**Applying the residue theorem.** By the residue theorem,

$$\int_{|z|=4} \frac{dz}{z\cos z} = 2\pi i \sum \text{Res}(f, z_k)$$

where the sum is over all poles inside the contour. The problem gives us:
- $\text{Res}(f, 0) = 1$
- $\text{Res}\!\left(f, \tfrac{\pi}{2}\right) + \text{Res}\!\left(f, -\tfrac{\pi
}{2}\right) = -\dfrac{4}{\pi}$

Therefore the total sum of residues inside is

$$\sum \text{Res} = 1 + \left(-\frac{4}{\pi}\right) = 1 - \frac{4}{\pi} = \frac{
\pi - 4}{\pi} = -\frac{4-\pi}{\pi}.$$

**Computing the full expression.** Now

$$\int_{|z|=4} \frac{dz}{z\cos z} = 2\pi i \cdot \left(-\frac{4-\pi}{\pi}\right)
 = -2i(4-\pi).$$

Multiplying by the prefactor $\dfrac{i}{4-\pi}$:

$$\frac{i}{4-\pi}\int_{|z|=4} \frac{dz}{z\cos z} = \frac{i}{4-\pi}\cdot\bigl(-2i
(4-\pi)\bigr) = i\cdot(-2i) = -2i^2 = 2.$$

**Result.**

$$\boxed{2}$$
