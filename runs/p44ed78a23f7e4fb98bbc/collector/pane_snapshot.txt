## Proof

**Notation.** Write $p' := \{p_1, \dots, p_l\}$ for the set of primes dividing $
|G|$ other than $p$. A *Hall $p'$-subgroup* of $G$ has order $p_1^{n_1}\cdots p_
l^{n_l}$.

---

**Step 1: $l \geq 1$.**

Since $G$ is a non-$p$-group, $|G|$ is divisible by at least two distinct primes
. From $|G| = p_1^{n_1}\cdots p_l^{n_l}\, p^{m+n}$ with $p_1,\dots,p_l,p$ distin
ct, if $l=0$ then $|G|=p^{m+n}$, making $G$ a $p$-group — a contradiction. Hence
 $l\geq 1$.

---

**Step 2: $A$ contains a Hall $p'$-subgroup of $G$.**

Since $|G:A|=p^n$, we have $|A|=p_1^{n_1}\cdots p_l^{n_l}\,p^m$. The $p'$-part o
f $|A|$ is $p_1^{n_1}\cdots p_l^{n_l}$, which equals the $p'$-part of $|G|$. Sin
ce $G$ is solvable, so is $A$, and by **Hall's theorem** $A$ possesses Hall $p'$
-subgroups of order $p_1^{n_1}\cdots p_l^{n_l}$. Any such subgroup is a $p'$-sub
group of $G$ whose order equals the full $p'$-part of $|G|$; hence it is a Hall
$p'$-subgroup of $G$ lying inside $A$.

---

**Step 3: Extending to a Sylow basis of $G$.**

Since $G$ is solvable, $G$ admits a **Sylow basis** (Hall). Let
$$\mathcal{S}_0 = \{Q_1,\dots,Q_l,Q\}$$
be a Sylow basis of $G$, where $Q_i$ is a Sylow $p_i$-subgroup and $Q$ is a Sylo
w $p$-subgroup. Because the elements of a Sylow basis pairwise permute, the prod
uct
$$H_0 := Q_1 Q_2 \cdots Q_l$$
is a subgroup — it is the Hall $p'$-subgroup of $G$ determined by $\mathcal{S}_0
$.

By Step 2, there exists a Hall $p'$-subgroup $H$ of $G$ with $H\subseteq A$. By
**conjugacy of Hall subgroups** in the solvable group $G$, there exists $g\in G$
 with $H = gH_0g^{-1}$.

Now conjugate the entire Sylow basis:
$$\mathcal{S} := g\,\mathcal{S}_0\,g^{-1} = \{gQ_1g^{-1},\;\dots,\;gQ_lg^{-1},\;
gQg^{-1}\}.$$

Conjugation preserves both the Sylow property and pairwise permutability, so $\m
athcal{S}$ is again a Sylow basis of $G$. Its Hall $p'$-subgroup is
$$gQ_1g^{-1}\cdot gQ_2g^{-1}\cdots gQ_lg^{-1} = g(Q_1\cdots Q_l)g^{-1} = gH_0g^{
-1} = H \subseteq A.$$

Setting $P_i := gQ_ig^{-1}$ for $1\le i\le l$ and $P:=gQg^{-1}$, we obtain a Syl
ow basis
$$\{P_1, P_2, \dots, P_l, P\}$$
of $G$ with $l\ge 1$, and for every $1\le i\le l$:
$$P_i \subseteq H \subseteq A.$$

---

**Conclusion.** Yes — such a Sylow basis exists. The argument uses only that $G$
 is solvable (for the existence and conjugacy of Sylow bases and Hall subgroups)
, that $|G:A|=p^n$ (so that $A$ contains the full $p'$-part of $|G|$), and that
$G$ is a non-$p$-group (so $l\ge 1$). $\blacksquare$
