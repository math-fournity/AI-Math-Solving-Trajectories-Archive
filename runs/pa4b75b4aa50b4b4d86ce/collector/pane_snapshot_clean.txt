## Answer

**The statement is TRUE.**

## Proof

Let $H$ be a Hilbert space, $B(H)$ the algebra of all bounded operators on $H$,
and $L^1(H)$ the Banach space of trace-class operators on $H$.

**Key facts:**

1. **$L^1(H)$ is the predual of $B(H)$.** Every trace-class operator $A \in L^1(
H)$ defines a bounded linear functional $\varphi_A$ on $B(H)$ by
$$\varphi_A(T) = \operatorname{tr}(AT), \quad T \in B(H).$$
The map $A \mapsto \varphi_A$ is an isometric isomorphism $L^1(H) \cong B(H)_*$,
 where $B(H)_*$ denotes the predual. (This is a classical theorem; see e.g. the
duality $L^1(H)^* \cong B(H)$ via the trace pairing.)

2. **The ultraweak (= W*-continuous) topology on $B(H)$ is $\sigma(B(H), L^1(H))
$.** By definition, the W*-topology (ultraweak / $\sigma$-weak topology) on $B(H
) = L^1(H)^*$ is the weak-$^*$ topology $\sigma(B(H), L^1(H))$, i.e., the weakes
t topology on $B(H)$ making all the functionals $T \mapsto \operatorname{tr}(AT)
$, $A \in L^1(H)$, continuous.

3. **General weak-$^*$ duality.** For any Banach space $X$, the continuous dual
of $(X^*, \sigma(X^*, X))$ is exactly $X$ itself. This is a standard consequence
 of the bipolar theorem (or Mackey's theorem): the dual of $X^*$ equipped with i
ts weak-$^*$ topology is precisely the predual $X$, not the full dual $X^{**}$.

**Applying this:** Taking $X = L^1(H)$ so that $X^* = B(H)$, the W*-continuous (
ultraweakly continuous) linear functionals on $B(H)$ are exactly the elements of
 the predual $L^1(H)$.

**Conclusion:** Every W*-continuous linear functional on $B(H)$ is of the form $
T \mapsto \operatorname{tr}(AT)$ for a unique $A \in L^1(H)$, and conversely eve
ry such functional is W*-continuous. Hence the space of W*-continuous linear fun
ctionals is precisely $L^1(H)$, and the statement is **true**. $\blacksquare$
