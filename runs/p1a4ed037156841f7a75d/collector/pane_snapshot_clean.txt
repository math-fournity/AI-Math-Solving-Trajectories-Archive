## Solution

Let
$$I(t)=\int_0^1 e^{t f(x)}\,dx.$$
Differentiating under the integral sign twice,
$$I''(t)=\int_0^1 f(x)^2 e^{t f(x)}\,dx,$$
so the desired quantity is
$$I''(0)=\int_0^1 f(x)^2\,dx.$$

### Reducing $f(x)$ to a sum of binary digits

Fix $n$. For $x\in(0,1]$ written in its non-terminating binary expansion $x=0.a_
1a_2a_3\ldots$, let $S_n(x)=a_1+\cdots+a_n$ be the number of $1$'s among the fir
st $n$ binary digits. The number of $0$'s among them is $n-S_n$. Hence
$$f(x)=S_n(x)-(n-S_n(x))=2S_n(x)-n.$$

### Treating $x\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]$ as a random variable

The map $x\mapsto (a_1,a_2,\ldots)$ sends the Lebesgue measure on $[0,1]$ to the
 product measure under which the $a_i$ are i.i.d. $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)$. The
 non-terminating-expansion convention only affects dyadic rationals, a set of me
asure zero, so it does not change any integral. Therefore
$$\int_0^1 f(x)^2\,dx=\mathbb{E}\bigl[(2S_n-n)^2\bigr],\qquad S_n\sim\mathrm{Bin
omial}(n,\tfrac12).$$

### Computing the moments

For $S_n\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(n,1/2)$:
$$\mathbb{E}[S_n]=\frac n2,\qquad \mathrm{Var}(S_n)=\frac n4,\qquad \mathbb{E}[S
_n^2]=\mathrm{Var}(S_n)+\mathbb{E}[S_n]^2=\frac n4+\frac{n^2}{4}.$$

Thus
$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[(2S_n-n)^2\bigr]=4\,\mathbb{E}[S_n^2]-4n\,\mathbb{E}[S_n]+n^2.
$$

Substituting:
$$=4\!\left(\frac n4+\frac{n^2}{4}\right)-4n\cdot\frac n2+n^2=(n+n^2)-2n^2+n^2=n
.$$

### Result

$$\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\!\left(\int_0^1 e^{t f(x)}\,dx\right)\bigg|_{t=0}=\int_0^1 f(
x)^2\,dx=\boxed{n}.$$
