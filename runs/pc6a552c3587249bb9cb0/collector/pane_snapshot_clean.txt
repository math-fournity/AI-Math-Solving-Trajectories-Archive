## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle \oint_C \frac{z^2+1}{e^{z/10}-1}\,dz$ where $C$ is th
e unit circle $|z|=1$ traversed counterclockwise.

**Step 1: Identify the singularities.**

The integrand $f(z)=\dfrac{z^2+1}{e^{z/10}-1}$ has singularities where the denom
inator vanishes:
$$e^{z/10}-1=0 \iff z/10 = 2\pi i k,\; k\in\mathbb{Z} \iff z = 20\pi i k,\; k\in
\mathbb{Z}.$$

**Step 2: Determine which singularities lie inside $C$.**

We need $|20\pi i k| \le 1$, i.e., $20\pi|k| \le 1$. Since $20\pi \approx 62.83
> 1$, the only value of $k$ satisfying this is $k=0$, giving the singularity $z=
0$.

**Step 3: Classify the singularity at $z=0$.**

Near $z=0$,
$$e^{z/10}-1 = \frac{z}{10} + \frac{z^2}{200} + \cdots = \frac{z}{10}\left(1 + \
frac{z}{20} + \cdots\right),$$
so the denominator has a **simple zero** at $z=0$. The numerator at $z=0$ is $0^
2+1=1\neq 0$. Hence $z=0$ is a **simple pole** of $f$.

**Step 4: Compute the residue at $z=0$.**

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z) = \lim_{z\to 0} z\cdot\frac{z^2+1}{e^{z/10}-1} =
 \lim_{z\to 0}\frac{z(z^2+1)}{e^{z/10}-1}.$$

Since $e^{z/10}-1 \sim \dfrac{z}{10}$ as $z\to 0$,
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z) = \lim_{z\to 0}\frac{z(z^2+1)}{z/10} = \lim_{z\t
o 0}10(z^2+1) = 10\cdot 1 = 10.$$

**Step 5: Apply the residue theorem.**

Since $z=0$ is the only singularity enclosed by $C$,
$$\oint_C \frac{z^2+1}{e^{z/10}-1}\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f
(z) = 2\pi i \cdot 10 = 20\pi i.$$

$$\boxed{\oint_C \frac{z^2+1}{e^{z/10}-1}\,dz = 20\pi i}$$
