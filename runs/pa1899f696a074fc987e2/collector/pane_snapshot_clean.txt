Yes, $A = B$.

Let $H$ be a Hilbert space and let $A, B$ be closed subspaces of $H$ with $A^{\p
erp} = B^{\perp}$.

**Key fact.** For any closed subspace $M$ of a Hilbert space $H$, we have the or
thogonal decomposition
$$H = M \oplus M^{\perp},$$
and consequently
$$M = (M^{\perp})^{\perp}.$$

*Proof of the key fact.* The decomposition $H = M \oplus M^{\perp}$ is the stand
ard projection theorem: since $M$ is closed and convex (being a closed subspace)
, every $x \in H$ has a unique closest point $P_M x \in M$, and $x - P_M x \in M
^{\perp}$.

From $H = M \oplus M^{\perp}$, taking the orthogonal complement of both sides gi
ves $H = M^{\perp} \oplus M^{\perp\perp}$. Since $M \subseteq M^{\perp\perp}$ al
ways holds (any element of $M$ is orthogonal to every element of $M^{\perp}$), a
nd the decomposition $H = M^{\perp} \oplus M^{\perp\perp}$ shows that every $x \
in M^{\perp\perp}$ can be written as $x = m + n$ with $m \in M^{\perp}$ and $n \
in M^{\perp\perp}$, we get $x - n = m \in M^{\perp} \cap M^{\perp\perp} = \{0\}$
, so $x = n \in M$. Thus $M^{\perp\perp} \subseteq M$, giving $M = M^{\perp\perp
}$. $\square$

**Applying the key fact.** Since $A$ and $B$ are closed subspaces, the key fact
gives:
$$A = (A^{\perp})^{\perp} \quad \text{and} \quad B = (B^{\perp})^{\perp}.$$

By hypothesis $A^{\perp} = B^{\perp}$. Therefore
$$A = (A^{\perp})^{\perp} = (B^{\perp})^{\perp} = B.$$

Hence $A = B$. $\blacksquare$
