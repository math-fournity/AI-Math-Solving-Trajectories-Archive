We solve the eigenvalue problem

$$-\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}+x^2y=\lambda y,\qquad y\in L^2(\mathbb{R}).$$

This is the quantum harmonic-oscillator equation. We prove that the spectrum is
purely discrete and that the eigenvalues are exactly

$$\boxed{\lambda_n=2n+1,\qquad n=0,1,2,\dots}$$

---

## 1. Factorization and ladder operators

Define the operators (on the Schwartz space $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})$ first, the
n extended)

$$a=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Big(x+\frac{d}{dx}\Big),\qquad a^\dagger=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2
}}\Big(x-\frac{d}{dx}\Big).$$

A direct computation gives the **canonical commutation relation**

$$[a,a^\dagger]=aa^\dagger-a^\dagger a=1.$$

Indeed,
$$aa^\dagger=\tfrac12\big(x-\tfrac{d}{dx}\big)\big(x+\tfrac{d}{dx}\big)=\tfrac12
\big(x^2-\tfrac{d^2}{dx^2}+1\big),$$
$$a^\dagger a=\tfrac12\big(x+\tfrac{d}{dx}\big)\big(x-\tfrac{d}{dx}\big)=\tfrac1
2\big(x^2-\tfrac{d^2}{dx^2}-1\big),$$
so $aa^\dagger-a^\dagger a=1$. The Hamiltonian is

$$H=-\frac{d^2}{dx^2}+x^2=2a^\dagger a+1.$$

Setting $N=a^\dagger a$ (the number operator), $H=2N+1$, so eigenvalues of $H$ a
re $\lambda=2\nu+1$ where $\nu$ ranges over eigenvalues of $N$.

---

## 2. $N$ has nonneg integer eigenvalues

**Nonnegativity.** For any $\psi\in\mathcal{S}$,
$$\langle\psi,N\psi\rangle=\langle\psi,a^\dagger a\,\psi\rangle=\|a\psi\|^2\ge 0
.$$
So every eigenvalue $\nu$ of $N$ satisfies $\nu\ge 0$.

**Ladder structure.** Using $[a,a^\dagger]=1$:
$$[N,a]= -a,\qquad [N,a^\dagger]=a^\dagger.$$
Thus if $N\psi=\nu\psi$, then
$$N(a\psi)=(\nu-1)(a\psi),\qquad N(a^\dagger\psi)=(\nu+1)(a^\dagger\psi).$$
So $a$ lowers and $a^\dagger$ raises the eigenvalue by $1$.

**Ground state.** Repeatedly applying $a$ produces eigenvectors with eigenvalues
 $\nu,\nu-1,\nu-2,\dots$. Since all eigenvalues are $\ge 0$, this chain must ter
minate: for some $k\ge 0$, $a^{k+1}\psi=0$ while $a^k\psi\neq 0$. Let $\psi_0=a^
k\psi/\|a^k\psi\|$. Then $a\psi_0=0$, i.e.
$$\Big(x+\frac{d}{dx}\Big)\psi_0=0\implies \psi_0(x)=C\,e^{-x^2/2}.$$
For $\psi_0\in L^2$ we need $C\neq 0$ (and $\|\psi_0\|^2=|C|^2\sqrt\pi$). Then
$$N\psi_0=a^\dagger a\,\psi_0=0\implies \nu_0=0.$$
Therefore every eigenvalue of $N$ is a nonneg integer $\nu\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}$, r
eached from $\nu_0=0$ by repeated application of $a^\dagger$.

---

## 3. Existence of all eigenfunctions $\psi_n$

Define recursively
$$\psi_n=\frac{(a^\dagger)^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\,\psi_0,\qquad \psi_0(x)=\pi^{-1/4}e^{-
x^2/2}.$$

**Normalization.** If $\|\psi_n\|=1$, then using $a(a^\dagger\phi)=a^\dagger(a\p
hi)+\phi$ (i.e. $aa^\dagger=N+1$):
$$\|a^\dagger\psi_n\|^2=\langle\psi_n,aa^\dagger\psi_n\rangle=\langle\psi_n,(N+1
)\psi_n\rangle=n+1.$$
Hence $\|\psi_{n+1}\|^2=\|a^\dagger\psi_n\|^2/(n+1)=1$. By induction all $\psi_n
$ are normalized.

**Eigenvalue.** $N\psi_n=n\psi_n$, so
$$H\psi_n=(2N+1)\psi_n=(2n+1)\psi_n.$$

**Explicit form.** Since $a^\dagger=\frac1{\sqrt2}(x-d/dx)$ and $\psi_0\propto e
^{-x^2/2}$,
$$\psi_n(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^n n!}}\Big(x-\frac{d}{dx}\Big)^n\psi_0(x)=\frac{1}{
\pi^{1/4}\sqrt{2^n n!}}\,H_n(x)\,e^{-x^2/2},$$
where $H_n$ are the Hermite polynomials (defined by $H_n(x)=(-1)^n e^{x^2}\frac{
d^n}{dx^n}e^{-x^2}$, equivalently generated by the Rodrigues/raising formula abo
ve). These are square-integrable, so each $\lambda_n=2n+1$ is realized.

---

## 4. Completeness (no other eigenvalues)

The functions $\{H_n(x)e^{-x^2/2}\}$ form a complete orthogonal system in $L^2(\
mathbb{R})$ (Hermite functions). One standard proof: $\{e^{-x^2/2}x^k\}_{k\ge0}$
 has dense span (Gaussian times polynomials; equivalently the measure $e^{-x^2}d
x$ has determinate moment problem), and Gram–Schmidt on these produces exactly t
he Hermite functions. Completeness also follows from the fact that the harmonic
oscillator $H$ with domain $\mathcal{S}$ is essentially self-adjoint and has com
pact resolvent (the inverse is Hilbert–Schmidt, since the Gaussian weight makes
the Green kernel square-integrable), so its spectrum is a discrete sequence with
 no finite accumulation point.

Therefore there are **no** eigenvalues other than $\{2n+1:n\ge0\}$.

---

## 5. Conclusion

The eigenvalues of $-\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}+x^2y=\lambda y$ on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ are

$$\lambda_n=2n+1,\qquad n=0,1,2,3,\dots$$

with corresponding normalized eigenfunctions

$$y_n(x)=\frac{1}{\pi^{1/4}\sqrt{2^n n!}}\,H_n(x)\,e^{-x^2/2}.$$
