## Solution

**Setting up the equation.** Let $N = 100A+10B+C$, $X = 10B+C$ (last two digits)
, $Y = 100A+10B = 10(10A+B)$ (the three-digit number ending in 0). The condition
 is

$$N^2 = X^2 + Y^2.$$

Since $N = Y + C$, expanding:

$$(Y+C)^2 = X^2 + Y^2 \implies 2YC + C^2 = X^2 \implies C(2Y+C) = X^2.$$

Substituting $Y = 10(10A+B)$ and $X = 10B+C$:

$$C\bigl(20(10A+B)+C\bigr) = (10B+C)^2.$$

Expanding both sides:

$$20(10A+B)C + C^2 = 100B^2 + 20BC + C^2.$$

Canceling $C^2$ and $20BC$ from both sides:

$$200AC = 100B^2 \implies \boxed{B^2 = 2AC}.$$

**Solving $B^2 = 2AC$** with $A\in\{1,\dots,9\}$, $B,C\in\{0,\dots,9\}$.

Since $2AC$ is even, $B^2$ is even, so $B$ is even: $B \in \{0,2,4,6,8\}$.
