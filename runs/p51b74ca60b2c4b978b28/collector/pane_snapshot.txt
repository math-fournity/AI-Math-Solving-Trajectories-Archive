## Proof

Consider a simple symmetric random walk on the two-dimensional integer lattice $
\mathbb{Z}^2$, starting at the origin $(0,0)$, where at each step the walker mov
es to one of the four nearest neighbors with equal probability $\tfrac{1}{4}$.

**Parity invariant.** Define $S_n = x_n + y_n$, the sum of the coordinates after
 $n$ steps. At each step, exactly one coordinate changes by $\pm 1$, so $S_n$ ch
anges by exactly $\pm 1$ at every step. Since $S_0 = 0$ (even), by induction:

$$S_n \equiv n \pmod{2}.$$

**Return to origin requires even $n$.** To be at the origin at time $n$, we need
 $(x_n, y_n) = (0,0)$, hence $S_n = 0$, which is even. By the parity invariant,
this forces $n$ to be even.

**Conclusion.** For any odd $n$, the walk cannot be at the origin:

$$\Pr\big[(x_n, y_n) = (0,0)\big] = 0 \quad \text{for all odd } n.$$

Therefore, the probability that the 2D random walk returns to the origin after a
n odd number of steps is

$$\boxed{0}.$$
