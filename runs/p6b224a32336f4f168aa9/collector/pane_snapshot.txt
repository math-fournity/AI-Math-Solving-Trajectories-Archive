**Answer: Yes.** Every cover of $\mathbb{R}$ by half-open intervals $[a,b)$ admi
ts a countable subcover. (This is the classical result that the Sorgenfrey line
is Lindelöf.)

---

**Proof.**

Let $\mathscr{U} = \{[a_\alpha, b_\alpha) : \alpha \in I\}$ be a cover of $\math
bb{R}$, where each $a_\alpha < b_\alpha$.

**Step 1: Pass to the interiors.** Define the open (in the usual topology) set
$$G \;=\; \bigcup_{\alpha \in I} (a_\alpha,\, b_\alpha).$$
Since $G$ is open in the usual topology on $\mathbb{R}$, it decomposes uniquely
as a countable union of pairwise disjoint open intervals (its connected componen
ts):
$$G = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} (c_n,\, d_n),$$
where each $c_n \in [-\infty,+\infty)$ and $d_n \in (-\infty,+\infty]$, and $c_n
 < d_n$.

**Step 2: Cover each component $(c_n, d_n)$ countably.** For each fixed $n$, the
 collection $\{(a_\alpha, b_\alpha) : \alpha \in I\}$ is an open cover of $(c_n,
 d_n)$ in the usual topology. Since $(c_n, d_n)$ with the subspace topology is s
econd-countable (hence Lindelöf), there exists a countable set $I_n \subset I$ s
uch that
$$(c_n, d_n) \;\subset\; \bigcup_{\alpha \in I_n} (a_\alpha, b_\alpha) \;\subset
\; \bigcup_{\alpha \in I_n} [a_\alpha, b_\alpha).$$
The union $\bigcup_n I_n$ is countable (countable union of countable sets), so t
he intervals $\{[a_\alpha, b_\alpha) : \alpha \in \bigcup_n I_n\}$ form a counta
ble subcollection of $\mathscr{U}$ that covers all of $G$.

**Step 3: The leftover $\mathbb{R} \setminus G$ is countable.** We claim
$$\mathbb{R} \setminus G \;\subseteq\; \{\,c_n : n \in \mathbb{N},\; c_n \in \ma
thbb{R}\,\},$$
which is countable.

*Proof of claim.* Let $x \in \mathbb{R} \setminus G$. Since $\mathscr{U}$ covers
 $\mathbb{R}$, there is $\alpha \in I$ with $x \in [a_\alpha, b_\alpha)$. If $x
\in (a_\alpha, b_\alpha)$ then $x \in G$, contradiction. Hence $x = a_\alpha$. N
ow $(x,\, b_\alpha) = (a_\alpha, b_\alpha) \subset G$. Being a connected subset
of $G$, the interval $(x, b_\alpha)$ lies entirely inside a single component $(c
_k, d_k)$ for some $k$. Since $x \notin G \supset (c_k, d_k)$, we have $x \leq c
_k$; and since every point of $(x, b_\alpha)$ lies in $(c_k, d_k)$, taking the i
nfimum gives $c_k \leq x$. Therefore $x = c_k$, with $c_k \in \mathbb{R}$. $\qua
d\checkmark$

**Step 4: Cover the leftover.** For each $x \in \mathbb{R}\setminus G$ (countabl
y many points by Step 3), pick one index $\alpha(x) \in I$ with $x \in [a_{\alph
a(x)},\, b_{\alpha(x)})$ (which exists since $\mathscr{U}$ covers $\mathbb{R}$).
 This gives a countable subcollection $\{[a_{\alpha(x)}, b_{\alpha(x)}) : x \in
\mathbb{R}\setminus G\}$ covering $\mathbb{R}\setminus G$.

**Step 5: Combine.** The union of
- the countable family from Step 2 (covering $G$), and
- the countable family from Step 4 (covering $\mathbb{R}\setminus G$)

is a countable subcover of $\mathscr{U}$. $\quad\blacksquare$
