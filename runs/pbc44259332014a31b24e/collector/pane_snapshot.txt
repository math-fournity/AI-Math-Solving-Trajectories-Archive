Yes. A simple example suffices.

**Claim.** There exists a separable metric compactification $\overline{X}$ of a
separable metric space $X$ with $X$ open in $\overline{X}$.

**Proof.** Take $X = (0,1)$ with its usual metric, and $\overline{X} = [0,1]$ wi
th its usual metric, with the inclusion map $i \colon X \hookrightarrow \overlin
e{X}$.

1. **$X$ is a separable metric space.** $(0,1)$ is a subspace of $\mathbb{R}$, h
ence metrizable and second countable, hence separable (e.g. $\mathbb{Q} \cap (0,
1)$ is countable and dense).

2. **$\overline{X}$ is a separable metric compact space.** $[0,1]$ is compact (H
eine–Borel), metrizable, and compact metric spaces are separable.

3. **$i$ is a homeomorphism onto its image.** The subspace topology on $(0,1) \s
ubseteq [0,1]$ coincides with the usual topology on $(0,1)$.

4. **$X$ is dense in $\overline{X}$.** Every point of $[0,1]$ is a limit of a se
quence in $(0,1)$: interior points are themselves in $(0,1)$; $0 = \lim_{n\to\in
fty} \tfrac{1}{n}$ and $1 = \lim_{n\to\infty}(1-\tfrac{1}{n})$ with $\tfrac{1}{n
}, 1-\tfrac{1}{n} \in (0,1)$.

5. **$X$ is open in $\overline{X}$.** In the subspace topology of $[0,1] \subset
eq \mathbb{R}$,
   $$(0,1) = [0,1] \cap (0,1),$$
   and $(0,1)$ is open in $\mathbb{R}$, so $(0,1)$ is open in $[0,1]$. Equivalen
tly, $\overline{X}\setminus X = \{0,1\}$ is closed (finite) in $[0,1]$.

Thus $\overline{X} = [0,1]$ is a separable metric compactification of the separa
ble metric space $X = (0,1)$, and $X$ is open in $\overline{X}$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark (characterization).** More generally, a separable metrizable space $X$
admits a metrizable compactification in which $X$ is open **iff** $X$ is locally
 compact (and non-compact, for a non-degenerate remainder). Necessity: an open s
ubspace of a compact Hausdorff space is locally compact. Sufficiency: the one-po
int compactification of a non-compact, locally compact, separable metrizable spa
ce is metrizable, and $X$ is open in it. The example $(0,1) \subset [0,1]$ (or e
quivalently $(0,1) \subset S^1$ via the one-point compactification) is the canon
ical instance.
