## Proof

Let $x = (x_1, x_2, \dots) \in l^\infty$. The $n$-th component of $S(x)$ is
$$
(Sx)_n = \frac{1}{2^n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i.
$$

**Step 1: $S$ is well-defined and bounded, with $\|S\| \le 2$.**

For any $x \in l^\infty$ with $\|x\|_\infty \le 1$, we have $|x_i| \le 1$ for al
l $i$, so by the triangle inequality
$$
\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i\right| \le \sum_{i=1}^{n} |x_i| \le n.
$$
Therefore
$$
\|Sx\|_1 = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^n}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i\right| \le
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n}{2^n}.
$$
Using the identity $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} n\,r^n = \frac{r}{(1-r)^2}$ for $|r|<1$
with $r = \tfrac{1}{2}$:
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n}{2^n} = \frac{1/2}{(1/2)^2} = 2.
$$
Hence $\|Sx\|_1 \le 2$ for every $x$ with $\|x\|_\infty \le 1$, which shows $Sx
\in l^1$ (so $S$ is well-defined) and $\|S\| \le 2$.

**Step 2: $\|S\| \ge 2$.**

Take $x = (1, 1, 1, \dots) \in l^\infty$ with $\|x\|_\infty = 1$. Then $\sum_{i=
1}^{n} x_i = n$, so
$$
\|Sx\|_1 = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n}{2^n} = 2.
$$
Thus $\|S\| = \sup_{\|x\|_\infty \le 1} \|Sx\|_1 \ge 2$.

**Conclusion.**

Combining both steps,
$$
\boxed{\|S\| = 2.}
$$
