Looking at this problem, I need to determine for which $n \in \{1, \ldots, 20\}$
 there are only finitely many positive multiples of $n$ having exactly $n$ divis
ors.

## Key Framework

Let $n = p_1^{a_1} \cdots p_s^{a_s}$. A positive multiple $m$ of $n$ with $d(m)
= n$ has prime factorization $m = q_1^{b_1} \cdots q_t^{b_t}$ where $\prod(b_j+1
) = n$ and $n \mid m$.

**Divisibility constraint:** Each prime $p_i \mid n$ must appear among the $q_j$
's with exponent $b_j \geq a_i$, i.e., assigned to a slot with $c_j := b_j + 1 \
geq a_i + 1$. Since distinct primes occupy distinct slots, we need at least $s$
slots.

**Free slots produce infinitely many solutions:** If a factorization pattern of
$n$ into factors $\geq 2$ has $t > s$ slots and the $s$ primes can be feasibly a
ssigned (each $p_i$ to a slot with $c_j \geq a_i+1$), then the remaining $t - s$
 slots are filled with arbitrary primes (infinitely many choices), yielding infi
nitely many $m$.

**No free slots means finitely many:** If every feasible pattern has $t = s$, al
l slots are occupied by primes of $n$, giving at most finitely many $m$ (finitel
y many patterns × finitely many assignments).

**Feasibility test:** Sort requirements $r_i = a_i + 1$ and slot sizes $c_j$ bot
h in decreasing order. Assignment is feasible iff $c_{(i)} \geq r_i$ for all $i
= 1, \ldots, s$ (greedy matching).

**Conclusion:** There are infinitely many qualifying multiples $\iff$ some facto
rization of $n$ into factors $\geq 2$ has $t > s$ slots and is feasible.
