## Solution

**Setup.** We distribute 100 identical bearings into $k$ bins ($k \geq 2$). The
Uniformity Constraint requires that any two bins differ in count by at most 1. T
he problem states that for each valid choice of $k$, there is **exactly one** wa
y to distribute.

**Key analysis.** Write $100 = qk + r$ with $0 \le r < k$. The Uniformity Constr
aint forces exactly $r$ bins to hold $q+1$ bearings and $k - r$ bins to hold $q$
 bearings.

- If the bins are **distinguishable**, the number of distinct distributions is $
\binom{k}{r}$ (choosing which $r$ bins get the larger count).
- If the bins are **indistinguishable**, the distribution is always uniquely det
ermined for every $k$, giving 99 choices — which would make the problem trivial
and the "exactly one way" condition vacuous.

Since the problem treats "exactly one way" as a **nontrivial condition that sele
cts** which values of $k$ are allowed, the bins must be distinguishable, and we
need:

$$\binom{k}{r} = 1.$$

This holds iff $r = 0$ or $r = k$. Since $r < k$ by definition, the only possibi
lity is $r = 0$, i.e., $k \mid 100$.

**Counting divisors.** We need the number of divisors of 100 that are at least 2
.

$$100 = 2^2 \cdot 5^2, \qquad d(100) = (2+1)(2+1) = 9.$$

The divisors are $\{1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20, 25, 50, 100\}$. Excluding $k = 1$ (the p
roblem requires at least two bins), the valid choices are:

$$k \in \{2, 4, 5, 10, 20, 25, 50, 100\}.$$

**Verification of "exactly one way."** For each such $k$, since $k \mid 100$, we
 have $r = 0$, so every bin holds exactly $100/k$ bearings. There is only $\bino
m{k}{0} = 1$ way. ✓

For a non-divisor, e.g. $k = 3$: $100 = 33\cdot3 + 1$, so one bin holds 34 and t
wo hold 33, giving $\binom{3}{1} = 3 \neq 1$ ways. ✗

**Answer.** The number of different possible quantities of bins the supervisor c
an choose is

$$\boxed{8}.$$
