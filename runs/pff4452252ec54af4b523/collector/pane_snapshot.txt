## Proof

We work in the hyperreal field ${}^*\mathbb{R}$, constructed (for concreteness)
as the ultrapower $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}/\mathcal{U}$ where $\mathcal{U}$ is a
 free ultrafilter on $\mathbb{N}$. The **standard part** $\operatorname{st}(x)$
is defined for every *finite* (limited) hyperreal $x$ as the unique real number
$r$ such that $x - r$ is infinitesimal. In particular,

$$\operatorname{st}(x) = 0 \iff x \text{ is infinitesimal} \iff |x| < \tfrac{1}{
n} \text{ for every standard } n\in\mathbb{N}^{>0}.$$

So the set we must count is the **monad (halo) of $0$**:
$$\mu(0) = \{x \in {}^*\mathbb{R} : \operatorname{st}(x) = 0\}.$$

---

### Upper bound: $|\mu(0)| \le 2^{\aleph_0}$.

The whole hyperreal field has cardinality
$$|{}^*\mathbb{R}| = |\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}| = (2^{\aleph_0})^{\aleph_0} = 2^{
\aleph_0\cdot\aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_0}.$$
Since $\mu(0)\subseteq{}^*\mathbb{R}$, we immediately get $|\mu(0)|\le 2^{\aleph
_0}$.

---

### Lower bound: $|\mu(0)| \ge 2^{\aleph_0}$.

For each real number $r\in\mathbb{R}$, consider the sequence $(a^{(r)}_n)$ with
$a^{(r)}_n = r/n$. Its equivalence class $[a^{(r)}]\in{}^*\mathbb{R}$ is infinit
esimal, since for every fixed $k\in\mathbb{N}^{>0}$,
$$\{n : |r/n| < 1/k\} = \{n : n > |r|k\}$$
is cofinite, hence belongs to the free ultrafilter $\mathcal{U}$. Thus $\operato
rname{st}([a^{(r)}])=0$, i.e. $[a^{(r)}]\in\mu(0)$.

The map $r\mapsto [a^{(r)}]$ is injective: if $r\neq s$, then
$$\{n : r/n = s/n\} = \{n : r = s\} = \varnothing \notin \mathcal{U},$$
so $[a^{(r)}]\neq [a^{(r)}]$ in the ultrapower. Hence $\mu(0)$ contains at least
 $|\mathbb{R}| = 2^{\aleph_0}$ distinct elements.

---

### Conclusion.

Combining the two bounds,
$$|\mu(0)| = 2^{\aleph_0}.$$

Equivalently, the hyperreals with standard part $0$ are **uncountably many** — e
xactly continuum-many. (Indeed every monad $\mu(r)=r+\mu(0)$ has the same cardin
ality $2^{\aleph_0}$, and the finite hyperreals are the disjoint union of the $2
^{\aleph_0}$ many monads indexed by $\mathbb{R}$, consistently giving $2^{\aleph
_0}\cdot 2^{\aleph_0}=2^{\aleph_0}$.)

**The number of hyperreal numbers whose standard part equals $0$ is $\boxed{2^{\
aleph_0}}$ (the cardinality of the continuum).**
