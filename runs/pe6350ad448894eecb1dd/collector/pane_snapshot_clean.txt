## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, the roots of $f(x)$ are solvable by radicals.

**Key facts used:**

1. **Galois correspondence for solvability by radicals.** A polynomial $f(x)\in\
mathbb{Q}[x]$ is solvable by radicals if and only if the Galois group of its spl
itting field over $\mathbb{Q}$ is a *solvable* group. (This is the central theor
em connecting radical solvability to group theory.)

2. **Every $p$-group is solvable.** A finite group whose order is a power of a p
rime $p$ is called a $p$-group, and every $p$-group is solvable.

**Proof of the argument:**

Let $K$ be the splitting field of $f(x)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. By definition $K/\ma
thbb{Q}$ is a Galois extension, and we are given
$$:\mathbb{Q}] = 16.$$

Since $K/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois, the Galois group $G = \mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})
$ has order $|G| = :\mathbb{Q}] = 16 = 2^4$.

Thus $G$ is a $2$-group.

**Proof that every $p$-group is solvable (by induction on $|G|$):**

*Base case:* If $|G| = p$, then $G$ is cyclic, hence abelian, hence solvable.

*Inductive step:* Suppose $|G| = p^n$ with $n \geq 2$. By the class equation, th
e center $Z(G)$ is nontrivial (every $p$-group has a nontrivial center). Choose
an element of order $p$ in $Z(G)$, and let $N = \langle g \rangle$ be the cyclic
 subgroup it generates. Since $N \subseteq Z(G)$, $N$ is normal in $G$. Then:
- $N$ is cyclic of order $p$, hence solvable.
- $G/N$ has order $p^{n-1}$, hence is a $p$-group of smaller order, hence solvab
le by the induction hypothesis.

Since both $N$ and $G/N$ are solvable, $G$ is solvable (solvability is closed un
der group extensions). $\square$

Applying this to our situation: $G = \mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ has order $2^4$
, so $G$ is a $2$-group, hence solvable.

By the Galois-theoretic characterization of solvability by radicals (fact 1), si
nce the Galois group of the splitting field of $f(x)$ is solvable, the roots of
$f(x)$ are solvable by radicals.

**Conclusion:** The roots of $f(x)$ are solvable by radicals. The essential reas
on is that $16 = 2^4$ is a prime power, so the Galois group is a $p$-group, and
every $p$-group is solvable.
