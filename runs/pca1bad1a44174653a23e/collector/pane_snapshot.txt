# Solution

**Answer: No.** A 0-dimensional analytic subset $A$ of a compact complex variety
 $X$ must be finite.

## Proof

**Step 1: $A$ is discrete.**

Recall that the dimension of an analytic set is the supremum of the dimensions o
f its irreducible components. Since $\dim A = 0$, every irreducible component of
 $A$ has dimension $0$. An irreducible analytic set of dimension $0$ is, set-the
oretically, a single point. By the local structure theorem for analytic sets (Gr
auert–Remmert), an analytic subset of a complex space has **locally finitely man
y irreducible components**. Hence, in a neighborhood of any point of $X$, the se
t $A$ consists of only finitely many points. In particular, every point of $A$ i
s isolated in $A$; that is, $A$ is a **discrete** subset of $X$.

**Step 2: $A$ is closed.**

Analytic subsets are closed by definition (they are locally the common zero locu
s of finitely many holomorphic functions, hence closed locally, and closedness i
s local on the target). So $A$ is a closed subset of $X$.

**Step 3: Compactness forces finiteness.**

The underlying topological space of a complex variety (with the analytic topolog
y) is Hausdorff. Since $X$ is compact, it is countably compact; equivalently (fo
r Hausdorff spaces), **every infinite subset has an accumulation point**.

Suppose for contradiction that $A$ is infinite. Then $A$ has an accumulation poi
nt $p \in X$. Since $A$ is closed, $p \in A$. But $A$ is discrete, so $\{p\}$ is
 open in the subspace topology of $A$; i.e., there exists an open neighborhood $
U$ of $p$ in $X$ with $U \cap A = \{p\}$. This means $p$ is **not** an accumulat
ion point of $A$—a contradiction.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

The contradiction shows that $A$ cannot be infinite. Therefore $A$ is a finite s
et of points. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The compactness hypothesis is essential: in the non-compact variety
$\mathbb{C}$, the integers $\mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{C}$ form an infinite $0$-
dimensional analytic subset (each point is the zero locus of $z - n$, locally).
Compactness is precisely what rules out such infinite discrete analytic subsets.
