## Setup and notation

Let $G=(V,E)$ be the mesh graph, with $E$ taken as the set of **directed** edges
 (each undirected edge $\{v,w\}$ contributes both $(v,w)$ and $(w,v)$). For a ve
rtex $v$ write $N(v)=\{w:(v,w)\in E\}$. Define the rest and deformed edge vector
s

$$e_{vw}:=\bar x_v-\bar x_w,\qquad \tilde e_{vw}:=\bar y_v-\bar y_w.$$

The energy is

$$E(R,\bar y)=\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{w\in N(v)}\bigl\|R_v\,e_{vw}-\tilde e_{vw}\bigr
\|_2^2.$$

The constraint $R_v^\top R_v=I$ makes each $R_v$ an orthogonal $2\times2$ matrix
 (rotations **and** reflections are admissible, since $\det R_v$ is not pinned).
 The vertices in $V_0$ are pinned: $\bar y_v=\bar y_v^{\text{fix}}$ for $v\in V_
0$.

The problem is **biconvex**: convex in $\bar y$ with $R$ fixed, and (a Procruste
s problem) in each $R_v$ with $\bar y$ fixed. The standard and globally converge
nt (to a stationary point) approach is the **local/global alternating minimizati
on** of Sorkine & Alexa.

---

## 1. Local step — solve for the rotations $R_v$ (fix $\bar y$)

The terms involving a single $R_v$ are

$$E_v(R_v)=\sum_{w\in N(v)}\bigl\|R_v e_{vw}-\tilde e_{vw}\bigr\|^2.$$

Expand, using $R_v^\top R_v=I\Rightarrow\|R_v e_{vw}\|^2=\|e_{vw}\|^2$:

$$E_v(R_v)=\sum_{w}\bigl(\|e_{vw}\|^2+\|\tilde e_{vw}\|^2\bigr)-2\sum_{w}\tilde
e_{vw}^\top R_v e_{vw}.$$

The first sum is constant in $R_v$, so minimizing $E_v$ is equivalent to **maxim
izing**

$$\Phi(R_v):=\sum_{w\in N(v)}\tilde e_{vw}^\top R_v e_{vw}
=\sum_{w}\operatorname{tr}\!\bigl(R_v\,e_{vw}\tilde e_{vw}^\top\bigr)
=\operatorname{tr}\!\Bigl(R_v\,\underbrace{\sum_{w}e_{vw}\tilde e_{vw}^\top}_{=:
S_v}\Bigr).$$

$S_v$ is the $2\times2$ covariance matrix of the rest and deformed edge neighbor
hoods of $v$. Take its SVD

$$S_v=U_v\Sigma_v V_v^\top,\qquad \Sigma_v=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1,\sigma_2
),\ \sigma_i\ge0.$$

Then

$$\Phi(R_v)=\operatorname{tr}(R_v U_v\Sigma_v V_v^\top)=\operatorname{tr}(V_v^\t
op R_v U_v\,\Sigma_v).$$

Set $Q:=V_v^\top R_v U_v$. Since $V_v,U_v,R_v$ are orthogonal, $Q$ ranges over a
ll orthogonal matrices as $R_v$ does. Thus

$$\Phi=\operatorname{tr}(Q\Sigma_v)=\sum_i Q_{ii}\sigma_i\le\sum_i\sigma_i,$$

because $|Q_{ii}|\le1$ for an orthogonal matrix and $\sigma_i\ge0$. Equality hol
ds iff $Q_{ii}=1$ for every $i$ with $\sigma_i>0$, which is attained by $Q=I$, i
.e.

$$\boxed{\,R_v=V_v\,U_v^\top\,}.$$

(If a proper-rotation restriction $\det R_v=+1$ were additionally imposed, one w
ould instead use $R_v=V_v\,\operatorname{diag}(1,\det(V_vU_v^\top))\,U_v^\top$;
under the stated constraint $R_v^\top R_v=I$ alone, the unconstrained optimum $R
_v=V_vU_v^\top$ is exact.)

This closes the **local step**: each $R_v$ is obtained independently by an SVD o
f a $2\times2$ matrix.

## 2. Global step — solve for the positions $\bar y$ (fix $R$)

With all $R_v$ fixed, $E$ is a convex quadratic in $\bar y$. Differentiate w.r.t
. a free vertex $\bar y_v$ ($v\notin V_0$). The vertex $\bar y_v$ appears in two
 kinds of terms:

* as the **tail** of edge $(v,w)$: $\|R_v e_{vw}-(\bar y_v-\bar y_w)\|^2$, gradi
ent $2(\bar y_v-\bar y_w-R_v e_{vw})$;
* as the **head** of edge $(w,v)$: $\|R_w e_{wv}-(\bar y_w-\bar y_v)\|^2$, gradi
ent $2(\bar y_v-\bar y_w+R_w e_{wv})$.

Using $e_{wv}=-e_{vw}$, summing over $w\in N(v)$ and setting the gradient to zer
o:

$$\sum_{w\in N(v)}\Bigl[(\bar y_v-\bar y_w-R_v e_{vw})+(\bar y_v-\bar y_w+R_w e_
{wv})\Bigr]=0,$$

$$\sum_{w\in N(v)}\Bigl[2(\bar y_v-\bar y_w)-R_v e_{vw}-R_w e_{vw}\Bigr]=0,$$

$$\boxed{\;\sum_{w\in N(v)}(\bar y_v-\bar y_w)=\tfrac12\sum_{w\in N(v)}(R_v+R_w)
\,e_{vw}\;}$$

The left-hand side is exactly the **graph Laplacian** $L$ applied to $\bar y$:

$$(L\bar y)_v=\sum_{w\in N(v)}(\bar y_v-\bar y_w),\qquad L=D-A,$$

with $D$ the degree matrix and $A$ the adjacency matrix of $G$. Defining the rig
ht-hand side

$$b_v=\tfrac12\sum_{w\in N(v)}(R_v+R_w)\,e_{vw},$$

the global step reduces to the **sparse linear system**

$$\boxed{\;L\,\bar y=b,\qquad \bar y_v=\bar y_v^{\text{fix}}\ \text{for }v\in V_
0.\;}$$

$L$ is symmetric positive semi-definite; the pinned vertices $V_0$ remove the nu
ll-space (rigid motions), making the reduced system symmetric positive definite
and uniquely solvable. Because $L$ depends only on the fixed connectivity, it is
 **prefactorized once** (sparse Cholesky) and reused at every iteration — only $
b$ changes.

---

## 3. The local/global algorithm

1. **Initialize** $\bar y$: set $\bar y_v=\bar y_v^{\text{fix}}$ for $v\in V_0$
and initialize the free vertices (e.g. $\bar y_v=\bar x_v$, or with a harmonic/l
inear blend of the handles).
2. **Repeat** until $\|\bar y^{(k+1)}-\bar y^{(k)}\|<\varepsilon$:
   - **Local step.** For every $v\in V$ compute
     $$S_v=\sum_{w\in N(v)}e_{vw}\,\tilde e_{vw}^\top,\qquad S_v=U_v\Sigma_v V_v
^\top\ \text{(SVD)},\qquad R_v\leftarrow V_v U_v^\top.$$
   - **Global step.** Assemble $b_v=\tfrac12\sum_{w\in N(v)}(R_v+R_w)e_{vw}$ and
 solve
     $$L\bar y=b,\quad \bar y|_{V_0}=\bar y^{\text{fix}}.$$

---

## 4. Why this solves the stated problem

* **Optimality of each subproblem.** The local step gives the *global* maximizer
 of $\Phi(R_v)$ over $\{R:R^\top R=I\}$ (Procrustes), so each $R_v$ is exactly o
ptimal given $\bar y$. The global step solves the normal equations of a convex q
uadratic with linear (pinning) constraints, so $\bar y$ is exactly optimal given
 $R$.
* **Monotone descent.** Each step can only decrease (or leave unchanged) the bou
nded-below objective $E\ge0$. Therefore $E^{(k)}$ converges; every accumulation
point is a coordinate-wise minimum, hence a stationary point of the constrained
problem.
* **Constraint satisfaction.** $R_v=V_vU_v^\top$ is orthogonal by construction,
so $R_v^\top R_v=I$ holds at every iteration. Pinning $\bar y_v=\bar y_v^{\text{
fix}}$ on $V_0$ is enforced as a Dirichlet condition in the global solve.
* **Uniqueness of the global step.** With at least one pinned vertex (so $V_0$ r
emoves the two-dimensional rigid-motion null-space of $L$), the reduced system $
L_{\text{free}}\bar y_{\text{free}}=b_{\text{free}}$ is SPD and has a unique sol
ution, so the global step is well-defined.

Thus the alternating local/global procedure computes the as-rigid-as-possible de
formation: rotations that best align each rest neighborhood with its deformed ne
ighborhood, coupled through a Laplacian solve that propagates the handle constra
ints over the mesh while remaining as close to rigid as the local rotations allo
w.
