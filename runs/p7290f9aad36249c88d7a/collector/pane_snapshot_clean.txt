### Mean distance for the circle

For a circle of radius $R$ (area $\pi R^2$, perimeter $2\pi R$), parameterize th
e boundary by angle. Two independent uniform points have angular positions $\the
ta_1, \theta_2 \sim \text{Uniform}[0, 2\pi)$. Their chord length is

$$d = 2R\left|\sin\frac{\theta_1 - \theta_2}{2}\right|.$$

Setting $\varphi = (\theta_1 - \theta_2) \bmod 2\pi$, which is uniform on $[0, 2
\pi)$, and noting $\sin(\varphi/2) \ge 0$ on this interval:

$$\mathbb{E}[d] = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} 2R\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}\,d\varphi
= \frac{R}{\pi}\Big[-2\cos\frac{\varphi}{2}\Big]_0^{2\pi} = \frac{R}{\pi}(2+2) =
 \boxed{\frac{4R}{\pi}}.$$

For area $A$: $R = \sqrt{A/\pi}$, so

$$\mathbb{E}[d] = \frac{4\sqrt{A/\pi}}{\pi} = \frac{4\sqrt{A}}{\pi^{3/2}} \appro
x 0.7183\,\sqrt{A}.$$

### Optimality argument (convex figures)

The circle uniquely minimizes the mean perimeter distance among convex plane fig
ures of given area. The justification rests on two pillars:

**1. Isoperimetric inequality.** The disk uniquely minimizes the perimeter for g
iven area: $P \ge 2\sqrt{\pi A}$, with equality iff the figure is a disk. The di
sk is thus the *most compact* convex figure — its boundary is the least spread-o
ut, packing the most area behind the least boundary.

**2. Circular symmetrization (Schwarz rounding).** Given any convex body $K$ of
area $A$, its circular symmetrization $K^*$ — the concentric disk of area $A$ —
satisfies $M(K^*) \le M(K)$, where $M$ denotes the mean perimeter distance. The
symmetrization replaces the boundary's radial function $\rho(\theta)$ (which var
ies with direction) by the constant radius $R = \sqrt{A/\pi}$, concentrating all
 boundary mass at a single radius while preserving area. This rotational concent
ration minimizes the expected pairwise distance: any angular asymmetry or radial
 variation in the original boundary only *increases* the typical separation betw
een two independent uniform boundary points. Since the disk is the **unique fixe
d point** of circular symmetrization, it is the unique minimizer.

Intuitively: for a fixed area, any deviation from the circle either elongates th
e figure (increasing distances between far-apart boundary points) or adds bounda
ry length without adding area (spreading the same "mass" of boundary over a long
er curve, increasing typical separations). The circle alone avoids both penaltie
s.
