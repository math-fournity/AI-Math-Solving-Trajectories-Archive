## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** The meromorphic continuation of $G(s)$ must have poles on $\Re(
s)=\beta$.

**Key tool: Uniqueness of meromorphic continuation (the identity theorem for mer
omorphic functions).**

If two meromorphic functions agree on a non-empty open subset of their common do
main, they are identical on the entire connected component of that domain.

**Setup.** We are given:
- $G(s)=\int_0^\infty g(x)\,x^{s-1}\,dx$, convergent (hence holomorphic) on the
half-plane $H_+:=\{s:\Re(s)>\beta\}$.
- $K(s)$ is meromorphic on $H_0:=\{s:\Re(s)>0\}$, with simple poles on the line
$\Re(s)=\beta$.
- $G(s)=K(s)$ for all $s\in H_+$.

**Step 1: $K$ is the unique meromorphic continuation of $G$ to $H_0$.**

$G$ is holomorphic on $H_+$, and $K$ is meromorphic on $H_0\supsetneq H_+$. Sinc
e $G=K$ on the open set $H_+$, which has accumulation points inside $H_0$, the i
dentity theorem for meromorphic functions forces $K$ to be the **unique** meromo
rphic continuation of $G$ from $H_+$ to $H_0$. Any meromorphic continuation of $
G$ to $H_0$ must coincide with $K$.

**Step 2: The poles of $K$ on $\Re(s)=\beta$ are genuine poles of the continuati
on.**

By hypothesis, $K$ has simple poles at points on the line $\Re(s)=\beta$. A simp
le pole is a genuine singularity of order 1 — it is not removable. Since the mer
omorphic continuation of $G$ to $H_0$ **is** $K$ (by Step 1), the singularities
of the continuation are exactly the singularities of $K$ on $H_0$.

In particular, every simple pole of $K$ lying on $\Re(s)=\beta$ is a simple pole
 of the meromorphic continuation of $G$.

**Step 3: No cancellation is possible.**

One might ask whether the Mellin-transform origin of $G$ could somehow "remove"
these poles. It cannot: uniqueness of meromorphic continuation is absolute. Ther
e is exactly one meromorphic function on $H_0$ that agrees with $G$ on $H_+$, na
mely $K$. The poles of $K$ on $\Re(s)=\beta$ are intrinsic to this unique contin
uation and cannot be canceled by any property of $g$ — they are already determin
ed by the values of $G$ on $H_+$ via analytic continuation.

**Conclusion.** Since $K$ is the unique meromorphic continuation of $G$ to $\Re(
s)>0$, and $K$ has simple poles on $\Re(s)=\beta$, the meromorphic continuation
of $G(s)$ has poles on $\Re(s)=\beta$. $\blacksquare$
