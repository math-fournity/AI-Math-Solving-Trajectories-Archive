## Solution

**Setup.** Wu starts with 1 coin. Each year, if he has $n$ coins, he flips all $
n$ simultaneously. If $H$ heads appear, his new count is $n + H - (n-H) = 2H$, w
here $H\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(n,\tfrac12)$. He stops when the count reaches $0$ (
i.e., all tails). We want the probability of stopping **exactly at year 5**: ali
ve after years 1–4, then transition to $0$ at year 5.

**Transition rule.** From $n$ coins, the probability of moving to $2j$ coins is
$\binom{n}{j}/2^n$ for $j=0,1,\dots,n$. The $j=0$ case (all tails, probability $
1/2^n$) is the stopping event.

Let $p_t(k)$ = probability Wu has $k>0$ coins after year $t$ and has survived ye
ars $1,\dots,t$.

**Year 1** (from 1 coin): $p_1(2)=\tfrac12$.

**Year 2** (from 2 coins): $p_2(2)=\tfrac14$, $p_2(4)=\tfrac18$.

**Year 3** (from 2, 4 coins):
- $p_3(2)=\tfrac14\cdot\tfrac12+\tfrac18\cdot\tfrac14=\tfrac{5}{32}$
- $p_3(4)=\tfrac14\cdot\tfrac14+\tfrac18\cdot\tfrac{6}{16}=\tfrac{7}{64}$
- $p_3(6)=\tfrac18\cdot\tfrac14=\tfrac{1}{32}$
- $p_3(8)=\tfrac18\cdot\tfrac{1}{16}=\tfrac{1}{128}$

(Check: sum $=\tfrac{39}{128}$; cumulative stopped $=\tfrac12+\tfrac18+\tfrac{9}
{128}=\tfrac{89}{128}$; total $=1$. ✓)

**Year 4** (from 2, 4, 6, 8 coins), using $\binom{n}{j}/2^n$:

| $k$ | $p_4(k)$ (simplified) | $p_4(k)$ (over $2^{15}$) |
|-----|----------------------|--------------------------|
| 2  | $445/4096$   | $3560/32768$ |
| 4  | $723/8192$   | $2892/32768$ |
| 6  | $159/4096$   | $1272/32768$ |
| 8  | $267/16384$  | $534/32768$  |
| 10 | $19/4096$    | $152/32768$  |
| 12 | $11/8192$    | $44/32768$   |
| 14 | $1/4096$     | $8/32768$    |
| 16 | $1/32768$    | $1/32768$    |

Sum $= \tfrac{8463}{32768}$ (alive after year 4). Cross-check: stop at year 4 $=
\sum p_3(k)/2^k = \tfrac{1521}{32768}$; cumulative stopped $=\tfrac{16384+4096+2
304+1521}{32768}=\tfrac{24305}{32768}$; $24305+8463=32768$. ✓

**Year 5 stop probability.** Wu stops at year 5 iff, from each alive state $k$ a
t year 4, he rolls all tails (probability $1/2^k$):

$$P(\text{stop at year 5})=\sum_{k} p_4(k)\cdot\frac{1}{2^k}.$$

Putting everything over $2^{31}$:

| $k$ | term | numerator over $2^{31}$ |
|-----|------|--------------------------|
| 2  | $445/2^{14}$  | $58\,327\,040$ |
| 4  | $723/2^{17}$  | $11\,845\,632$ |
| 6  | $159/2^{18}$  | $1\,302\,528$  |
| 8  | $267/2^{22}$  | $136\,704$     |
| 10 | $19/2^{22}$   | $9\,728$       |
| 12 | $11/2^{25}$   | $704$          |
| 14 | $1/2^{26}$    | $32$           |
| 16 | $1/2^{31}$    | $1$            |

Summing:

$$58\,327\,040+11\,845\,632+1\,302\,528+136\,704+9\,728+704+32+1 = 71\,622\,369.
$$

So

$$P(\text{stop at year 5})=\frac{71\,622\,369}{2^{31}}.$$

The numerator $71\,622\,369$ is odd (ends in 9), so this is already in the form
$\tfrac{a}{2^b}$ with $a$ odd: $a=71\,622\,369$, $b=31$.

$$a+b = 71\,622\,369 + 31 = 71\,622\,400.$$
