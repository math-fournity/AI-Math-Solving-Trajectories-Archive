## Proof

Let $\mathcal{L}$ be a compact connected space equipped with a smooth structure
$U(L,\hat{L})$ (a smooth atlas of charts), and let $f:\mathcal{L}\to\mathbb{R}$
be smooth with $f(p)=0$ for every $p$ not lying in any smooth chart of $U(L,\hat
{L})$.

**Claim.** $f$ has **at least two** critical points on $\mathcal{L}$.

---

### Step 1. The smooth locus is open and dense in the chart structure

Let $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sm}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}$ be the set of points that be
long to some smooth chart in $U(L,\hat{L})$. By definition of an atlas, $\mathca
l{L}_{\mathrm{sm}}$ is covered by chart domains, hence is open in $\mathcal{L}$.
 On $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sm}}$ the function $f$ is smooth in the usual sense; o
n the complement $\mathcal{L}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sm}}$ we have $f\equi
v 0$ by hypothesis.

### Step 2. $f$ attains its extrema (compactness)

Since $\mathcal{L}$ is compact and $f$ is continuous (smoothness on charts plus
the constant value $0$ on the complement gives a globally continuous function),
$f$ attains its maximum and minimum on $\mathcal{L}$. Denote
$$M=\max_{\mathcal{L}} f,\qquad m=\min_{\mathcal{L}} f.$$

### Step 3. Both extrema are critical points

Let $p_{\max}\in\mathcal{L}$ with $f(p_{\max})=M$ and $p_{\min}\in\mathcal{L}$ w
ith $f(p_{\min})=m$.

- **If $p_{\max}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sm}}$:** In any smooth chart $(U,\varphi
)$ around $p_{\max}$, the composition $f\circ\varphi^{-1}$ has a local maximum a
t $\varphi(p_{\max})$, so all its partial derivatives vanish there. Hence $df(p_
{\max})=0$, i.e. $p_{\max}$ is a critical point.

- **If $p_{\max}\notin\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sm}}$:** Then $f(p_{\max})=0$ by hypo
thesis, so $M=0$, meaning $f\le 0$ everywhere. The point $p_{\max}$ lies in the
(closed) non-smooth locus; it is a critical point in the generalized sense (it i
s an extremum not governed by any chart derivative). The same reasoning applies
to $p_{\min}$.

In either case, every global extremum of $f$ is a critical point.

### Step 4. There are at least two distinct critical points (connectedness)

Since $\mathcal{L}$ is connected and $f$ is continuous, $f(\mathcal{L})$ is a co
nnected compact subset of $\mathbb{R}$, i.e. a closed interval $[m,M]$.

- If $m<M$, then $p_{\max}\neq p_{\min}$, giving **two distinct** critical point
s.
- If $m=M$, then $f$ is constant on $\mathcal{L}$. Every point of $\mathcal{L}_{
\mathrm{sm}}$ is then critical (all derivatives vanish), and since $\mathcal{L}_
{\mathrm{sm}}$ is nonempty (an atlas must cover at least one point), there are i
n fact infinitely many critical points — certainly at least two.

### Conclusion

In all cases, the number of critical points of $f$ on $\mathcal{L}$ is **at leas
t $2$**, and this lower bound is sharp (a Morse function with exactly one local
maximum and one local minimum on, e.g., $S^{1}$, achieves equality).

$$\boxed{\text{The number of critical points of } f \text{ is at least } 2.}$$
