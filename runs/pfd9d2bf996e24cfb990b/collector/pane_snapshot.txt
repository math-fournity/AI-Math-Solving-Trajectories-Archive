## Proof

**Step 1: Express the sum via the polylogarithm.**

The polylogarithm is $\operatorname{Li}_s(z) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{z^n}{n^s}
$, valid for $|z|\le 1$, $z\neq 1$, $\operatorname{Re}(s)>0$ (by Dirichlet's tes
t, since partial sums of $z^n$ are bounded and $1/n^s\to 0$ monotonically).

Writing $\cos n = \operatorname{Re}(e^{in})$ with $j:=1$ (radian):

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\cos n}{n^k} = \operatorname{Re}\!\left(\sum_{n=1}^\in
fty \frac{e^{inj}}{n^k}\right) = \operatorname{Re}\!\big(\operatorname{Li}_k(e^{
ij})\big).$$

**Step 2: Show $\operatorname{Li}_s(z) \to \frac{z}{1-z}$ as $s\to 0^+$ for $|z|
=1$, $z\neq 1$.**

Use the integral representation (valid for $\operatorname{Re}(s)>0$, $z\notin[1,
\infty)$):

$$\operatorname{Li}_s(z) = \frac{z}{\Gamma(s)}\int_0^\infty \frac{t^{s-1}}{e^t -
 z}\,dt.$$

Split the integral at $t=1$. The tail $\int_1^\infty \frac{t^{s-1}}{e^t-z}dt$ co
nverges boundedly as $s\to 0$, and is multiplied by $1/\Gamma(s)\to 0$, so it va
nishes.

For the near part, decompose:

$$\frac{1}{e^t-z} = \frac{1}{1-z} + \underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{e^t-z}-\frac{1}{1
-z}\right)}_{=\,O(t)\text{ near }0}.$$

The $O(t)$ remainder gives $\int_0^1 t^{s-1}\cdot O(t)\,dt = O(1)$, which vanish
es when multiplied by $1/\Gamma(s)\sim s\to 0$.

The main term:

$$\frac{z}{\Gamma(s)}\int_0^1 \frac{t^{s-1}}{1-z}\,dt = \frac{z}{\Gamma(s)(1-z)}
\cdot\frac{1}{s} = \frac{z}{s\,\Gamma(s)\,(1-z)}.$$

Since $\Gamma(s) = \frac{1}{s}-\gamma+O(s)$, we have $s\,\Gamma(s)\to 1$ as $s\t
o 0$. Therefore:

$$\lim_{s\to 0^+}\operatorname{Li}_s(z) = \frac{z}{1-z}.$$

**Step 3: Compute the real part.**

With $z = e^{ij}$, $j=1$ (so $z\neq 1$ since $1$ is not a multiple of $2\pi$):

$$\frac{e^{ij}}{1-e^{ij}} = \frac{e^{ij}(1-e^{-ij})}{|1-e^{ij}|^2} = \frac{e^{ij
}-1}{2-2\cos j} = \frac{(\cos j - 1)+i\sin j}{2(1-\cos j)}.$$

Taking the real part:

$$\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\frac{e^{ij}}{1-e^{ij}}\right) = \frac{\cos j - 1}{2(
1-\cos j)} = -\frac{1}{2}.$$

**Step 4: Conclude.**

Combining Steps 1–3:

$$\lim_{k\to 0^+}\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\cos n}{n^k} = \operatorname{Re}\!\left
(\lim_{k\to 0^+}\operatorname{Li}_k(e^{ij})\right) = \operatorname{Re}\!\left(\f
rac{e^{ij}}{1-e^{ij}}\right) = -\frac{1}{2}.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{k\to 0^+}\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\cos n}{n^k} = -\fr
ac{1}{2}}$$
