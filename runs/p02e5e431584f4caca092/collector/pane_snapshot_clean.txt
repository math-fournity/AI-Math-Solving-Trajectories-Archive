## Key Setup

Label vertices $0, 1, \ldots, n-1$ on a circle. Drone $i$ starts at vertex $i$.
A permutation $\sigma$ sends drone $i$ to vertex $\sigma(i)$. A trio $\{i,j,k\}$
 is **stable** if the triangle type (acute/right/obtuse) of $\{i,j,k\}$ equals t
hat of $\{\sigma(i),\sigma(j),\sigma(k)\}$.

A trio's type depends on its gap pattern $(a,b,c)$ with $a+b+c=n$, $a,b,c \geq 1
$:
- **Right** iff some gap $= n/2$ (only possible when $n$ is even; the trio conta
ins a diameter).
- **Obtuse** iff some gap $> n/2$.
- **Acute** iff all gaps $< n/2$.

## Even $n$: All even $n \in S$

**Claim:** For every even $n \geq 4$ and every permutation $\sigma$, there exist
s a stable right trio.

**Proof:** The diameters are $d_j = \{j, j+n/2\}$ for $j=0,\ldots,n/2-1$. A trio
 is right iff it contains a diameter. Define $P_j = \sigma^{-1}(d_j)$; these $n/
2$ pairs partition all vertices. A trio $T$ maps to a right trio under $\sigma$
iff $T$ contains some $P_j$ (since $\sigma(P_j)=d_j$ is a diameter).

For each $P_j = \{x,y\}$:
- **If $P_j$ is a diameter:** $T = P_j \cup \{c\}$ for any $c \notin P_j$ is rig
ht (contains $P_j$) and contains $P_j$, so $\sigma(T)$ is right.
- **If $P_j$ is not a diameter:** $x,y$ are not opposite. Let $d_x = \{x, x+n/2\
}$. Then $y \notin d_x$, so $T = d_x \cup \{y\} = \{x, x+n/2, y\}$ is right (con
tains diameter $d_x$) and contains $P_j = \{x,y\}$, so $\sigma(T)$ is right.

In both cases, a stable right trio exists. $\square$

So $4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14 \in S$.

## Odd $n$: Counting argument

For odd $n$, no right triangles exist — only acute and obtuse. A trio is obtuse
iff one gap $> n/2$, meaning the three vertices lie in an open semicircle. Count
ing obtuse trios: each has a unique "widest gap" starting vertex, giving

$$|\text{Obtuse}| = n \binom{(n-1)/2}{2} = \frac{n(n-1)(n-3)}{8}.$$

$$|\text{Acute}| = \binom{n}{3} - |\text{Obtuse}| = \frac{n(n-1)(n+1)}{24}.$$

These are equal iff $\frac{n-3}{8} = \frac{n+1}{24}$, i.e., $3(n-3)=n+1$, i.e.,
$n=5$.

**For odd $n \neq 5$:** The counts are unequal. Since $\sigma$ is a bijection on
 trios, making all trios unstable requires mapping every obtuse trio to an acute
 one and vice versa — impossible when counts differ. So $n \in S$.

This gives $3, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15 \in S$.

**For $n = 3$:** Only one trio (all three vertices); it's always equilateral/acu
te, and any permutation maps it to itself as a set. Always stable. $3 \in S$. ✓
