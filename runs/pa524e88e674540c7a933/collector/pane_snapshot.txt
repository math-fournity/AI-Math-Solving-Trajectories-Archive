## Proof

**Step 1: Substitution.** Let $I_n = \int_0^e (\log x)^n\,dx$. Set $x = e^t$, $d
x = e^t\,dt$; when $x\to 0^+$, $t\to-\infty$; when $x=e$, $t=1$:

$$I_n = \int_{-\infty}^{1} t^n e^t\,dt.$$

**Step 2: Decompose $I_n$.** Substitute $u = -t$:

$$I_n = (-1)^n\int_{-1}^{\infty} u^n e^{-u}\,du = (-1)^n\left(\int_{-1}^{0} u^n
e^{-u}\,du + \int_0^{\infty} u^n e^{-u}\,du\right).$$

The second integral is $\Gamma(n+1)=n!$. For the first, set $u=-v$:

$$\int_{-1}^{0} u^n e^{-u}\,du = (-1)^n\int_0^1 v^n e^v\,dv.$$

Define $J_n := \int_0^1 v^n e^v\,dv$. Then:

$$\boxed{I_n = (-1)^n\, n! + J_n}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad \frac{I_n}{n!} = (-1)^n
 + \frac{J_n}{n!}.$$

**Step 3: Asymptotics of $J_n$.** Expand $e^v = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{v^k}{k!}
$ and integrate termwise:

$$J_n = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!\,(n+k+1)}.$$

For large $n$, $\frac{1}{n+k+1} = \frac{1}{n}\left(1 - \frac{k+1}{n} + O(n^{-2})
\right)$, giving:

$$J_n = \frac{e}{n} - \frac{2e}{n^2} + O(n^{-3}),$$

using $\sum \frac{1}{k!}=e$ and $\sum \frac{k+1}{k!}=2e$. In particular $J_n \si
m \frac{e}{n}$, so:

$$\frac{J_n}{n!} \sim \frac{e}{n\cdot n!} \to 0, \qquad n\cdot\frac{J_n}{n!} \si
m \frac{e}{n!}\to 0.$$

**Step 4: Evaluate $\left(\frac{I_n}{n!}\right)^n$.** Write:

$$\left(\frac{I_n}{n!}\right)^n = \left((-1)^n + \frac{J_n}{n!}\right)^n = (-1)^
{n^2}\left(1 + (-1)^n\frac{J_n}{n!}\right)^n.$$

Since $n^2\equiv n\pmod{2}$, we have $(-1)^{n^2}=(-1)^n$. Taking logarithms:

$$n\log\!\left(1 + (-1)^n\frac{J_n}{n!}\right) = n\left((-1)^n\frac{J_n}{n!} + O
\!\left(\frac{J_n^2}{(n!)^2}\right)\right) = (-1)^n\frac{n\,J_n}{n!} + O\!\left(
\frac{n\,J_n^2}{(n!)^2}\right).$$

Both terms $\to 0$ (since $nJ_n/n!\sim e/n!\to 0$ and $nJ_n^2/(n!)^2\to 0$). The
refore:

$$\left(1 + (-1)^n\frac{J_n}{n!}\right)^n \longrightarrow e^0 = 1,$$

and:

$$\left(\frac{I_n}{n!}\right)^n = (-1)^n\cdot(1+o(1)) \longrightarrow (-1)^n.$$

**Step 5: Conclusion.** Along even $n$: $\left(\frac{I_n}{n!}\right)^n \to +1$.
Along odd $n$: $\left(\frac{I_n}{n!}\right)^n \to -1$.

*Verification with small values:* $I_0=e,\; I_1=0,\; I_2=e,\; I_3=-2e,\; I_4=9e,
\; I_5=-44e,\; I_6=265e,\; I_7=-1854e$ (recurrence $I_n = e - nI_{n-1}$). Then $
\frac{I_6}{6!}=\frac{53e}{144}\approx 1.00048$ and $\frac{I_7}{7!}=\frac{-103e}{
280}\approx -0.99994$, confirming convergence to $+1$ and $-1$ respectively.

Since the two subsequences converge to different limits, **the limit does not ex
ist**.

$$\boxed{\text{The limit does not exist.}}$$
