## Proof

Let $F$ be an uncountable field and $V = F^n$ an $n$-dimensional vector space ov

### Covering Lemma

**Lemma.** *If $F$ is an infinite field and $n \ge 2$, then $V = F^n$ cannot be
expressed as a union of fewer than $|F|$ proper subspaces.*

**Proof of Lemma.** Consider the $|F|$ points
$$p_a = (1, a, a^2, \dots, a^{n-1}), \quad a \in F.$$
Any $n$ distinct such points $p_{a_1}, \dots, p_{a_n}$ are linearly independent,
 since the matrix with these rows is a Vandermonde matrix with determinant $\pro
d_{i < j}(a_j - a_i) \neq 0$.

Therefore any proper subspace $W \subseteq V$ (of dimension $\le n-1$) contains
**at most $n-1$** of the points $\{p_a : a \in F\}$, for if it contained $n$ of
them, they would be linearly dependent—a contradiction.

Suppose $V = W_1 \cup \cdots \cup W_k$ with $k < |F|$ proper subspaces. Then the
 $|F|$ points $\{p_a\}$ are covered by $W_1, \dots, W_k$, but each $W_i$ contain
s at most $n-1$ of them, so the total covered is at most $k(n-1)$. Since $k < |F
|$, $n-1$ is a fixed finite number, and $|F|$ is infinite, we have $k(n-1) < |F|
$. This contradicts the fact that there are $|F|$ points to cover. $\square$

### Main Construction

**Case $n = 1$:** $V = F$. Take $S = F \setminus \{0\}$, which is uncountable. A

$$B_\alpha = \{0\} \;\cup\; \bigcup_{\substack{W \subseteq S_\alpha \\ |W| = n-1

**The forbidden set does not cover $V$:** Each $\operatorname{span}(W)$ for $|W|

**Distinctness:** When $|S_\alpha| \ge 1$, taking $n-1 = 1$ shows $v_\alpha \not
