## Proof

We wish to evaluate
$$L = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{n!}{n^{\log\log n}}$$
and determine which of $n!$ and $n^{\log\log n}$ grows faster.

**Step 1: Reduce to a comparison of logarithms.** Since $\log$ is increasing and
 maps $(0,\infty)$ onto $\mathbb{R}$, it suffices to study
$$\log\!\left(\frac{n!}{n^{\log\log n}}\right) = \log(n!) - (\log\log n)(\log n)
.$$

**Step 2: Estimate $\log(n!)$ via Stirling's formula.** Stirling's formula gives
$$\log(n!) = n\log n - n + \tfrac12\log(2\pi n) + O(1/n).$$
In particular,
$$\log(n!) = n\log n - n + O(\log n).$$

**Step 3: Form the difference.** Let $a_n = \log(n!) - (\log\log n)(\log n)$. Su
bstituting,
$$a_n = n\log n - n + O(\log n) - (\log\log n)(\log n).$$
Group the leading terms:
$$a_n = \log n\bigl(n - \log\log n\bigr) - n + O(\log n).$$

**Step 4: Show $a_n \to +\infty$.** We prove the stronger statement that $a_n \t
o +\infty$ by exhibiting a lower bound tending to $+\infty$.

Since $\log\log n = o(n)$, for all sufficiently large $n$ we have $n - \log\log
n \ge n/2$. Hence
$$a_n \ge \tfrac{n}{2}\log n - n + O(\log n) = n\!\left(\tfrac{\log n}{2} - 1\ri
ght) + O(\log n).$$
For $n \ge e^2$, $\tfrac{\log n}{2} - 1 \ge 0$, and in fact $\tfrac{\log n}{2} -
 1 \to \infty$, so
$$n\!\left(\tfrac{\log n}{2} - 1\right) \to +\infty,$$
while $O(\log n)$ is negligible in comparison. Therefore
$$a_n \to +\infty.$$

**Step 5: Conclude.** Since $\log\!\left(\frac{n!}{n^{\log\log n}}\right) = a_n
\to +\infty$, exponentiating (and using continuity/increase of $\exp$) gives
$$\frac{n!}{n^{\log\log n}} \to +\infty.$$

**Interpretation of growth rates.** The exponent of $n!$ is governed by $\log(n!

\log n)$. The ratio of these exponents is
$$\frac{n\log n}{(\log\log n)(\log n)} = \frac{n}{\log\log n} \to +\infty,$$
so $n!$ dominates $n^{\log\log n}$ by an overwhelming margin. Equivalently, $n!$
 grows **much faster** than $n^{\log\log n}$ as $n\to\infty$.

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{n!}{n^{\log\log n}} = +\infty, \qquad n! \text{
