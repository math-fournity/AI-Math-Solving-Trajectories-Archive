## Solution

**Setup.** Let the eight numbers be $a_1=1, a_2=4, a_3=1, a_4=2, a_5=0, a_6=7, a
_7=0, a_8=8$, with operators $o_1,\dots,o_7$ chosen independently and uniformly
from $\{+,-,\times\}$.

**Key structural observation.** With standard order of operations, multiplicatio
n binds tighter than $+$ and $-$. The expression decomposes into a sum of **bloc
ks**, where each block is a maximal consecutive run of numbers joined by $\times
$, and blocks are combined left-to-right via $+$/$-$. Crucially, since $+$ and $
-$ have equal precedence and are left-associative, each block's sign is determin
ed **solely** by the operator immediately preceding it (the first block has sign
 $+1$).

**Block decomposition.** A consecutive interval $[j,k]$ forms a block when:
- Internal operators $o_j,\dots,o_{k-1}$ are all $\times$ (probability $(1/3)^{k
-j}$, or $1$ if $k=j$),
- Left boundary $o_{j-1} \in \{+,-\}$ (probability $2/3$), or $j=1$ (no constrai
nt, sign $= +1$),
- Right boundary $o_k \in \{+,-\}$ (probability $2/3$), or $k=8$ (no constraint)
.

**Linearity of expectation.** By linearity:

$$E[\text{result}] = \sum_{1 \le j \le k \le 8} \prod_{i=j}^{k} a_i \cdot E\!\le
ft[\operatorname{sign}(j)\cdot \mathbf{1}([j,k]\text{ is a block})\right]$$

**The vanishing lemma.** For $j > 1$, the sign is $+1$ if $o_{j-1}=+$, $-1$ if $
o_{j-1}=-$, and the block doesn't start at $j$ if $o_{j-1}=\times$. Thus:

$$E\!\left[\operatorname{sign}(j)\cdot \mathbf{1}(o_{j-1}\in\{+,-\})\right] = \t
frac{1}{3}(+1) + \tfrac{1}{3}(-1) + \tfrac{1}{3}(0) = 0$$

So **every block with $j>1$ has expected contribution $0$**. Only blocks startin
g at $j=1$ survive.

**Reduced formula.** With $j=1$ fixed (sign $=+1$, no left-boundary constraint):

$$E[\text{result}] = \sum_{k=1}^{8} \left(\prod_{i=1}^{k} a_i\right) \cdot \left
(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{k-1} \cdot P(\text{right boundary at }k)$$

where $P(\text{right boundary}) = 2/3$ for $k<8$ and $1$ for $k=8$.

**Computation.** Since $a_5 = 0$, all products for $k \ge 5$ vanish:

| $k$ | $\prod_{i=1}^k a_i$ | $(1/3)^{k-1}$ | Right bdy | Term |
|-----|---------------------|---------------|-----------|------|
| 1 | $1$ | $1$ | $2/3$ | $2/3$ |
| 2 | $4$ | $1/3$ | $2/3$ | $8/9$ |
| 3 | $4$ | $1/9$ | $2/3$ | $8/27$ |
| 4 | $8$ | $1/27$ | $2/3$ | $16/81$ |
| 5–8 | $0$ | — | — | $0$ |

**Summation** (common denominator $81$):

$$E = \frac{2}{3} + \frac{8}{9} + \frac{8}{27} + \frac{16}{81} = \frac{54 + 72 +
 24 + 16}{81} = \frac{166}{81}$$

**Verification of the framework.** Checked against the 2-number case ($E = (2a+a
b)/3$) and the 3-number case ($E = (6a+2ab+abc)/9$) by exhaustive enumeration —
both match exactly.

Since $\gcd(166, 81) = 1$ (as $166 = 2 \times 83$ and $81 = 3^4$), the fraction
is already in lowest terms.

$$\boxed{\dfrac{166}{81}}$$
