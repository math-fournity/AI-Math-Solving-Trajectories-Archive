## Characteristic Lines and Classification of the Second-Order Semi-Linear PDE

### The equation

$$a(x_1,x_2)\,u_{x_1x_1} + 2b(x_1,x_2)\,u_{x_1x_2} + c(x_1,x_2)\,u_{x_2x_2} + f(
u,\nabla u) = 0.$$

The **principal part** (highest-order terms) is

$$a\,u_{x_1x_1} + 2b\,u_{x_1x_2} + c\,u_{x_2x_2}.$$

The lower-order term $f(u,\nabla u)$ does **not** affect the characteristics or
the classification, since both depend only on the principal part.

---

### Derivation of the characteristic equation

A **characteristic line** is a curve $\Gamma$ along which the PDE degenerates: t
he second derivatives of $u$ cannot be uniquely determined from the data on $\Ga
mma$. We look for a change of variables that reveals this degeneracy.

Let $\varphi(x_1,x_2)=\text{const}$ define a family of curves. Set new coordinat
es

$$\xi = \varphi(x_1,x_2), \qquad \eta = \eta(x_1,x_2) \quad (\text{arbitrary, in
dependent of }\xi).$$

By the chain rule the second derivatives transform as

$$u_{x_1x_1} = \varphi_{x_1}^2\,u_{\xi\xi} + \cdots,$$
$$u_{x_1x_2} = \varphi_{x_1}\varphi_{x_2}\,u_{\xi\xi} + \cdots,$$
$$u_{x_2x_2} = \varphi_{x_2}^2\,u_{\xi\xi} + \cdots,$$

where "$\cdots$" denotes terms involving $u_{\xi\eta}, u_{\eta\eta}, u_\xi, u_\e
ta$ (lower-order in $\xi$). The coefficient of $u_{\xi\xi}$ in the transformed P
DE is therefore

$$a\,\varphi_{x_1}^2 + 2b\,\varphi_{x_1}\varphi_{x_2} + c\,\varphi_{x_2}^2.$$

A curve $\varphi=\text{const}$ is **characteristic** precisely when this coeffic
ient vanishes, i.e., the PDE no longer determines $u_{\xi\xi}$ from data on the
curve:

$$\boxed{\;a\,\varphi_{x_1}^2 + 2b\,\varphi_{x_1}\varphi_{x_2} + c\,\varphi_{x_2
}^2 = 0.\;}$$

This is the **characteristic equation** (in implicit/PDE form).

---

### Characteristic equation in differential form

Let the characteristic curve be parametrized as $x_2 = x_2(x_1)$. Along $\varphi
(x_1,x_2)=\text{const}$ we have $d\varphi = 0$, so

$$\varphi_{x_1}\,dx_1 + \varphi_{x_2}\,dx_2 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{
\varphi_{x_1}}{\varphi_{x_2}} = -\frac{dx_2}{dx_1}.$$

Denoting $x_2' = dx_2/dx_1$ and substituting $\varphi_{x_1} = -\varphi_{x_2}\,x_
2'$ into the characteristic equation (assuming $\varphi_{x_2}\neq 0$):

$$a\,x_2'^{\,2}\,\varphi_{x_2}^2 - 2b\,x_2'\,\varphi_{x_2}^2 + c\,\varphi_{x_2}^
2 = 0,$$

which gives the **ODE form of the characteristic equation**:

$$\boxed{\;a\,(x_2')^2 - 2b\,x_2' + c = 0,\qquad x_2'=\frac{dx_2}{dx_1}.\;}$$

Equivalently, in symmetric differential form:

$$\boxed{\;a\,(dx_2)^2 - 2b\,dx_1\,dx_2 + c\,(dx_1)^2 = 0.\;}$$

Solving the quadratic for the slope $x_2'$:

$$x_2' = \frac{b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - ac}}{a}.$$

---

### Classification via the discriminant

The nature of the characteristic curves is governed by the **discriminant**

$$\boxed{\;\Delta := b^2 - ac.\;}$$

Three cases arise.

#### Case 1: $\Delta = b^2 - ac > 0$ — **Hyperbolic**

The square root $\sqrt{b^2-ac}$ is real and nonzero, so there are **two distinct
 real characteristic directions** at every point:

$$x_2' = \frac{b + \sqrt{b^2-ac}}{a}, \qquad x_2' = \frac{b - \sqrt{b^2-ac}}{a}.
$$

Two distinct families of real characteristic curves exist. Using them as coordin
ates $(\xi,\eta)$ reduces the PDE to **canonical form**

$$u_{\xi\eta} = F(\xi,\eta,u,u_\xi,u_\eta),$$

the classical hyperbolic canonical form (e.g., the wave equation $u_{tt}-c^2u_{x
x}=0$).

#### Case 2: $\Delta = b^2 - ac = 0$ — **Parabolic**

The two roots coincide: $x_2' = b/a$ (single real root, multiplicity 2). There i
s **one** family of real characteristic curves (with a double direction). The PD
E reduces to **canonical form**

$$u_{\eta\eta} = F(\xi,\eta,u,u_\xi,u_\eta),$$

the classical parabolic canonical form (e.g., the heat equation $u_t - u_{xx}=0$
).

#### Case 3: $\Delta = b^2 - ac < 0$ — **Elliptic**

The square root is purely imaginary; there are **no real characteristic curves**
 (the characteristics are complex). No real characteristic coordinates exist. Th
e PDE reduces to **canonical form**

$$u_{\xi\xi} + u_{\eta\eta} = F(\xi,\eta,u,u_\xi,u_\eta),$$

the classical elliptic canonical form (e.g., Laplace's equation $\Delta u = 0$).

---

### Summary table

| Discriminant $\Delta=b^2-ac$ | Type | Real characteristics | Canonical form |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\Delta>0$ | Hyperbolic | Two distinct families | $u_{\xi\eta}=F$ |
| $\Delta=0$ | Parabolic | One (double) family | $u_{\eta\eta}=F$ |
| $\Delta<0$ | Elliptic | None (complex) | $u_{\xi\xi}+u_{\eta\eta}=F$ |

Since $a,b,c$ depend on $(x_1,x_2)$, the type can in principle vary from point t
o point (mixed-type equations); the classification above is **local**, applied a
t each point of the domain.
