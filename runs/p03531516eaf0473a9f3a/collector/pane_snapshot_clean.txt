Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether for every bounded open set
$V \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ ($n > 1$), one can find infinitely many pairwise disj
oint open sets $C_n \subseteq \overline{V}$ with $\partial C_n \subseteq \partia
l(\overline{V})$.

**Answer: No.**

**Proof.**

We provide a counterexample. Let $V = B(0,1)$, the open unit ball in $\mathbb{R}
^n$ (for any $n > 1$). Then:
- $\overline{V} = \bar{B}(0,1)$, the closed unit ball.
- $\text{int}(\overline{V}) = B(0,1) = V$.
- $\partial(\overline{V}) = S^{n-1}$, the unit sphere.

Suppose $C$ is an open set (in $\mathbb{R}^n$) satisfying $C \subseteq \overline
{V}$ and $\partial C \subseteq \partial(\overline{V}) = S^{n-1}$.

**Step 1: $C \subseteq B(0,1)$.** Since $C$ is open in $\mathbb{R}^n$ and $C \su
bseteq \bar{B}(0,1)$, every point of $C$ is an interior point of $\bar{B}(0,1)$,
 so $C \subseteq \text{int}(\bar{B}(0,1)) = B(0,1)$.

**Step 2: $C$ is closed in $B(0,1)$.** The condition $\partial C \subseteq S^{n-
1}$ means $\overline{C} \setminus C \subseteq S^{n-1}$. Since $\overline{C} \sub
seteq \bar{B}(0,1)$ (as $\bar{B}(0,1)$ is closed and contains $C$), we have:
$$\overline{C} \cap B(0,1) = (C \cup \partial C) \cap B(0,1) = C \cap B(0,1) = C
,$$
since $\partial C \cap B(0,1) \subseteq S^{n-1} \cap B(0,1) = \emptyset$. This s
hows $C$ is closed in the subspace topology of $B(0,1)$.

**Step 3: $C$ is clopen in $B(0,1)$.** Since $C$ is open in $\mathbb{R}^n$ and $
C \subseteq B(0,1)$, $C$ is open in $B(0,1)$. By Step 2, $C$ is also closed in $
B(0,1)$. Thus $C$ is clopen in $B(0,1)$.

**Step 4: $C \in \{\emptyset, B(0,1)\}$.** The open ball $B(0,1)$ is connected (
for all $n \geq 1$), so its only clopen subsets are $\emptyset$ and $B(0,1)$ its
elf.

**Conclusion.** The only open sets $C$ satisfying $C \subseteq \overline{V}$ and
 $\partial C \subseteq \partial(\overline{V})$ are $\emptyset$ and $B(0,1)$. Sin
ce $B(0,1)$ is the unique non-empty option, we cannot find even two pairwise dis
joint non-empty such sets, let alone an infinite sequence.

Therefore, it is **not** possible in general. $\blacksquare$
