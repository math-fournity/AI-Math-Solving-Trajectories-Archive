## Proof

**Key result (sign of the discriminant).** Let $K/\mathbb{Q}$ be a number field
of degree $n$ with $r_1$ real embeddings and $2r_2$ complex embeddings ($n = r_1
 + 2r_2$). Then the sign of the discriminant of any basis of $K$ is $(-1)^{r_2}$
.

*Proof of the key result.* Let $\{\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n\}$ be a basis and l
et $\sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_n$ be the $n$ embeddings $K \hookrightarrow \mathbb
{C}$, ordered so that $\sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_{r_1}$ are real and the remainin
g come in conjugate pairs $(\sigma_{r_1+1}, \overline{\sigma_{r_1+1}}), \ldots,
(\sigma_{r_1+r_2}, \overline{\sigma_{r_1+r_2}})$. Set $M = (\sigma_j(\alpha_i))$
 (rows indexed by $i$, columns by $j$) and $\Delta = \det(M)$. The discriminant
is
$$D = \det(\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha_i \alpha_j)) = \det(M^T M) = \Delta^2.$$
Since $D$ is rational, $\Delta^2 \in \mathbb{R}$. Now $\overline{\Delta} = \det(
\overline{M})$, and $\overline{M}$ is obtained from $M$ by conjugating every ent
ry, which swaps each conjugate pair of columns. This requires $r_2$ transpositio
ns, so
$$\overline{\Delta} = (-1)^{r_2} \Delta.$$
- If $r_2$ is even, $\overline{\Delta} = \Delta$, so $\Delta \in \mathbb{R}$ and
 $D = \Delta^2 > 0$: **positive**.
- If $r_2$ is odd, $\overline{\Delta} = -\Delta$, so $\Delta$ is purely imaginar
y, say $\Delta = iy$ with $y \in \mathbb{R}$, and $D = -y^2 < 0$: **negative**.

Hence $\operatorname{sgn}(D) = (-1)^{r_2}$. $\square$

---

**Application to $K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$ with $\zeta$ a primitive $p^r$-th root
of unity.**

The degree is $:\mathbb{Q}] = \varphi(p^r) = p^{r-1}(p-1)$.

**Real vs. complex embeddings.** For $p^r \geq 3$, every conjugate $\zeta^a$ (wi
th $\gcd(a, p^r) = 1$) satisfies $|\zeta^a| = 1$ and $\zeta^a \neq \pm 1$, hence
 is non-real. So $K$ is **totally complex**: $r_1 = 0$ and
$$r_2 = \frac{\varphi(p^r)}{2}.$$
(The exceptional case $p^r = 2$, i.e., $p=2, r=1$, gives $\zeta = -1$ and $K = \
mathbb{Q}$, with $r_2 = 0$ and discriminant $1 > 0$.)

By the key result, for $p^r \geq 3$:
$$\boxed{\operatorname{sgn}(D) = (-1)^{\varphi(p^r)/2}.}$$

---

**Explicit evaluation.**

*Case $p$ odd.* Then $\varphi(p^r) = p^{r-1}(p-1)$, and since $p-1$ is even,
$$\frac{\varphi(p^r)}{2} = p^{r-1} \cdot \frac{p-1}{2}.$$
Since $p^{r-1}$ is odd, the parity of $\varphi(p^r)/2$ is the parity of $(p-1)/2
$:
- $p \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$: $(p-1)/2$ is even → $\operatorname{sgn}(D) = +1$ (**pos
itive**).
- $p \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$: $(p-1)/2$ is odd → $\operatorname{sgn}(D) = -1$ (**nega
tive**).

*Case $p = 2$.* Then $\varphi(2^r) = 2^{r-1}$:
- $r = 1$: $K = \mathbb{Q}$, $r_2 = 0$ → $\operatorname{sgn}(D) = +1$ (**positiv
e**, $D = 1$).
- $r = 2$: $\varphi(4) = 2$, $r_2 = 1$ → $\operatorname{sgn}(D) = (-1)^1 = -1$ (
**negative**, $D = -4$).
- $r \geq 3$: $r_2 = 2^{r-2}$ is even → $\operatorname{sgn}(D) = +1$ (**positive
**).

---

**Summary.** The sign of the discriminant of the power basis $\{1, \zeta, \ldots
, \zeta^{\varphi(p^r)-1}\}$ of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]$ is
$$(-1)^{\varphi(p^r)/2},$$
i.e., **positive** when $p \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$ (or $p = 2$ with $r \neq 2$), and
**negative** when $p \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$ (or $p = 2, r = 2$).
