## Example

Let $X = D^2$ be the closed unit disk in $\mathbb{R}^2$, and let $A = S^1 = \par
tial D^2$ be its boundary circle, equipped with the subspace topology.

## Justification

**Step 1: Compute $\pi_1(A)$.** The circle $S^1$ has fundamental group $\pi_1(S^
1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by the homotopy class of the loop $\gamma(t) = (
\cos 2\pi t, \sin 2\pi t)$ for $t \in [0,1]$.

**Step 2: Compute $\pi_1(X)$.** The closed disk $D^2$ is convex (hence contracti
ble). Therefore $\pi_1(D^2) = 0$, the trivial group.

**Step 3: Identify the induced homomorphism.** The inclusion $i: S^1 \hookrighta
rrow D^2$ sends every loop in $S^1$ to the same loop regarded as a loop in $D^2$
. The induced map on fundamental groups is

$$i_*: \pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z} \longrightarrow \pi_1(D^2) = 0.$$

Since the codomain is the trivial group, $i_*$ must be the zero homomorphism: $i
_*(n) = 0$ for every $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

**Step 4: Show $i_*$ is not injective.** The kernel of $i_*$ is all of $\mathbb{
Z}$:

$$\ker(i_*) = \mathbb{Z} \neq \{0\}.$$

In particular, the generator $1 \in \mathbb{Z}$ (the class of the loop $\gamma$
going once around $S^1$) is a nontrivial element mapped to the identity. Concret
ely, $\gamma$ is not null-homotopic *within* $S^1$, but once we allow the homoto
py to range over the entire disk $D^2$, the linear homotopy

$$H(s,t) = (1-s)\,\gamma(t) + s\cdot(0,0)$$

contracts $\gamma$ to the constant loop at the origin. Thus $i_*([\gamma]) = 0$
while $[\gamma] \neq 0$ in $\pi_1(S^1)$.

Therefore $i_*: \pi_1(A) \to \pi_1(X)$ is **not injective**. $\blacksquare$
