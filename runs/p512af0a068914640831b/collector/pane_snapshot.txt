## Proof

We work in the category of abelian groups (equivalently, $\mathbb{Z}$-modules).

**Key facts.**

1. **Injective $\Leftrightarrow$ divisible.** By Baer's criterion applied to the
 PID $\mathbb{Z}$ (whose only ideals are $n\mathbb{Z}$), an abelian group $G$ is
 injective if and only if it is *divisible*: for every $g \in G$ and every posit
ive integer $n$, there exists $g' \in G$ with $n g' = g$.

2. **Quotients of divisible groups are divisible.** Let $D$ be a divisible abeli
an group and $N \le D$ any subgroup. For any coset $d + N \in D/N$ and any posit
ive integer $n$, divisibility of $D$ gives $d' \in D$ with $n d' = d$. Then in $
D/N$:
$$n(d' + N) = nd' + N = d + N.$$
Hence $D/N$ is divisible.

**Applying to the problem.**

Since $f: H \to G$ is a monomorphism, $f(H)$ is a subgroup of $G$ isomorphic to
$H$. Since $G$ is injective, by fact (1) $G$ is divisible. The quotient $G/f(H)$
 is therefore a quotient of the divisible group $G$ by the subgroup $f(H)$. By f
act (2), $G/f(H)$ is divisible.

**Conclusion.** Yes — the quotient group $G/f(H)$ is divisible. $\blacksquare$
