## Proof

Let $f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}$ be analytic with $f(0)=0$, and suppose $f(\mathb
b{D}_r)=\mathbb{D}_r$ for some $0<r<1$, where $\mathbb{D}_r=\{z:|z|<r\}$.

**Step 1: Schwarz Lemma setup.** Since $f(0)=0$ and $f(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\math
bb{D}$, the Schwarz Lemma gives $|f(z)|\le|z|$ for all $z\in\mathbb{D}$. Define
$h(z)=f(z)/z$, which extends to an analytic function on $\mathbb{D}$ (since $f(0
)=0$). The Schwarz Lemma gives $|h(z)|\le 1$ for all $z\in\mathbb{D}$.

**Step 2: $f(\overline{\mathbb{D}_r})=\overline{\mathbb{D}_r}$.** From $|f(z)|\l
e|z|\le r$ for $|z|\le r$, we get $f(\overline{\mathbb{D}_r})\subseteq\overline{
\mathbb{D}_r}$. Since $f$ is continuous, $f(\overline{\mathbb{D}_r})$ is compact
. It contains $f(\mathbb{D}_r)=\mathbb{D}_r$, so being a closed set containing $
\mathbb{D}_r$, it contains $\overline{\mathbb{D}_r}$. Thus $f(\overline{\mathbb{
D}_r})=\overline{\mathbb{D}_r}$.

**Step 3: Existence of a boundary point with $|f(z_0)|=r$.** We have
$$f(\overline{\mathbb{D}_r})=f(\mathbb{D}_r)\cup f(\partial\mathbb{D}_r)=\mathbb
{D}_r\cup f(\partial\mathbb{D}_r)=\overline{\mathbb{D}_r}=\mathbb{D}_r\cup\parti
al\mathbb{D}_r.$$
Since $\mathbb{D}_r\cap\partial\mathbb{D}_r=\varnothing$ and $f(\partial\mathbb{
D}_r)\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{D}_r}$, taking the set difference with $\mathbb{
D}_r$ yields $f(\partial\mathbb{D}_r)\setminus\mathbb{D}_r=\partial\mathbb{D}_r$
, so $\partial\mathbb{D}_r\subseteq f(\partial\mathbb{D}_r)$.

Therefore there exists $z_0\in\partial\mathbb{D}_r$ with $|f(z_0)|=r$.

**Step 4: Applying the Maximum Modulus Principle.** At this point $z_0$:
$$|h(z_0)|=\frac{|f(z_0)|}{|z_0|}=\frac{r}{r}=1.$$
Since $r<1$, we have $z_0\in\partial\mathbb{D}_r\subset\mathbb{D}$, so $z_0$ is
an **interior point** of $\mathbb{D}$. The function $|h|$ achieves its maximum v
alue $1$ at an interior point of $\mathbb{D}$. By the Maximum Modulus Principle,
 $h$ is constant.

**Step 5: Conclusion.** Since $h\equiv c$ is constant with $|c|=1$ (as $|h(z_0)|
=1$), we conclude
$$f(z)=cz\quad\text{for all }z\in\mathbb{D},\quad|c|=1.$$

**Verification.** Any rotation $f(z)=cz$ with $|c|=1$ satisfies all conditions:
$f(0)=0$, $f(\mathbb{D})=\mathbb{D}$, and $f(\mathbb{D}_r)=\mathbb{D}_r$ for eve
ry $r$. $\blacksquare$
