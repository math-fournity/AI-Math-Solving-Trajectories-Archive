Define $f(z) = z(z^3+z^2+rz+1) = z^4+z^3+rz^2+z$. A real number $r$ is *repetiti
ve* iff there exist distinct $z_1,z_2$ on the unit circle, not equal to $\{-i,i\
}$, with $f(z_1)=f(z_2)$.

**Preliminary observation.** $f(i)=i(-i-1+ri+1)=i\cdot i(r-1)=1-r$ and $f(-i)=-i
(i-1-ri+1)=-i\cdot i(1-r)=1-r$. So $\{i,-i\}$ satisfies $f(z_1)=f(z_2)$ for *eve
ry* $r$ — exactly the pair being excluded.

**Symmetric reduction.** Write $z_1=e^{i\alpha}, z_2=e^{i\beta}$, $\psi=\tfrac{\
alpha+\beta}{2}$, $\phi=\tfrac{\alpha-\beta}{2}$, so $s=z_1+z_2=2\cos\phi\cdot e
^{i\psi}$, $p=z_1z_2=e^{2i\psi}$, with $\rho:=2\cos\phi\in(-2,2)\setminus\{0\}$
(the value $\rho=0$ gives $s=0$, i.e. $z_2=-z_1$, which forces $p=1$ and hence $
\{z_1,z_2\}=\{i,-i\}$, the excluded case).

Dividing $f(z_1)-f(z_2)=0$ by $z_1-z_2$ and using $s,p$:
$$s^3+s^2+rs+1-p(2s+1)=0.$$

**Case A: $2s+1\neq 0$.** Then $p=\frac{s^3+s^2+rs+1}{2s+1}$. Using $p=s/\bar s$
 (from $|z_1|=|z_2|=1$) and substituting $s=\rho e^{i\psi}$, $u=e^{i\psi}$, this
 becomes the cubic in $u$:
$$\rho(\rho^2-2)\,u^3+(\rho^2-1)\,u^2+r\rho\,u+1=0,\qquad |u|=1. \tag{$\ast$}$$

Since the coefficients are real, a root on the unit circle is either $u=\pm1$, o
r a non-real conjugate pair $u_2,u_3$ with $u_2u_3=1$.

**Case A1: non-real conjugate pair on the unit circle.** With $a_3=\rho(\rho^2-2
), a_2=\rho^2-1, a_1=r\rho, a_0=1$, the condition $a_3^2-a_1a_3=a_0^2-a_0a_2$ gi
ves
$$\rho^2(\rho^2-2)(\rho^2-2-r)=-(\rho^2-2).$$
For $\rho^2\neq 2$: $r=\frac{(\rho^2-1)^2}{\rho^2}$. Setting $t=\rho^2\in(0,4)\s
etminus\{2\}$, $r=h(t)=t+1/t-2$. The sum $u_2+u_3=-1/\rho$, so distinct non-real
 roots on the circle require $|1/\rho|<2$, i.e. $t>1/4$. On $(1/4,4)\setminus\{2
\}$, $h$ has minimum $0$ at $t=1$ and approaches $9/4$ at both $t\to 1/4^+$ and
$t\to 4^-$. This yields $r\in[0,9/4)$.

**Case A2: $u=1$.** Substituting into ($\ast$): $\rho(\rho^2+\rho+r-2)=0$, so $r
=2-\rho-\rho^2$. On $\rho\in(-2,2)\setminus\{0\}$ this ranges over $(-4,9/4]$ (m
aximum $9/4$ at $\rho=-1/2$; approaching $0$ at $\rho\to-2$; approaching $-4$ at
 $\rho\to2$). The pair $\{e^{i\phi},e^{-i\phi}\}$ with $\cos\phi=\rho/2$ is dist
inct, on the unit circle, and not $\{i,-i\}$ since $\rho\neq0$.

**Case A3: $u=-1$.** Substituting: $r=2+\rho-\rho^2$, which by symmetry also ran
ges over $(-4,9/4]$.

**Case B: $2s+1=0$, i.e. $s=-1/2$.** Then $s^3+s^2+rs+1=0$ gives $-\tfrac18+\tfr
ac14-\tfrac r2+1=0$, i.e. $r=\tfrac94$. Here $p=1$, $z_{1,2}=\tfrac{-1\pm i\sqrt
{15}}{4}$, with $|z_{1,2}|^2=\tfrac{16}{16}=1$, distinct, and not $\{i,-i\}$. So
 $r=\tfrac94$ is repetitive. (This is the degenerate endpoint of Case A2 at $\rh
o=-1/2$.)

**Combining.** The union of all cases is
$$[0,9/4)\;\cup\;(-4,9/4]\;\cup\;(-4,9/4]\;\cup\;\{9/4\}=(-4,9/4].$$

**Boundary verification.**
- $r=9/4$: achieved (Case A2 with $\rho=-1/2$, or Case B). Repetitive. ✓ (closed
).
- $r=-4$: Case A2 needs $\rho=2$ (excluded, gives $z_1=z_2$) or $\rho=-3$ (out o
f range); Case A3 needs $\rho=-2$ (excluded) or $\rho=3$ (out of range); Case A1
 has $h(t)\geq 0$. Not repetitive. ✓ (open).

Therefore $a=-4$ and $b=\tfrac94$, so
$$|a|+|b|=4+\tfrac94=\tfrac{25}{4}.$$
