Looking at this problem, I need to find all real $m$ such that $x^2+xy+y^2=m$ in
tersects $xy=n$ **only** at the two vertices of the hyperbola, for each nonzero
integer $n \in [-81, 81]$.

## Setting Up the Intersection

Substituting $xy = n$ into the energy equation:
$$x^2 + y^2 + n = m \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^2 + y^2 = m - n$$

So intersection points satisfy both $xy = n$ and $x^2 + y^2 = R^2$ where $R^2 =
m - n$.

## Analyzing the Intersection

From the two equations:
$$(x+y)^2 = R^2 + 2n, \qquad (x-y)^2 = R^2 - 2n$$

For real solutions, both right-hand sides must be $\geq 0$, requiring $R^2 \geq
2|n|$.

**Case $R^2 > 2|n|$ (i.e., $m - n > 2|n|$):** Both $(x+y)^2 > 0$ and $(x-y)^2 >
0$, yielding **4** intersection points — too many.

**Case $R^2 < 2|n|$ (i.e., $m - n < 2|n|$):** One squared term is negative — **n
o** real intersection. Doesn't satisfy "makes contact."

**Case $R^2 = 2|n|$ (i.e., $m - n = 2|n|$):** Exactly one of $(x+y)^2$ or $(x-y)
^2$ equals zero, giving exactly **2** intersection points.

- **$n > 0$:** $(x-y)^2 = 0 \Rightarrow x = y$; then $2x^2 = 2n \Rightarrow x =
\pm\sqrt{n}$. Points $(\sqrt{n},\sqrt{n})$ and $(-\sqrt{n},-\sqrt{n})$ — precise
ly the **vertices** of $xy = n$. ✓
- **$n < 0$:** $(x+y)^2 = 0 \Rightarrow x = -y$; then $2x^2 = -2n \Rightarrow x
= \pm\sqrt{|n|}$. Points $(\sqrt{|n|},-\sqrt{|n|})$ and $(-\sqrt{|n|},\sqrt{|n|}
)$ — precisely the **vertices** of $xy = n$. ✓

(The vertices of $xy = n$ are the points on the hyperbola closest to the origin,
 which occur at $x = y$ for $n>0$ and $x = -y$ for $n<0$.)

## The Unique $m$ for Each $n$

The condition $m - n = 2|n|$ gives:
$$m = n + 2|n| = \begin{cases} 3n & \text{if } n > 0 \\ -n = |n| & \text{if } n
< 0 \end{cases}$$

## Computing the Total Sum

**Positive $n$ ($n = 1, 2, \ldots, 81$):** $m = 3n$
$$\sum_{n=1}^{81} 3n = 3 \cdot \frac{81 \cdot 82}{2} = 3 \cdot 3321 = 9963$$

**Negative $n$ ($n = -1, -2, \ldots, -81$):** $m = |n| = -n$
$$\sum_{n=-1}^{-81} (-n) = \sum_{k=1}^{81} k = \frac{81 \cdot 82}{2} = 3321$$

**Total sum:**
$$9963 + 3321 = 13284$$

## Verification with Examples

- $n = 1$: $m = 3$. Then $x^2+y^2 = 2$, $xy=1$ $\Rightarrow$ $(x-y)^2=0$, $x=y=\
pm1$. Two vertices. ✓
- $n = -1$: $m = 1$. Then $x^2+y^2 = 2$, $xy=-1$ $\Rightarrow$ $(x+y)^2=0$, $x=-
y=\pm1$. Two vertices. ✓

## Result

The total sum is $13284$, whose last three digits are:

$$\boxed{284}$$
