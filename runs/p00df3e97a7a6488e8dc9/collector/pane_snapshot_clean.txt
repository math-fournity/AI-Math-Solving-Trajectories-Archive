## Solution

Let me set up the problem. Let $\angle A = \angle D = \theta$.

**Triangle Alpha:** $AB = 20$, $BC = 13$, $AC = x$.
**Triangle Delta:** $DE = 20$, $EF = 13$, $DF = y$.

Given: $x - y = 10$.

### Applying the Law of Cosines at the shared angle

For Triangle Alpha, the angle $\theta$ is at vertex $A$, between sides $AB$ and
$AC$:

$$BC^2 = AB^2 + AC^2 - 2 \cdot AB \cdot AC \cdot \cos\theta$$
$$169 = 400 + x^2 - 40x\cos\theta \implies \cos\theta = \frac{x^2 + 231}{40x}$$

For Triangle Delta, the angle $\theta$ is at vertex $D$, between sides $DE$ and
$DF$:

$$EF^2 = DE^2 + DF^2 - 2 \cdot DE \cdot DF \cdot \cos\theta$$
$$169 = 400 + y^2 - 40y\cos\theta \implies \cos\theta = \frac{y^2 + 231}{40y}$$

### Equating the two expressions for $\cos\theta$

$$\frac{x^2 + 231}{x} = \frac{y^2 + 231}{y}$$

Cross-multiplying:

$$y(x^2 + 231) = x(y^2 + 231)$$
$$x^2 y - xy^2 = 231x - 231y$$
$$xy(x - y) = 231(x - y)$$

Since $x - y = 10 \neq 0$, we can divide by $(x-y)$:

$$xy = 231$$

### Solving the system

We have $x - y = 10$ and $xy = 231$. Substituting $x = y + 10$:

$$(y+10)y = 231 \implies y^2 + 10y - 231 = 0$$
$$y = \frac{-10 \pm \sqrt{100 + 924}}{2} = \frac{-10 \pm 32}{2}$$

Taking the positive root: $y = 11$, so $x = 21$.

**Verification:** $AC - DF = 21 - 11 = 10$ ✓, and $xy = 231$ ✓.

Triangle Alpha sides $(20, 13, 21)$ satisfy the triangle inequality: $20+13>21$,
 $20+21>13$, $13+21>20$ ✓.

### Computing the area

$$\cos\theta = \frac{x^2 + 231}{40x} = \frac{441 + 231}{840} = \frac{672}{840} =
 0.8$$

$$\sin\theta = \sqrt{1 - 0.64} = 0.6$$

The area of Triangle Alpha (using the two sides enclosing $\theta$, namely $AB$
and $AC$):

$$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot AB \cdot AC \cdot \sin\theta = \tfrac{1}{2} \
cdot 20 \cdot 21 \cdot 0.6 = 126$$
