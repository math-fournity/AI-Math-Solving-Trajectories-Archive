# Problem

An equilateral triangle $ABC$ with side length $a$ is fixed. A square $ABPQ$ is initially placed such that its side $AB$ coincides with the side $AB$ of the triangle, and the square is outside the triangle. The square "rolls" around the triangle $ABC$ in a counterclockwise direction without slipping. One "full round" consists of the square rolling until its side $AB$ (which might not be the original side $AB$) again coincides with the original side $AB$ of the triangle.

Suppose the rolling process is continued until each of the four vertices $A, B, P, Q$ of the square first returns to its original position in space.
1. How many full rounds of the triangle $ABC$ are performed?
2. How many full rotations of the square relative to its own center $M$ are performed in total?

Let $n$ be the number of rounds and $r$ be the number of rotations. Find $10n + r$.
