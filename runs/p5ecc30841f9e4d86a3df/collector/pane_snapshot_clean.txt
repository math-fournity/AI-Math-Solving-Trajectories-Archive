## Proof

**Answer: No.** The condition $\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\alpha\,P[X>\alpha]=0$ does
 **not** imply $E[X]<\infty$.

We construct an explicit counterexample.

**Construction.** Define a positive random variable $X$ via its survival functio
n
$$
P[X > x] = \begin{cases} 1, & 0 \le x \le e,\\[4pt] \dfrac{e}{x\log x}, & x > e.
 \end{cases}
$$
This is a valid tail: it is non-increasing, right-continuous, equals $1$ at $x=0
$, tends to $0$ as $x\to\infty$, and is continuous at $x=e$ since $\frac{e}{e\lo
g e}=\frac{e}{e}=1$.

**Verifying the hypothesis.** For $\alpha > e$,
$$
\alpha\,P[X>\alpha] = \alpha\cdot\frac{e}{\alpha\log\alpha}=\frac{e}{\log\alpha}
\xrightarrow[\alpha\to\infty]{}0.
$$
So the condition $\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\alpha\,P[X>\alpha]=0$ is satisfied.

**Computing the expectation.** For a non-negative random variable,
$$
E[X]=\int_0^\infty P[X>x]\,dx.
$$
Splitting the integral,
$$
E[X]=\int_0^e 1\,dx+\int_e^\infty \frac{e}{x\log x}\,dx = e + e\int_e^\infty \fr
ac{dx}{x\log x}.
$$
The remaining integral diverges:
$$
\int_e^\infty \frac{dx}{x\log x}=\Big[\log\log x\Big]_e^\infty = \lim_{x\to\inft
y}\log\log x - \log\log e = \infty - 0 = \infty.
$$
Therefore $E[X]=\infty$.

**Conclusion.** We have exhibited a positive random variable $X$ with $\lim_{\al
pha\to\infty}\alpha\,P[X>\alpha]=0$ yet $E[X]=\infty$. Hence the condition does
not imply finite expectation.

**Remark.** The condition $\alpha\,P[X>\alpha]\to 0$ is *necessary* for $E[X]<\i
nfty$ (since $\alpha\,P[X>\alpha]\le\int_\alpha^\infty P[X>t]\,dt\to 0$ when $E[
X]<\infty$), but as shown above it is not *sufficient*. The gap is that $\alpha\
,P[X>\alpha]\to 0$ only controls the pointwise decay of the tail, whereas $E[X]<
\infty$ requires the *integrability* of the tail $P[X>t]$ over $[0,\infty)$, a s
trictly stronger requirement.
