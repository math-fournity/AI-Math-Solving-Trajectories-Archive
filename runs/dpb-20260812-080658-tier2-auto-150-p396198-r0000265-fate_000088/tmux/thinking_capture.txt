Proof that $|\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})| = p-1$

order $p$ under addition.

Step 1: Identify the generators of $G$

The group $G = { \bar{0}, \bar{1}, \bar{2}, \dots, \overline{p-1} }$ is cyclic
of order $p$. An element $\bar{a} \in G$ is a generator (i.e., has order $p$) if
and only if $\gcd(a, p) = 1$.

Proof of this fact: The order of $\bar{a}$ in $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ is $p /
\gcd(a, p)$. Since $p$ is prime, $\gcd(a, p) = 1$ for every $a \in {1, 2, \dots,
p-1}$, and $\gcd(0, p) = p$. Hence every nonzero element $\bar{a}$ with $1 \le a
\le p-1$ has order $p$ and is a generator, while $\bar{0}$ has order $1$.

So $G$ has exactly $p - 1$ generators: $\bar{1}, \bar{2}, \dots,
\overline{p-1}$.

Step 2: An automorphism is determined by the image of a generator

Since $G$ is cyclic, any homomorphism $\varphi: G \to G$ is completely
determined by $\varphi(\bar{1})$, because every element $\bar{a} = a \cdot
\bar{1}$, so $\varphi(\bar{a}) = a \cdot \varphi(\bar{1})$.

A homomorphism $\varphi: G \to G$ is an automorphism if and only if it is
bijective. Since $G$ is finite, bijectivity is equivalent to injectivity, which
is equivalent to $\varphi$ being surjective, which is equivalent to $
\varphi(\bar{1})$ being a generator of $G$.

From Step 1, the generators are exactly $\bar{1}, \bar{2}, \dots,
\overline{p-1}$ — there are $p - 1$ of them.

is surjective because $\bar{a}$ is a generator, so $\varphi_a(\bar{1}) =
\bar{a}$ generates $G$. Hence $\varphi_a$ is an automorphism.

Conversely, every automorphism $\varphi$ equals $\varphi_a$ where $a$ is the
representative of $\varphi(\bar{1})$, by Step 2. The map $a \mapsto \varphi_a$
