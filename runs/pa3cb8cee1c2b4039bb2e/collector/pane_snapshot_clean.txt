# Boundary of $S^1 \times I^2$

## Setup and notation

Let $S^1$ denote the unit circle (a compact 1-manifold without boundary) and let
 $I = [0,1]$, so $I^2 = [0,1]\times[0,1]$ is the closed unit square (a compact 2
-manifold with boundary). The product $S^1 \times I^2$ is a compact 3-manifold w
ith boundary, and we determine its boundary.

## Key fact: boundary of a product of manifolds

For manifolds with boundary $M$ (dimension $m$) and $N$ (dimension $n$), the pro
duct $M\times N$ is an $(m+n)$-manifold with boundary, and

$$\partial(M \times N) \;=\; (\partial M \times N)\;\cup\;(M \times \partial N).
$$

This follows from the local model: near a boundary point, $M$ looks like $\mathb
b{H}^m = \{x_m \ge 0\}$ and $N$ looks like $\mathbb{H}^n = \{y_n \ge 0\}$, so $M
\times N$ looks like $\mathbb{H}^m\times\mathbb{H}^n = \{(x,y): x_m\ge 0,\; y_n\
ge 0\}$. A point of this set is a boundary point (lies in $\{x_m=0\}\cup\{y_n=0\
}$, i.e. has a neighborhood modeled on $\mathbb{H}^{m+n}$ rather than $\mathbb{R
}^{m+n}$) exactly when $x_m = 0$ **or** $y_n = 0$. This gives the union decompos
ition above.

## Applying the fact

Take $M = S^1$ and $N = I^2$.

- $S^1$ is a 1-manifold **without** boundary, so $\partial S^1 = \emptyset$.
- $I^2 = [0,1]^2$ is a 2-manifold with boundary
  $$\partial I^2 = (\{0\}\times I)\cup(\{1\}\times I)\cup(I\times\{0\})\cup(I\ti
mes\{1\}),$$
  i.e. the four edges of the square. This is a piecewise-linear simple closed cu
rve, hence
  $$\partial I^2 \cong S^1.$$

Substituting into the product formula:

$$\partial(S^1 \times I^2) = (\underbrace{\partial S^1}_{=\emptyset}\times I^2)\
;\cup\;(S^1 \times \partial I^2) = \emptyset \;\cup\; (S^1 \times \partial I^2)
= S^1 \times \partial I^2.$$

Since $\partial I^2 \cong S^1$,

$$\partial(S^1 \times I^2) \;\cong\; S^1 \times S^1 \;=\; T^2.$$

## Geometric sanity check

The square $I^2$ is homeomorphic to the closed disk $D^2$ (e.g. by radial projec
tion from the center), so

$$S^1 \times I^2 \;\cong\; S^1 \times D^2,$$

which is the **solid torus**. The boundary of the solid torus is well known to b
e the 2-torus $T^2 = S^1\times S^1$, confirming the computation.

## Conclusion

$$\boxed{\;\partial(S^1 \times I^2) \;=\; S^1 \times \partial I^2 \;\cong\; S^1
\times S^1 \;=\; T^2\;}$$

The boundary of $S^1 \times I^2$ is the 2-torus $T^2$.
