## Proof

**Setup and the one-dimensionality of $\Lambda^n(V)$.**

Let $V$ be an $n$-dimensional real vector space. The space $\Lambda^n(V)$ of alt
ernating $n$-fold multilinear maps on $V$ is one-dimensional: $\dim \Lambda^n(V)
 = 1$. Concretely, fixing a basis $e_1,\dots,e_n$ of $V$, the determinant
$$\det(v_1,\dots,v_n) := \sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\,v_{\sig
ma(1)}^1\cdots v_{\sigma(n)}^n$$
(where $v_i = \sum_j v_i^j e_j$) is a nonzero element of $\Lambda^n(V)$, hence a
 basis. Therefore every $T\in\Lambda^n(V)$ satisfies
$$T(v_1,\dots,v_n) = \alpha\cdot \det(v_1,\dots,v_n)$$
for a unique scalar $\alpha = T(e_1,\dots,e_n)$.

**Geometric meaning of $\alpha$ in general.**

The determinant $\det(v_1,\dots,v_n)$ is the **signed (oriented) volume** of the
 parallelepiped $\mathcal{P}(v_1,\dots,v_n)$ spanned by $v_1,\dots,v_n$, normali
zed so that the unit parallelepiped $\mathcal{P}(e_1,\dots,e_n)$ has volume $1$.
 Hence $T = \alpha\det$ measures signed volume **scaled by** $\alpha$:
$$T(v_1,\dots,v_n) = \alpha\cdot \operatorname{Vol}_{\text{or}}\bigl(\mathcal{P}
(v_1,\dots,v_n)\bigr).$$
Equivalently, $\alpha = T(e_1,\dots,e_n)$ is the **oriented volume that $T$ assi
gns to the reference unit parallelepiped** — i.e. the **volume-scaling factor**
(the "density") of the form $T$ relative to the standard volume form $\det$. It
is the single degree of freedom in $\Lambda^n(V)\cong\mathbb{R}$, encoding how m
uch $T$ stretches or shrinks (and possibly reverses) oriented volume.

**The specific $T$ defined by a single linear functional $\varphi:V\to\mathbb{R}
$.**

Now consider the particular form
$$T(v_1,\dots,v_n) := \sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\,\varphi(v_
{\sigma(1)})\cdots\varphi(v_{\sigma(n)}).$$
This is exactly the Leibniz formula for the determinant of the $n\times n$ matri
x $A$ whose every row is the same row vector $\bigl(\varphi(v_1),\dots,\varphi(v
_n)\bigr)$:
$$A_{ij} = \varphi(v_j), \qquad i=1,\dots,n.$$
Indeed,
$$\det A = \sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\,A_{1\sigma(1)}\cdots
A_{n\sigma(n)} = \sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\,\varphi(v_{\sig
ma(1)})\cdots\varphi(v_{\sigma(n)}) = T(v_1,\dots,v_n).$$
Since all $n$ rows of $A$ are identical, $A$ has rank $\le 1 < n$, and so
$$\det A = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad T(v_1,\dots,v_n) = 0 \quad\text{for all }
 v_1,\dots,v_n\in V.$$
(Equivalently, $T$ is alternating, and substituting $v_i = v_j$ for any $i\neq j
$ leaves the summands merely permuted, forcing $T=0$; or: $T$ is multilinear and
 alternating in arguments that all enter through the *same* scalar $\varphi$, so
 it vanishes by the defining property of alternating maps once two arguments coi
ncide after projection.)

Therefore $T$ is the **zero** $n$-form, and comparing with $T = \alpha\det$ give
s
$$\alpha = 0.$$

**Geometric interpretation of this $\alpha = 0$.**

A single linear functional $\varphi:V\to\mathbb{R}$ projects every vector onto a
 **one-dimensional** quotient — it records only *one* scalar "measurement" per v
ector. The form $T$ attempts to build an $n$-dimensional oriented volume out of
$n$ such measurements, but since all $n$ measurements come from the *same* proje
ction $\varphi$, the $n$ "rows" of data are linearly dependent (in fact identica
l). Oriented $n$-volume requires $n$ **independent** directions of measurement;
a single projection collapses $V$ onto a line, annihilating all $n$-dimensional
volume content. The vanishing $\alpha = 0$ is precisely the geometric statement
that **no nonzero $n$-dimensional oriented volume can be recovered from a single
 scalar projection** — the parallelepiped's volume, as seen through one linear f
unctional, is degenerate and collapses to zero.

**Conclusion.**

In the relation $T(v_1,\dots,v_n)=\alpha\det(v_1,\dots,v_n)$, the scalar $\alpha
$ is, in general, the **oriented volume that $T$ assigns to the unit reference p
arallelepiped** — the **volume-scaling factor** (density) of the alternating $n$
-form $T$ relative to the standard volume form, the unique coordinate of $T$ in
the one-dimensional space $\Lambda^n(V)\cong\mathbb{R}$. For the specific $T$ bu
ilt from a single linear functional $\varphi$, the associated matrix has identic
al rows, so $T\equiv 0$ and consequently $\alpha = 0$: geometrically, a single p
rojection $\varphi:V\to\mathbb{R}$ collapses $n$-dimensional oriented volume to
zero, since $n$ independent measurement directions are needed to capture $n$-vol
ume but only one is provided.
