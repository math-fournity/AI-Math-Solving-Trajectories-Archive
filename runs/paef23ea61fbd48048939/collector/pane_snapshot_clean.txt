## Derivation of the Euler-Lagrange Equations for Multivector Fields

### Setup

Let $\{\psi_i\}$ be multivector fields over some region $\Omega \subset \mathbb{
R}^n$, and let $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}(\psi_i,\, a\cdot\nabla\,\psi_i)$ be a
scalar-valued Lagrangian density depending on the fields and their directional d
erivatives along a fixed vector $a$. The action is

$$S = \int_\Omega \mathcal{L}\bigl(\psi_i,\, a\cdot\nabla\,\psi_i\bigr)\,d^nx.$$

We vary each field $\psi_i \to \psi_i + \varepsilon\,\delta\psi_i$ with $\delta\
psi_i$ arbitrary (within the grade subspace of $\psi_i$) and vanishing on $\part
ial\Omega$.

### Key properties of geometric algebra

Three properties underpin the derivation and ensure that non-commutativity intro
duces no extra terms:

1. **Symmetry of the scalar part.** For any multivectors $A, B$:
$$\langle AB\rangle_0 = \langle BA\rangle_0,$$
since $\langle A_r B_s\rangle_0 = 0$ unless $r = s$, and the scalar product $A_r
 * B_r := \langle A_r B_r\rangle_0$ is symmetric.

2. **Exact Leibniz rule.** The directional derivative $a\cdot\nabla$ is a deriva
tion on the geometric product:
$$a\cdot\nabla(AB) = (a\cdot\nabla\, A)\,B + A\,(a\cdot\nabla\, B).$$
There are no additional terms (unlike, e.g., commutator corrections).

3. **Grade preservation.** The operator $a\cdot\nabla$ maps grade-$r$ multivecto
rs to grade-$r$ multivectors, so $\langle\, a\cdot\nabla\, M\,\rangle_0 = a\cdot
\nabla\,\langle M\rangle_0$.

4. **Non-degeneracy of the scalar product.** If $\langle \delta\psi\, M\rangle_0
 = 0$ for all admissible $\delta\psi$ (of the same grade structure as $\psi$), t
hen $M = 0$.

### Variation of the Lagrangian

For a scalar-valued function $\mathcal{L}$ of multivector arguments, the multive
ctor derivative $\partial_{\psi_i}\mathcal{L}$ is defined by

$$\delta\mathcal{L} = \Big\langle \delta\psi_i\;\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\part
ial\psi_i}\Big\rangle_0,$$

where $\partial_{\psi_i}\mathcal{L}$ is a multivector of the same grade structur
e as $\psi_i$. Applying this to both arguments:

$$\delta\mathcal{L} = \Big\langle \delta\psi_i\;\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\part
ial\psi_i}\Big\rangle_0 + \Big\langle \delta(a\cdot\nabla\,\psi_i)\;\frac{\parti
al\mathcal{L}}{\partial(a\cdot\nabla\,\psi_i)}\Big\rangle_0.$$

Since $a$ and $\nabla$ are independent of the variation, $\delta(a\cdot\nabla\,\
psi_i) = a\cdot\nabla(\delta\psi_i)$. Define

$$P_i := \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(a\cdot\nabla\,\psi_i)},$$

a multivector field. The variation of the action is

$$\delta S = \int_\Omega \Big[\Big\langle \delta\psi_i\;\frac{\partial\mathcal{L
}}{\partial\psi_i}\Big\rangle_0 + \big\langle (a\cdot\nabla\,\delta\psi_i)\, P_i
 \big\rangle_0\Big]\,d^nx.$$

### Integration by parts

Apply the Leibniz rule to the product $\delta\psi_i\, P_i$:

$$a\cdot\nabla(\delta\psi_i\, P_i) = (a\cdot\nabla\,\delta\psi_i)\,P_i + \delta\
psi_i\,(a\cdot\nabla\, P_i).$$

Rearranging and taking the scalar part (using grade preservation, property 3):

$$\big\langle (a\cdot\nabla\,\delta\psi_i)\, P_i \big\rangle_0 = a\cdot\nabla\,\
big\langle \delta\psi_i\, P_i \big\rangle_0 - \big\langle \delta\psi_i\,(a\cdot\
nabla\, P_i)\big\rangle_0.$$

The first term on the right is a total directional derivative of a scalar. Integ
rating over $\Omega$:

$$\int_\Omega a\cdot\nabla\,\big\langle \delta\psi_i\, P_i \big\rangle_0\,d^nx =
 \oint_{\partial\Omega} \langle \delta\psi_i\, P_i\rangle_0\,(a\cdot\hat{n})\,dS
 = 0,$$

since $\delta\psi_i\big|_{\partial\Omega} = 0$. Therefore:

$$\int_\Omega \big\langle (a\cdot\nabla\,\delta\psi_i)\, P_i \big\rangle_0\,d^nx
 = -\int_\Omega \big\langle \delta\psi_i\,(a\cdot\nabla\, P_i)\big\rangle_0\,d^n
x.$$

### Assembling the variation

Substituting back:

$$\delta S = \int_\Omega \Big\langle \delta\psi_i\;\bigg[\frac{\partial\mathcal{
L}}{\partial\psi_i} - a\cdot\nabla\, P_i\bigg]\Big\rangle_0\,d^nx = \int_\Omega
\Big\langle \delta\psi_i\;\bigg[\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\psi_i} - a\c
dot\nabla\!\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(a\cdot\nabla\,\psi_i)}\righ
t)\bigg]\Big\rangle_0\,d^nx.$$

Here we used the linearity of the scalar part to factor $\delta\psi_i$ out of bo
th terms. This factoring is valid because $\langle \delta\psi_i\, A\rangle_0 - \
langle \delta\psi_i\, B\rangle_0 = \langle \delta\psi_i\,(A - B)\rangle_0$.

### Fundamental lemma

Requiring $\delta S = 0$ for all admissible variations $\delta\psi_i$ (arbitrary
 in the interior, vanishing on the boundary), and invoking the non-degeneracy of
 the scalar product (property 4), the integrand must vanish for each $i$:

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\psi_i} - a\cdot\nabla\!\left(\frac{
\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(a\cdot\nabla\,\psi_i)}\right) = 0 \qquad \text{for
 each } i.}$$

### Remarks on non-commutativity

Despite the geometric product being non-commutative, the Euler-Lagrange equation
s take the **same form** as in the commutative (scalar field) case. This is beca
use:

- The **scalar part** $\langle\cdot\rangle_0$ is symmetric, so the ordering of $
\delta\psi_i$ and the derivative of $\mathcal{L}$ does not affect the variation.
- The **Leibniz rule** for $a\cdot\nabla$ on the geometric product is exact—no c
ommutator corrections arise—so integration by parts proceeds without extra terms
.
- The **grade-preserving** nature of $a\cdot\nabla$ ensures the total-derivative
 term is a pure boundary term that vanishes under the boundary condition.

The result is a multivector equation (one equation per grade component of $\psi_
i$), generalizing the classical scalar Euler-Lagrange equation to multivector fi
elds in geometric algebra.
