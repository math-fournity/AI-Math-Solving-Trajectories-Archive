## Answer: **True**

A closed compact 2-manifold embedded in Euclidean 3-space is always orientable.

## Proof

Let $M \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ be a closed (compact, without boundary) 2-manifold,
 embedded in $\mathbb{R}^3$. We show $M$ is orientable.

**Step 1: Reduction to connected components.**

Write $M = M_1 \sqcup \cdots \sqcup M_k$ as a disjoint union of its connected co
mponents. Each $M_i$ is itself a compact, connected, embedded 2-manifold without
 boundary in $\mathbb{R}^3$. If each $M_i$ is orientable, then $M$ is orientable
. So it suffices to prove the result for each connected component, and we hencef
orth assume $M$ is connected.

**Step 2: The Jordan–Brouwer separation theorem.**

The Jordan–Brouwer separation theorem states: if $\Sigma \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ i
s a compact, connected, embedded $(n{-}1)$-manifold without boundary, then $\mat
hbb{R}^n \setminus \Sigma$ has exactly two connected components — a bounded comp
onent (the *interior*) and an unbounded component (the *exterior*) — and $\Sigma
$ is the boundary of each.

Applying this with $n = 3$ and $\Sigma = M$, we obtain:
$$\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus M = \Omega_{\text{int}} \sqcup \Omega_{\text{ext}},$$
where $\Omega_{\text{int}}$ is bounded, $\Omega_{\text{ext}}$ is unbounded, and
$\partial \Omega_{\text{int}} = \partial \Omega_{\text{ext}} = M$.

**Step 3: Constructing a global normal field.**

Since $M$ is embedded in $\mathbb{R}^3$, at every point $p \in M$ the tangent pl
ane $T_p M$ is a 2-dimensional subspace of $\mathbb{R}^3$, and its orthogonal co
mplement is 1-dimensional. Thus there are exactly two unit normal vectors at $p$
, say $\pm \nu(p)$, varying continuously in any local patch (because locally $M$
 is a graph, and the normal to a graph is continuous).

We now make a *global* choice. Define $\nu(p)$ to be the unit normal at $p$ poin
ting toward the exterior $\Omega_{\text{ext}}$, i.e., for small $\varepsilon > 0
$,
$$p + \varepsilon\, \nu(p) \in \Omega_{\text{ext}}.$$

This is well-defined because the two sides of $M$ at $p$ correspond to the two c
omponents $\Omega_{\text{int}}$ and $\Omega_{\text{ext}}$ (a consequence of the
separation theorem: locally, $M$ separates a small ball into two halves, one bel
onging to each global component). The assignment $p \mapsto \nu(p)$ is continuou
s, since locally the normal varies continuously and the choice of "exterior side
" is consistent across overlapping patches (the exterior is a single connected c
omponent). Thus $\nu: M \to S^2$ is a **continuous global unit normal field**.

**Step 4: A global normal field implies orientability.**

We use the standard equivalence: *a 2-manifold embedded in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is ori
entable if and only if it admits a continuous global unit normal field.*

($\Rightarrow$) If $M$ is orientable, a consistent choice of orientation, combin
ed with the embedding, yields a global normal via the right-hand rule.

($\Leftarrow$) Given a continuous global normal field $\nu$, define an orientati
on on $M$ as follows. At each $p \in M$, declare an ordered basis $(v, w)$ of $T
_p M$ to be *positively oriented* if and only if $(v, w, \nu(p))$ is a positivel
y oriented basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$. Since $\nu$ is continuous and globally defin
ed, this orientation varies continuously and is consistent on overlaps of coordi
nate patches — there is no way for the orientation to "flip" when traversing a l
oop, because $\nu$ returns to itself (not to its negative) by continuity and glo
bal single-valuedness. Hence $M$ is orientable.

**Conclusion.**

By Steps 3 and 4, each connected component $M_i$ admits a continuous global norm
al field (via the Jordan–Brouwer separation) and is therefore orientable. Since
every component is orientable, $M$ itself is orientable.

Therefore, every closed compact 2-manifold embedded in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is orienta
ble. $\blacksquare$
