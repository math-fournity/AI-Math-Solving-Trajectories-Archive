# Classification of Splittings up to $A$-Conjugacy

## Setup and the $Q$-module structure on $A$

Since $A$ is abelian and $\iota(A)=\ker\pi\trianglelefteq G$, conjugation by any
 $g\in G$ with $\pi(g)=q$ induces an automorphism of $\iota(A)\cong A$. This aut
omorphism depends only on $q$: if $g'=\iota(a)\,g$ also satisfies $\pi(g')=q$, t
hen conjugation by $g'$ on $\iota(A)$ equals conjugation by $g$ (since $\iota(A)
$ is abelian, $\iota(a)$ acts trivially by conjugation on $\iota(A)$). Thus $Q$
acts on $A$ by $q\cdot a := s(q)\,a\,s(q)^{-1}$ (transported to $A$ via $\iota$)
, well-defined independently of any choice. This makes $A$ a $\mathbb{Z}[Q]$-mod
ule.

## Splittings ↔ 1-cocycles

Assume at least one splitting $s_0:Q\to G$ exists. Given any splitting $s$, for
each $q\in Q$ both $s(q)$ and $s_0(q)$ lie over $q$, so
$$s(q)\,s_0(q)^{-1}\in\ker\pi=\iota(A).$$
Define $f_s:Q\to A$ by $s(q)=\iota(f_s(q))\,s_0(q)$.

**Claim:** $f_s\in Z^1(Q,A)$, i.e. $f_s(q_1q_2)=f_s(q_1)+q_1\cdot f_s(q_2)$.

*Proof.* From $s(q_1q_2)=s(q_1)s(q_2)$:
$$\iota(f_s(q_1q_2))\,s_0(q_1q_2)=\iota(f_s(q_1))\,s_0(q_1)\,\iota(f_s(q_2))\,s_
0(q_2).$$
Moving $s_0(q_1)$ past $\iota(f_s(q_2))$:
$$s_0(q_1)\,\iota(f_s(q_2))\,s_0(q_1)^{-1}=\iota(q_1\cdot f_s(q_2)),$$
so the right side equals $\iota\bigl(f_s(q_1)+q_1\cdot f_s(q_2)\bigr)\,s_0(q_1q_
2)$. Comparing gives the cocycle identity. $\square$

Conversely, given $f\in Z^1(Q,A)$, define $s_f(q)=\iota(f(q))\,s_0(q)$. The same
 computation in reverse shows $s_f$ is a homomorphism with $\pi\circ s_f=\mathrm
{id}$, hence a splitting. These constructions are mutually inverse, so:

> **(Bijection 1)** Fixing $s_0$, the map $s\mapsto f_s$ is a bijection $\{\text
{splittings}\}\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} Z^1(Q,A)$.

## $A$-conjugacy ↔ 1-coboundaries

Two splittings $s,s'$ are $A$-**conjugate** if $\exists\, a\in A$ with $s'(q)=\i
ota(a)\,s(q)\,\iota(a)^{-1}$ for all $q$. (Since $A$ is abelian, this is a genui
ne action of $A$ on the set of splittings.)

Compute the effect of conjugating $s_f$ by $\iota(a)$:
$$\iota(a)\,s_f(q)\,\iota(a)^{-1}=\iota(a)\,\bigl(s_0(q)\,\iota(a)^{-1}s_0(q)^{-
1}\bigr)\,s_0(q)=\iota\bigl(a-q\cdot a\bigr)\,\iota(f(q))\,s_0(q).$$
So conjugation by $a$ sends $f$ to $f+(a-q\cdot a)$. The map $a\mapsto\bigl(q\ma
psto a-q\cdot a\bigr)$ has image $\{-(q\cdot b-b):b\in A\}=B^1(Q,A)$, the subgro
up of **1-coboundaries** $\delta^0(b)(q)=q\cdot b-b$.

Therefore $s_f$ and $s_{f'}$ are $A$-conjugate $\iff$ $f'-f\in B^1(Q,A)$.

> **(Bijection 2)** Under Bijection 1, $A$-conjugacy classes of splittings corre
spond to cosets in $Z^1(Q,A)/B^1(Q,A)=H^1(Q,A)$.

## Main Theorem

**Theorem.** Let $1\to A\xrightarrow{\iota}G\xrightarrow{\pi}Q\to 1$ be a short
exact sequence with $A$ abelian, and let $Q$ act on $A$ by conjugation through t
he extension. If the set of splittings is nonempty, then:

1. The set of splittings is a **torsor** (principal homogeneous space) for $Z^1(
Q,A)$.
2. The set of $A$-conjugacy classes of splittings is a **torsor for $H^1(Q,A)=Z^
1(Q,A)/B^1(Q,A)$**.

In particular, fixing any splitting $s_0$ yields a canonical bijection
$$\{A\text{-conjugacy classes of splittings}\}\;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\;H^1(Q,A)
,\qquad [s]\mapsto[f_s],$$
where $f_s\in Z^1(Q,A)$ is the unique cocycle with $s(q)=\iota(f_s(q))\,s_0(q)$.

If no splitting exists, the set of splittings (and hence of $A$-conjugacy classe
s) is empty.

## Summary

The $A$-conjugacy classes of splittings of the extension are classified by the *
*first group cohomology** $H^1(Q,A)$, where $Q$ acts on $A$ via the conjugation
action induced by the extension. The correspondence is: choose a base splitting
$s_0$; every other splitting differs from $s_0$ by a unique 1-cocycle $f\in Z^1(
Q,A)$, and two splittings are $A$-conjugate precisely when their cocycles differ
 by a 1-coboundary. Thus the classification set is $H^1(Q,A)$ (a torsor, canonic
ally trivialized once a splitting is chosen).
