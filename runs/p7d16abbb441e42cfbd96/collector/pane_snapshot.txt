## Claim

The series converges if and only if $\alpha > \dfrac{k^2}{2}$.

---

## Proof

### Step 1: Reduction to an integral

Set $g(x_1,\dots,x_k) = (x_1^2+\cdots+x_k^2)^{-\alpha/k}$, defined on $(0,\infty
)^k$. This function is positive and **decreasing in each variable** on $[1,\inft
y)^k$. For every lattice point $(n_1,\dots,n_k)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^k$ and every $
(x_1,\dots,x_k)$ in the unit cube $Q_{\mathbf n}=[n_1,n_1{+}1]\times\cdots\times
[n_k,n_k{+}1]$, monotonicity gives

$$
g(n_1{+}1,\dots,n_k{+}1)\;\le\; g(x_1,\dots,x_k)\;\le\; g(n_1,\dots,n_k).
$$

Integrating over $Q_{\mathbf n}$ (volume $1$) and summing over all $\mathbf n\in
\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^k$:

$$
\sum_{\mathbf n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^k} g(n_1{+}1,\dots,n_k{+}1)\;\le\;\int_{[1,\in
fty)^k} g(\mathbf x)\,d\mathbf x\;\le\;\sum_{\mathbf n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^k} g(n_
1,\dots,n_k).
$$

The left sum is a translate of the original series (indices shifted by $1$), so
it converges iff the original series converges. Hence **the series converges $\i
ff$ the integral $I:=\int_{[1,\infty)^k}(x_1^2+\cdots+x_k^2)^{-\alpha/k}\,d\math
bf x$ converges.**

### Step 2: Spherical-coordinate analysis of the integral

Pass to $k$-dimensional spherical coordinates: $r^2 = x_1^2+\cdots+x_k^2$, $d\ma
thbf x = r^{k-1}\,dr\,d\sigma$ where $d\sigma$ is the surface measure on the uni
t sphere $S^{k-1}$. The integrand becomes $r^{-2\alpha/k}$, so

$$
I \;=\; \int_{S^{k-1}_+}\int_{\rho(\theta)}^{\infty} r^{\,k-1-2\alpha/k}\,dr\,d\
sigma(\theta),
$$

where $S^{k-1}_+ = S^{k-1}\cap(0,\infty)^k$ is the portion of the sphere in the
positive orthant and $\rho(\theta)\ge 1$ is the radial distance at which the ray
 in direction $\theta$ exits the region $[1,\infty)^k$. Since the region $[1,\in
fty)^k$ stays away from the origin, $\rho(\theta)$ is bounded above and below by
 positive constants, so the **convergence of $I$ is governed entirely by the rad
ial integral**

$$
\int_{1}^{\infty} r^{\,k-1-2\alpha/k}\,dr,
$$

while the angular integral $\int_{S^{k-1}_+}d\sigma$ is a finite positive consta
nt (the solid angle of the positive orthant).

### Step 3: Convergence criterion for the radial integral

The integral $\int_1^\infty r^{p}\,dr$ converges if and only if $p < -1$. Here $
p = k-1-\dfrac{2\alpha}{k}$, so convergence requires

$$
k-1-\frac{2\alpha}{k} < -1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k < \frac{2\alpha}{k}\q
uad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \alpha > \frac{k^2}{2}.
$$

### Step 4: Verification on special cases

- **$k=1$:** the series is $\sum_{n\ge1} n^{-2\alpha}$, a $p$-series converging
iff $2\alpha>1$, i.e. $\alpha>\tfrac12=\tfrac{1^2}{2}$. ✓
- **$k=2$:** the series is $\sum_{n_1,n_2\ge1}(n_1^2+n_2^2)^{-\alpha/2}$; the ra
dial integral is $\int r^{1-\alpha}\,dr$, converging iff $\alpha>2=\tfrac{2^2}{2
}$. ✓

Both agree with the threshold $\alpha > k^2/2$.

---

## Conclusion

Combining Steps 1–3: the original $k$-fold series converges if and only if the r
adial integral converges, which happens if and only if

$$
\boxed{\alpha > \frac{k^2}{2}.}
$$
