## Proof

The statement is **true**.

**Definitions.** A metric space $(X, d)$ is *boundedly compact* (also called *pr
oper*) if every closed bounded subset of $X$ is compact. A space is *σ-compact*
if it is a countable union of compact subsets.

**Construction.** Fix a base point $x_0 \in X$. For each $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $n
\geq 1$, consider the closed ball
$$K_n := \overline{B}(x_0, n) = \{x \in X : d(x, x_0) \leq n\}.$$

**Each $K_n$ is compact.** $K_n$ is closed (as a closed ball in a metric space)
and bounded (it is contained in $B(x_0, n+1)$, say, so its diameter is at most $
2n$). Since $X$ is boundedly compact, every closed bounded subset is compact. Th
erefore $K_n$ is compact.

**The union covers $X$.** For any $x \in X$, the distance $d(x, x_0)$ is a finit
e non-negative real number. Choose any integer $n \geq 1$ with $n \geq d(x, x_0)
$ (such $n$ exists by the Archimedean property of $\mathbb{R}$). Then $x \in K_n
$. Hence
$$X = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} K_n.$$

**Conclusion.** $X$ is expressed as a countable union $\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} K_
n$ of compact subsets $K_n$. Therefore every boundedly compact metric space is σ
-compact, i.e., a countable union of its compact subsets. $\blacksquare$
