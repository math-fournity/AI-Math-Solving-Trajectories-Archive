# Problem

In a specialized logistics yard, a team of architects is designing a four-sided boundary for a new storage facility. To establish the boundary, they must fix four specific survey markers. 

The first two markers are permanently installed at fixed coordinates $(0,0)$ and $(1,0)$ on a grid measured in meters. The remaining two markers must be placed at two distinct "smart-sensor" nodes located within a rectangular grid of potential sites. These sites are defined by integer coordinates $(x, y)$ such that the horizontal coordinate $x$ is an integer between $0$ and $59$ inclusive, and the vertical coordinate $y$ is an integer between $1$ and $5$ inclusive.

The architects must ensure the resulting boundary satisfies two strict structural criteria:
1. The four markers must form a non-self-intersecting concave quadrilateral (a four-sided polygon where one interior angle is strictly greater than $180^\circ$).
2. The boundary lines connecting the markers must not cross one another.

How many different pairs of smart-sensor nodes can be chosen to complete such a boundary?
