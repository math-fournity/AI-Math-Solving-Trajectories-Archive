## Solution

### Key distributional identity

For the derivative of the delta distribution acting on a smooth test function $f
$, the defining property is

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(t)\,\delta'(t)\,dt \;=\; -f'(0).
$$

This follows from the general rule $\langle \delta', f\rangle = -\langle \delta,
 f'\rangle = -f'(0)$, which is itself a consequence of integration by parts in t
he distributional sense: the boundary terms vanish because $\delta$ is supported
 at the origin, and the minus sign comes from differentiating the test function
rather than the delta.

### Applying it

Here the test function is $f(t) = e^{-2t}$, which is smooth. Its derivative is

$$
f'(t) = -2e^{-2t}, \qquad f'(0) = -2e^{0} = -2.
$$

Therefore,

$$
I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-2t}\,\delta'(t)\,dt = -f'(0) = -(-2) = 2.
$$

$$
