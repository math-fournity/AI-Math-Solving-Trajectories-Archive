Yes. The dimension of $\mathcal{C}(X)$ as a real vector space is at least $n$.

**Proof.**

Let $X$ be a non-empty compact Hausdorff space with at least $n$ points. Choose
$n$ distinct points $x_1, \dots, x_n \in X$.

Since $X$ is compact Hausdorff, it is normal (the $T_4$ property: compact Hausdo
rff $\Rightarrow$ normal). In particular $X$ is $T_1$, so every singleton $\{x_i
\}$ is closed, and every finite set is closed.

For each $i \in \{1, \dots, n\}$, consider the two disjoint closed sets
$$A_i = \{x_i\}, \qquad B_i = \{x_1, \dots, x_n\} \setminus \{x_i\}.$$
Both are closed (a singleton and a finite set, respectively), and they are disjo
int. By Urysohn's lemma (applicable since $X$ is normal), there exists a continu
ous function $f_i : X \to [0,1]$ such that
$$f_i(x_i) = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad f_i|_{B_i} \equiv 0,$$
i.e. $f_i(x_j) = 0$ for every $j \neq i$.

We claim $f_1, \dots, f_n$ are linearly independent in $\mathcal{C}(X)$. Suppose
$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i f_i = 0$$
for some scalars $a_1, \dots, a_n \in \mathbb{R}$. Evaluating at the point $x_j$
 gives
$$0 = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i f_i(x_j) = a_j f_j(x_j) = a_j \cdot 1 = a_j,$$
since $f_i(x_j) = 0$ for $i \neq j$ and $f_j(x_j) = 1$. Thus $a_j = 0$ for every
 $j$.

Hence $\{f_1, \dots, f_n\}$ is a linearly independent set in $\mathcal{C}(X)$ of
 cardinality $n$, so
$$\dim \mathcal{C}(X) \geq n.$$

Therefore the answer is **yes**: whenever $X$ is a non-empty compact Hausdorff s
pace with at least $n$ points, the dimension of $\mathcal{C}(X)$ is at least $n$
.
