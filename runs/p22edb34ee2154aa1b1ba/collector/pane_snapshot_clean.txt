## Proof

**Claim:** Such a set $X$ exists, and moreover, if $X$ satisfies conditions (1)–
(3), then no rational point of $\mathbb{R}^2$ is a limit point of $X$.

### Part 1: Justification that no rational point is a limit point of $X$

Suppose $X$ satisfies conditions (2) and (3): $X$ is closed and $X \cap \mathbb{
Q}^2 = \emptyset$.

Since $X$ is closed, $X$ contains all its limit points; equivalently, the set of
 limit points of $X$ is a subset of $X$. Now suppose for contradiction that some
 rational point $q \in \mathbb{Q}^2$ is a limit point of $X$. Then $q \in X$ (si
nce $X$ is closed and contains its limit points). But this contradicts condition
 (2), which says no rational point belongs to $X$. $\square$

### Part 2: Construction of $X$

Consider the line
$$L = \{(x,\, \sqrt{2}\,x + \sqrt{3}) : x \in \mathbb{R}\} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^
2.$$

We verify all three conditions:

**Condition (3) — $L$ is closed.** The function $f:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$ de
fined by $f(x,y)=y-\sqrt{2}\,x-\sqrt{3}$ is continuous, and $L = f^{-1}(\{0\})$.
 Since $\{0\}$ is closed in $\mathbb{R}$, $L$ is closed in $\mathbb{R}^2$.

**Condition (1) — Every point of $L$ is a limit point of $L$.** The map $x \maps
to (x, \sqrt{2}\,x+\sqrt{3})$ is a homeomorphism from $\mathbb{R}$ onto $L$. Sin
ce every point of $\mathbb{R}$ is a limit point of $\mathbb{R}$, every point of
$L$ is a limit point of $L$.

**Condition (2) — No rational point belongs to $L$.** Suppose $(p,q)\in\mathbb{Q
}^2$ lies on $L$, so $q = \sqrt{2}\,p + \sqrt{3}$.

- If $p = 0$: then $q = \sqrt{3}\notin\mathbb{Q}$, contradiction.
- If $p \neq 0$ and $q \neq 0$: then $\sqrt{2} = (q-\sqrt{3})/p$, so $2p^2 = (q-
\sqrt{3})^2 = q^2 - 2q\sqrt{3}+3$, giving $\sqrt{3} = (q^2+3-2p^2)/(2q)\in\mathb
b{Q}$, contradiction.
- If $p\neq 0$ and $q=0$: then $\sqrt{3}=-\sqrt{2}\,p$, so $3=2p^2$, i.e., $p^2=
3/2$, which has no rational solution, contradiction.

In all cases we reach a contradiction, so $L\cap\mathbb{Q}^2=\emptyset$.

**$L$ is non-empty** (e.g., $(0,\sqrt{3})\in L$).

### Conclusion

The set $X = L = \{(x,\sqrt{2}\,x+\sqrt{3}):x\in\mathbb{R}\}$ is a non-empty clo
sed subset of $\mathbb{R}^2$, every point of $X$ is a limit point of $X$, and $X
$ contains no rational points. Furthermore, as shown in Part 1, the closedness o
f $X$ together with the absence of rational points guarantees that no rational p
oint of $\mathbb{R}^2$ is a limit point of $X$.
