**Theorem.** Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $P$ a finitely generated projecti
ve $R$-module. Then there exist $f_1,\dots,f_n\in R$ with $(f_1,\dots,f_n)=R$ su
ch that $P_{f_i}$ is a free $R_{f_i}$-module for each $i$.

**Proof.** The argument has three steps.

**Step 1: $P$ is finitely presented.** Since $P$ is finitely generated, there is
 a surjection $\pi:R^k\twoheadrightarrow P$. Since $P$ is projective, $\pi$ spli
ts, giving $R^k \cong P\oplus Q$ for some $Q$. The projection onto $P$ along thi
s decomposition is an idempotent endomorphism $e\in \operatorname{End}_R(R^k)=M_
k(R)$, and $P\cong \operatorname{im}(e)=\operatorname{coker}(\mathrm{id}-e)$. Th
us $P$ admits a finite presentation
$$R^k \xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{id}-e\ } R^k \longrightarrow P \longrightarrow 0,$$
so $P$ is finitely presented. (No noetherian hypothesis is used.)

**Step 2: $P_{\mathfrak p}$ is free over $R_{\mathfrak p}$ for every prime $\mat
hfrak p$.** Localization preserves projectivity and finite generation, so $P_{\m
athfrak p}$ is a finitely generated projective over the local ring $R_{\mathfrak
 p}$. A standard Nakayama argument shows every finitely generated projective ove
r a local ring is free: let $\mathfrak m$ be the maximal ideal of $R_{\mathfrak
p}$ and let $x_1,\dots,x_n\in P_{\mathfrak p}$ lift a basis of $P_{\mathfrak p}/
\mathfrak m P_{\mathfrak p}$. The induced map $\varphi:R_{\mathfrak p}^n\to P_{\
mathfrak p}$ is surjective by Nakayama; since $P_{\mathfrak p}$ is projective, $
\varphi$ splits, $R_{\mathfrak p}^n\cong P_{\mathfrak p}\oplus K$. Reducing mod
$\mathfrak m$ shows $K/\mathfrak m K=0$, hence $K=0$ by Nakayama, so $\varphi$ i
s an isomorphism.

**Step 3: The free locus is open (spreading out).** We show: for every $\mathfra
k p$ with $P_{\mathfrak p}$ free, there exists $f\notin\mathfrak p$ such that $P
_f$ is free over $R_f$.

Using the finite presentation from Step 1, write an exact sequence
$$R^m \xrightarrow{\ A\ } R^k \longrightarrow P \longrightarrow 0$$
with $A\in M_{k\times m}(R)$. At $\mathfrak p$, Step 2 gives $P_{\mathfrak p}\co
ng R_{\mathfrak p}^{\,n}$ for some $n$, so
$$R_{\mathfrak p}^m \xrightarrow{\ A_{\mathfrak p}\ } R_{\mathfrak p}^k \longrig
htarrow R_{\mathfrak p}^{\,n} \longrightarrow 0$$
is exact. Hence $\operatorname{im}(A_{\mathfrak p})$ is a direct summand of $R_{
\mathfrak p}^k$ of rank $k-n$. After reordering rows and columns, some $(k-n)\ti
mes(k-n)$ minor $\Delta$ of $A$ becomes a unit in $R_{\mathfrak p}$, i.e. $\Delt
a\notin\mathfrak p$. The standard Gaussian elimination that puts $A_{\mathfrak p
}$ into the block form $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} I_{k-n} & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{small
matrix}\bigr)$ uses only the invertibility of $\Delta$ and of certain row/column
 operations; all of these invertibilities are witnessed by finitely many element
s of $R\setminus\mathfrak p$. Their product $f$ lies in $R\setminus\mathfrak p$,
 and over $R_f$ the matrix $A_f$ is equivalent to $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} I_{
k-n} & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$. Therefore
$$P_f \;\cong\; \operatorname{coker}(A_f) \;\cong\; R_f^{\,n},$$
so $P_f$ is free over $R_f$.

**Step 4: Extract a finite cover.** By Steps 2 and 3, the family $\{D(f)\}_{\mat
hfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}R}$ — where $D(f)=\{\mathfrak q:f\notin\mathfrak q\
}$ and $f$ is chosen so that $P_f$ is free — covers $\operatorname{Spec}R$. Sinc
e $\operatorname{Spec}R$ is quasi-compact (this holds for *every* commutative ri
ng), this cover admits a finite subcover $D(f_1),\dots,D(f_n)$. The $D(f_i)$ cov
er $\operatorname{Spec}R$ iff no prime contains all the $f_i$, iff $(f_1,\dots,f
_n)=R$. And $P_{f_i}$ is free over $R_{f_i}$ for each $i$ by construction.

This is exactly the desired conclusion. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the hypotheses.** The proof never used that $R$ is noetherian: finit

f $\operatorname{Spec}R$ is unconditional. Thus the statement is true a fortiori
 under the given noetherian assumption.
