## Proof

**Construction.** We modify the Cantor set construction by removing, at each ste
p, a *smaller* fraction of each remaining interval—specifically a fraction that
shrinks fast enough so that the total removed length is strictly less than 1.

Define the sequence $\alpha_n = \frac{1}{2^{n+1}}$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ Not
e that $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \alpha_n = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{8} + \frac{1}{16}
+ \cdots = \frac{1}{2} < \infty.$

**Inductive construction.** Set $C_0 = [0,1]$. Given $C_{n-1}$ (a finite union o
f closed intervals), form $C_n$ by removing, from each closed interval $I \subse
teq C_{n-1}$, the *middle open sub-interval* of length $\alpha_n \cdot |I|$. Thi
s splits each interval into two closed sub-intervals of equal length. Define

$$C = \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} C_n.$$

**$C$ is closed.** Each $C_n$ is a finite union of closed intervals, hence close
d. An arbitrary intersection of closed sets is closed.

**$C$ is nowhere dense.** After step $n$, the set $C_n$ consists of $2^n$ closed
 intervals, each of length

$$\ell_n = \frac{1}{2^n}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_k).$$

Since $\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1-\alpha_k)$ converges to a positive finite limit (e
stablished below) while $2^n \to \infty$, we have $\ell_n \to 0$. Therefore $C$
contains no interval of positive length, i.e., $\operatorname{int}(C) = \emptyse
t$. A closed set with empty interior is nowhere dense.

**Sum of removed lengths is not 1.** At step $n$, the total length removed equal
s $\alpha_n$ times the total length remaining before step $n$. The total length
remaining after step $n$ is

$$m(C_n) = \prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_k),$$

so the total length removed over all steps is

$$\sum_{\text{all removed}} = 1 - \lim_{n\to\infty} m(C_n) = 1 - \prod_{k=1}^{\i
nfty}(1-\alpha_k).$$

By the standard criterion for infinite products: for $0 < \alpha_k < 1$, the pro
duct $\prod(1-\alpha_k) > 0$ if and only if $\sum \alpha_k < \infty$. Since $\su
m \alpha_k = \frac{1}{2} < \infty$, the product is strictly positive:

$$\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(1 - \frac{1}{2^{k+1}}\right) = \frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac
{7}{8}\cdot\frac{15}{16}\cdot\frac{31}{32}\cdots > 0.$$

Hence the sum of removed lengths $= 1 - \prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1-\alpha_k) < 1$, w
hich is **not equal to 1**.

**Lebesgue measure of $C$.** The measure of $C$ equals the limit of the measures
 of $C_n$ (by continuity from above, since $C_n \supseteq C_{n+1}$ and $m(C_1) <
 \infty$):

$$m(C) = \lim_{n\to\infty} m(C_n) = \prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1-\alpha_k) > 0.$$

**Conclusion.** The set $C$ is a closed, nowhere dense subset of $[0,1]$ for whi
ch the sum of the lengths of the removed intervals is strictly less than 1 (henc
e not equal to 1). Since $m(C) = \prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1-\alpha_k) > 0$, **this s
et does NOT have Lebesgue measure zero**—it has positive Lebesgue measure. This
is a "fat Cantor set" (Smith–Volterra–Cantor type set): the key difference from
the classical Cantor set is that the fractions $\alpha_n$ are summable, so the r
emoved lengths do not exhaust the entire interval.
