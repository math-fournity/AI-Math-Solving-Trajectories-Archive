Looking at this problem, I need to find $R$ (smallest positive $r$ where the ine
quality holds for all $r \geq R$) and $L$ (largest negative $r$ where it holds f
or all $r \leq L$), given the constraint $ab + bc + ca + abc = 4$ with $a, b, c
\geq 0$.

## Setup: Substitution to Symmetric Variables

Let $u = ab$, $v = bc$, $w = ca$. Then $u, v, w \geq 0$ and the constraint becom
es $u + v + w + \sqrt{uvw} = 4$ (since $uvw = (abc)^2$).

Setting $s = u+v+w$, $p = uv+vw+wu$, $q = uvw$, we get $\sqrt{q} = 4 - s$, so $q
 = (4-s)^2$ with $s \leq 4$.

The inequality $(r+u)(r+v)(r+w) \geq (r+1)^3$ expands to:
$$r^2(s-3) + r(p-3) + (s-3)(s-5) \geq 0$$

(using $(4-s)^2 - 1 = (s-3)(s-5)$).

## Determining the Feasible Range of $s$

By AM-GM, $\sqrt{uvw} \leq (s/3)^{3/2}$, so $4 - s \leq (s/3)^{3/2}$. At $s = 3$
: both sides equal 1. For $s < 3$: $4-s > 1 > (s/3)^{3/2}$, contradiction. So $s
 \in [3, 4]$.

## Extremal $p$ Values: Two Variables Equal

For fixed $s$ and $q = (4-s)^2$, the extremal values of $p$ occur when two of $u
, v, w$ are equal (standard result from cubic discriminant theory). So I use the
 parametrization $b = c = t$, giving $a = (2-t)/t$ for $t \in (0, 2]$.

This yields $u = w = 2-t$, $v = t^2$, and:
- $s = (t-1)^2 + 3$
- $p - 3 = (t-1)^2(1 - 2t)$

Substituting into the inequality and factoring out $(t-1)^2 \geq 0$:

$$r^2 + (1-2t)r + (t^2 - 2t - 1) \geq 0 \quad \text{for } t \neq 1$$

This quadratic in $r$ has discriminant $\Delta = 5 + 4t > 0$, with roots:
$$r_{\pm}(t) = \frac{(2t-1) \pm \sqrt{5+4t}}{2}$$

## Branch Analysis

For $s \in [3,4]$, there are two $t$-values: $t \in (0,1)$ and $t' = 2-t \in (1,
2)$. I showed $p(t) - p(2-t) = -4(t-1)^3$, so for $t \in (0,1)$: $p(t) > p(2-t)$
.

- **For $r > 0$**: coefficient of $p$ is positive, so worst case is $p_{\min}$ (
the $t \in (1,2)$ branch). Need $r \geq r_+(t)$ for all $t \in (1,2]$.
- **For $r < 0$**: coefficient of $p$ is negative, so worst case is $p_{\max}$ (
the $t \in (0,1)$ branch). Need $r \leq r_-(t)$ for all $t \in (0,1)$.

## Finding $R$

$r_+'(t) = 1 + \frac{1}{\sqrt{5+4t}} > 0$, so $r_+(t)$ is increasing. Maximum at
 $t = 2$:
$$R = r_+(2) = \frac{3 + \sqrt{13}}{2}$$

**Verification**: At $t = 2$ ($a = 0, b = c = 2$), we get $(u,v,w) = (0,4,0)$, a
nd the inequality becomes $r^2(r+4) \geq (r+1)^3$, i.e., $r^2 - 3r - 1 \geq 0$,
giving $r \geq \frac{3+\sqrt{13}}{2}$. ✓

## Finding $L$

$r_-'(t) = 1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{5+4t}} > 0$ for $t > 0$, so $r_-(t)$ is increasing
. Infimum as $t \to 0^+$:
$$L = r_-(0) = \frac{-1 - \sqrt{5}}{2}$$

**Verification**: As $t \to 0^+$, $(u,v,w) \to (2, 0, 2)$, giving $r(r+2)^2 \geq
 (r+1)^3$, i.e., $r^2 + r - 1 \geq 0$, with roots $\frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{5}}{2}$. T
he smaller root is $\frac{-1-\sqrt{5}}{2}$. ✓

At $r = L$, the quadratic evaluates to $t(t + \sqrt{5} - 1) > 0$ for all $t > 0$
, confirming the inequality holds (strictly, since the limit is not achieved by
any finite $(a,b,c)$).

## Final Computation

$$2R - 3 = 2 \cdot \frac{3+\sqrt{13}}{2} - 3 = \sqrt{13}$$
$$2L + 1 = 2 \cdot \frac{-1-\sqrt{5}}{2} + 1 = -\sqrt{5}$$

$$(2R-3)^2 + (2L+1)^2 = 13 + 5 = \boxed{18}$$
