## Proof

Let $a_n = \tan\!\left(n^{-p/2}\right)\cdot \ln\!\left(1+n^{-2p}\right)$, so the
 series is $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^n a_n$.

**Step 1: Asymptotics of $a_n$.**

For large $n$ and $p>0$, both arguments tend to $0$, so using $\tan x \sim x$ an
d $\ln(1+x)\sim x$:

$$\tan\!\left(n^{-p/2}\right) \sim n^{-p/2}, \qquad \ln\!\left(1+n^{-2p}\right)\
sim n^{-2p}.$$

More precisely, $\tan(n^{-p/2}) = n^{-p/2}+O(n^{-3p/2})$ and $\ln(1+n^{-2p})=n^{
-2p}+O(n^{-4p})$, hence

$$a_n = n^{-p/2}\cdot n^{-2p}\bigl(1+O(n^{-p})\bigr) \sim n^{-5p/2}.$$

**Step 2: Convergence of the alternating series (Leibniz test).**

For $p>0$: $a_n \sim n^{-5p/2}\to 0$, and $a_n$ is eventually positive and decre
asing (each factor is decreasing in $n$). By the Leibniz alternating series test
, $\sum(-1)^n a_n$ converges for every $p>0$.

For $p\le 0$: $n^{-p/2}\not\to 0$ (it is constant at $p=0$ or grows at $p<0$), s
o $a_n\not\to 0$ and the series diverges.

**Step 3: Absolute convergence vs. divergence.**

Since $a_n>0$ for large $n$ and $a_n\sim n^{-5p/2}$, by limit comparison with $\
sum n^{-5p/2}$:

- $\sum a_n$ **diverges** when $\frac{5p}{2}\le 1$, i.e. $p\le \frac{2}{5}$,
- $\sum a_n$ **converges** when $\frac{5p}{2}>1$, i.e. $p>\frac{2}{5}$.

**Step 4: Conditional convergence region.**

Combining Steps 2 and 3:

- $p\le 0$: series diverges (terms don't vanish).
- $0<p\le \tfrac{2}{5}$: alternating series converges (Leibniz), absolute series
 diverges $\Rightarrow$ **conditional convergence**.
- $p>\tfrac{2}{5}$: both converge $\Rightarrow$ absolute convergence (not condit
ional).

**Step 5: The boundary $p=\tfrac{2}{5}$.**

At $p=\frac{2}{5}$: $a_n\sim n^{-1}$, so $\sum a_n$ diverges (harmonic), while $
\sum(-1)^n a_n$ converges by Leibniz. Hence conditional convergence holds at $p=
\frac{2}{5}$.

For any $p>\frac{2}{5}$, the series converges absolutely, so it is **not** condi
tionally convergent.

**Conclusion.** The greatest value of $p$ for which the series converges conditi
onally is

$$\boxed{p=\dfrac{2}{5}}.$$
