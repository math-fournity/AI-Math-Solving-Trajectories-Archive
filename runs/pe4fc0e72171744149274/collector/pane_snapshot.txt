## Proof

We construct several representations of $M_g$ that do not factor through $Sp(2g,
\mathbb{Z})$, covering all genera $g \geq 2$. (For $g=1$, $M_1 \cong Sp(2,\mathb
b{Z})$ and the symplectic representation is an isomorphism, so every representat
ion factors through it.)

### Preliminaries

The **symplectic representation** $\psi: M_g \to Sp(2g, \mathbb{Z})$ is defined
by the action of $M_g$ on $H_1(\Sigma_g, \mathbb{Z})$. Its kernel is the **Torel
li group** $\mathcal{I}_g$. A representation $\rho: M_g \to G$ factors through $
Sp(2g,\mathbb{Z})$ if and only if $\rho|_{\mathcal{I}_g}$ is trivial. Thus it su
ffices to find representations that are nontrivial on $\mathcal{I}_g$.

**Key fact:** $\mathcal{I}_g$ is nontrivial for $g \geq 2$. Indeed, if $\gamma$
is a separating simple closed curve on $\Sigma_g$, then $[\gamma] = 0 \in H_1(\S
igma_g, \mathbb{Z})$, so the Dehn twist $T_\gamma$ acts trivially on $H_1$, i.e.
, $T_\gamma \in \mathcal{I}_g$. Since $T_\gamma$ has infinite order in $M_g$, $\
mathcal{I}_g$ is nontrivial (in fact infinite).

### Construction 1 (Infinite-dimensional, all $g \geq 2$): The Dehn–Nielsen–Baer
 representation

**Construction.** The mapping class group acts on $\pi_1(\Sigma_g)$ by outer aut
omorphisms. The **Dehn–Nielsen–Baer theorem** states that this gives an injectiv
e homomorphism:
$$\rho_{\text{DNB}}: M_g \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Out}^+(\pi_1(\Sigma_g)),$
$
where $\operatorname{Out}^+$ denotes outer automorphisms acting trivially on $H_
2(\Sigma_g,\mathbb{Z})$.

The symplectic representation factors as $\psi = \bar{\rho} \circ \rho_{\text{DN
B}}$, where $\bar{\rho}: \operatorname{Out}^+(\pi_1(\Sigma_g)) \to Sp(2g,\mathbb
{Z})$ is induced by abelianization $\pi_1 \to H_1$.

**Non-factorization.** Since $\rho_{\text{DNB}}$ is injective, $\rho_{\text{DNB}
}|_{\mathcal{I}_g}$ is injective. But $\mathcal{I}_g$ is nontrivial, so $\rho_{\
text{DNB}}$ is nontrivial on $\mathcal{I}_g$. If $\rho_{\text{DNB}}$ factored th
rough $Sp(2g,\mathbb{Z})$, then $\rho_{\text{DNB}}|_{\mathcal{I}_g}$ would be tr
ivial (since $\psi|_{\mathcal{I}_g} = 1$), contradiction.

**Explicit witness.** Let $\gamma$ be a separating simple closed curve. Then $T_
\gamma \in \mathcal{I}_g$, so $\psi(T_\gamma) = I$. But $T_\gamma$ acts nontrivi
ally on $\pi_1(\Sigma_g)$ (it is a nontrivial outer automorphism, as can be seen
 by its action on loops crossing $\gamma$), so $\rho_{\text{DNB}}(T_\gamma) \neq
 1$. Hence $\rho_{\text{DNB}}$ does not factor through $Sp(2g,\mathbb{Z})$. $\sq
uare$

### Construction 2 (Finite, $g = 2$): The abelianization

**Construction.** A classical result (see e.g. Mumford, Birman) gives the abelia
nization:
$$H_1(M_2, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}_{10}.$$
Let $\alpha: M_2 \to \mathbb{Z}_{10}$ be the abelianization map.

On the other hand, for $g \geq 2$, the abelianization of $Sp(2g, \mathbb{Z})$ is
:
$$Sp(2g, \mathbb{Z})^{\text{ab}} \cong \mathbb{Z}_2.$$
(For $g = 2$: $Sp(4, \mathbb{F}_2) \cong S_6$, so $Sp(4,\mathbb{Z}) \to Sp(4,\ma
thbb{F}_2) \to S_6 \xrightarrow{\text{sgn}} \mathbb{Z}_2$ is surjective, and one
 checks this is the full abelianization.)

**Non-factorization.** If $\alpha$ factored through $Sp(4, \mathbb{Z})$, then $\
alpha$ would factor through $Sp(4,\mathbb{Z})^{\text{ab}} \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$, s
o $\alpha(M_2)$ would be a quotient of $\mathbb{Z}_2$. But $\alpha(M_2) = \mathb
b{Z}_{10}$, which has order 10 and is not a quotient of $\mathbb{Z}_2$. Contradi
ction. $\square$

### Construction 3 (Finite, $g \geq 3$): Action on nilpotent quotients of $\pi_1
$

**Construction.** Let $\Gamma_k = \Gamma_k(\pi_1(\Sigma_g))$ be the $k$-th term
of the lower central series. Each $\Gamma_k$ is a characteristic subgroup, so $M
_g$ acts on $\pi_1(\Sigma_g)/\Gamma_k$ by outer automorphisms:
$$\rho_k: M_g \to \operatorname{Out}(\pi_1(\Sigma_g)/\Gamma_k).$$

For $k=2$, $\pi_1/\Gamma_2 = H_1(\Sigma_g,\mathbb{Z})$, so $\rho_2 = \psi$ facto
rs through $Sp(2g,\mathbb{Z})$.

For $k=3$: the group $N := \pi_1(\Sigma_g)/\Gamma_3$ is a finitely generated tor
sion-free nilpotent group of class 2, with:
- $N^{\text{ab}} \cong H_1(\Sigma_g, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{2g}$,
- $[N,N] \cong \Gamma_2/\Gamma_3 \cong \bigwedge^2 H_{\mathbb{Z}} / \langle \ome
ga \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}^{2g^2 - g - 1}$, where $\omega$ is the symplectic cl
ass.

**The Johnson homomorphism.** The restriction of $\rho_3$ to $\mathcal{I}_g$ is
measured by the **Johnson homomorphism** (Johnson, 1980):
$$\tau: \mathcal{I}_g \to \bigwedge^3 H_{\mathbb{Z}} / H_{\mathbb{Z}},$$
which is surjective for $g \geq 3$. Here $H_{\mathbb{Z}} = H_1(\Sigma_g, \mathbb
{Z})$, and $\bigwedge^3 H_{\mathbb{Z}} / H_{\mathbb{Z}}$ has rank $\binom{2g}{3}
 - 2g = \frac{2g(2g-1)(2g-2)}{6} - 2g = \frac{2g(2g-1)(g-1)}{3} - 2g > 0$ for $g
 \geq 3$.

Concretely, for $f \in \mathcal{I}_g$ and $x \in \pi_1(\Sigma_g)$, the element $
f_*(x) \cdot x^{-1} \in \Gamma_2$ defines a homomorphism $H_{\mathbb{Z}} \to \Ga
mma_2/\Gamma_3$, and $\tau(f)$ is the corresponding element of $\bigwedge^3 H_{\
mathbb{Z}} / H_{\mathbb{Z}}$.

**Non-factorization of $\rho_3$.** Since $\tau$ is surjective (hence nontrivial)
 for $g \geq 3$, there exists $f \in \mathcal{I}_g$ with $\tau(f) \neq 0$, meani
ng $f$ acts nontrivially on $\Gamma_2/\Gamma_3$ and hence on $N = \pi_1/\Gamma_3
$. So $\rho_3(f) \neq 1$ while $\psi(f) = I$. Therefore $\rho_3: M_g \to \operat
orname{Out}(N)$ does not factor through $Sp(2g, \mathbb{Z})$. $\square$

**Finite quotient.** To obtain a **finite** representation, reduce mod $n$. Defi
ne $N_n$ to be the quotient of $N = \pi_1/\Gamma_3$ by the characteristic subgro
up generated by $n$-th powers of all elements (equivalently, $N_n = \pi_1 / (\Ga
mma_3 \cdot \Gamma_2^{(n)})$ where $\Gamma_2^{(n)}$ is generated by $n$-th power
s in $\Gamma_2$). Then $N_n$ is a finite nilpotent group of class 2, and:
$$\rho_{3,n}: M_g \to \operatorname{Out}(N_n)$$
is a finite representation. Since $\tau \bmod n: \mathcal{I}_g \to (\bigwedge^3
H_{\mathbb{Z}} / H_{\mathbb{Z}}) \otimes \mathbb{Z}/n$ is surjective for $n \geq
 2$ (as $\tau$ is surjective over $\mathbb{Z}$), the Torelli group acts nontrivi
ally on $N_n$. Hence $\rho_{3,n}$ does not factor through $Sp(2g, \mathbb{Z})$.

The group $N_n$ has order $n^{2g} \cdot n^{2g^2 - g - 1} = n^{2g^2 + g - 1}$, an
d $\operatorname{Out}(N_n)$ is a finite group, so $\rho_{3,n}$ is a genuine fini
te representation. $\square$
