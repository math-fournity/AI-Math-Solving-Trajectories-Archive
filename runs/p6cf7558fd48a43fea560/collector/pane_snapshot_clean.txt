**Answer: Yes.** Below is the proof.

Let $(\Omega, F, P)$ be a probability space and $(\Omega, F', P')$ its completio
n. Recall that every $A \in F'$ can be written $A = E \cup N$ where $E \in F$ an
d $N \subset D$ for some $D \in F$ with $P(D)=0$.

**Step 1 (Reduction for simple functions).** Let $s = \sum_{i=1}^{m} a_i \mathbf
{1}_{A_i}$ be an $F'$-measurable simple function, with $\{A_i\}$ a partition of
$\Omega$ and $A_i \in F'$. Write $A_i = E_i \cup N_i$ with $E_i \in F$, $N_i \su
bset D_i$, $D_i \in F$, $P(D_i)=0$. Set $D = \bigcup_i D_i \in F$; then $P(D)=0$
. On $\Omega \setminus D$ we have $A_i \cap (\Omega\setminus D) = E_i \cap (\Ome
ga\setminus D)$, so defining
$$\tilde s := \sum_{i=1}^{m} a_i \mathbf{1}_{E_i},$$
which is $F$-measurable, gives $\tilde s = s$ on $\Omega \setminus D$, hence a.e
.

**Step 2 (Approximation of $f$).** Since $X$ is a compact metric space, it is se
parable. For each $n \ge 1$, cover $X$ by finitely many open balls of radius $1/
(2n)$ and refine to a Borel partition $\{B_1^{(n)},\dots,B_{k_n}^{(n)}\}$ of $X$
 with $\operatorname{diam}(B_i^{(n)}) < 1/n$. Pick $x_i^{(n)} \in B_i^{(n)}$ and
 set
$$f_n(\omega) := x_i^{(n)} \quad\text{when } f(\omega) \in B_i^{(n)}.$$
Each $f_n$ is an $F'$-measurable simple function (because $f^{-1}(B_i^{(n)}) \in
 F'$), and for every $\omega$,
$$d\bigl(f_n(\omega), f(\omega)\bigr) < 1/n,$$
so $f_n \to f$ pointwise (in fact uniformly).

**Step 3 (Lifting each $f_n$).** By Step 1, for each $n$ there is an $F$-measura
ble simple function $g_n$ and a set $D_n \in F$ with $P(D_n)=0$ such that $g_n =
 f_n$ on $\Omega \setminus D_n$. Set
$$N := \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} D_n \in F, \qquad P(N) = 0.$$
On $\Omega \setminus N$ we have $g_n = f_n$ for every $n$, and therefore $g_n \t
o f$ pointwise on $\Omega \setminus N$.

**Step 4 (Defining $g$).** Fix any $x_0 \in X$ and define
$$g(\omega) := \begin{cases} \displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty} g_n(\omega), & \text
{if the limit exists in } X,\\ x_0, & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$$
The set $C := \{\omega : \lim_n g_n(\omega) \text{ exists}\}$ is $F$-measurable
(it is the set where $(g_n)$ is Cauchy, expressible via countable operations on
$F$-measurable preimages of open sets), and $g|_C = \lim g_n$ is $F$-measurable;
 extending by the constant $x_0$ on $\Omega \setminus C$ preserves $F$-measurabi
lity. Hence $g$ is $F$-measurable.

On $\Omega \setminus N \subset C$ the limit exists and equals $f$, so $g = f$ on
 $\Omega \setminus N$. Since $P(N)=0$, we conclude $g = f$ $P'$-a.e. (equivalent
ly $P$-a.e.).

Thus an $F$-measurable modification of $f$ always exists. (The nonatomic hypothe
sis is not needed for this result.)
