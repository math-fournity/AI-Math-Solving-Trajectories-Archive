## Step 1: Evaluate the inner integral via contour integration

Fix $x > 0$ (which holds throughout since $x \in [\Lambda/s, \Lambda]$ with $s,
\Lambda > 0$). Consider:
$$I(x) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty \frac{i}{y^2 - x^2 + i\epsilon}\,dy$$

The integrand decays as $i/y^2$ for large $|y|$, so the semicircular arc contrib
ution vanishes. The poles are at $y^2 = x^2 - i\epsilon$, i.e., $y = \pm\sqrt{x^
2 - i\epsilon}$.

For small $\epsilon > 0$ and $x > 0$:
- $y_1 = +\sqrt{x^2 - i\epsilon} \approx x - \frac{i\epsilon}{2x}$ — **lower** h
alf-plane
- $y_2 = -\sqrt{x^2 - i\epsilon} \approx -x + \frac{i\epsilon}{2x}$ — **upper**
half-plane

Closing the contour in the **upper** half-plane, only $y_2$ is enclosed. The res
idue is:
$$\operatorname{Res}_{y=y_2}\frac{i}{(y-y_1)(y-y_2)} = \frac{i}{y_2 - y_1} = \fr
ac{i}{-2\sqrt{x^2-i\epsilon}}$$

By the residue theorem:
$$I(x) = 2\pi i \cdot \frac{i}{-2\sqrt{x^2-i\epsilon}} = \frac{\pi}{\sqrt{x^2-i\
epsilon}}$$

As $\epsilon \to 0^+$: $\sqrt{x^2 - i\epsilon} \to x$ (since $x > 0$), giving:
$$\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^+} I(x) = \frac{\pi}{x}$$

## Step 2: Verification via Sokhotski–Plemelj

Factor $y^2 - x^2 = (y-x)(y+x)$.

**Near $y = x$:** $(y-x)(y+x) \approx 2x(y-x)$, so locally $\frac{i}{2x(y-x)+i\e
psilon} = \frac{i}{2x}\cdot\frac{1}{(y-x)+i\epsilon/(2x)}$. By Plemelj, $\frac{1
}{u+i\epsilon'}\to \mathrm{P.V.}\frac{1}{u}-i\pi\delta(u)$, giving a delta contr
ibution $\frac{i}{2x}(-i\pi)=\frac{\pi}{2x}$.

**Near $y = -x$:** $(y-x)(y+x)\approx -2x(y+x)$, so locally $\frac{-i}{2x}\cdot\
frac{1}{(y+x)-i\epsilon/(2x)}$. By Plemelj, $\frac{1}{u-i\epsilon'}\to \mathrm{P
.V.}\frac{1}{u}+i\pi\delta(u)$, giving a delta contribution $\frac{-i}{2x}(i\pi)
=\frac{\pi}{2x}$.

**Total delta contribution:** $\frac{\pi}{2x}+\frac{\pi}{2x}=\frac{\pi}{x}$.

The principal value integrals $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{P.V.}\frac{1}{y\mp
 x}\,dy$ vanish (odd integrands), confirming $I(x)\to\frac{\pi}{x}$. ✓

## Step 3: Evaluate the outer integral

$$\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^+}\int_{\Lambda/s}^{\Lambda} I(x)\,dx = \int_{\Lambda/s}^{
\Lambda}\frac{\pi}{x}\,dx = \pi\Big[\ln x\Big]_{\Lambda/s}^{\Lambda} = \pi\left(
\ln\Lambda - \ln\frac{\Lambda}{s}\right)=\pi\ln s$$
