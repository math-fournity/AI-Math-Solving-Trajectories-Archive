## Solution

**Setup.** Place the apex $A$ at the origin with the cone's axis along the posit
ive $z$-axis. Since the height and base radius are both $h = 12+12\sqrt{2} = 12(
1+\sqrt{2})$, the half-angle $\alpha$ satisfies $\tan\alpha = 1$, so $\alpha = 4
5°$. The cone is $x^2+y^2 = z^2$, $0 \le z \le h$.

**Inscribed sphere.** The sphere centered at $B=(0,0,b)$ on the axis, tangent to
 the lateral surface, has radius $r = b\sin 45° = b/\sqrt{2}$. Tangent to the ba
se: $b + r = h$, giving
$$b = \frac{h}{1+1/\sqrt{2}} = h(2-\sqrt{2}) = 12(1+\sqrt{2})(2-\sqrt{2}) = 12\s
qrt{2}, \qquad r = 12.$$

**The tangent plane.** Let $P$ pass through $Q=(0,0,q)$ on segment $AB$ (so $0<q
\le b$) with unit normal $\hat n = (\sin\phi,0,\cos\phi)$. Tangency to the spher
e requires
$$\text{dist}(B,P) = \cos\phi\,(b-q) = r = 12 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \cos\phi
 = \frac{12}{12\sqrt{2}-q} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}-q/12}.\tag{*}$$
The plane equation is $z = q - x\tan\phi$.

**Closest point to the apex.** On the cone, $x^2+y^2=z^2$, so $|AX| = \sqrt{x^2+
y^2+z^2} = \sqrt{2}\,|z|$. Thus the closest point $X$ minimizes $|z|$. Substitut
ing $z=q-x\tan\phi$ into $x^2+y^2=z^2$ and setting $y=0$ (extreme points of the
ellipse in the $xz$-plane) yields $z = q/(1\pm\tan\phi)$. The minimum is
$$z_X = \frac{q}{1+\tan\phi}, \qquad AX = \frac{\sqrt{2}\,q}{1+\tan\phi} = 6.\ta
g{**}$$

**Solving for $q$ and $\phi$.** Writing $q=12s$, equation $(*)$ gives $\tan\phi
= \sqrt{(\sqrt{2}-s)^2-1}$, and equation $(**)$ gives $\tan\phi = 2\sqrt{2}\,s -
 1$. Equating:
$$(2\sqrt{2}\,s-1)^2 = (\sqrt{2}-s)^2 - 1$$
$$8s^2 - 4\sqrt{2}\,s + 1 = 1 - 2\sqrt{2}\,s + s^2$$
$$7s^2 - 2\sqrt{2}\,s = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad s = \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{7} \qua
d (s\neq 0).$$
Hence $q = \frac{24\sqrt{2}}{7}$ and $\tan\phi = \frac{1}{7}$ (verified: $\cos\p
hi = \frac{7\sqrt{2}}{10}$, $\tan\phi = \frac{1}{7}$ ✓).

**Ellipse area.** With $t = \tan\phi = 1/7$, the intersection curve is
$$\tfrac{48}{49}x^2 + y^2 + \tfrac{2q}{7}x - q^2 = 0.$$
Completing the square:
$$\frac{48}{49}\!\left(x+\tfrac{7q}{48}\right)^{\!2} + y^2 = \frac{49q^2}{48}.$$
The semi-axes are $a = \frac{49q}{48}$ and $b = \frac{7q}{4\sqrt{3}}$, so
$$\text{Area} = \pi ab = \pi\cdot\frac{49q}{48}\cdot\frac{7q}{4\sqrt{3}} = \frac
{343\,\pi\, q^2}{192\sqrt{3}}.$$
Substituting $q^2 = \frac{1152}{49}$:
$$\text{Area} = \frac{343\,\pi\cdot 1152}{192\sqrt{3}\cdot 49} = \frac{7\cdot 6\
,\pi}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{42\pi}{\sqrt{3}} = 14\sqrt{3}\,\pi.$$

**Verification.** $z_{\min} = q/(1+t) = \frac{24\sqrt{2}/7}{8/7} = 3\sqrt{2}$, g
iving $AX = \sqrt{2}\cdot 3\sqrt{2} = 6$ ✓. Both $z$-extremes ($3\sqrt{2}$ and $
4\sqrt{2}$) lie well within $[0, h]$, confirming a complete ellipse.

$$\boxed{14\sqrt{3}\,\pi}$$
