## Proof

ix} a & x \\ x^* & b \end{pmatrix} \geq 0$, the Schur complement yields
$$\begin{pmatrix} a & x \\ x^* & b \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} I & 0 \\ x^*
a^{-1} & I \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} a & 0 \\ 0 & b - x^* a^{-1} x \end{pmat
rix} \begin{pmatrix} I & a^{-1} x \\ 0 & I \end{pmatrix},$$
so $b - x^* a^{-1} x \geq 0$, i.e., $x^* a^{-1} x \leq b$. Set $y = a^{-1/2} x \
, b^{-1/2} \in M$. Then
$$y^* y = b^{-1/2} x^* a^{-1} x \, b^{-1/2} \leq b^{-1/2} b \, b^{-1/2} = 1,$$
so $\|y\| \leq 1$, and $x = a^{1/2} y \, b^{1/2}$.

**Step 2 (General case).** For $\epsilon > 0$, set $a_\epsilon = a + \epsilon \c
dot 1$ and $b_\epsilon = b + \epsilon \cdot 1$. Since $a, b \geq 0$, both $a_\ep

$$\begin{pmatrix} a_\epsilon & x \\ x^* & b_\epsilon \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmat

By Step 1, there exists $y_\epsilon \in M$ with $\|y_\epsilon\| \leq 1$ such tha

$$x = a_\epsilon^{1/2} \, y_\epsilon \, b_\epsilon^{1/2}. \tag{$*$}$$

Since $M$ is a von Neumann algebra, $M \cong (M_*)^*$, and by the Banach–Alaoglu
 theorem its closed unit ball is $\sigma$-weakly (weak*) compact. Hence there ex
ists a net $(\epsilon_i)$ with $\epsilon_i \to 0$ such that $y_{\epsilon_i} \to
y$ $\sigma$-weakly for some $y \in M$ with $\|y\| \leq 1$.

Since $a_\epsilon \to a$ and $b_\epsilon \to b$ in norm, and the square-root map

to a^{1/2}$ and $b_{\epsilon_i}^{1/2} \to b^{1/2}$ in norm.

**Claim:** $a_{\epsilon_i}^{1/2} y_{\epsilon_i} b_{\epsilon_i}^{1/2} \to a^{1/2}
 y \, b^{1/2}$ $\sigma$-weakly.

Write the difference as three terms:
$$\underbrace{(a_{\epsilon_i}^{1/2} - a^{1/2})\, y_{\epsilon_i}\, b_{\epsilon_i}
^{1/2}}_{\text{(I)}} + \underbrace{a^{1/2}(y_{\epsilon_i} - y)\, b_{\epsilon_i}^
{1/2}}_{\text{(II)}} + \underbrace{a^{1/2} y\, (b_{\epsilon_i}^{1/2} - b^{1/2})}
_{\text{(III)}}.$$

- **Terms (I) and (III)** tend to $0$ in norm (hence $\sigma$-weakly), since $\|
y_{\epsilon_i}\|, \|y\| \leq 1$ and $\|a_{\epsilon_i}^{1/2} - a^{1/2}\|, \|b_{\e
psilon_i}^{1/2} - b^{1/2}\| \to 0$.

- **Term (II):** Split further as
$$a^{1/2}(y_{\epsilon_i} - y)\, b^{1/2} + a^{1/2}(y_{\epsilon_i} - y)(b_{\epsilo
n_i}^{1/2} - b^{1/2}).$$
The second summand tends to $0$ in norm ($\|y_{\epsilon_i} - y\| \leq 2$, $\|b_{
\epsilon_i}^{1/2} - b^{1/2}\| \to 0$). The first summand tends to $0$ $\sigma$-w
eakly: in a von Neumann algebra, left and right multiplication by a fixed elemen
t are $\sigma$-weakly continuous (they map normal functionals to normal function

This establishes the claim.

By ($*$), $a_{\epsilon_i}^{1/2} y_{\epsilon_i} b_{\epsilon_i}^{1/2} = x$ for eve

$$a^{1/2} y \, b^{1/2} = x.$$
