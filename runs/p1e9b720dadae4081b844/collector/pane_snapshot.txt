**Step 2: Reduce to the standard mean-value integral.**

**Step 3: Apply the mean-value property of harmonic functions.**

Consider $g(z) = 1 + \tfrac{i}{2}z$. Its unique zero is at $z_0 = -\frac{2}{i} =
 2i$, and $|z_0| = 2 > 1$, so $g$ has **no zeros in the closed unit disk** $|z|\
le 1$. Hence $\log g(z)$ is holomorphic in a neighborhood of $|z|\le 1$, and $\l
n|g(z)| = \operatorname{Re}\log g(z)$ is harmonic there.

By the mean-value property of harmonic functions, the average of $\ln|g|$ over t

$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\ln\!\left|1 + \tfrac{i}{2}e^{iy}\right|dy = \ln\!\

$$I = \int_0^{2\pi}\ln 2\,dx + \int_0^{2\pi}\ln\!\left|1 + \tfrac{i}{2}e^{-ix}\r
