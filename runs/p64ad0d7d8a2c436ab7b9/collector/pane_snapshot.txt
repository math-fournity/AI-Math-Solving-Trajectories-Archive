# Uniformization Theorem for Compact Riemann Surfaces

## Statement of the Theorem

**Theorem (Uniformization).** Let $S$ be a compact, connected, 2-dimensional Rie
mann surface (without boundary) equipped with a Riemannian metric $g$, with Gaus
sian curvature $K = K(g)$ and Euler characteristic $\chi(S)$. Then there exists
a smooth function $u: S \to \mathbb{R}$ such that the conformally equivalent met
ric

$$\tilde g = e^{2u}\, g$$

has **constant** Gaussian curvature $\tilde K \equiv c$, where the sign of the c
onstant $c$ is determined by the sign of the Euler characteristic:

- **$\chi(S) > 0$** (i.e., $S \cong \mathbb{CP}^1$, genus $0$): $\quad c > 0$. T
he surface admits a metric of **positive** constant curvature (the round sphere
metric, $c = 1$ after normalization).
- **$\chi(S) = 0$** (i.e., $S$ is a torus, genus $1$): $\quad c = 0$. The surfac
e admits a **flat** metric.
- **$\chi(S) < 0$** (i.e., genus $\geq 2$): $\quad c < 0$. The surface admits a
metric of **negative** constant curvature (a hyperbolic metric, $c = -1$ after n
ormalization).

Equivalently, every compact Riemann surface carries a conformal metric of consta
nt Gaussian curvature whose sign agrees with that of $\chi(S)$. This is the **me
tric uniformization theorem** (the conformal-class version of the classical unif
ormization theorem).

## Proof: Key Steps

### Step 1. Conformal change of metric and curvature transformation

On a 2-dimensional Riemannian manifold, any metric conformal to $g$ has the form
 $\tilde g = e^{2u}g$ for a smooth function $u$. A standard formula of Riemannia
n geometry (valid in dimension $2$, where the Weyl tensor vanishes and the Ricci
 tensor determines curvature) gives the transformation of Gaussian curvature und
er a conformal change:

$$\boxed{\tilde K = e^{-2u}\!\left(K - \Delta_g\, u\right)}$$

where $\Delta_g$ is the **Laplace–Beltrami operator** of $g$ (with the sign conv
ention $\Delta_g f = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\det g}}\partial_i(\sqrt{\det g}\, g^{ij}\pa
rtial_j f)$, so that $\Delta_g$ is non-positive on constants in the analyst's co
nvention, or non-negative — we fix the convention so that $\Delta_g u = K - \til
de K\, e^{2u}$). We adopt the convention where the formula reads

$$\Delta_g\, u = K - \tilde K\, e^{2u}.$$

### Step 2. Reduction to a nonlinear PDE (the prescribed-curvature / Liouville e
quation)

We seek $u$ such that $\tilde K \equiv c$ is constant. Substituting into the cur
vature transformation formula yields the **nonlinear elliptic PDE**:

$$\boxed{\Delta_g\, u \;=\; K \;-\; c\, e^{2u} \qquad \text{on } S.}$$

This is the **Liouville equation** (also called the *prescribed Gaussian curvatu
re equation*). The uniformization theorem is thus **equivalent** to proving that
 this PDE admits a smooth solution $u$ on $S$ for an appropriate constant $c$ wh
ose sign matches $\chi(S)$.

### Step 3. The constant $c$ is fixed by Gauss–Bonnet (a necessary condition)

Integrating the PDE over $S$ and using the Gauss–Bonnet theorem gives a **necess
ary condition** that pins down the sign of $c$. The area form of $\tilde g$ is $
d\tilde A = e^{2u}\, dA_g$. Gauss–Bonnet for $\tilde g$ reads

$$\int_S \tilde K\, d\tilde A = 2\pi\,\chi(S).$$

Since $\tilde K = c$ is constant,

$$c \int_S e^{2u}\, dA_g = 2\pi\,\chi(S).$$

The integral $\int_S e^{2u}\, dA_g = \mathrm{Area}(\tilde g) > 0$ is strictly po
sitive, so

$$\operatorname{sgn}(c) = \operatorname{sgn}\!\big(\chi(S)\big).$$

This shows the three cases are **forced** by topology. After rescaling the metri
c (which rescales $c$), we normalize:

| Genus | $\chi(S)$ | Normalized $c$ |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| $0$ (sphere) | $2$ | $+1$ |
| $1$ (torus) | $0$ | $0$ |
| $\geq 2$ | $< 0$ | $-1$ |

### Step 4. Solving the PDE — case by case

We now show the PDE $\Delta_g u = K - c\, e^{2u}$ is solvable in each case.

---

#### Case $\chi(S) = 0$ (torus, $c = 0$): the linear case

The PDE reduces to the **linear** Poisson equation

$$\Delta_g\, u = K.$$

A necessary and sufficient condition for solvability of $\Delta_g u = f$ on a co
mpact manifold is $\int_S f\, dA_g = 0$ (the kernel of $\Delta_g$ consists of co
nstants, so the range is the $L^2$-orthogonal complement of constants). By Gauss
–Bonnet for the original metric,

$$\int_S K\, dA_g = 2\pi\,\chi(S) = 0.$$

Hence $K$ lies in the range of $\Delta_g$, and there exists a smooth solution $u
$. The metric $\tilde g = e^{2u}g$ is flat. $\checkmark$

---

#### Case $\chi(S) > 0$ (sphere, $c > 0$): variational / direct method

We normalize $c = 1$ (after rescaling). The PDE is

$$\Delta_g\, u = K - e^{2u}.$$

**Integrability condition.** Integrating: $\int_S e^{2u}\, dA_g = \int_S K\, dA_
g = 2\pi\chi(S) = 4\pi > 0$, consistent.

**Variational formulation.** The PDE is the Euler–Lagrange equation of the funct
ional

$$J[u] = \int_S \left(\tfrac{1}{2}|\nabla u|_g^2 + K\, u\right) dA_g + \pi \log\
!\left(\int_S e^{2u}\, dA_g\right)$$

(up to the constraint $\int e^{2u} dA_g = \text{const}$, handled by a Lagrange m
ultiplier that is absorbed into $c$). Equivalently, one works on the constrained
 set $\mathcal{M} = \{u : \int_S e^{2u} dA_g = 4\pi\}$ and minimizes

$$I[u] = \int_S \left(\tfrac{1}{2}|\nabla u|_g^2 + K\,u\right) dA_g \quad \text{
subject to } \int_S e^{2u}\, dA_g = 4\pi.$$

**Key estimate (Moser–Trudinger / Onofri inequality on $S^2$).** On the sphere,
the **Onofri inequality**

$$\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{S^2} |\nabla u|^2\, dA + \frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{S^2} u\, dA
\;\geq\; \log\!\left(\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{S^2} e^{2u}\, dA\right)$$

provides the coercivity needed to show $I$ is bounded below on $\mathcal{M}$ and
 attains its infimum. The minimizer (after adjusting by a constant to satisfy th
e constraint) solves the PDE. The direct method of calculus of variations (compa
ctness from Sobolev embedding $W^{1,2} \hookrightarrow L^2$ on a compact 2-manif
old, weak lower semicontinuity of the Dirichlet energy) yields a smooth minimize
r by elliptic regularity. $\checkmark$

---

#### Case $\chi(S) < 0$ (genus $\geq 2$, $c < 0$): variational method

We normalize $c = -1$. The PDE is

$$\Delta_g\, u = K + e^{2u}.$$

**Integrability condition.** $\int_S e^{2u}\, dA_g = -\int_S K\, dA_g = -2\pi\ch
i(S) > 0$, consistent.

**Variational formulation.** This is the Euler–Lagrange equation of

$$J[u] = \int_S \left(\tfrac{1}{2}|\nabla u|_g^2 + K\, u + \tfrac{1}{2}e^{2u}\ri
ght) dA_g.$$

**Coercivity.** The term $\tfrac{1}{2}e^{2u}$ grows superlinearly as $u \to +\in
fty$, while the linear term $K\,u$ is dominated. As $u \to -\infty$, $e^{2u} \to
 0$ but $K\,u \to +\infty$ (since $\int K\, dA = 2\pi\chi < 0$ means $K$ is "neg
ative on average", so $K\,u \to +\infty$ when $u \to -\infty$). More precisely,
decompose $u = \bar u + v$ with $\int v = 0$. The Poincaré inequality controls $
\|v\|_{W^{1,2}}$ by the Dirichlet energy, and the linear term $K\,u = \bar u \in
t K + \int K\,v$ forces $\bar u$ to stay bounded (since $\int K < 0$, large posi
tive $\bar u$ makes $J \to +\infty$; the exponential term prevents $\bar u \to -
\infty$). Thus $J$ is **coercive** on $W^{1,2}(S)$ and bounded below.

**Existence of minimizer.** By the direct method: take a minimizing sequence $\{
u_n\}$; coercivity gives boundedness in $W^{1,2}$; compactness gives a weakly co
nvergent subsequence $u_n \rightharpoonup u$; weak lower semicontinuity of $\int
 |\nabla u|^2$ and convexity of $e^{2u}$ (which is convex, so weakly lower semic
ontinuous) give $J[u] \leq \liminf J[u_n]$. The minimizer satisfies the Euler–La
grange equation, and elliptic regularity ($L^p$ estimates + bootstrapping) gives
 $u \in C^\infty(S)$. $\checkmark$

---

### Step 5. Elliptic regularity (common to all cases)

In every case, once a weak (distributional) solution $u \in W^{1,2}(S)$ of $\Del
ta_g u = K - c\,e^{2u}$ is obtained, the right-hand side $K - c\,e^{2u}$ is in $
L^\infty$ (since $u \in W^{1,2} \hookrightarrow L^p$ for all $p < \infty$ in 2D,
 hence $e^{2u} \in L^p$ for all $p$). Standard **Schauder / $L^p$ elliptic regul
arity** for the Laplace–Beltrami operator on a compact manifold bootstraps:

$$u \in W^{1,2} \;\Rightarrow\; u \in W^{2,p}\;\forall\, p < \infty \;\Rightarro
w\; u \in C^{1,\alpha} \;\Rightarrow\; e^{2u} \in C^{1,\alpha} \;\Rightarrow\; u
 \in C^{3,\alpha} \;\Rightarrow\; \cdots \;\Rightarrow\; u \in C^\infty(S).$$

### Step 6. Conclusion

In all three cases ($\chi > 0$, $\chi = 0$, $\chi < 0$), we have produced a smoo
th solution $u$ of the nonlinear PDE $\Delta_g u = K - c\,e^{2u}$ with $c$ of th
e correct sign. Setting $\tilde g = e^{2u} g$ yields a metric of constant Gaussi
an curvature $c$, with $\operatorname{sgn}(c) = \operatorname{sgn}(\chi(S))$. Th
is completes the proof of the uniformization theorem for compact Riemann surface
s. $\blacksquare$

**Summary of the logical structure:**

1. **Conformal ansatz** $\tilde g = e^{2u}g$ reduces the problem to finding a si
ngle scalar function $u$.
2. **Curvature transformation formula** $\tilde K = e^{-2u}(K - \Delta_g u)$ con
verts "constant curvature" into the **nonlinear elliptic PDE** $\Delta_g u = K -
 c\,e^{2u}$ (Liouville equation).
3. **Gauss–Bonnet** forces $\operatorname{sgn}(c) = \operatorname{sgn}(\chi(S))$
, splitting the problem into three cases.
4. **Case $\chi = 0$**: linear Poisson equation, solvable by Fredholm alternativ
e ($\int K = 0$).
5. **Case $\chi > 0$**: variational method + Onofri/Moser–Trudinger inequality p
rovides coercivity; minimizer solves the PDE.
6. **Case $\chi < 0$**: variational method; coercivity from the exponential term
 and the sign of $\int K$; direct method yields minimizer.
7. **Elliptic regularity** promotes weak solutions to smooth solutions in all ca
ses.
