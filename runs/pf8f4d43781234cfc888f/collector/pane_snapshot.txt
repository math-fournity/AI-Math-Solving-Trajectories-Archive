## Proof

an isomorphism of categories, i.e., bijective on objects and bijective on morphi

**Key hypothesis:** $G$ is simply connected, hence connected.

### Step 1: $d$ is bijective on morphisms (fully faithful)

The morphisms in $\operatorname{Rep}(G)$ are $G$-equivariant linear maps, and in
 $\operatorname{Rep}(\mathfrak{g})$ are $\mathfrak{g}$-equivariant linear maps.
The functor $d$ acts as the identity on the underlying linear maps. We must show
: a linear map $T: V_1 \to V_2$ is $G$-equivariant if and only if it is $\mathfr
ak{g}$-equivariant.

**($\Rightarrow$)** If $T\pi_1(g) = \pi_2(g)T$ for all $g \in G$, differentiate
at the identity: $T\,d\pi_1(X) = d\pi_2(X)\,T$ for all $X \in \mathfrak{g}$. So
$T$ is $\mathfrak{g}$-equivariant.

**($\Leftarrow$)** Suppose $T\,d\pi_1(X) = d\pi_2(X)\,T$ for all $X \in \mathfra
k{g}$. Define
$$H = \{g \in G : T\pi_1(g) = \pi_2(g)T\}.$$
This is a closed subgroup of $G$. Its Lie algebra is
$$\mathfrak{h} = \{X \in \mathfrak{g} : T\,d\pi_1(X) = d\pi_2(X)\,T\} = \mathfra
k{g},$$
by the $\mathfrak{g}$-equivariance of $T$. Since $\mathfrak{h} = \mathfrak{g}$,
the subgroup $H$ contains a neighborhood of the identity in $G$, so $H$ is an op
en subgroup of $G$. Since $G$ is connected, the only open subgroup is $G$ itself
. Therefore $H = G$, and $T$ is $G$-equivariant.

Thus $d$ is bijective on Hom-sets. $\checkmark$

### Step 2: $d$ is injective on objects

Suppose $\pi_1, \pi_2: G \to \operatorname{GL}(V)$ are two representations with
$d\pi_1 = d\pi_2$. Consider the set
$$K = \{g \in G : \pi_1(g) = \pi_2(g)\}.$$
This is a closed subgroup of $G$ with Lie algebra
$$\mathfrak{k} = \{X \in \mathfrak{g} : d\pi_1(X) = d\pi_2(X)\} = \mathfrak{g}.$
$
As above, $K$ is an open subgroup of the connected group $G$, so $K = G$, giving
 $\pi_1 = \pi_2$. $\checkmark$

### Step 3: $d$ is surjective on objects

Let $\rho: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{gl}(V)$ be any Lie algebra representation.
 By the **Lie integration theorem** (Lie's third theorem for representations): s
ince $G$ is simply connected, there exists a unique Lie group homomorphism $\pi:
 G \to \operatorname{GL}(V)$ with $d\pi = \rho$.

*Sketch of the integration argument:* The graph of $\rho$ defines a Lie subalgeb
ra of $\mathfrak{g} \oplus \mathfrak{gl}(V)$, which corresponds to a connected L

Thus every $\mathfrak{g}$-representation is $d\pi$ for some $G$-representation $
\pi$. $\checkmark$

### Conclusion

The functor $d$ is:
- **bijective on morphisms** (Step 1: fully faithful),
- **bijective on objects** (Step 2: injective; Step 3: surjective).

Therefore $d: \operatorname{Rep}(G) \to \operatorname{Rep}(\mathfrak{g})$ is an
**isomorphism of categories**.

The crucial ingredient is the simple connectivity of $G$: it guarantees both the
 existence (integration) and uniqueness (connectedness) needed for bijectivity o
n objects, while connectedness alone suffices for bijectivity on morphisms.
