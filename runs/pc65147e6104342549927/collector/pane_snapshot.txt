**Yes, $R \cong \mathbb{C}[G]$.**

**Step 1 (Cocycle setup).** Since $R = \bigoplus_{g \in G} R_g$ with $\dim_{\mat
hbb{C}} R_g = 1$, choose $x_g \in R_g \setminus \{0\}$ with $x_0 = 1_R$. Since $
R$ is a domain, $x_g \cdot x_h \neq 0$ for all $g, h$. As $x_g \cdot x_h \in R_{
g+h}$ and $\dim R_{g+h} = 1$, there exist unique $c(g,h) \in \mathbb{C}^*$ with
$$x_g \cdot x_h = c(g,h)\, x_{g+h}.$$

Associativity yields $c(g,h)\,c(g+h,k) = c(h,k)\,c(g,h+k)$; commutativity yields
 $c(g,h) = c(h,g)$; and $x_0 = 1_R$ yields $c(0,g) = 1$. Thus $c \in Z^2_{\mathr
m{sym}}(G, \mathbb{C}^*)$ is a normalized symmetric 2-cocycle.

**Step 2 (Every symmetric 2-cocycle is a coboundary).** We claim there exists $f
: G \to \mathbb{C}^*$ with $f(0)=1$ and $c(g,h) = f(g)\,f(h)\,/\,f(g+h)$.

*Proof of claim.* The group $\mathbb{C}^*$ is **divisible**: for every $z \in \m
athbb{C}^*$ and every $n \geq 1$, the equation $w^n = z$ has a solution $w \in \
mathbb{C}^*$ (since $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraically closed). Divisible abelian gro
ups are injective objects in the category of abelian groups, so $\operatorname{E
xt}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(G, \mathbb{C}^*) = 0$ for every abelian group $G$.

For an abelian group $G$ with trivial action on $A$, symmetric 2-cocycles modulo
 coboundaries classify abelian extensions of $G$ by $A$, which are classified by
 $\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(G, A)$:
$$Z^2_{\mathrm{sym}}(G, A) \,/\, B^2(G, A) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Ext}^1_{\math
bb{Z}}(G, A).$$

Setting $A = \mathbb{C}^*$ and using $\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(G, \math
bb{C}^*) = 0$, we conclude $Z^2_{\mathrm{sym}}(G, \mathbb{C}^*) = B^2(G, \mathbb
{C}^*)$, so $c$ is a coboundary. $\square$

**Step 3 (Rescaling).** Let $f: G \to \mathbb{C}^*$ with $f(0) = 1$ and $c(g,h)
= f(g)\,f(h)\,/\,f(g+h)$. Set $y_g = f(g)^{-1}\, x_g$. Then $y_0 = 1_R$ and
$$y_g \cdot y_h = f(g)^{-1} f(h)^{-1}\, c(g,h)\, x_{g+h} = \frac{c(g,h)}{f(g)\,f
(h)}\, x_{g+h} = \frac{1}{f(g+h)}\, x_{g+h} = y_{g+h}.$$

**Step 4 (Isomorphism).** Define $\varphi: \mathbb{C}[G] \to R$ by $\varphi(e_g)

- $\varphi(e_g \cdot e_h) = \varphi(e_{g+h}) = y_{g+h} = y_g \cdot y_h = \varphi
(e_g)\,\varphi(e_h)$, so $\varphi$ is a ring homomorphism.
- $\varphi(e_0) = y_0 = 1_R$, so $\varphi$ is unital and a $\mathbb{C}$-algebra
homomorphism.
- $\{y_g\}_{g \in G}$ is a $\mathbb{C}$-basis of $R$ (each $y_g$ is a nonzero el
ement of the 1-dimensional space $R_g$), so $\varphi$ is bijective.

Therefore $\varphi$ is an isomorphism of $\mathbb{C}$-algebras (indeed, of $G$-g
