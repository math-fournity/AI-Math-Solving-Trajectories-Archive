## Solution

We find two linearly independent solutions of Bessel's equation of order $\tfrac
{1}{2}$:

$$t^2 \frac{d^2 y}{dt^2} + t \frac{dy}{dt} + \left(t^2 - \frac{1}{4}\right)y = 0
.$$

### Substitution

The coefficient $\tfrac{1}{4} = \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^2$ suggests the standa
rd substitution that removes the first-derivative term and the constant term. Se
t

$$y(t) = t^{-1/2}\, u(t).$$

Compute the derivatives:

$$\frac{dy}{dt} = -\tfrac{1}{2}t^{-3/2}u + t^{-1/2}u',$$

$$\frac{d^2y}{dt^2} = \tfrac{3}{4}t^{-5/2}u - t^{-3/2}u' + t^{-1/2}u''.$$

### Substituting into the equation

$$t^2\!\left(\tfrac{3}{4}t^{-5/2}u - t^{-3/2}u' + t^{-1/2}u''\right) + t\!\left(
-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{-3/2}u + t^{-1/2}u'\right) + (t^2 - \tfrac{1}{4})\,t^{-1/2}u = 0
.$$

Expanding each term:

$$\tfrac{3}{4}t^{-1/2}u - t^{1/2}u' + t^{3/2}u'' - \tfrac{1}{2}t^{-1/2}u + t^{1/
2}u' + t^{3/2}u - \tfrac{1}{4}t^{-1/2}u = 0.$$

**Collecting the $u$ terms:**

$$\left(\tfrac{3}{4} - \tfrac{1}{2} - \tfrac{1}{4}\right)t^{-1/2}u = 0.$$

**Collecting the $u'$ terms:**

$$\left(-t^{1/2} + t^{1/2}\right)u' = 0.$$

Both vanish identically, leaving

$$t^{3/2}u'' + t^{3/2}u = 0.$$

### Reduced equation

Dividing by $t^{3/2}$ (for $t > 0$):

$$u'' + u = 0.$$

This is the simple harmonic oscillator equation, with general solution

$$u(t) = A\cos t + B\sin t.$$

### Two linearly independent solutions

Reverting to $y = t^{-1/2}u$:

$$y(t) = \frac{A\cos t + B\sin t}{\sqrt{t}}.$$

Taking $(A,B) = (1,0)$ and $(A,B) = (0,1)$ gives the two solutions

$$\boxed{y_1(t) = \frac{\cos t}{\sqrt{t}}, \qquad y_2(t) = \frac{\sin t}{\sqrt{t
}}.}$$

### Verification of linear independence

Suppose $c_1 y_1 + c_2 y_2 = 0$ on $(0,\infty)$. Multiplying by $\sqrt{t}$ gives

$$c_1 \cos t + c_2 \sin t = 0 \quad \text{for all } t > 0.$$

Evaluating at $t = 0^+$ (taking the limit) yields $c_1 = 0$; differentiating and
 evaluating at $t = 0^+$ yields $c_2 = 0$. Hence $y_1, y_2$ are linearly indepen
dent. $\blacksquare$
