Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $\text{im}(D \oplus L)$ is
necessarily closed when $D$ is Fredholm.

**Key reduction.** The image of $D \oplus L: X \oplus Z \to Y$, defined by $(x,z
) \mapsto Dx + Lz$, is:
$$\text{im}(D \oplus L) = \text{im}(D) + \text{im}(L).$$
So the question becomes: if $M := \text{im}(D)$ is closed with finite codimensio
n (since $D$ is Fredholm), is $M + \text{im}(L)$ necessarily closed for an arbit
rary bounded $L$?

**Proof.**

Since $D$ is Fredholm, $M = \text{im}(D)$ is a closed subspace of $Y$ with $\dim
(Y/M) = \operatorname{codim}(M) < \infty$.

Let $\pi: Y \to Y/M$ be the canonical quotient map, which is continuous and open
.

**Claim:** $M + \text{im}(L) = \pi^{-1}(\pi(\text{im}(L)))$.

*Proof of claim.*
- ($\subseteq$): If $y = m + Lz$ with $m \in M$, $z \in Z$, then $\pi(y) = \pi(m
) + \pi(Lz) = 0 + \pi(Lz) = \pi(Lz) \in \pi(\text{im}(L))$, so $y \in \pi^{-1}(\
pi(\text{im}(L)))$.
- ($\supseteq$): If $y \in \pi^{-1}(\pi(\text{im}(L)))$, then $\pi(y) = \pi(Lz)$
 for some $z \in Z$, so $y - Lz \in \ker\pi = M$, hence $y = (y - Lz) + Lz \in M
 + \text{im}(L)$. $\square$

Now, $Y/M$ is finite-dimensional. Every subspace of a finite-dimensional Hausdor
ff topological vector space is closed. Therefore $\pi(\text{im}(L))$ is a closed
 subspace of $Y/M$.

Since $\pi$ is continuous, $\pi^{-1}(\pi(\text{im}(L)))$ is closed in $Y$.

By the claim, $\text{im}(D \oplus L) = M + \text{im}(L) = \pi^{-1}(\pi(\text{im}
(L)))$ is closed. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The essential ingredient is that Fredholmness of $D$ forces $\text{i
m}(D)$ to have *finite codimension*, making the quotient $Y/\text{im}(D)$ finite
-dimensional, where every subspace is automatically closed. The continuity of th
e quotient map then pulls this closedness back to $Y$. No condition on $L$ (beyo
nd boundedness) is needed.
