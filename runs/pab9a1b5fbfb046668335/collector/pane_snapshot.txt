## Setup

We need all natural numbers $n$ with $1 \le n \le 120$ such that $2880 \mid P$ w
here
$$P = (n^2-4)(n^2-1)(n^2+3) = (n-2)(n+2)(n-1)(n+1)(n^2+3).$$

Factor the modulus: $2880 = 2^6 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5$.

We analyze each prime-power condition separately.

## Condition 1: $5 \mid P$

$n^2 \bmod 5 \in \{0,1,4\}$.

| $n^2 \bmod 5$ | $n^2{-}4$ | $n^2{-}1$ | $n^2{+}3$ | $P \bmod 5$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 | 4 | 3 | 2 |
| 1 | 2 | **0** | 4 | 0 |
| 4 | **0** | 3 | 2 | 0 |

So $5 \mid P \iff n^2 \not\equiv 0 \pmod{5} \iff 5 \nmid n$.

## Condition 2: $9 \mid P$

$n^2 \bmod 9 \in \{0,1,4,7\}$.

| $n^2 \bmod 9$ | $n^2{-}4$ | $n^2{-}1$ | $n^2{+}3$ | $P \bmod 9$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 5 | 8 | 3 | $5\cdot8\cdot3=120\equiv 3$ |
| 1 | 6 | **0** | 4 | 0 |
| 4 | **0** | 3 | 7 | 0 |
| 7 | 3 | 6 | 1 | $3\cdot6\cdot1=18\equiv 0$ |

So $9 \mid P \iff n^2 \not\equiv 0 \pmod{9} \iff 3 \nmid n$. (When $3\mid n$, $v
_3(P)=1$ exactly.)

## Condition 3: $64 \mid P$ (i.e. $2^6 \mid P$)

**Case A: $n$ even, $n=2k$, $k$ even (i.e. $4\mid n$).**
Then $n^2-4=4(k^2-1)$, $n^2-1$ and $n^2+3$ are both odd. So $v_2(P)=2+v_2(k^2-1)
$. Since $k$ even, $k\pm1$ are odd, giving $v_2(k^2-1)=0$. Thus $v_2(P)=2<6$. **
Fails.**

**Case B: $n$ even, $n=2k$, $k$ odd (i.e. $n\equiv 2\pmod 4$).**
Write $k=2m+1$, so $n=4m+2$. Then $v_2(P)=2+v_2\big((k{-}1)(k{+}1)\big)=2+v_2(2m
)+v_2(2(m{+}1))=4+v_2(m)+v_2(m{+}1)$.

Since $m,m+1$ are consecutive, $v_2(m)+v_2(m+1)\ge 1$. We need $\ge 6$, so $v_2(
m)+v_2(m+1)\ge 2$.

This **fails** when $v_2(m)+v_2(m+1)=1$, i.e. $m\equiv 1$ or $2\pmod 4$, corresp
onding to $n\equiv 6$ or $10\pmod{16}$.

**Succeeds** when $m\equiv 0$ or $3\pmod 4$, i.e. $n\equiv 2$ or $14\pmod{16}$.

**Case C: $n$ odd.**
$n^2-4$ is odd, $n^2-1$ and $n^2+3$ are even. Since $n^2\equiv 1\pmod 8$, we get
 $n^2+3\equiv 4\pmod 8$, so $v_2(n^2+3)=2$.

$v_2(P)=v_2(n^2-1)+2=v_2(n-1)+v_2(n+1)+2$.

For odd $n$, $n-1$ and $n+1$ are consecutive even numbers (one $\equiv 0\pmod 4$
, the other $\equiv 2\pmod 4$).

| $n \bmod 8$ | $v_2(n{-}1)$ | $v_2(n{+}1)$ | sum | $v_2(P)$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $\ge 3$ | 1 | $\ge 4$ | $\ge 6$ ✓ |
| 3 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 5 ✗ |
| 5 | 2 | 1 | 3 | 5 ✗ |
| 7 | 1 | $\ge 3$ | $\ge 4$ | $\ge 6$ ✓ |

So for odd $n$: $64\mid P \iff n\equiv 1$ or $7\pmod 8$.

## Combining the 2-adic condition

Valid residues mod 16: $\{1, 2, 7, 9, 14, 15\}$ (odd: $1,7,9,15$ from $n\equiv\p
m1\pmod 8$; even: $2,14$).

$$2880 \mid P \iff \begin{cases} 3\nmid n,\\ 5\nmid n,\\ n\bmod 16 \in \{1,2,7,9
,14,15\}.\end{cases}$$
