## Solution

**Setup.** Let $X_t = W_t - bt$. Then $X_t$ is a Brownian motion with drift $\mu
 = -b$ (i.e. $X_t = W_t + \mu t$ with $\mu = -b$), starting at $X_0 = 0$. The st
opping time becomes

$$\tau = \inf\{t \ge 0 : X_t = a\}, \qquad a < 0,$$

the first hitting time of level $a<0$ by a drifted Brownian motion started at $0
$.

**When is $\tau$ finite a.s. (so that $E[\tau]<\infty$ is meaningful)?** The lin
e $a+bt$ starts at $a<0$, below $W_0=0$. For a hit to occur, either the drift of
 $X_t$ must carry it toward $a$, i.e. $\mu = -b < 0$ (so $b>0$), giving a.s. hit
ting; or $\mu=0$ ($b=0$), also a.s. hitting but with $E[\tau]=a^2$. If $b<0$ ($\
mu>0$), the drift pushes $X_t$ away from $a<0$, and $\Pr(\tau<\infty)=e^{2\mu a}
=e^{-2ab}<1$, so $E[\tau]=\infty$. Hence the hypothesis $E[\tau]<\infty$ forces
$b\ge 0$, and the linear-boundary formula corresponds to $b>0$.

**Computing $E[\tau]$ via optional stopping (case $b>0$).** The process

$$M_t := X_t - \mu t = W_t$$

is a martingale. Since $b>0$ we have $\mu=-b<0$ and $a<0$, so $X_t$ drifts towar
d $a$ and $\tau<\infty$ a.s. With the standing assumption $E[\tau]<\infty$, opti
onal stopping applies (the stopped martingale $W_{t\wedge\tau}$ is uniformly int
egrable: $|W_{t\wedge\tau}|=|X_{t\wedge\tau}-\mu(t\wedge\tau)|\le |a|+|\mu|\tau$
, integrable). Thus

$$E[W_\tau] = E[W_0] = 0.$$

At the hitting time, $X_\tau = a$, i.e. $W_\tau - b\tau = a$, so $W_\tau = a + b
\tau$. Taking expectations:

$$0 = E[W_\tau] = E[a + b\tau] = a + b\,E[\tau].$$

Solving,

$$\boxed{E[\tau] = -\frac{a}{b} = \frac{|a|}{b}}, \qquad (a<0,\; b>0).$$

**Sanity checks.**
- *Dimensions:* $a$ has units of $W$ (distance), $b$ has units of $W/t$ (velocit
y), so $a/b$ has units of time. ✓
- *Scaling limit $b\to 0^+$:* $E[\tau]\to\infty$, consistent with the driftless
case $b=0$ where $E[\tau]=a^2<\infty$ — the formula diverges because the hitting
 mechanism changes character (diffusion-dominated rather than drift-dominated).
The finiteness assumption selects the drift-dominated regime $b>0$.
- *Sign:* $a<0$, $b>0$ gives $-a/b>0$. ✓
- *Drift intuition:* $X_t$ has mean $-bt$; the deterministic time to reach level
 $a$ at the mean drift rate $-b$ is exactly $|a|/b = -a/b$, and the martingale (
noise) part averages to zero. ✓

**Edge case $b=0$.** Then $\tau=\inf\{t:W_t=a\}$ with $a<0$ is the usual one-sid
ed hitting time of standard BM; $E[\tau]=a^2$, which is finite but is the separa
te diffusion-dominated regime, not captured by $-a/b$.

### Final answer

For $a<0$ and $b>0$ (the regime in which $E[\tau]<\infty$ via drift-dominated hi
tting),

$$E[\tau] = -\frac{a}{b}.$$
