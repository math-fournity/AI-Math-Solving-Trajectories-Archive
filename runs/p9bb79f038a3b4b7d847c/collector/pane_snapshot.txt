## Proof

**Step 1: The Lovász form of the Kruskal–Katona theorem.**

We use the following classical result:

> **Theorem (Lovász, 1979).** *If a graph $G$ has $\binom{x}{r}$ copies of $K_r$
 for some real $x \ge r$ (where $\binom{x}{r} = \frac{x(x-1)\cdots(x-r+1)}{r!}$)
, then $G$ has at most $\binom{x}{r+1}$ copies of $K_{r+1}$.*

*Proof sketch:* Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the family of $(r{+}1)$-cliques, $|\mathcal
{F}|=\binom{y}{r+1}$. Its lower shadow $\partial\mathcal{F}$ (all $r$-subsets of
 members of $\mathcal{F}$) consists of $r$-cliques, so $|\partial\mathcal{F}|\le
\binom{x}{r}$. By Kruskal–Katona, $|\partial\mathcal{F}|\ge\binom{y}{r}$. Since
$\binom{\cdot}{r}$ is increasing on $[r{-}1,\infty)$, we get $y\le x$, hence $|\
mathcal{F}|=\binom{y}{r+1}\le\binom{x}{r+1}$. $\square$

**Step 2: Applying with $r=3$.**

For any graph $G$ with $f(G)\ge 1$ triangles, let $x\ge 3$ be the unique real nu
mber with $\binom{x}{3}=f(G)$. By the theorem:
$$g(G)\le\binom{x}{4}.$$
Therefore:
$$\frac{g(G)^3}{f(G)^4}\le\frac{\binom{x}{4}^3}{\binom{x}{3}^4}.$$

**Step 3: Computing the ratio.**

Since $\binom{x}{4}=\frac{x(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)}{24}$ and $\binom{x}{3}=\frac{x(x-1)(
x-2)}{6}$, we have $\binom{x}{4}=\frac{x-3}{4}\binom{x}{3}$, so:

$$\frac{\binom{x}{4}^3}{\binom{x}{3}^4}=\left(\frac{x-3}{4}\right)^3\cdot\frac{1
}{\binom{x}{3}}=\frac{(x-3)^3}{64}\cdot\frac{6}{x(x-1)(x-2)}=\frac{3(x-3)^3}{32\
,x(x-1)(x-2)}=:h(x).$$

**Step 4: $h(x)$ is strictly increasing for $x>3$, with $\lim_{x\to\infty}h(x)=\
frac{3}{32}$.**

The limit is immediate: $h(x)\to\frac{3\cdot x^3}{32\cdot x^3}=\frac{3}{32}$.

For monotonicity, compute:
$$\frac{d}{dx}\ln h(x)=\frac{3}{x-3}-\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{x-1}-\frac{1}{x-2}.$$

We show this is positive for $x>3$. Cross-multiplying, this is equivalent to:
$$3\,x(x-1)(x-2)>(3x^2-6x+2)(x-3).$$

Expanding: LHS $=3x^3-9x^2+6x$, RHS $=3x^3-15x^2+20x-6$. The difference is:
$$6x^2-14x+6=2(3x^2-7x+3).$$

The quadratic $3x^2-7x+3$ has roots at $x=\frac{7\pm\sqrt{13}}{6}\approx 0.57,\;
1.77$, so it is positive for $x>1.77$, in particular for all $x>3$. Hence $h$ is
 strictly increasing on $(3,\infty)$.

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

- If $f(G)=0$: then $g(G)=0$ (every $K_4$ contains triangles), so $0\le c\cdot 0
$ holds for any $c$.
- If $f(G)\ge 1$: then $x\ge 3$ and $\frac{g(G)^3}{f(G)^4}\le h(x)<\frac{3}{32}$
.

Thus $g(G)^3\le\frac{3}{32}\,f(G)^4$ for every graph $G$.

**Step 6: Tightness.**

For the complete graph $K_n$: $f(K_n)=\binom{n}{3}$, $g(K_n)=\binom{n}{4}$, so $
x=n$ and:
$$\frac{g(K_n)^3}{f(K_n)^4}=h(n)=\frac{3(n-3)^3}{32\,n(n-1)(n-2)}\xrightarrow{n\
to\infty}\frac{3}{32}.$$

Since the supremum $\frac{3}{32}$ is approached (but never attained) by complete
 graphs, no smaller constant suffices.

$$\boxed{c=\dfrac{3}{32}}$$
