## Solution

**Key observation.** Since $r+1$ is a root of $Q(x)=x^3+cx^2+dx+1$, the value $r
$ is a root of $Q(x+1)$. Since $P$ is an irreducible cubic over $\mathbb{Q}$, it
 is the minimal polynomial of $r$, so $P(x)=Q(x+1)$ (both monic cubics).

**Expanding** $Q(x+1)$:
$$Q(x+1)=(x+1)^3+c(x+1)^2+d(x+1)+1=x^3+(3+c)x^2+(3+2c+d)x+(2+c+d).$$

Matching with $P(x)=x^3+ax^2+bx-1$:
$$a=3+c,\quad b=3+2c+d,\quad 2+c+d=-1.$$
From the third, $c+d=-3$; substituting into the second gives $b=3+2c+(-3-c)=c$,
hence $a=3+b$, i.e.
$$\boxed{a-b=3.}$$

**Consequence for the roots.** The roots of $Q$ are $r+1,\,s+1,\,t+1$ where $r,s
,t$ are the roots of $P$. The product of roots of $Q$ is $-1$:
$$(r+1)(s+1)(t+1)=-1.$$
Expanding and using $rst=1$, $r+s+t=-a$, $rs+rt+st=b$:
$$1+b-a+1=-1 \implies a-b=3,$$
confirming consistency.

**Eliminating $a,b$.** With $t=1/(rs)$ and $b=rs+\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{s}$ (from
$rs+rt+st$) and also $b=-(r+s+t)-3$ (from $r+s+t=-(b+3)$):
$$rs+\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{s}=-r-s-\frac{1}{rs}-3.$$
Multiplying through by $rs$:
$$r^2s^2+r^2s+rs^2+3rs+r+s+1=0.$$

**Solving for $s$.** Treating this as a quadratic in $s$:
$$r(r+1)\,s^2+(r^2+3r+1)\,s+(r+1)=0.$$
The discriminant is
$$(r^2+3r+1)^2-4r(r+1)^2=r^4+2r^3+3r^2+2r+1=(r^2+r+1)^2,$$
a perfect square. Therefore
$$s=\frac{-(r^2+3r+1)\pm(r^2+r+1)}{2r(r+1)}.$$

The two solutions are:
$$s_1=\frac{-2r}{2r(r+1)}=-\frac{1}{r+1},\qquad s_2=\frac{-2(r+1)^2}{2r(r+1)}=-\
frac{r+1}{r}.$$

**Verification.** With $s=-\frac{1}{r+1}$ and $t=-\frac{r+1}{r}$:
- $rst = r\cdot\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{r+1}\right)\!\cdot\!\left(-\tfrac{r+1}{r}\righ
t)=1$. ✓
- $rs+rt+st = -\tfrac{r}{r+1}-(r+1)+\tfrac{1}{r}=\tfrac{-r^2-r(r+1)^2+(r+1)}{r(r
+1)}=\tfrac{-(r^3+3r^2)+1}{r(r+1)}=0$ using $r^3+3r^2=1$ (from $r^3+(b+3)r^2+br-
1=0$ with the relation). ✓

Both other roots are expressed purely in terms of $r$. Taking either one as "ano
ther root $s$":

$$\boxed{s=-\dfrac{1}{r+1}}$$

(and the third root is $-\dfrac{r+1}{r}$, neither depending on $a,b,c,d$).
