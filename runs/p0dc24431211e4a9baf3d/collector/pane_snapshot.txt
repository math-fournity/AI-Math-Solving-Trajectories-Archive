## Proof

**Claim:** The only polynomial $p \in \mathbb{C}[x]$ satisfying $p(0)=1$ and $|p
(z)|\le 1$ for all $|z|=1$ is the constant polynomial $p\equiv 1$.

**Proof.**

Let $p\in\mathbb{C}[x]$ satisfy the two conditions. Since $p$ is a polynomial, i
t is holomorphic on all of $\mathbb{C}$, hence on the closed unit disk $\overlin
e{D}=\{z:|z|\le 1\}$.

By the **maximum modulus principle**, the maximum of $|p|$ on $\overline{D}$ is
attained on the boundary $\partial D=\{z:|z|=1\}$. The hypothesis gives $|p(z)|\
le 1$ on $\partial D$, so

$$|p(z)|\le 1 \quad\text{for all } z\in\overline{D}.$$

In particular, $\max_{z\in\overline{D}}|p(z)|\le 1$.

Now evaluate at the origin:

$$|p(0)| = |1| = 1.$$

Thus $|p|$ attains the value $1$ at the **interior point** $z=0$, which equals $
\max_{z\in\overline{D}}|p(z)|$. By the maximum modulus principle (in its strong
form: if $|f|$ attains its maximum at an interior point of a connected domain, t
hen $f$ is constant), $p$ must be constant on $\overline{D}$, and hence on all o
f $\mathbb{C}$.

Since $p(0)=1$, the constant is $1$, so

$$p(z)\equiv 1.$$

**Verification.** The polynomial $p(z)=1$ satisfies $p(0)=1$ and $|p(z)|=1\le 1$
 for every $z$ with $|z|=1$. ✓

**Conclusion.** The unique polynomial satisfying both conditions is $p\equiv 1$.
