## Proof

**Setup.** Define $Q(a) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i a_{i+1}$ (cyclic, $a_{n+1}=a_1$). T
his is a quadratic form $Q = \tfrac{1}{2} a^T M a$, where $M$ is the adjacency m
atrix of the cycle $C_n$ (symmetric, $M_{i,i+1}=M_{i+1,i}=1$ cyclically, else $0
$). The factor $\tfrac12$ arises since each edge $\{i,i+1\}$ contributes twice i
n $a^T M a$.

**Eigenvalues.** The eigenvalues of $M$ are $\lambda_k = 2\cos(2\pi k/n)$ for $k
=0,1,\dots,n-1$, with eigenvector $v_k=(1,\omega^k,\omega^{2k},\dots,\omega^{(n-
1)k})$, $\omega=e^{2\pi i/n}$. The $k=0$ eigenvector is $(1,1,\dots,1)$ with $\l
ambda_0=2$.

**Restriction to the hyperplane.** The constraint $\sum a_i = 0$ means $a \perp
(1,\dots,1)$, i.e., $a$ has no $k=0$ component. On this hyperplane, the eigenval
ues of $Q$ are
$$\mu_k = \tfrac{1}{2}\lambda_k = \cos\!\left(\tfrac{2\pi k}{n}\right), \quad k=
1,2,\dots,n-1.$$

**Condition for $Q\le 0$.** We need $Q\le 0$ for all $a$ on the hyperplane, i.e.
, all eigenvalues $\mu_k \le 0$. The largest eigenvalue is $\mu_1 = \cos(2\pi/n)
$ (since $2\pi/n$ is the angle closest to $0$ among $\{2\pi k/n : 1\le k\le n-1\
}$, and cosine is decreasing on $[0,\pi]$ then increasing). So the condition red
uces to:
$$\cos\!\left(\tfrac{2\pi}{n}\right) \le 0 \iff \tfrac{2\pi}{n} \ge \tfrac{\pi}{
2} \iff n \le 4.$$

Combined with $n\ge 3$: **$n \in \{3, 4\}$**.

**Verification.**

*$n=3$:* $Q = a_1a_2+a_2a_3+a_3a_1$. From $(a_1+a_2+a_3)^2=0$:
$$a_1^2+a_2^2+a_3^2 + 2Q = 0 \implies Q = -\tfrac{1}{2}(a_1^2+a_2^2+a_3^2)\le 0.
 \checkmark$$

*$n=4$:* $Q = a_1a_2+a_2a_3+a_3a_4+a_4a_1 = (a_1+a_3)(a_2+a_4)$. With $\sum a_i=
0$, set $s=a_1+a_3=-(a_2+a_4)$, so $Q = s\cdot(-s)=-s^2\le 0. \checkmark$

*$n=5$ (counterexample):* Take $a_1=a_2=1$, $a_3=a_4=a_5=-\tfrac{2}{3}$. Sum $=
2-2=0$. Then
$$Q = 1 + (-\tfrac{2}{3}) + \tfrac{4}{9} + \tfrac{4}{9} + (-\tfrac{2}{3}) = \tfr
ac{9-12+8}{9} = \tfrac{5}{9} > 0. \quad\checkmark$$

For $n\ge 5$, the eigenvector $v_1$ (restricted to the hyperplane) gives $Q>0$,
so the inequality fails.

## Conclusion

The integers $n\ge 3$ for which $a_1a_2 + a_2a_3 + \cdots + a_na_1 \le 0$ holds
for all reals with $\sum a_i = 0$ are exactly $\boxed{n=3 \text{ and } n=4}$.
