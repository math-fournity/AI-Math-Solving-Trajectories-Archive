## Proof

Let $S^2 \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ be the unit sphere, and let $P_n$ denote the spac
e of restrictions to $S^2$ of complex-valued polynomials in $(x,y,z)$ of total d
egree $\leq n$.

**Step 1: Spherical harmonics as eigenfunctions.**

The eigenfunctions of the spherical Laplacian $\Delta_{\text{sph}}$ on $S^2$ are
 the spherical harmonics $Y_\ell^m$, $\ell = 0,1,2,\dots$, $m = -\ell,\dots,\ell
$, satisfying
$$\Delta_{\text{sph}} Y_\ell^m = -\ell(\ell+1)\,Y_\ell^m.$$
For each $\ell$, the space $\mathcal{H}_\ell = \mathrm{span}\{Y_\ell^m : -\ell \
le m \le \ell\}$ has dimension $2\ell+1$.

Concretely, $\mathcal{H}_\ell$ is the restriction to $S^2$ of the space of **har
monic homogeneous polynomials** of degree $\ell$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$ (polynomials
$h$ with $\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^3} h = 0$). Every such polynomial has total degree
exactly $\ell$, so $\mathcal{H}_\ell \subset P_\ell \subset P_n$ for all $\ell \
le n$.

**Step 2: $P_n$ is invariant under $\Delta_{\text{sph}}$, and the decomposition.
**

On $S^2$ we have $r^2 = x^2+y^2+z^2 = 1$. The key algebraic fact is the decompos
ition of homogeneous polynomials:
$$\{\text{homogeneous polynomials of degree } \ell\} = \mathcal{H}_\ell \oplus r
^2\,\mathcal{H}_{\ell-2} \oplus r^4\,\mathcal{H}_{\ell-4} \oplus \cdots$$
where $r^2 = x^2+y^2+z^2$. This follows because the Laplacian $\Delta_{\mathbb{R
}^3}$ maps homogeneous polynomials of degree $\ell$ onto homogeneous polynomials
 of degree $\ell-2$, with kernel exactly the harmonic polynomials $\mathcal{H}_\
ell$.

Restricting to $S^2$ where $r^2 = 1$, the factor $r^{2k}$ becomes $1$, so the re
striction of any homogeneous polynomial of degree $\ell$ to $S^2$ lies in
$$\mathcal{H}_\ell \oplus \mathcal{H}_{\ell-2} \oplus \mathcal{H}_{\ell-4} \oplu
s \cdots.$$
Summing over homogeneous degrees $0, 1, \dots, n$, every term $\mathcal{H}_j$ wi
th $j \le n$ appears. Therefore
$$P_n = \bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{n} \mathcal{H}_\ell.$$

**Step 3: Dimension check.**

$$\dim P_n = \sum_{\ell=0}^{n}(2\ell+1) = (n+1)^2.$$

Independently, the space of polynomials of degree $\le n$ in 3 variables has dim
ension $\binom{n+3}{3}$, and on $S^2$ the relation $r^2=1$ identifies a polynomi
al $p$ with $r^2 p$, killing a subspace of dimension $\binom{n+1}{3}$ (polynomia
ls of degree $\le n-2$ multiplied by $r^2$). Thus
$$\dim P_n = \binom{n+3}{3} - \binom{n+1}{3} = \frac{(n+1)[(n+3)(n+2)-n(n-1)]}{6
} = \frac{(n+1)(6n+6)}{6} = (n+1)^2,$$
confirming the decomposition is complete.

**Step 4: Eigenvalues and multiplicities.**

Since $P_n = \bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{n} \mathcal{H}_\ell$ and each $\mathcal{H}_\ell
$ is the eigenspace of $\Delta_{\text{sph}}$ with eigenvalue $-\ell(\ell+1)$, th
e eigenvalues of $\Delta_{\text{sph}}$ on $P_n$ are:

$$\boxed{\lambda_\ell = -\ell(\ell+1), \quad \ell = 0, 1, \dots, n, \quad \text{
with multiplicity } 2\ell+1.}$$

The total dimension checks out: $\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}(2\ell+1) = (n+1)^2 = \dim P_n
$. ∎
