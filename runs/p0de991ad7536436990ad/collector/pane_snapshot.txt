## Solution

We determine all triples $(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^3$ satisfying
$$
\begin{cases}
x^2+xy = y^2+z^2 \quad &(1)\\
z^2+zy = y^2+x^2 \quad &(2)
\end{cases}
$$

### Step 1. A key factorization

From (2): $\;x^2 = z^2+zy-y^2$.
From (1): $\;x^2 = y^2+z^2-xy$.

Equating the two expressions for $x^2$:
$$
z^2+zy-y^2 = y^2+z^2-xy
\;\Longrightarrow\; zy-y^2 = y^2-xy
\;\Longrightarrow\; y(x+z) = 2y^2
\;\Longrightarrow\; y(x+z-2y)=0.
$$

Hence either **$y=0$** or **$x+z=2y$**.

### Step 2. Case A: $y=0$

With $y=0$, equation (1) becomes $x^2=z^2$, i.e. $z=\pm x$. Equation (2) becomes
 $z^2=x^2$, which is the same condition. Thus every pair $(x,z)$ with $x^2=z^2$
works, giving two families:
$$
(t,0,t)\quad\text{and}\quad (t,0,-t),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.
$$

### Step 3. Case B: $y\neq 0$ and $x+z=2y$

Then $z=2y-x$. Substitute into (1):
$$
x^2+xy = y^2+(2y-x)^2 = y^2+4y^2-4xy+x^2 = 5y^2-4xy+x^2.
$$
Canceling $x^2$:
$$
xy = -4xy+5y^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; 5xy=5y^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; xy=y^2.
$$
Since $y\neq 0$, we get $x=y$, and then $z=2y-x=y$. So $x=y=z$, giving the famil
y
$$
(t,t,t),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.
$$
A direct check confirms (1) and (2): both sides equal $2t^2$.

### Step 4. Verification that no solutions are missed

The factorization in Step 1 is an equivalence: every solution must satisfy $y(x+
z-2y)=0$, and we treated both branches exhaustively. Within each branch the subs
equent algebra was reversible (substitution and cancellation are equivalences, a
nd division by $y$ in Case B is legitimate since $y\neq 0$ there). Hence the thr
ee families together constitute exactly the solution set.

### Step 5. The complete solution set

Collecting the families (the origin $(0,0,0)$ belongs to all three, which is har
mless for a union):

$$
\boxed{\,(t,t,t),\;\; (t,0,t),\;\; (t,0,-t),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.\,}
$$

These are three lines through the origin in $\mathbb{R}^3$: the main diagonal, a
nd the two lines in the $xz$-plane bisecting the angles between the $x$- and $z$
-axes.
