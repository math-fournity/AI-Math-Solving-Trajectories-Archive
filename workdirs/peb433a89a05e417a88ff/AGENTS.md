# Problem

In a futuristic circular city, a ring of $n$ high-tech docking stations is arranged such that they form the vertices of a regular $n$-gon. To ensure the city's aesthetic diversity, the Grand Architect mandates that each station must be assigned exactly one of three neon signal lights: Red, Yellow, or Blue.

The Department of Urban Symmetry is searching for the smallest positive integer $n$ that guarantees a specific structural pattern will always emerge, regardless of how the colors are distributed among the $n$ stations. Specifically, they are looking for the minimum $n$ such that there will always be four stations assigned the same color that serve as the corners of an isosceles trapezoid. For this requirement, an isosceles trapezoid is defined as a convex quadrilateral with at least one pair of parallel sides and a pair of non-parallel sides of equal length.

Find the smallest positive integer $n$ for which this color-coded symmetry is mathematically guaranteed to exist.
