**Proof.** Let $\kappa$ be a regular uncountable cardinal, and let $\langle C_\a
lpha : \alpha < \kappa\rangle$ be a sequence of club subsets of $\kappa$. Define
 the diagonal intersection
$$D \;=\; \bigtriangledown_{\alpha<\kappa} C_\alpha \;=\; \{\xi < \kappa : \fora
ll \beta < \xi,\; \xi \in C_\beta\}.$$
We show $D$ is closed and unbounded in $\kappa$.

---

**$D$ is unbounded.** Let $\gamma < \kappa$; we find $\xi \in D$ with $\xi > \ga
mma$.

Build an increasing sequence $\langle \gamma_n : n < \omega\rangle$ as follows.
Set $\gamma_0 = \gamma + 1$. Given $\gamma_n < \kappa$, the set $\bigcap_{\beta
\le \gamma_n} C_\beta$ is an intersection of fewer than $\kappa$ many clubs (sin
ce $\gamma_n < \kappa$ and $\kappa$ is regular), hence is itself club in $\kappa
$. In particular it is unbounded, so choose
$$\gamma_{n+1} > \gamma_n \quad\text{with}\quad \gamma_{n+1} \in \bigcap_{\beta
\le \gamma_n} C_\beta.$$

Since $\kappa$ is regular and the sequence has length $\omega < \kappa$, the sup
remum
$$\xi \;=\; \sup_{n<\omega} \gamma_n \;<\; \kappa.$$
Clearly $\xi > \gamma_0 > \gamma$. We claim $\xi \in D$.

Let $\beta < \xi$. Since $\langle \gamma_n\rangle$ is increasing with supremum $
\xi$, there exists $n$ with $\beta < \gamma_n$. Then $\gamma_{n+1} \in \bigcap_{
\beta' \le \gamma_n} C_{\beta'} \subseteq C_\beta$, and likewise $\gamma_m \in C
_\beta$ for every $m > n$ (since $\beta < \gamma_n \le \gamma_{m-1}$). Thus $\la
ngle \gamma_m : m > n\rangle$ is an increasing sequence of elements of $C_\beta$
 with supremum $\xi$. As $C_\beta$ is closed, $\xi \in C_\beta$.

Since $\beta < \xi$ was arbitrary, $\xi \in D$. Hence $D$ is unbounded.

---

**$D$ is closed.** Let $\delta < \kappa$ be a limit ordinal, and let $\langle \x
i_i : i < \lambda\rangle$ (for some limit $\lambda$) be an increasing sequence o
f elements of $D$ with $\sup_{i<\lambda} \xi_i = \delta$. We show $\delta \in D$
.

Let $\beta < \delta$. Since $\delta = \sup_{i<\lambda} \xi_i$ and the sequence i
s increasing, there exists $i_0 < \lambda$ with $\beta < \xi_{i_0}$. For every $
i \ge i_0$, we have $\xi_i \in D$ and $\beta < \xi_{i_0} \le \xi_i$, so by defin
ition of $D$, $\xi_i \in C_\beta$. Therefore $\langle \xi_i : i_0 \le i < \lambd
a\rangle \subseteq C_\beta$ is an increasing sequence with supremum $\delta$. Si
nce $C_\beta$ is closed, $\delta \in C_\beta$.

Since $\beta < \delta$ was arbitrary, $\delta \in D$. Hence $D$ is closed.

---

**Conclusion.** $D = \bigtriangledown_{\alpha<\kappa} C_\alpha$ is both closed a
nd unbounded in $\kappa$, i.e., $D$ is a club set. $\blacksquare$
