# Problem

In the competitive world of high-tech agriculture, a triangular plot of fertile land, denoted as Field $ABC$, has a total production capacity of exactly $1$ unit of biofuel. Two automated irrigation lines, $BE$ and $CD$, are installed across the field such that point $D$ lies on the boundary $AB$ and point $E$ lies on the boundary $AC$. These two lines intersect at a central control hub, point $P$.

The landscaping team must adjust the positions of the connection points $D$ and $E$ along their respective boundaries. However, due to soil pressure constraints, they must always maintain a specific equilibrium: the area of the quadrilateral region $BCED$ must be exactly twice the area of the triangular buffer zone $PBC$.

As the points $D$ and $E$ shift to satisfy this equilibrium, the area of the surveillance zone $PDE$ (the triangle formed by the intersection $P$ and the boundary points $D$ and $E$) changes. 

Calculate the maximum possible area that the surveillance zone $PDE$ can reach.
