## Proof

**Claim:** The set $S := \{f \in C^{2}[0,1] : f(0) = f(1) = 0\}$ is dense in $L^
{2}[0,1]$.

The strategy is to exhibit a well-known dense subspace of $L^{2}[0,1]$ that is e
ntirely contained in $S$, from which the claim follows immediately.

---

### Step 1. A dense subspace contained in $S$

Let $C_{c}^{\infty}(0,1)$ denote the space of smooth (infinitely differentiable)
 functions on $(0,1)$ with compact support contained in $(0,1)$. We show:

$$C_{c}^{\infty}(0,1) \subseteq S \subseteq L^{2}[0,1].$$

The second inclusion is trivial since $C^{2}[0,1] \subset C[0,1] \subset L^{2}[0
,1]$ (the interval is compact, so every continuous function is square-integrable
).

For the first inclusion, let $\varphi \in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,1)$. By definition th
ere exists $\delta > 0$ such that $\operatorname{supp}(\varphi) \subseteq [\delt
a, 1-\delta]$. In particular $\varphi$ vanishes on $[0,\delta)$ and on $(1-\delt
a, 1]$. Extending $\varphi$ by $0$ to all of $[0,1]$ gives a function that is:

- **Smooth on $[0,1]$:** near $0$, $\varphi \equiv 0$ on $[0,\delta)$, so all de
rivatives exist and equal $0$ at $x=0$; similarly at $x=1$. Hence $\varphi \in C
^{\infty}[0,1] \subseteq C^{2}[0,1]$.
- **Vanishing at the endpoints:** $\varphi(0) = 0$ and $\varphi(1) = 0$.

Therefore $\varphi \in S$, establishing $C_{c}^{\infty}(0,1) \subseteq S$.

---

### Step 2. $C_{c}^{\infty}(0,1)$ is dense in $L^{2}[0,1]$

We prove this in two stages.

**Stage 2a. $C[0,1]$ is dense in $L^{2}[0,1]$.**

The simple functions (finite linear combinations of characteristic functions $\m
athbf{1}_{E}$ of measurable sets $E \subseteq [0,1]$) are dense in $L^{2}[0,1]$
by the definition of the Lebesgue integral / standard approximation theorem. By
regularity of Lebesgue measure, for every measurable $E \subseteq [0,1]$ and eve
ry $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists a compact $K$ and an open $U$ with $K \subsete
q E \subseteq U$ and $m(U \setminus K) < \varepsilon$. Urysohn's lemma gives a c
ontinuous $h : [0,1] \to [0,1]$ with $h|_{K} \equiv 1$ and $h|_{[0,1]\setminus U
} \equiv 0$, so $\|\mathbf{1}_{E} - h\|_{2}^{2} \leq m(U \setminus K) < \varepsi
lon$. By linearity and density of simple functions, $C[0,1]$ is dense in $L^{2}[
0,1]$.

**Stage 2b. Every $g \in C[0,1]$ is approximable in $L^{2}$ by elements of $C_{c
}^{\infty}(0,1)$.**

Fix $g \in C[0,1]$ and $\varepsilon > 0$.

*Cut-off.* Choose a smooth function $\eta_{\delta} \in C^{\infty}[0,1]$ with $0
\leq \eta_{\delta} \leq 1$, $\eta_{\delta} \equiv 1$ on $[\delta, 1-\delta]$, an
d $\eta_{\delta} \equiv 0$ on $[0,\delta/2] \cup [1-\delta/2, 1]$, for small $\d
elta > 0$. Set $g_{\delta} := g \cdot \eta_{\delta}$. Then $g_{\delta} \in C[0,1
]$ with $g_{\delta} \equiv 0$ near $0$ and $1$, and

$$\|g - g_{\delta}\|_{2}^{2} = \int_{0}^{\delta}|g|^{2}(1-\eta_{\delta})^{2} + \
int_{1-\delta}^{1}|g|^{2}(1-\eta_{\delta})^{2} \leq 2\|g\|_{\infty}^{2}\,\delta
\xrightarrow{\delta \to 0} 0.$$

So for $\delta$ small enough, $\|g - g_{\delta}\|_{2} < \varepsilon/2$.

*Mollification.* Extend $g_{\delta}$ by $0$ to all of $\mathbb{R}$ (this extensi
on is continuous since $g_{\delta}$ vanishes near the endpoints of $[0,1]$). Let
 $\rho_{\sigma}(x) = \sigma^{-1}\rho(x/\sigma)$ be a standard smooth mollifier w
ith $\operatorname{supp}(\rho) \subseteq [-1,1]$. For $\sigma < \delta/2$, the c
onvolution

$$g_{\delta} * \rho_{\sigma}$$

is smooth on $\mathbb{R}$, has support in $[\delta/2 - \sigma,\, 1-\delta/2 + \s
igma] \subseteq (0,1)$, hence restricts to an element of $C_{c}^{\infty}(0,1)$.
Standard mollification estimates give $\|g_{\delta} - g_{\delta}*\rho_{\sigma}\|
_{\infty} \to 0$ as $\sigma \to 0$ (since $g_{\delta}$ is uniformly continuous),
 and therefore $\|g_{\delta} - g_{\delta}*\rho_{\sigma}\|_{2} \to 0$. Choose $\s
igma$ small enough that $\|g_{\delta} - g_{\delta}*\rho_{\sigma}\|_{2} < \vareps
ilon/2$.

Combining,

$$\|g - g_{\delta}*\rho_{\sigma}\|_{2} < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsil
on}{2} = \varepsilon, \qquad g_{\delta}*\rho_{\sigma} \in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,1).$$

Thus $C_{c}^{\infty}(0,1)$ is dense in $C[0,1]$ with respect to $\|\cdot\|_{2}$,
 and since $C[0,1]$ is dense in $L^{2}[0,1]$, we conclude $C_{c}^{\infty}(0,1)$
is dense in $L^{2}[0,1]$.

---

### Step 3. Conclusion

We have the chain of inclusions

$$C_{c}^{\infty}(0,1) \subseteq S = \{f \in C^{2}[0,1] : f(0)=f(1)=0\} \subseteq
 L^{2}[0,1].$$

Since $C_{c}^{\infty}(0,1)$ is dense in $L^{2}[0,1]$ (Step 2) and is contained i
n $S$, for every $g \in L^{2}[0,1]$ and every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $\v
arphi \in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,1) \subseteq S$ with $\|g - \varphi\|_{2} < \varepsil
on$. Hence $S$ is dense in $L^{2}[0,1]$.

**The set $\{f \in C^{2}[0,1] : f(0) = f(1) = 0\}$ is dense in $L^{2}[0,1]$.** $
\blacksquare$
