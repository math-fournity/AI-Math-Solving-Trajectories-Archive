## Proof

**Setup and notation.** Let $B_1, B_2$ be independent standard Brownian motions.
 For $a \neq 0$, set $B^a_i(t) = a^{-1} B_i(a^2 t)$ ($i=1,2$); each $B^a_i$ is a
 standard Brownian motion (scaling invariance, using that $-B_i$ is BM when $a<0
$). Define
$$T = \inf\{t\ge 0: B_2(t)=b\}, \qquad T^a = \inf\{t\ge 0: B^a_2(t)=b\}.$$

**Step 1: Express $T^a$ in terms of $B_2$.** Since $B^a_2(t)=b \iff a^{-1}B_2(a^
2 t)=b \iff B_2(a^2 t)=ab$, with the change of variable $s=a^2 t$,
$$T^a = \frac{1}{a^2}\,T_{ab}, \qquad T_{ab}:=\inf\{s\ge 0: B_2(s)=ab\}.$$
In particular $a^2 T^a = T_{ab}$, and $T = T_b$.

**Step 2: Express $B^a_1(T^a)$.**
$$B^a_1(T^a) = a^{-1} B_1(a^2 T^a) = a^{-1} B_1(T_{ab}).$$

**Step 3: Use independence of $B_1$ and the hitting times.** Since $B_1 \perp B_
2$, the random variable $B_1$ is independent of any functional of $B_2$, in part
icular of $T_b$ and $T_{ab}$. Conditioning on the hitting time $\tau$ (either $T
_b$ or $T_{ab}$):
$$B_1(\tau)\mid \tau = s \;\sim\; N(0,s),$$
so $B_1(\tau) \stackrel{d}{=} \sqrt{\tau}\, Z$ with $Z\sim N(0,1)$ independent o
f $\tau$.

**Step 4: Scaling of hitting times.** For a standard Brownian motion, $T_c \stac
krel{d}{=} c^2\, T_1$ (the Lévy distribution satisfies $P(T_c\in dt)=\frac{|c|}{
\sqrt{2\pi t^3}}e^{-c^2/(2t)}dt$, which is the law of $c^2 T_1$). Hence
$$T_b \stackrel{d}{=} b^2 T_1, \qquad T_{ab} \stackrel{d}{=} a^2 b^2\, T_1.$$

**Step 5: Compare the two distributions.**
$$B_1(T) = B_1(T_b) \stackrel{d}{=} \sqrt{T_b}\, Z \stackrel{d}{=} \sqrt{b^2 T_1
}\, Z = |b|\sqrt{T_1}\, Z,$$
$$B^a_1(T^a) = a^{-1} B_1(T_{ab}) \stackrel{d}{=} a^{-1}\sqrt{T_{ab}}\, Z \stack
rel{d}{=} a^{-1}\sqrt{a^2 b^2 T_1}\, Z = a^{-1}|a|\,|b|\sqrt{T_1}\, Z = \operato
rname{sgn}(a)\,|b|\sqrt{T_1}\, Z.$$

**Step 6: Symmetry removes the sign.** The random variable $|b|\sqrt{T_1}\, Z$ i
s symmetric about $0$ (since $Z\sim N(0,1)$ is symmetric and independent of $T_1
$). Therefore
$$\operatorname{sgn}(a)\,|b|\sqrt{T_1}\, Z \stackrel{d}{=} |b|\sqrt{T_1}\, Z.$$

Combining Steps 5 and 6,
$$B^a_1(T^a) \stackrel{d}{=} B_1(T).$$

**Conclusion.** Yes, $B_1(T)$ and $B^a_1(T^a)$ have the same distribution. The t
ime-rescaling that sends $T$ to $T^a$ exactly compensates the spatial rescaling
$a^{-1}$ in $B^a_1$, up to a sign $\operatorname{sgn}(a)$ that is absorbed by th
e symmetry of the centered Gaussian mixture $|b|\sqrt{T_1}\,Z$.
