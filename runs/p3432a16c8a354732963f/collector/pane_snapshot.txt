## Solution

We evaluate
$$I_k := \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} e^{ik(x-\sin x)}\,dx, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}
.$$

### Step 1. Generating function of the Bessel functions

The Bessel functions of the first kind $\{J_n(z)\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}$ are defined
 by the generating function (Jacobi–Anger expansion)
$$e^{iz\sin\theta} = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} J_n(z)\,e^{in\theta}, \qquad z,\t
heta\in\mathbb{R}.$$
Since $\{e^{in\theta}\}$ is an orthonormal system on $[0,2\pi]$ (with normalizat
ion $2\pi$), Fourier extraction gives the integral representation
$$J_n(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} e^{iz\sin\theta}\,e^{-in\theta}\,d\theta
= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} e^{i(z\sin\theta - n\theta)}\,d\theta. \tag{1}$$

### Step 2. Match the integrand

Rewrite the exponent in $I_k$:
$$ik(x-\sin x) = i(kx - k\sin x) = -i(k\sin x - kx).$$
So
$$I_k = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} e^{-ik\sin x}\,e^{ikx}\,dx.$$

Comparing with (1), $e^{iz\sin\theta}e^{-in\theta}$, we match
$$z = -k, \qquad -n = k \;\Longrightarrow\; n = -k.$$
Hence
$$I_k = J_{-k}(-k). \tag{2}$$

### Step 3. Use the reflection symmetries of $J_n$

For integer order, the Bessel functions satisfy
$$J_{-n}(z) = (-1)^n J_n(z), \qquad J_n(-z) = (-1)^n J_n(z).$$
Applying both to (2):
$$I_k = J_{-k}(-k) = (-1)^k J_k(-k) = (-1)^k\cdot(-1)^k J_k(k) = J_k(k).$$

(Equivalently, one may observe directly that $J_k(k)$ is real for real $k$, so t
he complex conjugate of the integral equals itself, and the sign convention drop
s out.)

### Step 4. Closed form

$$\boxed{\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} e^{ik(x-\sin x)}\,dx \;=\; J_k(k)\;}$$

where $J_k$ is the **Bessel function of the first kind** of order $k$.

### Remark on the "modified Bessel" hint

The modified Bessel function of the first kind $I_n$ is related to $J_n$ by $I_n
(z) = i^{-n}J_n(iz)$ (equivalently $J_n(z) = i^n I_n(-iz)$). The integral here,
with the oscillatory factor $e^{-ik\sin x}$, produces the *ordinary* Bessel func
tion $J_k(k)$, not $I_k$. The closed form in terms of the modified function woul
d be $I_k(k)\,i^{k}\cdot i^{-k}$ only under an analytic-continuation substitutio
n that does not match the real, oscillatory integral as stated; the genuine clos
ed form is $J_k(k)$.
