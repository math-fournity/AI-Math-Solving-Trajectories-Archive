## Solution

**Setup.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category and let $I = \varnothing$ be the empt
y category (no objects, no morphisms). There is a unique functor $X: \varnothing
 \to \mathcal{C}$.

For any object $A \in \mathcal{C}$, denote by $\Delta A: \varnothing \to \mathca
l{C}$ the constant functor at $A$ (which is also the unique functor $\varnothing
 \to \mathcal{C}$).

---

### a) The slice category $\mathcal{C}_{/X}$

Recall that for a functor $X: I \to \mathcal{C}$, the slice category $\mathcal{C
}_{/X}$ is defined as follows:

- **Objects** are pairs $(A, \alpha)$ where $A \in \operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal{C}
)$ and $\alpha: \Delta A \to X$ is a natural transformation.
- **Morphisms** $f: (A, \alpha) \to (B, \beta)$ are morphisms $f: A \to B$ in $\
mathcal{C}$ such that for every $i \in \operatorname{Ob}(I)$, the naturality squ
are commutes: $\beta_i \circ \Delta A(\operatorname{id}_i) = X(\operatorname{id}
_i) \circ \alpha_i$.

Now specialize to $I = \varnothing$:

**Objects.** A natural transformation $\alpha: \Delta A \to X$ consists of, for
each object $i \in \varnothing$, a morphism $\alpha_i: \Delta A(i) \to X(i)$ in
$\mathcal{C}$, subject to naturality. Since $\varnothing$ has **no objects**, th
ere are no components to specify and no naturality conditions to check. Therefor
e, for every $A \in \operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal{C})$, there is **exactly one** na
tural transformation $\Delta A \to X$ (the empty natural transformation). Hence
the objects of $\mathcal{C}_{/X}$ are in bijection with $\operatorname{Ob}(\math
cal{C})$.

**Morphisms.** A morphism $f: (A, \alpha) \to (B, \beta)$ in $\mathcal{C}_{/X}$
is a morphism $f: A \to B$ in $\mathcal{C}$ satisfying the naturality condition
for every $i \in \operatorname{Ob}(\varnothing)$. Since there are no objects in
$\varnothing$, this condition is **vacuously satisfied** for every morphism $f:
A \to B$.

Therefore the forgetful functor $\mathcal{C}_{/X} \to \mathcal{C}$, sending $(A,
 \alpha) \mapsto A$, is:

- **Bijective on objects** (each $A$ pairs with the unique empty natural transfo
rmation), and
- **Bijective on morphisms** (every $\mathcal{C}$-morphism lifts, and the lift i
s unique since the condition is vacuous).

This functor is fully faithful and essentially surjective, hence an equivalence
of categories. In fact it is an **isomorphism of categories**:

$$\boxed{\mathcal{C}_{/X} \cong \mathcal{C}.}$$

---

### b) The limit of $X$

The limit of $X: I \to \mathcal{C}$ is a **terminal object** in the category of
cones over $X$, which is precisely the slice category $\mathcal{C}_{/X}$. Concre
tely, a limit is an object $(L, \lambda) \in \mathcal{C}_{/X}$ such that for eve
ry $(A, \alpha) \in \mathcal{C}_{/X}$, there exists a unique morphism $f: (A, \a
lpha) \to (L, \lambda)$ in $\mathcal{C}_{/X}$.

By part (a), $\mathcal{C}_{/X} \cong \mathcal{C}$, and under this isomorphism:

- The object $(A, \alpha)$ corresponds to $A \in \mathcal{C}$,
- A morphism $f: (A, \alpha) \to (L, \lambda)$ corresponds to a morphism $f: A \
to L$ in $\mathcal{C}$ (with no extra condition, since the naturality requiremen
t is vacuous).

Thus, $(L, \lambda)$ is terminal in $\mathcal{C}_{/X}$ if and only if for every
$A \in \mathcal{C}$, there is a **unique** morphism $A \to L$ in $\mathcal{C}$.
This is exactly the definition of $L$ being a **terminal object** of $\mathcal{C
}$.

Therefore:

$$\boxed{\lim X \;\cong\; \text{terminal object of } \mathcal{C},}$$

provided $\mathcal{C}$ possesses a terminal object. If $\mathcal{C}$ has no term
inal object, then $X$ has no limit.

**Remark.** This recovers the standard fact that the limit of the empty diagram
is a terminal object: a cone over the empty diagram places no constraints (no le
gs to fill), so every object supports a unique cone, and the universal property
reduces to the terminal-object condition.
