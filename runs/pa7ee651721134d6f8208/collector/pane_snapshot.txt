Answer

Yes. Every bounded operator on a Hilbert space is weakly compact. The reason is
that every Hilbert space is reflexive, and on a reflexive Banach space every
bounded operator is automatically weakly compact.

Below is the complete proof.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Preliminaries

Let $X, Y$ be Banach spaces. A bounded linear operator $T: X \to Y$ is weakly
compact if $T(B_X)$ is relatively weakly compact in $Y$, where $B_X = {x \in X :
|x| \le 1}$ is the closed unit ball. Equivalently (since the weak closure of
$T(B_X)$ is weakly compact iff $T(B_X)$ is relatively weakly compact), $T$ is
weakly compact iff $\overline{T(B_X)}^{,w}$ is weakly compact.

A Banach space $X$ is reflexive if the canonical embedding $J: X \to X^{**}$,
defined by $J(x)(f) = f(x)$, is surjective (hence an isometric isomorphism).

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 1. Every Hilbert space is reflexive

Let $H$ be a Hilbert space over $\mathbb{K}$ ($\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$). By
the Riesz representation theorem, the map $$R: H \to H^*, \qquad R(y)(x) =
\langle x, y\rangle,$$ is an isometric (conjugate-linear if $\mathbb{K}
=\mathbb{C}$, linear if $\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}$) bijection.

We show the canonical embedding $J: H \to H^{}$ is surjective. Let $\Phi \in
H^{}$, i.e. $\Phi: H^* \to \mathbb{K}$ is a bounded linear functional. Define $
\varphi: H \to \mathbb{K}$ by $$\varphi(y) = \overline{\Phi(R(y))} \quad
(\text{for } \mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}), \qquad \varphi(y) = \Phi(R(y)) \quad
(\text{for } \mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}).$$ One checks $\varphi$ is a bounded linear
functional on $H$ (the conjugation in the complex case compensates for the
conjugate-linearity of $R$, making $\varphi$ linear). By Riesz again, there
exists $z \in H$ with $\varphi(y) = \langle y, z\rangle$ for all $y \in H$.

Now for any $f \in H^*$, write $f = R(y)$ for a unique $y \in H$. Then $$J(z)(f)
= f(z) = R(y)(z) = \langle z, y\rangle = \overline{\langle y, z\rangle} =
\overline{\varphi(y)} = \Phi(R(y)) = \Phi(f).$$ (For $\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}$ the
conjugations are trivial.) Hence $J(z) = \Phi$, proving surjectivity. Therefore
$H$ is reflexive.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 2. Reflexivity implies weak compactness of the closed unit ball

Theorem (Kakutani). A Banach space $X$ is reflexive iff $B_X$ is weakly compact.

Proof of ($\Rightarrow$). Assume $X$ is reflexive, so $J: X \to X^{}$ is an
isometric isomorphism with $J(B_X) = B_{X^{}}$. By the Banach–Alaoglu theorem,
$B_{X^{}}$ is compact in the weak$^*$ topology $\sigma(X^{}, X^)$. The weak
topology $\sigma(X, X^)$ on $X$ is, via $J$, exactly the restriction of $
\sigma(X^{}, X^*)$ to $J(X) = X^{}$. Since $J$ is a homeomorphism between $(X,
\sigma(X,X^))$ and $(X^{}, \sigma(X^{},X^))$, and $B_{X^{}}$ is $
\sigma(X^{},X^)$-compact, $B_X$ is $\sigma(X,X^)$-compact, i.e. weakly compact.
$\square$

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 3. A bounded linear operator is weak-to-weak continuous

Let $T: X \to Y$ be bounded and linear. Suppose $(x_\alpha)$ is a net with
$x_\alpha \to x$ weakly in $X$, i.e. $f(x_\alpha) \to f(x)$ for every $f \in
X^$. For any $g \in Y^$, the adjoint satisfies $T^g \in X^$ and $$g(Tx_\alpha) =
(T^*g)(x_\alpha) \to (T^*g)(x) = g(Tx).$$ Hence $Tx_\alpha \to Tx$ weakly in
$Y$. So $T: (X, \text{weak}) \to (Y, \text{weak})$ is continuous.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 4. On a reflexive domain, every bounded operator is weakly compact

Let $X$ be reflexive and $T: X \to Y$ bounded. By Step 2, $B_X$ is weakly
compact. By Step 3, $T$ is continuous from $(X,\text{weak})$ to $(Y,
\text{weak})$. The continuous image of a compact set is compact, so $T(B_X)$ is
weakly compact in $Y$. In particular $T(B_X)$ is relatively weakly compact, so
$T$ is weakly compact.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 5. Conclusion

Let $H$ be a Hilbert space and $T: H \to H$ any bounded operator. By Step 1, $H$
is reflexive. By Step 4 (with $X = Y = H$), $T$ is weakly compact.

Therefore every bounded operator on a Hilbert space is weakly compact. $
\blacksquare$
