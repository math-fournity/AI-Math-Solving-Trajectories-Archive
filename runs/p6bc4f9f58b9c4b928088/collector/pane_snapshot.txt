## Solution

Let $a = \sqrt{e-1}$. We evaluate

$$I = \int_0^\infty \frac{1 - \cos(ax)}{x\, e^x}\, dx.$$

**Step 1: Rewrite the numerator as an integral.**

For $x > 0$,

$$\int_0^a \sin(tx)\, dt = \left[-\frac{\cos(tx)}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=a} = \frac{
1 - \cos(ax)}{x}.$$

Hence

$$I = \int_0^\infty e^{-x} \int_0^a \sin(tx)\, dt\, dx.$$

**Step 2: Justify interchanging the order of integration.**

We check absolute integrability:

$$\int_0^a \int_0^\infty \left|e^{-x}\sin(tx)\right| dx\, dt \le \int_0^a \int_0
^\infty e^{-x}\, dx\, dt = \int_0^a 1\, dt = a < \infty.$$

Since $a = \sqrt{e-1}$ is finite, Fubini's theorem applies, giving

$$I = \int_0^a \left(\int_0^\infty e^{-x}\sin(tx)\, dx\right) dt.$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the inner integral (Laplace transform of $\sin$).**

This is the standard integral

$$\int_0^\infty e^{-x}\sin(tx)\, dx = \frac{t}{1 + t^2},$$

obtained by integrating $e^{-x}e^{itx}$ and taking the imaginary part, or by two
 integrations by parts.

**Step 4: Evaluate the outer integral.**

$$I = \int_0^a \frac{t}{1+t^2}\, dt = \frac{1}{2}\ln(1+t^2)\Big|_0^a = \frac{1}{
2}\ln(1+a^2).$$

**Step 5: Substitute $a = \sqrt{e-1}$.**

Since $a^2 = e - 1$, we have $1 + a^2 = e$, so

$$I = \frac{1}{2}\ln(e) = \frac{1}{2}.$$

$$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}}$$
