**Problem.** Let $A$ be the set of nonnegative sequences $(a_n)$ with $\sum_{n=1

## Step 1: The product is well-defined

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \log(1+a_n) \leq \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n = 1 < \infty,$$

_n)\bigr)$ is a well-defined finite positive real number.

$$\log P = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \log(1+a_n) \leq \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n = 1,$$

 1$. Since at least one $a_n > 0$, the inequality is strict:
$$P < e.$$

## Step 3: Lower bound — $P \geq 2$, with equality

$$\prod_{n=1}^\infty (1+a_n) = 1 + \sum_n a_n + \sum_{i<j} a_i a_j + \cdots \geq

Equality holds iff all cross-terms vanish, i.e., at most one $a_n$ is positive.
With $\sum a_n = 1$, this means $a_k = 1$ for some $k$ and $a_n = 0$ otherwise,
giving $P = 2$. So $2$ is achieved.
