## Proof

Let $X_1, X_2, \dots$ be a sequence of i.i.d. standard normal random variables $
X_i \sim N(0,1)$ on a common probability space. For each $n$, let $X^{(n)} = (X_
1, \dots, X_n) \in \mathbb{R}^n$. Then $X^{(n)}$ has density

$$
f_n(x) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}} e^{-\|x\|^2/2},
$$

which is exactly the Gaussian density appearing in the integrand. Therefore the
integral in question is the probability

$$
\int_{\|x\|^2 \le n} \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}} e^{-\|x\|^2/2}\,dx \;=\; P\!\left(\|
X^{(n)}\|^2 \le n\right) \;=\; P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i^2 \le n\right).
$$

**Step 1: Reduce to a sum of i.i.d. random variables.**

Set $Y_i = X_i^2$. Each $Y_i$ is a $\chi^2_1$ random variable, so

$$
\mathbb{E}[Y_i] = 1, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(Y_i) = 2, \qquad \mathbb{E}[Y_i^2
] = 3.
$$

The $Y_i$ are i.i.d. with finite mean $\mu = 1$ and finite, nonzero variance $\s
igma^2 = 2$. The quantity of interest is

$$
P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} Y_i \le n\right) = P\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} Y_
i \le 1\right) = P\!\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} Y_i - n}{\sqrt{2n}} \le 0\right).
$$

**Step 2: Apply the Central Limit Theorem.**

Since $Y_1, Y_2, \dots$ are i.i.d. with mean $\mu = 1$ and variance $\sigma^2 =
2 > 0$, the classical Central Limit Theorem (Lindeberg–Lévy) gives

$$
Z_n := \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} Y_i - n\mu}{\sigma\sqrt{n}} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} X
_i^2 - n}{\sqrt{2n}} \;\xrightarrow{d}\; Z \sim N(0,1).
$$

**Step 3: Convert convergence in distribution to convergence of the cumulative d
istribution at a continuity point.**

Convergence in distribution $Z_n \xrightarrow{d} Z$ is equivalent to pointwise c
onvergence of the cumulative distribution functions at every continuity point of
 $F_Z$:

$$
F_{Z_n}(t) = P(Z_n \le t) \;\longrightarrow\; F_Z(t) = \Phi(t) \quad \text{for e
very } t \text{ at which } \Phi \text{ is continuous}.
$$

The standard normal CDF $\Phi(t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{t} e^{-u
^2/2}\,du$ is continuous everywhere. In particular, at $t = 0$,

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty} P(Z_n \le 0) = \Phi(0) = \frac{1}{2}.
$$

**Step 4: Conclude.**

Observing that $P(Z_n \le 0)$ is exactly the original integral:

$$
P(Z_n \le 0) = P\!\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i^2 - n}{\sqrt{2n}} \le 0\right)
= P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i^2 \le n\right) = \int_{\|x\|^2 \le n} \frac{1}{(2\
pi)^{n/2}} e^{-\|x\|^2/2}\,dx.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{\lim_{n \to \infty} \int_{\|x\|^2 \le n} \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}} e^{-\|x\|
^2/2}\,dx = \frac{1}{2}.}
$$
