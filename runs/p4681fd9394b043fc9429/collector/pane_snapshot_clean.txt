## Solution

**Setup.** Let the original disk have radius $R$. Two perpendicular folds throug
h the center divide it into four quadrants. Three quadrants are overlapped to fo
rm the lateral surface of a right circular cone; the fourth remains as the openi
ng.

**Slant height.** The apex of the cone is the original center of the disk, and e
very point on the boundary of the disk is at distance $R$ from the center. Hence
 the slant height of the cone is

$$\ell = R.$$

**Base radius.** The boundary of the three-quadrant section is $\tfrac{3}{4}$ of
 the full circumference of the disk:

$$\text{arc length} = \tfrac{3}{4}\cdot 2\pi R = \tfrac{3\pi R}{2}.$$

This arc becomes the circular base of the cone, so $2\pi r = \tfrac{3\pi R}{2}$,
 giving

$$r = \tfrac{3}{4}R.$$

(Equivalently, matching lateral areas: $\pi r\ell = \tfrac{3}{4}\pi R^2 \Rightar
row \pi r R = \tfrac{3}{4}\pi R^2 \Rightarrow r = \tfrac{3}{4}R$.)

**Half-angle at the apex.** Let $\alpha = \theta/2$ be the half-angle between th
e cone's axis and a generator. In the right triangle formed by the axis, the bas
e radius, and a generator,

$$\sin\alpha = \frac{r}{\ell} = \frac{3R/4}{R} = \frac{3}{4}.$$

**Vertex angle.** Using the double-angle identity,

$$\cos\theta = \cos(2\alpha) = 1 - 2\sin^2\alpha = 1 - 2\left(\frac{9}{16}\right
) = 1 - \frac{9}{8} = -\frac{1}{8}.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{\theta = \arccos\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{8}\right) \approx 97.18^\circ.}$$
