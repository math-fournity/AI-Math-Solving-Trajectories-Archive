## Proof

We consider all combinations of 6 distinct numbers chosen from {1, 2, ..., 49},
sorted in ascending order. Each combination is categorized by the value in the 5
th position (the second largest number). We seek the value $k$ that maximizes th
e number of combinations having $k$ in position 5.

### Counting combinations with $k$ in position 5

For a fixed value $k$ to occupy position 5 (second largest):
- Positions 1–4 must be filled with 4 distinct numbers chosen from $\{1, \dots,
k-1\}$, in ascending order. The number of ways is $\binom{k-1}{4}$.
- Position 6 (the largest) must be a single number chosen from $\{k+1, \dots, 49
\}$. The number of ways is $49 - k$.

Thus the count is:
$$f(k) = \binom{k-1}{4}\,(49 - k), \qquad 5 \le k \le 48.$$

### Finding the maximum

We examine the ratio of consecutive values:
$$\frac{f(k+1)}{f(k)} = \frac{\binom{k}{4}}{\binom{k-1}{4}} \cdot \frac{48-k}{49
-k} = \frac{k}{k-4}\cdot\frac{48-k}{49-k}.$$

The function $f$ is increasing when this ratio exceeds 1 and decreasing when it
is below 1. Setting the ratio equal to 1:

$$\frac{k(48-k)}{(k-4)(49-k)} = 1.$$

Expanding both sides:
$$48k - k^2 = (k-4)(49-k) = 53k - k^2 - 196,$$
$$48k = 53k - 196,$$
$$5k = 196 \implies k = 39.2.$$

So $f$ is increasing for $k \le 39$ and decreasing for $k \ge 40$. The maximum i
s at $k = 39$ or $k = 40$. We compare:

$$\frac{f(40)}{f(39)} = \frac{39}{35}\cdot\frac{9}{10} = \frac{351}{350} > 1,$$

so $f(40) > f(39)$. Therefore the maximum is at $k = 40$.

### Verification by direct computation

$$f(39) = \binom{38}{4}\cdot 10 = 73815 \cdot 10 = 738{,}150,$$
$$f(40) = \binom{39}{4}\cdot 9 = 82251 \cdot 9 = 740{,}259,$$
$$f(41) = \binom{40}{4}\cdot 8 = 91390 \cdot 8 = 731{,}120.$$

Indeed $f(40) = 740{,}259$ exceeds both $f(39)$ and $f(41)$, confirming the maxi
mum.

### Conclusion

The number that appears most frequently as the second largest (5th position) val
ue across all $\binom{49}{6}$ combinations is $\boxed{40}$.
