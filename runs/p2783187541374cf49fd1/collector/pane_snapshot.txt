We compute the total outward flux of

$$\vec{v}(x,y,z) = (y^2+z^2)\,\vec{i} + (xy+y^2)\,\vec{j} + (xz+z)\,\vec{k}$$

through the closed surface $\Sigma$ of the cylinder $x^2+y^2\le 1$, $0\le z\le 1
$.

### Step 1 — Divergence Theorem

Since $\Sigma$ is a closed surface bounding the solid cylinder $V$, the Divergen
ce Theorem gives

$$\iint_\Sigma \vec{v}\cdot d\vec{S} = \iiint_V (\nabla\cdot\vec{v})\,dV.$$

Compute the divergence:

$$\nabla\cdot\vec{v} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(y^2+z^2) + \frac{\partial}{\p
artial y}(xy+y^2) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(xz+z) = 0 + (x+2y) + (x+1) = 2x+
2y+1.$$

### Step 2 — Integrate over the cylinder

$$\iiint_V (2x+2y+1)\,dV = \iiint_V 2x\,dV + \iiint_V 2y\,dV + \iiint_V 1\,dV.$$

The region $V$ is symmetric under $x\mapsto -x$ and $y\mapsto -y$, while $2x$ an
d $2y$ are odd functions, so the first two integrals vanish:

$$\iiint_V 2x\,dV = 0, \qquad \iiint_V 2y\,dV = 0.$$

The remaining integral is the volume of the cylinder:

$$\iiint_V 1\,dV = \pi r^2 h = \pi(1)^2(1) = \pi.$$

### Step 3 — Direct surface-integral verification

**Bottom ($z=0$), outward normal $-\vec{k}$:**
$\vec{v}\cdot(-\vec{k}) = -(xz+z) = -(x\cdot 0 + 0) = 0$, so the flux is $0$.

**Top ($z=1$), outward normal $\vec{k}$:**
$\vec{v}\cdot\vec{k} = xz+z = x+1$. Thus
$$\iint_{\text{top}} (x+1)\,dA = \iint x\,dA + \iint 1\,dA = 0 + \pi = \pi,$$
using symmetry for the first term and the unit-disk area for the second.

**Side ($x^2+y^2=1$), outward normal $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta,0)$:** with $x=\cos
\theta$, $y=\sin\theta$,
$$\vec{v}\cdot\vec{n} = (y^2+z^2)\cos\theta + (xy+y^2)\sin\theta = 2\sin^2\theta
\cos\theta + z^2\cos\theta + \sin^3\theta.$$
Each term integrates to $0$ over $\theta\in[0,2\pi]$ (odd powers of $\sin$ or $\
cos$ over a full period), so the side flux is $0$.

Total surface flux $= 0 + \pi + 0 = \pi$, confirming the Divergence Theorem resu
lt.

### Conclusion

The total outward flux of $\vec{v}$ through $\Sigma$ is

$$\boxed{\pi}.$$
