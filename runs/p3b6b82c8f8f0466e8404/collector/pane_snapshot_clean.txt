Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the total variation distanc
e between the law of the tail process $(X_n, X_{n+1}, \ldots)$ under $P_x$ and t
he stationary process under $P_\pi$ converges to 0, for every $x \in S$.

**The statement is TRUE.**

**Proof.**

Let $\mu_n = \delta_x P^n$ denote the distribution of $X_n$ under $P_x$.

**Step 1: Marginal convergence.** The chain is irreducible ($\psi$-irreducible),
 positive recurrent, and aperiodic. For a $\psi$-irreducible chain, positive rec
urrence is equivalent to positive Harris recurrence (Meyn–Tweedie, Theorem 10.4.
4). The convergence theorem for general state space Markov chains then gives:

$$\|\delta_x P^n - \pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}} \to 0 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty, \tex
t{ for every } x \in S.$$

**Step 2: From marginal convergence to path-level convergence.** By the Markov p
roperty, the law of $(X_n, X_{n+1}, \ldots)$ under $P_x$ equals the law of $(X_0
, X_1, \ldots)$ under $P_{\mu_n}$ (the chain started with initial distribution $
\mu_n$). Explicitly, for any $A \in \mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{Z}_+}$:

$$P_x\big((X_n, X_{n+1}, \ldots) \in A\big) = \int_S P_y(X \in A)\, \mu_n(dy),$$

$$P_\pi(X \in A) = \int_S P_y(X \in A)\, \pi(dy),$$

where $X = (X_0, X_1, \ldots)$ and $P_y(X \in A)$ is the probability that the ch
ain started at $y$ yields a path in $A$.

The map $f_A(y) := P_y(X \in A)$ is measurable and takes values in $[0,1]$, so $
|f_A| \leq 1$. Therefore:

$$\left|P_x\big((X_n, X_{n+1}, \ldots) \in A\big) - P_\pi(X \in A)\right| = \lef
t|\int f_A\, d\mu_n - \int f_A\, d\pi\right| \leq \|\mu_n - \pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
$$

This bound holds for every $A \in \mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{Z}_+}$, so taking the sup
remum:

$$\sup_{A \in \mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{Z}_+}} \left|P_x\big((X_n, X_{n+1}, \ldots) \
in A\big) - P_\pi(X \in A)\right| \leq \|\mu_n - \pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.$$

**Step 3: Conclusion.** By Step 1, $\|\mu_n - \pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}} = \|\delta_x P
^n - \pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}} \to 0$. Hence:

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \sup_{A \in \mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{Z}_+}} \left|P_x\big((X_n
, X_{n+1}, \ldots) \in A\big) - P_\pi(X \in A)\right| = 0$$

for every $x \in S$. $\blacksquare$

**Key ideas:**
- The sup over all product-measurable sets $A$ is the total variation distance b
etween the path measures. The Markov property expresses the tail-path law as a m
ixture $\int P_y(\cdot)\,\mu_n(dy)$, so the path-level TV distance is bounded by
 the TV distance of the mixing distributions $\mu_n$ vs. $\pi$.
- For $\psi$-irreducible chains, positive recurrence implies positive Harris rec
urrence, so the convergence theorem yields $\|\delta_x P^n - \pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \to 0$ for **every** $x$ (not just $\pi$-a.e. $x$).
