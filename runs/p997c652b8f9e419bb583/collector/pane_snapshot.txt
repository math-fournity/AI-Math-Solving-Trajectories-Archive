## Proof

**Claim.** The profinite completion of $S^1$ is the trivial group $\{e\}$.

Recall that for a (topological) group $G$, its profinite completion is
$$\widehat{G} \;=\; \varprojlim_{N \trianglelefteq G,\; [G:N]<\infty} G/N,$$
the inverse limit ranging over all normal subgroups of finite index. Since $S^1$
 is abelian, every subgroup is normal, so we range over all finite-index subgrou
ps.

**Step 1. $S^1$ is divisible.**

A group $G$ (written multiplicatively) is *divisible* if for every $g\in G$ and
every integer $n\geq 1$ there exists $h\in G$ with $h^{n}=g$. For $S^1=\{z\in\ma
thbb{C}:|z|=1\}$, fix $z\in S^1$ and $n\geq 1$. Write $z=e^{i\theta}$. Then $h=e
^{i\theta/n}\in S^1$ satisfies $h^{n}=e^{i\theta}=z$. Hence $S^1$ is divisible.

**Step 2. Divisibility passes to quotients.**

Let $\pi:G\to G/N$ be the quotient map with $G$ divisible. Given $\bar g\in G/N$
 and $n\geq 1$, pick $g\in G$ with $\pi(g)=\bar g$. By divisibility choose $h\in
 G$ with $h^{n}=g$. Then $\pi(h)^{n}=\pi(h^{n})=\pi(g)=\bar g$. So $G/N$ is divi
sible.

**Step 3. A finite divisible group is trivial.**

Let $H$ be a finite group of order $m$ that is divisible. Divisibility for $n=m$
 says the power map $\varphi:H\to H,\ h\mapsto h^{m}$ is surjective; being a sel
f-map of a finite set, it is bijective. On the other hand, by Lagrange every $h\
in H$ has order dividing $m$, so $h^{m}=e$ for all $h$; i.e. $\varphi$ is the co
nstant map to $e$. A bijective constant map forces $H=\{e\}$.

**Step 4. $S^1$ has no proper finite-index subgroups.**

Let $N\leq S^1$ with $[S^1:N]<\infty$. Then $S^1/N$ is a finite divisible group
(Steps 1 and 2), hence trivial by Step 3. Therefore $N=S^1$.

**Step 5. The profinite completion is trivial.**

The inverse system defining $\widehat{S^{1}}$ ranges over finite-index normal su
bgroups, but the only such subgroup is $S^1$ itself. Thus the inverse system con
sists of the single term $S^1/S^1\cong\{e\}$, and
$$\widehat{S^{1}} \;=\; \varprojlim \{e\} \;=\; \{e\}.$$

**Remark (consistency with Pontryagin duality).** The Pontryagin dual of $S^1$ i
s $\mathbb{Z}$, and the profinite completion of $S^1$ should be dual to the tors
ion subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}$'s dual-theoretic counterpart. Concretely, $\widehat
{S^{1}}\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})_{\text{profinit
e}}$-dual; since $\mathbb{Z}$ is torsion-free, its profinite completion $\wideha
t{\mathbb{Z}}$ pairs with $S^1$'s torsion $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$, while the *pr
ofinite completion of $S^1$ itself* captures only the finite quotients of $S^1$,
 which are all trivial by divisibility. This agrees with the general fact that a
 compact connected divisible abelian group has trivial profinite completion.
