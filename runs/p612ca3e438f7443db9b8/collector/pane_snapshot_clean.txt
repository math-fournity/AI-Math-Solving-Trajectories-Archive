## Proof

Let $R_n = \mathbb{Z}[x,y]/(x^2 - y^n)$. We claim $R_n \cong R_m$ if and only if
 $n = m$.

The "if" direction is trivial. We prove the "only if" direction.

### Step 1. Reduction to $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$

Any ring isomorphism $R_n \xrightarrow{\sim} R_m$ maps $1\mapsto 1$, hence fixes
 the prime subring $\mathbb{Z}$, so it is a $\mathbb{Z}$-algebra isomorphism. Ba
se-changing along $\mathbb{Z}\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ yields a $\overline{\math
bb{Q}}$-algebra isomorphism
$$\overline{\mathbb{Q}}[x,y]/(x^2-y^n)\;\cong\;\overline{\mathbb{Q}}[x,y]/(x^2-y
^m).$$
It suffices to show this forces $n=m$.

### Step 2. The singular locus

Over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ (characteristic $0$), the Jacobian ideal of $f=x^2-
y^n$ is $(2x,\; ny^{n-1})$, which vanishes only at $(x,y)=(0,0)$. Thus each ring
 $A_n:=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}[x,y]/(x^2-y^n)$ has a **unique** singular point, na
mely the origin $\mathfrak{m}=(x,y)$. An isomorphism $A_n\cong A_m$ must carry t
he unique singular maximal ideal to the unique singular maximal ideal, inducing
an isomorphism of the local rings $(A_n)_{\mathfrak m}\cong (A_m)_{\mathfrak m}$
.

### Step 3. Two intrinsic invariants of the local ring

For the local ring of a curve singularity, the following are intrinsic (preserve
d by local ring isomorphism):

- **$r$** = number of branches = number of minimal primes of the local ring.
- **$\delta$** = the $\delta$-invariant $= \dim_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\bigl(\wi
detilde{R}/R\bigr)$, where $\widetilde{R}$ is the normalization.

We compute these for $A_n$ at the origin.

**Number of branches.** Over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, $x^2-y^n$ factors as:
- $n$ **odd**: $y^n$ is not a square, so $x^2-y^n$ is irreducible. Hence $r=1$.
- $n$ **even**, $n=2a$: $x^2-y^{2a}=(x-y^a)(x+y^a)$, two distinct irreducible fa
ctors. Hence $r=2$.

**$\delta$-invariant.** We use the formula $\delta = \sum_i \delta_i + \sum_{i<j
} I(C_i,C_j)$, where $\delta_i$ is the $\delta$-invariant of branch $i$ and $I(C
_i,C_j)$ is the intersection multiplicity.

- **$n$ odd**: a single branch $x^2=y^n$, parametrized by $x=t^n,\; y=t^2$ (sinc
e $t^{2n}=t^{2n}$). The branch is the image of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}[[t]]\to\ov
erline{\mathbb{Q}}[[x,y]]$, $t\mapsto$ with $x=t^n,y=t^2$; the normalization is
$\overline{\mathbb{Q}}[[t]]$ and
$$\delta = \dim_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}[[t]]\,/\,\overline{
\mathbb{Q}}[[t^2,t^n]] = \#\{\text{gaps of }\langle 2,n\rangle\} = \frac{n-1}{2}
,$$
since the numerical semigroup $\langle 2,n\rangle$ (with $n$ odd) has gaps $\{1,
3,5,\dots,n-2\}$, totaling $(n-1)/2$.

- **$n$ even**, $n=2a$: two smooth branches $C_1: x=y^a$ and $C_2: x=-y^a$. Each
 is smooth, so $\delta_1=\delta_2=0$. Their intersection multiplicity is
$$I(C_1,C_2)=\dim_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}[[x,y]]/(x-y^a,\;x
+y^a)=\dim_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}[[y]]/(2y^a)=a=\frac{n}{2
}$$
($2$ is a unit in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$). Hence $\delta = 0+0+a = \dfrac{n}{2}
$.

### Step 4. The invariants determine $n$

From the two cases:
$$n = \begin{cases} 2\delta + 1 & \text{if } r=1\;(n\text{ odd}),\\ 2\delta & \t
ext{if } r=2\;(n\text{ even}).\end{cases}$$
Equivalently $n = 2\delta + (2-r)$.

Since $r$ and $\delta$ are invariants of the local ring at the unique singular p
oint, they are preserved by the isomorphism $A_n\cong A_m$. Therefore
$$2\delta_n + (2-r_n) = n = m = 2\delta_m + (2-r_m),$$
which forces $n=m$.

### Conclusion

We have shown $R_n\cong R_m \Rightarrow n=m$, and the converse is immediate. The
refore

$$\boxed{\mathbb{Z}[x,y]/(x^2-y^n)\;\cong\;\mathbb{Z}[x,y]/(x^2-y^m)\quad\Longle
ftrightarrow\quad n=m.}$$
