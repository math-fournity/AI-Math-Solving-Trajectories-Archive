**Problem.** Are fields that are finitely generated as $\mathbb{Z}$-algebras nec
essarily finite?

**Answer: Yes.** Every field that is finitely generated as a $\mathbb{Z}$-algebr
a is finite.

---

**Proof.**

Let $K$ be a field that is finitely generated as a $\mathbb{Z}$-algebra, so $K \
cong \mathbb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_n]/\mathfrak{m}$ for a maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$.
 Consider the canonical map $\varphi:\mathbb{Z}\to K$. Its kernel is a prime ide
al of $\mathbb{Z}$, hence either $(0)$ or $(p)$ for a prime $p$.

**Case 1: $\ker\varphi=(p)$.** Then $K$ is a field of characteristic $p$, and th
e map $\mathbb{F}_p=\mathbb{Z}/(p)\hookrightarrow K$ makes $K$ a finitely genera
ted $\mathbb{F}_p$-algebra (generated by the images of $x_1,\dots,x_n$). By **Za
riski's lemma**, a field that is finitely generated as an algebra over a field i
s a finite algebraic extension of that field. Thus $K$ is a finite extension of
$\mathbb{F}_p$, hence $K$ is a finite field.

**Case 2: $\ker\varphi=(0)$.** Then $\mathbb{Z}\hookrightarrow K$, so $K$ has ch
aracteristic $0$ and contains $\mathbb{Q}$ (the field of fractions of $\mathbb{Z
}$). Since $K=\mathbb{Z}[a_1,\dots,a_n]$ for some $a_i\in K$ and $\mathbb{Z}\sub
seteq\mathbb{Q}\subseteq K$, the same elements $a_1,\dots,a_n$ generate $K$ as a
 $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra. By Zariski's lemma, $K$ is a finite algebraic extension o
f $\mathbb{Q}$, i.e. $K$ is a **number field**.

We now show that no number field can be finitely generated as a $\mathbb{Z}$-alg
ebra, yielding a contradiction.

Suppose, for contradiction, that the number field $K$ is generated as a $\mathbb
{Z}$-algebra by $a_1,\dots,a_n\in K$. Let $\mathcal{O}_K$ be the ring of integer
s of $K$. Each $a_i$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$, so by clearing denominators
 there exists an integer $D\ge 1$ with $a_i\in\tfrac{1}{D}\mathcal{O}_K$ for all
 $i$. Consequently every polynomial expression in the $a_i$ with $\mathbb{Z}$-co
efficients lies in the localization $\mathcal{O}_K[1/D]$, so
$$K=\mathbb{Z}[a_1,\dots,a_n]\subseteq\mathcal{O}_K[1/D]\subseteq K.$$

But $\mathcal{O}_K[1/D]$ is a **proper subring** of $K$. Indeed, $\mathcal{O}_K[
1/D]$ is a Dedekind domain (a localization of the Dedekind domain $\mathcal{O}_K
$), and it is not a field: any nonzero prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{
O}_K$ with $\mathfrak{p}\nmid(D)$ survives in $\mathcal{O}_K[1/D]$ as a nonzero
prime ideal, whereas a field has no nonzero prime ideals. (Such a $\mathfrak{p}$
 exists because only finitely many primes divide $D$, while $\mathcal{O}_K$ has
infinitely many prime ideals — e.g. those lying above rational primes $q\nmid D$
.) Hence $\mathcal{O}_K[1/D]\subsetneq K$, contradicting $K\subseteq\mathcal{O}_
K[1/D]\subseteq K$.

Therefore Case 2 is impossible.

**Conclusion.** Only Case 1 can occur, and in that case $K$ is finite. Hence eve
ry field finitely generated as a $\mathbb{Z}$-algebra is finite. $\blacksquare$
