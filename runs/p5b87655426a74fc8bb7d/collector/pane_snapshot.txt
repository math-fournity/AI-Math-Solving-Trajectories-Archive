## Proof

The unitary irreducible representations of ${\rm SU}(2)$ are indexed by the spin
 $j \in \{0, \tfrac12, 1, \tfrac32, 2, \dots\}$, with representation $\rho_j$ of
 dimension $\dim \rho_j = 2j+1$. **Even-dimensional irreps correspond to half-in
teger $j$** ($j = \tfrac12, \tfrac32, \tfrac52, \dots$, giving dimensions $2, 4,
 6, \dots$). We show that **every** irrep $\rho_j$ has determinant $+1$, hence i
n particular the even-dimensional ones do.

**Step 1: Reduction to diagonal elements.** Every $g \in {\rm SU}(2)$ is conjuga
te to a diagonal matrix
$$
d(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix} e^{i\theta} & 0 \\ 0 & e^{-i\theta} \end{pmatrix}, \
qquad \theta \in [0, 2\pi),
$$
since ${\rm SU}(2)$ is compact and connected with maximal torus $T = \{d(\theta)
\}$. The determinant is a class function (invariant under conjugation: $\det(\rh
o_j(hgh^{-1})) = \det(\rho_j(g))$), so it suffices to compute $\det \rho_j(d(\th
eta))$.

**Step 2: Eigenvalues via weights.** The representation $\rho_j$ has weights $-j
, -j+1, \dots, j-1, j$ (the $2j+1$ values symmetric about $0$). On the torus ele
ment $d(\theta)$, the weight-$m$ eigenspace carries eigenvalue $e^{im\theta}$. H
ence the eigenvalues of $\rho_j(d(\theta))$ are
$$
e^{-ij\theta},\; e^{i(-j+1)\theta},\; \dots,\; e^{ij\theta}.
$$

**Step 3: Compute the determinant.**
$$
\det \rho_j(d(\theta)) = \prod_{m=-j}^{j} e^{im\theta} = \exp\!\Big(i\theta \sum
_{m=-j}^{j} m\Big).
$$
The weights are symmetric about $0$, so
$$
\sum_{m=-j}^{j} m = 0,
$$
whether $j$ is integer or half-integer (the summands pair as $m$ and $-m$, with
$m=0$ contributing $0$ when $j$ is integer). Therefore
$$
\det \rho_j(d(\theta)) = e^{0} = 1.
$$

**Step 4: Conclude.** Since $\det \rho_j = 1$ on every diagonal element and the
determinant is conjugation-invariant, $\det \rho_j(g) = 1$ for all $g \in {\rm S
U}(2)$. This holds for **all** $j$, and in particular for every half-integer $j$
, i.e. for every even-dimensional unitary irreducible representation.

**Remark (alternative viewpoint).** ${\rm SU}(2)$ is perfect: its Lie algebra $\
mathfrak{su}(2)$ is simple, so $[\mathfrak{su}(2),\mathfrak{su}(2)] = \mathfrak{
su}(2)$, and for a connected Lie group the commutator subgroup has Lie algebra $
[\mathfrak g, \mathfrak g]$; hence $[{\rm SU}(2), {\rm SU}(2)] = {\rm SU}(2)$. T
he map $\det \circ\, \rho_j : {\rm SU}(2) \to U(1)$ is a continuous homomorphism
 into an abelian group, so it factors through the abelianization, which is trivi
al. Thus $\det \rho_j \equiv 1$ for every representation—confirming the weight c
omputation above.

**Answer:** Yes. The even-dimensional unitary irreducible representations of ${\
rm SU}(2)$ all have determinant $+1$; in fact, *every* irreducible representatio
n of ${\rm SU}(2)$ has determinant $+1$.
