## Counterexample

Let $A = \mathbb{Z}$, $K = \mathbb{Q}$, $L = \mathbb{Q}(i)$, and $G = \operatorn
ame{Gal}(L/K) = \{1, \sigma\}$ where $\sigma(i) = -i$.

The integral closure of $\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ is $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ (the
ring of Gaussian integers).

Set $B = \mathbb{Z}[i]\!\left[\frac{1}{2+i}\right]$, i.e., the localization of $
\mathbb{Z}[i]$ at the multiplicative set $\{(2+i)^n : n \geq 0\}$.

### Verification of all hypotheses

**(1) $A$ is a Dedekind domain, $K$ its fraction field.** $\mathbb{Z}$ is a Dede
kind domain with fraction field $\mathbb{Q}$. ✓

**(2) $B$ is a Dedekind domain with $A \subset B$.** $B$ is a localization of th
e Dedekind domain $\mathbb{Z}[i]$, hence is Dedekind. Clearly $\mathbb{Z} \subse
t \mathbb{Z}[i] \subset B$. ✓

**(3) $L$ is the fraction field of $B$.** Since $\mathbb{Z}[i] \subset B \subset
 \mathbb{Q}(i)$ and $\operatorname{Frac}(\mathbb{Z}[i]) = \mathbb{Q}(i)$, we hav
e $\operatorname{Frac}(B) = \mathbb{Q}(i) = L$. ✓

**(4) $L/K$ is Galois with group $G$.** $\mathbb{Q}(i)/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois wit
h group $\{1, \sigma\}$. ✓

**(5) $B^G = A$.** This is the key verification. Every element of $B$ has the fo
rm $\frac{a}{(2+i)^n}$ with $a \in \mathbb{Z}[i]$, $n \geq 0$. Suppose $b = \fra
c{a}{(2+i)^n} \in B^G$, so $\sigma(b) = b$:

$$\frac{\sigma(a)}{(2-i)^n} = \frac{a}{(2+i)^n} \implies \sigma(a)\,(2+i)^n = a\
,(2-i)^n \quad \text{in } \mathbb{Z}[i].$$

Now, $2+i$ and $2-i$ are **distinct prime elements** of $\mathbb{Z}[i]$: each ha
s prime norm $N(2\pm i) = 5$, and they are not associates since $(2+i)/(2-i) = (
3+4i)/5 \notin \mathbb{Z}[i]$. Being distinct primes, they are coprime. By uniqu
e factorization in $\mathbb{Z}[i]$, from $(2+i)^n \mid a\,(2-i)^n$ and $\gcd((2+
i)^n, (2-i)^n) = 1$, we get $(2+i)^n \mid a$.

Write $a = (2+i)^n c$ for some $c \in \mathbb{Z}[i]$. Substituting back:

$$\sigma(a) = (2-i)^n \sigma(c), \quad \text{so} \quad (2-i)^n \sigma(c)\,(2+i)^
n = (2+i)^n c\,(2-i)^n,$$

which gives $\sigma(c) = c$, hence $c \in \mathbb{Z}[i]^G = \mathbb{Z}$. Therefo
re:

$$b = \frac{(2+i)^n c}{(2+i)^n} = c \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

Conversely, every integer lies in $B$ and is fixed by $G$. Thus $B^G = \mathbb{Z
} = A$. ✓

### $B$ is not the integral closure of $A$ in $L$

The integral closure of $\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ is $\mathbb{Z}[i]$. But
$B = \mathbb{Z}[i][1/(2+i)] \supsetneq \mathbb{Z}[i]$, since $\frac{1}{2+i} = \f
rac{2-i}{5} \in B \setminus \mathbb{Z}[i]$.

To confirm $\frac{1}{2+i} \notin \mathbb{Z}[i]$: if $\frac{2-i}{5} = a+bi$ for $
a,b \in \mathbb{Z}$, then $5a=2, 5b=-1$, which has no integer solutions.

To confirm $\frac{1}{2+i}$ is not integral over $\mathbb{Z}$: its minimal polyno
mial over $\mathbb{Q}$ is $x^2 - \frac{4}{5}x + \frac{1}{5}$, whose coefficients
 are not integers.

### Conclusion

All hypotheses are satisfied: $A = \mathbb{Z} \subset B = \mathbb{Z}[i][1/(2+i)]
$, both Dedekind domains; $L = \mathbb{Q}(i)$, $K = \mathbb{Q}$, $L/K$ Galois wi
th $B^G = A$; yet $B$ is **not** the integral closure of $A$ in $L$ (it strictly
 contains $\mathbb{Z}[i]$).

The key mechanism: localizing at the prime $(2+i)$, which **splits** in the exte
nsion (its conjugate $(2-i)$ is a distinct prime), enlarges $B$ beyond the integ
ral closure while the coprimality of the split conjugate primes ensures no new $
G$-invariant elements appear. $\boxed{B \text{ need not be the integral closure
of } A \text{ in } L.}$
