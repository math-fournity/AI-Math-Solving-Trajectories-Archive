## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. If $S$ is a $C^k$ compact hypersurface in a manifold $M$ and $F:
M\to M'$ is a $C^l$ diffeomorphism, then $F(S)$ is a compact hypersurface in $M'
$, of regularity $C^{\min(k,l)}$.

We verify the two defining properties — compactness, and being a (codimension‑on
e) embedded submanifold — together with the regularity of the latter.

### 1. Compactness

A $C^l$ diffeomorphism is in particular continuous. $S$ is compact by hypothesis
, and the continuous image of a compact set is compact. Hence $F(S)$ is compact.

### 2. $F(S)$ is an embedded hypersurface of class $C^{\min(k,l)}$

Since $S$ is a $C^k$ hypersurface, it is a $C^k$ embedded submanifold of codimen
sion $1$. Concretely, for every $p\in S$ there exist an open neighborhood $U\sub
set M$ of $p$ and a $C^k$ submersion $g:U\to\mathbb{R}$ (i.e. $dg_q\neq 0$ for a
ll $q\in U\cap S$) such that
$$U\cap S = g^{-1}(0).$$

Fix $p\in S$ and set $q = F(p)\in F(S)$. Because $F$ is a diffeomorphism, $F(U)$
 is an open neighborhood of $q$ in $M'$. Define
$$h := g\circ F^{-1} : F(U)\longrightarrow \mathbb{R}.$$

Since $F^{-1}$ is $C^l$ and $g$ is $C^k$, the composition $h$ is $C^{\min(k,l)}$
.

**Zero set.** Using that $F$ is a bijection,
$$F(U)\cap F(S) \;=\; F(U\cap S) \;=\; F\!\bigl(g^{-1}(0)\bigr) \;=\; \{y\in F(U
): g(F^{-1}(y))=0\} \;=\; h^{-1}(0).$$

**Regularity.** We check that $0$ is a regular value of $h$. By the chain rule,
$$dh_{q} \;=\; dg_{p}\circ d(F^{-1})_{q}.$$
Now $d(F^{-1})_{q}$ is a linear isomorphism (because $F^{-1}$ is a diffeomorphis
m), and $dg_{p}:\,T_{p}M\to\mathbb{R}$ is surjective, i.e. nonzero, because $0$
is a regular value of $g$ at $p\in S$. The composition of a surjective linear ma
p with an isomorphism is again surjective, so $dh_{q}\neq 0$. As $q=F(p)$ was ar
bitrary in $F(S)$, $0$ is a regular value of $h$.

Thus every point of $F(S)$ admits a neighborhood on which $F(S)$ is the zero loc
us of a $C^{\min(k,l)}$ submersion into $\mathbb{R}$. This is precisely the loca
l characterization of a $C^{\min(k,l)}$ embedded submanifold of codimension $1$,
 i.e. a $C^{\min(k,l)}$ hypersurface.

### 3. Conclusion

$F(S)$ is compact (Step 1) and is a $C^{\min(k,l)}$ hypersurface (Step 2). There
fore $F(S)$ is a compact hypersurface. The regularity is the minimum of the regu
larities of $S$ and $F$; in particular $F(S)$ is at least $C^{\min(k,l)}$, and t
his bound is sharp in general (composing a $C^k$ parametrization with a $C^l$ ma
p cannot yield better than $C^{\min(k,l)}$ regularity).
