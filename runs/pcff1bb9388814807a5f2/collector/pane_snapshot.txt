# Solution

**Claim.** Yes. Every affine $k$-variety has the form $\operatorname{Spec} A$ wh
ere $A$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra.

## Proof

Recall the standard definition (scheme-theoretic convention, as in EGA/Hartshorn
e): a **$k$-variety** is a scheme $X$ equipped with a morphism $X \to \operatorn
ame{Spec} k$ that is **of finite type** (some conventions additionally require s
eparatedness or geometric integrality, but the finite-type hypothesis is always
present). An **affine $k$-variety** is a $k$-variety that is affine as a scheme.

Let $X$ be an affine $k$-variety. We verify two things:

**Step 1: $X = \operatorname{Spec} A$ for some ring $A$, and $A$ carries a $k$-a
lgebra structure.**

Since $X$ is affine, by definition there exists a commutative ring $A$ with $X \
cong \operatorname{Spec} A$. The structure morphism $\varphi : X \to \operatorna
me{Spec} k$ is a morphism of schemes over $\operatorname{Spec} k$. Under the ant
i-equivalence of categories between affine schemes and commutative rings, $\varp
hi$ corresponds to a ring homomorphism
$$
\varphi^\# : k \longrightarrow A,
$$
which makes $A$ into a $k$-algebra.

**Step 2: $A$ is finitely generated as a $k$-algebra.**

By hypothesis $\varphi : X \to \operatorname{Spec} k$ is a morphism **of finite
type**. Since both $X = \operatorname{Spec} A$ and $\operatorname{Spec} k$ are a
ffine, the definition of a finite-type morphism between affine schemes specializ
es to the following algebraic condition (see EGA II, (1.4.2) or Hartshorne, II.3
):

> A morphism $\operatorname{Spec} A \to \operatorname{Spec} R$ is of finite type
 if and only if $A$ is a finitely generated $R$-algebra.

Applying this with $R = k$, the finite-type hypothesis on $\varphi$ is equivalen
t to $A$ being a finitely generated $k$-algebra, i.e., there exist elements $a_1
, \dots, a_n \in A$ and a surjective $k$-algebra homomorphism
$$
k[x_1, \dots, x_n] \twoheadrightarrow A, \qquad x_i \mapsto a_i.
$$

**Conclusion.** Combining Steps 1 and 2, $X = \operatorname{Spec} A$ where $A$ i
s a finitely generated $k$-algebra.

**Converse (for completeness).** If $A$ is any finitely generated $k$-algebra, t
hen $\operatorname{Spec} A \to \operatorname{Spec} k$ is a finite-type morphism
(by the same affine criterion applied in reverse), and $\operatorname{Spec} A$ i
s affine. Hence $\operatorname{Spec} A$ is an affine $k$-variety.

Therefore the affine $k$-varieties are **exactly** the schemes of the form $\ope
ratorname{Spec} A$ with $A$ a finitely generated $k$-algebra. $\blacksquare$
