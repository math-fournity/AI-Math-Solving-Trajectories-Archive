**Answer: No.** We construct a counterexample.

Let $L$ denote the **open long ray**: the linearly ordered set $\omega_1 \times
[0,1) \setminus \{(0,0)\}$ with the lexicographic order topology, where $\omega_
1$ is the first uncountable ordinal. Set $M = L \times \mathbb{R}$.

**$M$ is a connected topological 2-manifold.** The open long ray $L$ is a connec
ted 1-manifold (Hausdorff, locally homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}$ — every point ha
s an order-interval neighborhood homeomorphic to an open interval in $\mathbb{R}
$). Therefore $M = L \times \mathbb{R}$ is Hausdorff, connected (product of conn
ected spaces), and locally homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R} = \math
bb{R}^2$.

**$\pi_1(M)$ is finitely generated free (in fact, trivial).** Since $\pi_1(M) =
\pi_1(L) \times \pi_1(\mathbb{R})$, it suffices to show $\pi_1(L) = 1$. Let $f:
S^1 \to L$ be continuous. Since $S^1$ is compact and $L$ is Hausdorff, $f(S^1)$
is compact. The projection of $f(S^1)$ onto the $\omega_1$-factor is a compact s
ubset of $\omega_1$ (with the order topology), hence **bounded**: any unbounded
subset of $\omega_1$ has $\omega_1$ as a limit point, and the open cover $\{[0,
\alpha) : \alpha < \omega_1\}$ admits no finite subcover. Thus $f(S^1) \subset \
{\alpha : \alpha \leq \beta\} \times [0,1)$ for some countable ordinal $\beta <
\omega_1$. Since $\beta$ is countable, the set $\{\alpha : \alpha \leq \beta\} \
times [0,1)$ (with the lexicographic order) is order-isomorphic to a closed inte
rval in $\mathbb{R}$, hence contractible. Therefore $f$ is null-homotopic, givin
g $\pi_1(L) = 1$ and $\pi_1(M) = 1$ (the trivial group, which is free of rank 0)
.

**$M$ is not homeomorphic to any closed surface with finitely many punctures.**
Any closed surface with finitely many punctures is second-countable: it is obtai
ned from a compact metrizable (hence second-countable) space by removing finitel
y many points, and second-countability is preserved under passing to subspaces a
nd removing finitely many points. However, $M = L \times \mathbb{R}$ is **not**
second-countable:

- $L$ is not second-countable: the sets $\{\alpha\} \times (0,1)$ for $\alpha <
\omega_1$ are uncountably many pairwise disjoint open subsets of $L$ (each is th
e open interval $((\alpha, 0), (\alpha, 1))$ in the lexicographic order), and a
second-countable space admits at most countably many pairwise disjoint open sets
.
- If $L \times \mathbb{R}$ were second-countable, then $L$ would be second-count
able: fixing any $y_0 \in \mathbb{R}$, the collection $\{B \cap (L \times \{y_0\
}) : B \in \mathcal{B}\}$ (where $\mathcal{B}$ is a countable base for $L \times
 \mathbb{R}$) would be a countable base for $L \times \{y_0\} \cong L$. This con
tradicts the previous point.

Since $M$ is not second-countable, it cannot be homeomorphic to any closed surfa
ce with finitely many punctures.

**Conclusion.** $M = L \times \mathbb{R}$ is a connected topological 2-manifold
(Hausdorff, locally $\mathbb{R}^2$) with $\pi_1(M) = 1$ (finitely generated free
) that is not homeomorphic to a closed surface with finitely many punctures. The
 answer is **no**.
