Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether an $n$-linked set $Q$ must
"leave open" some value $c \in F$ for a name $\dot{a}$ forced to lie in $F$, whe
re $|F| \leq n$.

**Answer: Yes**, such a $c \in F$ exists.

**Proof.**

Recall that $Q$ being $n$-linked means every subset of $Q$ of cardinality $\leq
n$ is compatible, i.e., admits a common extension (lower bound). In particular,
when $n = \omega$, every countable subset of $Q$ is compatible.

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that no such $c$ exists. Then for every $c \in
F$ there is some $p_c \in Q$ with

$$p_c \Vdash \dot{a} \neq c.$$

Consider the family $\{p_c : c \in F\} \subseteq Q$. Since $|F| \leq n$, this fa
mily has cardinality $\leq n$. By the $n$-linkedness of $Q$, there exists a cond
ition $r \in \mathbb{P}$ extending every $p_c$, i.e., $r \leq p_c$ for all $c \i
n F$.

Forcing is monotone: if $p \Vdash \varphi$ and $q \leq p$, then $q \Vdash \varph
i$. Applying this to each $p_c \Vdash \dot{a} \neq c$, we obtain

$$r \Vdash \dot{a} \neq c \quad \text{for every } c \in F.$$

Equivalently, $r \Vdash \dot{a} \notin F$.

But we are given $\Vdash \dot{a} \in F$, so by monotonicity $r \Vdash \dot{a} \i
n F$ as well. Combining,

$$r \Vdash \dot{a} \in F \;\wedge\; \dot{a} \notin F,$$

i.e., $r \Vdash \bot$. This is impossible: no condition can force a contradictio
n, since every condition $r$ belongs to some generic filter (generic filters exi
st over any condition), and a generic filter cannot contain a condition forcing
falsehood.

This contradiction shows our supposition was false. Hence there exists $c \in F$
 such that for all $p \in Q$,

$$p \nVdash \dot{a} \neq c. \qquad \blacksquare$$
