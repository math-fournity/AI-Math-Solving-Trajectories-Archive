Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there exists a smooth proje
ctive curve over an algebraically closed field that admits finite morphisms to ℙ
¹ of both degree 4 and degree 5.

**Answer: Yes, such curves exist.**

## Proof

Let *k* be an algebraically closed field. We exhibit explicit examples.

### Example 1: The projective line ℙ¹

ℙ¹_k is a smooth projective curve. The morphism

$$\varphi_4 : \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^1, \quad [X:Y] \mapsto [X^4:Y^4]$$

is a finite morphism of degree 4 (the generic fiber consists of 4 points, corres
ponding to the 4th roots). Likewise,

$$\varphi_5 : \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^1, \quad [X:Y] \mapsto [X^5:Y^5]$$

is a finite morphism of degree 5. Thus ℙ¹ itself admits finite morphisms to ℙ¹ o
f both degree 4 and degree 5. ∎

### Example 2 (more interesting): Elliptic curves

Let *E* be an elliptic curve over *k* with origin *O*. We show *E* admits finite
 morphisms to ℙ¹ of every degree *d* ≥ 2, in particular degrees 4 and 5.

**Step 1: h⁰(dO) = d for d ≥ 1.** By Riemann–Roch (genus *g* = 1, canonical divi
sor *K* ~ 0):

$$h^0(dO) = \deg(dO) + 1 - g + h^0(K - dO) = d + h^0(-dO).$$

Since *d* > 0, the divisor *−dO* has negative degree, so *h*⁰(−*dO*) = 0. Thus *
h*⁰(*dO*) = *d*.

**Step 2: |dO| is base-point-free for d ≥ 2.** For any point *P* ∈ *E*, the divi
sor *dO* − *P* has degree *d* − 1 ≥ 1, so *h*⁰(*dO* − *P*) = *d* − 1 < *d* = *h*
⁰(*dO*). Hence *P* is not a base point of |*dO*|. Since *P* was arbitrary, |*dO*
| is base-point-free.

**Step 3: A general pencil in |dO| is base-point-free for d ≥ 2.** The complete
linear system |*dO*| gives a morphism *E* → ℙ^{*d*−1}. A pencil (2-dimensional l
inear subspace *V* ⊂ *H*⁰(*dO*)) gives a rational map *E* ⇢ ℙ¹, which is a morph
ism iff *V* is base-point-free.

A point *P* is a base point of the pencil *V* iff *V* ⊂ *H*⁰(*dO* − *P*), a subs
pace of codimension 1 in *H*⁰(*dO*) (dimension *d* − 1). For each *P*, this cond
ition cuts out a sub-Grassmannian Gr(2, *d*−1) inside Gr(2, *d*), of codimension
 2. As *P* ranges over the 1-dimensional curve *E*, the union of these loci has
codimension ≥ 1 in Gr(2, *d*). Therefore a **general** pencil *V* ⊂ *H*⁰(*dO*) i
s base-point-free.

(For *d* = 2, Gr(2, 2) is a single point and |2*O*| is itself a pencil, already
shown base-point-free in Step 2.)

**Step 4: The resulting morphism has degree d.** A base-point-free pencil whose
divisors all have degree *d* defines a finite morphism *E* → ℙ¹ of degree *d* (t
he degree equals the degree of the divisors in the pencil, since the map is give
n by a degree-*d* linear system without base points).

**Conclusion.** Taking *d* = 4 and *d* = 5, the elliptic curve *E* admits finite
 morphisms to ℙ¹ of degree 4 and degree 5 respectively. Since *E* is a smooth pr
ojective curve over *k*, this confirms the existence of such curves. ∎

### Remark

The same argument works for curves of genus *g* ≤ 5 with appropriate gonality. F
or instance, a general curve of genus 5 has gonality 4 (a *g*¹₄) and also has a
*g*¹₅ (since the Brill–Noether number ρ(5, 1, 5) = 3 ≥ 0). The key point is that
 having a pencil of degree *d* does not preclude having pencils of other degrees
, and for low-genus curves, pencils of many degrees coexist.
