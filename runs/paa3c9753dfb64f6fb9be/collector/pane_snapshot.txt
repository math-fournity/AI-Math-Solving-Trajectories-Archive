Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether DCC on non-finitely generat
ed ideals in a commutative domain implies ACC on all ideals (Noetherianity).

**Answer: Yes, every ascending chain of ideals terminates.**

**Proof.**

Suppose for contradiction that $R$ is not Noetherian. Then there exists a non-fi
nitely generated ideal. Since the non-fg ideals satisfy DCC, the (non-empty) set
 of non-fg ideals has a minimal element $M$ with respect to inclusion.

**Key Lemma (Cancellation):** If $I$ is a non-fg ideal and $0 \ne a \in R$, then
 $aI$ is non-fg.

*Proof of Lemma.* Suppose $aI$ is fg, say $aI = (b_1, \dots, b_k)$. Each $b_i \i
n aI$, so $b_i = ac_i$ for some $c_i \in I$. For any $x \in I$, $ax \in aI = (ac
_1, \dots, ac_k)$, so $ax = a\bigl(\sum r_i c_i + \sum n_i c_i\bigr)$ for some $
r_i \in R$, $n_i \in \mathbb{Z}$. Since $R$ is a domain and $a \ne 0$, we cancel
 $a$: $x = \sum r_i c_i + \sum n_i c_i \in (c_1, \dots, c_k)$. Thus $I \subseteq
 (c_1, \dots, c_k) \subseteq I$, so $I$ is fg—contradiction. $\square$

**Deriving the contradiction.** Since $M$ is non-fg, $M \ne \{0\}$, so pick $a \
in M \setminus \{0\}$.

1. **$aM$ is non-fg:** By the Lemma, since $M$ is non-fg and $a \ne 0$.

2. **$aM \subseteq M$:** Since $M$ is an ideal of $R$ and $a \in R$.

3. **$aM = M$:** $aM$ is a non-fg ideal contained in $M$. By minimality of $M$ a
mong non-fg ideals, we must have $aM = M$.

4. **$aM \subseteq (a)$:** Every element $am \in aM$ lies in $aR \subseteq (a)$.

5. **$(a) \subseteq M$:** Since $a \in M$ and $M$ is an ideal, the ideal $(a)$ g
enerated by $a$ is contained in $M$.

6. **$(a) = M$:** Combining steps 3–5: $M = aM \subseteq (a) \subseteq M$.

But $(a)$ is finitely generated (principal), while $M$ is non-fg. This is a cont
radiction.

The contradiction arises from assuming $R$ is not Noetherian. Therefore $R$ is N
oetherian, i.e., every ascending chain of ideals in $R$ terminates. $\blacksquar
