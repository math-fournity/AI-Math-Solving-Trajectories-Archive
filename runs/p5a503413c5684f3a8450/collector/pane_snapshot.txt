## Solution

We evaluate
$$I = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \int_{\gamma} z^7 \cos\!\left(\frac{1}{z^2}\right) dz,$$
where $\gamma$ is a positively oriented circle centered at the origin with radiu
s $R$.

**Step 1: Identify the singularity.**

The integrand $f(z) = z^7 \cos(1/z^2)$ is analytic everywhere except at $z = 0$,
 where $\cos(1/z^2)$ has an essential singularity (since $1/z^2 \to \infty$ as $
z \to 0$). For any $R > 0$, the contour $\gamma$ encloses the origin, so by the
Residue Theorem,
$$I = \operatorname{Res}(f,\, 0),$$
the residue of $f$ at $z = 0$, i.e., the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ in the Laurent
expansion of $f$ about $0$.

**Step 2: Laurent expansion.**

Using the Taylor series $\cos w = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n\, w^{2n}}{(2n
)!}$ with $w = 1/z^2$:
$$\cos\!\left(\frac{1}{z^2}\right) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{(2n)!\, z
^{2n}}.$$

Multiplying by $z^7$:
$$f(z) = z^7 \cos\!\left(\frac{1}{z^2}\right) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n
}{(2n)!}\, z^{7-2n}.$$

**Step 3: Extract the residue.**

The coefficient of $z^{-1}$ occurs when $7 - 2n = -1$, i.e., $2n = 8$, so $n = 4
$. The corresponding coefficient is
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,\,0) = \frac{(-1)^4}{(2\cdot 4)!} = \frac{1}{8!} = \frac{
1}{40320}.$$

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Therefore,
$$\boxed{I = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \int_{\gamma} z^7 \cos\!\left(\frac{1}{z^2}\right)
 dz = \frac{1}{40320}.}$$
