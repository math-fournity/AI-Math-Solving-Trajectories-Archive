## Proof

Let $K_1 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m})$ and $K_2 = \mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3})$. Let $L$ be the
 Galois closure of $K_2/\mathbb{Q}$, so $L = \mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3}, \zeta_3)$ with
$\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_3$. The unique quadratic subfield of $
L$ is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}) = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$ (the discriminant of $x^3-2$
 is $-108$).

### Frobenius interpretation of the two conditions

For a prime $p$ unramified in both extensions:

- **Split completely in $K_1$**: the Frobenius at $p$ in $\operatorname{Gal}(K_1
/\mathbb{Q})$ is trivial (automatic when $m=1$, since $K_1=\mathbb{Q}$).
- **Inert in $K_2$**: the factorization type in $K_2$ is $(3)$, i.e. a single pr
ime of residue degree $3$. Under the standard correspondence between factorizati
on types in $K_2$ and cycle types of Frobenius in $S_3$ (acting on the three roo
ts of $x^3-2$):
  - type $(1,1,1)$ $\leftrightarrow$ identity,
  - type $(2,1)$ $\leftrightarrow$ transposition,
  - type $(3)$ $\leftrightarrow$ 3-cycle,

  "inert in $K_2$" is equivalent to Frobenius being a **3-cycle** in $S_3$ (2 el
ements).

### Case 1: $m \neq 1$ and $m \neq -3$

Since $_1:\mathbb{Q}]=2$ and $_2:\mathbb{Q}]=3$ are coprime, $K_1 \cap K_2 =
 \mathbb{Q}$. Moreover $K_1 \cap L = \mathbb{Q}$, because the only quadratic sub
field of $L$ is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}) \neq \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m})$. Hence $K_1$ a
nd $L$ are linearly disjoint over $\mathbb{Q}$, the Galois closure of the compos
itum $M = K_1 K_2$ is $N = K_1 L$, and

$$\operatorname{Gal}(N/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \operatorname{Gal}(K_1/\mathbb{Q}) \tim
es \operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times S_3,$$

of order $12$. The Frobenius at $p$ is $(\sigma, \tau) \in \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z
} \times S_3$.

- Split completely in $K_1$: $\sigma = \mathrm{id}$ (1 choice).
- Inert in $K_2$: $\tau$ a 3-cycle (2 choices).

Qualifying elements: $1 \times 2 = 2$. By Chebotarev's density theorem,

$$\delta = \frac{2}{12} = \frac{1}{6}.$$

### Case 2: $m = -3$

Then $K_1 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}) = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3) \subset L$, so $M = N =
L$ and $\operatorname{Gal}(N/\mathbb{Q}) = S_3$.

- Split completely in $K_1 = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$: Frobenius $\in \operatorname{
Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)) = A_3 = \{e, (123), (132)\}$ (the subgroup of order
3).
- Inert in $K_2$: Frobenius is a 3-cycle.

Both conditions: Frobenius $\in \{(123), (132)\}$, i.e. 2 elements out of 6:

$$\delta = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}.$$

### Case 3: $m = 1$

$K_1 = \mathbb{Q}$, so every prime splits completely in $K_1$. We only need $p$
inert in $K_2$, i.e. Frobenius a 3-cycle: 2 elements out of 6:

$$\delta = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}.$$

### Conclusion

The finitely many ramified primes (those dividing $6m$) form a set of Dirichlet
density $0$ and do not affect the result. The explicit Dirichlet density is

$$\boxed{\delta = \begin{cases} \dfrac{1}{3} & \text{if } m \in \{1,\, -3\}, \\[
6pt] \dfrac{1}{6} & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}}$$
