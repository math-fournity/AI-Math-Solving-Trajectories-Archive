## Computing $S(x)$ for small $x$

**Case $x = 0$:**
$$S(0) = \frac{365!}{365^1} \cdot \frac{0}{364!} = 0 < 1. \quad \checkmark \text
{ (not satisfied)}$$

**Case $x = 1$:**
$$S(1) = \frac{365!}{365^2}\left(\frac{0}{364!} + \frac{1}{363!}\right) = \frac{
365!}{365^2 \cdot 363!} = \frac{365 \cdot 364}{365^2} = \frac{364}{365}.$$
Since $364 < 365$, we have $S(1) = \frac{364}{365} < 1$. $\checkmark$ (not satis
fied)

**Case $x = 2$:**
$$S(2) = \frac{365!}{365^3}\left(\frac{0}{364!} + \frac{1}{363!} + \frac{2}{362!
}\right) = \frac{365!}{365^3 \cdot 363!} + \frac{2 \cdot 365!}{365^3 \cdot 362!}
.$$

Computing each term:
$$\frac{365!}{363!} = 365 \cdot 364, \qquad \frac{365!}{362!} = 365 \cdot 364 \c
dot 363.$$

So:
$$S(2) = \frac{365 \cdot 364}{365^3} + \frac{2 \cdot 365 \cdot 364 \cdot 363}{36
5^3} = \frac{364}{365^2} + \frac{2 \cdot 364 \cdot 363}{365^2} = \frac{364(1 + 7
26)}{365^2} = \frac{364 \cdot 727}{365^2}.$$

Now:
$$364 \cdot 727 = 264{,}628, \qquad 365^2 = 133{,}225.$$

Since $264{,}628 > 133{,}225$, we have $S(2) = \frac{264628}{133225} \approx 1.9
86 \ge 1$. $\checkmark$ (satisfied)

## Monotonicity argument

For $x \ge 2$, $S(x)$ is increasing in $x$: each increment of $x$ adds the non-n
egative term $\frac{x \cdot 365!}{365^{x+1}(364-x)!}$ (for $x \ge 1$, this term
is positive), and the existing terms are rescaled by $\frac{365^{x+1}}{365^{x+2}
} = \frac{1}{365}$, but the new dominant term more than compensates. More precis
ely, $S(x+1) = \frac{S(x)}{365} + \frac{(x+1) \cdot 365!}{365^{x+2}(363-x)!}$, a
nd one can verify the sequence is increasing for $x \ge 1$ since the added term
dominates. Therefore once $S(2) \ge 1$, all larger $x$ also satisfy the inequali
ty.

## Conclusion

- $S(0) = 0 < 1$
- $S(1) = \frac{364}{365} < 1$
- $S(2) = \frac{264628}{133225} > 1$

The smallest integer $x$ is $\boxed{2}$.
