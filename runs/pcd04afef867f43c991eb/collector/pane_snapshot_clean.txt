## Proof

Let $U \sim \text{Uniform}[-\pi,\pi]$, $X_k = \cos(kU)$, and $S_n = \sum_{k=1}^n
 X_k$.

**Step 1: Pointwise limit of $S_n/n$ for fixed $U \neq 0$.**

Using the standard trigonometric identity
$$
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\cos(kU) = \frac{\sin\!\bigl(\tfrac{nU}{2}\bigr)\cos\!\bigl(\tfrac
{(n+1)U}{2}\bigr)}{\sin\!\bigl(\tfrac{U}{2}\bigr)},
$$
we obtain, for every $U \in [-\pi,\pi]\setminus\{0\}$,
$$
\left|\frac{S_n}{n}\right| \le \frac{1}{n\,|\sin(U/2)|} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty}
 0.
$$
Hence $\frac{S_n}{n} \to 0$ pointwise on $[-\pi,\pi]\setminus\{0\}$.

**Step 2: Uniform bound.**

Since $|X_k|=|\cos(kU)|\le 1$, we have
$$
\left|\frac{S_n}{n}\right| \le \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|X_k| \le 1,
$$
for every $n$ and every $U$. The constant function $1$ is integrable on the prob
ability space $([-\pi,\pi],\text{Lebesgue}/2\pi)$.

**Step 3: Apply Dominated Convergence.**

The pointwise limit is $0$ for all $U\neq 0$, and $\{0\}$ has Lebesgue measure $
0$. Therefore $\frac{S_n}{n}\to 0$ almost surely. By the Dominated Convergence T
heorem (with dominating function $1$),
$$
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left|\frac{S_n}{n}\right|\right] \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.
$$

**Step 4: Conclude via Markov's inequality.**

For any $\epsilon > 0$,
$$
P\!\left(\left|\frac{S_n}{n}\right| \ge \epsilon\right) \le \frac{1}{\epsilon}\,
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left|\frac{S_n}{n}\right|\right] \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.
$$
Therefore
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty} P\!\left(\left|\frac{S_n}{n}\right| < \epsilon\right) = 1.
$$
