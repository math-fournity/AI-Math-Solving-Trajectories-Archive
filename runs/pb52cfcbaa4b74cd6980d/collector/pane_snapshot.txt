**Claim:** $\operatorname{rank} H_{n-1}(M, M - \{p\}) = 0$.

Let $U$ be an open neighborhood of $p$ in $M$ homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^n$ (a
coordinate chart). Since $\overline{U}$ can be taken inside a larger chart, exci

$$H_k(M, M - \{p\}) \cong H_k(U, U - \{p\})$$

for every $k$. Identifying $U \cong \mathbb{R}^n$ with $p \leftrightarrow 0$, we
 have

$$H_k(M, M - \{p\}) \cong H_k(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}^n - \{0\}).$$

For $k \geq 2$, the contractibility of $\mathbb{R}^n$ makes the connecting map a

$$H_k(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}^n - \{0\}) \cong H_{k-1}(\mathbb{R}^n - \{0\}) \c

We need $H_{n-1}(M, M - \{p\}) \cong \widetilde{H}_{n-2}(S^{n-1})$.

The reduced homology of $S^{n-1}$ is $\mathbb{Z}$ in degree $n-1$ and $0$ elsewh
ere. Therefore $\widetilde{H}_{n-2}(S^{n-1}) = 0$ for every $n \geq 1$:

- $n = 1$: $\widetilde{H}_{-1}(S^0) = 0$ (homology is zero in negative degrees).
- $n = 2$: $\widetilde{H}_0(S^1) = 0$ (the circle is connected).
- $n \geq 3$: $n - 2 \neq n - 1$, so $\widetilde{H}_{n-2}(S^{n-1}) = 0$.

Hence $H_{n-1}(M, M - \{p\}) = 0$.

**Conclusion.**

The group $H_{n-1}(M, M - \{p\})$ is the trivial group, so its rank is

$$\operatorname{rank} H_{n-1}(M, M - \{p\}) = 0.$$
