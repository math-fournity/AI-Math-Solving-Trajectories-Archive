## Proof

We wish to evaluate
$$L = \lim_{\epsilon\to 0^+} \frac{3}{4\pi\epsilon^3}\oint_{S_\epsilon} F \cdot
\hat{N}\,dS,$$
where $F(x,y,z) = (yx^2,\; xyz,\; x^2y^2)$ and $S_\epsilon$ is the sphere of rad
ius $\epsilon$ centered at the origin, with outward unit normal $\hat N$.

**Step 1: Apply the Divergence Theorem.**

Let $B_\epsilon = \{(x,y,z): x^2+y^2+z^2 \le \epsilon^2\}$ be the ball enclosed
by $S_\epsilon$. Since $F$ is a polynomial (hence $C^1$ everywhere), the Diverge
nce Theorem gives
$$\oint_{S_\epsilon} F\cdot\hat N\,dS = \iiint_{B_\epsilon} \nabla\cdot F\,dV.$$

**Step 2: Compute the divergence.**
$$\nabla\cdot F = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(yx^2) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y
}(xyz) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(x^2y^2) = 2xy + xz + 0 = 2xy + xz.$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the volume integral by symmetry.**

The ball $B_\epsilon$ is symmetric under the reflections $(x,y,z)\mapsto(-x,y,z)
$, $(x,y,z)\mapsto(x,-y,z)$, and $(x,y,z)\mapsto(x,y,-z)$.

- The function $2xy$ is odd in $x$ (and also odd in $y$). Since $B_\epsilon$ is
symmetric in $x$, $\iiint_{B_\epsilon} 2xy\,dV = 0$.
- The function $xz$ is odd in $x$ (and also odd in $z$). Since $B_\epsilon$ is s
ymmetric in $x$, $\iiint_{B_\epsilon} xz\,dV = 0$.

Therefore
$$\iiint_{B_\epsilon} (2xy + xz)\,dV = 0 + 0 = 0,$$
and consequently
$$\oint_{S_\epsilon} F\cdot\hat N\,dS = 0$$
for every $\epsilon > 0$.

**Step 4: Take the limit.**

Since the surface integral equals $0$ identically for all $\epsilon>0$,
$$L = \lim_{\epsilon\to 0^+} \frac{3}{4\pi\epsilon^3}\cdot 0 = \lim_{\epsilon\to
 0^+} 0 = 0.$$

**Conclusion.** The limit is $\boxed{0}$.
