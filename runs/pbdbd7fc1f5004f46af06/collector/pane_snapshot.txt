## Proof

Let $T_a = \{(x,y): x\ge 0,\, y\ge 0,\, x+y\le a\}$. We seek
$$L = \lim_{a\to\infty} a^4\, e^{-a^3}\!\iint_{T_a} e^{x^3+y^3}\,dx\,dy.$$

**Step 1. Locate the maximum.** On $T_a$, the function $f(x,y)=x^3+y^3$ is conve
x, so its maximum is attained on the boundary. On the edge $x+y=a$ (with $y=a-x$
, $x\in[0,a]$):
$$f = x^3+(a-x)^3 = a^3 - 3a^2 x + 3ax^2 = a^3\bigl(1-3p(1-p)\bigr),\quad p=x/a.
$$
Since $p(1-p)\ge 0$ with equality only at $p=0,1$, the maximum $a^3$ is attained
 **only** at the two corners $(a,0)$ and $(0,a)$. The minimum along this edge is
 $a^3/4$ at $p=1/2$.

**Step 2. Symmetry reduction.** The triangle and integrand are symmetric under $
x\leftrightarrow y$, so the contributions near $(a,0)$ and $(0,a)$ are equal. Le
t $I_1$ denote the contribution from a neighborhood of $(a,0)$; the total is $2I
_1$ up to negligible error.

**Step 3. Local coordinates near $(a,0)$.** Set $u = a-x$, $v=y$. The constraint
 $x+y\le a$ becomes $(a-u)+v\le a$, i.e. $0\le v\le u$, with $0\le u\le a$. Then
$$(a-u)^3 + v^3 = a^3 - 3a^2 u + 3au^2 - u^3 + v^3,$$
so
$$I_1 = e^{a^3}\int_0^a\int_0^u e^{-3a^2 u}\, e^{\,3au^2 - u^3 + v^3}\,dv\,du.$$

**Step 4. Rescale.** Set $s = 3a^2 u$ (so $u = s/(3a^2)$, $du = ds/(3a^2)$) and
$v = u\,r$ with $r\in[0,1]$ (so $dv = u\,dr = \frac{s}{3a^2}\,dr$). Then $du\,dv
 = \frac{s}{9a^4}\,ds\,dr$, and the exponent becomes
$$-s + \frac{s^2}{3a^3} - \frac{s^3(1-r^3)}{27a^6}.$$
Thus
$$I_1 = \frac{e^{a^3}}{9a^4}\int_0^{3a^3}\int_0^1 s\,e^{-s}\,\exp\!\Bigl(\frac{s
^2}{3a^3}-\frac{s^3(1-r^3)}{27a^6}\Bigr)\,dr\,ds.$$

**Step 5. Take the limit inside.** For $s=O(1)$ (the dominant range, since $s\,e
^{-s}$ is integrable), the correction exponent is $O(1/a^3)\to 0$, so the inner
integral $\to\int_0^1 dr = 1$. To justify dominated convergence: for $s\le a^{3/
2}$, the exponent is bounded in absolute value by $\frac{s^2}{3a^3}\le \frac13$
and $\frac{s^3}{27a^6}\le a^{-3/2}$, giving a uniform bound $e^{1/3}$, so the in
tegrand is dominated by $s\,e^{-s}e^{1/3}$, integrable on $[0,\infty)$. For $s>a
^{3/2}$, we use $\frac{s^2}{3a^3}\le \frac{s}{3a^{3/2}}$ (since $s\le 3a^3$), so
$$s\,e^{-s}\,e^{s^2/(3a^3)} \le s\,e^{-s(1-1/(3a^{3/2}))},$$
and $\int_{a^{3/2}}^\infty s\,e^{-s(1-1/(3a^{3/2}))}\,ds \to 0$ as $a\to\infty$
(the lower limit tends to $\infty$). Hence by dominated convergence,
$$\int_0^{3a^3}\int_0^1 s\,e^{-s}\,e^{(\cdots)}\,dr\,ds \;\longrightarrow\; \int
_0^\infty s\,e^{-s}\,ds \cdot 1 = 1.$$
Therefore
$$I_1 = \frac{e^{a^3}}{9a^4}\,(1+o(1)).$$

**Step 6. The remainder is negligible.**
- *Interior / lower edge:* On $\{x+y\le a-d\}$ for any fixed $d>0$, we have $x^3
+y^3\le (a-d)^3$, so the contribution is at most $a^2 e^{(a-d)^3}$, and $a^4 e^{
-a^3}\cdot a^2 e^{(a-d)^3} = a^6 e^{-3a^2 d+O(ad)}\to 0$.
- *Middle of the hypotenuse:* On the edge $x+y=a$ with $x\in[\delta,\,a-\delta]$
 (fixed $\delta>0$), the gap $a^3 - f = 3a^2 x(1-x/a)\ge 3a^2\cdot\delta\cdot(\d
elta/a)=3a\delta^2\to\infty$. A strip of width $O(a)$ around this segment has ar
ea $O(a^2)$ and integrand $\le e^{a^3-3a\delta^2}$, contributing $a^4 e^{-a^3}\c
dot O(a^2)\,e^{a^3-3a\delta^2}=O(a^6 e^{-3a\delta^2})\to 0$.

So the entire integral equals $I_1+I_2+o(e^{a^3}/a^4) = \dfrac{2\,e^{a^3}}{9a^4}
\,(1+o(1))$.

**Conclusion.**
$$L = \lim_{a\to\infty} a^4 e^{-a^3}\iint_{T_a} e^{x^3+y^3}\,dx\,dy = \lim_{a\to
\infty} a^4\cdot\frac{2\,e^{a^3}}{9a^4}(1+o(1)) = \boxed{\dfrac{2}{9}}.$$
