## Proof

**Answer: Yes, such an isotopy exists.**

**Setup and key facts.** Let $M$ and $N$ be submanifolds of a manifold $P$, with
 $M$ compact, $N$ closed, and $\dim(M) + \dim(N) < \dim(P)$. We are given that $
M$ and $N$ can be made transversal.

**Step 1: Apply the transversality theorem.**

By the Thom Transversality Theorem (in the form applicable to a compact submanif
old $M$ and a closed submanifold $N$ of $P$), there exists an isotopy
$$F: M \times [0,1] \to P$$
with $F(\cdot, 0) = \iota$ (the inclusion $M \hookrightarrow P$) such that $F(M,
 1)$ is transverse to $N$. The compactness of $M$ and closedness of $N$ are prec
isely the hypotheses needed to guarantee that the transversality can be achieved
 via an isotopy (an arbitrarily small perturbation of the inclusion). The proble
m statement confirms that such a transversal configuration is achievable.

**Step 2: Compute the dimension of the transverse intersection.**

When $F(M,1)$ is transverse to $N$ in $P$, the intersection $F(M,1) \cap N$ is a
 (possibly empty) submanifold of $P$, and its dimension is given by the transver
sality dimension formula:
$$\dim\bigl(F(M,1) \cap N\bigr) = \dim(M) + \dim(N) - \dim(P).$$

**Step 3: The dimension is negative, so the intersection is empty.**

By hypothesis, $\dim(M) + \dim(N) < \dim(P)$, so:
$$\dim\bigl(F(M,1) \cap N\bigr) = \dim(M) + \dim(N) - \dim(P) < 0.$$

A manifold cannot have negative dimension. The only submanifold consistent with
a negative dimension formula is the empty set. Therefore:
$$F(M,1) \cap N = \emptyset.$$

**Conclusion.** The isotopy $F$ furnished by the transversality theorem (which e
xists by the given hypothesis that $M$ and $N$ can be made transversal, together
 with the compactness of $M$ and closedness of $N$) satisfies $F(M,1) \cap N = \
emptyset$. The strict dimension inequality $\dim(M) + \dim(N) < \dim(P)$ forces
the transverse intersection to be a manifold of negative dimension, hence empty.
