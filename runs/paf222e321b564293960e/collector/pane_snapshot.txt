Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the only $L^2$ harmonic fun
ction on the strip $S = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^3 : a < x_1 < b\}$ is the zero functi
on.

**Answer: No.** There exist nonzero $L^2$ harmonic functions on the strip. Here
is the proof by explicit construction.

---

## Proof

**Setup.** The strip $S = (a,b) \times \mathbb{R}^2$ is bounded in $x_1$ and unb
ounded in $x' = (x_2, x_3)$. We construct a nonzero function $u \in L^2(S)$ with
 $\Delta u = 0$ in $S$.

**Construction.** Let $f \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^2)$ be any nonzero smooth fun
ction supported in the annulus $\{1 \le |\xi| \le 2\}$. Define
$$
u(x_1, x') = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^2} f(\xi)\, e^{|\xi|\, x_1}\, e^{i\,
\xi\cdot x'}\, d\xi, \qquad \xi = (\xi_2,\xi_3),\; x'=(x_2,x_3).
$$

**Smoothness.** Since $f$ is compactly supported and $e^{|\xi|x_1}$ is smooth in
 $(x_1,\xi)$ on the compact support of $f$, we may differentiate under the integ
ral sign freely. Thus $u \in C^\infty(S)$.

**Harmonicity.** Computing directly:
$$
\partial_{x_1}^2 u = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int f(\xi)\, |\xi|^2\, e^{|\xi|x_1}\, e^{i\x
i\cdot x'}\, d\xi,
$$
$$
\Delta_{x'} u = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int f(\xi)\, (-|\xi|^2)\, e^{|\xi|x_1}\, e^{i\xi\
cdot x'}\, d\xi,
$$
since $\Delta_{x'} e^{i\xi\cdot x'} = -|\xi|^2 e^{i\xi\cdot x'}$. Therefore
$$
\Delta u = \partial_{x_1}^2 u + \Delta_{x'} u = 0.
$$

**$L^2$ bound.** By Plancherel's theorem in the $x'$ variables, for each $x_1$:
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}^2} |u(x_1,x')|^2\, dx' = \int_{\mathbb{R}^2} |f(\xi)|^2\, e^{2|
\xi|\, x_1}\, d\xi.
$$
Integrating over $x_1 \in (a,b)$:
$$
\|u\|_{L^2(S)}^2 = \int_a^b \int_{\mathbb{R}^2} |f(\xi)|^2\, e^{2|\xi|\, x_1}\,
d\xi\, dx_1.
$$
On the support of $f$, $|\xi| \le 2$, and $x_1 \in (a,b)$, so $2|\xi|\, x_1 \le
4\max(|a|,|b|)$. Hence
$$
\|u\|_{L^2(S)}^2 \le (b-a)\, e^{4\max(|a|,|b|)} \int_{\mathbb{R}^2} |f(\xi)|^2\,
 d\xi < \infty.
$$

**Nonvanishing.** For any fixed $x_1 \in (a,b)$, the function $\xi \mapsto f(\xi
) e^{|\xi|x_1}$ is nonzero in $L^2(\mathbb{R}^2)$ (since $f \neq 0$ and $e^{|\xi
|x_1} > 0$). By injectivity of the Fourier transform on $L^2$, $u(x_1, \cdot) \n
ot\equiv 0$. Hence $u$ is not the zero function.

**Conclusion.** We have exhibited a nonzero $L^2$ harmonic function on $S$. Ther
efore the zero function is **not** the only $L^2$ harmonic function in the strip
.

---

**Remark.** The key point is that the strip is *bounded* in $x_1$, so the growin
g mode $e^{|\xi|x_1}$ remains bounded on $(a,b)$ for every fixed $\xi$. This lea
ves the $L^2$ constraint to act only in the $x'$ directions, which is satisfied
by any $L^2$ spectral density $f(\xi)$ with bounded $|\xi|$-support. This contra
sts sharply with $\mathbb{R}^3$ itself (where $L^2$ harmonic functions must vani
sh by the mean-value property) and with half-spaces with additional boundary con
ditions.
