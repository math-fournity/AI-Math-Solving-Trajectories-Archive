## Proof

**Setup.** Let $E$ be an $n$-dimensional vector space over a field $k$ with basi
s $\{\beta_1,\dots,\beta_n\}$. Identifying vectors with their coordinate columns
 in this basis, the action $M_n(k)\times E\to E$, $(A,v)\mapsto A\cdot v$, is th
e standard matrix–vector product. This realizes $M_n(k)\cong \operatorname{End}_
k(E)$ acting on $E$ as its natural module.

**Claim.** $E$ is a simple $M_n(k)$-module, so its only submodules are $\{0\}$ a
nd $E$. Hence there are exactly **2** submodules.

**Proof of simplicity.** Let $W\subseteq E$ be any nonzero $M_n(k)$-submodule. P
ick $0\neq w\in W$. We show $M_n(k)\cdot w = E$, which forces $W=E$.

Since $w\neq 0$, extend it to a $k$-basis $\{w, e_2,\dots,e_n\}$ of $E$. For any
 target $v\in E$, define a $k$-linear map $T:E\to E$ by $T(w)=v$ and $T(e_i)=0$
for $i\geq 2$. This $T$ is an endomorphism of $E$, hence corresponds to some $A\
in M_n(k)$, and $A\cdot w = T(w) = v$.

Thus every $v\in E$ lies in $M_n(k)\cdot w\subseteq W$, giving $E\subseteq W$, s
o $W=E$. $\square$

**Conclusion.** The only $M_n(k)$-submodules of $E$ are $\{0\}$ and $E$.
