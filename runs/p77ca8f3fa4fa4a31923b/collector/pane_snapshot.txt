**Answer: Yes, such a binary operation exists.**

**Proof.**

Let $S$ be the free semigroup on countably many generators $x_1, x_2, x_3, \dots
$. Concretely, the elements of $S$ are all finite non-empty words over the alpha
bet $\{x_1, x_2, \dots\}$, and the binary operation is **concatenation**: if $w
= w_1 w_2 \cdots w_m$ and $v = v_1 v_2 \cdots v_n$ are two words, then $w \cdot
v = w_1 \cdots w_m v_1 \cdots v_n$.

**$S$ is countably infinite.** For each fixed length $k \geq 1$, the set of word
s of length $k$ over a countable alphabet is a countable set (it is a countable
union of countable sets, or equivalently $\mathbb{N}^k$ which is countable). The
n $S = \bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty} \{\text{words of length } k\}$ is a countable unio
n of countable sets, hence countable. Since there are infinitely many words (e.g
., $x_1, x_1 x_1, x_1 x_1 x_1, \dots$), $S$ is countably infinite: $|S| = \aleph
_0$.

**Counting the closed subsets.** For each subset $A \subseteq \mathbb{N}^+ = \{1
, 2, 3, \dots\}$, define
$$S_A = \{\text{all finite non-empty words using only generators } x_i \text{ wi
th } i \in A\}.$$
Each $S_A$ is closed under concatenation: if $w, v \in S_A$, then $w$ and $v$ us
e only generators indexed by $A$, so $w \cdot v$ also uses only generators index
ed by $A$, hence $w \cdot v \in S_A$. Thus $S_A$ is a subsemigroup (a subset clo
sed under the operation).

**The map $A \mapsto S_A$ is injective.** Suppose $A \neq B$; without loss of ge
nerality there exists $i \in A \setminus B$. Then the single-letter word $x_i$ b
elongs to $S_A$ (it uses only generator $x_i$ with $i \in A$), but $x_i \notin S
_B$ (every word in $S_B$ uses only generators $x_j$ with $j \in B$, and $i \noti
n B$, so $x_i$ cannot appear). Therefore $S_A \neq S_B$.

**Conclusion.** There are $2^{\aleph_0}$ subsets of $\mathbb{N}^+$, so by inject
ivity there are at least $2^{\aleph_0}$ subsets of $S$ that are closed under the
 operation. Since $S$ itself has exactly $2^{\aleph_0}$ subsets in total, the nu
mber of operation-closed subsets is exactly $2^{\aleph_0}$. By Cantor's theorem,
$$2^{\aleph_0} > \aleph_0 = |S|.$$

Therefore, there exists a binary operation on a countably infinite set (namely,
concatenation on the free semigroup of countable rank) for which the number of c
losed subsets exceeds the cardinality of the set. $\blacksquare$
