## Proof

We wish to determine whether
$$L := \lim_{N \to \infty} e^N \int_{N}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-x}}{\log x}\,dx$$
equals $0$.

**Step 1: Rewrite via substitution.** Let $u = x - N$, so $x = N + u$ and $dx =
du$. Then
$$e^N \int_{N}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-x}}{\log x}\,dx = e^N \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{
e^{-(N+u)}}{\log(N+u)}\,du = \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-u}}{\log(N+u)}\,du.$$

So we need to show
$$\lim_{N\to\infty} \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-u}}{\log(N+u)}\,du = 0.$$

**Step 2: Pointwise limit of the integrand.** For each fixed $u \geq 0$,
$$\log(N+u) \to \infty \quad\text{as } N\to\infty,$$
hence
$$\frac{e^{-u}}{\log(N+u)} \to 0.$$

**Step 3: Dominating function.** For $N \geq 2$ and all $u \geq 0$, we have $N +
 u \geq 2$, so $\log(N+u) \geq \log 2 > 0$. Therefore
$$0 \leq \frac{e^{-u}}{\log(N+u)} \leq \frac{e^{-u}}{\log 2}.$$
The function $g(u) = \frac{e^{-u}}{\log 2}$ is integrable on $[0,\infty)$ since
$$\int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-u}}{\log 2}\,du = \frac{1}{\log 2} < \infty.$$

**Step 4: Apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem.** The integrands $f_N(u) = \f
rac{e^{-u}}{\log(N+u)}$ are dominated by the integrable function $g(u) = \frac{e
^{-u}}{\log 2}$ (for $N\geq 2$) and converge pointwise to $0$. By the Dominated
Convergence Theorem,
$$\lim_{N\to\infty} \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-u}}{\log(N+u)}\,du = \int_0^\infty \
lim_{N\to\infty} \frac{e^{-u}}{\log(N+u)}\,du = \int_0^\infty 0\,du = 0.$$

**Conclusion.** The limit converges, and its value is $0$:
$$\boxed{\lim_{N \to \infty} e^N \int_{N}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-x}}{\log x}\,dx = 0
.}$$
