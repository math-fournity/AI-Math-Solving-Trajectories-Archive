## Proof

**Answer: Yes, $p(\mathcal{L})$ holds.**

Let $R = \{\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n\} \subseteq K$ be the set of roots of $f$ i
n $K$ (listed with multiplicity). Since $p(K)$ holds, $f$ splits completely in $
K[x]$, so $f = c\prod_{i=1}^n (x - \alpha_i)$ with $c \in F^\times$ and $\alpha_
i \in K$.

**Key claim.** For any intermediate field $L$ ($F \subseteq L \subseteq K$), the
 property $p(L)$ holds if and only if $R \subseteq L$.

*Proof of claim.* If $p(L)$ holds, then $f = c\prod_{i=1}^n (x - \beta_i)$ with
$\beta_i \in L$. Since $L[x] \subseteq K[x]$ and factorization in the UFD $K[x]$
 is unique, $\{\beta_1,\dots,\beta_n\} = \{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n\} = R$, so $R
 \subseteq L$. Conversely, if $R \subseteq L$, then $f = c\prod(x-\alpha_i)$ wit
h each $\alpha_i \in L$, so $f$ splits into linear factors in $L[x]$. $\square$

Now consider the family
$$\mathcal{F} := \{L \mid F \subseteq L \subseteq K,\; R \subseteq L\}.$$
By the claim, $\mathcal{L} = \bigcap \mathcal{F}$. This family is nonempty since
 $K \in \mathcal{F}$ (as $p(K)$ holds, equivalently $R \subseteq K$).

**$\mathcal{L}$ is a field.** The intersection of any nonempty family of subfiel
ds of $K$ is again a subfield of $K$: if $a, b \in \mathcal{L}$, then $a, b \in
L$ for every $L \in \mathcal{F}$, so $a+b, ab, a^{-1}$ (for $a \neq 0$) all lie
in every such $L$, hence in $\mathcal{L}$.

**$\mathcal{L}$ contains $F$.** Every $L \in \mathcal{F}$ contains $F$ by defini
tion, so $\mathcal{L} \supseteq F$.

**$\mathcal{L}$ contains $R$.** Every $L \in \mathcal{F}$ contains $R$ by defini
tion, so every root $\alpha_i \in \mathcal{L}$. Thus $R \subseteq \mathcal{L}$.

**Conclusion.** Since $\mathcal{L}$ is a field with $F \subseteq \mathcal{L} \su
bseteq K$ and $R \subseteq \mathcal{L}$, by the claim $p(\mathcal{L})$ holds. Co
ncretely, $f = c\prod_{i=1}^n (x - \alpha_i)$ with all $\alpha_i \in \mathcal{L}
$, so $f$ factors completely into linear factors in $\mathcal{L}[x]$.

**Remark.** In fact $\mathcal{L}$ is precisely the splitting field of $f$ over $
F$ inside $K$, i.e., $\mathcal{L} = F(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)$: it is the small
est subfield of $K$ containing $F$ and all roots of $f$, being the intersection
of all such subfields.
