**Proof.**

Let
$$
M=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\ C&D\end{pmatrix},\qquad J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\ -I&0\end{p
matrix},
$$
so $M$ is $2n\times 2n$ and $J^T=-J$.

**Step 1. Compute $MJM^{T}$.** Since $MJ=\begin{pmatrix}-B&A\\ -D&C\end{pmatrix}
$,
$$
MJM^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}-B&A\\ -D&C\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A^{T}&C^{T}\\ B^{T
}&D^{T}\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}AB^{T}-BA^{T}&AD^{T}-BC^{T}\\ CB^{T}-DA^{T}&CD^{T}-DC^{T}\end{pm
atrix}.
$$

Now use the hypotheses:
- $AB^{T}$ symmetric $\Rightarrow AB^{T}-BA^{T}=0$ (top-left).
- $CD^{T}$ symmetric $\Rightarrow CD^{T}-DC^{T}=0$ (bottom-right).
- $AD^{T}-BC^{T}=I$ (top-right).

For the bottom-left entry, note that $MJM^{T}$ is **always skew-symmetric**, sin
ce
$$
(MJM^{T})^{T}=M J^{T} M^{T}=-MJM^{T}.
$$
Hence the bottom-left block equals the negative transpose of the top-right block
:
$$
CB^{T}-DA^{T}=-(AD^{T}-BC^{T})^{T}=-I^{T}=-I.
$$

Therefore
$$
MJM^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\ -I&0\end{pmatrix}=J. \tag{$\ast$}
$$

**Step 2. $M$ is invertible.** Taking determinants in $(\ast)$,
$$
\det(M)\det(J)\det(M^{T})=\det(J)\;\Longrightarrow\;\det(M)^{2}=1,
$$
so $\det(M)\neq 0$ and $M$ is invertible.

**Step 3. Derive $M^{T}JM=J$.** From $(\ast)$, left-multiply by $M^{-1}$:
$$
JM^{T}=M^{-1}J.
$$
Take the inverse of both sides of $(\ast)$ (using $J^{-1}=-J$):
$$
(M^{T})^{-1}J^{-1}M^{-1}=J^{-1}\;\Longrightarrow\;(M^{T})^{-1}(-J)M^{-1}=-J\;\Lo
ngrightarrow\;(M^{T})^{-1}JM^{-1}=J.
$$
Left-multiply by $M^{T}$ and right-multiply by $M$:
$$
JM=M^{T}JM\;\Longrightarrow\;M^{T}JM=J. \tag{$\ast\ast$}
$$

**Step 4. Read off the desired identity.** Directly,
$$
M^{T}JM=\begin{pmatrix}A^{T}&C^{T}\\ B^{T}&D^{T}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&I\
\ -I&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\ C&D\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}A^{T}C-C^{T}A&A^{T}D-C^{T}B\\ B^{T}C-D^{T}A&B^{T}D-D^{T}B\end{pm
atrix}.
$$
Comparing the **top-right block** of $(\ast\ast)$ with $J$:
$$
A^{T}D-C^{T}B=I.
$$

This completes the proof. $\blacksquare$
