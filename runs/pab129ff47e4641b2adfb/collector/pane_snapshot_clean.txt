## Proof

### Upper Bound: $|Q(A)| < \frac{1}{2}|A|^4$

Define the involution $\sigma$ on tuples $(a,b,c,d) \in A^4$ with $c \neq d$ by
$\sigma(a,b,c,d) = (b,a,d,c)$.

**$\sigma$ preserves the ratio:** $\frac{b-a}{d-c} = \frac{-(a-b)}{-(c-d)} = \fr
ac{a-b}{c-d}$.

**$\sigma$ is fixed-point-free:** If $(a,b,c,d) = (b,a,d,c)$, then $a=b$ and $c=
d$, contradicting $c \neq d$.

**$\sigma$ preserves the domain:** $c \neq d \implies d \neq c$, so the swapped
tuple also satisfies the constraint.

Therefore every value in $Q(A)$ is attained by an **even** number of tuples — in
 particular, at least **2**. The total number of tuples is $|A|^2 \cdot |A|(|A|-
1) = |A|^3(|A|-1)$, so:

$$|Q(A)| \leq \frac{|A|^3(|A|-1)}{2} = \frac{|A|^4 - |A|^3}{2} < \frac{|A|^4}{2}
.$$

Thus $|Q(A)| \leq \frac{1}{2}|A|^4$ for all $A$.

### Lower Bound: $\lambda = \frac{1}{2}$ is sharp

We construct sets $A$ with $|Q(A)|/|A|^4 \to \frac{1}{2}$.

Let $t > 1$ be a **transcendental** real number and set $A_n = \{1, t, t^2, \ldo
ts, t^{n-1}\}$, $|A_n| = n$.

**Claim:** The only coincidences among the ratios $\frac{t^i - t^j}{t^k - t^l}$
(with $k \neq l$) are those forced by the involution $(i,j,k,l) \mapsto (j,i,l,k
)$.

*Proof of claim.* The case $i = j$ gives ratio $0$, and all such tuples are pair
ed by the involution.

For $i \neq j, k \neq l$: by adjusting signs, any ratio can be written as $\vare
psilon \cdot t^s \cdot \frac{t^p - 1}{t^q - 1}$ where $p = |i-j| \geq 1$, $q = |
k-l| \geq 1$, $s \in \mathbb{Z}$, and $\varepsilon \in \{+1, -1\}$ depends on th
e sign pattern.

**Sign mismatch is impossible for $t > 1$:** If $\varepsilon \neq \varepsilon'$,
 we'd need $t^s \cdot \frac{t^p-1}{t^q-1} = -t^{s'} \cdot \frac{t^{p'}-1}{t^{q'}
-1}$. For $t > 1$, each factor $t^m - 1 > 0$ and $t^s > 0$, so the left side is
positive while the right side is negative — contradiction.

**Same sign:** Suppose $t^s \cdot \frac{t^p-1}{t^q-1} = t^{s'} \cdot \frac{t^{p'
}-1}{t^{q'}-1}$. Since $t$ is transcendental, this is an identity of rational fu
nctions. Cross-multiplying:

$$(t^{p'}-1)(t^q - 1) = t^{s-s'}(t^p - 1)(t^{q'}-1).$$

Expanding both sides as polynomials in $t$:

$$\underbrace{t^{p'+q}}_{+} - \underbrace{t^{p'}}_{-} - \underbrace{t^q}_{-} + \
underbrace{1}_{+} = \underbrace{t^{s-s'+p+q'}}_{+} - \underbrace{t^{s-s'+p}}_{-}
 - \underbrace{t^{s-s'+q'}}_{-} + \underbrace{t^{s-s'}}_{+}.$$

Matching the **positive-coefficient** exponents: $\{p'+q,\; 0\} = \{s-s'+p+q',\;
 s-s'\}$.

- **Subcase A:** $s - s' = 0$ and $p'+q = p+q'$. Then matching negative exponent
s: $\{p', q\} = \{p, q'\}$.
  - If $p'=p, q=q'$: trivial, $(p,q,s)=(p',q',s')$.
  - If $p'=q', q=p$: then $p'+q = q'+p = p+q'$ ✓, but $p'=q'$ forces $\frac{t^{p
'}-1}{t^{q'}-1}=1$ and $q=p$ forces $\frac{t^p-1}{t^q-1}=1$. Both ratios equal $
t^s$ with $s=s'$ — trivially identical.

- **Subcase B:** $s-s' = p'+q$ and $0 = s-s'+p+q'$, giving $s-s' = -(p+q') < 0$,
 but also $s-s' = p'+q > 0$ — contradiction.

This proves the claim: every nonzero ratio is achieved by **exactly 2** tuples (
related by the involution). $\square$

**Counting:** The number of tuples with $a \neq b, c \neq d$ is $n(n-1) \cdot n(
n-1) = n^2(n-1)^2$, giving $n^2(n-1)^2/2$ distinct nonzero ratios. Adding the si
ngle value $0$:

$$|Q(A_n)| = \frac{n^2(n-1)^2}{2} + 1.$$

Therefore:

$$\frac{|Q(A_n)|}{|A_n|^4} = \frac{(n-1)^2}{2n^2} + \frac{1}{n^4} \xrightarrow{n
 \to \infty} \frac{1}{2}.$$

For any $\lambda < \frac{1}{2}$, choosing $n$ sufficiently large yields $|Q(A_n)
| > \lambda |A_n|^4$.

### Conclusion

The upper bound gives $|Q(A)| < \frac{1}{2}|A|^4$ for all $A$, and the geometric
-progression construction shows the ratio $|Q(A)|/|A|^4$ can be made arbitrarily
 close to $\frac{1}{2}$. Therefore the smallest real number $\lambda$ such that
$|Q(A)| \leq \lambda|A|^4$ for all finite sets $A$ with $|A| \geq 2$ is:

$$\boxed{\lambda = \dfrac{1}{2}}$$
