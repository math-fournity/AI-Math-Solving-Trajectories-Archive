## Proof

**Answer: Yes, the vector $(X(t_1),\dots,X(t_n))$ is independent of $B(1)$.**

**Step 1: Joint Gaussianity.** The vector $(B(t_1),\dots,B(t_n),B(1))$ is jointl
y Gaussian, since Brownian motion has jointly Gaussian finite-dimensional distri
butions. Since each $X(t_i) = B(t_i) - t_i B(1)$ is a linear combination of comp
onents of this Gaussian vector, the augmented vector
$$(X(t_1),\dots,X(t_n),B(1))$$
is also jointly Gaussian.

**Step 2: Compute the covariances.** For each $i = 1,\dots,n$, using $\operatorn
ame{Cov}(B(s),B(t)) = \min(s,t)$:
$$\operatorname{Cov}(X(t_i), B(1)) = \operatorname{Cov}\!\big(B(t_i) - t_i B(1),
\, B(1)\big) = \operatorname{Cov}(B(t_i),B(1)) - t_i\operatorname{Var}(B(1)).$$
Since $0 < t_i < 1$, we have $\operatorname{Cov}(B(t_i),B(1)) = \min(t_i,1) = t_
i$, and $\operatorname{Var}(B(1)) = 1$. Therefore
$$\operatorname{Cov}(X(t_i),B(1)) = t_i - t_i\cdot 1 = 0.$$

**Step 3: Conclude independence.** For jointly Gaussian random variables, zero c
ovariance implies independence. Since every component $X(t_i)$ of the vector $(X
(t_1),\dots,X(t_n))$ is uncorrelated with $B(1)$, and the full vector $(X(t_1),\
dots,X(t_n),B(1))$ is jointly Gaussian, it follows that the random vector $(X(t_
1),\dots,X(t_n))$ is independent of $B(1)$.

**Remark.** This is the fundamental property of the Brownian bridge: it is preci
sely the component of Brownian motion on $[0,1]$ that is *orthogonal* to (and, b
y Gaussianity, *independent* of) the terminal value $B(1)$. Conditioning $B(t)$
on $B(1)=0$ yields $X(t)$, and the conditional distribution does not depend on t
he value of $B(1)$ precisely because of this independence.
