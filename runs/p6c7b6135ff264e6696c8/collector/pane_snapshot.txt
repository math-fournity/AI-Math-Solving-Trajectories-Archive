# Cohen's Theorem

**Claim:** Yes. If every prime ideal of a commutative ring $R$ is finitely gener
ated, then $R$ is Noetherian (every ideal is finitely generated).

## Proof

Assume, towards a contradiction, that $R$ is not Noetherian. Let
$$\Sigma = \{\, I \trianglelefteq R : I \text{ is not finitely generated}\,\},$$
which is nonempty by assumption. We partially order $\Sigma$ by inclusion and ve
rify the hypothesis of Zorn's lemma.

**$\Sigma$ is inductive.** Let $\{I_\alpha\}_{\alpha\in A}$ be a chain in $\Sigm
a$ and set $J = \bigcup_{\alpha} I_\alpha$, which is an ideal. If $J$ were finit
ely generated, say $J = (x_1,\dots,x_n)$, then each $x_i \in I_{\alpha_i}$ for s
ome $\alpha_i$; since the $I_\alpha$ form a chain, all $x_i$ lie in a single $I_
\beta$ (take $\beta = \max_i \alpha_i$). Then $J \subseteq I_\beta \subseteq J$,
 so $I_\beta = J$ is finitely generated — contradicting $I_\beta \in \Sigma$. He
nce $J \in \Sigma$, and $J$ is an upper bound for the chain.

By Zorn's lemma, $\Sigma$ admits a maximal element $P$.

**$P$ is prime.** Suppose $a,b \in R$ with $ab \in P$ but $a \notin P$ and $b \n
otin P$. We derive a contradiction.

Since $a \notin P$, the ideal $P + (a)$ strictly contains $P$, so by maximality
of $P$ in $\Sigma$ it is finitely generated. Write
$$P + (a) = (p_1, \dots, p_m, a), \qquad p_i \in P.$$

Consider the colon ideal $(P : a) = \{\, r \in R : ra \in P \,\}$. Since $ab \in
 P$ and $b \notin P$, we have $b \in (P : a) \setminus P$, so $P \subsetneq (P :
 a)$. Again by maximality, $(P : a)$ is finitely generated, say
$$(P : a) = (t_1, \dots, t_k).$$

Now we show $P$ is finitely generated, contradicting $P \in \Sigma$. Take any $x
 \in P \subseteq P + (a) = (p_1,\dots,p_m,a)$, so
$$x = r_1 p_1 + \cdots + r_m p_m + s a \quad\text{for some } r_i, s \in R.$$
Since $x \in P$ and each $r_i p_i \in P$, we get $sa = x - \sum r_i p_i \in P$,
hence $s \in (P : a) = (t_1,\dots,t_k)$. Thus $s = c_1 t_1 + \cdots + c_k t_k$ f
or some $c_j \in R$, and
$$x = r_1 p_1 + \cdots + r_m p_m + c_1(t_1 a) + \cdots + c_k(t_k a).$$
This shows
$$P \subseteq (p_1, \dots, p_m, t_1 a, \dots, t_k a).$$
The reverse inclusion is immediate: each $p_i \in P$, and each $t_j a \in P$ bec
ause $t_j \in (P : a)$. Therefore
$$P = (p_1, \dots, p_m, t_1 a, \dots, t_k a),$$
a finitely generated ideal — contradicting $P \in \Sigma$.

Hence no such $a, b$ exist: $P$ is prime.

**Conclusion.** We have produced a prime ideal $P$ that is not finitely generate
d, contradicting the hypothesis that every prime ideal of $R$ is finitely genera
ted. Therefore $\Sigma$ must be empty, i.e. every ideal of $R$ is finitely gener
ated, and $R$ is Noetherian. $\blacksquare$
