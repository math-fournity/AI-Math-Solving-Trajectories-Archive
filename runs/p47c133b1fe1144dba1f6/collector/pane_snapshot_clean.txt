## Proof

**The implication is true.** In fact, it suffices that $K_t$ be compact for a *s
ingle* $t_0 > 0$.

### Step 1: $K$ is non-negative

Since $K$ is self-adjoint, its spectrum $\sigma(K) \subset \mathbb{R}$. The cond
ition $\|e^{-tK}\| \le 1$ for all $t > 0$ implies, via the spectral mapping theo
rem, that for every $\lambda \in \sigma(K)$ we have $e^{-t\lambda} \in \sigma(e^
{-tK})$ and hence $|e^{-t\lambda}| \le 1$. Since $\lambda$ is real, $e^{-t\lambd
a} \le 1$ gives $\lambda \ge 0$. Thus
$$\sigma(K) \subseteq [0, \infty),$$
i.e., $K \ge 0$.

### Step 2: $e^{-t_0 K}$ is injective

Fix any $t_0 > 0$. By the spectral theorem, $e^{-t_0 K} = \int_0^\infty e^{-t_0
\lambda}\, dE(\lambda)$. For any $x$ in the Hilbert space,
$$\|e^{-t_0 K}x\|^2 = \int_0^\infty e^{-2t_0\lambda}\, d\mu_x(\lambda),$$
where $\mu_x$ is the spectral measure associated to $x$. Since $e^{-2t_0\lambda}
 > 0$ for all $\lambda \in [0,\infty)$, the integral vanishes if and only if $\m
u_x \equiv 0$, i.e., $x = 0$. Therefore
$$\ker(e^{-t_0 K}) = \{0\}.$$

### Step 3: Apply the spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators

$T := e^{-t_0 K}$ is compact and self-adjoint with $\ker(T) = \{0\}$. By the spe
ctral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators, there exists a (finite or coun
table) orthonormal basis $\{e_n\}$ of the Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ consisting
 of eigenvectors of $T$, with
$$T e_n = \mu_n e_n, \quad \mu_n > 0, \quad \mu_n \to 0,$$
and each $\mu_n$ has **finite multiplicity**. (Since $\ker(T) = \{0\}$, $0$ is n
ot an eigenvalue; the eigenvectors for the nonzero eigenvalues span all of $\mat
hcal{H}$.)

### Step 4: Transfer to eigenstructure of $K$

Since $T = e^{-t_0 K}$ and $K$ is self-adjoint, the operators $T$ and $K$ are si
multaneously diagonalizable (they are functions of the same spectral measure). C
oncretely, $T e_n = \mu_n e_n$ implies
$$K e_n = \lambda_n e_n, \quad \text{where } \lambda_n = -\frac{1}{t_0}\ln \mu_n
.$$

Since $\mu_n \in (0, 1]$ (because $\lambda_n \ge 0$) and $\mu_n \to 0^+$, we obt
ain
$$\lambda_n = -\frac{\ln \mu_n}{t_0} \to +\infty.$$

Each $\lambda_n$ has **finite multiplicity** (inherited from $\mu_n$), and the e
igenvectors $\{e_n\}$ form an **orthonormal basis** of $\mathcal{H}$.

### Step 5: Conclude purely discrete spectrum

We have shown:
- $\sigma(K) = \{\lambda_n : n = 1, 2, \dots\}$ (pure point spectrum, since the
eigenvectors form an ONB).
- Each $\lambda_n$ is an eigenvalue of **finite multiplicity**.
- $\lambda_n \to +\infty$, so the eigenvalues have **no finite accumulation poin
t**.

This is precisely the characterization of a self-adjoint operator with **purely
discrete spectrum**, i.e., $\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(K) = \emptyset$.

### Remark on the hypothesis strength

The hypothesis requires compactness for *every* $t > 0$, but the proof only used
 compactness at a single $t_0 > 0$. Hence the given (stronger) hypothesis certai
nly implies the conclusion. $\blacksquare$
