$$D^2 = \sum_{\sigma,\tau}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)\pro

**Separation of the integral.** Since the integrand factorizes over $x_1,x_2,x_3

$$I = \sum_{\sigma,\tau}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)\prod_

**The inner integral.** Using $\sin mx\sin nx=\tfrac12[\cos(m-n)x-\cos(m+n)x]$ a

$$M_{mn}=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin(mx)\sin(nx)\,dx=\pi\bigl(\delta_{m,n}-\delta_{m,-
n}\bigr).$$

So $M=\pi N$ where $N_{mn}=\delta_{m,n}-\delta_{m,-n}$.

**Collapsing the double sum.** I claim $I=6\det(M)$. Indeed, the inner sum over
$\sigma$:

$$\sum_{\sigma}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i}M_{\sigma(i),\tau(i)}.$$

Substituting $\rho=\sigma\circ\tau^{-1}$ gives $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)=\oper
atorname{sgn}(\rho)\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)$ and $\prod_i M_{\sigma(i),\tau(i)}=

$$\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)\sum_{\rho}\operatorname{sgn}(\rho)\prod_j M_{\rho(j),
j}=\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)\det(M^T)=\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)\det(M).$$

**Evaluating $\det(N)$.** The matrix $N$ has entries $N_{ij}=\delta_{\lambda_i,\
lambda_j}-\delta_{\lambda_i,-\lambda_j}$, i.e.:
