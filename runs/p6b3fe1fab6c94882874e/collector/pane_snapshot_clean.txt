## Solution

**Claim.** The largest topology on a set $X$ that contains a given family of sub
sets $\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)$ is the discrete topology $\mathcal{P}(
X)$ (the power set of $X$).

---

### Proof

We verify the three required facts.

**1. $\mathcal{P}(X)$ is a topology on $X$.**

- $\varnothing\in\mathcal{P}(X)$ and $X\in\mathcal{P}(X)$, since both are subset
s of $X$.
- **Arbitrary unions.** Let $\{U_i\}_{i\in I}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)$. Each $U_i
\subseteq X$, so $\bigcup_{i\in I}U_i\subseteq X$, hence $\bigcup_{i\in I}U_i\in
\mathcal{P}(X)$.
- **Finite intersections.** Let $U_1,\dots,U_n\in\mathcal{P}(X)$. Each $U_k\subs
eteq X$, so $\bigcap_{k=1}^{n}U_k\subseteq X$, hence $\bigcap_{k=1}^{n}U_k\in\ma
thcal{P}(X)$.

Thus $\mathcal{P}(X)$ satisfies all the axioms of a topology.

**2. $\mathcal{P}(X)$ contains $\mathcal{S}$.**

By hypothesis $\mathcal{S}$ is a family of subsets of $X$, i.e. $\mathcal{S}\sub
seteq\mathcal{P}(X)$. Hence every member of $\mathcal{S}$ is open in the discret
e topology.

**3. $\mathcal{P}(X)$ is the largest such topology.**

Let $\tau$ be *any* topology on $X$ that contains $\mathcal{S}$. By definition o
f a topology, every open set is a subset of $X$, so $\tau\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)
$. Therefore $\mathcal{P}(X)$ contains every topology on $X$, and in particular
contains every topology that contains $\mathcal{S}$. Being itself a topology con
taining $\mathcal{S}$, it is the maximum element of the partially ordered set

$$\{\,\tau\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X):\tau\text{ is a topology on }X\text{ and }\mat
hcal{S}\subseteq\tau\,\}$$

ordered by inclusion.

---

### Remark (the coarsest / smallest case)

The question as literally stated has the trivial answer above. The non-trivial a
nd standard companion result is the **smallest** (coarsest) topology containing
$\mathcal{S}$, namely the topology *generated by $\mathcal{S}$ as a subbasis*:

$$\tau(\mathcal{S})\;=\;\Bigl\{\,\bigcup_{\alpha}\Bigl(\bigcap_{j=1}^{n_\alpha}
S_{\alpha,j}\Bigr)\;:\;S_{\alpha,j}\in\mathcal{S},\;n_\alpha\in\mathbb{N}\,\Bigr
\},$$

i.e. the collection of all arbitrary unions of finite intersections of members o
f $\mathcal{S}$ (with the convention that the empty intersection is $X$ and the
empty union is $\varnothing$). This is the intersection of all topologies on $X$
 containing $\mathcal{S}$, and it is the minimum element of the same poset above
. By contrast, $\mathcal{P}(X)$ is the maximum element, as proved.

---

Combining parts 1–3, the largest topology on $X$ containing $\mathcal{S}$ is the
 discrete topology $\mathcal{P}(X)$.
