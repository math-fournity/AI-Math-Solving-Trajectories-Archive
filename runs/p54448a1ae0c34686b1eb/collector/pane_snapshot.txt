## Proof

We evaluate
$$L = \lim_{k\to\infty}\int_0^k x^n\left(1-\frac{x}{k}\right)^k dx$$
for a fixed nonnegative integer $n$.

**Step 1: Substitution.** Let $x = kt$, so $dx = k\,dt$ and the limits $x:0\to k
$ become $t:0\to 1$. Then
$$L_k := \int_0^k x^n\left(1-\frac{x}{k}\right)^k dx = k^{n+1}\int_0^1 t^n(1-t)^
k\,dt.$$

**Step 2: Beta function.** The integral is the Beta function:
$$\int_0^1 t^n(1-t)^k\,dt = B(n+1,\,k+1) = \frac{\Gamma(n+1)\,\Gamma(k+1)}{\Gamm
a(k+n+2)} = \frac{n!\,k!}{(k+n+1)!},$$
since $n$ is a nonnegative integer (so $\Gamma(n+1)=n!$) and $k$ is a positive i
nteger (so $\Gamma(k+1)=k!$, $\Gamma(k+n+2)=(k+n+1)!$).

Therefore
$$L_k = k^{n+1}\cdot\frac{n!\,k!}{(k+n+1)!} = n!\cdot\frac{k^{n+1}}{(k+1)(k+2)\c
dots(k+n+1)}.$$

**Step 3: The asymptotic.** We have
$$\frac{k^{n+1}}{(k+1)(k+2)\cdots(k+n+1)} = \prod_{j=1}^{n+1}\frac{k}{k+j} = \pr
od_{j=1}^{n+1}\frac{1}{1+j/k}.$$
For each fixed $j$, $\frac{1}{1+j/k}\to 1$ as $k\to\infty$, and the product has
a fixed finite number ($n+1$) of factors. Hence
$$\lim_{k\to\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n+1}\frac{1}{1+j/k} = 1.$$

**Step 4: Conclusion.**
$$L = \lim_{k\to\infty} L_k = n!\cdot\lim_{k\to\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n+1}\frac{1}{
1+j/k} = n!\cdot 1 = n!.$$

$$\boxed{\lim_{k \to \infty} \int_0^k x^n \left(1 - \frac{x}{k} \right)^k \, dx
= n!}$$
