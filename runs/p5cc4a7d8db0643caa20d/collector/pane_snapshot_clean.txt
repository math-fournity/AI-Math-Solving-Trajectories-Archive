## Proof

**Setup.** Let the square have side length $s$. Place $A=(0,0)$, $B=(s,0)$, $D=(
0,s)$, $C=(s,s)$. Rabbit at $B$ runs along the $x$-axis toward $A$; rabbit at $D
$ runs along the $y$-axis toward $A$. Each rabbit's speed is at most $1$.

**Step 1: Rabbits' optimal strategy is full speed.**

A rabbit at position $(x,0)$ (distance $x$ from $A$) chooses speed $\alpha \in [
0,1]$. If it slows down (smaller $\alpha$), two effects occur: (i) it takes long
er to reach $A$, giving the fox more time, and (ii) at any fixed time $t$, it re
mains at a larger $x$-coordinate, closer to corner $C$ where the fox starts. Bot
h effects make it easier for the fox to intercept. Formally, the fox intercepts
rabbit $B$ at time $t$ only if $v t \ge \sqrt{(\alpha t)^2 + s^2}$, i.e., $t \ge
 \frac{s}{\sqrt{v^2 - \alpha^2}}$; this minimum interception time is decreasing
in $\alpha$, so a slower rabbit is caught sooner. Since the rabbit's goal is to
reach $A$ without being caught, running at full speed $\alpha = 1$ is strictly d
ominant. This applies to both rabbits independently.

**Step 2: Upper bound — for any $v > \sqrt{2}$, the fox can catch both.**

With both rabbits at full speed, rabbit $B$ is at $(s-t, 0)$ at time $t$, reachi
ng $A$ at time $t=s$. The fox starts at $C=(s,s)$.

*First catch (rabbit $B$):* The fox runs straight to intercept rabbit $B$ at tim
e $t_1$. The distance from $C$ to $(s-t_1, 0)$ is $\sqrt{t_1^2 + s^2}$, so we ne
ed
$$v\, t_1 \ge \sqrt{t_1^2 + s^2} \implies t_1 \ge \frac{s}{\sqrt{v^2-1}}.$$
For $v > \sqrt{2}$, we have $v^2 - 1 > 1$, so $t_1^* = \frac{s}{\sqrt{v^2-1}} <
s$. Set $t_1 = t_1^*$; the fox catches rabbit $B$ at position $(s - t_1, 0)$ bef
ore it reaches $A$.

*Second catch (rabbit $D$):* Let $a = s - t_1 > 0$ (the $x$-coordinate of the fi
rst catch point, equal to the remaining distance of rabbit $B$ to $A$). Rabbit $
D$ is at $(0, s-t)$ at time $t$. The fox must reach $(0, s-t_2)$ at time $t_2 \i
n (t_1, s)$. Set $b = s - t_2 \in (0, a)$. The distance from $(a, 0)$ to $(0, b)
$ is $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$, and the time available is $t_2 - t_1 = a - b$. We need:
$$v^2(a-b)^2 \ge a^2 + b^2.$$
Substituting $r = b/a \in (0,1)$, this becomes $(v^2-1)(1+r^2) - 2v^2 r \ge 0$,
a quadratic in $r$ with discriminant $4v^4 - 4(v^2-1)^2 = 4(2v^2-1) > 0$ (for $v
 > \sqrt{2}$). The smaller root is:
$$r_- = \frac{v^2 - \sqrt{2v^2 - 1}}{v^2 - 1}.$$
We verify $0 < r_- < 1$: $r_- > 0$ since $v^4 > 2v^2 - 1$ (equivalently $(v^2-1)
^2 > 0$, true for $v > 1$); $r_- < 1$ since $\sqrt{2v^2-1} > 1$ (true for $v > 1
$). Setting $r = r_-$, the constraint is satisfied with $b = r_- \cdot a > 0$, s
o $t_2 = s - b < s$. The fox catches rabbit $D$ at position $(0, b)$ with $b > 0
$, i.e., before it reaches $A$. ✓

**Step 3: Lower bound — for $v \le \sqrt{2}$, the rabbits escape.**

With both rabbits at full speed, the fox must intercept at least one rabbit befo
re time $s$. The earliest the fox can reach rabbit $B$ at $(s-t, 0)$ is at time
$t = \frac{s}{\sqrt{v^2-1}}$. For $v = \sqrt{2}$, this gives $t = s$, meaning th
e fox can only arrive at $A$ exactly when the rabbit does — not *before*. For $v
 < \sqrt{2}$, $v^2 - 1 < 1$, so $t > s$: the fox cannot intercept either rabbit
before it reaches $A$.

At $v = \sqrt{2}$, the fox can reach $A$ at time $s$ (distance $s\sqrt{2}$, spee
d $\sqrt{2}$, time $s$), but both rabbits also arrive at $A$ at time $s$. Catchi
ng them *at* $A$ does not satisfy "before they reach $A$." No alternative path h
elps: any path from $C$ to a point on a rabbit's trajectory has length at least
the straight-line distance, and we showed even the straight-line interception re
quires $t \ge s$.

Since the rabbits' dominant strategy (full speed) forces $v > \sqrt{2}$, and for
 every $v > \sqrt{2}$ the fox has a winning strategy, the minimum value is:

$$\boxed{v = \sqrt{2}}$$
