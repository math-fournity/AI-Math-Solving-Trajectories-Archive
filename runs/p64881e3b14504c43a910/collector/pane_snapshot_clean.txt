## Solution

We work in $\mathcal{K}$, the family of nonempty closed subsets of $[0,1]$ equip
ped with the Hausdorff metric

$$
d_H(A,B)=\max\Big\{\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}|a-b|,\ \sup_{b\in B}\inf_{a\in A}|
a-b|\Big\}.
$$

A useful equivalent form when the sets are intervals or singletons: $d_H(A,B)$ i
s the smallest $\varepsilon\ge 0$ such that $A\subseteq B_\varepsilon$ and $B\su
bseteq A_\varepsilon$, where $S_\varepsilon$ denotes the closed $\varepsilon$-ne
ighborhood of $S$.

We treat each part in turn.

---

### (a) $A_n=[0,\,1/n]$, converges to $\{0\}$

**Claim.** $A_n\to\{0\}$ in $(\mathcal{K},d_H)$.

*Proof.* Compute $d_H(A_n,\{0\})$ directly.

- For every $a\in[0,1/n]$, $\inf_{b\in\{0\}}|a-b|=a\le 1/n$, with equality at $a
=1/n$. Hence
  $$
  \sup_{a\in A_n}\inf_{b\in\{0\}}|a-b|=\frac1n.
  $$
- For $b=0\in\{0\}$, $\inf_{a\in[0,1/n]}|b-a|=0$ (since $0\in[0,1/n]$). Hence
  $$
  \sup_{b\in\{0\}}\inf_{a\in A_n}|a-b|=0.
  $$

Therefore
$$
d_H(A_n,\{0\})=\max\Big\{\frac1n,0\Big\}=\frac1n\longrightarrow 0.
$$

So $A_n\to\{0\}$. $\square$

---

### (b) $B_n=\{1/n\}$, converges to $\{0\}$

**Claim.** $B_n\to\{0\}$ in $(\mathcal{K},d_H)$.

*Proof.* Both sets are singletons, so the Hausdorff distance reduces to the ordi
nary distance between the two points:
$$
d_H(\{1/n\},\{0\})=\big|\tfrac1n-0\big|=\frac1n\longrightarrow 0.
$$

(Indeed, $\sup_{a\in\{1/n\}}\inf_{b\in\{0\}}|a-b|=1/n$ and $\sup_{b\in\{0\}}\inf
_{a\in\{1/n\}}|a-b|=1/n$, so $d_H=1/n$.)

So $B_n\to\{0\}$. $\square$

---

### (c) $C_n=[1/2-1/n,\,1/2+1/n]$ for $n\ge 2$, converges to $\{1/2\}$

**Claim.** $C_n\to\{1/2\}$ in $(\mathcal{K},d_H)$.

*Proof.* For $n\ge 2$ we have $1/2-1/n\ge 0$ and $1/2+1/n\le 1$, so $C_n\subsete
q[0,1]$ and is a nonempty closed subset, i.e. $C_n\in\mathcal{K}$.

Compute $d_H(C_n,\{1/2\})$.

- For every $a\in[1/2-1/n,\,1/2+1/n]$, the closest point of $\{1/2\}$ is $1/2$,
and $|a-1/2|\le 1/n$, with equality at the endpoints $a=1/2\pm 1/n$. Hence
  $$
  \sup_{a\in C_n}\inf_{b\in\{1/2\}}|a-b|=\frac1n.
  $$
- For $b=1/2\in\{1/2\}$, since $1/2\in C_n$, $\inf_{a\in C_n}|b-a|=0$. Hence
  $$
  \sup_{b\in\{1/2\}}\inf_{a\in C_n}|a-b|=0.
  $$

Therefore
$$
d_H(C_n,\{1/2\})=\max\Big\{\frac1n,0\Big\}=\frac1n\longrightarrow 0.
$$

So $C_n\to\{1/2\}$. $\square$

### Summary

| Sequence | Converges? | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| (a) $A_n=[0,1/n]$ | Yes | $\{0\}$ |
| (b) $B_n=\{1/n\}$ | Yes | $\{0\}$ |
| (c) $C_n=[1/2-1/n,\,1/2+1/n]$ | Yes | $\{1/2\}$ |

All three sequences converge in $(\mathcal{K},d_H)$, and the limits are as liste
d above. Note that (a) and (b) illustrate a key feature of the Hausdorff metric:
 a "shrinking interval" $[0,1/n]$ and the "approaching point" $\{1/n\}$ both con
verge to the *same* limit $\{0\}$, even though the sets $A_n$ and $B_n$ are quit
e different geometrically — the Hausdorff metric only sees how far each set is f
rom the other, not their internal structure.
