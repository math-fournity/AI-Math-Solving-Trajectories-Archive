Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $S = \sum_{h=1}^{N-1}|\hat{
A}(h)| < N|A|$ whenever $|A| \geq N/2$.

**Answer: The claim is TRUE.** Here is the proof.

---

**Proof.**

Let $f = \mathbf{1}_A$ be the indicator function of $A$ on $\mathbb{Z}_N$. Then
$\hat{A}(h) = \hat{f}(h)$ is the discrete Fourier transform of $f$.

**Step 1 (Parseval).** By the Parseval/Plancherel identity for the DFT on $\math
bb{Z}_N$:

$$\sum_{h=0}^{N-1} |\hat{A}(h)|^2 = N \sum_{x \in \mathbb{Z}_N} |f(x)|^2 = N|A|.
$$

Since $\hat{A}(0) = \sum_{a \in A} e_N(0) = |A|$, we obtain

$$\sum_{h=1}^{N-1} |\hat{A}(h)|^2 = N|A| - |A|^2 = |A|(N - |A|). \tag{1}$$

**Step 2 (Cauchy–Schwarz).** Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to the sum $
S = \sum_{h=1}^{N-1} |\hat{A}(h)|$ over $N-1$ terms:

$$S^2 = \left(\sum_{h=1}^{N-1} |\hat{A}(h)|\right)^2 \leq (N-1)\sum_{h=1}^{N-1}
|\hat{A}(h)|^2 = (N-1)\,|A|\,(N - |A|), \tag{2}$$

where the last equality uses (1).

**Step 3 (Comparison with $N|A|$).** It suffices to show

$$(N-1)\,|A|\,(N-|A|) < N^2 |A|^2. \tag{3}$$

Since $|A| \geq 1$ (as $|A| \geq N/2 \geq 1$ for $N \geq 2$; the case $N=1$ is v
acuous since the sum is empty and $S=0 < |A| = 1$), we may divide both sides by
$|A| > 0$. Inequality (3) is equivalent to

$$(N-1)(N - |A|) < N^2 |A|. \tag{4}$$

Rearranging:

$$N^2|A| - (N-1)(N-|A|) = N^2|A| - (N-1)N + (N-1)|A| = |A|(N^2 + N - 1) - N(N-1)
.$$

So (4) is equivalent to

$$|A|(N^2 + N - 1) > N(N-1), \quad\text{i.e.,}\quad |A| > \frac{N(N-1)}{N^2 + N
- 1}. \tag{5}$$

**Step 4 (Verifying the threshold).** We claim that when $|A| \geq N/2$, conditi
on (5) is satisfied. It suffices to show

$$\frac{N}{2} \geq \frac{N(N-1)}{N^2+N-1},$$

i.e., (dividing by $N > 0$)

$$\frac{1}{2} \geq \frac{N-1}{N^2+N-1}, \quad\text{i.e.,}\quad N^2 + N - 1 \geq
2(N-1) = 2N - 2,$$

i.e., $N^2 - N + 1 \geq 0$, which holds for all integers $N \geq 1$ (the discrim
inant of $N^2 - N + 1$ is $1 - 4 = -3 < 0$, so it is always positive).

Moreover, the inequality is **strict**: $N^2 - N + 1 > 0$ for all $N$, so $\frac
{N}{2} > \frac{N(N-1)}{N^2+N-1}$, and thus $|A| \geq N/2$ implies (5) holds stri
ctly.

**Step 5 (Conclusion).** Combining Steps 2–4:

$$S^2 \leq (N-1)\,|A|\,(N-|A|) < N^2|A|^2.$$

Taking square roots (all quantities are non-negative):

$$\boxed{S < N|A|.}$$

This holds for every $A \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_N$ with $|A| \geq N/2$ (and $N \geq
 2$; for $N=1$ the sum is empty so $S = 0 < |A| = N|A|$ trivially). $\blacksquar
e$

---

**Remark on tightness.** The Cauchy–Schwarz bound $\sqrt{(N-1)|A|(N-|A|)}$ is ac
hieved when all $|\hat{A}(h)|$ for $h = 1, \dots, N-1$ are equal, which occurs f
or certain difference-set-like constructions. Even in that extremal case, the bo
und remains strictly below $N|A|$ by the margin established in Step 4, so the in
equality $S < N|A|$ is never violated.
