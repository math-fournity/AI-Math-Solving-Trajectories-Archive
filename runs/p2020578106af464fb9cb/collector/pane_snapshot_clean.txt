**Answer: Yes. Every uncountable subset $S\subseteq[0,1]$ has an accumulation po
int that belongs to $S$.**

**Proof.**

Recall that $x$ is an *accumulation point* of $S$ if every open neighborhood of
$x$ contains a point of $S\setminus\{x\}$, and that a point $s\in S$ is *isolate
d* (in $S$) precisely when $s$ is **not** an accumulation point of $S$.

Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that $S$ has **no** accumulation point be
longing to $S$. Then every point of $S$ is isolated in $S$: for each $s\in S$ th
ere exists an open interval $U_s\subseteq\mathbb{R}$ with
$$U_s\cap S=\{s\}.$$

Within each $U_s$ pick an open interval $I_s$ with **rational endpoints** such t
hat $s\in I_s\subseteq U_s$. (Such an interval exists because $\mathbb{Q}$ is de
nse in $\mathbb{R}$.) The collection of all open intervals with rational endpoin
ts is countable.

We claim the assignment $s\mapsto I_s$ is injective. Indeed, suppose $s\neq t$ i
n $S$ but $I_s=I_t=I$. Then $I\subseteq U_s$, so
$$I\cap S\subseteq U_s\cap S=\{s\},$$
yet $t\in I\cap S$ with $t\neq s$ — a contradiction. Hence $s\mapsto I_s$ is inj
ective.

This embeds the uncountable set $S$ into the countable set of rational-endpoint
intervals, which is impossible. The contradiction forces us to reject the assump
tion.

Therefore $S$ must possess at least one accumulation point that lies in $S$. $\b
lacksquare$
