Let
\[
S_k=\sum_{j=0}^{k}2^{\,k-j}\binom{k+j}{j}.
\]

We prove by induction that $S_k=4^k$ for every nonnegative integer $k$.

**Base case.** $S_0=2^0\binom{0}{0}=1=4^0$.

**Inductive step.** Assume $S_k=4^k$. We establish the recurrence $S_{k+1}=4S_k$
.

Using Pascal's identity $\binom{k+1+j}{j}=\binom{k+j}{j}+\binom{k+j}{j-1}$ (vali
d for $j\ge 1$, with the $j=0$ term equal to $1$), we split
\[
S_{k+1}=\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}2^{k+1-j}\binom{k+1+j}{j}
=2^{k+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}2^{k+1-j}\binom{k+j}{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}2^{k+1-j}\binom
{k+j}{j-1}.
\]

*First sum.* Separating the $j=k+1$ term and comparing with $S_k$:
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}2^{k+1-j}\binom{k+j}{j}
=2\sum_{j=1}^{k}2^{k-j}\binom{k+j}{j}+\binom{2k+1}{k+1}
=2\bigl(S_k-2^{k}\bigr)+\binom{2k+1}{k+1}
=2S_k-2^{k+1}+\binom{2k+1}{k+1}.
\]

*Second sum.* Substituting $i=j-1$ (so $i$ runs from $0$ to $k$):
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}2^{k+1-j}\binom{k+j}{j-1}
=\sum_{i=0}^{k}2^{k-i}\binom{k+1+i}{i}
=\frac12\sum_{i=0}^{k}2^{k+1-i}\binom{k+1+i}{i}
=\frac12\Bigl(S_{k+1}-\binom{2k+2}{k+1}\Bigr),
\]
since the full sum $\sum_{i=0}^{k+1}2^{k+1-i}\binom{k+1+i}{i}=S_{k+1}$ and the m
issing $i=k+1$ term is $2^0\binom{2k+2}{k+1}=\binom{2k+2}{k+1}$.

Combining the three pieces,
\[
S_{k+1}=2^{k+1}+\bigl(2S_k-2^{k+1}+\tbinom{2k+1}{k+1}\bigr)+\tfrac12\bigl(S_{k+1
}-\tbinom{2k+2}{k+1}\bigr).
\]
The $2^{k+1}$ terms cancel, giving
\[
S_{k+1}=2S_k+\binom{2k+1}{k+1}+\frac12 S_{k+1}-\frac12\binom{2k+2}{k+1}.
\]
Now
\[
\binom{2k+2}{k+1}=\frac{2k+2}{k+1}\binom{2k+1}{k+1}=2\binom{2k+1}{k+1},
\]
so the binomial terms cancel:
\[
\binom{2k+1}{k+1}-\frac12\binom{2k+2}{k+1}=\binom{2k+1}{k+1}-\binom{2k+1}{k+1}=0
.
\]
Thus
\[
\frac12 S_{k+1}=2S_k\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad S_{k+1}=4S_k.
\]

By the induction hypothesis $S_k=4^k$, so $S_{k+1}=4\cdot 4^k=4^{k+1}$.

**Conclusion.** By induction,
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}2^{\,k-j}\binom{k+j}{j}=4^k=2^{2k}.}
\]
