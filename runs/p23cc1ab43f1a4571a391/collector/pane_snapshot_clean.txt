## Solution

**Identify singularities of $f(z) = \frac{1}{z\cos z}$ inside $|z|=4$.**

The singularities occur at:
- $z = 0$ (from the factor $z$ in the denominator),
- $z = \frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2}$, the zeros of $\cos z$.

Checking which zeros of $\cos z$ lie inside $|z|<4$:

| $n$ | $z$ | $|z|$ | Inside? |
|-----|-----|-------|---------|
| $0$ | $\pi/2 \approx 1.571$ | $1.571$ | ✓ |
| $-1$ | $-\pi/2 \approx -1.571$ | $1.571$ | ✓ |
| $1$ | $3\pi/2 \approx 4.712$ | $4.712$ | ✗ |
| $-2$ | $-3\pi/2 \approx -4.712$ | $4.712$ | ✗ |

So the singularities inside $|z|=4$ are $z = 0,\; \pi/2,\; -\pi/2$, all **simple
 poles**.

**Compute residues.**

**At $z=0$:** Since $\cos(0)=1\neq 0$, this is a simple pole with
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{1}{z\cos z} = \frac{1}{\cos 0} = 1.$$

**At $z=a$ where $\cos a = 0$ (simple zero):** Using $\frac{d}{dz}\cos z = -\sin
 z$,
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=a}\frac{1}{z\cos z} = \frac{1}{a\cdot(-\sin a)}.$$

- At $z=\pi/2$: $\sin(\pi/2)=1$, so $\displaystyle\operatorname{Res} = \frac{1}{
(\pi/2)(-1)} = -\frac{2}{\pi}.$

- At $z=-\pi/2$: $\sin(-\pi/2)=-1$, so $\displaystyle\operatorname{Res} = \frac{
1}{(-\pi/2)\cdot(-(-1))} = \frac{1}{(-\pi/2)(1)} = -\frac{2}{\pi}.$

**Sum of residues:**
$$\sum \operatorname{Res} = 1 - \frac{2}{\pi} - \frac{2}{\pi} = 1 - \frac{4}{\pi
} = \frac{\pi - 4}{\pi}.$$

**Apply the Residue Theorem.**
$$\oint_{|z|=4} \frac{1}{z\cos z}\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot \frac{\pi-4}{\pi} = \frac{2
\pi i(\pi-4)}{\pi}.$$

**Compute $K$:**
$$K = \frac{i}{4-\pi}\cdot \frac{2\pi i(\pi-4)}{\pi}.$$

Since $\pi - 4 = -(4-\pi)$:
$$K = \frac{i}{4-\pi}\cdot \frac{2\pi i\cdot(-(4-\pi))}{\pi} = \frac{i\cdot(-2\p
i i)(4-\pi)}{(4-\pi)\pi} = \frac{-2\pi i^2}{\pi} = \frac{-2\pi(-1)}{\pi} = \frac
{2\pi}{\pi} = \boxed{2}.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
