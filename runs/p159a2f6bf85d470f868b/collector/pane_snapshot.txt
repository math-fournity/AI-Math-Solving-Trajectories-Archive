## Proof

**Setup.** Let $K$ be the triangulated circle with three vertices $v_0, v_1, v_2
$ and three oriented edges
$$e_0 = [v_0, v_1],\quad e_1 = [v_1, v_2],\quad e_2 = [v_2, v_0].$$
The simplicial chain complex is
$$C_1 = \mathbb{Z}\langle e_0, e_1, e_2\rangle \xrightarrow{\;\partial_1\;} C_0
= \mathbb{Z}\langle v_0, v_1, v_2\rangle,$$
with $\partial_1(e_0)=v_1-v_0$, $\partial_1(e_1)=v_2-v_1$, $\partial_1(e_2)=v_0-
v_2$. The cycle $z = e_0+e_1+e_2$ generates $H_1(K)\cong\mathbb{Z}$.

Since every pair of distinct vertices spans an edge ($K_3$ is the complete graph
), any vertex map $\{v_0,v_1,v_2\}\to\{v_0,v_1,v_2\}$ is a valid simplicial map.

**The map.** Define the simplicial map $f:K\to K$ by the cyclic permutation
$$f(v_0)=v_1,\quad f(v_1)=v_2,\quad f(v_2)=v_0.$$

**Induced chain map.** On $C_1$:
$$f_\#(e_0)=[f(v_0),f(v_1)]=[v_1,v_2]=e_1,$$
$$f_\#(e_1)=[f(v_1),f(v_2)]=[v_2,v_0]=e_2,$$
$$f_\#(e_2)=[f(v_2),f(v_0)]=[v_0,v_1]=e_0.$$

The matrix of $f_\#$ on $C_1$ (basis $e_0,e_1,e_2$) is the 3-cycle permutation m
atrix
$$\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},$$
which has **trace $0$**.

On $C_0$: $f_\#(v_0)=v_1$, $f_\#(v_1)=v_2$, $f_\#(v_2)=v_0$, giving the same 3-c
ycle permutation matrix with **trace $0$**.

The identity chain map has trace $3$ on both $C_1$ and $C_0$. Thus the traces di
ffer.

**Chain map property verified.** For each generator:
- $\partial_1(f_\#(e_0))=\partial_1(e_1)=v_2-v_1=f_\#(v_1-v_0)=f_\#(\partial_1 e
_0)$. ✓
- $\partial_1(f_\#(e_1))=\partial_1(e_2)=v_0-v_2=f_\#(v_2-v_1)=f_\#(\partial_1 e
_1)$. ✓
- $\partial_1(f_\#(e_2))=\partial_1(e_0)=v_1-v_0=f_\#(v_0-v_2)=f_\#(\partial_1 e
_2)$. ✓

**$f$ is homotopic to the identity.** The induced map on homology:
$$f_\#(z)=f_\#(e_0+e_1+e_2)=e_1+e_2+e_0=z.$$
So $f_*:H_1(S^1)\to H_1(S^1)$ is the identity, i.e., $f$ has **degree $1$**. Sin
ce homotopy classes of maps $S^1\to S^1$ are completely classified by degree, $f
$ is homotopic to the identity.

(Geometrically, $f$ is a $120°$ rotation of the triangle, which is clearly homot
opic to the identity on the circle — just rotate back continuously.)

**Consistency check via Lefschetz number.** The Lefschetz number of the identity
 is $\chi(S^1)=0$. For $f$:
$$L(f)=\operatorname{tr}(f_\#|_{C_0})-\operatorname{tr}(f_\#|_{C_1})=0-0=0.$$
Both give $0$, confirming the Lefschetz number is homotopy-invariant — while the
 individual traces on each chain group are not.

**Conclusion.** The cyclic permutation $f(v_0)=v_1, f(v_1)=v_2, f(v_2)=v_0$ is a
 simplicial map on the 3-vertex triangulated circle that is homotopic to the ide
ntity (degree 1), yet its induced chain map has trace $0$ on both $C_0$ and $C_1
$, differing from the identity chain map's trace of $3$.
