**Problem.** Find all $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ satisfying $f(xf(x)+2y)=f(x^2)

Setting $y=0$: $f(xf(x))=f(x^2)+c+x-1$, i.e.,
$$h(xf(x))=h(x^2)+d-xh(x). \tag{2}$$

$$h(xf(x)+2y)=h(x^2)+h(y)-xh(x). \tag{$*$}$$
Replacing $y$ by $(t-xf(x))/2$ and using $h(v/2)=h(v)-d$ (from (1)):
$$h(t)-h(t-xf(x))=h(x^2)-d-xh(x). \tag{$****$}$$
Setting $t=xf(x)$: $h(xf(x))-d=h(x^2)-d-xh(x)$, so $h(xf(x))=h(x^2)-xh(x)$. Comp

$$h(2y)=h(y). \tag{3}$$

$$h(xf(x)+y)=h(x^2)+h(y)-xh(x)=h(xf(x))+h(y), \tag{$**$}$$
using $h(xf(x))=h(x^2)-xh(x)$. So for every $s\in S$: $h(s+y)=h(s)+h(y)$ for all

$$h(x^2)=x\,h(x) \quad\text{for all }x. \tag{$\diamond$}$$

verified satisfies the equation. This is the unique solution.

$$\boxed{f(x)=x+1}$$
