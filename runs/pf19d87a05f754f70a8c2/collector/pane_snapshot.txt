## Proof

Let $E \subset [0,1]$ with Lebesgue measure $|E| = 0$, and let $f(x) = x^3$.

**Key observation.** The function $f(x) = x^3$ is continuously differentiable on
 $[0,1]$, with
$$f'(x) = 3x^2 \leq 3 \quad \text{for all } x \in [0,1].$$
By the Mean Value Theorem, for any $x, y \in [0,1]$ there exists $c$ between $x$
 and $y$ with
$$|f(x) - f(y)| = |f'(c)|\,|x - y| \leq 3\,|x - y|.$$
Thus $f$ is **Lipschitz** on $[0,1]$ with constant $L = 3$.

**Covering argument.** Let $\varepsilon > 0$. Since $|E| = 0$, there exists a co
untable collection of open intervals $\{I_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ covering $E$ such
that
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \ell(I_n) < \varepsilon,$$
where $\ell(I_n)$ denotes the length of $I_n$. Write $I_n = (a_n, b_n)$.

Since $f$ is Lipschitz with constant $3$,
$$f(I_n) \subset \bigl(f(a_n), f(b_n)\bigr) \quad \text{(if } a_n < b_n \text{)}
,$$
and the length of the image interval satisfies
$$\ell\bigl(f(I_n)\bigr) \leq |f(b_n) - f(a_n)| \leq 3\,|b_n - a_n| = 3\,\ell(I_
n).$$
(If $I_n$ extends outside $[0,1]$, we intersect with $[0,1]$ first; the Lipschit
z bound still holds on the intersection.)

Therefore $f(E) \subset \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} f(I_n)$, and
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \ell\bigl(f(I_n)\bigr) \leq 3 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \ell(I_n
) < 3\varepsilon.$$

Since $\varepsilon > 0$ was arbitrary, the outer measure of $f(E)$ satisfies
$$|f(E)| \leq 3\varepsilon \quad \text{for every } \varepsilon > 0,$$
hence $|f(E)| = 0$. $\blacksquare$
