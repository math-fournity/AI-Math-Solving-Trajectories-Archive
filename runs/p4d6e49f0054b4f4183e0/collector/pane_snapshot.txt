## Answer

**Yes.** A $C_0$ semigroup can be strongly continuous at $0$ without being unifo
rmly continuous at $0$. Below is a concrete example and justification.

---

### Setup

Let $X = L^2(\mathbb{R})$ and define, for each $t \geq 0$, the **right-translati
on operator**

$$(T(t)f)(s) = f(s - t), \qquad f \in L^2(\mathbb{R}).$$

Each $T(t)$ is a bounded linear operator with $\|T(t)\| = 1$ (translation preser
ves the $L^2$ norm), and the family $\{T(t)\}_{t \geq 0}$ satisfies the semigrou
p laws $T(0) = I$ and $T(t+s) = T(t)T(s)$.

### Strong continuity at $0$

We show $\lim_{t \to 0^+} \|T(t)f - f\|_2 = 0$ for every $f \in L^2(\mathbb{R})$
.

First take $f \in C_c(\mathbb{R})$, continuous with compact support. Then $f$ is
 uniformly continuous, so $|f(s-t) - f(s)| \to 0$ uniformly as $t \to 0^+$, and
the supports of $T(t)f$ and $f$ are both contained in a common compact set for s
mall $t$. By dominated convergence,

$$\|T(t)f - f\|_2^2 = \int_{\mathbb{R}} |f(s-t) - f(s)|^2\, ds \longrightarrow 0
.$$

Since $C_c(\mathbb{R})$ is dense in $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ and $\|T(t)\| = 1$ uniform
ly, a standard $\varepsilon/3$ argument extends the convergence to all $f \in L^
2(\mathbb{R})$. Hence $\{T(t)\}$ is a $C_0$ semigroup.

### Failure of uniform continuity at $0$

Uniform continuity would require $\lim_{t \to 0^+} \|T(t) - I\| = 0$. We show th
is fails: in fact $\|T(t) - I\| \geq \sqrt{2}$ for **every** $t > 0$.

Fix $t > 0$ and set $f = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\,\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}$, so $\|f\|_2 =
 1$. Then

$$T(t)f = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\,\mathbf{1}_{[t,\,2t]}.$$

The intervals $[0,t]$ and $[t,2t]$ overlap only on a set of Lebesgue measure $0$
, so $f$ and $T(t)f$ are orthogonal in $L^2(\mathbb{R})$. Therefore

$$\|T(t)f - f\|_2^2 = \|T(t)f\|_2^2 + \|f\|_2^2 = 1 + 1 = 2,$$

giving $\|T(t)f - f\|_2 = \sqrt{2}$. Since $\|f\|_2 = 1$,

$$\|T(t) - I\| = \sup_{\|g\|_2 = 1} \|T(t)g - g\|_2 \geq \sqrt{2}, \qquad \foral
l\, t > 0.$$

Thus $\|T(t) - I\| \not\to 0$ as $t \to 0^+$, and the semigroup is **not** unifo
rmly continuous at $0$.

### Conclusion

The right-translation semigroup on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ is strongly continuous at $
0$ (it is a $C_0$ semigroup) but is not uniformly continuous at $0$, since $\|T(
t) - I\| \geq \sqrt{2} > 0$ for all $t > 0$. This shows that strong continuity a
t $0$ does **not** imply uniform continuity at $0$ for $C_0$ semigroups.
