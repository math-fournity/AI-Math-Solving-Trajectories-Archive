## Statement of the Theorem

Let $O = \mathrm{colim}_{n \to \infty}\, O(n)$ denote the **infinite orthogonal
group** (the direct limit of the orthogonal groups under the standard inclusions
 $O(n) \hookrightarrow O(n+1)$), equipped with the direct-limit (weak) topology.
 Let $BO$ be its classifying space.

**Bott Periodicity Theorem (real form).** *There is a homotopy equivalence*
$$\Omega^{8}(\mathbb{Z} \times BO)\;\simeq\;\mathbb{Z} \times BO,$$
*where $\Omega$ denotes the (based) loop-space functor.*

Since $BO$ is the classifying space of the topological group $O$, we have $\Omeg
a\, BO \simeq O$. Applying this repeatedly:

$$\Omega(\mathbb{Z}\times BO)\simeq \Omega BO \simeq O,\qquad \Omega^{2}(\mathbb
{Z}\times BO)\simeq \Omega O,\quad \ldots,\quad \Omega^{8}(\mathbb{Z}\times BO)\
simeq \Omega^{7} O.$$

The theorem then gives $\Omega^{7} O \simeq \mathbb{Z}\times BO$, and looping on
ce more:

$$\boxed{\;\Omega^{8} O \;\simeq\; O\;}$$

(up to the component structure; the identity component of $O$, namely $SO$, sati
sfies $\Omega^{8} SO \simeq SO$). Passing to homotopy groups, this yields **8-fo
ld periodicity**:

$$\pi_{k}(O)\;\cong\;\pi_{k+8}(O),\qquad k\geq 0.$$

## The Periodic Table

Combining periodicity with the computation of the first eight groups, we obtain:

| $k \bmod 8$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ | $5$ | $6$ | $7$ |
|:-----------:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| $\pi_k(O)$  | $\mathbb{Z}/2$ | $\mathbb{Z}/2$ | $0$ | $\mathbb{Z}$ | $0$ | $0$
 | $0$ | $\mathbb{Z}$ |

Explicitly, for $k \in \mathbb{Z}_+$:

$$\pi_k(O)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}/2 & k\equiv 0,1\pmod 8,\\[2pt] \mathbb{Z} & k
\equiv 3,7\pmod 8,\\[2pt] 0 & k\equiv 2,4,5,6\pmod 8.\end{cases}$$

## Explanation of the Individual Groups

### $\pi_0(O) = \mathbb{Z}/2$

The orthogonal group $O(n)$ has two connected components, distinguished by the d
eterminant: $\det = +1$ (the component $SO(n)$) and $\det = -1$. This persists i
n the direct limit, so $\pi_0(O)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2$.

### $\pi_1(O) = \mathbb{Z}/2$

For $n\geq 3$, the spin group $\mathrm{Spin}(n)\to SO(n)$ is the universal (doub
le) cover, so $\pi_1(SO(n))\cong\mathbb{Z}/2$. The stabilization map $SO(3)\to S
O(\infty)$ induces an isomorphism on $\pi_1$ (this follows from the fibration $S
O(n)\to SO(n+1)\to S^{n}$ and the long exact sequence, using $\pi_1(S^n)=0$ and
$\pi_2(S^n)=0$ for $n\geq 3$). Hence $\pi_1(O)=\pi_1(SO)=\mathbb{Z}/2$.

### $\pi_2(O) = 0$

A classical theorem of É. Cartan asserts that $\pi_2(G)=0$ for every Lie group $
G$. (One proof: for any topological group $G$, the classifying space $BG$ satisf
ies $\pi_{k+1}(BG)\cong\pi_k(G)$ via the contractible total space $EG$; since $H
_2(G;\mathbb{Z})=0$ for Lie groups by Cartan's computation, and $\pi_2$ of an $H
$-space is torsion-free, one gets $\pi_2(G)=0$.) Thus $\pi_2(O)=0$.

### $\pi_3(O) = \mathbb{Z}$

For $n\geq 5$, $\pi_3(SO(n))\cong\mathbb{Z}$. This can be seen inductively from
the fibration
$$SO(n)\longrightarrow SO(n+1)\longrightarrow S^{n}$$
and its long exact homotopy sequence: for $n\geq 5$, $\pi_4(S^n)=0$ and $\pi_3(S
^n)=0$, so the map $\pi_3(SO(n))\to\pi_3(SO(n+1))$ is an isomorphism. The base c
ase $\pi_3(SO(5))\cong\mathbb{Z}$ follows from $\mathrm{Spin}(5)\cong \mathrm{Sp
}(2)$ (a compact symplectic group, which has $\pi_3=\mathbb{Z}$ by the general f
act $\pi_3$ of any compact simple Lie group is $\mathbb{Z}$, generated by the Dy
nkin index). Stabilizing gives $\pi_3(O)=\mathbb{Z}$.

### $\pi_4(O)=\pi_5(O)=\pi_6(O)=0$ and $\pi_7(O)=\mathbb{Z}$

These three vanishing results and the $\mathbb{Z}$ in degree 7 are the **non-tri
vial content** of Bott's theorem—they cannot be obtained from elementary coverin
g-space or fibration arguments alone. They are established by Bott's **Morse the
ory on loop spaces of symmetric spaces**.

**Sketch of the method.** Bott considered the eight-step chain of homogeneous sp
aces (the *Bott chain*):

$$O,\quad O/U,\quad U/\mathrm{Sp},\quad B\mathrm{Sp},\quad \mathrm{Sp},\quad \ma
thrm{Sp}/U,\quad U/O,\quad BO,\quad (\mathbb{Z}\times BO),$$

where $U=\mathrm{colim}\,U(n)$ is the infinite unitary group and $\mathrm{Sp}=\m
athrm{colim}\,\mathrm{Sp}(n)$ the infinite symplectic group. Each space in the c
hain is (up to a $\mathbb{Z}$-factor) the loop space of the next:

$$\Omega(O/U)\simeq O,\quad \Omega(U/\mathrm{Sp})\simeq O/U,\quad \ldots,\quad \
Omega(\mathbb{Z}\times BO)\simeq O.$$

Bott analyzed each homogeneous space $G/K$ via Morse theory on its loop space $\
Omega(G/K)$. The key observation is that the **index** of the geodesic flow (the
 Morse index) on these symmetric spaces grows in arithmetic progressions whose c
ommon differences are:

| Space $X$ | Index jump |
|:---------:|:----------:|
| $O/U$ | $1,5$ |
| $U/\mathrm{Sp}$ | $2,6$ |
| $\mathrm{Sp}$ | $1,5$ |
| $\mathrm{Sp}/U$ | $2,6$ |
| $U/O$ | $1,3,5,7$ |
| $O$ | $1,3,5,7$ |

(These are the dimensions in which cells appear.) Because the Morse function has
 only **even-index** or **odd-index** critical points in each case (after approp
riate parity adjustment), the boundary maps in the resulting CW-decomposition va
nish, and the loop space $\Omega X$ is a product of Eilenberg–MacLane spaces. Re
ading off the homotopy groups from these product decompositions yields:

- $\Omega O \simeq O/U$ has cells only in dimensions $\equiv 1,5\pmod 8$ relativ
e to the base, contributing $\mathbb{Z}$'s in those degrees and nothing else—thi
s forces $\pi_4, \pi_5, \pi_6$ of $O$ (which are $\pi_5,\pi_6,\pi_7$ of $O/U$, r
ead from the cell structure) to vanish, and $\pi_7(O)=\mathbb{Z}$.

More concretely, the cell decomposition of $O$ itself (as a CW-complex) has cell
s **only in dimensions $0,1,3,7$** (and their translates by $8$). This is the ce
lebrated result:

> *The infinite orthogonal group $O$ has a CW-structure with one cell in each di
mension $\equiv 0, 1, 3, 7 \pmod{8}$ and no cells in other dimensions.*

Since the cells in dimensions $0$ and $1$ account for $\pi_0=\mathbb{Z}/2$ and $
\pi_1=\mathbb{Z}/2$ (the $0$-cell and $1$-cell form the bottom $\mathbb{R}P^1\su
bset O$), and the $3$-cell and $7$-cell each contribute a free $\mathbb{Z}$ (wit
h no attaching maps creating relations, by the Morse-theoretic parity argument),
 we read off:

$$\pi_3(O)=\mathbb{Z},\qquad \pi_7(O)=\mathbb{Z},\qquad \pi_4=\pi_5=\pi_6=0,$$

and periodicity $\pi_k\cong\pi_{k+8}$ then fills in the rest of the table.

## Connection to $KO$-Theory

The space $\mathbb{Z}\times BO$ is the classifying space for **real $K$-theory**
 (reduced $KO$-theory): $KO^0(X)=[X,\mathbb{Z}\times BO]$ and $\widetilde{KO}^{-
k}(X)=[X,\Omega^k(\mathbb{Z}\times BO)]$. Bott periodicity $\Omega^8(\mathbb{Z}\
times BO)\simeq\mathbb{Z}\times BO$ is therefore equivalent to the **8-fold peri
odicity of $KO$-theory**:

$$KO^{n}(X)\cong KO^{n+8}(X).$$

Evaluating at a point $X=\mathrm{pt}$ recovers the homotopy groups of $O$ via $\
pi_k(O)=\widetilde{KO}^{-k}(\mathrm{pt})=KO^{-k}(\mathrm{pt})$, giving the table
 above. In particular:

$$KO^{-k}(\mathrm{pt})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}&k\equiv 0,4\pmod 8,\\ \mathbb{Z}/
2&k\equiv 1,2\pmod 8,\\ 0&k\equiv 3,5,6,7\pmod 8,\end{cases}$$

which is the same table (shifted by the loop-space relation $\pi_k(O)=KO^{-k-1}(
\mathrm{pt})$... up to the standard index convention).

## Summary

**Bott periodicity** states that the infinite orthogonal group $O$ satisfies $\O
mega^8 O\simeq O$, inducing an 8-fold periodicity $\pi_k(O)\cong\pi_{k+8}(O)$. T
he homotopy groups, for $k\in\mathbb{Z}_+$, are:

$$\pi_k(O)\;=\;\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}/2 & k\equiv 0,1\pmod{8},\\ \mathbb{Z} & k
\equiv 3,7\pmod{8},\\ 0 & k\equiv 2,4,5,6\pmod{8}.\end{cases}$$

The groups $\pi_0,\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3$ are obtained by classical Lie-group methods
 (component structure, the Spin double cover, Cartan's theorem $\pi_2=0$, and th
e fibration $SO(n)\to SO(n+1)\to S^n$). The remaining groups $\pi_4=\pi_5=\pi_6=
0$ and $\pi_7=\mathbb{Z}$, together with the periodicity itself, constitute the
deep content of Bott's theorem, proved via Morse theory on loop spaces of symmet
ric spaces, which shows that $O$ admits a CW-structure with cells only in dimens
ions $\equiv 0,1,3,7\pmod 8$.
