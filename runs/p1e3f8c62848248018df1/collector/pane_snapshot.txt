## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** If $\dim(S^{-1}R)=0$ for every multiplicative subset $S\subsete
q R$, then $\dim R=0$.

**Key fact.** For any prime ideal $\mathfrak p\subset R$, the localization $R_{\
mathfrak p}=(R\setminus\mathfrak p)^{-1}R$ satisfies
$$\dim(R_{\mathfrak p})=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p),$$
where $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)=\sup\{n:\mathfrak p_0\subsetneq\mathfrak p
_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak p_n=\mathfrak p\}$ is the height of $\math
frak p$.

*Justification.* The prime ideals of $R_{\mathfrak p}$ are in order-preserving b
ijection with the prime ideals $\mathfrak q$ of $R$ satisfying $\mathfrak q\subs
eteq\mathfrak p$ (via $\mathfrak q\mapsto \mathfrak q R_{\mathfrak p}$). Chains
of primes in $R_{\mathfrak p}$ therefore correspond exactly to chains of primes
in $R$ terminating at $\mathfrak p$. Hence the supremum of the lengths of such c
hains equals $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)$.

**Main argument.** Let $\mathfrak p$ be any prime ideal of $R$. The set $S=R\set
minus\mathfrak p$ is multiplicative (since $\mathfrak p$ is prime), so $S^{-1}R=
R_{\mathfrak p}$ is among the localizations covered by the hypothesis. Thus
$$\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)=\dim(R_{\mathfrak p})=0.$$

This holds for **every** prime $\mathfrak p$. In particular, there is no prime $
\mathfrak q$ with $\mathfrak q\subsetneq\mathfrak p$ for any $\mathfrak p$: if s
uch a strict inclusion existed, we would have $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)\ge
q 1$, contradicting $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)=0$.

Therefore there are **no strict inclusions at all** among prime ideals of $R$. E
quivalently, every prime ideal is simultaneously minimal and maximal. By definit
ion, $\dim R=0$.

**Remark.** Only the localizations at primes $R_{\mathfrak p}$ were needed; the
full strength of the hypothesis (all multiplicative subsets) is more than suffic
ient. The converse also holds: if $\dim R=0$, then every prime is maximal, so ev
ery localization $S^{-1}R$ (whose primes correspond to primes of $R$ disjoint fr
om $S$) also has all primes maximal, giving $\dim(S^{-1}R)=0$. Thus the conditio
n is in fact equivalent to $\dim R=0$.
