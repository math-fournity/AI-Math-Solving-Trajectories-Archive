## Proof

**Setup.** Model the table with m rows and n columns (row 1 at top). Each bacter
ium has a horizontal body (length ≥ 1) with vertical feet hanging below it, each
 foot having length ≥ 0.

**Key observation.** In each column, the topmost cell (row 1) must be a body cel
l — a foot can only hang below a body, and no body can exist above row 1. Theref
ore **every cell in row 1 is a body cell**.

**Structure of a valid tiling.** Define *body cells* as cells occupied by horizo
ntal bodies; all other cells are *foot cells*, each belonging to the body direct
ly above it in the same column. Since row 1 is entirely body cells, every cell b
elow has a body above it, ensuring complete coverage with no gaps or overlaps.

In each column, body cells appear at rows 1 = r₁ < r₂ < ⋯ < rₖ, with foot cells
filling the gaps between them (and below rₖ). Foot lengths are determined by the
 gaps and are automatically nonnegative. No additional vertical constraints aris
e.

**Independence across rows.** The choice of body cells and their grouping into h
orizontal segments (bodies) in each row is independent across rows, subject only
 to row 1 being entirely body cells. The vertical foot structure is then fully d
etermined and always valid.

**Row 1.** All n cells are body cells, partitioned into horizontal segments form
ing a composition of n. The number of compositions of n is **2^(n−1)**.

**Rows 2 through m.** In each such row, we choose a collection of disjoint horiz
ontal segments in [n], each of length ≥ 1 (possibly empty — no bodies in that ro
w). We count these configurations via a transfer matrix.

**Transfer matrix for a single row.** Process positions 1,…,n left to right. At
each position, the state is:
- **0**: not a body cell.
- **1**: body cell, start of a new segment.
- **2**: body cell, continuing the previous segment.

The transition matrix is:

$$T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

(From state 0: can go to 0 or 1, not 2. From states 1 or 2: can go to 0, 1, or 2
.)

The initial vector (position 1) is (1, 1, 0)ᵀ — position 1 can be non-body or a
segment start, but cannot continue a nonexistent previous segment. The count for
 a row of length n is:

$$a_n = (1,\, 1,\, 0)\; T^{n-1}\; (1,\, 1,\, 1)^T$$

Computing: a₁ = 2, a₂ = 5, a₃ = 13, a₄ = 34.

**Closed form.** The characteristic polynomial of T is λ(λ² − 3λ + 1) = 0, so aₙ
 satisfies:

$$a_{n+1} = 3\,a_n - a_{n-1}, \quad a_1 = 2,\; a_2 = 5.$$

The Fibonacci numbers Fₖ (with F₁ = F₂ = 1) satisfy Fₖ₊₂ = 3Fₖ − Fₖ₋₂ (derived f
rom two applications of the Fibonacci recurrence). Setting k = 2n+1 gives F₂ₙ₊₃
= 3F₂ₙ₊₁ − F₂ₙ₋₁, the same recurrence. Since F₃ = 2 = a₁ and F₅ = 5 = a₂, we con
clude:

$$a_n = F_{2n+1}$$

**Verification of small cases:**
- m=1, n=1: 2⁰ · F₃⁰ = 1 ✓ (single bacterium, body length 1, no feet)
- m=1, n=2: 2¹ · F₅⁰ = 2 ✓ (compositions of 2: (2) or (1,1))
- m=2, n=1: 2⁰ · F₃¹ = 2 ✓ (one body with foot, or two stacked bodies)
- m=2, n=2: 2¹ · F₅¹ = 2·5 = 10 ✓ (verified by complete enumeration of all 10 va
lid tilings)

**Total count.** Since rows are independent — row 1 contributes 2^(n−1) configur
ations and each of the remaining m−1 rows contributes F₂ₙ₊₁ configurations — the
 total number of valid tilings is:

$$\boxed{2^{n-1} \cdot F_{2n+1}^{\,m-1}}$$

where Fₖ is the k-th Fibonacci number with F₁ = F₂ = 1.
