 successive moves" forces alternation between horizontal (H) and vertical (V) mo

an H move doesn't change rows), and likewise for vertical moves and columns. So
every pawn alternates H, V, H, V, ... (or V, H, V, H, ...).

**Residue class analysis.** Partition the board into four classes by $(i \bmod 2

- $(0,0)$: $10 \times 10 = 100$
- $(0,1)$: $10 \times 9 = 90$
- $(1,0)$: $9 \times 10 = 90$
- $(1,1)$: $9 \times 9 = 81$

An H-starting pawn beginning in class $(a,b)$ cycles through classes with period
 4:
$$(a,b) \to (a, 1{-}b) \to (1{-}a, 1{-}b) \to (1{-}a, b) \to (a,b) \to \cdots$$

her way, **each pawn visits all four residue classes** over any 4 consecutive st

$$h_{11}+v_{11},\quad h_{10}+v_{01},\quad h_{00}+v_{00},\quad h_{01}+v_{10}$$
Each must be $\leq 81$. Also, the occupancy of classes $(0,1)$ and $(1,0)$ at va
