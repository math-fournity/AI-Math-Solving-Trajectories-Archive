### Step 2. $f'$ is integer-valued on a dense subset of $[0,1]$.

For any prime $p$ and $k \in \{0,1,\dots,p\}$, the number $k/p$ has denominator
$p$ when $1 \le k \le p-1$ and denominator $1$ when $k \in \{0,p\}$. In every ca
se $p\cdot f(k/p) \in \mathbb{Z}$ (since $f(k/p)$ is rational with denominator d
ividing $p$).

By the Mean Value Theorem, for each $k \in \{0,\dots,p-1\}$ there exists $c_{p,k
} \in (k/p,\,(k+1)/p)$ with
$$f'(c_{p,k}) \;=\; \frac{f((k+1)/p)-f(k/p)}{1/p} \;=\; p\,f((k+1)/p) - p\,f(k/p
) \;\in\; \mathbb{Z}.$$

The set $\{c_{p,k}\}$ over all primes $p$ and $0\le k\le p-1$ is **dense** in $[
0,1]$: given any open interval $(\alpha,\beta)\subset[0,1]$, pick a prime $p > 1
/(\beta-\alpha)$; then $1/p < \beta-\alpha$, so some subinterval $(k/p,(k+1)/p)$
 lies inside $(\alpha,\beta)$, giving $c_{p,k}\in(\alpha,\beta)$.

### Step 3. $f'$ is constant on $\mathbb{R}$.

Since $f'$ is continuous and takes values in the discrete set $\mathbb{Z}$ on a
dense subset of $[0,1]$, the image $f'([0,1])$ — being a connected subset of $\m
athbb{R}$ that intersects $\mathbb{Z}$ — must be a single integer. So $f' \equiv

The same argument applies on every interval $[n,n+1]$, $n\in\mathbb{Z}$: the shi
fted points $n + k/p = (np+k)/p$ satisfy $\gcd(np+k,\,p)=\gcd(k,p)$, preserving
the denominator structure. Thus $f'$ is constant on each $[n,n+1]$, and by conti
nuity of $f'$, constant on all of $\mathbb{R}$.

Therefore $f(x) = mx + c$ with $m\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $c = f(0)\in\mathbb{Z}$.

### Step 4. Determining $m$ and $c$.

For $q = a/b$ in lowest terms:
$$f(q) = \frac{ma}{b} + c = \frac{ma + bc}{b}, \qquad \gcd(ma+bc,\, b) = \gcd(ma

So $m\in\{1,-1\}$ and $c\in\mathbb{Z}$ is arbitrary.

Both are $C^1$ and satisfy the condition for every $c\in\mathbb{Z}$.
