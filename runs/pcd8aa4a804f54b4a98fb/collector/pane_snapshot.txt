## Proof that $S_1 = S_2$

We prove both inclusions.

### Notation and preliminaries

Since $F$ is continuous, non-decreasing, and differentiable with density $f = F'
$, we have $f \geq 0$ and
$$F(b) - F(a) = \int_a^b f(t)\,dt \quad \text{for all } a < b.$$
In particular, for any $s$ and $\varepsilon > 0$:
$$F(s+\varepsilon) - F(s-\varepsilon) = \int_{s-\varepsilon}^{s+\varepsilon} f(t
)\,dt.$$

---

### Inclusion 1: $S_1 \subseteq S_2$

Let $s \in S_1$. Then for every $\varepsilon > 0$,
$$\int_{s-\varepsilon}^{s+\varepsilon} f(t)\,dt = F(s+\varepsilon) - F(s-\vareps
ilon) > 0.$$

Suppose for contradiction that $s \notin S_2 = \operatorname{cl}(\{t : f(t) > 0\
})$. Then there exists $\delta > 0$ such that
$$(s-\delta,\, s+\delta) \cap \{t : f(t) > 0\} = \emptyset,$$
i.e., $f(t) = 0$ for all $t \in (s-\delta, s+\delta)$. Consequently,
$$\int_{s-\delta}^{s+\delta} f(t)\,dt = 0,$$
contradicting $s \in S_1$. Hence $s \in S_2$, giving $S_1 \subseteq S_2$.

---

### Inclusion 2: $S_2 \subseteq S_1$

We proceed in two steps: first show $\{t : f(t) > 0\} \subseteq S_1$, then show
$S_1$ is closed (so the closure is also contained).

**Step 2a: $\{t : f(t) > 0\} \subseteq S_1$.**

Let $t_0$ with $f(t_0) = F'(t_0) = L > 0$. By definition of the derivative,
$$\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{F(t_0 + h) - F(t_0)}{h} = L > 0.$$

For sufficiently small $h > 0$:
- $\frac{F(t_0+h) - F(t_0)}{h} > 0$, so $F(t_0 + h) > F(t_0)$;
- $\frac{F(t_0-h) - F(t_0)}{-h} > 0$, so $F(t_0 - h) < F(t_0)$.

Hence $F(t_0 + h) > F(t_0) > F(t_0 - h)$, i.e., $F(t_0 + h) - F(t_0 - h) > 0$ fo
r all small $h > 0$.

For **arbitrary** $\varepsilon > 0$: choose $\delta \in (0, \varepsilon)$ small
enough that $F(t_0+\delta) - F(t_0-\delta) > 0$. Since $f \geq 0$ and $(t_0-\del
ta, t_0+\delta) \subseteq (t_0-\varepsilon, t_0+\varepsilon)$,
$$F(t_0+\varepsilon) - F(t_0-\varepsilon) = \int_{t_0-\varepsilon}^{t_0+\varepsi
lon} f \geq \int_{t_0-\delta}^{t_0+\delta} f = F(t_0+\delta)-F(t_0-\delta) > 0.$
$

Thus $t_0 \in S_1$, proving $\{t : f(t) > 0\} \subseteq S_1$.

**Step 2b: $S_1$ is closed.**

Let $(s_n) \subseteq S_1$ with $s_n \to s$. Fix any $\varepsilon > 0$. For $n$ l
arge enough, $|s_n - s| < \varepsilon/2$, so
$$s_n - \tfrac{\varepsilon}{2} \in (s-\varepsilon,\, s), \qquad s_n + \tfrac{\va
repsilon}{2} \in (s,\, s+\varepsilon).$$

Since $s_n \in S_1$, $F(s_n + \tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}) > F(s_n - \tfrac{\varepsil
on}{2})$. Using that $F$ is non-decreasing:
$$F(s+\varepsilon) \;\geq\; F\!\left(s_n + \tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\right) \;>\; F
\!\left(s_n - \tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\right) \;\geq\; F(s-\varepsilon).$$

So $F(s+\varepsilon) > F(s-\varepsilon)$ for every $\varepsilon > 0$, hence $s \
in S_1$. Therefore $S_1$ is closed.

**Combining:** Since $\{t : f(t) > 0\} \subseteq S_1$ and $S_1$ is closed,
$$S_2 = \operatorname{cl}(\{t : f(t) > 0\}) \subseteq \operatorname{cl}(S_1) = S
_1.$$

---

### Conclusion

From Inclusion 1, $S_1 \subseteq S_2$. From Inclusion 2, $S_2 \subseteq S_1$. Th
erefore

$$\boxed{S_1 = S_2.}$$

**Intuition.** $S_1$ is the set of points where $F$ strictly increases in every
neighborhood — the "support of increase" of $F$. $S_2$ is the closure of the sup
port of the density $f$. Since $F$'s increase is governed exactly by where $f >
0$, and both sets are closed (the former by a monotonicity/limit argument, the l
atter by construction), the two supports coincide.
