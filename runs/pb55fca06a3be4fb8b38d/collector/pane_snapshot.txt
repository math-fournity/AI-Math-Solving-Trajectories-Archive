## Proof

$$F_n := E^* \setminus B_n, \qquad n \in \mathbb{N}.$$

**Properties of the $F_n$.**

1. **Each $F_n$ is closed in $E^*$**, since $B_n$ is open in $E^*$.
2. **$F_n \subseteq E$ for every $n$**, since $\infty \in B_n$ implies $\infty \
notin F_n$.
3. **$(F_n)$ is nondecreasing**: $B_n$ nonincreasing means $B_{n+1} \subseteq B_
n$, so $F_n \subseteq F_{n+1}$.
4. **$\bigcup_{n} F_n = E$**: By De Morgan's law,
$$\bigcup_{n} F_n = E^* \setminus \bigcap_{n} B_n = E^* \setminus \{\infty\} = E
.$$

**Arguing by contradiction.** Suppose $x \neq \infty$, i.e., $x \in E$.

Since $E = \bigcup_n F_n$ and $(F_n)$ is nondecreasing, there exists an index $N
$ such that $x \in F_N$.

**Deriving the contradiction.** For every $n \geq N$:
- Since $(B_n)$ is nonincreasing, $B_n \subseteq B_N$, i.e., $B_n \subseteq E^*
\setminus F_N$.
- Since $x_n \in B_n$, we get $x_n \in E^* \setminus F_N$, meaning $x_n \notin F
_N$.

So $x_n \notin F_N$ for all $n \geq N$.

On the other hand, $F_N$ is **closed** in $E^*$ (property 1), $x \in F_N$, and $
x_n \to x$ in $E^*$. By the definition of convergence in a topological space, ev
ery open neighborhood of $x$ contains $x_n$ for all sufficiently large $n$. Sinc
e $F_N$ is closed, $E^* \setminus F_N$ is open, and $x \notin E^* \setminus F_N$
 (because $x \in F_N$). Equivalently, the open set $E^* \setminus F_N$ does not
contain $x$, so its complement $F_N$ must contain $x_n$ for all sufficiently lar
ge $n$—that is, there exists $M$ such that $x_n \in F_N$ for all $n \geq M$.

For $n \geq \max(N, M)$, we simultaneously have $x_n \notin F_N$ (from the bulle
t above) and $x_n \in F_N$ (from convergence). **Contradiction.**

**Conclusion.** The assumption $x \in E$ is impossible, so $x = \infty$. $\black
square$
