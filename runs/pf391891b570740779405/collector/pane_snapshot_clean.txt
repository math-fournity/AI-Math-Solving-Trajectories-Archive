## Proof

Let $A$ be a commutative ring and $\mathcal{R} = TA[[T]]$ the ideal of formal po
wer series with zero constant term. An element of $\mathcal{R}$ has the form
$$f(T) = a_1 T + a_2 T^2 + a_3 T^3 + \cdots, \qquad a_i \in A.$$

The operation is **composition**: $(f \circ g)(T) = f(g(T))$. The identity eleme
nt is $\mathrm{id}(T) = T$.

**Claim.** $\mathcal{R}^* = \bigl\{\, f(T) = \sum_{n\ge 1} a_n T^n \in \mathcal{
R} : a_1 \in A^* \,\bigr\}$, where $A^*$ denotes the group of units of $A$.

---

### Necessity ($\Rightarrow$)

Suppose $f(T) = \sum_{n\ge 1} a_n T^n$ has a compositional inverse $g(T) = \sum_
{n\ge 1} b_n T^n \in \mathcal{R}$, so that $f(g(T)) = T$.

Expanding the lowest-degree term:
$$f(g(T)) = a_1\, g(T) + a_2\, g(T)^2 + \cdots = a_1(b_1 T + \text{higher}) + \c
dots = a_1 b_1\, T + \text{(terms of degree } \ge 2).$$

For this to equal $T$, the coefficient of $T$ must be $1$ and all higher coeffic
ients must be $0$. In particular:
$$a_1 b_1 = 1.$$

Thus $a_1$ has a multiplicative inverse $b_1$ in $A$, so $a_1 \in A^*$. $\square
$

---

### Sufficiency ($\Leftarrow$)

Suppose $a_1 \in A^*$. We construct the compositional inverse $g(T) = \sum_{n\ge
 1} b_n T^n$ by solving $f(g(T)) = T$ recursively, determining $b_n$ one coeffic
ient at a time.

**Base case ($n=1$).** From the coefficient of $T$ in $f(g(T))=T$ we obtain $a_1
 b_1 = 1$, so we set
$$b_1 = a_1^{-1} \in A.$$

**Inductive step.** Assume $b_1, \ldots, b_{n-1}$ have been determined (each in
$A$). We extract the coefficient of $T^n$ in $f(g(T))$ and set it equal to $0$ (
for $n \ge 2$; equal to $1$ for $n=1$, already handled).

Write $g(T) = b_1 T + b_2 T^2 + \cdots = b_1 T + h(T)$ where $h(T) = \sum_{k\ge
2} b_k T^k$ has order $\ge 2$. Then
$$f(g(T)) = \sum_{m\ge 1} a_m\, g(T)^m = a_1 g(T) + \sum_{m\ge 2} a_m\, g(T)^m.$
$

The term $a_1 g(T) = a_1 b_n T^n + \text{(known lower terms)}$ contributes $a_1
b_n$ to the coefficient of $T^n$.

For $m \ge 2$: $g(T)^m = (b_1 T + h(T))^m$. Since $h$ has order $\ge 2$, the coe
fficient of $T^n$ in $g(T)^m$ depends only on $b_1, \ldots, b_{n-m+1}$ (when $n
- m + 1 \ge 1$), i.e., on $b_1, \ldots, b_{n-1}$ at most. Crucially, **none of t
hese terms involves $b_n$**, because any occurrence of $b_n T^n$ inside $g(T)^m$
 with $m \ge 2$ requires the remaining $m-1$ factors to contribute degree $0$, w
hich is impossible since every factor has order $\ge 1$.

Therefore the coefficient of $T^n$ in $f(g(T))$ has the form
$$a_1 b_n + P_n(b_1, \ldots, b_{n-1}),$$
where $P_n$ is a polynomial expression in the already-determined coefficients $b
_1, \ldots, b_{n-1}$ with coefficients built from $a_2, \ldots, a_n$.

Setting this equal to the target ($0$ for $n \ge 2$) and using $a_1 \in A^*$:
$$b_n = -a_1^{-1}\, P_n(b_1, \ldots, b_{n-1}) \in A.$$

This determines $b_n$ uniquely. By induction, all coefficients $b_n$ exist in $A
$, yielding a well-defined $g(T) \in \mathcal{R}$ with $f(g(T)) = T$.

**Two-sided inverse.** The same argument with roles reversed shows $g(f(T)) = T$
: since $b_1 = a_1^{-1} \in A^*$, the series $g$ also satisfies the unit conditi
on, so it has a right inverse $g'$ with $g(g'(T)) = T$. Then
$$g' = (f \circ g) \circ g' = f \circ (g \circ g') = f \circ \mathrm{id} = f,$$
so $g(f(T)) = T$ as well. Hence $g$ is the two-sided compositional inverse of $f
$.

---

### Conclusion

The group of units $\mathcal{R}^*$ under composition consists exactly of those f
ormal power series whose **linear coefficient is a unit of $A$**:
$$\boxed{\mathcal{R}^* = \left\{\, f(T) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n T^n \in TA[[T]
] \;\middle|\; a_1 \in A^* \,\right\}.}$$

Equivalently, $f(T) = a_1 T + a_2 T^2 + \cdots$ is invertible under composition
if and only if $a_1$ is invertible under multiplication in $A$. The compositiona
l inverse is uniquely determined by recursively solving for its coefficients, ea
ch step requiring only the invertibility of $a_1$.
