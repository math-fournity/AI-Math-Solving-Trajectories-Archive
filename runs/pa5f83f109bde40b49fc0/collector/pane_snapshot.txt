  2. $\phi_N \circ \pi = \phi_M$ (state preservation),

$N' \subseteq \pi(M)'$, hence $\pi(M)'' \subseteq N'' = N$. Thus $\pi(M) =
\pi(M)'' \subseteq N$ is a von Neumann subalgebra of $N$. Since $\pi$ is unital,
$1_N = \pi(1_M) \in \pi(M)$.

Step 2: $\pi(M)$ is invariant under the modular automorphism group $
\sigma_t^{\phi_N}$.

For any $x \in M$ and $t \in \mathbb{R}$, using the modular group commutation: $
$\sigma_t^{\phi_N}(\pi(x)) = \pi(\sigma_t^{\phi_M}(x)) \in \pi(M).$$ Since every
element of $\pi(M)$ is of the form $\pi(x)$, this shows $\sigma_t^{\phi_N}
(\pi(M)) \subseteq \pi(M)$ for all $t$. Applying $\sigma_{-t}^{\phi_N}$ gives
the reverse inclusion, so $\sigma_t^{\phi_N}(\pi(M)) = \pi(M)$ for all $t \in
\mathbb{R}$.

By Takesaki's theorem (1972): if $N$ is a von Neumann algebra with faithful
normal state $\phi_N$, and $P \subseteq N$ is a von Neumann subalgebra with $1_N

\to P$ if and only if $P$ is invariant under $\sigma_t^{\phi_N}$.

conditional expectation (norm-one projection) $$E: N \to \pi(M), \quad
