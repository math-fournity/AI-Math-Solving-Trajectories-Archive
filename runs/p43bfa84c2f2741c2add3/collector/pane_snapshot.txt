## Proof

**Setup.** Let $X = (X_1, \dots, X_d)$ and $Y = (Y_1, \dots, Y_d)$ be independen
t random vectors, each uniformly distributed on the unit cube $C = [-\tfrac12, \
tfrac12]^d$ centered at the origin. The angle between them is

$$\theta(X, Y) = \arccos\!\left(\frac{X \cdot Y}{\lVert X\rVert\, \lVert Y\rVert
}\right),$$

which is well-defined almost surely since $X$ and $Y$ are continuously distribut
ed, so $\lVert X\rVert, \lVert Y\rVert > 0$ a.s.

**Key symmetry.** The cube $C$ is centrally symmetric about the origin: $y \in C
 \iff -y \in C$. Consequently, if $Y \sim \mathrm{Unif}(C)$, then $-Y \sim \math
rm{Unif}(C)$ as well, i.e.,

$$Y \stackrel{d}{=} -Y.$$

Since $X$ and $Y$ are independent, the joint distribution satisfies

$$(X, Y) \stackrel{d}{=} (X, -Y).$$

**Relating the two angles.** For any nonzero vectors $x, y$,

$$\theta(x, -y) = \arccos\!\left(\frac{x \cdot (-y)}{\lVert x\rVert\,\lVert -y\r
Vert}\right) = \arccos\!\left(-\frac{x \cdot y}{\lVert x\rVert\,\lVert y\rVert}\
right).$$

Using the identity $\arccos(-t) = \pi - \arccos(t)$ for $t \in [-1, 1]$,

$$\theta(x, -y) = \pi - \arccos\!\left(\frac{x \cdot y}{\lVert x\rVert\,\lVert y
\rVert}\right) = \pi - \theta(x, y).$$

**Computing the expectation.** Applying the distributional equality $(X,Y) \stac
krel{d}{=} (X,-Y)$ to the (bounded, measurable) function $\theta$:

$$\mathbb{E}[\theta(X,Y)] = \mathbb{E}[\theta(X,-Y)] = \mathbb{E}[\pi - \theta(X
,Y)] = \pi - \mathbb{E}[\theta(X,Y)].$$

Rearranging,

$$2\,\mathbb{E}[\theta(X,Y)] = \pi \implies \mathbb{E}[\theta(X,Y)] = \frac{\pi}
{2}.$$

**Sanity checks.**

- *Dimension $d = 1$:* The "angle" between two real numbers is $0$ if they share
 a sign and $\pi$ if they have opposite signs. By symmetry each case has probabi
lity $\tfrac12$, giving $\mathbb{E}[\theta] = \tfrac12 \cdot 0 + \tfrac12 \cdot
\pi = \tfrac{\pi}{2}$. ✓

- *Generality:* The argument uses only central symmetry of the distribution ($Y
\stackrel{d}{=} -Y$) and independence of $X, Y$. It therefore holds for **every*
* dimension $d \geq 1$ and, in fact, for any centrally symmetric distribution (n
ot just the uniform cube).

**Conclusion.** The expected angle between two independent random vectors unifor
mly sampled from the unit cube centered at the origin in $d$-dimensional space i
s

$$\boxed{\dfrac{\pi}{2}}$$

for all $d \geq 1$.
