## Proof

**Theorem.** Among all undirected 2‑connected graphs on $n$ vertices ($n\ge 3$),
 the sum of all pairwise distances $S(G)=\sum_{x,y\in V(G)}d_G(x,y)$ is maximize
d uniquely by the cycle $C_n$.

Write $S(G)=\sum_{v\in V}D(v)$ where $D(v)=\sum_{u\in V}d(v,u)$. We prove the pe
r‑vertex bound

$$D(v)\le \Big\lfloor \frac{n^{2}}{4}\Big\rfloor \qquad\text{for every }v\in V(G
),$$

with equality for all $v$ simultaneously only when $G\cong C_n$. Summing then gi
ves $S(G)\le n\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor = S(C_n)$.

### Lemma 1 (Eccentricity bound)

For every vertex $v$ of a 2‑connected $n$‑vertex graph, $\operatorname{ecc}(v)\l
e \lfloor n/2\rfloor$.

*Proof.* Let $u$ be a vertex with $d(v,u)=\operatorname{ecc}(v)=:r$. By 2‑connec
tivity (Menger), there exist two internally vertex‑disjoint $v$–$u$ paths $P_1,P
_2$. Their union is a cycle on $|P_1|+|P_2|$ distinct vertices, so $|P_1|+|P_2|\
le n$. Hence
$$r=d(v,u)\le \min(|P_1|,|P_2|)\le \frac{|P_1|+|P_2|}{2}\le \frac n2.\qquad\squa
re$$

### Lemma 2 (Layer sizes)

Let $L_0=\{v\},L_1,\dots,L_r$ be the BFS distance layers from $v$, $r=\operatorn
ame{ecc}(v)$. Then $|L_i|\ge 2$ for every $1\le i\le r-1$ (and $|L_r|\ge 1$).

*Proof.* Suppose $|L_i|=1$, say $L_i=\{w\}$, for some $1\le i\le r-1$. Since $i<
r$, the set $L_{i+1}\cup\dots\cup L_r$ is nonempty. Any path from a vertex of $L
_{i+1}$ to $v$ must pass through some vertex at distance $i$ from $v$, i.e. thro
ugh $w$. Moreover no edge joins $L_{i-1}$ to $L_{i+1}$ (that would put an $L_{i+
1}$ vertex at distance $\le i$). Thus removing $w$ separates $L_{i+1}\cup\dots\c
up L_r$ from $v$—so $w$ is a cut vertex, contradicting 2‑connectivity. $\square$

### Lemma 3 (Per‑vertex distance sum bound)

$D(v)\le r(n-r)\le \lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor$, where $r=\operatorname{ecc}(v)$.

*Proof.* Set $a_i=|L_i|$. By Lemma 2, $a_i\ge 2$ for $1\le i\le r-1$ and $a_r\ge
 1$, with $\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_i=n-1$. Thus
$$D(v)=\sum_{i=1}^{r} i\,a_i.$$
To maximize this with the given lower bounds and fixed total, push all free mass
 to the largest index $r$: the minimum admissible values are $a_1=\dots=a_{r-1}=
2$, leaving $a_r=(n-1)-2(r-1)=n-2r+1\ge 1$ (using $r\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ from
Lemma 1). Any unit of mass moved from layer $r$ down to layer $i<r$ decreases th
e sum by $r-i>0$, so this is the unique maximizer:
$$D(v)\le 2(1+2+\dots+(r-1))+r(n-2r+1)=r(r-1)+r(n-2r+1)=r(n-r).$$
The quadratic $r(n-r)$ is increasing on $[0,n/2]$, so by Lemma 1
$$D(v)\le r(n-r)\le \Big\lfloor\frac n2\Big\rfloor\Big(n-\Big\lfloor\frac n2\Big
\rfloor\Big)=\Big\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\Big\rfloor.\qquad\square$$

### Completing the upper bound

Summing Lemma 3 over all vertices,
$$S(G)=\sum_{v\in V}D(v)\le n\Big\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\Big\rfloor.$$
For the cycle, from any vertex the distances are $1,1,2,2,\dots$, giving $D_{C_n
}(v)=\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor$ for every $v$ (direct check: $n=2k$ gives $k^{2}$;
$n=2k+1$ gives $k(k+1)$). Hence
$$S(C_n)=n\Big\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\Big\rfloor,$$
so $S(G)\le S(C_n)$ for every 2‑connected $G$.

### Equality characterization

Suppose $S(G)=S(C_n)$. Then $D(v)=\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor$ for *every* $v$. By th
e equality conditions in Lemma 3, each vertex $v$ satisfies $\operatorname{ecc}(
v)=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ and the extremal layer profile $a_1=\dots=a_{r-1}=2$, $a_
r=n-2r+1$. In particular $a_1=2$, i.e. $\deg(v)=2$, for every vertex $v$.

A 2‑connected graph in which every vertex has degree $2$ is a cycle. Therefore $
G\cong C_n$.
