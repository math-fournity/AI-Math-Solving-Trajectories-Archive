## Proof

**Setup and simplification.** Let $\tau = t_1 - t_0 > 0$ and define the positive
 weights
$$w_n = B_n e^{-\lambda_n t_0} > 0.$$

Then the definition of $\lambda$ becomes
$$\lambda = \frac{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \lambda_n w_n}{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} w_n},$$
i.e., $\lambda$ is the weighted average of the $\lambda_n$ with weights $w_n$.

**Rewriting both sides.** The left-hand side of the claimed inequality is
$$\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n e^{-\lambda_n t_1}}{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n e^{-
\lambda_n t_0}} = \frac{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} w_n e^{-\lambda_n(t_1-t_0)}}{\sum_{n
=1}^{\infty} w_n} = \frac{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} w_n e^{-\lambda_n \tau}}{\sum_{n=1
}^{\infty} w_n},$$
since $B_n e^{-\lambda_n t_1} = B_n e^{-\lambda_n t_0} \cdot e^{-\lambda_n(t_1-t
_0)} = w_n e^{-\lambda_n \tau}$.

The right-hand side is
$$\frac{e^{-\lambda t_1}}{e^{-\lambda t_0}} = e^{-\lambda(t_1-t_0)} = e^{-\lambd
a \tau}.$$

**Reduction to a probabilistic statement.** Introduce the probability distributi
on $p_n = w_n / \sum_k w_k$ (all $p_n > 0$, $\sum p_n = 1$). Then $\lambda = \su
m_n p_n \lambda_n = \mathbb{E}[\lambda_n]$, and the inequality becomes
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} p_n\, e^{-\lambda_n \tau} \;\ge\; e^{-\lambda \tau} = e^{-
\mathbb{E}[\lambda_n]\,\tau},$$
i.e.,
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{-\lambda_n \tau}\right] \;\ge\; e^{-\mathbb{E}[\lambda_n]
\,\tau}.$$

**Applying Jensen's inequality.** Consider the function $f(x) = e^{-\tau x}$ for
 $x > 0$, with $\tau > 0$ fixed. Its derivatives are
$$f'(x) = -\tau e^{-\tau x}, \qquad f''(x) = \tau^2 e^{-\tau x} > 0.$$
Since $f''(x) > 0$ for all $x$, the function $f$ is **strictly convex** on $(0,\
infty)$.

By **Jensen's inequality** for a convex function applied to the discrete random
variable $\lambda_n$ (with distribution $p_n$),
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[f(\lambda_n)\right] \;\ge\; f\!\left(\mathbb{E}[\lambda_n]\r
ight),$$
that is,
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} p_n\, e^{-\lambda_n \tau} \;\ge\; e^{-\left(\sum_{n} p_n \
lambda_n\right)\tau} = e^{-\lambda \tau}.$$

This is precisely the desired inequality.

**Conclusion.** The inequality
$$\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n e^{-\lambda_n t_1}}{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n e^{-
\lambda_n t_0}} \ge \frac{e^{-\lambda t_1}}{e^{-\lambda t_0}}$$
**always holds** for $t_1 > t_0 > 0$ and positive $\lambda_n, B_n, t_0$, with $\
lambda$ defined as the weighted average above. It is a direct consequence of the
 convexity of $x \mapsto e^{-\tau x}$ (for $\tau > 0$) and Jensen's inequality.

Equality holds if and only if all the $\lambda_n$ (with $B_n e^{-\lambda_n t_0}
> 0$) are identical, in which case both sides reduce to $e^{-\lambda \tau}$.
