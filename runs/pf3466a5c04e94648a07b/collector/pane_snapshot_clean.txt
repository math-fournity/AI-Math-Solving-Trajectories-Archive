## Proof

**Example.** Let $G = \mu_2$, the group of second roots of unity, defined as the
 closed subgroup of $\mathbb{G}_m = \mathrm{GL}_1$ given by
$$\mu_2 = \{x \in \mathbb{G}_m : x^2 = 1\} = \mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb{Q}[t]/(t^2 -
 1).$$
This is a linear algebraic group defined over $\mathbb{Q}$ (hence over $\mathbb{
C}$ by base change).

**$\mu_2$ is linear algebraic.** It is a closed subgroup of $\mathbb{G}_m = \mat
hrm{GL}_1$, which is linear algebraic. Closed subgroups of linear algebraic grou
ps are linear algebraic, so $\mu_2$ is linear algebraic.

**$\mu_2$ has exactly two rational points.** The $\mathbb{Q}$-rational points ar
e
$$\mu_2(\mathbb{Q}) = \{q \in \mathbb{Q}^\times : q^2 = 1\} = \{1,\, -1\}.$$
Indeed, $q^2 = 1 \iff (q-1)(q+1) = 0$, and since $\mathbb{Q}$ is a field, $q = 1
$ or $q = -1$. Both are rational, so $|\mu_2(\mathbb{Q})| = 2$.

**Group structure.** The two points $\{1, -1\}$ form a group under multiplicatio
n, isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, which is consistent with $\mu_2$ bein
g a group scheme.

Thus $G = \mu_2$ is a linear algebraic group over $\mathbb{C}$ (defined over $\m
athbb{Q}$) with exactly two rational points, namely $\{1, -1\}$.
