## Solution

We compute $\displaystyle I = \oint_{L}\frac{e^{1/(z-a)}}{z}\,dz$, where $L = \{
z \in \mathbb{C}: |z|=r\}$ with $r > |a|$.

### Singularities inside the contour

Since $r > |a|$, the circle $|z|=r$ encloses both singularities of the integrand
 $f(z) = \dfrac{e^{1/(z-a)}}{z}$:

- $z = 0$: a simple pole (from the factor $1/z$),
- $z = a$: an essential singularity (from $e^{1/(z-a)}$).

By the Residue Theorem,
$$I = 2\pi i\Big(\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f + \operatorname{Res}_{z=a} f\Big).$$

### Residue at $z = 0$

This is a simple pole, so
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f = \lim_{z\to 0}\, z\cdot\frac{e^{1/(z-a)}}{z} = e^{
1/(0-a)} = e^{-1/a}.$$

### Residue at $z = a$ (essential singularity)

Set $w = z - a$, so $z = w + a$. We need the coefficient of $w^{-1}$ in the Laur
ent expansion of
$$\frac{e^{1/w}}{w+a}$$
around $w = 0$.

For $|w| < |a|$,
$$\frac{1}{w+a} = \frac{1}{a}\cdot\frac{1}{1+w/a} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1
)^n}{a^{n+1}}\,w^n.$$

Also,
$$e^{1/w} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\,w^{-k}.$$

The product is
$$\frac{e^{1/w}}{w+a} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n}{a^{n
+1}\,k!}\,w^{\,n-k}.$$

The coefficient of $w^{-1}$ requires $n - k = -1$, i.e. $k = n+1$. Summing over
all $n \ge 0$:
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=a} f = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n}{a^{n+1}\,(n+1)!}
 = \sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{m!\,a^m}, \quad (m = n+1).$$

Now observe
$$e^{-1/a} = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^m}{m!\,a^m} = 1 + \sum_{m=1}^{\infty}
\frac{(-1)^m}{m!\,a^m},$$
so
$$\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^m}{m!\,a^m} = e^{-1/a} - 1,$$
and therefore
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=a} f = -\big(e^{-1/a}-1\big) = 1 - e^{-1/a}.$$

### Sum of residues

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f + \operatorname{Res}_{z=a} f = e^{-1/a} + \big(1 -
e^{-1/a}\big) = 1.$$

### Final result

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \oint_{L}\frac{e^{1/(z-a)}}{z}\,dz = 2\pi i.}$$
