**Problem.** Find an upper bound for the operator norm $|e^A|$ of an $n \times n
$ matrix $A$ in terms of $|A|$ and $n$.

**Interpretation of the norm.** We take $|A| = \max_{i,j} |a_{ij}|$, the maximum
 absolute value of the entries (the sup-norm on matrix entries). The operator no
rm $\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ (induced by the Euclidean vector norm) satisfies $\|B\|
_{\mathrm{op}} \leq \|B\|_F \leq n\,|B|$, so bounding $|e^A|$ in the entry-sup-n
orm also bounds the operator norm up to a factor of $n$. We work directly with t
he entry-sup-norm throughout and obtain a bound involving both $|A|$ and $n$.

---

**Proof.**

Define $|A| = \max_{1 \le i,j \le n} |a_{ij}|$. We establish the bound

$$|e^A| \;\le\; 1 + \frac{e^{\,n|A|} - 1}{n}.$$

**Step 1: Submultiplicativity with factor $n$.**

For any two $n\times n$ matrices $A = (a_{ij})$ and $B = (b_{ij})$, the $(i,j)$-
entry of $AB$ is

$$(AB)_{ij} = \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_{ik}\, b_{kj}.$$

By the triangle inequality and the definition of $|A|,|B|$:

$$|(AB)_{ij}| \le \sum_{k=1}^{n} |a_{ik}|\,|b_{kj}| \le \sum_{k=1}^{n} |A|\,|B|
= n\,|A|\,|B|.$$

Taking the maximum over $i,j$:

$$|AB| \le n\,|A|\,|B|. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2: Bound on $|A^k|$.**

By induction on $k$ using (1):

- $|A^1| = |A| \le n^{0}\,|A|^1$. ✓
- If $|A^k| \le n^{k-1}|A|^k$, then $|A^{k+1}| = |A^k \cdot A| \le n\,|A^k|\,|A|
 \le n \cdot n^{k-1}|A|^k \cdot |A| = n^{k}|A|^{k+1}$. ✓

Therefore, for all $k \ge 1$:

$$|A^k| \le n^{k-1}\,|A|^k. \tag{2}$$

**Step 3: Bound on $|e^A|$.**

The matrix exponential is defined by the absolutely convergent series

$$e^A = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{A^k}{k!}.$$

Applying the triangle inequality for the entry-sup-norm (which is a norm, hence
subadditive):

$$|e^A| = \left|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{A^k}{k!}\right| \le \sum_{k=0}^{\infty
} \frac{|A^k|}{k!}.$$

Separating the $k=0$ term ($A^0 = I$, so $|I| = 1$) and applying (2) for $k \ge
1$:

$$|e^A| \le 1 + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{n^{k-1}\,|A|^k}{k!} = 1 + \frac{1}{n}\
sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{(n|A|)^k}{k!}.$$

Recognizing the Taylor series of the exponential:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{(n|A|)^k}{k!} = e^{n|A|} - 1.$$

Therefore:

$$\boxed{|e^A| \;\le\; 1 + \frac{e^{\,n|A|} - 1}{n}.} \tag{3}$$

**Step 4: Simpler (coarser) bound.**

Since $1 + \frac{e^{n|A|}-1}{n} \le 1 + \frac{e^{n|A|}}{n} \le e^{n|A|}$ (the la
st inequality holding because $1 + \frac{x}{n} \le e^{x/n} \le e^x$ for $x = n|A
| \ge 0$), we also have the simpler bound:

$$|e^A| \le e^{\,n|A|}. \tag{4}$$

**Step 5: Relation to the operator norm.**

If one specifically wants the operator norm $\|e^A\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ (spectral no
rm), then since $\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \|B\|_F \le n\,|B|$ for any matrix $B$:

$$\|e^A\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le n\,|e^A| \le n\left(1 + \frac{e^{n|A|}-1}{n}\right)
= n + e^{n|A|} - 1 \le n\, e^{n|A|}. \tag{5}$$

Alternatively, if $|A|$ itself denotes the operator norm $\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ (
which is submultiplicative without any $n$ factor), the standard argument gives
the $n$-independent bound $\|e^A\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le e^{\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}}$, a
nd the role of $n$ is absorbed into the relation $\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le n\max|
a_{ij}|$.

---

**Summary.** With $|A| = \max_{i,j}|a_{ij}|$, the sharp bound from this method i
s

$$|e^A| \le 1 + \frac{e^{n|A|} - 1}{n},$$

and the coarser but simpler bound is $|e^A| \le e^{n|A|}$. Both express the uppe
r bound in terms of $|A|$ and the dimension $n$. $\blacksquare$
