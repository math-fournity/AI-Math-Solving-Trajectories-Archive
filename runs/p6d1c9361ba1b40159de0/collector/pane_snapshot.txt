$$T_n^{-1} \xrightarrow{\|\cdot\|} S.$$
We also have $T_n \xrightarrow{\|\cdot\|} T$.

**Key fact (continuity of operator multiplication).** If $A_n \to A$ and $B_n \t
o B$ in operator norm, then $A_n B_n \to AB$. Indeed,
$$\|A_n B_n - AB\| \le \|A_n\|\,\|B_n - B\| + \|A_n - A\|\,\|B\|,$$
and $\|A_n\|$ is bounded (convergent sequences are bounded), so both terms tend
to $0$.

**Applying this.** Since $T_n^{-1} \to S$ and $T_n \to T$, by continuity of mult
iplication:

1. **Right inverse:** $T_n^{-1} T_n = I_X$ for every $n$ (each $T_n$ is invertib

$$I_X = \lim_{n\to\infty} T_n^{-1} T_n = S\, T.$$
Hence $ST = I_X$.

2. **Left inverse:** $T_n T_n^{-1} = I_Y$ for every $n$. Taking the limit,
$$I_Y = \lim_{n\to\infty} T_n T_n^{-1} = T\, S.$$
Hence $TS = I_Y$.

**Conclusion.** We have shown $ST = I_X$ and $TS = I_Y$. This means $T: X \to Y$
 is bijective with two-sided inverse $S: Y \to X$. Since $S \in B(Y,X)$ is bound
ed, $T$ is invertible in the Banach-space sense (i.e., $T \in B(X,Y)$ is bijecti

$$T^{-1} = S. \qquad \blacksquare$$
