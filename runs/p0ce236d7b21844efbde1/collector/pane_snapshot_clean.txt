## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, there exists a finitely presentable torsion-free group whose abe
lianization has torsion.

**Example:** The Klein bottle group
$$G = \langle\, a, b \mid aba^{-1} = b^{-1} \,\rangle.$$

This is the fundamental group of the Klein bottle. It is finitely presented (2 g
enerators, 1 relator). We verify the two required properties.

---

### 1. $G$ is torsion-free

The relation $aba^{-1} = b^{-1}$ gives $ab^n = b^{(-1)\,n}a$ and more generally
$$a^m b^n = b^{(-1)^m n}\, a^m, \qquad m,n \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

Every element of $G$ has a unique normal form $a^m b^n$ (this is the semidirect
product $\mathbb{Z}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}$ where the generator of the first $\mathbb{
Z}$ acts on the second by $n\mapsto -n$). Using the normal form:

$$(a^m b^n)^2 = a^m b^n\, a^m b^n = a^{2m}\, b^{(-1)^m n + n}.$$

- If $m$ is odd: $(-1)^m n + n = 0$, so $(a^m b^n)^2 = a^{2m}$.
- If $m$ is even: $(a^m b^n)^2 = a^{2m} b^{2n}$.

By induction, $(a^m b^n)^k = a^{km}\, b^{c_k}$ for some integer $c_k$ depending
on $m,n,k$. In particular, the $a$-exponent of $(a^m b^n)^k$ is $km$.

Suppose $(a^m b^n)^k = e$ with $k > 0$. Then $km = 0$, so $m = 0$. The element i
s then $b^n$, and $b^{nk}=e$ forces $n=0$. Hence the only element of finite orde
r is the identity, and $G$ is torsion-free.

---

### 2. The abelianization $G_{\mathrm{ab}}$ has torsion

The abelianization is obtained by adding the commutativity relation $ab=ba$, i.e
. $aba^{-1}=b$. Combining with the defining relation $aba^{-1}=b^{-1}$ yields
$$b = b^{-1}, \quad\text{i.e.}\quad b^2 = e.$$

Thus
$$G_{\mathrm{ab}} = \langle\, a, b \mid b^2 = e,\; ab=ba \,\rangle \cong \mathbb
{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.$$

The class of $b$ has order $2$ in $G_{\mathrm{ab}}$, so the abelianization has t
orsion.

---

### Conclusion

The Klein bottle group $G=\langle a,b\mid aba^{-1}=b^{-1}\rangle$ is finitely pr
esented, torsion-free, and has abelianization $\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\math
bb{Z}$ with torsion. Therefore such a group exists. $\blacksquare$
