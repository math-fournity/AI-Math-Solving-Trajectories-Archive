The condition $\|f\|_{H^1} < \varepsilon$ is a red herring — it only concerns th

**Proof.**

Recall that $f \in H^2(\mathbb{R})$ means
$$\|f\|_{H^2}^2 = \int_{\mathbb{R}} (1+|\xi|^2)^2\,|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^2\,d\xi <
\infty,$$
i.e. $(1+|\xi|^2)\widehat{f} \in L^2(\mathbb{R})$.

**Key observation (Fourier-side Sobolev embedding).** In one dimension, $H^s(\ma
thbb{R}) \hookrightarrow C^k_b(\mathbb{R})$ whenever $s > k + \tfrac12$. Here $s
=2$ and $k=1$, and $2 > \tfrac32$, so $H^2(\mathbb{R}) \hookrightarrow C^1_b(\ma
thbb{R}) \subset C^1(\mathbb{R})$.

We verify this directly.

**Step 1: $\widehat{f} \in L^1$, so $f$ is continuous.** Factor
$$\widehat{f}(\xi) = \frac{1}{1+\xi^2}\cdot(1+\xi^2)\widehat{f}(\xi).$$
Since $(1+\xi^2)^{-1} \in L^2(\mathbb{R})$ (because $\int (1+\xi^2)^{-2}\,d\xi <
 \infty$) and $(1+\xi^2)\widehat{f} \in L^2(\mathbb{R})$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives
$$\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^1} \le \bigl\|(1+\xi^2)^{-1}\bigr\|_{L^2}\,\bigl\|(1+\xi^2)
\widehat{f}\bigr\|_{L^2} = C\,\|f\|_{H^2} < \infty.$$
By the Fourier inversion theorem, $\widehat{f}\in L^1$ implies $f(x) = \int \wid
ehat{f}(\xi)e^{2\pi i x\xi}\,d\xi$ is continuous (in fact bounded and uniformly
continuous).

**Step 2: $\widehat{f'} = 2\pi i\,\xi\,\widehat{f} \in L^1$, so $f'$ is continuo
us.** Factor
$$\xi\,\widehat{f}(\xi) = \frac{\xi}{1+\xi^2}\cdot(1+\xi^2)\widehat{f}(\xi).$$
The function $\xi(1+\xi^2)^{-1}$ belongs to $L^2(\mathbb{R})$: near $\xi=0$ it b
ehaves like $\xi$ (integrable), and as $|\xi|\to\infty$ it behaves like $\xi^{-1
}$, so $\int \xi^2(1+\xi^2)^{-2}\,d\xi < \infty$. Applying Cauchy–Schwarz again,
$$\|\xi\,\widehat{f}\|_{L^1} \le \bigl\|\xi(1+\xi^2)^{-1}\bigr\|_{L^2}\,\bigl\|(
1+\xi^2)\widehat{f}\bigr\|_{L^2} = C'\,\|f\|_{H^2} < \infty.$$
Hence $\widehat{f'}\in L^1$, and by Fourier inversion $f'(x) = \int 2\pi i\,\xi\
,\widehat{f}(\xi)\,e^{2\pi i x\xi}\,d\xi$ is continuous (and bounded).

**Conclusion.** Both $f$ and $f'$ are continuous, so $f \in C^1(\mathbb{R})$. Th
e hypothesis $\|f\|_{H^1}<\varepsilon$ is superfluous for this conclusion — it m
erely bounds the magnitude of $f$ and does not affect the regularity, which is e
ntirely determined by the membership $f\in H^2(\mathbb{R})$. $\blacksquare$
