Looking at this problem, I need to analyze a card-dealing process with redistrib
ution and find the probability of a perfectly balanced final state.

## Setup and Key Definitions

Six players, 48 cards. Each round: (1) give card to random player, (2) pick Z fr
om max-card players, (3) pick D from min-card players (≠ Z), (4) Z gives one car
d to D.

**Define "Type A" (almost balanced):** For $n$ cards, each player has $\lfloor n
/6 \rfloor$ or $\lceil n/6 \rceil$ cards.

**Define "Type B":** The state $(m+1, m, m, m, m, m-1)$ at $n = 6m$ (one player
above, one below the average).

## Critical Lemma: Only Type A and Type B are reachable

I verify the transitions from Type A at each residue mod 6. Let $n = 6k + r$, Ty
pe A state:

**Range-1 case (step 1 gives to a non-extreme player):** After step 1, range = 1
. Z (max) gives to D (min), which **swaps** their counts — the multiset is prese
rved. Stay in Type A.

**Range-2 case (step 1 gives to a max player):** The recipient becomes the **uni
que** new max. Z = that player, D = a min player. Transfer reduces both toward t
he mean. Checking each residue:

| $r$ | Type A state | Give to max → | After transfer | Type A at $n{+}1$? |
|-----|-------------|--------------|----------------|:---:|
| 0 | $(k,k,k,k,k,k)$ | $(k{+}1,k,k,k,k,k)$, range 1 | swap | ✓ |
| 1 | $(k{+}1,k,k,k,k,k)$ | $(k{+}2,k,k,k,k,k)$ | $(k{+}1,k{+}1,k,k,k,k)$ | ✓ |
| 2 | $(k{+}1,k{+}1,k,k,k,k)$ | $(k{+}2,k{+}1,k,k,k,k)$ | $(k{+}1,k{+}1,k{+}1,k,
k,k)$ | ✓ |
| 3 | $(k{+}1,k{+}1,k{+}1,k,k,k)$ | → | $(k{+}1,k{+}1,k{+}1,k{+}1,k,k)$ | ✓ |
| 4 | $(k{+}1,k{+}1,k{+}1,k{+}1,k,k)$ | → | $(k{+}1,k{+}1,k{+}1,k{+}1,k{+}1,k)$
| ✓ |
| 5 | $(k{+}1,k{+}1,k{+}1,k{+}1,k{+}1,k)$ | → | $(k{+}1,k{+}1,k{+}1,k{+}1,k{+}1,
k{+}1)$ | ✓ |

In every range-2 case, the unique new max transfers to a min player, and the res
ult is Type A. **Crucially, since all min players share the same count, the rand
om choice of D doesn't affect the resulting multiset.**

## The Only Deviation

At $n \equiv 5 \pmod{6}$, Type A = $(k{+}1, k{+}1, k{+}1, k{+}1, k{+}1, k)$ with
 **5 players at $k{+}1$ and 1 at $k$**:

- **Give to a $k{+}1$ player (prob $5/6$):** State becomes $(k{+}2, k{+}1, k{+}1
, k{+}1, k{+}1, k)$, range 2. Z = $k{+}2$ (unique), D = $k$ (unique). Transfer →
 all $k{+}1$. **Type A at $6(k{+}1)$.** ✓

- **Give to the $k$ player (prob $1/6$):** State becomes all $(k{+}1)$. Range 0.
 Z = random among 6, D = random among remaining 5. Transfer: one player → $k{+}2
$, one → $k$, rest stay $k{+}1$. **Type B at $6(k{+}1)$.** ✗

## Recovery from Type B

Type B at $6m$: $(m{+}1, m, m, m, m, m{-}1)$. Step 1 gives to any player:

- **To $m{+}1$:** $(m{+}2, m, m, m, m, m{-}1)$. Z=$m{+}2$, D=$m{-}1$. → $(m{+}1,
 m, m, m, m, m)$. Type A. ✓
- **To $m$:** $(m{+}1, m{+}1, m, m, m, m{-}1)$. Z = one of the two $m{+}1$, D =
$m{-}1$. → $(m{+}1, m, m, m, m, m)$. Type A. ✓
- **To $m{-}1$:** $(m{+}1, m, m, m, m, m)$. Range 1. Swap. Type A. ✓

**From Type B, we always return to Type A in one round**, regardless of which pl
ayer receives the card.

## The Independence Argument

The key structural insight: **at every $n \equiv 5 \pmod{6}$, the state is alway
s Type A**, regardless of whether we were in Type A or Type B at the previous mu
ltiple of 6:

- From Type A at $6m$: → Type A at $6m{+}1$ → ... → Type A at $6m{+}5$. ✓
- From Type B at $6m$: → Type A at $6m{+}1$ (recovery) → ... → Type A at $6m{+}5
$. ✓

Since the state at $n \equiv 5 \pmod{6}$ is **always** Type A = $(m{+}1, m{+}1,
m{+}1, m{+}1, m{+}1, m)$, the transition to $n \equiv 0 \pmod{6}$ is always:

$$P(\text{Type A at } 6(m{+}1)) = \frac{5}{6}, \quad P(\text{Type B at } 6(m{+}1
)) = \frac{1}{6}$$

**This probability is the same at every multiple of 6, independent of all previo
us history.**

## Computing the Answer

At $n = 48 = 6 \times 8$, the state at $n = 47 = 6 \times 7 + 5$ is Type A = $(8
, 8, 8, 8, 8, 7)$ regardless of history. The transition to $n = 48$ yields:

- **Type A (all 8s) with probability $\frac{5}{6}$:** card goes to one of the fi
ve 8-card players.
- **Type B $(9, 8, 8, 8, 8, 7)$ with probability $\frac{1}{6}$:** card goes to t
he unique 7-card player.

$$P(\text{each player has exactly 8 cards}) = \frac{5}{6}$$

Since $\gcd(5, 6) = 1$, the fraction $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{5}{6}$ is already irre
ducible.

$$a + b = 5 + 6 = \boxed{11}$$
