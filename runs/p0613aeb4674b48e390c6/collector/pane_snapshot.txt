## Solution

**Setup.** A bijection $\varphi: \{1,\ldots,2n\} \to \{1,\ldots,n\}\times\{0,1\}
$ is a permutation of the $2n$ elements $(1,0),(1,1),(2,0),(2,1),\ldots,(n,0),(n
,1)$. We choose $\varphi$ uniformly at random. Index $k\in\{1,\ldots,2n-1\}$ is
*adjacent* if $\pi_0(\varphi(k))=\pi_0(\varphi(k+1))$, i.e., the two consecutive
 elements share the same first coordinate (so they are $(i,0)$ and $(i,1)$ for s
ome $i$, in either order). $E_n$ is the expected number of adjacent indices.

**Linearity of expectation.** Let $X_k = \mathbf{1}[k\text{ is adjacent}]$. Then
$$E_n = \sum_{k=1}^{2n-1} \mathbb{E}[X_k] = \sum_{k=1}^{2n-1} \Pr[X_k = 1].$$

**Computing $\Pr[X_k=1]$.** In a uniformly random permutation of $2n$ distinct e
lements, the pair $(\varphi(k), \varphi(k+1))$ is a uniformly random ordered pai
r of distinct elements. The total number of such ordered pairs is $(2n)(2n-1)$.

The favorable ordered pairs $(a,b)$ with $a\neq b$ and $\pi_0(a)=\pi_0(b)$: for
each $i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$, the two elements $(i,0),(i,1)$ give exactly $2$ order
ed pairs, namely $((i,0),(i,1))$ and $((i,1),(i,0))$. Hence there are $2n$ favor
able pairs.

Therefore
$$\Pr[X_k = 1] = \frac{2n}{(2n)(2n-1)} = \frac{1}{2n-1},$$
independent of $k$.

**Result.**
$$E_n = (2n-1)\cdot\frac{1}{2n-1} = 1.$$

This holds for every $n\geq 1$ (verified: $n=1$ gives the single pair $(1,0),(1,
1)$ which is always adjacent, so $E_1=1$; $n=2$ gives $E_2 = 3\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}=
1$). Since $E_n=1$ for all $n$,

$$\limsup_{n\to\infty} E_n = 1.$$
