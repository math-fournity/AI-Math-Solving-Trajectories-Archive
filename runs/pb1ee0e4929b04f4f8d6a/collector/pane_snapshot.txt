## Setup and Separation of Variables

The PDE is the axisymmetric Helmholtz (Laploltz) equation in spherical coordinat
es $(\rho, \phi)$, with no $\theta$-dependence:

$$\frac{1}{\rho^2}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial \rho}\!\left(\rho^2 \frac{\part
ial u}{\partial \rho}\right) + \frac{1}{\sin\phi}\frac{\partial}{\partial \phi}\
!\left(\sin\phi\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial \phi}\right)\right] = -\lambda^2 u.$
$

This is $\nabla^2 u = -\lambda^2 u$ on the **upper hemisphere** $\{0<\rho<a,\; 0
<\phi<\pi/2\}$ with Dirichlet conditions on the spherical cap ($\rho=a$) and the
 equatorial disk ($\phi=\pi/2$).

Set $u(\rho,\phi) = R(\rho)\,\Phi(\phi)$ and substitute:

$$\frac{1}{\rho^2 R}\frac{d}{d\rho}\!\left(\rho^2 R'\right) + \frac{1}{\rho^2\si
n\phi\,\Phi}\frac{d}{d\phi}\!\left(\sin\phi\,\Phi'\right) = -\lambda^2.$$

Multiply by $\rho^2$ and separate with constant $\mu$:

$$\underbrace{\frac{1}{R}\frac{d}{d\rho}\!\left(\rho^2 R'\right) + \lambda^2 \rh
o^2}_{\text{radial}} = -\underbrace{\frac{1}{\sin\phi\,\Phi}\frac{d}{d\phi}\!\le
ft(\sin\phi\,\Phi'\right)}_{=\,\mu} = \mu.$$

## Angular Equation

$$\frac{1}{\sin\phi}\frac{d}{d\phi}\!\left(\sin\phi\,\Phi'\right) + \mu\,\Phi =
0, \qquad 0<\phi<\pi/2.$$

Substitute $x = \cos\phi$ (so $dx/d\phi = -\sin\phi$, and $\phi=0 \Rightarrow x=
1$, $\phi=\pi/2 \Rightarrow x=0$). A standard computation gives

$$\frac{d}{dx}\!\left[(1-x^2)\frac{d\Phi}{dx}\right] + \mu\,\Phi = 0,$$

which is **Legendre's equation**. Boundedness at $\phi=0$ ($x=1$) excludes $Q_\e
ll$ (singular at $x=1$) and forces $\mu = \ell(\ell+1)$ with $\ell \in \{0,1,2,\
dots\}$, yielding $\Phi(\phi) = P_\ell(\cos\phi)$.

The boundary condition $\Phi(\pi/2)=0$ becomes $P_\ell(0)=0$. Using the explicit
 values

$$P_0(0)=1,\quad P_1(0)=0,\quad P_2(0)=-\tfrac12,\quad P_3(0)=0,\quad P_4(0)=\tf
rac38,\;\dots$$

and the parity property $P_\ell(-x)=(-1)^\ell P_\ell(x)$ (so $P_\ell(0)=0$ iff $
\ell$ is odd), we obtain

$$\boxed{\ell = 2n+1,\qquad n = 0,1,2,\dots}$$

with angular eigenvalues $\mu_n = (2n+1)(2n+2)$ and eigenfunctions $\Phi_n(\phi)
 = P_{2n+1}(\cos\phi)$.

## Radial Equation

$$\frac{1}{\rho^2}\frac{d}{d\rho}\!\left(\rho^2 R'\right) + \left[\lambda^2 - \f
rac{\ell(\ell+1)}{\rho^2}\right]R = 0, \qquad 0<\rho<a.$$

This is the **spherical Bessel equation**. Its general solution is

$$R(\rho) = A\,j_\ell(\lambda\rho) + B\,y_\ell(\lambda\rho),$$

where $j_\ell, y_\ell$ are spherical Bessel functions of the first and second ki
nd. Boundedness at $\rho=0$ forces $B=0$ (since $y_\ell$ is singular at the orig
in). Thus $R(\rho) = j_\ell(\lambda\rho)$.

The boundary condition $R(a)=0$ gives

$$j_\ell(\lambda a) = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \lambda a = \alpha_{\ell,k},$$

where $\alpha_{\ell,k}$ is the $k$-th positive zero of $j_\ell$ ($k=1,2,3,\dots$
).

## Orthogonality and Completeness

The eigenfunctions are mutually orthogonal on the upper hemisphere with weight $
\rho^2\sin\phi$:

$$\int_0^a\!\!\int_0^{\pi/2} u_{n,k}\,u_{n',k'}\,\rho^2\sin\phi\,d\phi\,d\rho =
0 \quad\text{for }(n,k)\neq(n',k'),$$

because the angular parts $P_{2n+1}$ are orthogonal on $[0,\pi/2]$ (by symmetry
of Legendre polynomials on $[0,\pi]$ restricted to odd $\ell$) and the radial pa
rts $j_{2n+1}$ are orthogonal on $[0,a]$ with weight $\rho^2$ (standard Sturm–Li
ouville theory for the spherical Bessel equation). Completeness follows from the
 completeness of the Legendre polynomials (odd subset) and the spherical Bessel
zeros, giving a complete orthonormal basis for $L^2$ of the upper hemisphere.
