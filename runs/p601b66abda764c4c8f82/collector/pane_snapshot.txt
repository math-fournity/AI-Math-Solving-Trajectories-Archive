**Proof that the space of connections modulo gauge equivalence on a compact
four-manifold is paracompact in the Sobolev topology.**

Let me organize this cleanly.

Setup:
- M: compact, closed, oriented 4-manifold.
- P → M: principal G-bundle, G compact Lie group.
- A = A^{k,2}(P): space of L^{k,2} connections, k ≥ 2 (so that connections are
continuous and the gauge group acts smoothly).
- G = G^{k+1,2}(P): L^{k+1,2} gauge group.
- M = A/G: moduli space (quotient).

The topology on A is the L^{k,2} Sobolev topology (induced from the Hilbert
space structure).

**Step 1: A is a separable Hilbert space (affine).**
A is an affine space modeled on L^{k,2}(Ω^1(M, ad P)), which is a separable
Hilbert space (M compact ⇒ L^{k,2} is separable). Hence A is second-countable,
metrizable, and paracompact.

**Step 2: The quotient map π: A → M is open.**
The gauge group G acts on A by homeomorphisms (in fact, smooth maps). For any
group action by homeomorphisms, the quotient map is open.

**Step 3: M is second-countable.**
The open image of a second-countable space is second-countable. (If {U_i} is a
countable base for A, then {π(U_i)} is a countable family of open sets in M that
forms a base, since π is open and continuous.)

**Step 4: M is Hausdorff.**
This is the key step. We need to show the orbit relation R = {(A', B') ∈ A × A :
∃g ∈ G, B' = g·A'} is closed in A × A.

Suppose (A_n, B_n) ∈ R with A_n → A and B_n → B in L^{k,2}. So B_n = g_n · A_n
for some g_n ∈ G. We must show A ~ B.

Since A_n → A in L^{k,2} (k ≥ 2), F(A_n) → F(A) in L^{k-1,2}. By Sobolev
embedding on the 4-manifold (L^{k-1,2} ↪ L^p for appropriate p > 2 when k ≥ 2),
||F(A_n)||_{L^p} is uniformly bounded for some p > 2. Similarly, ||F(B_n)||
_{L^p} = ||g_n F(A_n) g_n^{-1}||_{L^p} = ||F(A_n)||_{L^p} is uniformly bounded
(G compact ⇒ conjugation preserves the L^p norm).

By Uhlenbeck's weak compactness theorem (for p > 2 = dim M / 2), there exist
gauge transformations h_n (on M \ {x_1,...,x_N}) such that h_n · A_n converges
in L^{1,p}_{loc}(M \ {x_1,...,x_N}) to a connection A_∞. Similarly, there exist
k_n such that k_n · B_n converges in L^{1,p}_{loc}(M \ {y_1,...,y_M}) to B_∞.

On M \ ({x_i} ∪ {y_j}), both h_n · A_n → A_∞ and k_n · B_n → B_∞, and h_n · A_n
~ k_n · B_n (gauge equivalent) for all n. By the local uniqueness of Coulomb
gauge (or by the fact that the gauge group action on L^{1,p} connections with
bounded curvature has closed orbit relation locally), A_∞ and B_∞ are gauge
equivalent on M \ ({x_i} ∪ {y_j}).

By the removable singularities theorem for connections with bounded L^p
curvature (p > 2, Uhlenbeck), A_∞ and B_∞ extend to connections on all of M, and
the gauge equivalence extends across the singular points. Moreover, A_∞ is gauge
equivalent to A (since h_n · A_n → A_∞ and A_n → A, the limiting connection A_∞
is in the gauge orbit of A). Similarly, B_∞ is gauge equivalent to B.

Therefore A ~ B, proving R is closed, and M is Hausdorff. ∎

**Step 5: M is completely regular (T_{3.5}).**
Since M is Hausdorff, it is T1, so every orbit O(A') = π^{-1}([A']) is closed in
A. Let [A] ∈ M and F ⊂ M closed with [A] ∉ F. Then:
- O(A) is closed in A (T1).
- π^{-1}(F) is closed in A (continuity of π).
- O(A) ∩ π^{-1}(F) = ∅.

Since A is a metric space, define:
- f(x) = d(x, O(A)) (distance to the orbit, ≥ 0, = 0 iff x ∈ O(A)).
- h(x) = d(x, π^{-1}(F)) (≥ 0, = 0 iff x ∈ π^{-1}(F)).

Both f and h are continuous and G-invariant (since O(A) and π^{-1}(F) are G-
invariant/saturated). The function:

φ(x) = f(x) / (f(x) + h(x))

is continuous, G-invariant, with φ = 0 on O(A) and φ = 1 on π^{-1}(F) (note: f +
h > 0 everywhere since O(A) and π^{-1}(F) are disjoint closed sets).

φ descends to a continuous function φ̃: M → [0,1] with φ̃([A]) = 0 and φ̃|_F = 1.
The open sets φ̃^{-1}([0,1/2)) and φ̃^{-1}((1/2,1]) separate [A] and F. Hence M is
completely regular (T_{3.5}).

**Step 6: M is metrizable, hence paracompact.**
M is second-countable (Step 3), Hausdorff (Step 4), and completely regular (Step
5). By the Urysohn metrization theorem (every second-countable regular Hausdorff
space is metrizable), M is metrizable. By Stone's theorem (every metric space is
paracompact), M is paracompact. ∎
