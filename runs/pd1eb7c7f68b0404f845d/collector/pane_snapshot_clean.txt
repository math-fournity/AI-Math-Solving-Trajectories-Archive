## Proof

**Graph-theoretic formulation.** Model the network as a graph $G$ on $n$ vertice
s where edges represent direct data links. The two protocols become:

1. Any two **adjacent** vertices have **0** common neighbors ($\lambda = 0$).
2. Any two **non-adjacent** vertices have **exactly 2** common neighbors ($\mu =
 2$).

We seek the smallest $n > 4$ for which such a graph exists.

---

**Step 1: The graph must be regular.**

Let $d(v)$ denote the degree of vertex $v$, and let $A$ be the adjacency matrix.
 The conditions on common neighbors give us, for the entries of $A^2$:

$$(A^2)_{ij} = \begin{cases} d(i) & \text{if } i = j \\ 0 & \text{if } i \sim j
\\ 2 & \text{if } i \not\sim j,\, i \neq j \end{cases}$$

This yields the matrix equation $A^2 = D + 2(J - I - A)$, where $D = \operatorna
me{diag}(d(1),\ldots,d(n))$ and $J$ is the all-ones matrix. Multiplying both sid
es by the all-ones vector $\mathbf{1}$:

$$(A + I)\mathbf{d} = 2(n-1)\mathbf{1}$$

where $\mathbf{d} = (d(1),\ldots,d(n))^T$. This means for every vertex $v$:

$$d(v) + \sum_{u \sim v} d(u) = 2(n-1) =: c.$$

Let $\Delta = \max_v d(v)$ and $\delta = \min_v d(v)$. For a vertex $v$ with $d(
v) = \Delta$:

$$c = \Delta + \sum_{u \sim v} d(u) \geq \Delta + \Delta \cdot \delta = \Delta(1
 + \delta).$$

For a vertex $u$ with $d(u) = \delta$:

$$c = \delta + \sum_{w \sim u} d(w) \leq \delta + \delta \cdot \Delta = \delta(1
 + \Delta).$$

Combining: $\Delta(1+\delta) \leq c \leq \delta(1+\Delta)$, which simplifies to
$\Delta \leq \delta$. Since $\Delta \geq \delta$ by definition, we conclude $\De
lta = \delta$: **the graph is regular** with every vertex having degree $k$.

**Step 2: Derive the parameter relation.**

Since the graph is regular of degree $k$, counting paths of length 2 from any ve
rtex $v$: each of the $k$ neighbors contributes $k-1$ other neighbors (since $\l
ambda = 0$, no neighbor is shared). The remaining $n - 1 - k$ non-neighbors each
 receive exactly $\mu = 2$ paths. So:

$$k(k - 1) = 2(n - k - 1) \implies n = \frac{k^2 + k + 2}{2}. \tag{$\star$}$$

**Step 3: Eigenvalue analysis forces $k - 1$ to be a perfect square.**

The graph is a strongly regular graph $\operatorname{srg}(n, k, 0, 2)$. Its non-
trivial eigenvalues are:

$$r, s = \frac{(\lambda - \mu) \pm \sqrt{(\lambda-\mu)^2 + 4(k-\mu)}}{2} = \frac
{-2 \pm \sqrt{4 + 4(k-2)}}{2} = -1 \pm \sqrt{k-1}.$$

**Case A: $k - 1$ is a perfect square.** Then $r$ and $s$ are integers, and the
multiplicities are integers (verified below). Set $k - 1 = t^2$ for a positive i
nteger $t$, so $k = t^2 + 1$ and:

$$n = \frac{(t^2+1)^2 + (t^2+1) + 2}{2} = \frac{t^4 + 3t^2 + 4}{2}.$$

ac{n^2 - n + 2}{2}$, so $n^2 - 3n + 2 = 0$, yielding $n \in \{1, 2\}$. Neither e

**Step 4: Find the smallest $n > 4$.**
