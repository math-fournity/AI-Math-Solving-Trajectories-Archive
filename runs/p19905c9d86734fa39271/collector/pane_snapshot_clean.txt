## Proof

**Setup.** Let $u_0$ be a positive, radially symmetric (even), nontrivial soluti
on of

$$-\tfrac{1}{2}\,u_0'' + \lambda\, u_0 - u_0^3 = 0, \qquad \lambda > 0,$$

on $\mathbb{R}$. Such a solution is the standard soliton/ground state, which dec
ays exponentially: $u_0(x),\, u_0'(x) \to 0$ as $|x|\to\infty$.

The linearized operator around $u_0$ is

$$L\,u = -\tfrac{1}{2}\,u'' + \lambda\, u - 3\,u_0^2\, u.$$

We claim $L\,(u_0^2) = -3\lambda\, u_0^2$.

---

**Step 1: First integral of the ODE.**

Rewrite the equation for $u_0$ as $u_0'' = 2\lambda\, u_0 - 2\,u_0^3$. Multiply
both sides by $u_0'$:

$$u_0'\, u_0'' = 2\lambda\, u_0\, u_0' - 2\,u_0^3\, u_0'.$$

Integrating:

$$\tfrac{1}{2}(u_0')^2 = \lambda\, u_0^2 - \tfrac{1}{2}\,u_0^4 + C.$$

Since $u_0$ and $u_0'$ both vanish at infinity, $C = 0$. Thus

$$(u_0')^2 = 2\lambda\, u_0^2 - u_0^4. \tag{1}$$

---

**Step 2: Compute $L(u_0^2)$.**

Using the product rule and the ODE $u_0'' = 2\lambda\, u_0 - 2\,u_0^3$:

$$(u_0^2)'' = 2\,(u_0')^2 + 2\,u_0\, u_0'' = 2\,(u_0')^2 + 4\lambda\, u_0^2 - 4\
,u_0^4.$$

Now apply $L$:

$$L(u_0^2) = -\tfrac{1}{2}(u_0^2)'' + \lambda\, u_0^2 - 3\,u_0^4$$

$$= -\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[2\,(u_0')^2 + 4\lambda\, u_0^2 - 4\,u_0^4\bigr] + \lambda
\, u_0^2 - 3\,u_0^4$$

$$= -(u_0')^2 - 2\lambda\, u_0^2 + 2\,u_0^4 + \lambda\, u_0^2 - 3\,u_0^4$$

$$= -(u_0')^2 - \lambda\, u_0^2 - u_0^4.$$

Substituting the first integral (1), $(u_0')^2 = 2\lambda\, u_0^2 - u_0^4$:

$$L(u_0^2) = -(2\lambda\, u_0^2 - u_0^4) - \lambda\, u_0^2 - u_0^4 = -2\lambda\,
 u_0^2 + u_0^4 - \lambda\, u_0^2 - u_0^4 = -3\lambda\, u_0^2.$$

---

**Step 3: Validity of the eigenfunction.**

Since $u_0$ is nontrivial and positive, $u_0^2 \not\equiv 0$. Since $u_0$ decays
 exponentially, so does $u_0^2$, and $(u_0^2)''$ is continuous and decays; hence
 $u_0^2 \in H^2(\mathbb{R}) \cap L^2(\mathbb{R})$, the domain of $L$.

---

**Conclusion.** We have shown $L\,(u_0^2) = -3\lambda\, u_0^2$ with $u_0^2 \not\
equiv 0$, so $-3\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $L$ with corresponding eigenfunctio
n $u_0^2$. $\blacksquare$
