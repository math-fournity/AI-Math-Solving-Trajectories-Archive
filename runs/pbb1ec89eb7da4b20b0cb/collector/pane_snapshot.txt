## Proof

**Key tool — Chebyshev's integral inequality.** If $f, g : [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}$
 are both non-decreasing and integrable, then
$$\int_0^1 f(x)g(x)\,dx \;\geq\; \left(\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx\right)\!\left(\int_0^1
g(x)\,dx\right),$$
with equality if and only if one of $f, g$ is constant a.e.

*Proof of the inequality:* Since $f$ and $g$ are both non-decreasing, for any $x
, y \in [0,1]$,
$$(f(x)-f(y))(g(x)-g(y)) \geq 0.$$
Integrating over $[0,1]^2$:
$$0 \leq \iint (f(x)-f(y))(g(x)-g(y))\,dx\,dy = 2\int_0^1 fg\,dx - 2\left(\int_0
^1 f\,dx\right)\!\left(\int_0^1 g\,dx\right).$$
Equality holds iff $(f(x)-f(y))(g(x)-g(y))=0$ a.e., which (for non-decreasing fu
nctions) forces one to be constant a.e. $\square$
