## Solution

**Step 1: Find AQ via Ptolemy's Theorem.**

Since Q lies on the circumcircle of △ABC, the quadrilateral ABQC is cyclic (with
 vertices in order A, B, Q, C around the circle). By Ptolemy's theorem:

$$AB \cdot QC + BQ \cdot AC = AQ \cdot BC$$

$$12 \cdot 10 + 6 \cdot 12 = AQ \cdot 12$$

$$120 + 72 = 12 \cdot AQ \implies AQ = 16$$

**Step 2: Find the ratio BD : DC.**

Since Q is on the circumcircle, inscribed angles subtending the same arc are equ
al:
- ∠BAQ = ∠BCQ (both subtend arc BQ)
- ∠QAC = ∠QBC (both subtend arc QC)

Using the cevian ratio formula with AB = AC = 12:

$$\frac{BD}{DC} = \frac{AB \cdot \sin\angle BAQ}{AC \cdot \sin\angle QAC} = \fra
c{\sin\angle BCQ}{\sin\angle QBC}$$

By the Law of Sines in △BQC:

$$\frac{\sin\angle BCQ}{\sin\angle QBC} = \frac{QB}{QC} = \frac{6}{10} = \frac{3
}{5}$$

So $BD : DC = 3 : 5$. With $BD + DC = BC = 12$:

$$BD = \frac{9}{2}, \qquad DC = \frac{15}{2}$$

**Step 3: Apply Power of a Point.**

Point D lies inside the circumcircle (on chord BC), and line AQ passes through D
. By the power of a point theorem (intersecting chords):

$$DA \cdot DQ = DB \cdot DC = \frac{9}{2} \cdot \frac{15}{2} = \frac{135}{4}$$

Since A is above line BC and Q is on the arc below BC, point D lies between A an
d Q on segment AQ, so:

$$DA + DQ = AQ = 16$$

**Step 4: Solve for QD.**

Let $DQ = x$, so $DA = 16 - x$. Then:

$$x(16 - x) = \frac{135}{4}$$

$$64x - 4x^2 = 135 \implies 4x^2 - 64x + 135 = 0$$

$$x = \frac{64 \pm \sqrt{4096 - 2160}}{8} = \frac{64 \pm \sqrt{1936}}{8} = \frac
{64 \pm 44}{8}$$

$$x = \frac{108}{8} = \frac{27}{2} \quad \text{or} \quad x = \frac{20}{8} = \fra
c{5}{2}$$

Since Q lies on the minor arc BC (close to side BC) while A is far from BC (at h
eight $6\sqrt{3}$), the distance from D to Q is smaller than the distance from D
 to A. Therefore $DQ < DA$, giving:

$$QD = \frac{5}{2}$$
