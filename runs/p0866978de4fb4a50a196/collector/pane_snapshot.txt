## Solution

**Example.** Let $X$ be the middle-thirds Cantor set $C \subset [0,1]$.

$C$ is a closed subset of the compact interval $[0,1]$, hence compact; it is met
rizable as a subspace of $\mathbb{R}$; and it is uncountable, so in particular i
t has at least two points. We claim $C^2 \cong C^3$ (indeed $C^n \cong C$ for ev
ery $n \geq 1$).

---

**Step 1: $C \cong \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$.**

Every $x \in C$ admits a ternary expansion $x = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} d_k 3^{-k}$
with each $d_k \in \{0,2\}$ (the middle-thirds construction removes exactly thos
e numbers whose ternary expansion requires the digit $1$). The map
$$\varphi : C \longrightarrow \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}, \qquad \varphi(x) = (d_1/2,\
, d_2/2,\, d_3/2, \dots)$$
is a bijection (the only ambiguity, dyadic-rational-style endpoints, is resolved
 consistently by the construction). It is continuous: convergence in $C$ forces
agreement on longer and longer initial segments of the ternary digits, which is
exactly coordinatewise convergence in $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ (with $\{0,1\}$ dis
crete). Since $C$ is compact and $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ is Hausdorff, a continuo
us bijection is a homeomorphism. Thus
$$C \cong \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}.$$

**Step 2: $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}} \times \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}} \cong \{0,1\}^{\math
bb{N}}$.**

Define the interleaving map
$$\Phi : \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}} \times \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}} \longrightarrow \{0,1\
}^{\mathbb{N}}, \qquad \Phi\big((a_1,a_2,\dots),\, (b_1,b_2,\dots)\big) = (a_1,
b_1, a_2, b_2, a_3, b_3, \dots).$$

This is a bijection, with inverse given by splitting a sequence into its odd- an
d even-indexed coordinates. It is continuous (coordinate functions are projectio
ns onto factors, which are continuous) with continuous inverse (each coordinate
of each factor is a coordinate projection of the interleaved sequence). Hence $\
Phi$ is a homeomorphism. The same argument with $n$-way interleaving gives
$$\big(\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\big)^{n} \cong \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}} \qquad \text{for
 every } n \geq 1.$$

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 1 and 2,
$$C^{2} \cong \big(\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\big)^{2} \cong \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}} \con
g C, \qquad C^{3} \cong \big(\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\big)^{3} \cong \{0,1\}^{\mathb
b{N}} \cong C.$$

Therefore
$$\boxed{C^{2} \cong C^{3}}$$

via the composite homeomorphism $C^{2} \xrightarrow{\;\varphi\times\varphi\;} \{
0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\times\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}} \xrightarrow{\;\Phi\;} \{0,1\}^{\ma
thbb{N}} \xrightarrow{\;\varphi^{-1}\;} C \xrightarrow{\;\varphi^{-1}\circ(\varp
hi\times\varphi\times\varphi)^{-1}\;} \cdots \cong C^{3}$, i.e. by chaining the
interleaving homeomorphism with the coordinatewise Cantor-space identification.

The middle-thirds Cantor set $C$ is thus a compact metric space with (uncountabl
y) more than two points satisfying $C^{2} \cong C^{3}$.
