## Proof

**Answer: No, such a continuous function $g$ need not exist.**

We provide a counterexample.

Let $X = \mathbb{R}$ with the usual topology (a locally compact Hausdorff space)
 and let $\mu$ be Lebesgue measure (a Radon measure). Define

$$f(x) = \begin{cases} 0, & x < 0, \\ 1, & x > 0, \\ 0, & x = 0. \end{cases}$$

This is the Heaviside step function (with value $0$ at the origin). It is contin
uous at every point of $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$ and discontinuous at $\{0\}$
. Setting $N = \{0\}$, we have $\mu(N) = 0$, so the hypotheses are satisfied.

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a continuous function $g \colon \m
athbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ with $f = g$ on $\mathbb{R} \setminus N = \mathbb{R} \s
etminus \{0\}$.

- For every $x > 0$, $g(x) = f(x) = 1$. Since $g$ is continuous and $(0,\infty)$
 is dense near $0$ from the right, $g(0) = \lim_{x \to 0^+} g(x) = 1$.
- For every $x < 0$, $g(x) = f(x) = 0$. Since $g$ is continuous and $(-\infty, 0
)$ is dense near $0$ from the left, $g(0) = \lim_{x \to 0^-} g(x) = 0$.

This gives $g(0) = 1$ and $g(0) = 0$, a contradiction. Hence no such continuous
$g$ exists.

**Justification.** The obstruction is that continuity of $f$ on $X \setminus N$
does not control the behavior of $f$ near points of $N$. Even though $N$ is $\mu
$-null, points of $N$ may be limit points of $X \setminus N$ where the left- and
 right-hand limits of $f$ disagree (or fail to exist). A continuous $g$ agreeing
 with $f$ on $X \setminus N$ would be forced—by density of $X \setminus N$ near
such points—to take conflicting values, which is impossible. The measure-theoret
ic condition $\mu(N) = 0$ is a *size* condition on $N$ and says nothing about th
e *topological* behavior of $f$ near $N$; in particular it does not guarantee th
at $f$ admits a continuous extension across $N$.
