## Proof

Let $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$ be a convergent series with $a_n > 0$ for all $n$. W
e prove that $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n^{n/(n+1)}$ converges.

**Key observation.** Since $0 < \frac{n}{n+1} < 1$, the map $x \mapsto x^{n/(n+1
)}$ is increasing and concave on $(0,\infty)$. For $0 < x < 1$ we have $x^{n/(n+
1)} > x$, so direct comparison with $a_n$ is insufficient. We instead **split th
e index set** according to the threshold $2^{-n}$ and bound each part separately
.

Define
$$
S_1 = \{n \ge 1 : a_n \le 2^{-n}\}, \qquad S_2 = \{n \ge 1 : a_n > 2^{-n}\}.
$$

**Part 1: terms with $a_n \le 2^{-n}$ (i.e. $n \in S_1$).**

Since $x \mapsto x^{n/(n+1)}$ is increasing,
$$
a_n^{n/(n+1)} \le (2^{-n})^{n/(n+1)} = 2^{-n^2/(n+1)}.
$$
Writing $n^2/(n+1) = n - \frac{n}{n+1} = n - 1 + \frac{1}{n+1}$, we get
$$
2^{-n^2/(n+1)} = 2^{-n+1-1/(n+1)} = 2 \cdot 2^{-n} \cdot 2^{-1/(n+1)} \le 2 \cdo
t 2^{-n}.
$$
Hence
$$
\sum_{n \in S_1} a_n^{n/(n+1)} \le \sum_{n \in S_1} 2 \cdot 2^{-n} \le 2\sum_{n=
1}^\infty 2^{-n} = 2 < \infty.
$$

**Part 2: terms with $a_n > 2^{-n}$ (i.e. $n \in S_2$).**

Factor
$$
a_n^{n/(n+1)} = a_n \cdot a_n^{-1/(n+1)}.
$$
Since $a_n > 2^{-n} > 0$ and $x \mapsto x^{-1/(n+1)}$ is decreasing,
$$
a_n^{-1/(n+1)} < (2^{-n})^{-1/(n+1)} = 2^{n/(n+1)} \le 2^1 = 2.
$$
Therefore
$$
a_n^{n/(n+1)} < 2\, a_n \qquad (n \in S_2),
$$
and so
$$
\sum_{n \in S_2} a_n^{n/(n+1)} \le 2 \sum_{n \in S_2} a_n \le 2 \sum_{n=1}^\inft
y a_n < \infty.
$$

**Combining.** Every index belongs to exactly one of $S_1, S_2$, so
$$
\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n^{n/(n+1)} = \sum_{n \in S_1} a_n^{n/(n+1)} + \sum_{n \in S
_2} a_n^{n/(n+1)} \le 2\sum_{n=1}^\infty 2^{-n} + 2\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n < \inft
y.
$$
Since each $a_n^{n/(n+1)} > 0$, the partial sums are increasing and bounded abov
e, hence the series converges. $\blacksquare$
