## Proof

Let $T: \ell^p \to \ell^p$ be defined by $Tx = (c_1 x_1, c_2 x_2, \ldots)$, wher
e $(c_k) \subset \mathbb{C}$ and $\lim_{k\to\infty} c_k = 0$. We claim

$$\|T\| = \sup_{k \ge 1} |c_k|.$$

Since $c_k \to 0$, the sequence $(c_k)$ is bounded, so $M := \sup_{k \ge 1} |c_k
| < \infty$. Moreover, since $c_k \to 0$, the supremum is attained: there exists
 $k_0$ with $|c_{k_0}| = M$ (a convergent sequence attains its supremum on a fin
ite initial segment).

---

### Upper bound: $\|T\| \le M$

**Case $1 \le p < \infty$.** For any $x = (x_k) \in \ell^p$,

$$\|Tx\|_p^p = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} |c_k x_k|^p \le M^p \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} |x_k|
^p = M^p \|x\|_p^p.$$

Hence $\|Tx\|_p \le M\|x\|_p$, giving $\|T\| \le M$.

**Case $p = \infty$.** For any $x \in \ell^\infty$,

$$\|Tx\|_\infty = \sup_k |c_k x_k| \le M \sup_k |x_k| = M\|x\|_\infty,$$

so $\|T\| \le M$.

---

### Lower bound: $\|T\| \ge M$

Let $k_0$ be an index with $|c_{k_0}| = M$. Let $e_{k_0} = (0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldot
s)$ be the standard basis vector with $1$ in position $k_0$. Then $\|e_{k_0}\|_p
 = 1$ for every $1 \le p \le \infty$, and

$$Te_{k_0} = (0,\ldots,0,c_{k_0},0,\ldots), \qquad \|Te_{k_0}\|_p = |c_{k_0}| =
M.$$

Therefore

$$\|T\| \ge \frac{\|Te_{k_0}\|_p}{\|e_{k_0}\|_p} = M.$$

---

### Conclusion

Combining both bounds,

$$\boxed{\|T\| = \sup_{k \ge 1} |c_k|.}$$

**Remark.** The condition $c_k \to 0$ guarantees that $T$ maps $\ell^p$ into $\e
ll^p$ (for $p<\infty$, the tail $\sum_{k>N}|c_k x_k|^p$ is controlled since $|c_
k|\to 0$; for $p=\infty$ it is immediate). It also guarantees $M<\infty$ and tha
t the supremum is attained, which makes the lower-bound argument via a single ba
sis vector clean. Even without attainment, one could approximate: pick $k_n$ wit
h $|c_{k_n}|\to M$ and use $e_{k_n}$ to get $\|T\|\ge M$ in the limit.
