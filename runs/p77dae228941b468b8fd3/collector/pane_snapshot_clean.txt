## Proof

Let $K = \mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3}, \omega)$ where $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$. This is the
 Galois closure of $\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3})$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, with $\operatorname{
Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_3$. The unique quadratic subfield of $K$ is $\mathbb{
Q}(\omega) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$.

Let $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m})$. We seek primes $p$ that (i) split completely in
$F$ and (ii) are inert in $\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3})$.

**Frobenius characterization of inertness.** The subfield $\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3}) \
subset K$ is fixed by a subgroup $H \leq S_3$ of order $2$ (generated by complex
 conjugation, a transposition). The coset space $S_3/H$ has $3$ elements. For an
 unramified prime $p$, the Frobenius conjugacy class $\mathcal{C}_p \subset S_3$
 acts on $S_3/H$ by left multiplication:

- $\mathcal{C}_p = \{e\}$: $3$ fixed points → $p$ splits completely (three degre
e-1 primes).
- $\mathcal{C}_p$ = a transposition: one fixed point + one 2-cycle → $p$ splits
as degree-1 + degree-2.
- $\mathcal{C}_p$ = a 3-cycle: one 3-cycle → **$p$ is inert** (single prime of r
esidue degree 3).

The 3-cycles $\{(123),(132)\}$ form a single conjugacy class of size $2$ in $S_3
$.

### Case 1: $m = -3$ (so $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}) = \mathbb{Q}(\omega) \subset
 K$)

Here $FK = K$. The subfield $F = \mathbb{Q}(\omega)$ corresponds to the subgroup
 $A_3 = \{e, (123), (132)\} \leq S_3$ (index 2). A prime $p$ (unramified, i.e. $
p \nmid 6$) splits completely in $F$ iff $\operatorname{Frob}_p \in A_3$.

Since every 3-cycle lies in $A_3$, the combined condition

$$\text{split completely in } \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}) \;\text{AND}\; \text{inert i
n } \mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3})$$

is equivalent to $\operatorname{Frob}_p \in \{(123),(132)\}$.

By the Chebotarev density theorem:

$$\delta = \frac{|\{(123),(132)\}|}{|S_3|} = \frac{2}{6} = \boxed{\frac{1}{3}}.$
$

### Case 2: $m \neq -3$ (so $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m}) \not\subset K$)

Since the only quadratic subfield of $K$ is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$ and $F \neq
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$, we have $F \cap K = \mathbb{Q}$. Thus $F$ and $K$ are li
nearly disjoint over $\mathbb{Q}$, and the compositum $L = FK$ satisfies

$$\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \times
 \operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_3 \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},$$

of order $12$. Under this isomorphism, $\operatorname{Frob}_p = (\sigma, \tau)$
with $\sigma \in S_3$, $\tau \in \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, and the two conditions
 decouple:

- **Split completely in $F$:** $\tau = e$ (identity in $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$)
.
- **Inert in $\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3})$:** $\sigma$ is a 3-cycle in $S_3$.

The number of elements $(\sigma, \tau) \in S_3 \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ sa
tisfying both is

$$|\{\text{3-cycles in } S_3\}| \times |\{e\}| = 2 \times 1 = 2.$$

By Chebotarev:

$$\delta = \frac{2}{12} = \boxed{\frac{1}{6}}.$$

### Conclusion

The Dirichlet density of primes $p$ that split completely in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m

$$\delta = \begin{cases} \dfrac{1}{3} & \text{if } m = -3, \\[6pt] \dfrac{1}{6}
& \text{if } m \neq -3 \text{ (} m \text{ square-free, non-zero).} \end{cases}$$
