## Proof

Let $\mathbb{D} = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| < 1\}$ and let $S = \{re^{i\theta} :
0 \le r < 1,\; \pi/4 < \theta < \pi/3\}$ be the given sector. The hypothesis sta
tes that $f(z) \to 0$ uniformly as $|z| \to 1^-$ with $z \in S$.

### Step 1: Radial limits vanish on an arc of positive measure

The uniform convergence to $0$ in $S$ means: for every $\varepsilon > 0$, there
exists $r_0 < 1$ such that $|f(re^{i\theta})| < \varepsilon$ for all $r \in (r_0
, 1)$ and all $\theta \in (\pi/4, \pi/3)$. In particular, for **every** $\theta
\in (\pi/4, \pi/3)$, the radial limit satisfies
$$f^*(e^{i\theta}) := \lim_{r \to 1^-} f(re^{i\theta}) = 0.$$

Set $E = \{e^{i\theta} : \theta \in (\pi/4, \pi/3)\} \subset \partial\mathbb{D}$
. Then $E$ has Lebesgue measure $|E| = \pi/12 > 0$, and $f^* = 0$ on all of $E$.

### Step 2: Existence of boundary values (Fatou's Theorem)

Since $f$ is bounded and holomorphic on $\mathbb{D}$ (i.e., $f \in H^\infty(\mat
hbb{D})$), **Fatou's theorem** guarantees that $f$ has non-tangential (hence rad
ial) limits $f^*(e^{i\theta})$ for almost every $\theta \in [0, 2\pi)$, with $f^
* \in L^\infty(\partial\mathbb{D})$ and $\|f^*\|_\infty \le \|f\|_\infty$.

From Step 1, $f^*(e^{i\theta}) = 0$ for all $\theta \in (\pi/4, \pi/3)$, so $f^*
 = 0$ on a set of positive measure.

### Step 3: Uniqueness theorem for $H^\infty$ functions

**Claim.** If $f \in H^\infty(\mathbb{D})$ and $f^* = 0$ on a set of positive Le
besgue measure on $\partial\mathbb{D}$, then $f \equiv 0$.

*Proof of Claim.* Suppose for contradiction that $f \not\equiv 0$. Let $m \ge 0$
 be the order of the zero of $f$ at the origin (with $m = 0$ if $f(0) \ne 0$), a
nd set
$$g(z) = \frac{f(z)}{z^m}.$$
Then $g \in H^\infty(\mathbb{D})$, $g \not\equiv 0$, $g(0) \ne 0$, and $g^* = 0$
 on $E$ (since $|z^m| = 1$ on $\partial\mathbb{D}$).

The function $u(z) = \log|g(z)|$ is **subharmonic** on $\mathbb{D}$: it equals $
-\infty$ at the (isolated) zeros of $g$ and is subharmonic elsewhere. By the sub
harmonic mean value property, for every $0 < r < 1$:
$$\log|g(0)| \le \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \log|g(re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta. \ta
g{1}$$

Set $M = \|g\|_\infty$. Then $\log|g(re^{i\theta})| \le \log M$, so the function
s
$$h_r(\theta) = \log M - \log|g(re^{i\theta})| \ge 0$$
are non-negative and measurable. By Fatou's theorem, $g(re^{i\theta}) \to g^*(e^
{i\theta})$ for a.e. $\theta$ as $r \to 1^-$, hence $h_r(\theta) \to \log M - \l
og|g^*(e^{i\theta})|$ a.e. By **Fatou's lemma**:
$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\bigl(\log M - \log|g^*(e^{i\theta})|\bigr)\,d\thet
a \;\le\; \liminf_{r\to 1^-}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\bigl(\log M - \log|g(re^
{i\theta})|\bigr)\,d\theta.$$

Rearranging (noting $\int h_r = 2\pi\log M - \int \log|g_r|$):
$$\limsup_{r \to 1^-}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \log|g(re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta
\;\le\; \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \log|g^*(e^{i\theta})|\,d\theta. \tag{2}$$

Combining (1) and (2):
$$\log|g(0)| \;\le\; \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \log|g^*(e^{i\theta})|\,d\theta
. \tag{3}$$

But $g^* = 0$ on $E$, which has positive measure, so $\log|g^*(e^{i\theta})| = -
\infty$ on $E$, making the right-hand side of (3) equal to $-\infty$. This **con
tradicts** $\log|g(0)| > -\infty$ (since $g(0) \ne 0$). $\square$

### Step 4: Conclusion

By the Claim, $f \equiv 0$ on $\mathbb{D}$. Since $f$ is identically zero on the
 open disk, its boundary values (wherever they exist) are also zero, so $f$ is i
dentically zero on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$. $\blacksquare$
