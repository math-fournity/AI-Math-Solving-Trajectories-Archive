## Proof

**Degree constraint.** Let $n = \deg P$ and $m = \deg Q$. Then $n^2 = 2m$, so $n

t{c})^2$, done. Hence assume $n \geq 2$.

**Factorization over $\mathbb{C}$.** Write $P(x) = a\prod_{j=1}^{s}(x - \gamma_j
)^{r_j}$ where $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_s$ are the distinct roots of $P$ (in $\ma

$$P(P(x)) = a\prod_{j=1}^{s}\bigl(P(x) - \gamma_j\bigr)^{r_j} = Q(x)^2.$$

**Coprimality.** For $i \neq j$, any common root $\alpha$ of $P(x)-\gamma_i$ and
 $P(x)-\gamma_j$ would satisfy $\gamma_i = P(\alpha) = \gamma_j$, a contradictio
n. So the polynomials $P(x)-\gamma_j$ are pairwise coprime.

**Consequence of the perfect-square condition.** Since $a\prod_j (P(x)-\gamma_j)
^{r_j}$ is a perfect square in $\mathbb{C}[x]$ and the factors are pairwise copr
ime, for every $j$ with $r_j$ **odd**, the polynomial $P(x) - \gamma_j$ must its
elf be a perfect square in $\mathbb{C}[x]$.

**Non-real roots cannot have odd multiplicity.** Suppose $\gamma_j$ is non-real
and $r_j$ is odd. Then $P(x) - \gamma_j = S_j(x)^2$ for some $S_j \in \mathbb{C}
[x]$, giving $P(x) = S_j(x)^2 + \gamma_j$. Substituting:

$$P(P(x)) = S_j(P(x))^2 + \gamma_j = Q(x)^2,$$

so $(Q(x) - S_j(P(x)))(Q(x) + S_j(P(x))) = \gamma_j \neq 0$. Both factors are no

$. Hence **every non-real root of $P$ has even multiplicity**.

**Real roots with odd multiplicity force $P$ to be a square.** Suppose $\gamma$
is a real root of $P$ with odd multiplicity. Then $P(x) - \gamma$ has real coeff

$$P(P(x)) = S(P(x))^2 + \gamma = Q(x)^2 \implies (Q - S\circ P)(Q + S\circ P) =
\gamma.$$

- **Case B:** $P(x) - \gamma = -T(x)^2$ for some $T \in \mathbb{R}[x]$. Then $P(

$$Q(x)^2 + T(P(x))^2 = \gamma \quad\text{(a constant)}.$$
