## Proof

The principal branch of the complex arccosine is

$$\arccos(z) = -i\,\mathrm{Log}\!\bigl(z + i\sqrt{1-z^{2}}\bigr),$$

where $\sqrt{\cdot}$ and $\mathrm{Log}$ denote the principal square root and pri
ncipal logarithm. Its branch cuts lie on the real axis for $|x|>1$.

**Restriction to the real segment $(-1,1)$.** For real $z=x\in(-1,1)$, we have $
1-x^{2}>0$, so $\sqrt{1-x^{2}}$ is real and positive. Hence $x+i\sqrt{1-x^{2}}$
is a point on the upper unit circle (since $|x+i\sqrt{1-x^{2}}|^{2}=x^{2}+1-x^{2
}=1$), with argument $\theta\in(0,\pi)$ satisfying $\cos\theta=x$. Therefore

$$\arccos(x)=-i\,\mathrm{Log}\!\bigl(e^{i\theta}\bigr)=-i\cdot i\theta=\theta=\a
rccos(x),$$

which is **real**, so $\operatorname{Im}(\arccos(z))=0$ for every $z$ on this se
gment. Thus approaching $-1$ "from a direction where the imaginary part of $\arc
cos(z)$ is zero" means taking $z=x\to -1^{+}$ along the real interval $(-1,1)$.

**Computing the limit.** On $(-1,1)$ the principal $\arccos$ agrees with the rea
l arccosine, which is continuous and strictly decreasing from $\pi$ (at $x=-1$)
to $0$ (at $x=1$). Therefore

$$\lim_{z\to -1,\ \operatorname{Im}(\arccos z)=0}\arccos(z)=\lim_{x\to -1^{+}}\a
rccos(x)=\pi.$$

**Direct verification via the formula.** As $x\to -1^{+}$, $\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\to 0^
{+}$, so $x+i\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\to -1+i\cdot 0^{+}$, i.e. the point $-1$ approached
from the upper half-plane. Its argument tends to $\pi$ (not $-\pi$), so $\mathrm
{Log}\to i\pi$, giving $\arccos\to -i\cdot i\pi=\pi$. This confirms the value.

i}$$
