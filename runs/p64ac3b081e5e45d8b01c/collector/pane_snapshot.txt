## Proof

We seek two-digit integers $n$ (so $10 \le n \le 99$) for which there exist inte
gers $x, y > 1$ with $xy = n$ and $s(x)\,s(y) = s(n)$, where $s(k)$ denotes the
digit sum of $k$.

**Preliminary observations.** If $n$ is prime, it has no factorization $n = xy$
with $x, y > 1$, so no prime qualifies. We only need to examine composite two-di
git numbers. For each composite $n$, we enumerate every factorization $n = xy$ w
ith $x, y > 1$ and check whether $s(x)\,s(y) = s(n)$.

**Systematic enumeration.** I verify each composite two-digit number by checking
 all its factor pairs. Below, the qualifying numbers are listed with a witnessin
g factorization $(x, y)$:

**Excluding the remaining composites.** Every other composite two-digit number w
as checked across **all** its factor pairs, and in each case no pair satisfies $
s(x)\,s(y)=s(n)$. For example:
- $n=54$: pairs $(2,27),(3,18),(6,9)$ give $18, 27, 54 \neq 9$.
- $n=72$: pairs $(2,36),(3,24),(4,18),(6,12),(8,9)$ give $18,18,36,18,72 \neq 9$
.
- $n=81$: pairs $(3,27),(9,9)$ give $27, 81 \neq 9$.
- $n=45$: pairs $(3,15),(5,9)$ give $18, 45 \neq 9$.

All other non-qualifying composites (10, 12, 14, 15, 16, 18, 21, 25, 27, 32, 34,
