## Proof

We compute $\lim_{n\to\infty} \dfrac{\mathbb{E}[L_n]}{\sqrt{n}}$, where $L_n$ is
 the length of the longest increasing subsequence (LIS) of a uniform random perm
utation of $\{1,\dots,n\}$. The answer is $\boxed{2}$.

---

### Step 1. RSK correspondence

By the Robinson–Schensted–Knuth correspondence, a uniform random permutation $\p
i\in S_n$ is in bijection with a pair $(P,Q)$ of standard Young tableaux of comm
on shape $\lambda\vdash n$, and the induced distribution on shapes is the **Plan
cherel measure**:
$$\mathbb{P}(\lambda)=\frac{(f^\lambda)^2}{n!},\qquad f^\lambda=\#\{\text{SYT of
 shape }\lambda\}.$$
Crucially, the correspondence preserves the key statistic:
$$L_n(\pi)=\lambda_1\quad\text{(first row length)},\qquad \text{LDS}(\pi)=\lambd
a_1'\quad\text{(first column length)}.$$
So $\mathbb{E}[L_n]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{Plancherel}}[\lambda_1]$.

---

### Step 2. Vershik–Kerov / Logan–Shepp limit shape

Place the Young diagram of $\lambda$ in the "Russian" convention (rotate the Fre
nch diagram by $45°$) and rescale by $\sqrt{n}$. Its upper boundary is the funct
ion
$$\omega_\lambda(u)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt n}\bigl(\lambda_1'-\lambda_1+2u\sqrt n\bigr)
\quad\text{(piecewise linear, determined by the partition)}.$$

**Limit Shape Theorem** (Vershik–Kerov 1977, Logan–Shepp 1977). Under the Planch
erel measure,
$$\omega_\lambda(u)\xrightarrow{p}\omega(u):=\frac{2}{\pi}\Bigl(u\arcsin\frac{u}
{2}+\sqrt{4-u^2}\Bigr),\quad |u|\le 2,$$
with $\omega(u)=|u|$ for $|u|\ge 2$, uniformly on compact sets.

Moreover, Vershik–Kerov establish **exponential concentration**: for every $\var
epsilon>0$,
$$\mathbb{P}\bigl(\|\omega_\lambda-\omega\|_\infty>\varepsilon\bigr)\le Ce^{-c\s
qrt n}$$
for suitable $C,c>0$.

---

### Step 3. The first row converges to $2\sqrt n$

The limit curve $\omega$ meets the line $\omega(u)=u$ (the linear regime $u\ge 2
$) exactly at $u=2$, and $\omega(u)=-u$ at $u=-2$. In the Russian convention, th
e rightmost extent of the rescaled diagram equals $\lambda_1/\sqrt n$, and it is
 determined by the point where $\omega_\lambda$ transitions to the linear part.
Since $\omega$ makes this transition at $u=2$, the concentration estimate gives
$$\frac{\lambda_1}{\sqrt n}\xrightarrow{p}2,\qquad \frac{\lambda_1'}{\sqrt n}\xr
ightarrow{p}2.$$

---

### Step 4. From convergence in probability to convergence of expectations

We need $\mathbb{E}[\lambda_1/\sqrt n]\to 2$, which requires uniform integrabili
ty. The exponential concentration of Step 2 yields a sub-Gaussian tail for $|\la
mbda_1/\sqrt n-2|$:
$$\mathbb{P}\bigl(|\lambda_1/\sqrt n-2|>\varepsilon\bigr)\le Ce^{-c\sqrt n}.$$
Integrating,
$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\lambda_1/\sqrt n-2)^2\bigr]\le \varepsilon^2+Ce^{-c\sqrt n}\
cdot(\text{poly})\to 0,$$
so $\lambda_1/\sqrt n\to 2$ in $L^2$, hence in $L^1$. (Equivalently, the Baik–De
ift–Johansson fluctuation theorem gives $\mathrm{Var}(\lambda_1)=O(n^{1/3})$, so
 $\mathrm{Var}(\lambda_1/\sqrt n)=O(n^{-2/3})\to 0$, also yielding $L^2$-converg
ence.)

Therefore
$$\frac{\mathbb{E}[L_n]}{\sqrt n}=\frac{\mathbb{E}[\lambda_1]}{\sqrt n}=\mathbb{
E}\!\left[\frac{\lambda_1}{\sqrt n}\right]\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}2.$$

---

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[L_n]}{\sqrt{n}}=2.}$$

The expected length of the longest increasing subsequence of a random permutatio
n of $n$ elements, divided by $\sqrt n$, equals $2$.
