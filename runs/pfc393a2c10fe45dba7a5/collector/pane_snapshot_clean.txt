## Proof

**Yes**, such spaces exist. I give two examples — a trivial one and a nontrivial
 one (where both spaces have more than one point).

### Trivial example

Let $Y = \{*\}$ be a single-point space (which is nonempty), and let $X$ be any
nonempty topological space. The projection map

$$\pi_X : X \times Y \to X, \qquad (x, *) \mapsto x$$

is a bijection (since $Y$ has one element, every $x \in X$ corresponds to exactl
y one point $(x,*)$). It is continuous (projections are always continuous), and
its inverse $x \mapsto (x,*)$ is continuous because for any open set $U \times V
 \subseteq X \times Y$, the preimage under $x \mapsto (x,*)$ is $U$ if $* \in V$
 and $\emptyset$ otherwise — both open. Hence $X \times Y \cong X$.

### Nontrivial example (both spaces have more than one point)

Let $X = \mathbb{N}$ and $Y = \mathbb{N}$, both equipped with the **discrete top
ology**. Both are nonempty and contain more than one point.

The product $X \times Y = \mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}$ with the product topolog
y is again a countable set with the discrete topology (the product of discrete s
paces is discrete: every singleton $\{(n,m)\} = \{n\}\times\{m\}$ is open).

Since $X \times Y$ and $X$ are both countably infinite discrete spaces, any bije
ction between them is a homeomorphism (in the discrete topology every subset is
open, so every bijection is continuous with continuous inverse). In particular,
$\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N} \cong \mathbb{N}$.

Thus $X \times Y \cong X$ with both $X$ and $Y$ nonempty (and even infinite).

### Conclusion

There exist two nonempty topological spaces $X$ and $Y$ such that $X \times Y \c
ong X$. The simplest instance is $Y$ a single point; a nontrivial instance is $X
 = Y = \mathbb{N}$ with the discrete topology.
