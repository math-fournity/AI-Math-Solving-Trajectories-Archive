## Proof

Let $V$ be the variety of all left zero semigroups, defined by the identity
$$
xy \approx x \qquad \text{for all } x, y.
$$

The language $\mathcal{L}_s$ is the language of semigroups: a single binary oper
ation symbol (written multiplicatively). An $\mathcal{L}_s$-term $t(X)$ over a s
et of variables $X$ is built recursively from variables in $X$ using this binary
 operation.

**Claim.** Every $\mathcal{L}_s$-term $t(X)$ is equivalent over $V$ to its leftm
ost variable — that is, to the variable occurring at the leftmost leaf of the te
rm tree when the term is written in standard infix notation.

**Proof by structural induction on $t$.**

*Base case.* If $t = x_i$ for some variable $x_i \in X$, then $t$ is already a s
ingle variable, and its leftmost variable is $x_i$ itself. The claim holds trivi
ally.

*Inductive step.* Suppose $t = t_1 \cdot t_2$ where $t_1, t_2$ are $\mathcal{L}_
s$-terms, and suppose by induction that $t_1 \equiv_V x$ and $t_2 \equiv_V y$, w
here $x$ is the leftmost variable of $t_1$ and $y$ is the leftmost variable of $
t_2$. Since $V$ satisfies $uv \approx u$ for all $u, v$, every algebra in $V$ sa
tisfies
$$
t_1 \cdot t_2 \;=\; t_1.
$$
Hence $t = t_1 \cdot t_2 \equiv_V t_1 \equiv_V x$. Since the leftmost variable o
f $t = t_1 \cdot t_2$ is precisely the leftmost variable of $t_1$, namely $x$, t
he claim holds.

By induction, the claim holds for all $\mathcal{L}_s$-terms.

**Conclusion.** For any $\mathcal{L}_s$-term $t(X)$ over $V$,
$$
t(X) \;\equiv_V\; x,
$$
where $x$ is the leftmost variable appearing in $t(X)$. That is, every term coll
apses over the variety of left zero semigroups to its first (leftmost) variable,
 because the defining identity $xy \approx x$ discards the entire right subterm
at each multiplication node.
