## Answer: Yes

**Proof.**

Let $(x_n)_{n\geq 1}$ be a Schauder basis of the real Banach space $X$ with $\|x
_n\|=1$, and let $(x_n^*)_{n\geq 1}\subset X^*$ be the biorthogonal functionals,
 $x_m^*(x_n)=\delta_{mn}$. Denote the partial-sum projections
$$
S_N x \;=\; \sum_{n=1}^{N} x_n^*(x)\,x_n , \qquad N\geq 1.
$$

**Step 1 — Uniform boundedness of $(S_N)$.**
For every $x\in X$ the series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_n^*(x)x_n$ converges to $x$,
 so $\sup_N \|S_N x\|<\infty$ for each $x$. By the Uniform Boundedness Principle
,
$$
M \;:=\; \sup_{N\geq 1}\|S_N\| \;<\;\infty.
$$

**Step 2 — Uniform boundedness of $(x_n^*)$.**
Since $x_n^*(x)\,x_n = S_n x - S_{n-1}x$ (with $S_0=0$),
$$
|x_n^*(x)|\,\|x_n\| \;\le\; \|S_n x\|+\|S_{n-1}x\| \;\le\; 2M\|x\|.
$$
Using $\|x_n\|=1$,
$$
\|x_n^*\| \;\le\; 2M \qquad\text{for all } n\geq 1. \tag{1}
$$

**Step 3 — Pointwise convergence $x_n^*(x)\to 0$.**
For each $x\in X$, the expansion $x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_n^*(x)x_n$ converges, s
o its general term tends to $0$: $\|x_n^*(x)x_n\|\to 0$. Because $\|x_n\|=1$, th
is gives
$$
x_n^*(x)\to 0 \qquad\text{for every } x\in X. \tag{2}
$$

**Step 4 — Equicontinuity of $\{x_n^*\}$.**
By (1), for all $n$ and all $x,y\in X$,
$$
|x_n^*(x)-x_n^*(y)| \;\le\; \|x_n^*\|\,\|x-y\| \;\le\; 2M\,\|x-y\|. \tag{3}
$$
Thus the family $\{x_n^*\}$ is equi-Lipschitz (hence equicontinuous) on $X$.

**Step 5 — Uniform convergence on the compact set $K$.**
Let $\varepsilon>0$. If $M=0$ then all $x_n^*=0$ and the claim is trivial, so as
sume $M>0$.

Since $K$ is compact, it is totally bounded: there exist $y_1,\dots,y_m\in K$ su
ch that
$$
K \;\subset\; \bigcup_{j=1}^{m} B\!\left(y_j,\,\tfrac{\varepsilon}{4M}\right). \
tag{4}
$$
By the pointwise convergence (2), for each $j\in\{1,\dots,m\}$ there is $N_j$ wi
th $|x_n^*(y_j)|\le \varepsilon/2$ for all $n\ge N_j$. Set
$$
n_0 \;:=\; \max_{1\le j\le m} N_j.
$$

Now take any $x\in K$ and $n\ge n_0$. By (4) choose $j$ with $\|x-y_j\|<\varepsi
lon/(4M)$. Using (3) and the choice of $n_0$,
$$
|x_n^*(x)| \;\le\; |x_n^*(x)-x_n^*(y_j)| + |x_n^*(y_j)|
\;\le\; 2M\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{4M} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}
\;=\; \frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
\;=\; \varepsilon.
$$

This holds for every $x\in K$ and every $n\ge n_0$, which is exactly the desired
 statement. $\blacksquare$
