## Proof

Let $N_t$ be a Poisson process with rate $\lambda > 0$, and let $\mathcal{F}_s =
 \sigma(N_s)$ with $0 \le s < t$.

**Step 1: Decompose using independent increments.**

By the independent-increments property of the Poisson process,
$$N_t = N_s + (N_t - N_s),$$
where $N_t - N_s \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda(t-s))$ and is **independent** of $\
mathcal{F}_s = \sigma(N_s)$.

Rewrite the centered quantity:
$$N_t - \lambda t = \bigl(N_s - \lambda s\bigr) + \bigl(N_t - N_s - \lambda(t-s)
\bigr).$$

**Step 2: Identify the $\mathcal{F}_s$-measurable part.**

The first summand $N_s - \lambda s$ is $\mathcal{F}_s$-measurable (it is a deter
ministic function of $N_s$). Hence, viewed as a random variable conditioned on $
\mathcal{F}_s$, it is a constant and contributes nothing to the conditional vari
ance:
$$\operatorname{Var}\!\bigl[N_s - \lambda s \mid \mathcal{F}_s\bigr] = 0.$$

**Step 3: Handle the independent part.**

The second summand $N_t - N_s - \lambda(t-s)$ is independent of $\mathcal{F}_s$.
 A standard property of conditional variance states: if $Y$ is independent of $\
mathcal{F}_s$, then
$$\operatorname{Var}[Y \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = \operatorname{Var}[Y] \quad \text{a
.s.}$$

Therefore,
$$\operatorname{Var}\!\bigl[N_t - N_s - \lambda(t-s) \mid \mathcal{F}_s\bigr] =
\operatorname{Var}[N_t - N_s] = \lambda(t-s),$$
using $\operatorname{Var}[\text{Poisson}(\mu)] = \mu$ with $\mu = \lambda(t-s)$.

**Step 4: Combine via conditional variance of a sum.**

For $X = A + B$ with $A$ being $\mathcal{F}_s$-measurable (hence a constant unde
r conditioning), the conditional variance is additive:
$$\operatorname{Var}[X \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = \operatorname{Var}[A \mid \mathcal{
F}_s] + \operatorname{Var}[B \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = 0 + \lambda(t-s).$$

(Formally, $\operatorname{Var}[A+B\mid\mathcal{F}_s] = \mathbb{E}[(A+B)^2\mid\ma
thcal{F}_s] - (\mathbb{E}[A+B\mid\mathcal{F}_s])^2$; with $A$ $\mathcal{F}_s$-me
asurable and $B$ independent, the cross terms cancel and we get $\operatorname{V
ar}[B\mid\mathcal{F}_s]$.)

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Var}\!\bigl[N_t - \lambda t \mid \mathcal{F}_s\bigr] = \l
ambda(t-s).}$$

This is consistent with the intuition that, given the information up to time $s$
, the only remaining randomness comes from the increment over $(s,t]$, which is
an independent Poisson variable of variance $\lambda(t-s)$.
