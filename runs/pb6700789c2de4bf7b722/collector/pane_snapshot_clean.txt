## Solution

Let $Z(t)$ be a standard Brownian motion with $Z(0)=0$. We want $P(Z(3) > Z(2) >
 0)$.

**Using the independent increments property of Brownian motion.**

Define the increments:
- $X = Z(2) - Z(0) = Z(2)$, so $X \sim N(0, 2)$.
- $Y = Z(3) - Z(2)$, so $Y \sim N(0, 1)$.

By the defining property of Brownian motion, non-overlapping increments are **in
dependent**, so $X$ and $Y$ are independent.

Now express the event in terms of $X$ and $Y$:
$$Z(2) = X, \qquad Z(3) = Z(2) + Y = X + Y.$$

The event $\{Z(3) > Z(2) > 0\}$ becomes:
$$\{X + Y > X > 0\} = \{Y > 0 \text{ and } X > 0\}.$$

Since $X$ and $Y$ are independent:
$$P(Y > 0 \text{ and } X > 0) = P(Y > 0)\cdot P(X > 0).$$

Both $X \sim N(0,2)$ and $Y \sim N(0,1)$ are centered (mean-zero) Gaussian, henc
e symmetric about $0$:
$$P(X > 0) = \tfrac{1}{2}, \qquad P(Y > 0) = \tfrac{1}{2}.$$

Therefore:
$$P(Z(3) > Z(2) > 0) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \boxed{\frac{1}{4}}.$$
