Looking at this problem, I need to find the probability of ending with 4 socks o
f different colors.

## Setup

5 colors, 2 socks each = 10 socks total. Draw 4; discard any complete pairs (per
manently removed); draw replacements from the bag. Repeat until bag is empty or
4 different colors are held.

**Key insight:** When a complete pair is discarded, that color is *eliminated* —
 both socks are gone forever. If 2 or more colors are eliminated, at most 3 colo
rs remain available, making 4 different colors impossible (pigeonhole).

## Round 1: Draw 4 from 10

Total ways: $\binom{10}{4} = 210$.

- **4 different colors:** $\binom{5}{4}\cdot 2^4 = 80$, probability $80/210 = 8/
21$ → **immediate success**.
- **1 pair + 2 singles:** $\binom{5}{1}\binom{4}{2}\cdot 2^2 = 120$, probability
 $120/210 = 4/7$.
- **2 pairs:** $\binom{5}{2} = 10$, probability $10/210 = 1/21$.

## From 2 pairs (probability 1/21)

Hand = $\{a,a,b,b\}$. Discard both pairs → colors $a,b$ eliminated. Only 3 color
s remain in the bag. Any 4 socks from 3 colors must contain a pair (pigeonhole)
→ **can never succeed**. Contributes **0**.

## From 1 pair (probability 4/7)

Hand = $\{a,a,b,c\}$. Discard $\{a,a\}$ → color $a$ eliminated. Hand = $\{b,c\}$
. Bag = $\{b,c,d,d,e,e\}$ (6 socks). Draw 2 from 6: $\binom{6}{2}=15$ ways.

**Success** requires drawing one $d$ and one $e$: $2\cdot 2 = 4$ ways, probabili
ty $4/15$.

**Any other outcome** (11/15) produces at least one pair. Discarding that pair e
liminates a *second* color, leaving only 3 colors → success impossible in all su
bsequent rounds.

So $P(\text{success} \mid \text{1 pair}) = 4/15$.

## Total probability

$$P = \frac{8}{21}\cdot 1 + \frac{4}{7}\cdot\frac{4}{15} + \frac{1}{21}\cdot 0 =
 \frac{8}{21} + \frac{16}{105} = \frac{40}{105} + \frac{16}{105} = \frac{56}{105
} = \boxed{\frac{8}{15}}$$
