## Computation of \(\pi_1(\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus A)\) for a circle \(A\subset\ma
thbb{R}^3\)

**Step 0 — Reduction from \(\mathbb{R}^3\) to \(S^3\).**

Identify \(\mathbb{R}^3 = S^3\setminus\{\infty\}\) with \(\infty\notin A\). Then
\[
\mathbb{R}^3\setminus A \;=\; (S^3\setminus A)\setminus\{\infty\}.
\]
The space \(S^3\setminus A\) is a connected open \(3\)-manifold. Removing a sing
le point from a connected manifold of dimension \(\ge 3\) does not change the fu
ndamental group: any loop \(S^1\to M\) and any homotopy \(S^1\times I\to M\) can
 be perturbed by general position (transversality) to avoid a point, since \(1<3
\) and \(2<3\). Hence
\[
\pi_1(\mathbb{R}^3\setminus A)\;\cong\;\pi_1(S^3\setminus A).
\]
It suffices to compute \(\pi_1(S^3\setminus A)\).

**Step 1 — Setup of the open cover.**

Let \(A\subset S^3\) be the (unknotted) circle. Choose two closed tubular neighb
orhoods \(T_1\subset T_2^\circ\) of \(A\) (a smaller one inside the interior of
a larger one), each a solid torus with core \(A\). Define two open subsets of \(
S^3\setminus A\):
\[
U \;=\; S^3\setminus T_1, \qquad V \;=\; T_2^\circ\setminus A.
\]
- \(U\) is open (complement of the closed set \(T_1\)) and lies in \(S^3\setminu
s A\).
- \(V\) is open in \(S^3\setminus A\).

**Covering check.** Take \(x\in S^3\setminus A\). If \(x\notin T_2^\circ\), then
 \(x\in S^3\setminus T_2\subset S^3\setminus T_1=U\) (since \(T_1\subset T_2\)).
 If \(x\in T_2^\circ\), then \(x\in T_2^\circ\setminus A=V\) (as \(x\notin A\)).
 The boundary \(\partial T_2\subset S^3\setminus T_1=U\) and \(\partial T_1\subs
et T_2^\circ\setminus A=V\). So \(U\cup V=S^3\setminus A\). Both \(U,V\), and \(
U\cap V\) are path-connected.

**Step 2 — The three pieces and their groups.**

*The intersection.*
\[
U\cap V \;=\; (S^3\setminus T_1)\cap(T_2^\circ\setminus A)\;=\;T_2^\circ\setminu
s T_1 \;\simeq\; \partial T_1\;\cong\;S^1\times S^1,
\]
an open annular region between two concentric solid tori, deformation retracting
 to the boundary torus. Let \(\mu\) denote the **meridian** (the small circle bo
unding a disk in \(T_1\)) and \(\lambda\) the **longitude** (parallel to the cor
e \(A\)). Then
\[
\pi_1(U\cap V)=\langle \mu,\lambda\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^2\quad(\text{free abelia
n}).
\]

*The set \(V\).*
\[
V=T_2^\circ\setminus A\;\cong\;(D^2\setminus\{0\})\times S^1\;\simeq\;S^1\times
S^1,
\]
so
\[
\pi_1(V)=\langle \mu,\lambda\mid [\mu,\lambda]\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]

*The set \(U\).* Since \(A\) is unknotted, the complement \(S^3\setminus T_1^\ci
rc\) of the open solid torus is another solid torus (the standard genus-\(1\) He
egaard splitting of \(S^3\)). Thus \(U=S^3\setminus T_1\) is an open solid torus
, deformation retracting onto its core circle (the "dual" circle linking \(A\) o
nce). Hence
\[
\pi_1(U)=\langle a\rangle\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

**Step 3 — The inclusion-induced maps.**

On the common torus \(\partial T_1=\partial(S^3\setminus T_1^\circ)\), the merid
ian \(\mu\) of \(T_1\) is the **longitude** of the complementary solid torus, an
d the longitude \(\lambda\) of \(T_1\) is the **meridian** of the complementary
solid torus. In a solid torus the meridian bounds a disk (hence is killed) and t
he longitude generates \(\pi_1\). Therefore, for the inclusion \(i:U\cap V\hookr
ightarrow U\),
\[
i_*(\mu)=a,\qquad i_*(\lambda)=1.
\]

For the inclusion \(j:U\cap V\hookrightarrow V\), since both \(U\cap V\) and \(V
\) deformation retract onto the same boundary torus \(\partial T_2\) with the sa
me generators,
\[
j_*(\mu)=\mu,\qquad j_*(\lambda)=\lambda.
\]

**Step 4 — Apply van Kampen.**

\[
\pi_1(S^3\setminus A)\;=\;\pi_1(U)*\pi_1(V)\Big/\Big\langle\!\Big\langle\, i_*(g
)\,j_*(g)^{-1}\;:\;g\in\pi_1(U\cap V)\Big\rangle\!\Big\rangle.
\]
Substituting the presentations:
\[
\pi_1(S^3\setminus A)=\big\langle a,\mu,\lambda\;\big|\;[\mu,\lambda],\; a\,\mu^
{-1},\; \lambda\big\rangle.
\]
The relation \(a=\mu\) identifies \(a\) with \(\mu\); the relation \(\lambda=1\)
 kills the longitude; and \([\mu,\lambda]\) becomes trivial once \(\lambda=1\).
What remains is
\[
\pi_1(S^3\setminus A)\;=\;\langle \mu\rangle\;\cong\;\mathbb Z.
\]

**Step 5 — Conclusion.**

By Step 0,
\[
\boxed{\;\pi_1(\mathbb{R}^3\setminus A)\;\cong\;\mathbb Z\;}
\]
generated by the meridian \(\mu\) — a small loop linking the circle \(A\) once.
Intuitively: a loop around the removed circle can be unwound only by passing thr
ough \(A\), which is forbidden, so each linking number gives a distinct homotopy
 class, and these are the only classes.
