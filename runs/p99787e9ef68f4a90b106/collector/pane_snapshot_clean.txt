## Solution

Let $T$ be a distribution on the unit circle $\Gamma$, with Fourier series

$$T(x) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n\, e^{inx}, \qquad c_n = \langle T,\, e^{-
inx}\rangle,$$

where the series converges in the distributional sense (i.e., $\langle T, \varph
i\rangle = \sum_n c_n \langle e^{inx}, \varphi\rangle$ for every smooth test fun
ction $\varphi$ on $\Gamma$).

---

### 1. Translation $T(x-c)$

The translated distribution $T(x-c)$ is defined by its action on a test function
 $\varphi$:

$$\langle T(x-c),\,\varphi(x)\rangle := \langle T(x),\,\varphi(x+c)\rangle.$$

Its $n$-th Fourier coefficient is

$$c_n' = \langle T(x-c),\, e^{-inx}\rangle = \langle T(x),\, e^{-in(x+c)}\rangle
 = e^{-inc}\,\langle T,\, e^{-inx}\rangle = e^{-inc}\, c_n.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{T(x-c) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n\, e^{-inc}\, e^{inx} = \sum_{n=-
\infty}^{\infty} c_n\, e^{in(x-c)}.}$$

Each Fourier coefficient is multiplied by the phase factor $e^{-inc}$.

---

### 2. Reflection $T(-x)$

The reflected distribution $T(-x)$ is defined by

$$\langle T(-x),\,\varphi(x)\rangle := \langle T(x),\,\varphi(-x)\rangle.$$

Its $n$-th Fourier coefficient is

$$c_n'' = \langle T(-x),\, e^{-inx}\rangle = \langle T(x),\, e^{-in(-x)}\rangle
= \langle T(x),\, e^{inx}\rangle = c_{-n}.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{T(-x) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_{-n}\, e^{inx} = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{
\infty} c_n\, e^{-inx}.}$$

Reflection reverses the order of the Fourier coefficients: the coefficient of $e
^{inx}$ in $T(-x)$ is $c_{-n}$, the coefficient of $e^{-inx}$ in $T(x)$.

---

### Summary

| Operation | Fourier coefficients | Fourier series |
|-----------|---------------------|----------------|
| $T(x)$ | $c_n$ | $\sum_n c_n e^{inx}$ |
| $T(x-c)$ | $c_n' = e^{-inc}\,c_n$ | $\sum_n c_n\, e^{in(x-c)}$ |
| $T(-x)$ | $c_n'' = c_{-n}$ | $\sum_n c_{-n}\, e^{inx}$ |

These are the exact distributional analogues of the classical rules for translat
ing and reflecting ordinary Fourier series, and they hold for **all** distributi
ons on $\Gamma$ (not merely integrable functions), since the derivation uses onl
y the definition of distributional action on test functions.
