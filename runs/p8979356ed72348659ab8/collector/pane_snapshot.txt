## Proof

**Claim.** The maximum number of pairwise equidistant points in $\mathbb{R}^n$ (
with pairwise distance $d>0$) is $n+1$.

We prove the upper bound $m \le n+1$ and the lower bound (existence of $n+1$ suc
h points) separately.

---

### Upper bound: $m \le n+1$

Let $p_1, p_2, \dots, p_m \in \mathbb{R}^n$ be pairwise equidistant with $|p_i -
 p_j| = d > 0$ for all $i \ne j$. Translate the configuration so that $p_1 = 0$.
 Define vectors
$$u_i := p_i - p_1 = p_i, \qquad i = 2, \dots, m.$$
These are $m-1$ vectors in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

For each $i \ge 2$: $|u_i|^2 = |p_i|^2 = |p_i - p_1|^2 = d^2$.

For $i \ne j$, $i,j \ge 2$: expanding $|u_i - u_j|^2 = |p_i - p_j|^2 = d^2$ give
s
$$d^2 = |u_i|^2 + |u_j|^2 - 2\langle u_i, u_j\rangle = 2d^2 - 2\langle u_i, u_j\
rangle,$$
so $\langle u_i, u_j\rangle = d^2/2$.

The Gram matrix $G$ of $u_2, \dots, u_m$ has diagonal entries $d^2$ and off-diag
onal entries $d^2/2$:
$$G = \frac{d^2}{2}\bigl(I_{m-1} + \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top\bigr),$$
where $\mathbf{1} \in \mathbb{R}^{m-1}$ is the all-ones vector. The matrix $I +
\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$ has eigenvalue $1$ with multiplicity $m-2$ (eigenvect
ors orthogonal to $\mathbf{1}$) and eigenvalue $1 + (m-1) = m$ (eigenvector $\ma
thbf{1}$). All eigenvalues are positive, so $G$ is positive definite with $\oper
atorname{rank}(G) = m-1$.

Since the rank of the Gram matrix equals the dimension of the span of $u_2, \dot
s, u_m$, these vectors span a subspace of dimension $m-1$. As they all lie in $\
mathbb{R}^n$, we require $m - 1 \le n$, hence
$$m \le n + 1.$$

---

### Lower bound: $n+1$ equidistant points exist in $\mathbb{R}^n$

Consider $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ with standard basis $e_1, \dots, e_{n+1}$. These $n+
1$ points are pairwise equidistant with $|e_i - e_j| = \sqrt{2}$ for all $i \ne
j$. They all lie in the affine hyperplane
$$H = \Bigl\{x \in \mathbb{R}^{n+1} : \sum_{k=1}^{n+1} x_k = 1\Bigr\},$$
which is an $n$-dimensional affine subspace (isometric to $\mathbb{R}^n$). There
fore $n+1$ pairwise equidistant points exist in an $n$-dimensional Euclidean spa
ce — the vertices of the regular $n$-simplex.

---

### Conclusion

The upper bound shows no configuration of more than $n+1$ pairwise equidistant p
oints exists in $\mathbb{R}^n$, and the lower bound exhibits a configuration of
exactly $n+1$. Therefore the maximum number of equidistant points in $n$-dimensi
onal Euclidean space is

$$\boxed{n+1}.$$
