## Proof

**Yes**, such a strong neighborhood always exists.

### Setup and Key Properties

Let $X, Y$ be smooth manifolds and $f: X \to Y$ continuous. Recall that the **st
rong topology** (Whitney $C^0$ topology) on $C^0(X, Y)$ has a basis of neighborh
oods of the form
$$\mathcal{U}_\epsilon = \bigl\{g \in C^0(X, Y) : d\bigl(f(x), g(x)\bigr) < \eps
ilon(x) \text{ for all } x \in X\bigr\},$$
where $\epsilon: X \to \mathbb{R}_{>0}$ is any continuous positive function and
$d$ is a compatible metric on $Y$.

We use two standard facts:

1. **Every smooth manifold is an ANR (absolute neighborhood retract).** This fol
lows because manifolds are locally Euclidean, hence locally contractible, and by
 the Hanner–Dugundji theorem, every locally contractible metrizable space is an
ANR.

2. **Every smooth manifold admits a proper embedding into some $\mathbb{R}^N$**
(Whitney embedding theorem), so $Y$ can be realized as a **closed** subset of $\
mathbb{R}^N$.

### Construction of the Neighborhood

**Step 1: Embed and retract.** Embed $Y$ as a closed subset of $\mathbb{R}^N$ vi
a a proper embedding $i: Y \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}^N$. Since $Y$ is an ANR an
d is closed in $\mathbb{R}^N$, there exists an open neighborhood $U$ of $Y$ in $
\mathbb{R}^N$ and a continuous retraction $r: U \to Y$ (so $r|_Y = \mathrm{id}_Y
$).

**Step 2: Define a continuous "thickness" function on $Y$.** Since $U$ is open a
nd $Y \subset U$, every point $y \in Y$ has positive distance to the complement
$\mathbb{R}^N \setminus U$. Define
$$\delta: Y \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}_{>0}, \qquad \delta(y) = \tfrac{1}{2}\, d
_{\mathbb{R}^N}\!\bigl(y,\; \mathbb{R}^N \setminus U\bigr).$$
This is continuous (distance to a closed set is continuous) and strictly positiv
e on all of $Y$ (since $Y \subset U$ and $U$ is open). Note that $\delta$ may ap
proach $0$; this is harmless for the strong topology.

**Step 3: Pull back to $X$.** Define
$$\epsilon: X \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}_{>0}, \qquad \epsilon(x) = \delta\bigl(
f(x)\bigr).$$
Since $f$ and $\delta$ are continuous, so is $\epsilon$, and $\epsilon > 0$ ever
ywhere.

**Step 4: Define the strong neighborhood.** Using the Euclidean metric $\|\cdot\
|$ on $\mathbb{R}^N$ (which induces a metric on $Y$ compatible with its topology
, and hence gives the same strong topology), set
$$\mathcal{U} = \bigl\{g \in C^0(X, Y) : \|g(x) - f(x)\| < \epsilon(x) \text{ fo
r all } x \in X\bigr\}.$$
This is a strong neighborhood of $f$.

### Verification: Every $g \in \mathcal{U}$ is Homotopic to $f$

Let $g \in \mathcal{U}$. Define the **straight-line homotopy in $\mathbb{R}^N$**
:
$$H: X \times [0,1] \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}^N, \qquad H(x, t) = (1 - t)\, f(x
) + t\, g(x).$$
This is continuous (as $f$, $g$ are continuous and the affine combination is con
tinuous).

**Claim:** $H(x, t) \in U$ for all $(x, t) \in X \times [0,1]$.

*Proof of claim.* For any $x \in X$ and $t \in [0,1]$:
$$\|H(x, t) - f(x)\| = t\,\|g(x) - f(x)\| < t\,\epsilon(x) \leq \epsilon(x) = \d
elta(f(x)) = \tfrac{1}{2}\, d\!\bigl(f(x),\, \mathbb{R}^N \setminus U\bigr).$$
Since $f(x) \in Y \subset U$ and $d(f(x), \mathbb{R}^N \setminus U) > 0$, the po
int $H(x,t)$ lies within distance $< \frac{1}{2}d(f(x), \mathbb{R}^N \setminus U
)$ of $f(x)$, hence lies in the open ball $B\bigl(f(x),\, d(f(x), \mathbb{R}^N \
setminus U)\bigr) \subset U$. $\square$

**Compose with the retraction.** Since $H$ maps into $U$, the composition
$$\widetilde{H} = r \circ H: X \times [0,1] \longrightarrow Y$$
is a well-defined continuous map. We verify the boundary conditions:

- At $t = 0$: $\widetilde{H}(x, 0) = r(f(x)) = f(x)$, since $f(x) \in Y$ and $r$
 is a retraction.
- At $t = 1$: $\widetilde{H}(x, 1) = r(g(x)) = g(x)$, since $g(x) \in Y$ and $r$
 is a retraction.

Thus $\widetilde{H}$ is a homotopy in $Y$ from $f$ to $g$.

### Conclusion

For any continuous $f: X \to Y$ between smooth manifolds, the strong neighborhoo
d $\mathcal{U}$ constructed above has the property that **every** $g \in \mathca
l{U}$ is homotopic to $f$. The essential ingredients are:

- $Y$ is an ANR (being a smooth manifold), providing the retraction $r: U \to Y$
.
- The strong topology permits the "thickness" $\epsilon(x)$ to vary over $X$, so
 even when $\delta$ (the distance from $Y$ to $\partial U$) becomes arbitrarily
small, the neighborhood remains well-defined.
- The straight-line homotopy in the ambient $\mathbb{R}^N$ stays within $U$ by c
onstruction, and the retraction pulls it back to a homotopy in $Y$.

Therefore, **yes**, such a strong neighborhood exists for every continuous $f: X
 \to Y$ between smooth manifolds.
