## Solution

**Setup and Strategy.** The surface $S = S_1 \cup S_2$ consists of:
- $S_1$: the cylinder $x^2+y^2=1$, $0 \le z \le 1$ (lateral surface),
- $S_2$: the upper hemisphere $x^2+y^2+(z-1)^2=1$, $z \ge 1$.

The top rim of $S_1$ (the circle $x^2+y^2=1, z=1$) coincides with the equator of
 $S_2$, so it is an **internal** shared boundary and cancels. The only boundary
of $S$ is the unit circle $\Gamma: x^2+y^2=1, z=0$ on the $xy$-plane, as stated.

By **Stokes' theorem**:
$$\iint_S (\nabla \times F)\cdot dS = \oint_\Gamma F \cdot dr.$$

**Evaluate $F$ on $\Gamma$.** On $\Gamma$, $z=0$, so
$$F(x,y,0) = (zx+z^2y+x,\; z^3yx+y,\; z^4x^2) = (x,\; y,\; 0).$$

**Parameterize $\Gamma$.** Take $r(t) = (\cos t, \sin t, 0)$, $t \in [0, 2\pi]$
(the orientation sign does not matter, as shown below). Then
$$dr = (-\sin t,\; \cos t,\; 0)\,dt, \qquad F(r(t)) = (\cos t,\; \sin t,\; 0).$$

**Compute the line integral:**
$$F \cdot dr = \cos t\cdot(-\sin t) + \sin t\cdot\cos t + 0 = -\cos t\sin t + \s
in t\cos t = 0.$$

Therefore
$$\oint_\Gamma F\cdot dr = \int_0^{2\pi} 0\,dt = 0.$$

**Orientation check.** If we reverse the orientation (clockwise: $r(t)=(\cos t,
-\sin t, 0)$), then $dr = (-\sin t, -\cos t, 0)\,dt$ and $F\cdot dr = \cos t(-\s
in t) + (-\sin t)(-\cos t) = -\cos t\sin t + \sin t\cos t = 0$. The result is **
independent of orientation** since the integrand vanishes identically.

**Verification via direct curl computation.** For completeness:
$$\nabla \times F = \bigl(-3z^2yx,\; x+2zy-2z^4x,\; z^3y-z^2\bigr).$$
On $\Gamma$ ($z=0$) this gives $(0, x, 0)$, consistent with the line integral yi
elding zero (the tangential component of $F$ along $\Gamma$ integrates to zero s
ince $F=(x,y,0)$ is the radial vector field, perpendicular to the tangent of the
 circle everywhere).

**Conclusion.** By Stokes' theorem,
$$\boxed{\iint_S \nabla \times F \cdot dS = 0}.$$
