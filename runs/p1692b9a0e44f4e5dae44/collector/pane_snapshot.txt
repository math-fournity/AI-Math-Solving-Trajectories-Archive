## Solution

We seek $\lim_{N\to\infty} \Pr\!\big[x^2 + y^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{35}\big]$, where $
x,y$ are uniform on $\{1,\dots,N\}$ (natural numbers in $(0,N)$).

### Step 1: Reduce via CRT

Since $35 = 5 \cdot 7$ with $\gcd(5,7)=1$, by the Chinese Remainder Theorem,
$$x^2+y^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{35} \iff \big(x^2+y^2\equiv 0 \pmod 5\big) \wedge \big(
x^2+y^2\equiv 0 \pmod 7\big).$$

As $N\to\infty$, the joint distribution of $(x \bmod 5,\, x \bmod 7)$ becomes un
iform and independent (CRT), so the two events are independent in the limit. Hen
ce
$$P = P_5 \cdot P_7,$$
where $P_p = \Pr[x^2+y^2\equiv 0\pmod p]$ computed over uniform residues mod $p$
.

### Step 2: Compute $P_5$

Quadratic residues mod 5, with multiplicities:
- $0^2\!=0,\; 1^2\!=1,\; 2^2\!=4,\; 3^2\!=4,\; 4^2\!=1$.

So residue $0$ has weight $1$, residue $1$ has weight $2$, residue $4$ has weigh
t $2$.

We need $a+b\equiv 0\pmod 5$ with $a,b\in\{0,1,4\}$:
- $(0,0)$: $0+0=0$ ✓ — weight $1\cdot 1 = 1$
- $(1,4)$: $1+4=5\equiv 0$ ✓ — weight $2\cdot 2 = 4$
- $(4,1)$: $4+1=5\equiv 0$ ✓ — weight $2\cdot 2 = 4$

Total favorable weight $= 1+4+4 = 9$, out of $25$ pairs.
$$P_5 = \frac{9}{25}.$$

### Step 3: Compute $P_7$

Quadratic residues mod 7, with multiplicities:
- $0^2\!=0,\;1^2\!=1,\;2^2\!=4,\;3^2\!=2,\;4^2\!=2,\;5^2\!=4,\;6^2\!=1$.

Residues: $0$ (weight $1$), $1$ (weight $2$), $2$ (weight $2$), $4$ (weight $2$)
.

We need $a+b\equiv 0\pmod 7$ with $a,b\in\{0,1,2,4\}$:
- $(0,0)$: $0+0=0$ ✓ — weight $1\cdot 1 = 1$
- $(1,6)$: $6\notin\{0,1,2,4\}$ ✗
- $(2,5)$: $5\notin\{0,1,2,4\}$ ✗
- $(4,3)$: $3\notin\{0,1,2,4\}$ ✗

Only $(0,0)$ works. Total favorable weight $= 1$, out of $49$ pairs.
$$P_7 = \frac{1}{49}.$$

### Step 4: Combine

$$P = P_5 \cdot P_7 = \frac{9}{25}\cdot\frac{1}{49} = \frac{9}{1225}.$$

### Verification of the limiting argument

For finite $N$, the count of pairs $(x,y)\in\{1,\dots,N\}^2$ with $x^2+y^2\equiv
 0\pmod{35}$ equals $\frac{9}{1225}N^2 + O(N)$, since the residues mod $35$ are
equidistributed up to a boundary error of $O(N)$ per residue class. Dividing by
$N^2$ and taking $N\to\infty$ gives exactly $\frac{9}{1225}$.

### Answer

$$\boxed{\dfrac{9}{1225}}$$
