**Key observations:**

1. Since all points lie on a circle, the convex hull of each color is a polygon
inscribed in the circle. Any point on the circle that is not a vertex of a polyg
on lies strictly outside that polygon (edges are chords, strictly inside the cir
cle). So with probability 1, no vertex of either polygon lies inside the other.

2. Therefore, the vertices of $F$ (the intersection) are exactly the crossing po
ints of blue edges with red edges. Two chords of a circle cross if and only if t
heir four endpoints alternate in color around the circle.

3. The number of such crossings equals $m$ (including $m=0$ when the hulls don't
 overlap, since then no edges cross).

**Counting crossings via arcs:**

Fix one blue point at position 0 (rotational symmetry). The remaining $n-1$ blue
 and $k$ red points are i.i.d. uniform on $[0,1)$. Sorting them gives a uniforml
y random sequence of $n-1$ B's and $k$ R's among $\binom{n+k-1}{n-1}$ equally li
kely sequences.

The $n$ blue points divide the circle into $n$ arcs. Let $r_i$ be the number of
red points in the $i$-th arc, so $(r_1,\ldots,r_n)$ is a weak composition of $k$
 into $n$ parts, uniformly distributed over all $\binom{n+k-1}{n-1}$ such compos
itions.

A blue edge (chord spanning the $i$-th arc) is crossed by a red edge iff the red
 edge has one endpoint on each side of the chord. The red points in arc $i$ form
 a consecutive block, and those outside form another. Exactly **2** red edges co
nnect these two blocks (one at each junction), provided $1 \le r_i \le k-1$. If
$r_i=0$ or $r_i=k$, no red edge crosses.

Therefore:
$$m = \sum_{i=1}^{n} 2\cdot\mathbf{1}[1 \le r_i \le k-1]$$

**Computing the expectation:**

By linearity and symmetry (all $r_i$ identically distributed):
$$E[m] = 2n\cdot P(1 \le r_1 \le k-1) = 2n\bigl(1 - P(r_1=0) - P(r_1=k)\bigr)$$

From the uniform composition model:
$$P(r_1 = 0) = \frac{\binom{k+n-2}{n-2}}{\binom{k+n-1}{n-1}} = \frac{n-1}{n+k-1}
$$
$$P(r_1 = k) = \frac{1}{\binom{k+n-1}{n-1}}$$

Thus:
$$P(1 \le r_1 \le k-1) = \frac{k}{n+k-1} - \frac{1}{\binom{n+k-1}{n-1}}$$

$$E[m] = \frac{2nk}{n+k-1} - \frac{2n}{\binom{n+k-1}{n-1}}$$

Simplifying the second term: $\frac{n}{\binom{n+k-1}{n-1}} = \frac{n!\,k!}{(n+k-
1)!} = \frac{n+k}{\binom{n+k}{n}}$.
