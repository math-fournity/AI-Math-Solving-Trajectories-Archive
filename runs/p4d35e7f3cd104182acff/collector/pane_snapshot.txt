## Gravitational Redshift in Schwarzschild Geometry

**Setup.** The Schwarzschild metric (using geometrized units $G=c=1$) is

$$
ds^2 = -\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)dt^2 + \left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1}dr^2 +
 r^2\,d\Omega^2 .
$$

The two observers $O_1, O_2$ are *static* (fixed spatial coordinates $(r_i,\thet
a_i,\phi_i)$). A static observer at radius $r$ has worldline $x^\mu(\tau) = (t(\
tau), r, \theta, \phi)$ with constant spatial coordinates, so the normalization
$u^\mu u_\mu = -1$ gives

$$
-g_{tt}\,(u^t)^2 = 1 \;\Longrightarrow\; u^t = \left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1/2
}, \qquad u^\mu = \left(\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1/2},\,0,\,0,\,0\right).
$$

**Conserved quantity.** The metric is independent of $t$, so $\xi = \partial_t$
is a Killing vector. For a photon with (null) 4-momentum $k^\mu$, the quantity

$$
E \;:=\; -\,k_\mu\,\xi^\mu \;=\; -\,k_t
$$

is conserved along the photon's geodesic. In particular, $k_t$ takes the *same*
value when the photon is emitted at $O_1$ and when it is absorbed at $O_2$.

**Observed frequency.** The frequency measured by an observer with 4-velocity $u
^\mu$ is

$$
\omega \;=\; -\,k_\mu\,u^\mu .
$$

For a static observer, only $u^t \neq 0$, so

$$
\omega \;=\; -\,k_t\,u^t \;=\; -\,k_t\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1/2}.
$$

Applying this at the two observers:

$$
\omega_1 = -\,k_t\left(1-\frac{2M}{r_1}\right)^{-1/2}, \qquad
\omega_2 = -\,k_t\left(1-\frac{2M}{r_2}\right)^{-1/2}.
$$

**Ratio.** Since $k_t$ is the same conserved value at both events, it cancels:

$$
\boxed{\;\frac{\omega_2}{\omega_1} \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{\,1-2M/r_1\,}{\,1-2M/r_2\,}
}\;}
$$

**Checks and remarks.**

- *Independence of angles:* The result depends only on $r_1, r_2, M$, not on $(\
theta_i,\phi_i)$. This is guaranteed by spherical symmetry: the conserved $k_t$
(energy) is the only quantity entering the static-observer frequency, and it is
independent of the photon's angular trajectory. The angular coordinates of the o
bservers are therefore irrelevant to the redshift — exactly as the problem reque
sts an expression in $r_1, r_2, M$ only.
- *Sign/limiting behavior:* If $r_1 < r_2$ (emitter closer to the mass), then $1
-2M/r_1 < 1-2M/r_2$, so $\omega_2/\omega_1 < 1$: the photon is *redshifted* clim
bing out of the well, as expected. Conversely, light falling inward is blueshift
ed.
- *Weak-field limit:* For $r \gg 2M$, $\sqrt{1-2M/r} \approx 1 - M/r$, giving $\
omega_2/\omega_1 \approx 1 - M/r_1 + M/r_2 = 1 + M(1/r_2 - 1/r_1)$, recovering t
he standard weak-field gravitational redshift $\Delta\omega/\omega \approx M(1/r
_2 - 1/r_1)$.
- *Horizon consistency:* As $r_1 \to 2M$ (emitter approaches the horizon), $1-2M
/r_1 \to 0$, so $\omega_2/\omega_1 \to 0$: signals from the horizon are infinite
ly redshifted, consistent with the known behavior of Schwarzschild.
