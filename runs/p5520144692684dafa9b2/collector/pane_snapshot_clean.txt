## Proof

Let $R = \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_n] \subset \mathbb{C}$, where $\zeta_n = e^{2\pi i/n}$
. We count the unital ring endomorphisms $f \colon R \to R$ (i.e. $\mathbb{Z}$-a
lgebra endomorphisms, since $f(1)=1$ forces $f(m)=m$ for all $m\in\mathbb{Z}$).

**Step 1: $R \cong \mathbb{Z}[x]/(\Phi_n(x))$.**

The element $\zeta_n$ is an algebraic integer whose minimal polynomial over $\ma
thbb{Q}$ is the $n$-th cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_n(x) \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$, whic
h is monic. Hence
$$\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_n] \cong \mathbb{Z}[x]/(\Phi_n(x)).$$

**Step 2: Endomorphisms correspond to roots of $\Phi_n$ in $R$.**

A unital ring endomorphism $f$ of $R$ is $\mathbb{Z}$-linear and is completely d
etermined by $\alpha := f(\zeta_n) \in R$. For $f$ to be well-defined, $\alpha$
must satisfy every polynomial relation satisfied by $\zeta_n$; equivalently (sin
ce $R \cong \mathbb{Z}[x]/(\Phi_n)$), the substitution $x \mapsto \alpha$ must a
nnihilate the ideal $(\Phi_n)$, i.e.
$$\Phi_n(\alpha) = 0.$$
Conversely, any $\alpha \in R$ with $\Phi_n(\alpha)=0$ defines a unique unital r
ing endomorphism via $\zeta_n \mapsto \alpha$. Therefore
$$N_n = \#\{\alpha \in R : \Phi_n(\alpha)=0\}.$$

**Step 3: The roots of $\Phi_n$ in $\mathbb{C}$ are exactly the primitive $n$-th
 roots of unity.**

By definition, $\Phi_n(x) = \prod_{\substack{1\le k \le n \\ \gcd(k,n)=1}} (x -
\zeta_n^k)$, so its roots in $\mathbb{C}$ are precisely $\{\zeta_n^k : \gcd(k,n)
=1\}$, of which there are $\varphi(n)$.

**Step 4: All primitive $n$-th roots of unity lie in $R$.**

For every $k$ with $\gcd(k,n)=1$, the element $\zeta_n^k = \zeta_n^k \in \mathbb
{Z}[\zeta_n] = R$. Hence all $\varphi(n)$ roots of $\Phi_n$ belong to $R$.

**Step 5: No other element of $R$ is a root of $\Phi_n$.**

Since $\Phi_n$ has degree $\varphi(n)$, it has at most $\varphi(n)$ roots in $\m
athbb{C}$ (hence in $R\subset\mathbb{C}$). Step 4 already exhibits $\varphi(n)$
distinct roots in $R$, so there are no further roots.

**Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 2–5,
$$N_n = \varphi(n).$$

Equivalently, every unital ring endomorphism of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_n]$ is of the
form $\zeta_n \mapsto \zeta_n^k$ with $\gcd(k,n)=1$, and these are exactly the $
\varphi(n)$ automorphisms of the cyclotomic extension (Galois conjugates). $\bla
cksquare$
