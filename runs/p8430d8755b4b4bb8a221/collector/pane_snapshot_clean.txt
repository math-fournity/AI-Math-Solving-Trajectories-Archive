Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every measurable antichain
in $2^{\mathbb{N}}$ (with the product measure from cylindrical sets) must have m
easure zero.

**Answer: Yes, $\mu(\mathcal{A}) = 0$.**

## Proof

We identify $2^{\mathbb{N}}$ with $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ via characteristic func
tions. The measure $\mu$ is the product (fair coin) measure: each coordinate is
independent with $P(x_i=0)=P(x_i=1)=\tfrac{1}{2}$. An antichain is a family wher
e no two distinct elements are comparable under coordinatewise ordering ($x \leq
 y$ iff $x_i \leq y_i$ for all $i$).

Let $\mathcal{A}\subseteq\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ be a measurable antichain.

**Fiber decomposition.** For each $n\geq 1$, decompose $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}=\{0
,1\}^n\times\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}_{>n}}$, so $\mu=\mu_n\otimes\mu_{\mathrm{tail}}^
{(n)}$, where $\mu_n$ is the uniform measure on $\{0,1\}^n$. By Fubini's theorem
 (applicable since $\mathcal{A}$ is measurable):

$$\mu(\mathcal{A})=\int_{\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}_{>n}}}\mu_n(\mathcal{A}_t)\,d\mu_{\
mathrm{tail}}^{(n)}(t),$$

where $\mathcal{A}_t=\{s\in\{0,1\}^n:(s,t)\in\mathcal{A}\}$ is the fiber over ta
il $t$.

**Each fiber is an antichain in $\{0,1\}^n$.** Suppose $s_1,s_2\in\mathcal{A}_t$
 with $s_1\leq s_2$ coordinatewise in $\{0,1\}^n$. Then $(s_1,t),(s_2,t)\in\math
cal{A}$ and $(s_1,t)\leq(s_2,t)$ in $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ (the first $n$ coordi
nates satisfy $s_1\leq s_2$; the tails are identical). Since $\mathcal{A}$ is an
 antichain, we must have $(s_1,t)=(s_2,t)$, hence $s_1=s_2$. So $\mathcal{A}_t$
is an antichain in the Boolean lattice $2^{[n]}$.

**Applying Sperner's theorem.** By Sperner's theorem, every antichain in $\{0,1\
}^n$ has size at most $\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$. Therefore:

$$\mu_n(\mathcal{A}_t)=\frac{|\mathcal{A}_t|}{2^n}\leq\frac{\binom{n}{\lfloor n/
2\rfloor}}{2^n}.$$

Substituting into the Fubini formula:

$$\mu(\mathcal{A})\leq\frac{\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}}{2^n}.$$

**Taking $n\to\infty$.** By Stirling's approximation:

$$\frac{\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}}{2^n}\sim\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi n}}\xrightarro
w{n\to\infty}0.$$

Since $\mu(\mathcal{A})\geq 0$ and $\mu(\mathcal{A})\leq\sqrt{2/(\pi n)}$ for ev
ery $n$, we conclude:

$$\boxed{\mu(\mathcal{A})=0.}$$

**Remark.** The argument extends to any non-degenerate product measure ($P(x_i=1
)=p\in(0,1)$) by replacing Sperner's theorem with the weighted LYM inequality, w
hich yields $\mu_n(\mathcal{A}_t)\leq\max_k\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k}\sim\frac{1
}{\sqrt{2\pi np(1-p)}}\to 0$.
