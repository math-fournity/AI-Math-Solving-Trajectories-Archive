## Proof

n$, where $V_i$ sits between $B_i$ and $B_{i+1}$ for $i < n$, and $V_n$ is east
of $B_n$. The adjacency structure forms a path:
$$B_1 - V_1 - B_2 - V_2 - \cdots - B_n - V_n.$$

**Step 1: Unique perfect matching.** The process succeeds iff all $n$ drones dep
osit into distinct valves, i.e., we obtain a perfect matching in this bipartite
graph. Since $B_1$ has degree 1 (only $V_1$), any perfect matching must pair $B_
1 \leftrightarrow V_1$. Then $V_1$ is taken, forcing $B_2 \leftrightarrow V_2$,
and inductively $B_i \leftrightarrow V_i$ for all $i$. So the **unique** perfect
 matching is $B_i \to V_i$ for all $i$.

**Step 2: Any wrong choice dooms the process.** Suppose bay $i \ge 2$ deposits i
nto $V_{i-1}$ (the wrong valve). Then bays $B_1, \ldots, B_{i-1}$ can only acces

-1}, V_j \subseteq \{V_1, \ldots, V_{i-1}\}$). With $V_{i-1}$ already occupied,
these $i-1$ bays must share $i-2$ valves $\{V_1, \ldots, V_{i-2}\}$—impossible b

 make the correct choice**.

So:
- If $B_{i-1}$ was processed **before** $B_i$ (i.e., $\sigma^{-1}(i-1) < \sigma^
{-1}(i)$): $V_{i-1}$ is occupied, bay $i$ is **forced** to $V_i$. Probability 1.
- If $B_{i-1}$ is processed **after** $B_i$ (i.e., $\sigma^{-1}(i) < \sigma^{-1}
(i-1)$): both valves empty, bay $i$ makes a **free choice**, correct with probab

$$p_n = \frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{a(\sigma)},$

$$\sum_{m=1}^{\infty} m^n x^m = \frac{x\, A_n(x)}{(1-x)^{n+1}}, \quad |x| < 1.$$

Setting $x = 1/2$:
