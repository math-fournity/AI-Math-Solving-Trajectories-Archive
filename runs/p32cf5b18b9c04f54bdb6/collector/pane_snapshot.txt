**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

We establish the deduction through a chain of functorial facts.

**Step 1: Abelianization.** For any path-connected space (and more generally, co
mponentwise), the Hurewicz theorem gives a natural isomorphism
$$H_1(X; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \pi_1(X)^{\mathrm{ab}},$$
and under this identification the induced map $H_1(f;\mathbb{Z})$ is exactly the
 abelianization of $\pi_1(f)$. Abelianization is a functor $\mathbf{Grp} \to \ma
thbf{Ab}$, and functors preserve isomorphisms. Since $\pi_1(f)$ is an isomorphis
m, its abelianization
$$H_1(f;\mathbb{Z}): H_1(X;\mathbb{Z}) \to H_1(X;\mathbb{Z})$$
is an isomorphism of abelian groups.

**Step 2: Universal Coefficient Theorem.** For $G = \mathbb{Z}_3$ and $n=1$, the
 UCT gives a natural short exact sequence
$$0 \to H_1(X;\mathbb{Z}) \otimes \mathbb{Z}_3 \to H_1(X;\mathbb{Z}_3) \to \math
rm{Tor}(H_0(X;\mathbb{Z}),\, \mathbb{Z}_3) \to 0.$$
Since $H_0(X;\mathbb{Z})$ is free abelian (a direct sum of copies of $\mathbb{Z}
$, one per path component), $\mathrm{Tor}(H_0(X;\mathbb{Z}), \mathbb{Z}_3) = 0$.
 Hence the first map is an isomorphism:
$$H_1(X;\mathbb{Z}) \otimes \mathbb{Z}_3 \xrightarrow{\;\cong\;} H_1(X;\mathbb{Z
}_3).$$

**Step 3: Naturality.** The UCT is natural in $X$, so for $f: X \to X$ the follo
wing diagram commutes:
$$\begin{array}{ccc}
H_1(X;\mathbb{Z}) \otimes \mathbb{Z}_3 & \xrightarrow{\cong} & H_1(X;\mathbb{Z}_
3) \\[4pt]
\downarrow\, \scriptstyle H_1(f;\mathbb{Z})\,\otimes\,\mathrm{id} & & \downarrow
\, \scriptstyle H_1(f;\mathbb{Z}_3) \\[4pt]
H_1(X;\mathbb{Z}) \otimes \mathbb{Z}_3 & \xrightarrow{\cong} & H_1(X;\mathbb{Z}_
3)
\end{array}$$
Thus $H_1(f;\mathbb{Z}_3)$ is identified, via the two horizontal isomorphisms, w
ith $H_1(f;\mathbb{Z}) \otimes \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{Z}_3}$.

**Step 4: Tensoring preserves isomorphisms.** If $\varphi: A \to B$ is an isomor
phism of abelian groups, then $\varphi \otimes \mathrm{id}_G: A \otimes G \to B
\otimes G$ is an isomorphism with inverse $\varphi^{-1} \otimes \mathrm{id}_G$,
since
$$(\varphi \otimes \mathrm{id})(\varphi^{-1} \otimes \mathrm{id}) = (\varphi\,\v
arphi^{-1}) \otimes \mathrm{id} = \mathrm{id} \otimes \mathrm{id} = \mathrm{id}.
$$
Applying this to $\varphi = H_1(f;\mathbb{Z})$ (an isomorphism by Step 1) and $G
 = \mathbb{Z}_3$, we conclude $H_1(f;\mathbb{Z}) \otimes \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{Z}
_3}$ is an isomorphism.

By the identification in Step 3, $H_1(f;\mathbb{Z}_3)$ is therefore an isomorphi
sm.

**Remark on the hypotheses.** The assumption that $\pi_1(X)$ is non-abelian play
s no role in the argument; the conclusion holds for any $X$ (abelian or non-abel
ian $\pi_1$) whenever $\pi_1(f)$ is an isomorphism. The non-abelian hypothesis i
s a red herring. $\blacksquare$
