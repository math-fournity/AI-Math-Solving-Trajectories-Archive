Looking at this problem, I need to find all functions $f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}$ suc
h that whenever $(x_n)$ has a Cesaro limit, $(f(x_n))$ also has a Cesaro limit.

**Answer: The supercontinuous functions are exactly the affine functions $f(x)=\
alpha x+\beta$.**

## Proof

### Affine functions are supercontinuous

If $f(x)=\alpha x+\beta$, then $\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^n f(x_k)=\alpha\cdot\frac{
1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^n x_k+\beta$. So if the Cesaro limit of $(x_k)$ is $L$, the Cesa
ro limit of $(f(x_k))$ is $\alpha L+\beta$. ∎

### Non-affine functions are not supercontinuous

Suppose $f$ is not affine. Then there exist $a,b\in[0,1]$ and $\lambda\in(0,1)$
such that, setting $c=\lambda a+(1-\lambda)b$,
$$f(c)\neq \lambda f(a)+(1-\lambda)f(b).$$
(Indeed, if $f(\lambda a+(1-\lambda)b)=\lambda f(a)+(1-\lambda)f(b)$ for all $a,
b,\lambda$, then setting $a=1,b=0$ gives $f(x)=xf(1)+(1-x)f(0)$, making $f$ affi
ne — contradiction.) Note $a\neq b$ (otherwise $c=a=b$ and the inequality fails)
.

Let $L_1=\lambda f(a)+(1-\lambda)f(b)$ and $L_2=f(c)$, with $\delta=|L_1-L_2|>0$
.

**Construction of the sequence.** Choose block sizes $N_1,N_2,\dots$ with $N_m/S
_{m-1}\to\infty$, where $S_m=\sum_{j=1}^m N_j$ (e.g., $N_m=2^{2^m}$).

- **Even block $m$** (size $N_m$): all entries equal $c$.
- **Odd block $m$** (size $N_m$): let $p_m=\lfloor\lambda N_m\rfloor$ and $q_m=N
_m-p_m$. Place $p_m$ copies of $a$ and $q_m$ copies of $b$ using the **balanced
arrangement**: at position $k$ within the block, place $a$ iff $\lfloor kp_m/N_m
\rfloor>\lfloor(k-1)p_m/N_m\rfloor$, else $b$.

This balanced arrangement ensures that at position $k$ within the block, the cou
nt of $a$'s is $A_k=\lfloor kp_m/N_m\rfloor$, so the running sum satisfies $|T_k
 - k\cdot c_m|\le|a-b|$, where $c_m=(p_m a+q_m b)/N_m$ is the block's average. S
ince $|c_m-c|\le|a-b|/N_m$, we get $|T_k-ck|\le 2|a-b|$ for all $k$.

**Cesaro limit of $(x_n)$ is $c$.** Let $A_m$ denote the Cesaro average of $(x_n
)$ at the end of block $m$. At position $k$ within block $m$:
$$\left|\text{Cesaro avg}-c\right|=\frac{|A_{m-1}-c|\cdot S_{m-1}+|T_k-ck|}{S_{m
-1}+k}\le|A_{m-1}-c|+\frac{2|a-b|}{S_{m-1}+k}.$$

At block endpoints: for even $m$, $A_m=\frac{A_{m-1}S_{m-1}+c\cdot N_m}{S_m}$, s
o $|A_m-c|=|A_{m-1}-c|\cdot S_{m-1}/S_m\to 0$. For odd $m$, $|A_m-c|\le\frac{|A_
{m-1}-c|S_{m-1}+|a-b|}{S_m}\to 0$. So $|A_m-c|\to 0$, and since $S_m\to\infty$,
the bound above shows the Cesaro average converges to $c$ everywhere. ∎

**Cesaro limit of $(f(x_n))$ does not exist.** Let $B_m$ denote the Cesaro avera
ge of $(f(x_n))$ at the end of block $m$, and $\bar{f}_m$ the $f$-average of blo
ck $m$:
$$B_m=\frac{B_{m-1}S_{m-1}+\bar{f}_m N_m}{S_m}=\bar{f}_m+\frac{(B_{m-1}-\bar{f}_
m)S_{m-1}}{S_m}.$$

For even $m$: $\bar{f}_m=f(c)=L_2$. For odd $m$: $\bar{f}_m=\lambda_m f(a)+(1-\l
ambda_m)f(b)$ where $\lambda_m=p_m/N_m\to\lambda$, so $\bar{f}_m\to L_1$.

Since $S_{m-1}/S_m\to 0$, we get $B_m\to\bar{f}_m$ for large $m$. Thus:
- For large even $m$: $|B_m-L_2|<\delta/2$.
- For large odd $m$: $|B_m-L_1|<\delta/2$.

Since $|L_1-L_2|=\delta>0$, the subsequences $(B_{2m})$ and $(B_{2m-1})$ converg
e to different limits, so $(B_m)$ diverges. Hence $(f(x_n))$ has no Cesaro limit
. ∎

### Conclusion

The supercontinuous functions on $[0,1]$ are precisely the affine functions $f(x
)=\alpha x+\beta$ for constants $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}$.
