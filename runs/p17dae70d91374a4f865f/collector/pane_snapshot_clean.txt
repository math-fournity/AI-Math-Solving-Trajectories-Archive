## Solution

**Claim.** The group of grouplike elements of the Hopf algebra $U(L)$ is the tri
vial group $\{1\}$, for every Lie algebra $L$.

**Setup.** The universal enveloping algebra $U(L)$ carries a Hopf algebra struct
ure defined on generators $x \in L$ by
$$\Delta(x) = x \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes x, \qquad \varepsilon(x) = 0, \qquad S(x)
= -x,$$
and extended as algebra homomorphisms. An element $g \in U(L)$ is *grouplike* if
 $g \neq 0$ and $\Delta(g) = g \otimes g$. Grouplike elements form a group under
 multiplication (with identity $1$ and inverse $S(g)$).

**Proof.** Equip $U(L)$ with the PBW filtration: $U_n$ is the span of products $
x_1 \cdots x_m$ with $m \leq n$ and $x_i \in L$, so $U_0 = k\cdot 1 \subset U_1
\subset \cdots$ and $\bigcup_n U_n = U(L)$. The coproduct preserves this filtrat
ion:
$$\Delta(U_n) \subseteq \sum_{i+j=n} U_i \otimes U_j =: (U \otimes U)_n.$$

Let $g$ be grouplike and let $n$ be minimal with $g \in U_n$. Suppose $n \geq 1$
. Then $g \in U_n \setminus U_{n-1}$, so the principal symbol $\sigma(g) \in \op
eratorname{gr}_n U(L) \cong S^n(L)$ is nonzero. Consider the element $g \otimes
g$:

- Its top-degree component in the tensor-product filtration is $\sigma(g) \otime
s \sigma(g) \in U_n \otimes U_n$, which lies in $(U\otimes U)_{2n} \setminus (U\
otimes U)_{2n-1}$. Hence the filtration degree of $g \otimes g$ is exactly $2n$.

On the other hand, since $\Delta(g) = g \otimes g$ and $\Delta(g) \in (U\otimes
U)_n$, the element $g \otimes g$ has filtration degree $\leq n$.

Combining: $2n \leq n$, so $n \leq 0$, contradicting $n \geq 1$. Therefore $n =
0$, i.e., $g \in U_0 = k\cdot 1$.

Write $g = c\cdot 1$. The counit axiom gives
$$g = (\varepsilon \otimes \operatorname{id})\Delta(g) = (\varepsilon \otimes \o
peratorname{id})(g \otimes g) = \varepsilon(g)\, g,$$
so $\varepsilon(g) = 1$ (since $g \neq 0$). But $\varepsilon(c\cdot 1) = c$, hen
ce $c = 1$. Equivalently, $\Delta(c\cdot 1) = c(1\otimes 1)$ and $g\otimes g = c
^2(1\otimes 1)$, giving $c = c^2$, so $c \in \{0,1\}$, and $c = 1$ since $g \neq
 0$.

Thus $g = 1$ is the only grouplike element. The group of grouplike elements is
$$\mathrm{G}(U(L)) = \{1\},$$
the trivial group, for every Lie algebra $L$. $\blacksquare$
