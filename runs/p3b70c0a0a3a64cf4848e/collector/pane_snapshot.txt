## Solution

**Answer: No.** Such a subset $E^{(k)}$ need not exist. We give a counterexample
 that works for every $k \ge 1$.

### Counterexample

Let
$$A = \omega = \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}, \qquad B = \{S \subseteq \omega : S \text{ is
 infinite}\}.$$
Set $V = A \sqcup B$ and define edges by
$$E = \big\{\{n, S\} : n \in \omega,\; S \in B,\; n \in S\big\}.$$
The graph $G = (V, E)$ is simple and undirected (edges only between $A$ and $B$,
 no loops, no multi-edges).

**Every vertex has degree $\ge \aleph_0$:**

- For $n \in A$: $\deg(n) = |\{S \in B : n \in S\}|$. The number of infinite sub
sets of $\omega$ containing a fixed $n$ is $2^{\aleph_0} \ge \aleph_0$.
- For $S \in B$: $\deg(S) = |S| = \aleph_0$, since $S$ is an infinite subset of
$\omega$.

So $\deg(v) \ge \aleph_0$ for every $v \in V$, and $G$ is infinite.

**Cardinalities of the two parts:**
$$|A| = \aleph_0, \qquad |B| = 2^{\aleph_0}, \qquad |A| \neq |B|.$$

### No $k$-regular spanning subgraph exists

$G$ is bipartite with bipartition $(A, B)$. Any spanning subgraph $(V, E^{(k)})$
 is also bipartite with the same bipartition. Suppose $(V, E^{(k)})$ were $k$-re
gular for some finite $k \ge 1$. Counting the edges of $E^{(k)}$ from each side:

- Each vertex of $A$ is incident to exactly $k$ edges, so $|E^{(k)}| = k \cdot |
A| = k \cdot \aleph_0 = \aleph_0$.
- Each vertex of $B$ is incident to exactly $k$ edges, so $|E^{(k)}| = k \cdot |
B| = k \cdot 2^{\aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_0}$.

These count the same set $E^{(k)}$, giving $\aleph_0 = 2^{\aleph_0}$, a contradi
ction (Cantor's theorem).

Therefore no $k$-regular spanning subgraph exists in this graph, for any $k \ge
1$.

### Conclusion

The condition $\deg(v) \ge \aleph_0$ for all $v$ does **not** guarantee the exis
tence of a $k$-regular spanning subgraph. The obstruction is cardinal: in a bipa
rtite graph whose two parts have different cardinalities, a finite-degree regula
r spanning subgraph is impossible (it would force $|A| = |B|$), yet both parts c
an still have every vertex of infinite degree.
