**Substitution.** Let
$$u = xy, \qquad v = x^2 - y^2.$$
The region $S$ in the first quadrant is bounded by $u=1, u=3, v=1, v=4$, so in $
(u,v)$-coordinates it becomes the rectangle $[1,3]\times[1,4]$.

**Jacobian.**
$$\frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(x,y)} = \det\begin{pmatrix} y & x \\ 2x & -2y\en
d{pmatrix} = -2y^2 - 2x^2 = -2(x^2+y^2).$$
Hence
$$\left|\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)}\right| = \frac{1}{2(x^2+y^2)}.$$

**One-to-one.** In the first quadrant ($x>0,y>0$), given $u>0$ and $v$, we recov
er
$$x^2+y^2 = \sqrt{(x^2-y^2)^2+4x^2y^2}=\sqrt{v^2+4u^2},$$
then $x^2=\tfrac12\!\left(v+\sqrt{v^2+4u^2}\right)>0$ and $y^2=\tfrac12\!\left(-
v+\sqrt{v^2+4u^2}\right)>0$ (since $\sqrt{v^2+4u^2}>|v|$), and $x,y$ are the pos
itive roots. So the map is bijective on the first quadrant.

**Transform the integral.** The integrand simplifies beautifully:
$$(x^2+y^2)\,dA = (x^2+y^2)\cdot\left|\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)}\right|
du\,dv = (x^2+y^2)\cdot\frac{1}{2(x^2+y^2)}\,du\,dv = \frac12\,du\,dv.$$

**Compute.**
$$\iint_S (x^2+y^2)\,dA = \int_1^4\!\!\int_1^3 \frac12\,du\,dv = \frac12\cdot(3-
1)\cdot(4-1) = \frac12\cdot 2\cdot 3 = 3.$$

$$\boxed{3}$$
