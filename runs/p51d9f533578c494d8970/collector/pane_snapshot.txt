## Solution

**Step 1: Identify the difficulty.** The integrand $f(z) = \frac{1}{z\sin(1/z)}$
 has singularities at $z=0$ and at $z = 1/(n\pi)$ for every nonzero integer $n$
(where $\sin(1/z)=0$). Since $1/(|n|\pi) \le 1/\pi < 1/2$, **all** of these lie
inside $|z|=1/2$, and they accumulate at the essential singularity $z=0$. The st
andard residue theorem (finitely many singularities) does not apply directly.

**Step 2: Substitute $w = 1/z$.** Set $w = 1/z$, so $z = 1/w$ and $dz = -dw/w^2$
. The contour $|z|=1/2$ traversed counterclockwise maps to $|w|=2$ traversed **c
lockwise** (since $z = \tfrac12 e^{i\theta} \Rightarrow w = 2e^{-i\theta}$). The
n:

$$\int_{|z|=1/2} \frac{dz}{z\sin(1/z)} = \int_{|w|=2,\ \text{cw}} \frac{-dw/w^2}
{(1/w)\sin w} = \int_{|w|=2,\ \text{cw}} \frac{-dw}{w\sin w} = \int_{|w|=2,\ \te
xt{ccw}} \frac{dw}{w\sin w}.$$

**Step 3: Find singularities of $g(w) = \frac{1}{w\sin w}$ inside $|w|=2$.** The
 zeros of $w\sin w$ are $w=0$ and $w = n\pi$. Since $\pi \approx 3.14 > 2$, the
only singularity inside $|w|<2$ is $w = 0$. The residue theorem now applies clea
nly (single isolated singularity).

**Step 4: Compute the residue at $w=0$.** Near $w=0$:

$$\sin w = w - \frac{w^3}{6} + \cdots, \qquad w\sin w = w^2 - \frac{w^4}{6} + \c
dots = w^2\!\left(1 - \frac{w^2}{6} + \cdots\right).$$

Therefore:

$$\frac{1}{w\sin w} = \frac{1}{w^2}\cdot\frac{1}{1 - w^2/6 + \cdots} = \frac{1}{
w^2}\left(1 + \frac{w^2}{6} + \cdots\right) = \frac{1}{w^2} + \frac{1}{6} + \cdo
ts$$

This is a **Laurent series with only even powers** of $w$ (reflecting that $w\si
n w$ is an even function, so $1/(w\sin w)$ is even). The coefficient of $1/w$ is
:

$$\operatorname{Res}_{w=0}\frac{1}{w\sin w} = 0.$$

**Step 5: Conclude.** By the residue theorem:

$$\int_{|z|=1/2} \frac{dz}{z\sin(1/z)} = \int_{|w|=2} \frac{dw}{w\sin w} = 2\pi
i \cdot \operatorname{Res}_{w=0}\frac{1}{w\sin w} = 2\pi i \cdot 0 = 0.$$

**Verification (cross-check in the $z$-plane).** The residues at the simple pole
s $z_0 = 1/(n\pi)$, $n\neq 0$, are:

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=1/(n\pi)} \frac{1}{z\sin(1/z)} = \frac{1}{z_0\cdot\cos(1
/z_0)\cdot(-1/z_0^2)} = \frac{-z_0^3}{\cos(n\pi)} = \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{(n\pi)^3}.
$$

Pairing $n$ with $-n$: since $(-1)^{-n+1}=(-1)^{n+1}$ and $(-n\pi)^3 = -(n\pi)^3
$, each pair $(n,-n)$ contributes $\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{(n\pi)^3} - \frac{(-1)^{n+1
}}{(n\pi)^3} = 0$. So the sum of residues at all simple poles vanishes, consiste
nt with the total integral being $0$ (and $\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}=0$ as well).

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{|z|=1/2} \frac{dz}{z\sin(1/z)} = 0}$$
