## Solution

**Answer: $n!$**

**Key idea:** Count terms with odd coefficients by reducing the product modulo 2
 (working over $\mathbb{F}_2$), where the product becomes the Vandermonde determ
inant.

### Proof

**Step 1: Reduce mod 2 and identify the Vandermonde.**

Over $\mathbb{F}_2$, we have $-1 \equiv 1$, so $x_i + x_j \equiv x_i - x_j \equi
v x_j - x_i$. Therefore:

$$\prod_{1 \le i < j \le n}(x_i + x_j) \equiv \prod_{1 \le i < j \le n}(x_j - x_
i) \pmod{2}.$$

The right-hand side is the classical **Vandermonde determinant**:

$$V(x_1, \dots, x_n) = \det\!\begin{pmatrix} 1 & x_1 & x_1^2 & \cdots & x_1^{n-1
} \\ 1 & x_2 & x_2^2 & \cdots & x_2^{n-1} \\ \vdots & & & & \vdots \\ 1 & x_n &
x_n^2 & \cdots & x_n^{n-1} \end{pmatrix} = \prod_{1 \le i < j \le n}(x_j - x_i).
$$

**Step 2: Expand the determinant over $\mathbb{F}_2$.**

Expanding via Leibniz's formula:

$$V = \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\, x_1^{\sigma(1)-1}\, x_2
^{\sigma(2)-1} \cdots x_n^{\sigma(n)-1}.$$

Over $\mathbb{F}_2$, every sign $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma) = \pm 1$ satisfies $
\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma) \equiv 1 \pmod{2}$. Hence:

$$\prod_{1 \le i < j \le n}(x_i + x_j) \equiv \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} x_1^{\sigma(
1)-1} x_2^{\sigma(2)-1} \cdots x_n^{\sigma(n)-1} \pmod{2}.$$

**Step 3: Count distinct monomials.**

For each permutation $\sigma \in S_n$, the exponent vector is

$$(\sigma(1)-1,\; \sigma(2)-1,\; \dots,\; \sigma(n)-1),$$

which is a **permutation of** $(0, 1, 2, \dots, n-1)$.

Two permutations $\sigma, \tau$ yield the same monomial if and only if $\sigma(i
) - 1 = \tau(i) - 1$ for all $i$, i.e., $\sigma = \tau$. Therefore all $n!$ mono
mials are **distinct**, and each appears with coefficient exactly $1$ (odd) modu
lo 2.

**Step 4: Verification.**

- $n = 1$: empty product $= 1$, one odd term. $1! = 1$. ✓
- $n = 2$: $x_1 + x_2$, two odd terms. $2! = 2$. ✓
- $n = 3$: given example has 6 odd terms. $3! = 6$. ✓

### Conclusion

The number of terms with odd coefficients in the expansion of $\displaystyle\pro
d_{1 \le i < j \le n}(x_i + x_j)$ is

$$\boxed{n!}.$$
