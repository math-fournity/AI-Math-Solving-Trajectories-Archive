## Proof

Let $\nabla$ denote the Levi-Civita connection of the Euclidean metric on $U\sub
seteq\mathbb{R}^2$ (the standard flat connection). Since $E_1,E_2$ is an orthono
rmal frame, metric compatibility gives skew-symmetry of the connection 1-forms:
$$\nabla E_i = \sum_j \omega_{ij}\,E_j, \qquad \omega_{ij}=-\omega_{ji}.$$
In dimension 2 there is a single independent connection form $\omega:=\omega_{12
}=-\omega_{21}$, so
$$\nabla E_1 = \omega\,E_2, \qquad \nabla E_2 = -\omega\,E_1.$$

Cartan's first structural equation (the connection is torsion-free, being Levi-C
ivita) reads
$$dE^i = -\sum_j \omega^i{}_j \wedge E^j.$$
Using $\omega^1{}_2=\omega$ and $\omega^2{}_1=-\omega$:
$$dE^1 = -\omega\wedge E^2, \qquad dE^2 = \omega\wedge E^1.$$

The hypothesis $dE^i=0$ gives
$$\omega\wedge E^2 = 0, \qquad \omega\wedge E^1 = 0.$$

Since $(E^1,E^2)$ is a coframe, the two 1-forms are pointwise linearly independe
nt. A 1-form $\omega$ with $\omega\wedge E^2=0$ must be a scalar multiple of $E^
2$, and $\omega\wedge E^1=0$ forces it to be a scalar multiple of $E^1$. The onl
y 1-form that is simultaneously a multiple of $E^1$ and of $E^2$ (with $E^1,E^2$
 independent) is
$$\omega \equiv 0.$$

Therefore
$$\nabla E_1 = 0, \qquad \nabla E_2 = 0.$$

Because $\nabla$ is the standard flat connection on $\mathbb{R}^2$, the conditio
n $\nabla E_i=0$ means each $E_i$ has identically vanishing ordinary (componentw
ise) derivative. Hence each $E_i$ is a constant vector field on $U$.

**Answer: Yes, the vector fields $E_1,E_2$ are constant on $U$.**

(Remark: the conclusion follows purely from $dE^i=0$ together with orthonormalit

forms $E^i$ to obtain global potential functions.)
