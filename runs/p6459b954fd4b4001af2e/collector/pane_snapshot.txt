### Counterexample

Let $R = \mathbb{Z}$, and let $M = \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ as a $\mathbb{Z}$-modu
le. Take the multiplicative set $S = \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}$, so $S^{-1}R =
\mathbb{Q}$.

**Step 1: $M = \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ is a cogenerator for $\mathbb{Z}$-modules.
**

It is a standard fact that $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ is an injective cogenerator i
n $\mathbf{Mod}\text{-}\mathbb{Z}$ (i.e., the category of abelian groups). To ve
rify the cogenerator property: given any nonzero abelian group $A$ and any nonze
ro element $a \in A$:

- If $a$ has infinite order, then $\langle a \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}$. Define $
f: \langle a \rangle \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ by $f(a) = \tfrac{1}{2} + \mathb
b{Z} \neq 0$. Since $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ is injective, $f$ extends to a homom
orphism $\tilde{f}: A \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ with $\tilde{f}(a) \neq 0$.

- If $a$ has finite order $n$, then $\langle a \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb
{Z}$. Define $f: \langle a \rangle \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ by $f(a) = \tfrac{
1}{n} + \mathbb{Z} \neq 0$. Again, injectivity of $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ extend
s $f$ to all of $A$.

Thus $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ is a cogenerator.

**Step 2: $S^{-1}M = 0$.**

Every element of $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ is torsion: for any $\bar{q} \in \mathb
b{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$, there exists a positive integer $n$ with $n \cdot \bar{q} = 0$
. Since $n \in S$, in the localized module $S^{-1}M$ we have:

$$\frac{\bar{q}}{1} = \frac{n \cdot \bar{q}}{n} = \frac{0}{n} = 0.$$

Hence $S^{-1}M = 0$.

**Step 3: The zero module is not a cogenerator over $\mathbb{Q}$.**

The ring $\mathbb{Q}$ is a nonzero $\mathbb{Q}$-module, and $\operatorname{Hom}_
{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{Q}, 0) = 0$. In particular, there is no nonzero homomorphis
m from $\mathbb{Q}$ to $0$, so the zero module fails to cogenerate the nonzero m
odule $\mathbb{Q}$. Therefore $0$ is not a cogenerator over $\mathbb{Q}$.

### Conclusion

The cogenerator $M = \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ over $R = \mathbb{Z}$ localizes to t
