## Solution

**Domino set identification.** A set with 45 tiles is the double-eight set: tile
s $(a,b)$ with $0 \le a \le b \le 8$, giving $9$ doubles $+\binom{9}{2}=36$ non-
doubles $=45$ tiles. Each number $k \in \{0,\dots,8\}$ appears in exactly $9$ ti
les. Two tiles are **compatible** if they share at least one number.

---

### Part 1: Probability that tile 2 fits tile 1, and tile 3 fits at least one of
 tiles 1–2

**Step 1 — $P(\text{tile 2 compatible with tile 1})$.**

After drawing tile 1, 44 tiles remain.

- **Tile 1 is a double** $(a,a)$ (prob $9/45=1/5$): 8 remaining tiles contain $a
$, so $P=8/44$.
- **Tile 1 is non-double** $(a,b)$, $a\ne b$ (prob $36/45=4/5$): tiles containin
g $a$ or $b$ (excluding tile 1) $= 8+8=16$, so $P=16/44$.

$$P_1 = \tfrac{1}{5}\cdot\tfrac{8}{44}+\tfrac{4}{5}\cdot\tfrac{16}{44}=\frac{8+6
4}{220}=\frac{72}{220}=\frac{18}{55}.$$

**Step 2 — $P(\text{tile 3 compatible with } \ge 1 \text{ of tiles 1,2} \mid \te
xt{tiles 1,2 compatible})$.**

We enumerate all ordered compatible pairs $(T_1,T_2)$ by case, count them, and f
or each case compute how many of the remaining 43 tiles share a number with the
set $S$ of numbers appearing in $T_1\cup T_2$.

| Case | Description | $|S|$ | Compatible pairs | Available tiles sharing $\ge 1
$ number in $S$ |
|------|-------------|-------|-------------------|------------------------------
------------------|
| A | $T_1$ double $(a,a)$, $T_2$ non-double containing $a$ | 2 | $9\times 8=72$
 | $\|A_a\cup A_x\|-2 = (9+9-1)-2=15$ |
| B | $T_1$ non-double $(a,b)$, $T_2$ double $(a,a)$ or $(b,b)$ | 2 | $36\times
2=72$ | $\|A_a\cup A_b\|-2 = (9+9-1)-2=15$ |
| C | Both non-double, share exactly one number | 3 | $36\times 14=504$ | $\|A_a
\cup A_b\cup A_d\|-2=(9+9+9-1-1-1)-2=22$ |

*Case C detail:* $T_1=(a,b)$, $T_2=(a,d)$ with $d\notin\{a,b\}$, so $S=\{a,b,d\}
$. By inclusion–exclusion over all 45 tiles: $|A_a\cup A_b\cup A_d|=9+9+9-1-1-1+
0=24$ (the three pairwise intersections are tiles $(a,b),(a,d),(b,d)$, each coun
ted once; no tile has 3 numbers). Subtracting the 2 drawn tiles gives $22$.

*Case C count:* For each non-double $T_1=(a,b)$, a non-double $T_2$ sharing exac
tly one number: 7 non-doubles containing $a$ (not $b$) + 7 containing $b$ (not $
a$) $=14$. Total $36\times14=504$.

**Verification of total compatible pairs:** $72+72+504=648$, and $\frac{648}{45\
cdot44}=\frac{648}{1980}=\frac{18}{55}=P_1$. ✓

**Conditional probability:**

$$P_2=\frac{72\cdot15+72\cdot15+504\cdot22}{648\cdot43}=\frac{1080+1080+11088}{2
7864}=\frac{13248}{27864}=\frac{184}{387}.$$

**Overall probability:**

$$\boxed{P = P_1\cdot P_2 = \frac{18}{55}\cdot\frac{184}{387}=\frac{3312}{21285}
=\frac{368}{2365}\approx 0.1556.}$$

---

### Part 2: Minimum number of attempts for $P(\ge 1\text{ success})\ge 0.9$

Each attempt is independent with success probability $p=\frac{368}{2365}$. We ne
ed:

$$1-\left(1-p\right)^n \ge 0.9 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \left(\frac{1997}{2365}
\right)^n \le 0.1.$$

Taking logarithms:

$$n \ge \frac{\ln(0.1)}{\ln(1997/2365)}.$$

Compute $\ln(1997/2365)=\ln(1-0.15560)$:

$$\ln(1-0.15560)\approx -0.15560-0.01211-0.00126-0.00015\approx -0.16913.$$

$$n \ge \frac{-2.30259}{-0.16913}\approx 13.62.$$

**Verification:**

- $n=13$: $13\times(-0.16913)=-2.1987$, $e^{-2.1987}\approx 0.1109 > 0.1$. ✗
- $n=14$: $14\times(-0.16913)=-2.3678$, $e^{-2.3678}\approx 0.0937 < 0.1$. ✓

$$\boxed{n=14 \text{ attempts.}}$$

---

**Summary.** The probability that the second tile fits the first and the third f
its at least one of the first two is $\dfrac{368}{2365}\approx 15.56\%$. At leas
t **14** independent attempts are needed for the probability of at least one suc
cess to reach $0.9$.
