## Setup via Stokes' Theorem

Stokes' theorem gives $\int_X d\omega = \int_{\partial X} \omega$, where $\parti
al X$ carries the orientation induced by the parametrization $(r,\theta)$.

The surface is the paraboloid $z = 1 - (x^2+y^2)$ (since $x^2+y^2=r^2$ and $z=1-
r^2$), with apex at $(0,0,1)$ and rim at $r=1$, $z=0$.

**Orientation.** The parametrization $X(r,\theta)$ gives the normal
$$\frac{\partial X}{\partial r}\times\frac{\partial X}{\partial \theta} = (2r^2\
cos\theta,\; 2r^2\sin\theta,\; r),$$
whose $z$-component $r>0$ points upward (outward from the bowl). The induced ori
entation on the boundary at $r=1$ is $+\partial_\theta$ (increasing $\theta$), s
ince the outward normal in the $r$-direction is $+\partial_r$ and $\partial_r\we
dge\partial_\theta$ matches the surface orientation. The boundary at $r=0$ is a
degenerate point and contributes nothing.

## Parametrize the Boundary

At $r=1$: $x=\cos\theta,\; y=\sin\theta,\; z=0$, with $\theta\in[0,2\pi]$. Hence
$$dx=-\sin\theta\,d\theta,\qquad dy=\cos\theta\,d\theta,\qquad dz=0.$$

Evaluate the coefficients of $\omega$ on $\partial X$ (using $x^2+y^2=1$ and $xz
=0$):

$$P = e^{xz}+2y\,e^{x^2+y^2} = 1 + 2e\sin\theta,$$
$$Q = \ln(2+z)+3x\,e^{x^2+y^2} = \ln 2 + 3e\cos\theta,$$
$$R = 3x^2y = 3\cos^2\theta\sin\theta.$$

## Pull $\omega$ Back to the Boundary

$$\omega\big|_{\partial X} = P\,dx + Q\,dy + R\,dz = \big[(1+2e\sin\theta)(-\sin
\theta) + (\ln 2+3e\cos\theta)(\cos\theta) + 0\big]\,d\theta$$

$$= \big[-\sin\theta - 2e\sin^2\theta + \ln 2\,\cos\theta + 3e\cos^2\theta\big]\
,d\theta.$$

## Integrate

$$\int_{\partial X}\omega = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\big[-\sin\theta + \ln 2\,\cos\theta
- 2e\sin^2\theta + 3e\cos^2\theta\big]\,d\theta.$$

Using the standard integrals over a full period:
- $\int_0^{2\pi}\sin\theta\,d\theta = 0$, $\;\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\theta\,d\theta =
0$,
- $\int_0^{2\pi}\sin^2\theta\,d\theta = \pi$, $\;\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^2\theta\,d\th
eta = \pi$,

we obtain

$$\int_{\partial X}\omega = 0 + 0 - 2e\pi + 3e\pi = e\pi.$$

## Verification (direct surface integral of $d\omega$)

Computing $d\omega = (Q_x-P_y)\,dx\wedge dy + (R_x-P_z)\,dx\wedge dz + (R_y-Q_z)
\,dy\wedge dz$ with
$$Q_x-P_y = e^{x^2+y^2}(1+6x^2-4y^2),\quad R_x-P_z = 6xy-xe^{xz},\quad R_y-Q_z =
 3x^2-\tfrac{1}{2+z},$$
and pulling back via $dx\wedge dy = r\,dr\wedge d\theta$, $dx\wedge dz = -2r^2\s
in\theta\,dr\wedge d\theta$, $dy\wedge dz = 2r^2\cos\theta\,dr\wedge d\theta$, t
he $\theta$-odd terms integrate to zero over $[0,2\pi]$, while the $dx\wedge dy$
 term yields
$$\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{2\pi} e^{r^2}\big(1+6r^2\cos^2\theta-4r^2\sin^2\theta\big)
\,r\,d\theta\,dr = \int_0^1 e^{r^2}\big(2\pi + r^2\pi(6-4)\big)\,r\,dr$$
$$= \int_0^1 e^{r^2}(2\pi+2\pi r^2)\,r\,dr = 2\pi\int_0^1 (1+r^2)e^{r^2}r\,dr.$$
With $u=r^2$, $du=2r\,dr$: $= \pi\int_0^1 (1+u)e^u\,du = \pi\big[(u+1)e^u - e^u\
big]_0^1 = \pi\big[u\,e^u\big]_0^1 = e\pi.$

The remaining terms ($dx\wedge dz$ and $dy\wedge dz$) contribute zero after $\th
eta$-integration (they are $\sin\theta$- and $\cos\theta$-weighted with $\theta$
-even or $\theta$-integrating-to-zero factors over the full period — the only su
rviving even-in-$\theta$ part of $R_y-Q_z = 3r^2\cos^2\theta - \frac{1}{3-r^2}$
gives $\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\theta\,d\theta = 0$). Hence the direct computation conf
irms

$$\int_X d\omega = e\pi.$$

## Result

$$\boxed{\int_X d\omega = e\pi}$$
