## Galois Group of $x^5 - x - 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$

**Claim.** The Galois group of $f(x) = x^5 - x - 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is $S_5$.

### Step 1: Irreducibility over $\mathbb{Q}$

We show $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_3$, which implies irreducibility ove
r $\mathbb{Q}$.

Over $\mathbb{F}_3$, $f(x) = x^5 + 2x + 2$. Checking for roots:
- $f(0) = 2 \neq 0$, $\;f(1) = 1+2+2 = 5 \equiv 2 \neq 0$, $\;f(2) = 32+4+2 = 38
 \equiv 2 \neq 0$.

No linear factors. Since $\deg f = 5$ is prime, any nontrivial factorization inc
ludes a factor of degree $\leq 2$, so it suffices to rule out degree-2 factors.
The three monic irreducible quadratics over $\mathbb{F}_3$ are $x^2+1$, $x^2+x+2
$, $x^2+2x+2$ (those with non-square discriminant $b^2-4c \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$).

Polynomial long division of $f$ by each (over $\mathbb{F}_3$):
- **$x^2+1$:** remainder $-1 \equiv 2 \neq 0$.
- **$x^2+x+2$:** remainder $x-1 \neq 0$.
- **$x^2+2x+2$:** remainder $x+2 \neq 0$.

No degree-2 factor divides $f$, so $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_3$, hence
 over $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Consequence:** $G = \operatorname{Gal}(f/\mathbb{Q})$ acts transitively on the
 5 roots, so $G$ is a transitive subgroup of $S_5$.

### Step 2: The discriminant is not a square

For the trinomial $x^n + px + q$, the discriminant is
$$\Delta = (-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\bigl(n^n q^{n-1} + (-1)^{n-1}(n-1)^{n-1}p^n\bigr).$$
(Verified against $n=2$: gives $p^2-4q$; and $n=3$: gives $-4p^3-27q^2$.)

For $f(x)=x^5-x-1$: $n=5$, $p=-1$, $q=-1$:
$$\Delta = (-1)^{10}\bigl(5^5\cdot(-1)^4 + (-1)^4\cdot 4^4\cdot(-1)^5\bigr) = 31
25 - 256 = 2869.$$

Factoring: $2869 = 19 \times 151$, with both $19$ and $151$ prime. Since $2869$
is not a perfect square, $G \not\subseteq A_5$.

### Step 3: Ruling out solvable transitive subgroups

The transitive subgroups of $S_5$ are (well-known classification for prime degre
e):
$$C_5,\quad D_5,\quad F_{20},\quad A_5,\quad S_5,$$
where $F_{20} = \operatorname{AGL}(1,5) \cong \mathbb{Z}_5 \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_4$

Since $G \not\subseteq A_5$:
- $C_5 \subseteq A_5$ (5-cycles are even) — ruled out.
- $D_5 \subseteq A_5$ (rotations are 5-cycles = even; reflections are products o
f 2 disjoint transpositions = even) — ruled out.
- $A_5$ — ruled out directly.

This leaves $G \in \{F_{20},\, S_5\}$.

### Step 4: Factorization mod 2 gives an element of order 6

Over $\mathbb{F}_2$: $f(x) = x^5 + x + 1$. We factor:
$$x^5 + x + 1 = (x^2+x+1)(x^3+x^2+1).$$
**Verification:** $(x^2+x+1)(x^3+x^2+1) = x^5+x^4+x^2+x^4+x^3+x+x^3+x^2+1 = x^5
+ 2x^4+2x^3+2x^2+x+1 \equiv x^5+x+1 \pmod{2}.$ ✓

Both factors are irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_2$:
- $x^2+x+1$: the unique irreducible quadratic over $\mathbb{F}_2$ (no roots: $0\
mapsto1$, $1\mapsto1$).
- $x^3+x^2+1$: no roots ($0\mapsto1$, $1\mapsto1+1+1=1$), hence irreducible (deg

Since $\Delta = 2869$ is odd, $2 \nmid \Delta$, so the factorization mod 2 is un
ramified. By the Frobenius density theorem, $G$ contains an element with cycle t
ype $(2,3)$ — a product of a 2-cycle and a disjoint 3-cycle — which has order $\
operatorname{lcm}(2,3) = 6$.

### Step 5: Conclusion

The group $F_{20} = \{x \mapsto ax+b \mid a \in \mathbb{F}_5^*,\; b \in \mathbb{
F}_5\}$ has element orders drawn from $\{1, 2, 4, 5\}$:
- $x \mapsto x+b$ ($b\neq 0$): order 5.
- $x \mapsto -x+b$: order 2 (since $(-x+b)\circ(-x+b) = x$).

No element of $F_{20}$ has order 3 or 6 (by Cauchy's theorem, $3 \nmid 20$, so t
here is no element of order 3; hence no element of order 6).

Since $G$ contains an element of order 6 but $F_{20}$ does not, we conclude:

$$\boxed{G = \operatorname{Gal}(x^5 - x - 1 / \mathbb{Q}) \cong S_5.}$$
