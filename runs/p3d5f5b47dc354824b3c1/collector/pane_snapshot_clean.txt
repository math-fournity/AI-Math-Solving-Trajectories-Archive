## Proof

We evaluate
$$L = \lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\zeta\!\left(1+\tfrac{1}{x}\right)}{\Gamma(x)}.$$

**Step 1: Asymptotics of the numerator.**

The Riemann zeta function has a simple pole at $s=1$ with residue $1$. Its Laure
nt expansion there is
$$\zeta(s)=\frac{1}{s-1}+\gamma+O(s-1),\qquad s\to 1,$$
where $\gamma$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant. Setting $s=1+\tfrac{1}{x}$ (so
$s-1=\tfrac{1}{x}\to 0^+$ as $x\to+\infty$) gives
$$\zeta\!\left(1+\tfrac{1}{x}\right)=x+\gamma+O\!\left(\tfrac{1}{x}\right).$$
In particular, $\zeta\!\left(1+\tfrac{1}{x}\right)\sim x$ as $x\to+\infty$; the
numerator grows **linearly** in $x$.

**Step 2: Asymptotics of the denominator.**

By Stirling's formula, for $x\to+\infty$,
$$\Gamma(x)\sim \sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{x}}\left(\frac{x}{e}\right)^{\!x}.$$
This grows to $+\infty$ **super-exponentially**: in particular $\Gamma(x)/x^k\to
+\infty$ for every fixed $k$, and certainly $\Gamma(x)/x\to+\infty$.

**Step 3: Combine.**

Since the numerator is $\Theta(x)$ and the denominator satisfies $\Gamma(x)/x\to
+\infty$,
$$0\le \frac{\zeta\!\left(1+\tfrac{1}{x}\right)}{\Gamma(x)} \sim \frac{x}{\Gamma
(x)}\to 0.$$

More explicitly, for $x$ large enough both $\zeta(1+1/x)\le 2x$ (using the Laure
nt expansion) and $\Gamma(x)\ge (x/e)^x$, so
$$0\le \frac{\zeta(1+1/x)}{\Gamma(x)}\le \frac{2x}{(x/e)^x}=2x\left(\frac{e}{x}\
right)^{\!x}\xrightarrow{x\to+\infty}0,$$
since $\left(\frac{e}{x}\right)^x\to 0$ super-exponentially while the prefactor
$2x$ grows only linearly.

By the squeeze theorem,
$$\boxed{\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\zeta\!\left(1+\tfrac{1}{x}\right)}{\Gamma(x)}=
0.}$$
