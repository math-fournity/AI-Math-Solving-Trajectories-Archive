# Computation of Ext and Tor over ℤ for finitely generated abelian groups

## Setup and classification

By the structure theorem, every finitely generated abelian group decomposes as

$$A \cong \mathbb{Z}^{r} \oplus \bigoplus_{i=1}^{p}\mathbb{Z}/a_i, \qquad B \con
g \mathbb{Z}^{s} \oplus \bigoplus_{j=1}^{q}\mathbb{Z}/b_j,$$

with $r,s \ge 0$ and $a_i, b_j \ge 2$. The free ranks are $r,s$; the torsion par
ts are $\bigoplus_i \mathbb{Z}/a_i$ and $\bigoplus_j \mathbb{Z}/b_j$.

## Key facts used

1. **Global dimension 1.** $\mathbb{Z}$ is a PID, so every module has a projecti
ve resolution of length $\le 1$. Hence
$$\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}}(A,B)=0,\qquad \operatorname{Tor}_{n}^{\mat
hbb{Z}}(A,B)=0 \quad\text{for all } n\ge 2.$$

2. **Additivity.** Both $\operatorname{Ext}$ and $\operatorname{Tor}$ are additi
ve in each variable over *finite* direct sums:
$$\operatorname{Ext}^{n}\!\Big(\bigoplus_k A_k,\, B\Big)\cong\bigoplus_k \operat
orname{Ext}^{n}(A_k,B),\qquad \operatorname{Tor}_{n}\!\Big(\bigoplus_k A_k,\, B\
Big)\cong\bigoplus_k \operatorname{Tor}_{n}(A_k,B),$$
and symmetrically in the second variable. So it suffices to compute the building
 blocks $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Z}/m$ in each slot.

3. **Free summands are trivial for derived functors.**
$$\operatorname{Ext}^{n}(\mathbb{Z},B)=0\;(n\ge 1),\quad \operatorname{Tor}_{n}(
\mathbb{Z},B)=0\;(n\ge 1),\quad \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z},B)\cong B,\quad \m
athbb{Z}\otimes B\cong B.$$

4. **Standard free resolution of $\mathbb{Z}/m$.**
$$0 \longrightarrow \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\;\times m\;} \mathbb{Z} \longrighta
rrow \mathbb{Z}/m \longrightarrow 0.$$

Applying $\operatorname{Hom}(-,B)$ (contravariant, so the map on $B$ is still $\
times m$):
$$0 \to B \xrightarrow{\;\times m\;} B \to 0.$$

Applying $-\otimes B$:
$$0 \to B \xrightarrow{\;\times m\;} B \to 0.$$

Reading off cohomology/homology of these two-term complexes:

$$\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/m,\,B)\cong B[m]:=\ker(\times m), \qquad \operat
orname{Ext}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}/m,\,B)\cong B/mB:=\operatorname{coker}(\times m),$$

$$\operatorname{Tor}_{0}(\mathbb{Z}/m,\,B)=(\mathbb{Z}/m)\otimes B\cong B/mB,\qq
uad \operatorname{Tor}_{1}(\mathbb{Z}/m,\,B)\cong B[m].$$

5. **Torsion and quotient of $B$ by $m$.** For $B=\mathbb{Z}^{s}\oplus\bigoplus_
j\mathbb{Z}/b_j$:
$$B[m]=\bigoplus_{j}\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,b_j),\qquad B/mB \cong (\mathbb{Z}/m)^{s}\
oplus\bigoplus_{j}\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,b_j).$$
(The free part $\mathbb{Z}^{s}$ contributes $0$ to $B[m]$ and $(\mathbb{Z}/m)^{s
}$ to $B/mB$; each $\mathbb{Z}/b_j$ contributes $\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,b_j)$ to both
, since $\ker(\times m)\cong\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,b_j)$ and $\operatorname{coker}(\t
imes m)\cong\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,b_j)$ on $\mathbb{Z}/b_j$.)

## Assembling the general answer

Write $A=\mathbb{Z}^{r}\oplus\bigoplus_{i}\mathbb{Z}/a_i$ and $B=\mathbb{Z}^{s}\
oplus\bigoplus_{j}\mathbb{Z}/b_j$.

### $\operatorname{Ext}^{0}_{\mathbb{Z}}(A,B)=\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)$

Using additivity and $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z},B)\cong B$, $\operatorname{H
om}(\mathbb{Z}/a_i,B)\cong B[a_i]$:

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)\;\cong\;\mathbb{Z}^{rs}\;\oplus\;\bigoplus_{j}(
\mathbb{Z}/b_j)^{\,r}\;\oplus\;\bigoplus_{i,\,j}\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(a_i,b_j).}$$

Here $\mathbb{Z}^{rs}$ comes from $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{r},\mathbb{Z}^
{s})$; $(\mathbb{Z}/b_j)^{r}$ comes from $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{r},\mat
hbb{Z}/b_j)$ (the $r$ free generators of $A$ can map arbitrarily into $\mathbb{Z
}/b_j$); and $\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(a_i,b_j)$ comes from $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z
}/a_i,\mathbb{Z}/b_j)$ (a map $\mathbb{Z}/a_i\to\mathbb{Z}/b_j$ is determined by
 the image of $1$, which must be killed by $a_i$, i.e. lie in $(\mathbb{Z}/b_j)[
a_i]\cong\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(a_i,b_j)$).

### $\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(A,B)$

Free summands of $A$ contribute $0$. Each $\mathbb{Z}/a_i$ contributes $B/a_i B\
cong(\mathbb{Z}/a_i)^{s}\oplus\bigoplus_j\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(a_i,b_j)$:

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(A,B)\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{i}(\mathbb{
Z}/a_i)^{\,s}\;\oplus\;\bigoplus_{i,\,j}\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(a_i,b_j).}$$

### $\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}}(A,B)=0$ for $n\ge 2$.

### $\operatorname{Tor}_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}}(A,B)=A\otimes B$

Using additivity, $\mathbb{Z}\otimes B\cong B$, and $(\mathbb{Z}/a_i)\otimes B\c
ong B/a_i B$:

$$\boxed{A\otimes B\;\cong\;\mathbb{Z}^{rs}\;\oplus\;\bigoplus_{j}(\mathbb{Z}/b_
j)^{\,r}\;\oplus\;\bigoplus_{i}(\mathbb{Z}/a_i)^{\,s}\;\oplus\;\bigoplus_{i,\,j}
\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(a_i,b_j).}$$

The four pieces come respectively from $\mathbb{Z}^{r}\otimes\mathbb{Z}^{s}$, $\
mathbb{Z}^{r}\otimes\bigoplus_j\mathbb{Z}/b_j$, $\bigoplus_i\mathbb{Z}/a_i\otime
s\mathbb{Z}^{s}$, and $\bigoplus_i\mathbb{Z}/a_i\otimes\bigoplus_j\mathbb{Z}/b_j
$.

### $\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}}(A,B)$

Free summands of $A$ contribute $0$. Each $\mathbb{Z}/a_i$ contributes $B[a_i]=\
bigoplus_j\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(a_i,b_j)$ (the free part of $B$ has no $a_i$-torsion):

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}}(A,B)\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{i,\,j}\math
bb{Z}/\gcd(a_i,b_j).}$$

### $\operatorname{Tor}_{n}^{\mathbb{Z}}(A,B)=0$ for $n\ge 2$.

## Structural observations

- **Only torsion of $A$ produces derived functors.** The free part $\mathbb{Z}^{
r}$ of $A$ contributes only to $\operatorname{Hom}$ and $\otimes$ (the degree-$0
$ functors); it is killed by the derived functors because $\mathbb{Z}$ is projec
tive. Dually, the free part $\mathbb{Z}^{s}$ of $B$ contributes to $\operatornam
e{Ext}^{1}$ (via $(\mathbb{Z}/a_i)^{s}$) and to $\otimes$ (via $(\mathbb{Z}/a_i)
^{s}$), but not to $\operatorname{Tor}_{1}$, because $\mathbb{Z}$ is flat.

- **The mixed torsion term $\bigoplus_{i,j}\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(a_i,b_j)$ appears in
every non-zero degree of both functors.** It is the "pure torsion–torsion" contr
ibution, governed by the elementary identity $(\mathbb{Z}/a)\otimes(\mathbb{Z}/b
)\cong\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(a,b)$, which simultaneously gives $\operatorname{Tor}_{0}$
, $\operatorname{Tor}_{1}$, $\operatorname{Hom}$, and $\operatorname{Ext}^{1}$ o
n two cyclic torsion groups.

- **Vanishing for $n\ge 2$** is a direct consequence of $\mathbb{Z}$ being a PID
 (hereditary ring, global dimension $1$): every submodule of a free $\mathbb{Z}$
-module is free, so the standard resolution has length $1$.

- **Sanity checks.** (i) If $A$ is free ($p=0$), then $\operatorname{Ext}^{1}=0$
 and $\operatorname{Tor}_{1}=0$, with $\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)\cong B^{r}$ and $
A\otimes B\cong B^{r}$, as expected. (ii) If $B$ is free ($q=0$), then $\operato
rname{Tor}_{1}=0$ (flat target) and $\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(A,B)\cong\bigoplus_i
(\mathbb{Z}/a_i)^{s}$, recovering the classical fact that $\operatorname{Ext}^{1
}(\mathbb{Z}/m,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/m$. (iii) For two cyclic groups, $\ope
ratorname{Ext}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}/a,\mathbb{Z}/b)\cong\operatorname{Tor}_{1}(\mathbb
{Z}/a,\mathbb{Z}/b)\cong\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(a,b)$, the well-known self-duality over
$\mathbb{Z}$.
