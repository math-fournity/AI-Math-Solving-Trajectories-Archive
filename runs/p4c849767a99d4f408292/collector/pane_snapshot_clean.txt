**Answer: Yes, $\|f_n\| \to \|f\|$.**

**Proof.**

We establish the convergence by a sandwich argument on $\liminf$ and $\limsup$ o
f the sequence $\{\|f_n\|\}$.

**Step 1 (Lower bound via pointwise convergence).** For every $x \in X$, since $
f_n(x) \to f(x)$, we have $|f_n(x)| \to |f(x)|$. Using $\|f_n\| \le 1$,

$$|f(x)| = \lim_{n\to\infty} |f_n(x)| = \liminf_{n\to\infty} |f_n(x)| \le \limin
f_{n\to\infty} \bigl(\|f_n\|\cdot\|x\|\bigr) = \Bigl(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|f_n\|
\Bigr)\,\|x\|.$$

Dividing by $\|x\|$ (for $x \ne 0$) and taking the supremum over all $x \ne 0$,

$$\|f\| \le \liminf_{n\to\infty} \|f_n\|.$$

Since $\|f\| = 1$, this gives

$$1 \le \liminf_{n\to\infty} \|f_n\|. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2 (Upper bound via the norm hypothesis).** The hypothesis $\|f_n\| \le 1$
 for all $n$ immediately yields

$$\limsup_{n\to\infty} \|f_n\| \le 1 = \|f\|. \tag{2}$$

**Step 3 (Sandwich).** Combining (1) and (2),

$$1 = \|f\| \le \liminf_{n\to\infty}\|f_n\| \le \limsup_{n\to\infty}\|f_n\| \le
1 = \|f\|.$$

Hence $\liminf \|f_n\| = \limsup \|f_n\| = 1 = \|f\|$, so the limit exists and

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_n\| = 1 = \|f\|.$$

**Remark on the role of the hypotheses.** The key ingredients are: (i) pointwise
 convergence, which gives the *lower* bound $\|f\| \le \liminf\|f_n\|$ (a Fatou-
type inequality valid in any Banach space, no Uniform Boundedness needed here si
nce the bound is given); and (ii) the *matching* upper bound $\|f_n\| \le \|f\|
= 1$, which supplies $\limsup\|f_n\| \le \|f\|$. Without hypothesis (ii) — i.e.,
 with only pointwise convergence and $\|f\|=1$ — the conclusion can fail: e.g. o
n $X = \ell^1$ take $f_n(x) = x_1 + n\,x_n$ (so $f_n \to f$ where $f(x)=x_1$, $\
|f\|=1$, but $\|f_n\| = n \to \infty$). The constraint $\|f_n\|\le 1 = \|f\|$ is
 precisely what closes the gap between $\liminf$ and $\limsup$.
