## Proof

 in probability of $T_n/n^2$.

**Step 1: Bounding $\operatorname{Var}(T_n/n^2)$.**

Since $\operatorname{Var}(Y_k) \le M$ for all $k$, each $Y_k$ is square-integrab
le, so $E[Y_k]$ exists. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, for any $i, j$:
$$|\operatorname{Cov}(Y_i, Y_j)| \le \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(Y_i)\,\operatornam
e{Var}(Y_j)} \le M.$$

The variance of the sum is:
$$\operatorname{Var}(T_n) = \operatorname{Var}\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^n Y_k\right) =
\sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^n \operatorname{Cov}(Y_i, Y_j) \le \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=
1}^n |\operatorname{Cov}(Y_i, Y_j)| \le M n^2.$$

Therefore:
$$\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac{T_n}{n^2}\right) = \frac{\operatorname{Var}(T_
n)}{n^4} \le \frac{M}{n^2} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.$$

**Step 2: The centered sequence converges to 0 in probability.**

By Chebyshev's inequality, for any $\varepsilon > 0$:
$$P\!\left(\left|\frac{T_n}{n^2} - E\!\left[\frac{T_n}{n^2}\right]\right| > \var
epsilon\right) \le \frac{\operatorname{Var}(T_n/n^2)}{\varepsilon^2} \le \frac{M
}{n^2 \varepsilon^2} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.$$

Hence $\frac{T_n}{n^2} - E\!\left[\frac{T_n}{n^2}\right] \to 0$ in probability.

**Step 3: The limit $X$ is a constant.**

By hypothesis, $\frac{T_n}{n^2} \to X$ in probability. We can write:
$$E\!\left[\frac{T_n}{n^2}\right] = \frac{T_n}{n^2} - \left(\frac{T_n}{n^2} - E\

The first term on the right converges to $X$ in probability; the second converge
s to $0$ in probability (Step 2). By Slutsky's theorem (or the algebra of conver
gence in probability):
$$E\!\left[\frac{T_n}{n^2}\right] \xrightarrow{P} X.$$

But $E\!\left[\frac{T_n}{n^2}\right] = \frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{k=1}^n E[Y_k]$ is a **
deterministic** sequence of real numbers. A deterministic sequence converges in

robability of a deterministic sequence is a constant. Therefore there exists a c

$$X = c \quad \text{a.s.}$$

**Step 4: Identifying the constant.**

The constant is:
$$c = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{k=1}^n E[Y_k],$$

and this limit is guaranteed to exist by the convergence established in Step 3.

(constant) random variable**. Specifically,
$$\frac{T_n}{n^2} \xrightarrow{P} c, \qquad c = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{1}{n^2}\
