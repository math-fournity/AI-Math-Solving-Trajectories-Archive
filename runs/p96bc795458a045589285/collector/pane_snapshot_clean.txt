Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a countable unital ring wit
h uncountably many right ideals must have uncountably many maximal right ideals.

**Answer: No.** I'll construct a counterexample.

---

**Construction.** Let $k = \mathbb{F}_2$ and $V = k^{(\mathbb{N})}$ (the countab
le-dimensional vector space of finitely-supported sequences over $k$). Define $R
 = k \oplus V$ with componentwise addition and multiplication
$$(a, v)\cdot(b, w) = (ab,\; aw + bv), \qquad a,b\in k,\; v,w\in V.$$

**$R$ is a commutative unital ring.** The identity is $(1,0)$. Associativity:
$$((a,v)(b,w))(c,u) = (abc,\; abu+acw+bcv) = (a,v)((b,w)(c,u)).$$
Commutativity: $(a,v)(b,w)=(ab,aw+bv)=(ab,bv+aw)=(b,w)(a,v)$. ✓

**$R$ is countable.** $k=\mathbb{F}_2$ is finite and $V=\mathbb{F}_2^{(\mathbb{N
})}$ is countable, so $R$ is countable. ✓

**$R$ is local with unique maximal ideal $J = \{0\}\oplus V$.** An element $(a,v
)$ is a unit iff $a\neq 0$: if $a=1$, then $(1,v)^{-1}=(1,v)$ since $(1,v)^2=(1,
v+v)=(1,0)$; if $a=0$, then $(0,v)(b,w)=(0,bw)$ has zero first component, so it'
s never $(1,0)$. The non-units form $J=\{0\}\oplus V$, which is an ideal with $J
^2=0$. Hence $R$ is local with exactly **one** maximal (right) ideal, $J$. ✓

**$R$ has uncountably many right ideals.** Since $R$ is commutative, right ideal
s = ideals. I claim the ideals contained in $J$ are exactly the $k$-subspaces of
 $V$ (identifying $J\ni(0,v)\leftrightarrow v\in V$):

- *Every $k$-subspace $W\subseteq V$ is an ideal:* For $(0,w)\in\{0\}\oplus W$ a
nd $(a,v)\in R$, $(0,w)(a,v)=(0,aw)\in\{0\}\oplus W$ since $W$ is a $k$-subspace
.
- *Every ideal $I\subseteq J$ is a $k$-subspace:* $I$ is an additive subgroup of
 $V$, and for $(0,w)\in I$, $(a,0)(0,w)=(0,aw)\in I$, so $I$ is $k$-closed.

The number of $k$-subspaces of $V=\mathbb{F}_2^{(\mathbb{N})}$ is $2^{\aleph_0}$
: for each $S\subseteq\mathbb{N}$, the subspace $\operatorname{span}\{e_i : i\in
 S\}$ is distinct (if $j\in S\setminus S'$, then $e_j$ lies in one but not the o
ther, by linear independence of the standard basis).

So $R$ has $2^{\aleph_0}$ ideals (the subspaces of $J$, plus $R$ itself), which
is uncountable. ✓

**Conclusion.** $R$ is a countable unital ring with $2^{\aleph_0}$ (uncountably
many) right ideals, yet only **one** maximal right ideal. Therefore, $R$ does **
not** necessarily have uncountably many maximal right ideals.

*(Concrete model: $R \cong \mathbb{F}_2[x_1,x_2,x_3,\ldots]/(x_i x_j : \text{all
 } i,j\geq 1)$, where $J=(x_1,x_2,\ldots)$ with $J^2=0$.)*

$\blacksquare$
