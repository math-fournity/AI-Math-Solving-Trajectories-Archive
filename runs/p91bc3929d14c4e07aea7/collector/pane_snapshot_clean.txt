## Proof

We compute $\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2 \setminus \{p_1, p_2\})$ for two distinct remove
d points.

### Method 1: Surface group presentation

The projective plane is the non-orientable surface $N_1$ (one crosscap). Removin
g $k$ points from a closed surface is homotopy-equivalent to removing $k$ open d
isks, producing a surface with $k$ boundary components. The standard presentatio
n for the non-orientable surface $N_g$ with $k$ boundary components is

$$\pi_1(N_{g,k}) = \bigl\langle a_1,\dots,a_g,\, c_1,\dots,c_k \;\big|\; a_1^2 a
_2^2\cdots a_g^2\, c_1 c_2\cdots c_k = 1 \bigr\rangle,$$

where $a_i$ are crosscap generators and $c_j$ are boundary loops. Setting $g=1$,
 $k=2$:

$$\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2\setminus\{p_1,p_2\}) = \langle a,\, c_1,\, c_2 \mid a^2\,
c_1\, c_2 = 1\rangle.$$

Eliminating $c_2 = c_1^{-1}a^{-2}$ via the single relation gives

$$\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2\setminus\{p_1,p_2\}) \cong \langle a,\, c_1 \mid \;\rangle
 \cong F_2.$$

### Method 2: Geometric argument via the Möbius band

A classical fact: $\mathbb{RP}^2$ minus one point is homeomorphic to a Möbius ba
nd $M$ (view $\mathbb{RP}^2$ as a disk with antipodal boundary identification; r
emoving an interior point yields $M$). Hence

$$\mathbb{RP}^2\setminus\{p_1,p_2\} \cong M\setminus\{p_2\}.$$

The Möbius band is an interval bundle over $S^1$ and deformation-retracts onto i
ts core circle. Removing an interior point $p_2$ and retracting, $M\setminus\{p_
2\}$ retracts to the core circle $S^1$ together with a small loop encircling $p_
2$ (joined to the core by an arc) — a wedge $S^1\vee S^1$. Therefore

$$\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2\setminus\{p_1,p_2\}) \cong \pi_1(S^1\vee S^1) \cong F_2.$$

### Consistency check (Euler characteristic)

$\chi(\mathbb{RP}^2)=1$, so $\chi(\mathbb{RP}^2\setminus\{p_1,p_2\}) = 1-2 = -1$
. A $K(F_r,1)$ graph has $\chi = 1-r$; solving $1-r=-1$ gives $r=2$, confirming
the result.

### Conclusion

The fundamental group of a projective plane with 2 points removed is **the free
group on two generators**, $F_2$.
