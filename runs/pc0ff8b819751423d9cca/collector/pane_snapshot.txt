### Construction for $\mathbb{R}$

Enumerate the rationals as $\mathbb{Q} = \{q_1, q_2, q_3, \ldots\}$ (a fixed enu
meration without repetition). For each $n \geq 1$, define
$$U_n \;=\; \mathbb{R} \setminus \{q_1, q_2, \ldots, q_n\}.$$

**Each $U_n$ is open.** The set $\{q_1, \ldots, q_n\}$ is finite, hence closed i
n $\mathbb{R}$; its complement $U_n$ is therefore open.

**Each rational belongs to only finitely many $U_n$.** Let $q_k$ be any rational
. Since the enumeration has no repetitions, $q_k \in \{q_1, \ldots, q_n\}$ if an
d only if $n \geq k$. Hence $q_k \in U_n$ if and only if $n < k$, i.e., $n \in \
{1, 2, \ldots, k-1\}$. This is a finite set (of size $k-1$).

**Each irrational belongs to infinitely many $U_n$.** Let $x$ be irrational. Sin
ce every $q_i$ is rational, $x \notin \{q_1, \ldots, q_n\}$ for every $n$. Thus
$x \in U_n$ for **every** $n \geq 1$, which is certainly infinitely many.

### Construction for $[0,1]$

Enumerate the rationals in $[0,1]$ as $\{q_1, q_2, q_3, \ldots\}$ and set
$$U_n \;=\; [0,1] \setminus \{q_1, q_2, \ldots, q_n\}.$$

The finite set $\{q_1, \ldots, q_n\}$ is closed in $[0,1]$, so $U_n$ is open in
the subspace topology of $[0,1]$. The same counting argument applies verbatim: a
 rational $q_k \in [0,1]$ lies in $U_n$ exactly for $n < k$ (finitely many), whi
le every irrational $x \in [0,1]$ lies in $U_n$ for all $n$ (infinitely many).

### Conclusion

In both cases $\{U_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ is a countable family of open sets with t
he required property: rationals are covered only finitely often, irrationals inf
initely often. $\blacksquare$
