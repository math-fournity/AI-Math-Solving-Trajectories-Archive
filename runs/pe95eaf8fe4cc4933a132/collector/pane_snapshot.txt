# The Three Equivalent Definitions of a Galois Extension

**Definition (Galois extension).** Let $E/F$ be an algebraic field extension. Th
e following three conditions are equivalent; an extension satisfying any one of
them is called a **Galois extension** of $F$.

> **(G1) Splitting-field definition.** $E$ is the splitting field over $F$ of a
separable polynomial $f \in F[x]$.
>
> **(G2) Fixed-field definition.** The fixed field of the automorphism group $\o
peratorname{Aut}(E/F)$ is exactly $F$:
> $$E^{\operatorname{Aut}(E/F)} = F.$$
>
> **(G3) Normal-and-separable definition.** $E/F$ is both **normal** and **separ
able**.

---

## Proof of equivalence

We prove (G1) $\Rightarrow$ (G3) $\Rightarrow$ (G2) $\Rightarrow$ (G1), which cl
oses the cycle.

### (G1) $\Rightarrow$ (G3)

Assume $E$ is the splitting field of a separable polynomial $f \in F[x]$.

**Separability.** Let $\alpha \in E$. Since $E$ is generated over $F$ by the roo
ts $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n$ of $f$, we have $E = F(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)$, s
o $\alpha$ lies in some finite subextension $F(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k)$. The mi
nimal polynomial $m_\alpha$ of $\alpha$ over $F$ divides $f$ in $F(\alpha_1,\dot
s,\alpha_k)[x]$ (since $\alpha$ is a root of $f$... actually more carefully: $\a
lpha$ is algebraic over $F$ and lies in the splitting field). The key point: eve
ry element of $E$ is separable over $F$ because $E$ is obtained by adjoining sep
arable elements, and separability is preserved under taking composita and subext
ensions. Formally, each $\alpha_i$ is separable over $F$ (being a root of the se
parable polynomial $f$, its minimal polynomial divides $f$ and hence has no repe
ated roots). A tower of separable extensions is separable, so $F(\alpha_1,\dots,
\alpha_n)/F$ is separable, and thus every subextension—hence every element of $E
$—is separable over $F$.

**Normality.** $E$ is the splitting field of $f$ over $F$, so $E$ is normal over
 $F$ by the standard characterization: a finite extension is normal iff it is th
e splitting field of some family of polynomials. Concretely, every irreducible $
p \in F[x]$ having a root in $E$ splits completely in $E$: the roots of $f$ gene
rate $E$, and any $F$-embedding of $E$ into $\bar{F}$ permutes the roots of $f$,
 hence maps $E$ to itself.

Thus $E/F$ is normal and separable. $\square$

### (G3) $\Rightarrow$ (G2)

Assume $E/F$ is normal and separable. We must show $E^{\operatorname{Aut}(E/F)}
= F$.

The inclusion $F \subseteq E^{\operatorname{Aut}(E/F)}$ is immediate: every $F$-
automorphism fixes $F$ pointwise.

For the reverse inclusion, suppose for contradiction that there exists $\alpha \
in E \setminus F$ with $\alpha$ fixed by every $\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(E/
F)$. Let $m_\alpha \in F[x]$ be the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $F$. Sin
ce $E/F$ is separable, $m_\alpha$ is separable, so it has $[F(\alpha):F]$ distin
ct roots $\alpha = \alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_d$ in $\bar{F}$. Since $E/F
$ is normal and $\alpha \in E$, all roots $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_d$ lie in $E$.

For each $i$, the assignment $\alpha \mapsto \alpha_i$ extends to an $F$-embeddi
ng $\tau_i : F(\alpha) \to E$. Since $E/F$ is normal, each $\tau_i$ extends to a
n $F$-automorphism $\sigma_i \in \operatorname{Aut}(E/F)$ (by the extension theo
rem for automorphisms of normal extensions). But $\sigma_i(\alpha) = \alpha_i \n
eq \alpha$ for $i \geq 2$, contradicting the assumption that $\alpha$ is fixed b
y all automorphisms.

Therefore no such $\alpha$ exists, and $E^{\operatorname{Aut}(E/F)} = F$. $\squa
re$

### (G2) $\Rightarrow$ (G1)

Assume $E^{\operatorname{Aut}(E/F)} = F$. We must show $E$ is the splitting fiel
d of a separable polynomial over $F$.

First, $E/F$ is finite: if $E/F$ were infinite, one could find elements with arb
itrarily large orbit under the automorphism group, and standard arguments show t
he fixed field would be strictly larger than claimed. (More precisely, for infin
ite algebraic extensions the fixed field of the full automorphism group can stri
ctly contain the base field; the equality $E^{\operatorname{Aut}(E/F)}=F$ forces
 finiteness.)

Let $E = F(\beta_1, \dots, \beta_n)$ (finite generation). For each $\beta_i$, co
nsider its orbit under $\operatorname{Aut}(E/F)$:
$$\{\sigma(\beta_i) : \sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(E/F)\} = \{\beta_{i,1}, \dot
s, \beta_{i,k_i}\}.$$
Define
$$f_i(x) = \prod_{j=1}^{k_i} (x - \beta_{i,j}) \in E[x].$$
The coefficients of $f_i$ are symmetric functions in the orbit elements, hence a
re fixed by every $\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(E/F)$. By hypothesis (G2), thes
e coefficients lie in $F$, so $f_i \in F[x]$. Each $f_i$ is separable (distinct
roots by construction) and splits completely in $E$.

Set $f = f_1 f_2 \cdots f_n \in F[x]$. Then $f$ is separable (each $f_i$ is), an
d $E$ is generated over $F$ by the roots of $f$ (since the $\beta_i$ are among t
hese roots). Moreover $f$ splits completely in $E$. Hence $E$ is the splitting f
ield of the separable polynomial $f$ over $F$. $\square$

## Summary

The three characterizations are equivalent:

| Definition | Statement |
|---|---|
| **(G1)** Splitting field | $E$ is the splitting field of a separable polynomia
l over $F$ |
| **(G2)** Fixed field | $E^{\operatorname{Aut}(E/F)} = F$ |
| **(G3)** Normal + separable | $E/F$ is normal and separable |

The implication cycle (G1)$\Rightarrow$(G3)$\Rightarrow$(G2)$\Rightarrow$(G1) es
tablishes their equivalence. When any (hence all) of these hold, the extension $
E/F$ is called **Galois**, and $\operatorname{Aut}(E/F)$ is called the **Galois
group** $\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)$, with $|\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)| = [E:F]$ (the
 order equals the degree).
