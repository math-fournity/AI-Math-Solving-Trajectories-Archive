## Solution

**Setup.** Place the semicircle $\Gamma$ with center $O$ at the origin and diame
ter $\overline{AB}$ on the $x$-axis: $A=(-7,0)$, $B=(7,0)$, so $\Gamma: x^2+y^2=
49$, $y\ge 0$. Let $P=(p,0)$ on the pier.

**Circle $\Omega$.** Since $\Omega$ is tangent to $AB$ at $P$ and must meet the
upper semicircular boundary, its center is $C=(p,r)$ with radius $r$:
$$(x-p)^2+(y-r)^2=r^2.$$

**Radical axis (line $QR$).** Subtracting $\Gamma$ from $\Omega$:
$$x^2+y^2-2px-2ry+p^2=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; px+ry=\frac{49+p^2}{2}.\tag{1}$$

**Using $\angle QPR=60°$.** Since $P,Q,R$ all lie on $\Omega$, $\angle QPR$ is a
n inscribed angle subtending chord $QR$. The corresponding central angle is $120
°$, so
$$QR = 2r\sin 60° = r\sqrt{3}.$$
Given $QR=3\sqrt{3}$, we get $\boxed{r=3}$.

**Using $QR=3\sqrt{3}$ on $\Gamma$.** The distance from $O$ to line $(1)$ is
$$d = \frac{49+p^2}{2\sqrt{p^2+r^2}}.$$
The chord length on $\Gamma$ (radius $7$) is $QR=2\sqrt{49-d^2}$, so $27=4(49-d^
2)$, giving $d^2=169/4$, i.e. $d=13/2$. Thus
$$\frac{(49+p^2)^2}{4(p^2+9)}=\frac{169}{4}\;\Longrightarrow\;(49+p^2)^2=169(p^2
+9).$$
Setting $u=p^2$:
$$u^2-71u+880=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; u=\frac{71\pm 39}{2}\in\{55,\,16\}.$$
Since $|p|\le 7$ (P lies on the pier), $p^2\le 49$, so $p^2=55$ is invalid. Henc
e $p^2=16$.

**Area of $\triangle PQR$.** The distance from $P=(p,0)$ to line $(1)$ (with $r=
3$) is
$$h = \frac{|p^2-(49+p^2)/2|}{\sqrt{p^2+9}}=\frac{49-p^2}{2\sqrt{p^2+9}}=\frac{4
9-16}{2\sqrt{25}}=\frac{33}{10}.$$
Therefore
$$[\triangle PQR]=\tfrac12\cdot QR\cdot h=\tfrac12\cdot 3\sqrt{3}\cdot\frac{33}{
10}=\frac{99\sqrt{3}}{20}.$$

Here $a=99$, $b=3$ (square-free), $c=20$, with $\gcd(99,20)=1$. Thus
$$a+b+c=99+3+20=\boxed{122}.$$
