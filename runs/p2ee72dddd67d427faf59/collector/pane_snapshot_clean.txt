## Proof

fty} \frac{z^n}{n!}$. Substituting $z = e^{ix}$:

$$e^{e^{ix}} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(e^{ix})^n}{n!} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \
frac{e^{inx}}{n!}.$$

**Step 2: Justify term-by-term integration.** Since $|e^{ix}| = 1$ for all real
$x$, we have $\left|\frac{e^{inx}}{n!}\right| = \frac{1}{n!}$. The series $\sum_
{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n!} = e$ converges. By the Weierstrass $M$-test, the ser
ies $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{e^{inx}}{n!}$ converges **uniformly** on $[0, 2\p
i]$. A uniformly convergent series of continuous functions may be integrated ter
m by term, so:

$$I = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n!}\int_0^{2\pi} e^{inx}\,dx.$$

**Step 3: Evaluate each integral.**

- **$n = 0$:** $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} e^{0}\,dx = \int_0^{2\pi} 1\,dx = 2\p
i$.

- **$n \geq 1$:** $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} e^{inx}\,dx = \left[\frac{e^{inx}}
{in}\right]_0^{2\pi} = \frac{e^{2\pi i n} - e^{0}}{in} = \frac{1 - 1}{in} = 0,$

  since $e^{2\pi i n} = 1$ for every integer $n$ (Euler's formula).

**Step 4: Sum.** Every term with $n \geq 1$ vanishes, leaving only the $n = 0$ t
erm:

$$I = \frac{1}{0!}\cdot 2\pi + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\cdot 0 = 1 \cdot
2\pi + 0 = 2\pi.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} e^{e^{ix}}\,dx = 2\pi.}$$
