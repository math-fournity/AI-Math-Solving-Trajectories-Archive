## Proof

Let $H$ be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space of dimension $n$, and let $\omega
:= e^{2\pi i/d}$. Let $\mathcal{U}_d$ denote the set of unitary operators on $H$
 whose spectrum is contained in $\{1,\omega,\omega^2,\dots,\omega^{d-1}\}$, equi
pped with the subspace topology inherited from the unitary group $\mathrm{U}(H)\
cong \mathrm{U}(n)$.

Every $U\in\mathcal{U}_d$ admits a unique spectral decomposition
$$
U=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\omega^k\,P_k,
$$
where the **spectral projections** $P_0,\dots,P_{d-1}$ are pairwise-orthogonal s
elf-adjoint projections satisfying $\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}P_k=I$. (Uniqueness follows
from the standard spectral theorem for normal operators on a finite-dimensional
space: the $P_k$ are the projections onto the eigenspaces $\ker(U-\omega^k I)$,
which are intrinsically determined by $U$.)

We claim the map
$$
\Phi:\mathcal{U}_d\longrightarrow \bigl(\mathrm{Proj}(H)\bigr)^d,\qquad \Phi(U):
=(P_0,\dots,P_{d-1})
$$
is continuous.

**Key observation — polynomial recovery via the discrete Fourier transform.** Si
nce the $P_k$ are pairwise orthogonal and sum to $I$, for every integer $j\ge 0$
,
$$
U^j=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\omega^{kj}\,P_k.
$$
Hence, for each $k\in\{0,\dots,d-1\}$,
$$
\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\omega^{-kj}\,U^j
=\sum_{\ell=0}^{d-1}P_\ell\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\omega^{(\ell-k)j}
=\sum_{\ell=0}^{d-1}P_\ell\cdot d\,\delta_{k\ell}
=d\,P_k,
$$
because the inner sum is a geometric series equal to $d$ when $\ell\equiv k\pmod
 d$ (and $0\le \ell,k\le d-1$ forces $\ell=k$) and to $0$ otherwise. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\;P_k=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\omega^{-kj}\,U^j,\qquad k=0,\dots,d-1.\
;}
$$

**Continuity.** The right-hand side is a **polynomial in the matrix entries of $
U$** (with fixed complex coefficients $\frac{1}{d}\omega^{-kj}$). Matrix multipl
ication and addition are continuous operations on $\mathrm{U}(H)\subset M_n(\mat
hbb{C})$, and a polynomial in $U$ is a composition of these. Concretely, each co
mponent map
$$
\Phi_k:\mathcal{U}_d\to\mathrm{Proj}(H),\qquad \Phi_k(U)=\tfrac{1}{d}\sum_{j=0}^
{d-1}\omega^{-kj}U^j
$$
is the restriction of a polynomial (hence continuous, indeed real-analytic) map
$M_n(\mathbb{C})\to M_n(\mathbb{C})$. The codomain restriction is legitimate bec
ause the formula produces a self-adjoint idempotent whenever $U\in\mathcal{U}_d$
 (verified above: it equals the genuine spectral projection $P_k$).

Since each component $\Phi_k$ is continuous, the tuple map $\Phi=(\Phi_0,\dots,\
Phi_{d-1})$ into the product space $\bigl(\mathrm{Proj}(H)\bigr)^d$ (with the pr
oduct topology, equivalently the topology inherited from $M_n(\mathbb{C})^d$) is
 continuous.

**Conclusion.** The map sending a unitary matrix whose eigenvalues are $d$-th ro
ots of unity to its tuple of spectral projections is continuous — in fact it is
the restriction of a polynomial (real-analytic) map on $M_n(\mathbb{C})$, given
explicitly by the discrete-Fourier-transform formula $P_k=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=0}^
{d-1}\omega^{-kj}U^j$.
