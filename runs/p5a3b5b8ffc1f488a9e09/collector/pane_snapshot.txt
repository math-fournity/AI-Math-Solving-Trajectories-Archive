# Proof

**Claim:** Yes, there exists a polynomial-time algorithm (in fact, a constant-ti
me algorithm given the degree $n$) to compute the arithmetic genus of a plane cu
rve $C \subset \mathbb{P}^2$ defined by a homogeneous polynomial $f(x,y,z) \in \
mathbb{F}_q[x,y,z]$ of degree $n$.

**Key fact.** The arithmetic genus $p_a(C)$ of a projective curve is a scheme-th
eoretic invariant determined entirely by the Hilbert polynomial of $C$:
$$p_a(C) = 1 - H_C(0).$$
For a **hypersurface** in $\mathbb{P}^2$, the Hilbert polynomial depends only on
 the degree $n$, not on the specific defining polynomial (and in particular not
on whether $C$ is singular).

**Derivation.** Let $C \subset \mathbb{P}^2$ be the curve cut out by a homogeneo
us polynomial of degree $n$. There is a short exact sequence of sheaves
$$0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^2}(-n) \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_{
\mathbb{P}^2} \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_C \longrightarrow 0,$$
which holds for *any* hypersurface of degree $n$, singular or not. The Hilbert p
olynomial of $\mathbb{P}^2$ is
$$H_{\mathbb{P}^2}(t) = \binom{t+2}{2} = \frac{(t+2)(t+1)}{2},$$
and that of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^2}(-n)$ is
$$H_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^2}(-n)}(t) = \binom{t-n+2}{2} = \frac{(t-n+2)(t-n+1
)}{2}.$$
By additivity on the exact sequence,
$$H_C(t) = \frac{(t+2)(t+1)}{2} - \frac{(t-n+2)(t-n+1)}{2}.$$
Expanding both numerators:
$$\frac{(t^2+3t+2) - (t^2 - 2nt + n^2 + 3t - 3n + 2)}{2} = \frac{2nt - n^2 + 3n}
{2} = nt - \frac{n(n-3)}{2}.$$
Therefore
$$p_a(C) = 1 - H_C(0) = 1 + \frac{n(n-3)}{2} = \frac{n^2 - 3n + 2}{2} = \frac{(n
-1)(n-2)}{2}.$$

**The algorithm.** The arithmetic genus equals $\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}$, a closed-
form expression depending only on $n$. The algorithm is:

> **Input:** the degree $n$ of $f$ (read off from any monomial of $f$).
> **Output:** $\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}$.

This requires a constant number of arithmetic operations on integers of size $O(
n^2)$, hence runs in time polynomial in $n$ (indeed, in $O(\log n)$ bit operatio
ns, far below any polynomial bound). Reading the input polynomial $f$ — which ha
s $\binom{n+2}{2} = O(n^2)$ coefficients — already costs $O(n^2)$ field-element
reads, so the overall procedure is polynomial time in $n$.

**Remark on the distinction with geometric genus.** The *geometric* genus $g = p
_a - \sum_p \delta_p$ (where $\delta_p$ are the delta-invariants of singular poi
nts) does depend on the singularities of $C$ and on the specific polynomial $f$;
 computing it requires analyzing the singular locus, which is a harder problem.
The arithmetic genus, by contrast, is a purely cohomological/Hilbert-polynomial
invariant and for plane curves is fixed by the degree alone.

**Conclusion.** Such an algorithm exists: it simply evaluates $\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}
{2}$, which is polynomial (constant) time in $n$.
