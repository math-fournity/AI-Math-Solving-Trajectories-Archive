## Answer

**No.** A primitive element (in the sense of generating the field extension) nee
d not be a generator of the multiplicative group. Here is a counterexample.

### Counterexample

Take $n = 4$, so the field is $\mathbb{F}_{16}$, with $|\mathbb{F}_{16}^{\times}
| = 2^4 - 1 = 15$.

Consider the polynomial
$$f(x) = x^4 + x^3 + x^2 + x + 1 = \Phi_5(x),$$
the 5th cyclotomic polynomial. Its roots are the primitive 5th roots of unity.

**Claim:** $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_2$.

The degree of the minimal polynomial of a primitive 5th root of unity over $\mat
hbb{F}_2$ equals $\operatorname{ord}_5(2)$, the multiplicative order of $2$ modu
lo $5$. Computing:
$$2^1 \equiv 2, \quad 2^2 \equiv 4, \quad 2^3 \equiv 3, \quad 2^4 \equiv 1 \pmod
{5}.$$
So $\operatorname{ord}_5(2) = 4 = \varphi(5)$, which means $\Phi_5(x)$ is irredu
cible over $\mathbb{F}_2$.

Now let $\alpha \in \overline{\mathbb{F}_2}$ be a root of $f$. Since $f$ is irre
ducible of degree $4$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$:
- $\mathbb{F}_2[\alpha] \cong \mathbb{F}_2[x]/(f)$ is a field of order $2^4 = 16
$, so $\alpha$ is a **primitive element** of the extension $\mathbb{F}_{16}/\mat
hbb{F}_2$.

But $\alpha$ is a root of $\Phi_5$, so $\alpha^5 = 1$, meaning the multiplicativ
e order of $\alpha$ divides $5$. Since $\alpha \neq 1$ (as $f(1) = 5 \equiv 1 \n
eq 0$ in $\mathbb{F}_2$), we have
$$\operatorname{ord}(\alpha) = 5 \neq 15 = 2^4 - 1.$$

Therefore $\alpha$ does **not** generate $\mathbb{F}_{16}^{\times}$.

### Conclusion

The two notions are distinct:
- **Primitive element** (field sense): $\mathbb{F}_2[\alpha] = \mathbb{F}_{2^n}$
, i.e., the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$ has degree $n$.
- **Primitive root** (multiplicative sense): $\operatorname{ord}(\alpha) = 2^n -
 1$.

The counterexample shows an element can satisfy the first condition without sati
sfying the second. An element $\alpha$ with $\mathbb{F}_2[\alpha] = \mathbb{F}_{
2^n}$ has multiplicative order dividing $2^n - 1$, and its order equals $2^n - 1
$ **if and only if** its minimal polynomial is a *primitive polynomial* (a speci
fic, stricter condition than mere irreducibility of degree $n$). Most irreducibl
e polynomials of degree $n$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$ are not primitive; only $\varphi
(2^n - 1)/n$ of the $N_q(n)$ irreducible polynomials of degree $n$ are primitive
.
