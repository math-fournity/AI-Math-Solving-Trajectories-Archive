## Proof

We seek the minimum of the Rayleigh-type quotient

$$J(u) = \frac{D(u)}{H(u)} = \frac{\int_D (u_x^2 + u_y^2)\,dx\,dy}{\int_D u^2\,d
x\,dy}$$

over all admissible functions $u \in H_0^1(D)$ (i.e., $u = 0$ on the boundary $S
 = \partial D$), with $u \not\equiv 0$.

---

### Step 1. Reduction to a constrained minimization

Since $J$ is homogeneous of degree zero ($J(cu) = J(u)$ for any constant $c \neq
 0$), minimizing $J$ is equivalent to minimizing $D(u)$ subject to the constrain
t $H(u) = 1$. We therefore introduce the Lagrangian

$$\mathcal{L}(u, \lambda) = D(u) - \lambda\,(H(u) - 1).$$

### Step 2. Euler–Lagrange equation

Let $\phi \in C_c^\infty(D)$ be an arbitrary test function (so $\phi = 0$ on $S$
). The first variation of $D$ in the direction $\phi$ is

$$\delta D(u)[\phi] = 2\int_D (u_x\,\phi_x + u_y\,\phi_y)\,dx\,dy.$$

Integrating by parts (Green's identity) and using $\phi|_S = 0$ so that the boun
dary term vanishes:

$$\delta D(u)[\phi] = -2\int_D (\Delta u)\,\phi\,dx\,dy,$$

where $\Delta u = u_{xx} + u_{yy}$. The first variation of $H$ is

$$\delta H(u)[\phi] = 2\int_D u\,\phi\,dx\,dy.$$

The stationarity condition $\delta \mathcal{L} = 0$ gives

$$-\Delta u = \lambda\, u \quad \text{in } D, \qquad u = 0 \quad \text{on } S. \
tag{$\star$}$$

Multiplying $(\star)$ by $u$ and integrating by parts (again the boundary term v
anishes):

$$\int_D |\nabla u|^2\,dx\,dy = \lambda \int_D u^2\,dx\,dy,$$

so the Lagrange multiplier equals the quotient:

$$\lambda = \frac{D(u)}{H(u)} = J(u).$$

Thus **every stationary point of $J$ is an eigenfunction of the Dirichlet Laplac
ian $-\Delta$ on $D$, and the value of $J$ at that point is the corresponding ei
genvalue.**

### Step 3. The minimum is the smallest (first) eigenvalue

The Dirichlet eigenvalue problem $-\Delta u = \lambda u$, $u|_S = 0$, on a bound
ed domain $D$ admits a discrete sequence of eigenvalues

$$0 < \lambda_1 \leq \lambda_2 \leq \lambda_3 \leq \cdots \to \infty$$

with corresponding $L^2$-orthonormal eigenfunctions $\{u_k\}$. This is the class
ical spectral theorem for the Dirichlet Laplacian (a self-adjoint, positive-defi
nite, compact-resolvent operator on $L^2(D)$).

By the **Rayleigh–Ritz variational principle** (a direct consequence of expandin
g any $u \in H_0^1(D)$ in the eigenbasis $\{u_k\}$ and using Parseval):

$$J(u) = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \lambda_k\, c_k^2}{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} c_k^2}
 \geq \lambda_1 \cdot \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} c_k^2}{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} c_k^2
} = \lambda_1,$$

where $c_k = \langle u, u_k\rangle_{L^2}$. Equality holds if and only if $c_k =
0$ for all $k \geq 2$, i.e., $u$ is a multiple of the first eigenfunction $u_1$.

### Step 4. Attainment

The infimum is attained: the first eigenfunction $u_1$ (which exists, is positiv
e inside $D$ by the maximum principle, and belongs to $H_0^1(D)$) satisfies

$$J(u_1) = \frac{D(u_1)}{H(u_1)} = \lambda_1.$$

Hence the minimum is achieved, not merely an infimum.

---

### Conclusion

The minimum value of $J(u) = D(u)/H(u)$ among all admissible functions vanishing
 on $S = \partial D$ is

$$\boxed{\lambda_1},$$

the **first (smallest) eigenvalue of the Dirichlet problem**

$$-\Delta u = \lambda\, u \;\text{ in } D, \qquad u = 0 \;\text{ on } S.$$

The minimizer is (up to a multiplicative constant) the corresponding first eigen
function $u_1$, characterized by $-\Delta u_1 = \lambda_1 u_1$ in $D$ and $u_1 =
 0$ on $S$.
