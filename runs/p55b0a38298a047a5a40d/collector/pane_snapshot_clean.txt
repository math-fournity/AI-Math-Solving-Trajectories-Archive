**Setup.** The nine weights are $\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,k\}$ with integer $k>8$. They
 are split into three triples, each satisfying *heaviest = sum of the other two*
.

**Parity constraint.** In any valid triple $\{a,b,c\}$ with $a\le b\le c$ and $c
=a+b$, the triple's total is $a+b+c=2c$, i.e. twice its heaviest element. Summin
g over the three trucks, the total weight equals twice the sum of the three per-
truck heaviest weights:
$$36+k \;=\; 2(h_1+h_2+h_3).$$
Hence $36+k$ is even, so **$k$ must be even**. (This already rules out $k=9,11,1
3,15$.)

**Locating $k$.** Since $k>8$ exceeds every other weight, $k$ is the heaviest in
 its truck, so $k=a+b$ for two distinct members $a,b\in\{1,\dots,8\}$. Let $h_1,
h_2$ be the heaviest of the other two trucks (both in $\{1,\dots,8\}$). Then
$$k = 36 - 2(h_1+h_2).$$
For $k>8$ we need $h_1+h_2<14$; for $k=a+b\le 7+8=15$ we need $h_1+h_2\ge 11$. T
hus $h_1+h_2\in\{11,12,13\}$, giving
$$k\in\{14,\,12,\,10\}.$$

**All three are achievable** — here are explicit valid partitions:

- $k=10$: $\{3,7,10\},\ \{1,4,5\},\ \{2,6,8\}$ — checks: $3+7=10,\ 1+4=5,\ 2+6=8
$. ✓
- $k=12$: $\{5,7,12\},\ \{1,3,4\},\ \{2,6,8\}$ — checks: $5+7=12,\ 1+3=4,\ 2+6=8
$. ✓
- $k=14$: $\{6,8,14\},\ \{1,3,4\},\ \{2,5,7\}$ — checks: $6+8=14,\ 1+3=4,\ 2+5=7
$. ✓

Each partition uses exactly $\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,k\}$ with no repetition, and ever
y triple satisfies heaviest = sum of the other two.

**No other values work.** The parity argument forces $k$ even; the only even val
ues $>8$ expressible as a sum of two distinct elements of $\{1,\dots,8\}$ are $1
0,12,14$, and a direct check confirms that every odd candidate ($9,11,13,15$) le
aves a 6-element remainder of $\{1,\dots,8\}$ that cannot be split into two vali
d triples (each candidate remainder-set was enumerated; none admits two disjoint
 triples of the form $a+b=c$).

**Conclusion.** The integer values of $k$ that allow the partitioning are
$$\boxed{k\in\{10,\,12,\,14\}}.$$
