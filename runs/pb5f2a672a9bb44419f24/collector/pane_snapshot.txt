## Proof

**Setup.** Let $\{X(t)\}_{t \geq 0}$ be a pure birth process with birth rates $\
lambda_k = k$ for $k = 1, 2, \dots$, starting at $X(0) = 1$. Let $T_4 = \inf\{t
\geq 0 : X(t) = 4\}$ be the first hitting time of state 4. We want
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^{T_4} X(t)\, dt\right].$$

**Step 1: Decompose via holding times.** A pure birth process is piecewise const
ant: it stays in each state $k$ for a random holding time $\tau_k$ and then jump
s to $k+1$. Since the rate out of state $k$ is $\lambda_k = k$, we have
$$\tau_k \sim \mathrm{Exp}(k), \qquad \mathbb{E}[\tau_k] = \frac{1}{k},$$
and the holding times $\tau_1, \tau_2, \tau_3, \dots$ are mutually independent (
by the strong Markov property / memoryless construction of the process).

Starting from state 1, the process visits states $1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 4$ in succes
sion, so
$$T_4 = \tau_1 + \tau_2 + \tau_3.$$

**Step 2: Express the integral as a sum.** During the interval the process is in
 state $k$, the integrand $X(t)$ equals $k$ constantly. Therefore
$$\int_0^{T_4} X(t)\, dt = 1\cdot \tau_1 + 2\cdot \tau_2 + 3\cdot \tau_3.$$

**Step 3: Take expectations.** By linearity of expectation and independence,
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^{T_4} X(t)\, dt\right] = 1\cdot\mathbb{E}[\tau_1] + 2
\cdot\mathbb{E}[\tau_2] + 3\cdot\mathbb{E}[\tau_3] = 1\cdot\frac{1}{1} + 2\cdot\
frac{1}{2} + 3\cdot\frac{1}{3} = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3.$$

**Conclusion.**
$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^{T_4} X(t)\, dt\right] = 3}.$$
