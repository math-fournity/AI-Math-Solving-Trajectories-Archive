## Answer

**No.** The implication does not hold. The fact that mollification sends $f$ int
o $L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}$ is a purely *local/integrability* consequence of $f\in
L^{1}\cap L^{p}$ (via Young's convolution inequality) and carries **no informati
on about spatial decay (moments)** of $f$. Membership in $L^{1}\cap L^{p}$ alone
 does not force $|x|^{p}|f|^{p}$ to be integrable. We exhibit an explicit counte
rexample.

### Counterexample

Work in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$. Fix $p=\tfrac{5}{4}\in(1,\infty)$ and pick
\[
\alpha=\frac{16}{5}=3.2,\qquad\text{so that}\quad 3<\alpha\le 1+\frac{3}{p}=\fra
c{17}{5}=3.4.
\]
Let $\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})$ be a smooth cutoff with $\chi\equiv
 0$ on $|x|\le \tfrac12$ and $\chi\equiv 1$ on $|x|\ge 1$, and set
\[
f(x)=\chi(x)\,|x|^{-\alpha}.
\]
Then $f$ is smooth and bounded near the origin, so all integrability questions a
re determined by the behaviour at infinity, where $f(x)\sim |x|^{-\alpha}$.

**1. $f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})$.** Using polar coordinates,
\[
\int_{|x|\ge 1}|f(x)|\,dx \sim 4\pi\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-\alpha}\,r^{2}\,dr
=4\pi\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{2-\alpha}\,dr.
\]
This converges iff $2-\alpha<-1$, i.e. $\alpha>3$. Since $\alpha=16/5>3$, we hav
e $f\in L^{1}$.

**2. $f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})$.** Similarly,
\[
\int_{|x|\ge 1}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx \sim 4\pi\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-\alpha p}\,r^{2}\,dr
=4\pi\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{2-\alpha p}\,dr,
\]
which converges iff $\alpha p>3$. Here $\alpha p=\tfrac{16}{5}\cdot\tfrac{5}{4}=
4>3$, so $f\in L^{p}$.

**3. Mollification lands in $L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}$ (consistent with the premise)
.** Let $\rho\in C^{\infty}_{c}$ be a standard mollifier with $\|\rho\|_{1}=1$,
and $f_{\varepsilon}=f*\rho_{\varepsilon}$. Since $\rho_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}$
with $\|\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{1}=1$ and also $\rho_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p'}$ (it
is bounded and compactly supported), Young's inequality gives
\[
\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{1}\le\|f\|_{1}\|\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{1}=\|f\|_{1}<\infty,
\qquad
\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\le\|f\|_{p}\|\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{p'}<\infty.
\]
So $f_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}$, exactly as asserted in the problem
. This is automatic from $f\in L^{1}\cap L^{p}$ and adds no extra constraint on
$f$.

**4. $|x|^{p}|f|^{p}\notin L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})$.** Compute
\[
\int_{|x|\ge 1}|x|^{p}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx
\sim 4\pi\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{p}\,r^{-\alpha p}\,r^{2}\,dr
=4\pi\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{\,p(1-\alpha)+2}\,dr.
\]
With $p=\tfrac54$ and $\alpha=\tfrac{16}{5}$,
\[
p(1-\alpha)+2=\tfrac54\!\left(1-\tfrac{16}{5}\right)+2
=\tfrac54\!\left(-\tfrac{11}{5}\right)+2
=-\tfrac{11}{4}+2=-\tfrac{3}{4}.
\]
Since $-\tfrac34>-1$, the integral $\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-3/4}\,dr$ **diverges**.
 Hence
\[
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{p}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx=+\infty.
\]

### Why the implication fails conceptually

Young's inequality for $f_{\varepsilon}=f*\rho_{\varepsilon}$ only controls *Leb
esgue norms* of $f_{\varepsilon}$ in terms of Lebesgue norms of $f$ and $\rho_{\
varepsilon}$. It is a statement about *size/integrability*, completely blind to
*where* the mass of $f$ sits. The moment weight $|x|^{p}$ probes the **tail deca
y** of $f$, which is an independent piece of information not encoded in any $L^{
q}$-norm. A function can be in every $L^{q}$ ($1\le q\le\infty$) yet have arbitr
arily heavy tails (as the power-law family $|x|^{-\alpha}$ with $\alpha$ just ab
ove the $L^{1}$ threshold $3$ demonstrates). Therefore no conclusion about $|x|^
{p}|f|^{p}\in L^{1}$ can be drawn from the mollification landing in $L^{1}\cap L
^{\infty}$.

### Conclusion

The implication is **false**. We have produced $f\in L^{1}\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^
{3})$ (with $p=5/4\in(1,\infty)$) whose mollification $f_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}\
cap L^{\infty}$ by Young's inequality, yet $|x|^{p}|f|^{p}\notin L^{1}(\mathbb{R
}^{3})$. Thus the answer to the question is **no**, the implication does not hol
d in general.
