## Proof

**Theorem.** Let $M$ be a closed (i.e. compact, without boundary) smooth manifol
d and $X$ a smooth vector field on $M$. Then the flow of $X$ exists for all time
 $t \in \mathbb{R}$.

**Proof.**

By the fundamental theorem on ordinary differential equations (Picard–Lindelöf,
transported to manifolds via local charts), for every $p \in M$ there exists a u
nique maximal integral curve
$$\gamma_p : (a_p, b_p) \longrightarrow M, \qquad \gamma_p(0)=p,\qquad \gamma_p'
(t)=X\bigl(\gamma_p(t)\bigr),$$
where $-\infty \le a_p < 0 < b_p \le +\infty$, and the interval $(a_p,b_p)$ is m
aximal in the sense that $\gamma_p$ admits no extension to a larger interval as
an integral curve. The flow is $\Phi_t(p)=\gamma_p(t)$, defined on the open set
$\mathcal{D}=\bigl\{(t,p): t\in(a_p,b_p)\bigr\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}\times M$. We
must show $\mathcal{D}=\mathbb{R}\times M$.

It suffices to prove $b_p=+\infty$ for every $p$; the argument for $a_p=-\infty$
 is identical (applied to $-X$).

**Step 1: A Riemannian metric and a global speed bound.**
Endow $M$ with any Riemannian metric $g$ (such metrics always exist on a smooth
manifold, e.g. by partition of unity). The function $|X|_g:M\to\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0
}$, $q\mapsto\sqrt{g_q(X_q,X_q)}$, is continuous. Since $M$ is compact, it is bo
unded: there exists $C>0$ with
$$|X|_g(q)\le C \qquad\text{for all } q\in M.$$

**Step 2: The curve has bounded speed, hence is Lipschitz.**
For $s<t$ in $(a_p,b_p)$,
$$d_g\bigl(\gamma_p(s),\gamma_p(t)\bigr)\le\int_s^t\bigl|\gamma_p'(\tau)\bigr|_g
\,d\tau=\int_s^t\bigl|X\bigl(\gamma_p(\tau)\bigr)\bigr|_g\,d\tau\le C\,(t-s),$$
where $d_g$ is the Riemannian distance. Thus $\gamma_p$ is $C$-Lipschitz.

**Step 3: If $b_p<\infty$, the curve converges as $t\to b_p^-$.**
Suppose for contradiction that $b_p<+\infty$. Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose $\de
lta=\varepsilon/C$. For $s,t\in(b_p-\delta,\,b_p)$,
$$d_g\bigl(\gamma_p(s),\gamma_p(t)\bigr)\le C\,|t-s|<C\delta=\varepsilon.$$
Hence $\gamma_p(t)$ is a Cauchy net as $t\to b_p^-$. A compact Riemannian manifo
ld is complete as a metric space (Hopf–Rinow), so Cauchy sequences converge; the
refore there exists
$$q:=\lim_{t\to b_p^-}\gamma_p(t)\in M.$$

**Step 4: Extending past $b_p$ — contradiction with maximality.**
By local existence of integral curves, there is $\varepsilon>0$ and a smooth cur
ve $\sigma:(b_p-\varepsilon,\,b_p+\varepsilon)\to M$ with $\sigma(b_p)=q$ and $\
sigma'(t)=X(\sigma(t))$. On the overlap $(b_p-\varepsilon,\,b_p)$ both $\sigma$
and $\gamma_p$ are integral curves of $X$ passing through $\gamma_p(b_p-\varepsi
lon/2)$; by uniqueness they coincide there. Hence
$$\tilde\gamma(t)=\begin{cases}\gamma_p(t),& t\in(a_p,b_p),\\ \sigma(t),& t\in[b
_p,b_p+\varepsilon)\end{cases}$$
is a well-defined integral curve extending $\gamma_p$ beyond $b_p$. This contrad
icts the maximality of $(a_p,b_p)$.

Therefore $b_p=+\infty$. Applying the same reasoning to $-X$ gives $a_p=-\infty$
.

**Conclusion.** Every maximal integral curve is defined on all of $\mathbb{R}$,
so the flow $\Phi:\mathbb{R}\times M\to M$ is globally defined. $\blacksquare$
