**Proof.**

Let $U$ be a neighborhood of $x \in X$. We must produce a compact set $C \subset
eq U$ that is a neighborhood of $x$ (i.e. $x \in \operatorname{int}(C)$).

Since $X$ is locally compact, $x$ possesses a compact neighborhood: there is a c
ompact set $K \subseteq X$ with $x \in \operatorname{int}(K)$.

The subspace $K$ is compact and Hausdorff (being a subspace of a Hausdorff space
), hence $K$ is regular. The set $U \cap \operatorname{int}(K)$ is an open neigh
borhood of $x$ in $X$, and it is contained in $K$, so it is also an open neighbo
rhood of $x$ in the subspace $K$. By regularity of $K$, there exists a set $O$ t
hat is open in $K$, with
$$x \in O \subseteq \operatorname{cl}_{K}(O) \subseteq U \cap \operatorname{int}
(K).$$

Since $O$ is open in $K$, write $O = K \cap O'$ for some $O'$ open in $X$. Set
$$W := O' \cap \operatorname{int}(K).$$
Then $W$ is open in $X$, and $x \in O \subseteq W \subseteq O \subseteq K$ (in f
act $W = O$ since $O \subseteq \operatorname{int}(K)$ and $O = K \cap O' \subset
eq \operatorname{int}(K) \cap O'$, while $W = \operatorname{int}(K) \cap O' \sub
seteq K \cap O' = O$; so $W = O$). In particular $x \in W$.

Now consider $C := \operatorname{cl}_{X}(W)$. Since $W \subseteq K$ and $K$ is c
ompact (hence closed in the Hausdorff space $X$), we have
$$C = \operatorname{cl}_{X}(W) \subseteq K.$$
Being a closed subset of the compact set $K$, $C$ is compact. Moreover, the clos
ure of $W$ in $X$ equals the closure of $W$ in $K$ (since $K$ is closed in $X$),
 so
$$C = \operatorname{cl}_{X}(W) = \operatorname{cl}_{K}(W) = \operatorname{cl}_{K
}(O) \subseteq U \cap \operatorname{int}(K) \subseteq U.$$
Finally, $W$ is open in $X$ with $x \in W \subseteq C$, so $x \in W \subseteq \o
peratorname{int}(C)$; thus $C$ is a neighborhood of $x$.

Therefore $C = \operatorname{cl}_{X}(W)$ is a compact neighborhood of $x$ contai
ned in $U$. $\blacksquare$
