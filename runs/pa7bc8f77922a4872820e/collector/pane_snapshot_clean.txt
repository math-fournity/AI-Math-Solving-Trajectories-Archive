## Solution

**Answer: False.**

### Proof

The statement claims that every curve of constant width must be a finite union o
f arcs of circles. We show this is false by exhibiting (indeed, by showing the a
bundance of) curves of constant width that are not finite unions of circular arc
s.

**Background on constant-width curves via support functions.**

A convex curve of constant width $w$ can be described by its support function $h
(\theta)$, where $h(\theta)+h(\theta+\pi)=w$ for all $\theta$. The radius of cur
vature is
$$\rho(\theta) = h(\theta) + h''(\theta),$$
and it satisfies
$$\rho(\theta) + \rho(\theta+\pi) = w, \qquad \rho(\theta) > 0.$$
Conversely, given any positive, sufficiently smooth function $\rho$ on $[0,2\pi)
$ satisfying $\rho(\theta)+\rho(\theta+\pi)=w$, one obtains a (smooth) curve of
constant width $w$.

**Key observation.** A circular arc has *constant* radius of curvature. Therefor
e, a curve that is a finite union of arcs of circles has a radius of curvature $
\rho(\theta)$ that is piecewise constant (constant on each arc, with jumps at th
e finitely many junction points).

**Constructing a counterexample.**

Choose any smooth, strictly positive function $f:[0,\pi]\to(0,w)$ that is *not*
constant on any subinterval — for instance,
$$f(\theta) = \tfrac{w}{2} + \tfrac{w}{4}\sin\theta, \qquad \theta\in[0,\pi].$$
Extend to $[0,2\pi)$ by setting $\rho(\theta+\pi) = w - f(\theta)$, i.e.,
$$\rho(\theta) = \begin{cases} \tfrac{w}{2}+\tfrac{w}{4}\sin\theta & 0\le\theta<
\pi,\\ w-\bigl(\tfrac{w}{2}+\tfrac{w}{4}\sin(\theta-\pi)\bigr) = \tfrac{w}{2}-\t
frac{w}{4}\sin(\theta-\pi) & \pi\le\theta<2\pi. \end{cases}$$
This $\rho$ is smooth, strictly positive, and satisfies $\rho(\theta)+\rho(\thet
a+\pi)=w$. It therefore defines a smooth curve of constant width $w$.

Since $\rho(\theta)=\tfrac{w}{2}+\tfrac{w}{4}\sin\theta$ is a *non-constant anal
ytic function* on $(0,\pi)$, it is not constant on any open subinterval. Hence $
\rho$ is not piecewise constant — it is not even locally constant anywhere excep
t at isolated critical points.

**Why this rules out a finite union of circular arcs.** A finite union of circul
ar arcs yields a radius of curvature that is piecewise constant with finitely ma
ny pieces. Such a function is constant on each of finitely many open intervals c
overing the circle (up to finitely many points). Our $\rho$ is non-constant on *
every* open subinterval of $(0,\pi)$, so it cannot coincide with any piecewise-c
onstant function with finitely many pieces. Therefore the resulting curve of con
stant width is **not** a finite union of arcs of circles.

**Remark.** This is not a pathological isolated example: the space of smooth con
stant-width curves is infinite-dimensional (parametrized by an arbitrary smooth
positive function on $[0,\pi]$ with the single constraint above), whereas finite
 unions of circular arcs form a finite-dimensional family. Thus "generic" curves
 of constant width are not finite unions of circular arcs. The Reuleaux triangle
 and its polygonal generalizations (Reuleaux polygons) are special, highly non-g
eneric members of the class.

### Conclusion

There exist curves of constant width that are not finite unions of arcs of circl
es. The statement is **False**.
