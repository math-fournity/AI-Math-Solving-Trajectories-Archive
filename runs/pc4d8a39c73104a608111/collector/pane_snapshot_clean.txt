## Proof

Let $G$ be a compact connected Lie group. We show $G$ is metrizable by establish
ing the hypotheses of the Urysohn metrization theorem, and then we note that the
 metric can be chosen to respect the compactness and connectedness.

**Step 1. $G$ is a topological manifold, hence Hausdorff and second countable.**

By definition, a Lie group is a group equipped with the structure of a smooth ma
nifold such that the group operations (multiplication $G \times G \to G$ and inv
ersion $G \to G$) are smooth. A smooth manifold is, in particular, a topological
 manifold: it is locally Euclidean, Hausdorff, and second countable. (Second cou
ntability is part of the standard definition of a manifold; it rules out the "lo
ng line"–type pathologies.) Therefore $G$ is Hausdorff and second countable.

**Step 2. $G$ is regular.**

Every Hausdorff space that is second countable is regular. Indeed, second counta
bility implies paracompactness (every open cover has a locally finite refinement
), and a paracompact Hausdorff space is regular. More concretely, given a point
$x$ and a closed set $F$ not containing $x$, second countability plus the Hausdo
rff property let us separate them by a countable basis argument: for each $y \in
 F$ choose disjoint basic neighborhoods $U_y \ni y$ and $V_y \ni x$; a finite su
bcover of $\{U_y\}$ over $F$ (using that $F$ is closed in a Lindelöf space, or d
irectly) yields disjoint open neighborhoods of $x$ and $F$.

Thus $G$ is regular and second countable.

**Step 3. Apply the Urysohn metrization theorem.**

The Urysohn metrization theorem states: *every regular, second countable topolog
ical space is metrizable.* Since $G$ is regular (Step 2) and second countable (S
tep 1), there exists a metric $d$ on $G$ that induces the original topology.

**Step 4. Compactness and connectedness are topological invariants.**

The properties "compact" and "connected" are properties of the topology, not of
any particular metric. Since $d$ induces exactly the original topology of $G$, t
he space $(G, d)$ is compact and connected if and only if $G$ with its original
topology is compact and connected. By hypothesis $G$ is compact and connected, s
o $(G, d)$ is a compact, connected metric space.

**Step 5 (Remark). A canonical metric exists.**

In fact one can write down an explicit metric. Since $G$ is a compact Lie group,
 it admits a bi-invariant Riemannian metric $g$ obtained by averaging any inner
product on the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ over the Haar measure (which has finit
e total mass by compactness). The Riemannian distance
$$d(x, y) = \inf_{\gamma} \int_0^1 \sqrt{g_{\gamma(t)}(\dot\gamma(t), \dot\gamma
(t))}\, dt,$$
where the infimum is over smooth curves joining $x$ to $y$, is a metric inducing
 the manifold topology. Bi-invariance makes $d$ left- and right-invariant, and c
ompactness guarantees the diameter is finite. This gives a concrete metric reali
zing the metrizability, but the existence already follows abstractly from the Ur
ysohn theorem above.

## Conclusion

A compact connected Lie group $G$ is a smooth manifold, hence Hausdorff and seco
nd countable, hence regular, hence metrizable by the Urysohn metrization theorem
. Since the metric induces the original topology, $G$ remains compact and connec
ted as a metric space. Therefore the topology on a compact connected Lie group i
s metrizable.
