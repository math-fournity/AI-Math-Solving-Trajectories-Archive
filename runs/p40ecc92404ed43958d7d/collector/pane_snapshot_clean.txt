## Problem

Determine whether the Lebesgue measure of a 2-dimensional Cantor set is 0.

## Proof

**Construction of the 2-dimensional Cantor set (Cantor dust).**

Let $C \subset [0,1]$ denote the standard middle-thirds Cantor set, obtained by
repeatedly removing the open middle third of each remaining interval. The **2-di
mensional Cantor set** is the Cartesian product

$$
\mathcal{C}_2 := C \times C \;\subset\; [0,1]^2.
$$

Equivalently, it is constructed directly in the plane by the following iterative
 procedure:

- **Stage 0.** Start with the closed unit square $Q_0 = [0,1]^2$.
- **Stage $n \to n+1$.** From each closed square $Q$ remaining at stage $n$, sub
divide $Q$ into a $3 \times 3$ grid of $9$ congruent closed subsquares of side $
3^{-(n+1)}$, and **keep only the 4 corner subsquares**, discarding the other 5.

By construction, $\mathcal{C}_2 = \bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty} Q_n$, where $Q_n$ is th
e union of all squares surviving stage $n$.

---

**Counting the surviving squares.**

At stage $n$, the number of surviving squares is $4^n$ (we keep 4 out of 9 at ea
ch step, starting from $4^0 = 1$). Each surviving square has side length $3^{-n}
$, hence area $3^{-2n} = 9^{-n}$.

Therefore the total area (2-dimensional Lebesgue measure) of the stage-$n$ appro
ximation is

$$
m_2(Q_n) \;=\; 4^n \cdot 9^{-n} \;=\; \left(\frac{4}{9}\right)^{\!n}.
$$

---

**The measure is zero.**

Since $\mathcal{C}_2 \subseteq Q_n$ for every $n$, by monotonicity and subadditi
vity of Lebesgue measure,

$$
0 \;\le\; m_2(\mathcal{C}_2) \;\le\; m_2(Q_n) \;=\; \left(\frac{4}{9}\right)^{\!
n} \quad \text{for all } n \ge 0.
$$

Because $0 < 4/9 < 1$, we have $(4/9)^n \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$. Taking the lim
it,

$$
m_2(\mathcal{C}_2) = 0.
$$

---

**Alternative argument via product measure (Fubini).**

The standard 1-dimensional Cantor set $C$ satisfies $m_1(C) = 0$ (its stage-$n$
cover has total length $(2/3)^n \to 0$). Since $\mathcal{C}_2 = C \times C$ is a
 measurable rectangle in $\mathbb{R}^2$, the product-measure identity gives

$$
m_2(C \times C) = m_1(C)\cdot m_1(C) = 0 \cdot 0 = 0,
$$

confirming the result by an independent route.

---

**Conclusion.** The 2-dimensional Cantor set (Cantor dust) has Lebesgue measure
**zero**. $\blacksquare$
