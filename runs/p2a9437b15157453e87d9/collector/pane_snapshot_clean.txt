## Proof

**Yes**, such a measure exists. I give two examples: a trivial atomic one and a
more interesting non-atomic singular one.

---

### Example 1 (atomic): the Dirac measure at the fixed point

The point $x = 0 \in [0,1)$ is a fixed point of $T$, since $T(0) = 2 \cdot 0 \bm
od 1 = 0$. Define $\mu = \delta_0$, the Dirac probability measure concentrated a
t $0$. For any Borel set $A \subseteq [0,1)$,

$$\mu(T^{-1}A) = 1 \iff 0 \in T^{-1}A \iff T(0) \in A \iff 0 \in A \iff \mu(A) =
 1,$$

so $\mu(T^{-1}A) = \mu(A)$; $\mu$ is $T$-invariant. It is a probability measure
and it is not Lebesgue measure (it is purely atomic). Hence it is an invariant n
on-Lebesgue probability measure.

---

### Example 2 (non-atomic, singular): a biased Bernoulli measure

This gives a richer example that is also singular with respect to Lebesgue.

**Conjugacy to the shift.** Let $\Sigma = \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ and let $\sigma
: \Sigma \to \Sigma$ be the one-sided left shift, $\sigma(a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots)
= (a_2, a_3, \dots)$. The binary-expansion map
$$\varphi : \Sigma \to [0,1), \qquad \varphi((a_n)) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{
a_n}{2^n}$$
satisfies $T \circ \varphi = \varphi \circ \sigma$ (this is exactly the identity
 $2 \sum a_n/2^n \bmod 1 = \sum a_{n+1}/2^n$). The map $\varphi$ is a Borel isom
orphism up to the countable set of dyadic rationals (which have two binary expan
sions), a set that is null for every measure we consider below.

**The biased Bernoulli measures.** For $p \in (0,1)$, let $\mu_p$ be the product
 measure on $\Sigma$ under which each coordinate is independent, equal to $1$ wi
th probability $p$ and $0$ with probability $1-p$. Being a product measure, $\mu
_p$ is $\sigma$-invariant: dropping the first coordinate leaves the law of the r
emaining tail unchanged.

Define the pushforward $\nu_p := \varphi_* \mu_p$ on $[0,1)$. Since $T \circ \va
rphi = \varphi \circ \sigma$ and $\mu_p$ is $\sigma$-invariant,
$$\nu_p(T^{-1}A) = \mu_p(\varphi^{-1}(T^{-1}A)) = \mu_p(\sigma^{-1}(\varphi^{-1}
A)) = \mu_p(\varphi^{-1}A) = \nu_p(A),$$
so $\nu_p$ is a $T$-invariant probability measure on $[0,1)$.

**Identifying Lebesgue.** When $p = \tfrac12$, $\mu_{1/2}$ is the fair-coin prod
uct measure, and $\nu_{1/2} = \varphi_*\mu_{1/2}$ is exactly Lebesgue measure on
 $[0,1)$ (each binary digit is an independent fair bit, which is the standard co
nstruction of Lebesgue measure).

**Singularity for $p \neq \tfrac12$.** By the strong law of large numbers, for $
\mu_p$-a.e. sequence $(a_n)$,
$$\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} p.$$
Set
$$A_p = \Bigl\{x \in [0,1) : \text{the frequency of }1\text{s in the binary expa
nsion of }x\text{ converges to }p\Bigr\}.$$
Then $\nu_p(A_p) = \mu_p(\varphi^{-1}A_p) = 1$. On the other hand, Lebesgue meas
ure equals $\nu_{1/2}$, so by the same strong law applied with $p = \tfrac12$, L
ebesgue-a.e. $x$ has digit frequency converging to $\tfrac12$, i.e. Lebesgue$(A_
p) = 0$ whenever $p \neq \tfrac12$. Thus $\nu_p$ and Lebesgue are mutually singu
lar:
$$\nu_p(A_p) = 1, \qquad \lambda(A_p) = 0.$$

In particular $\nu_p \neq \lambda$.

**Non-atomicity.** Each singleton $\{x\}$ corresponds under $\varphi^{-1}$ to at
 most two sequences, each of $\mu_p$-measure $0$ (a single infinite product of f
actors $< 1$), so $\nu_p(\{x\}) = 0$; $\nu_p$ is non-atomic.

---

### Conclusion

Taking, say, $p = \tfrac13$, the measure $\nu_{1/3}$ is a $T$-invariant, non-ato
mic probability measure on $[0,1)$ that is singular with respect to Lebesgue mea
sure, hence certainly not Lebesgue measure. (The Dirac measure $\delta_0$ alread
y gives a simpler atomic example.) Therefore the answer to the question is

$$\boxed{\text{Yes.}}$$
