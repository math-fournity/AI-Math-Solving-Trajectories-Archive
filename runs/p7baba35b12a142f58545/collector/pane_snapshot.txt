**Proof.**

A path from $(0,0)$ to $(2n,2n)$ consists of $2n$ East steps $E=(1,0)$ and $2n$
North steps $N=(0,1)$, arranged in a sequence of length $4n$. The total number o
f such paths is $\binom{4n}{2n}$.

**Step 1: Area below the path.** The path divides the square of area $4n^2$ (whi
ch is even) into two regions. Thus both regions have even area if and only if th
e region below the path (containing the bottom edge) has even area.

Let the $k$-th East step (counting from $1$) occur at position $i_k$ in the sequ
ence (so $1 \le i_1 < i_2 < \cdots < i_{2n} \le 4n$). Just before this step, $k-
1$ East steps and $i_k - k$ North steps have occurred, so the current height is
$i_k - k$. Each East step at height $h$ contributes a $1 \times h$ rectangle to
the area below the path, so

$$A = \sum_{k=1}^{2n}(i_k - k) = \sum_{k=1}^{2n} i_k - \frac{2n(2n+1)}{2} = \sum
_{k=1}^{2n} i_k - n(2n+1).$$

**Step 2: Parity condition.** Since $n(2n+1) = 2n^2 + n \equiv n \pmod{2}$, we h
ave

$$A \equiv \sum_{k=1}^{2n} i_k + n \pmod{2}.$$

Writing $b_j = 1$ if step $j$ is East and $b_j = 0$ otherwise, we have $\sum_{k=
1}^{2n} i_k = \sum_{j=1}^{4n} j\, b_j$. Since $j\, b_j \equiv b_j \pmod{2}$ for
odd $j$ and $j\, b_j \equiv 0 \pmod{2}$ for even $j$,

$$\sum_{k=1}^{2n} i_k \equiv \sum_{\substack{j=1\\j\text{ odd}}}^{4n} b_j \pmod{
2}.$$

Let $a$ denote the number of East steps in odd positions (positions $1,3,\dots,4
n-1$) and $b$ the number of East steps in even positions. Then $a+b = 2n$, and t
he condition $A \equiv 0\pmod{2}$ becomes

$$a \equiv n \pmod{2}.$$

**Step 3: Counting.** Among the $4n$ positions, exactly $2n$ are odd and $2n$ ar
e even. The number of paths with $a$ East steps in odd positions and $b = 2n - a
$ East steps in even positions is $\binom{2n}{a}\binom{2n}{b}$. We must sum over
 all $a$ with $a \equiv n \pmod{2}$:

$$S = \sum_{\substack{a=0\\a\,\equiv\, n\!\!\!\pmod{2}}}^{2n} \binom{2n}{a}\bino
m{2n}{2n-a}.$$

Let $S' = \sum_{a\,\not\equiv\, n\!\!\!\pmod{2}} \binom{2n}{a}\binom{2n}{2n-a}$.
 By Vandermonde's identity,

$$S + S' = \sum_{a=0}^{2n}\binom{2n}{a}\binom{2n}{2n-a} = \binom{4n}{2n}.$$

For the alternating sum, using $(-1)^{a-n} = (-1)^n(-1)^a$:

$$S - S' = \sum_{a=0}^{2n}(-1)^{a-n}\binom{2n}{a}\binom{2n}{2n-a} = (-1)^n \sum_
{a=0}^{2n}(-1)^{a}\binom{2n}{a}\binom{2n}{2n-a}.$$

The inner sum is the coefficient of $x^{2n}$ in $(1-x)^{2n}(1+x)^{2n} = (1-x^2)^
{2n} = \sum_{j=0}^{2n}\binom{2n}{j}(-1)^j x^{2j}$, which is $(-1)^n\binom{2n}{n}
$. Therefore

$$S - S' = (-1)^n \cdot (-1)^n \binom{2n}{n} = \binom{2n}{n}.$$

**Step 4: Conclusion.** Solving the system $S + S' = \binom{4n}{2n}$ and $S - S'
 = \binom{2n}{n}$ gives

$$S = \frac{\binom{4n}{2n} + \binom{2n}{n}}{2}.$$

This is exactly the number of paths dividing the square into two regions of even
 area. $\blacksquare$
