## Solution

**Claim:** Yes. For any collection of $d \times d$ matrices $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_
n$ (with $d \geq 1$), there exists a positive vector $x = (x_1, \dots, x_n) \in
\mathbb{R}^n_{>0}$ such that
$$\|x_1 A_1 + x_2 A_2 + \cdots + x_n A_n\|_* < d.$$

**Proof.**

The key property we use is the **positive homogeneity** of the nuclear norm: for
 every scalar $t > 0$ and every matrix $M$,
$$\|tM\|_* = t\,\|M\|_*.$$

Pick any positive vector $x \in \mathbb{R}^n_{>0}$ (for instance, $x = (1, 1, \d
ots, 1)$), and set
$$M(x) := x_1 A_1 + x_2 A_2 + \cdots + x_n A_n.$$

We distinguish two cases.

**Case 1: $M(x) = 0$.** Then $\|M(x)\|_* = 0 < d$ (since $d \geq 1$), and $x$ it
self is the desired positive vector.

**Case 2: $M(x) \neq 0$.** Then $\|M(x)\|_* > 0$. For any $t > 0$, the scaled ve
ctor $tx = (t x_1, \dots, t x_n)$ is still strictly positive (all entries $> 0$)
, and by homogeneity,
$$\|M(tx)\|_* = \big\|t\,M(x)\big\|_* = t\,\|M(x)\|_*.$$
Choosing
$$t < \frac{d}{\|M(x)\|_*}$$
(which is possible since the right-hand side is a positive real number) yields
$$\|M(tx)\|_* = t\,\|M(x)\|_* < d.$$

In both cases we have produced a positive vector $y$ (either $x$ or $tx$) with $
\|y_1 A_1 + \cdots + y_n A_n\|_* < d$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The argument does not depend on any special property of the matrices
 $A_1, \dots, A_n$ other than their common dimension $d$. The essential ingredie
nts are (i) the positive homogeneity of the nuclear norm, and (ii) the fact that
 the set of positive vectors $\mathbb{R}^n_{>0}$ is closed under positive scalin
g — scaling a positive vector by any $t > 0$ keeps it positive while the nuclear
 norm scales linearly, so it can be driven below any positive threshold $d$.
