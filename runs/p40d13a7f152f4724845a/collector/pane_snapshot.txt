## Proof

We determine the weak-* limit of $\mu_n = n\,\phi(nx)\,dx$ on $[-1,1]$, where $\
phi\in C_c^\infty([-1,1])$ with $\int_{-1}^{1}\phi(x)\,dx = 2$.

**Definition of weak-* convergence.** A sequence of measures $\mu_n$ converges t
o $\mu$ in the weak-* topology (i.e., vaguely / weakly against continuous test f
unctions) if for every $f\in C([-1,1])$,
$$\int_{-1}^{1} f(x)\,\mu_n(dx) \;\longrightarrow\; \int_{-1}^{1} f(x)\,\mu(dx).
$$

**Compute the pairing.** For any $f\in C([-1,1])$,
$$\int_{-1}^{1} f(x)\,\mu_n(dx) = \int_{-1}^{1} f(x)\,n\,\phi(nx)\,dx.$$

Perform the substitution $u = nx$, so $du = n\,dx$:
$$\int_{-1}^{1} f(x)\,n\,\phi(nx)\,dx = \int_{-n}^{n} f\!\left(\tfrac{u}{n}\righ
t)\phi(u)\,du.$$

Since $\phi$ has compact support in $[-1,1]$, the integrand vanishes outside $\o
peratorname{supp}\phi\subseteq[-1,1]$, so for all $n\ge 1$ the integral is effec
tively
$$\int_{-1}^{1} f\!\left(\tfrac{u}{n}\right)\phi(u)\,du.$$

**Take the limit.** For each fixed $u\in[-1,1]$,
$$f\!\left(\tfrac{u}{n}\right) \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} f(0)$$
by continuity of $f$. Moreover, the integrand is dominated:
$$\left|f\!\left(\tfrac{u}{n}\right)\phi(u)\right| \le \|f\|_\infty\,|\phi(u)|,$
$
and $\|f\|_\infty\,|\phi(u)|$ is integrable since $\phi$ is continuous with comp
act support. By the Dominated Convergence Theorem,
$$\int_{-1}^{1} f\!\left(\tfrac{u}{n}\right)\phi(u)\,du \;\longrightarrow\; \int
_{-1}^{1} f(0)\,\phi(u)\,du = f(0)\int_{-1}^{1}\phi(u)\,du = 2\,f(0).$$

**Identify the limit measure.** The functional $f\mapsto 2\,f(0)$ is exactly the
 action of the measure $2\,\delta_0$ (twice the Dirac mass at the origin):
$$\int_{-1}^{1} f(x)\,(2\delta_0)(dx) = 2\,f(0).$$

**Conclusion.** Since for every $f\in C([-1,1])$,
$$\int f\,d\mu_n \longrightarrow \int f\,d(2\delta_0),$$
the sequence $\mu_n$ converges to $2\,\delta_0$ in the weak-* topology. The fact
or $2$ arises precisely because $\phi$ is normalized to have integral $2$ rather
 than $1$; the mass is conserved and concentrated at the origin as the support o
f $n\phi(nx)$ shrinks to $\{0\}$.

$$\boxed{\mu_n \xrightarrow{w^*} 2\,\delta_0}$$
