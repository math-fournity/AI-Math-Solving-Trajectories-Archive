## Proof

**Claim.** If $M$ is a closed, orientable, connected $n$-manifold, then $H_{n-1}
(M;\mathbb{Z})$ is a **free abelian group** of rank equal to the first Betti num
ber $b_1(M)$:
$$H_{n-1}(M;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{\,b_1(M)}.$$

In particular, $H_{n-1}(M)$ is torsion-free, even when $H_1(M)$ has torsion.

---

**Step 1 — Top homology.** Since $M$ is closed, orientable, and connected, the f
undamental class $[M]$ exists and
$$H_n(M;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}, \qquad H_0(M;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}.
$$

**Step 2 — Poincaré duality.** Orientability gives a cap-product isomorphism
$$D\colon H^k(M;\mathbb{Z}) \xrightarrow{\;\cong\;} H_{n-k}(M;\mathbb{Z}).$$
Setting $k=1$:
$$H_{n-1}(M;\mathbb{Z}) \cong H^1(M;\mathbb{Z}).$$

**Step 3 — Universal coefficient theorem for cohomology.** The UCT gives a short
 exact sequence
$$0 \to \operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}\!\bigl(H_0(M),\mathbb{Z}\bigr) \to H^1
(M;\mathbb{Z}) \to \operatorname{Hom}\!\bigl(H_1(M),\mathbb{Z}\bigr) \to 0.$$
Since $H_0(M)\cong\mathbb{Z}$ is free, $\operatorname{Ext}^1(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{
Z})=0$, so the sequence collapses to an isomorphism
$$H^1(M;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}\!\bigl(H_1(M),\mathbb{Z}\bigr).$$

**Step 4 — Structure of $H_1(M)$.** Compactness of $M$ implies each $H_i(M)$ is
finitely generated. Decompose
$$H_1(M;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{\,r} \oplus T,$$
where $r = b_1(M)$ and $T$ is a finite (torsion) abelian group.

**Step 5 — Apply $\operatorname{Hom}(-,\mathbb{Z})$.** Using $\operatorname{Hom}
(\mathbb{Z}^r,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^r$ and $\operatorname{Hom}(T,\mathbb{Z}
)=0$ (since $\mathbb{Z}$ is torsion-free):
$$\operatorname{Hom}\!\bigl(H_1(M),\mathbb{Z}\bigr) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{\,r}.$$

**Step 6 — Combine.** Chaining the isomorphisms from Steps 2, 3, and 5:
$$H_{n-1}(M;\mathbb{Z}) \;\cong\; H^1(M;\mathbb{Z}) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}
\!\bigl(H_1(M),\mathbb{Z}\bigr) \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}^{\,b_1(M)}.$$

**Conclusion.** $H_{n-1}(M;\mathbb{Z})$ is a free abelian group of rank $b_1(M)$
. The torsion part of $H_1(M)$ is killed by $\operatorname{Hom}(-,\mathbb{Z})$,
which is why $H_{n-1}$ is torsion-free even when $H_1$ is not — that torsion rea
ppears (by duality and the UCT for homology) in $H_{n-2}(M)$ instead.
