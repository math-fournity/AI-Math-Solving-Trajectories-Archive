Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether, for a closed convex set $C
$ in a real Hilbert space $H$ and a boundary point $x \in \partial C$, there alw
ays exists $y \in H \setminus C$ such that the open ray from $x$ through $y$ sta
ys entirely outside $C$.

## Answer: No.

**Counterexample.** Let $H = \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$ and define
$$C = \left\{x = (x_n)_{n \geq 1} \in \ell^2 : \sum_{n=1}^\infty n\, x_n^2 \leq
1\right\}.$$

I claim $C$ is closed and convex, $0$ is a boundary point of $C$, and no ray fro
m $0$ stays entirely in $\ell^2 \setminus C$.

**$C$ is convex.** The functional $f(x) = \sum n\, x_n^2$ is convex (sum of conv
ex maps $x_n \mapsto n x_n^2$), so its sublevel set $C$ is convex.

**$C$ is closed.** $f$ is lower semicontinuous on $\ell^2$ as the supremum of th
e continuous partial sums $f_N(x) = \sum_{n=1}^N n\, x_n^2$. Hence $C = f^{-1}((
-\infty, 1])$ is closed.

**$C \subset \ell^2$.** If $\sum n\, x_n^2 \leq 1$ then $\sum x_n^2 \leq \sum n\
, x_n^2 \leq 1 < \infty$.

**$0$ is a boundary point.** $C$ has empty interior: for any $x \in C$ and $\var
epsilon > 0$, pick $N$ with $N\varepsilon^2 > 1$; then $y = x + \varepsilon\, e_
N$ satisfies $\|y - x\| = \varepsilon$ but $\sum n\, y_n^2 \geq N\varepsilon^2 >
 1$, so $y \notin C$. Since $C$ is closed with empty interior, every point of $C
$ is a boundary point, in particular $0 \in \partial C$.

**No valid ray exists.** Let $y \in \ell^2 \setminus C$, so $\sum n\, y_n^2 > 1$
. For $t > 0$:
$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty n\,(ty_n)^2 = t^2 \sum_{n=1}^\infty n\, y_n^2.$$
For $0 < t < \left(\sum n\, y_n^2\right)^{-1/2}$, this is $< 1$, so $ty \in C$.
Thus the half-line $\{ty : t > 0\}$ enters $C$ for all sufficiently small $t$, a
nd cannot lie entirely in $\ell^2 \setminus C$.

Since this holds for every $y \notin C$, no point $y \in H \setminus C$ satisfie
s the required condition. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** In *finite-dimensional* Hilbert spaces the answer is **yes**: the su
pporting hyperplane theorem provides a nonzero normal $v$ with $\langle v, c \ra
ngle \leq \langle v, x \rangle$ for all $c \in C$, and the ray $x + tv$ ($t > 0$
) lies outside $C$. The counterexample exploits the failure of the supporting hy
perplane theorem at boundary points of sets with empty interior in infinite dime
nsions.
