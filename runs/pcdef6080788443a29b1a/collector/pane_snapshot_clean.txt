Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a finite clopen algebra in
a compact metrizable space forces the Boolean algebra generated by connected com
ponents to also be finite.

**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

Let $X$ be a compact metrizable space and suppose $\operatorname{Clop}(X)$ is fi
nite. Since $X$ is metrizable, it is compact Hausdorff.

**Step 1 (Components = quasi-components in compact Hausdorff spaces).** Recall t
hat the *quasi-component* of a point $x \in X$ is
$$Q(x) = \bigcap\{U \in \operatorname{Clop}(X) : x \in U\}.$$
We use the classical theorem: *in a compact Hausdorff space, every quasi-compone
nt is connected*, so quasi-components coincide with connected components.

*Brief justification of the theorem:* Let $Q$ be a quasi-component and suppose $
Q = A \cup B$ with $A, B$ non-empty, disjoint, and closed in $X$. By normality o
f compact Hausdorff spaces, there exist disjoint open sets $V \supseteq A$, $W \
supseteq B$. Then $Q \subseteq V \cup W$, so $X \setminus (V \cup W)$ is closed
and disjoint from $Q = \bigcap_\alpha U_\alpha$. By compactness, finitely many c
lopen $U_{\alpha_1}, \dots, U_{\alpha_n}$ satisfy $\bigcap_{i=1}^n U_{\alpha_i}
\subseteq V \cup W$. Set $U = \bigcap_i U_{\alpha_i}$; this is clopen and contai
ns $Q$. Then $U \cap V$ and $U \cap W$ are disjoint, cover $U$, and each is clos
ed in $U$ (being the complement of the other within $U$), so both are clopen in
$X$. Since $A \subseteq U \cap V$ and $A \cap Q \neq \emptyset$, the clopen set
$U \cap V$ contains a point of $Q$, so by definition of quasi-component, $Q \sub
seteq U \cap V$. But $B \subseteq Q \cap (U \cap W) \subseteq (U \cap V) \cap (U
 \cap W) = \emptyset$, contradicting $B \neq \emptyset$. Hence $Q$ is connected.
 $\square$

**Step 2 (Finitely many quasi-components).** Two points $x, y$ lie in the same q
uasi-component if and only if they belong to exactly the same clopen sets. Since
 $\operatorname{Clop}(X)$ is finite (say with $m$ elements), the membership patt
ern of a point among the $m$ clopen sets determines its quasi-component. There a
re at most $2^m$ such patterns, so there are **finitely many** quasi-components.

Equivalently, the quasi-components are precisely the *atoms* of the finite Boole
an algebra $\operatorname{Clop}(X)$, and a finite Boolean algebra has finitely m
any atoms.

**Step 3 (Finitely many connected components).** By Step 1, connected components
 equal quasi-components. By Step 2, there are finitely many of them, say $K_1, \
dots, K_n$.

**Step 4 (The generated Boolean algebra is finite).** The connected components $
\{K_1, \dots, K_n\}$ form a partition of $X$. The Boolean algebra generated by t
hem is the collection of all unions $\bigcup_{i \in I} K_i$ for $I \subseteq \{1
, \dots, n\}$, which has exactly $2^n$ elements—finite.

**Conclusion.** If $X$ is compact metrizable and $\operatorname{Clop}(X)$ is fin
ite, then $X$ has finitely many connected components, and consequently the Boole
an algebra generated by the connected components is finite. $\blacksquare$
