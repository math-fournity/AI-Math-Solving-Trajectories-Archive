Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether two distinct irreducible fa
ctors of $x^n - 1$ over $\mathbb{Z}_p[x]$ with coprime reductions mod $p$ genera
te comaximal ideals.

**Answer: Yes, the ideals are comaximal.**

## Proof

**Key Lemma (Unit Criterion).** An element $u \in \mathbb{Z}_p[x]$ is a unit if
and only if its reduction $\overline{u} \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ is a unit (i.e., a
nonzero constant).

*Proof of Lemma.* The forward direction is immediate: if $uv = 1$ then $\overlin
e{u}\,\overline{v} = 1$. For the converse, suppose $\overline{u} = \bar{c} \in \
mathbb{F}_p^*$. Lift to $c \in \mathbb{Z}_p^*$ and write $u = c + p\,w$ for some
 $w \in \mathbb{Z}_p[x]$. Then $u = c(1 + c^{-1}pw)$. The factor $c$ is a unit i
n $\mathbb{Z}_p \subset \mathbb{Z}_p[x]$. The factor $1 + c^{-1}pw$ is a unit be
cause its inverse is the convergent $p$-adic series $\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-c^{-1}
pw)^k$ (each successive term has strictly higher $p$-adic valuation, and $\mathb
b{Z}_p[x]$ is $p$-adically complete). Hence $u$ is a product of units. $\square$

**Main Argument.**

Since $f$ and $g$ are irreducible factors of $x^n - 1$ in $\mathbb{Z}_p[x]$, we
may take them monic. The constant term of $x^n - 1$ is $-1$, a unit in $\mathbb{
Z}_p$, so the constant terms of $f$ and $g$ are also units. In particular, $\ove
rline{f}, \overline{g} \neq 0$ in $\mathbb{F}_p[x]$.

By hypothesis, $\gcd(\overline{f}, \overline{g}) = 1$ in $\mathbb{F}_p[x]$. By t
he Bézout identity in $\mathbb{F}_p[x]$, there exist $\overline{a}, \overline{b}
 \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ such that
$$\overline{a}\,\overline{f} + \overline{b}\,\overline{g} = 1.$$

Lift $\overline{a}, \overline{b}$ arbitrarily to $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}_p[x]$ (coe
fficient-wise, choosing any $p$-adic lift of each $\mathbb{F}_p$-coefficient). S
et
$$u := af + bg \in \mathbb{Z}_p[x].$$

Reducing modulo $p$:
$$\overline{u} = \overline{a}\,\overline{f} + \overline{b}\,\overline{g} = 1 \in
 \mathbb{F}_p[x].$$

Since $\overline{u} = 1$ is a unit in $\mathbb{F}_p[x]$, the **Unit Criterion**
implies that $u$ is a unit in $\mathbb{Z}_p[x]$. Therefore:
$$u^{-1}a \cdot f + u^{-1}b \cdot g = u^{-1}(af + bg) = u^{-1} u = 1.$$

This exhibits $1 \in (f) + (g)$, proving $(f) + (g) = \mathbb{Z}_p[x]$; that is,
 the ideals $(f)$ and $(g)$ are comaximal. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the hypothesis.** The coprimality of $\overline{f}$ and $\overline{g
}$ is automatic when $p \nmid n$ (since $x^n - 1$ is then separable over $\mathb
b{F}_p$, and Hensel's lemma gives a bijection between irreducible factors over $
\mathbb{Z}_p$ and over $\mathbb{F}_p$). When $p \mid n$, the reduction $\overlin
e{x^n - 1} = (x^m - 1)^{p^a}$ (with $n = p^a m$, $p \nmid m$) is not squarefree,
 so distinct irreducible factors over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ can share common factors mo
d $p$. The hypothesis rules this out, and the argument shows it is exactly the r
ight condition: comaximality of $(f)$ and $(g)$ is equivalent to coprimality of
$\overline{f}$ and $\overline{g}$, because every maximal ideal of $\mathbb{Z}_p[
x]$ contains $p$ (a maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ not containing $p$ would make $
\mathbb{Z}_p[x]/\mathfrak{m}$ a field containing $\mathbb{Q}_p$ that is simultan
eously integral over $\mathbb{Z}_p$, forcing it into the ring of integers of a $
p$-adic field — a DVR, not a field — a contradiction).
