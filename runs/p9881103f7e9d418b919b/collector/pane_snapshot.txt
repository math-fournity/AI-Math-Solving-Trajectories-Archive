## Proof

**Necessary condition.** Suppose $x_n \to 2$. Then $x_{n+1} \to 2$ as well. If t
he sequence is real-valued and converges, we need $x^{x_n}$ to be well-defined a
nd continuous near the limit, which requires $x > 0$ (for $x < 0$, non-integer p
owers are not real; for $x = 0$, $x_1 = 1$ and the sequence is constantly 1). Ta
king the limit in $x_{n+1} = e^{x^{x_n}}$ with $x > 0$:

$$2 = e^{x^2} \implies x^2 = \ln 2 \implies x = \sqrt{\ln 2}$$

(taking the positive root since $x > 0$). The negative root $x = -\sqrt{\ln 2}$
is excluded: $x_1 = e^{-\sqrt{\ln 2}} \approx 0.435$ is not an integer, so $x_2
= e^{(-\sqrt{\ln 2})^{0.435}}$ is not real. Thus the **only candidate** is $x =
\sqrt{\ln 2}$.

**Sufficiency: $x = \sqrt{\ln 2}$ works.** Set $x = \sqrt{\ln 2}$ and define $g(
t) = e^{x^t}$. Note $0 < x < 1$ since $0 < \ln 2 < 1$.

*Fixed point:* $g(2) = e^{x^2} = e^{\ln 2} = 2$. ✓

*Derivative:* $g'(t) = e^{x^t} \cdot x^t \cdot \ln x$. At $t = 2$:
$$g'(2) = 2 \cdot \ln 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}\ln(\ln 2) = \ln 2 \cdot \ln(\ln 2).$$
Since $0 < \ln 2 < 1$, we have $\ln(\ln 2) < 0$, so $g'(2) < 0$, and
$$|g'(2)| = \ln 2 \cdot \ln\!\left(\tfrac{1}{\ln 2}\right) \approx 0.693 \times
0.367 \approx 0.254 < 1.$$

*Contraction on an invariant interval.* Consider $I = [1.7,\, 2.3]$. Since $0 <
x < 1$, $x^t$ is decreasing in $t$, so $g$ is decreasing. On $I$, $u := x^t$ ran
ges over $[x^{2.3},\, x^{1.7}] \approx [0.656,\, 0.732]$, and:

$$|g'(t)| = e^u \cdot u \cdot |\ln x| \leq 2.079 \times 0.732 \times 0.183 \appr
ox 0.279 < 1 \quad \text{for all } t \in I.$$

So $g$ is a contraction on $I$ with constant $c \leq 0.279 < 1$.

*Invariant:* $g(I) = [g(2.3),\, g(1.7)] \approx [e^{0.656},\, e^{0.732}] \approx
 [1.927,\, 2.079] \subset I$. ✓

*Starting point:* $x_1 = e^x = e^{\sqrt{\ln 2}} \approx 2.299 \in I$. ✓

By the **Banach Fixed Point Theorem**, since $g: I \to I$ is a contraction and $
x_1 \in I$, the iterates $x_{n+1} = g(x_n)$ converge to the unique fixed point o
f $g$ in $I$, which is $2$.

**Conclusion.** The sequence converges to 2 if and only if $x = \sqrt{\ln 2}$.
