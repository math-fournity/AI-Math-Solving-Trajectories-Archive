## Proof

**Setup.** Let $X_1,\dots,X_n$ be iid $N(0,1)$, $X=(X_1,\dots,X_n)$, and
$$Z=\frac{1}{\|X\|}(X_1,\dots,X_m),\qquad \|X\|^2=\sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2.$$
We want $\mathbb{E}\|Z\|^2$.

**Step 1 — Reduce to a ratio of quadratic forms.**
$$\|Z\|^2=\frac{X_1^2+\cdots+X_m^2}{X_1^2+\cdots+X_n^2}=\frac{S_m}{S_n},\qquad S
_j:=\sum_{i=1}^j X_i^2.$$

**Step 2 — Symmetry gives the exact value.** The joint law of $(X_1^2,\dots,X_n^
2)$ is exchangeable (each $X_i^2\sim\chi^2_1$ iid). Hence for every $i$,
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{X_i^2}{S_n}\right]=\frac{1}{n}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\fra
c{\sum_{j=1}^n X_j^2}{S_n}\right]=\frac{1}{n}\cdot 1=\frac{1}{n}.$$
Summing over $i=1,\dots,m$:
$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}\|Z\|^2=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{S_m}{S_n}\right]=\sum_{i=1}^m
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{X_i^2}{S_n}\right]=\frac{m}{n}.}$$

**Step 3 — Concentration of measure perspective.** Write $S_n=S_m+T$ with $T=\su
m_{i=m+1}^n X_i^2\sim\chi^2_{n-m}$ independent of $S_m\sim\chi^2_m$. Then
$$\frac{S_m}{S_n}=\frac{S_m}{S_m+T}=\frac{1}{1+T/S_m}.$$
By the law of large numbers for chi-squared variables, $S_m/m\to 1$ and $T/(n-m)
\to 1$ a.s., so
$$\frac{S_m}{S_n}\xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}}\frac{m}{m+(n-m)}=\frac{m}{n}.$$
Moreover, concentration of measure for $\chi^2$ (a sub-exponential / log-Sobolev
 consequence on the sphere and Gaussian space) gives, for $S_n\sim\chi^2_n$,
$$\Pr\!\left(\left|\frac{S_n}{n}-1\right|\ge t\right)\le 2\exp\!\left(-c\,n\min(
t^2,t)\right),$$
and similarly for $S_m$. Applying this to both numerator and denominator shows $
S_m/S_n$ is tightly concentrated around $m/n$ with fluctuations of order $O(1/\s
qrt n)$. In particular its expectation equals (and is pinned at) the limiting va
lue $m/n$, consistent with the exact computation above.

**Conclusion.**
$$\mathbb{E}\|Z\|^2=\frac{m}{n}.$$
