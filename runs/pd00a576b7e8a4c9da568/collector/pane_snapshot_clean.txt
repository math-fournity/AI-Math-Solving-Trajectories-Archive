Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every total order on a numb
er field admits an order-preserving field homomorphism into ℝ.

**Answer: No.** I'll construct an explicit counterexample.

---

**Counterexample.** Let $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}) = \{a + b\sqrt{2} : a, b \in \
mathbb{Q}\}$. Since $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational, the representation $a + b\sqrt{2}$
 is unique. Define a total order $\le$ on $K$ by **lexicographic order on the co
efficients**:
$$a + b\sqrt{2} \;\le\; a' + b'\sqrt{2} \iff (a, b) \le_{\mathrm{lex}} (a', b').
$$
This is a total order on $K$ (lex order on $\mathbb{Q}^2$ is total, and the coef
ficient representation is unique).

**Claim:** No field homomorphism $\phi: K \to \mathbb{R}$ respects this order.

*Proof of claim.* Suppose $\phi: K \to \mathbb{R}$ is a field homomorphism with
$\phi(x) > \phi(y)$ whenever $x > y$.

**Step 1: $\phi$ fixes $\mathbb{Q}$.** Since $\phi$ is a field homomorphism, $\p
hi(1) = 1$, hence $\phi(n) = n$ for all $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $\phi(q) = q$ for
 all $q \in \mathbb{Q}$.

**Step 2: $\phi(\sqrt{2}) = \pm\sqrt{2}$.** We have $\phi(\sqrt{2})^2 = \phi(\sq
rt{2}^2) = \phi(2) = 2$, so $\phi(\sqrt{2}) \in \{\sqrt{2},\, -\sqrt{2}\}$.

**Step 3: Derive contradictions from the order.**

In our lexicographic order:
- $\sqrt{2} = 0 + 1\cdot\sqrt{2} > 0 + 0\cdot\sqrt{2} = 0$, since $(0,1) >_{\mat
hrm{lex}} (0,0)$.
- $1 = 1 + 0\cdot\sqrt{2} > 0 + 1\cdot\sqrt{2} = \sqrt{2}$, since $(1,0) >_{\mat
hrm{lex}} (0,1)$.

Since $\phi$ respects the order:
- From $\sqrt{2} > 0$: $\phi(\sqrt{2}) > \phi(0) = 0$.
- From $1 > \sqrt{2}$: $\phi(1) > \phi(\sqrt{2})$, i.e., $1 > \phi(\sqrt{2})$.

Combining: $0 < \phi(\sqrt{2}) < 1$.

But from Step 2, $\phi(\sqrt{2}) \in \{\sqrt{2}, -\sqrt{2}\}$:
- If $\phi(\sqrt{2}) = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$: this contradicts $\phi(\sqrt{2})
 < 1$.
- If $\phi(\sqrt{2}) = -\sqrt{2} \approx -1.414$: this contradicts $\phi(\sqrt{2
}) > 0$.

Both possibilities lead to contradictions. Therefore no such $\phi$ exists. $\sq
uare$

---

**Remark.** The essential point is that a *total order* on $K$ need not be compa
tible with the field structure (i.e., $K$ need not be an ordered field under $\l
e$). The lexicographic order above satisfies $\sqrt{2} > 0$ yet $1 > \sqrt{2}$,
which is incompatible with the algebraic relation $\sqrt{2}^2 = 2$ forcing $\phi
(\sqrt{2}) = \pm\sqrt{2}$. Any field homomorphism $K \to \mathbb{R}$ must respec
t this algebraic relation, but the arbitrary total order need not—so no order-pr
eserving homomorphism exists in general.
