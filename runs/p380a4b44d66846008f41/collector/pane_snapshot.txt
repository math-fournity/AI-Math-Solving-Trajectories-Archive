No. A totally disconnected space need not be discrete. The standard counterexamp
le is $\mathbb{Q}$ with the subspace topology inherited from $\mathbb{R}$.

## Proof

Recall the definitions:

- A space $X$ is **totally disconnected** if for every $x \neq y \in X$, there e
xists a separation $X = A \cup B$ with $A, B$ disjoint nonempty open (equivalent
ly clopen) sets, $x \in A$, $y \in B$. Equivalently, the only connected subsets
are the singletons.
- A space $X$ is **discrete** if every singleton $\{x\}$ is open.

**Claim.** $\mathbb{Q}$ (with the subspace topology from $\mathbb{R}$) is totall
y disconnected but not discrete.

### $\mathbb{Q}$ is totally disconnected

Let $x, y \in \mathbb{Q}$ with $x < y$. Since the irrationals are dense in $\mat
hbb{R}$, there exists an irrational number $\alpha$ with $x < \alpha < y$. Defin
e

$$A = \mathbb{Q} \cap (-\infty, \alpha), \qquad B = \mathbb{Q} \cap (\alpha, +\i
nfty).$$

Both $A$ and $B$ are open in $\mathbb{Q}$ (being intersections of $\mathbb{Q}$ w
ith open rays in $\mathbb{R}$). They are disjoint, their union is $\mathbb{Q}$ (
since $\alpha \notin \mathbb{Q}$, every rational is either below or above $\alph
a$), and $x \in A$, $y \in B$. Moreover, $A = \mathbb{Q} \cap (-\infty, \alpha]$
 is also closed in $\mathbb{Q}$ (since $(-\infty, \alpha]$ is closed in $\mathbb
{R}$ and $\alpha \notin \mathbb{Q}$), so $A$ and $B$ are clopen.

Thus any two distinct points of $\mathbb{Q}$ can be separated by a clopen partit
ion, so $\mathbb{Q}$ is totally disconnected. In particular, any subset $S \subs
eteq \mathbb{Q}$ with two or more points $x < y$ admits the separation $S = (S \
cap A) \cup (S \cap B)$ into two nonempty disjoint open-in-$S$ sets, so $S$ is d
isconnected. Hence the only connected subsets are singletons.

### $\mathbb{Q}$ is not discrete

Suppose for contradiction that $\mathbb{Q}$ is discrete. Then every singleton $\
{q\}$ is open in $\mathbb{Q}$, so there exists an open set $U \subseteq \mathbb{
R}$ with $U \cap \mathbb{Q} = \{q\}$. Since $U$ is open in $\mathbb{R}$ and $q \
in U$, there exists $\varepsilon > 0$ with $(q - \varepsilon, q + \varepsilon) \
subseteq U$. But $(q - \varepsilon, q + \varepsilon)$ contains infinitely many r
ationals (by density of $\mathbb{Q}$ in $\mathbb{R}$), so

$$U \cap \mathbb{Q} \supseteq (q - \varepsilon, q + \varepsilon) \cap \mathbb{Q}
 \neq \{q\},$$

a contradiction. Therefore no singleton in $\mathbb{Q}$ is open, and $\mathbb{Q}
$ is not discrete.

### Conclusion

$\mathbb{Q}$ is a totally disconnected space that is not discrete. Therefore, **
not every totally disconnected space is a discrete topology**.
