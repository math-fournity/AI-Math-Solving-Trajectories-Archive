**Answer: No, it does not follow in general.**

**Counterexample.** Let $H = \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$, the space of square-summable s
equences, which is an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space with inner product $\la
ngle x, y\rangle = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} x_k \overline{y_k}$. Let $a_n = e_n$, the
 $n$-th standard unit vector (with a $1$ in position $n$ and $0$ elsewhere). The
 sequence $\{e_n\}$ is orthonormal, so $\|e_n\| = 1$ for every $n$.

**Claim 1:** For every $x \in \ell^2$, $\langle x, e_n\rangle \to 0$.

*Proof.* For $x = (x_1, x_2, \dots) \in \ell^2$, we have $\langle x, e_n\rangle
= x_n$. By Bessel's inequality applied to the orthonormal family $\{e_n\}$,
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} |\langle x, e_n\rangle|^2 = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} |x_n|^2 =
\|x\|^2 < \infty.$$
A convergent series has terms tending to zero, so $|x_n|^2 \to 0$, hence $x_n =
\langle x, e_n\rangle \to 0$. $\square$

**Claim 2:** $e_n \not\to 0$ in norm.

*Proof.* $\|e_n - 0\| = \|e_n\| = 1$ for all $n$, so $\|e_n\| \not\to 0$. $\squa
re$

**Conclusion.** The condition "$\langle x, a_n\rangle \to 0$ for every $x \in H$
" is precisely *weak convergence* of $a_n$ to $0$. The counterexample above show
s that weak convergence to $0$ does **not** imply norm convergence to $0$ in an
infinite-dimensional Hilbert space.

**Remark (finite-dimensional case).** If $\dim H < \infty$, then the answer *is*
 yes: choosing $x = a_n$ itself (or using that weak convergence implies coordina
te-wise convergence in every orthonormal basis, which in finite dimensions gives
 norm convergence) yields $\|a_n\|^2 = \langle a_n, a_n\rangle \to 0$. So the im
plication holds exactly in finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and fails in every
infinite-dimensional one (every infinite-dimensional Hilbert space admits an inf
inite orthonormal sequence, which provides the same counterexample).
