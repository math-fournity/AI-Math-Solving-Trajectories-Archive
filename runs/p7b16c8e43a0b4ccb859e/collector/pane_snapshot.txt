## Solution

The answer is **yes**: every submultiplicative matrix norm on real matrices can
be extended to a submultiplicative matrix norm on complex matrices.

**Construction.** Let $\|\cdot\|$ be a submultiplicative norm on $M_n(\mathbb{R}
)$. For $A \in M_n(\mathbb{C})$, define

$$\|A\|_{\mathbb{C}} = \inf\left\{ \sum_{j=1}^{m} |c_j|\,\|A_j\| \;:\; A = \sum_
{j=1}^{m} c_j A_j,\; A_j \in M_n(\mathbb{R}),\; c_j \in \mathbb{C},\; m \in \mat
hbb{N} \right\}.$$

This is the *projective complexification* of $\|\cdot\|$.

---

**Finiteness.** Every $A \in M_n(\mathbb{C})$ decomposes as $A = B + iC$ with $B
, C \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$, giving the representation $A = 1\cdot B + i\cdot C$, s
o $\|A\|_{\mathbb{C}} \leq \|B\| + \|C\| < \infty$.

**Positive definiteness.** Clearly $\|A\|_{\mathbb{C}} \geq 0$. Suppose $\|A\|_{
\mathbb{C}} = 0$. Write $A = B + iC$ with $B, C \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$. For any re
presentation $A = \sum_j c_j A_j$ with $A_j \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$, taking real an
d imaginary parts gives
$$B = \sum_j \operatorname{Re}(c_j)\,A_j, \qquad C = \sum_j \operatorname{Im}(c_
j)\,A_j.$$
By the triangle inequality for $\|\cdot\|$:
$$\|B\| \leq \sum_j |\operatorname{Re}(c_j)|\,\|A_j\| \leq \sum_j |c_j|\,\|A_j\|
, \qquad \|C\| \leq \sum_j |\operatorname{Im}(c_j)|\,\|A_j\| \leq \sum_j |c_j|\,
\|A_j\|.$$
Taking the infimum over all representations yields $\|B\| \leq \|A\|_{\mathbb{C}
} = 0$ and $\|C\| \leq \|A\|_{\mathbb{C}} = 0$, so $B = C = 0$, hence $A = 0$.

**Absolute homogeneity.** For $\alpha \in \mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$, the substit
ution $c_j \mapsto \alpha c_j$ is a bijection between representations of $A$ and
 of $\alpha A$, so
$$\|\alpha A\|_{\mathbb{C}} = \inf \sum_j |\alpha c_j|\,\|A_j\| = |\alpha|\inf \
sum_j |c_j|\,\|A_j\| = |\alpha|\,\|A\|_{\mathbb{C}}.$$

**Triangle inequality.** Concatenating representations of $A$ and $B$ gives a re
presentation of $A+B$, so $\|A+B\|_{\mathbb{C}} \leq \|A\|_{\mathbb{C}} + \|B\|_
{\mathbb{C}}$.

**Extension property.** For $A \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$, the trivial representation
gives $\|A\|_{\mathbb{C}} \leq \|A\|$. Conversely, for any representation $A = \
sum_j c_j A_j$ with $A_j \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$, the imaginary part vanishes ($\su
m_j \operatorname{Im}(c_j) A_j = 0$) and $A = \sum_j \operatorname{Re}(c_j) A_j$
, so
$$\|A\| = \left\|\sum_j \operatorname{Re}(c_j)\,A_j\right\| \leq \sum_j |\operat
orname{Re}(c_j)|\,\|A_j\| \leq \sum_j |c_j|\,\|A_j\|.$$
Taking the infimum gives $\|A\| \leq \|A\|_{\mathbb{C}}$. Hence $\|A\|_{\mathbb{
C}} = \|A\|$ for all $A \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$.

**Submultiplicativity.** Let $A = \sum_j c_j A_j$ and $B = \sum_k d_k B_k$ with
$A_j, B_k \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$. Then $AB = \sum_{j,k} c_j d_k\, A_j B_k$ with $A
_j B_k \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$. Using submultiplicativity of $\|\cdot\|$:
$$\|AB\|_{\mathbb{C}} \leq \sum_{j,k} |c_j d_k|\,\|A_j B_k\| \leq \sum_{j,k} |c_
j|\,|d_k|\,\|A_j\|\,\|B_k\| = \left(\sum_j |c_j|\,\|A_j\|\right)\!\left(\sum_k |
d_k|\,\|B_k\|\right).$$
Taking the infimum over representations of $A$ and $B$ independently:
$$\|AB\|_{\mathbb{C}} \leq \|A\|_{\mathbb{C}} \cdot \|B\|_{\mathbb{C}}.$$

---

**Conclusion.** $\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{C}}$ is a submultiplicative norm on $M_n(\ma
thbb{C})$ that agrees with $\|\cdot\|$ on $M_n(\mathbb{R})$. The key idea is tha
t the projective complexification preserves both the norm values on the real sub
space (because real and imaginary parts of any representation are separately bou
nded by the triangle inequality) and submultiplicativity (because products of re
al matrices are real, and the submultiplicative inequality distributes over the
infimum). Therefore, **every** submultiplicative matrix norm on real matrices ex
tends to one on complex matrices. $\blacksquare$
