## Proof

**Answer: Yes.**

The Riemann zeta function satisfies the functional equation

$$\zeta(s) = \chi(s)\,\zeta(1-s), \qquad \chi(s) = 2^{s}\,\pi^{s-1}\,\sin\!\left
(\tfrac{\pi s}{2}\right)\,\Gamma(1-s).$$

**Claim:** The factor $\chi(s)$ is nonzero throughout the open critical strip $0
 < \operatorname{Re}(s) < 1$.

- $2^{s}$ and $\pi^{s-1}$ are everywhere nonzero.
- $\Gamma(1-s)$ is never zero (the gamma function has no zeros; its only singula
rities are poles at non-positive integers, located at $1-s \in \{0,-1,-2,\dots\}
$, i.e. $s \in \{1,2,3,\dots\}$, none of which lie in the open strip).
- $\sin(\pi s/2)$ vanishes only when $s \in 2\mathbb{Z}$, i.e. at even integers.
 No even integer lies in $0 < \operatorname{Re}(s) < 1$.

Hence $\chi(s) \neq 0$ for all $s$ in the critical strip, and within the strip

$$\zeta(s) = 0 \iff \zeta(1-s) = 0.$$

**Symmetry of zeros.** If $\rho$ is a zero of $\zeta$ in the critical strip, the
n $1-\rho$ is also a zero of $\zeta$ in the critical strip, since $\operatorname
{Re}(1-\rho) = 1 - \operatorname{Re}(\rho) \in (0,1)$. Moreover this reflection
swaps the two halves of the strip:

$$\operatorname{Re}(\rho) > \tfrac{1}{2} \iff \operatorname{Re}(1-\rho) < \tfrac
{1}{2}.$$

**Conclusion.** Suppose there are no zeros with real part greater than $1/2$. If
, for contradiction, there were a zero $\rho$ with $\operatorname{Re}(\rho) < 1/
2$, then by the symmetry above $1-\rho$ would be a zero with $\operatorname{Re}(
1-\rho) > 1/2$ — contradicting the hypothesis. Therefore no such zero can exist,
 and the absence of zeros with real part $> 1/2$ implies the absence of zeros wi
th real part $< 1/2$.
