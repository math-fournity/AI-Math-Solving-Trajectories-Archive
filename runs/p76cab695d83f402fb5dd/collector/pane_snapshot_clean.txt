Looking at this problem, I note that the condition "$x_0 = 0$" with "positive re
als" and "$x_0 \geq x_1 \geq \cdots$" is contradictory (positive reals cannot sa
tisfy $x_1 \leq 0$). This is the well-known IMO 1982 Problem 3, where the intend
ed condition is $x_0 = 1$. I solve it under this standard formulation.

---

**Proof.**

We consider sequences of positive reals with $x_0 = 1$ and $1 = x_0 \geq x_1 \ge
q x_2 \geq \cdots > 0$. Define

$$S_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \frac{x_k^2}{x_{k+1}}, \qquad n \geq 1.$$

We prove that for every such sequence there exists $n \geq 1$ with $S_n \geq 3.9
99$.

**Suppose for contradiction** that $S_n < 3.999$ for every $n \geq 1$.

**Step 1: The sequence tends to zero and $\sum x_k$ converges.**

Since $x_k \geq x_{k+1} > 0$, each term satisfies $\frac{x_k^2}{x_{k+1}} \geq \f
rac{x_k \cdot x_{k+1}}{x_{k+1}} = x_k$. Hence

$$S_n \geq \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} x_k =: A_n.$$

Since $S_n < 3.999$ for all $n$, the partial sums $A_n$ are bounded above by $3.
999$, so $A_n \to A_\infty \leq 3.999 < \infty$. In particular $x_k \to 0$.

**Step 2: Apply Cauchy–Schwarz (Engel/Titu form).**

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality applied to $\sum \frac{x_k^2}{x_{k+1}}$:

$$S_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \frac{x_k^2}{x_{k+1}} \geq \frac{\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
 x_k\right)^2}{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} x_{k+1}} = \frac{A_n^2}{\sum_{k=1}^{n} x_k}.$$

Since $\sum_{k=1}^{n} x_k = A_n - x_0 + x_n = A_n - 1 + x_n$, we obtain

$$S_n \geq \frac{A_n^2}{A_n - 1 + x_n}. \tag{$\star$}$$

(The denominator is positive for $n \geq 1$ since it equals $\sum_{k=1}^n x_k >
0$.)

**Step 3: Take $n \to \infty$.**

The sequence $(S_n)$ is increasing (each term $\frac{x_k^2}{x_{k+1}} > 0$), so $
S_n \to S_\infty \leq 3.999$. We have established $A_n \to A_\infty \leq 3.999$
and $x_n \to 0$. Taking the limit in $(\star)$:

$$S_\infty \geq \frac{A_\infty^2}{A_\infty - 1}.$$

This is valid since $A_\infty \geq x_0 = 1$, and in fact $A_\infty > 1$ (if $A_\
infty = 1$ then $\sum_{k=1}^\infty x_k = 0$, contradicting $x_k > 0$). Thus the
denominator $A_\infty - 1 > 0$.

**Step 4: Lower bound via AM–GM.**

Set $u = A_\infty - 1 > 0$. Then

$$\frac{A_\infty^2}{A_\infty - 1} = \frac{(u+1)^2}{u} = u + 2 + \frac{1}{u} \geq
 2 + 2 = 4,$$

where the inequality $u + \frac{1}{u} \geq 2$ is AM–GM, with equality iff $u = 1
$ (i.e., $A_\infty = 2$).

Therefore $S_\infty \geq 4$.

**Step 5: Contradiction.**

We assumed $S_n < 3.999$ for all $n$, giving $S_\infty \leq 3.999 < 4$. But Step
 4 gives $S_\infty \geq 4$. This is a contradiction.

Hence our assumption is false: there exists some $n \geq 1$ such that

$$\frac{x_0^2}{x_1} + \frac{x_1^2}{x_2} + \cdots + \frac{x_{n-1}^2}{x_n} \geq 3.
999. \qquad \blacksquare$$

**Remark.** The bound $4$ is sharp: the geometric sequence $x_k = 2^{-k}$ (with
$x_0 = 1$) gives $S_\infty = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{4^{-k}}{2^{-(k+1)}} = \su
m_{k=0}^{\infty} 2^{-k+1} = 4$, yet $S_n < 4$ for every finite $n$. This explain
s why the problem asks for $3.999$ rather than $4$.
