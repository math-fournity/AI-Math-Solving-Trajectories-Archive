## Proof

**Step 1: Integral representation.** Since $\frac{1}{k2^n+1} = \int_0^1 x^{k2^n}
\,dx$, we write:

$$S = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k} \sum_{n=0}^\infty \int_0^1 x^{k2^n}
\,dx$$

**Step 2: Justify swapping the order of summation.** The double sum is absolutel
y convergent:

$$\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{1}{k}\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{k2^n+1} \leq \sum_{k=1
}^\infty \frac{1}{k}\cdot\frac{2}{k} = 2\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{1}{k^2} < \infty
$$

so by Fubini's theorem we may swap the $k$ and $n$ sums:

$$S = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k} \int_0^1 x^{k2^n}
\,dx$$

**Step 3: Swap the $k$-sum with the integral.** For fixed $n$ and $x \in [0,1]$,
 the series $\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k} x^{k2^n}$ converges (by Diri
chlet's test, since $\frac{1}{k}\to 0$ monotonically and partial sums of $(-1)^{
k-1}x^{k2^n}$ are bounded). The partial sums are uniformly bounded, so by domina
ted convergence:

$$\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k} \int_0^1 x^{k2^n}\,dx = \int_0^1 \sum_{
k=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k} x^{k2^n}\,dx = \int_0^1 \ln\!\left(1 + x^{2^n}\
right) dx$$

using the Taylor series $\ln(1+y) = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k} y^k$
with $y = x^{2^n}$.

Thus: $S = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \int_0^1 \ln(1+x^{2^n})\,dx$.

**Step 4: Swap the $n$-sum with the integral.** Since $\ln(1+x^{2^n}) \geq 0$ fo
r $x \in [0,1]$, Tonelli's theorem gives:

$$S = \int_0^1 \sum_{n=0}^\infty \ln\!\left(1+x^{2^n}\right) dx$$

**Step 5: Apply the telescoping product identity.** For $|x|<1$:

$$(1-x)\prod_{n=0}^{N}(1+x^{2^n}) = 1 - x^{2^{N+1}} \xrightarrow{N\to\infty} 1$$

Therefore $\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1+x^{2^n}) = \frac{1}{1-x}$, and taking logarith
ms:

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \ln\!\left(1+x^{2^n}\right) = -\ln(1-x)$$

**Step 6: Evaluate the integral.**

$$S = \int_0^1 -\ln(1-x)\,dx$$

Substituting $u = 1-x$:

$$S = \int_0^1 -\ln u\,du = -\Big[u\ln u - u\Big]_0^1 = -\big[(0-1) - \lim_{u\to
 0^+}(u\ln u - u)\big] = -[-1 - 0] = 1$$
