## Solution

We consider the equation

$$\ddot{x} + \varepsilon(|x|-1)\dot{x} + x = 0, \qquad 0<\varepsilon\ll 1.$$

This is a weakly nonlinear oscillator of Liénard type with a **piecewise** dampi
ng $\varepsilon(|x|-1)\dot{x}$. We apply the Krylov–Bogoliubov (averaging) metho
d.

---

### 1. Energy balance and the averaged amplitude equation

Write the equation as

$$\ddot{x}+x = \varepsilon\,(1-|x|)\,\dot{x} \;=:\; \varepsilon\,f(x,\dot{x}).$$

Introduce the slowly varying amplitude and phase

$$x = a\cos\theta, \qquad \dot{x} = -a\sin\theta, \qquad \theta = t+\phi,$$

with $a,\phi$ constant to $O(1)$. The energy $E=\tfrac12\dot x^2+\tfrac12 x^2=\t
frac12 a^2$ satisfies

$$\frac{dE}{dt} = \dot{x}(\ddot{x}+x) = \varepsilon(1-|x|)\dot{x}^{2}.$$

Averaging over one period (with $a$ frozen):

$$\Big\langle\frac{dE}{dt}\Big\rangle = \varepsilon a^{2}\Big[\langle\sin^{2}\th
eta\rangle - a\,\big\langle|\cos\theta|\sin^{2}\theta\big\rangle\Big].$$

The two required averages are

$$\langle\sin^{2}\theta\rangle = \frac12, \qquad
\big\langle|\cos\theta|\sin^{2}\theta\big\rangle = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}
|\cos\theta|\sin^{2}\theta\,d\theta = \frac{4}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos\theta\s
in^{2}\theta\,d\theta = \frac{2}{3\pi}.$$

Hence, since $\dot E = a\dot a$,

$$\boxed{\;\dot a = \varepsilon\,a\Big(\frac12 - \frac{2a}{3\pi}\Big).\;}$$

### 2. Amplitude of the limit cycle

The limit cycle is the nontrivial fixed point of the averaged amplitude flow:

$$\frac12 - \frac{2a}{3\pi}=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\;a_{0} = \frac{3
\pi}{4}\approx 2.356\;}.$$

Stability: $\dot a>0$ for $a<a_{0}$ and $\dot a<0$ for $a>a_{0}$, so the limit c
ycle is **stable**.

### 3. Period

The averaged phase equation is

$$\dot\theta = 1 + \frac{\varepsilon}{a}\big\langle \cos\theta\,(1-|a\cos\theta|
)(-a\sin\theta)\big\rangle = 1,$$

because both $\langle\cos\theta\sin\theta\rangle=0$ and $\langle|\cos\theta|\cos
\theta\sin\theta\rangle=0$ (odd integrands). Thus there is **no $O(\varepsilon)$
 frequency correction**, and

$$\boxed{\;T = 2\pi + O(\varepsilon^{2}) \;\approx\; 2\pi.\;}$$

### 4. Polar equations for the phase paths near the limit cycle

In the phase plane set $x=r\cos\theta$, $\dot{x}=-r\sin\theta$ (so $r=a$). The a
veraged (slow-flow / polar) equations governing phase paths near the limit cycle
 are

$$\boxed{\;\dot r = \varepsilon\,r\Big(\frac12 - \frac{2r}{3\pi}\Big), \qquad \d
ot\theta = 1.\;}$$

Equivalently, eliminating time ($d\theta/dt=1$),

$$\boxed{\;\frac{dr}{d\theta} = \varepsilon\,r\Big(\frac12 - \frac{2r}{3\pi}\Big
).\;}$$

This is a logistic-type equation with stable equilibrium $r_{0}=3\pi/4$; lineari
zing about it gives $r(\theta)-r_{0}\propto e^{-\varepsilon\theta/2}$, confirmin
g the slow ($O(\varepsilon)$) spiral attraction to the limit cycle.

---

**Summary.** For $\ddot{x}+\varepsilon(|x|-1)\dot{x}+x=0$ with $0<\varepsilon\ll
1$:

- **Limit-cycle amplitude:** $\displaystyle a_{0}=\frac{3\pi}{4}\approx 2.356$ (
stable).
- **Period:** $\displaystyle T\approx 2\pi$ (no $O(\varepsilon)$ correction).
- **Polar phase-path equations near the cycle:** $\displaystyle \dot r=\varepsil
on r\!\left(\tfrac12-\tfrac{2r}{3\pi}\right),\;\dot\theta=1$, or $\displaystyle
\frac{dr}{d\theta}=\varepsilon r\!\left(\tfrac12-\tfrac{2r}{3\pi}\right)$.
