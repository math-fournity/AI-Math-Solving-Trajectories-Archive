## Solution

**Setup.** Let the line $y=mx$ make angle $\theta$ with the $x$-axis, so $\tan\t
heta = m$. Set $t = \tan(\theta/2)$, so $m = \frac{2t}{1-t^2}$ (with $0<t<1$ sin
ce $m>0$). A circle tangent to both lines has its center on an angle bisector.

**Centers and radii.** A circle tangent to the $x$-axis from above has center $(
h,r)$ with $r>0$. The distance to $y=mx$ equals $r$ gives $|mh-r|=r\sqrt{1+m^2}$
. Using $\sqrt{1+m^2}=\frac{1+t^2}{1-t^2}$:

- **Bisector 1** (internal, wedge between the two lines): $h = r/t$, center $(r/
t,\, r)$.
- **Bisector 2** (external): $h = -rt$, center $(-rt,\, r)$.

**Passing through $(9,6)$.** Since $(9-h)^2+(6-r)^2=r^2$, we get $(9-h)^2+36=12r
$.

*On bisector 1* ($h=r/t$):
$$12r = \left(9-\tfrac{r}{t}\right)^2+36 \;\Longrightarrow\; r^2 - r(18t+12t^2)+
117t^2=0. \tag{$\star$}$$

*On bisector 2* ($h=-rt$):
$$r^2t^2 + r(18t-12)+117=0. \tag{$\star\star$}$$

**Which bisector?** The discriminants are
$$\Delta_1 = 144\,t^2(t^2+3t-1), \qquad \Delta_2 = -144(t^2+3t-1).$$
For two distinct real circles on a given bisector we need the corresponding $\De
lta>0$. Since $\Delta_1$ and $\Delta_2$ have **opposite signs** (for $t>0$), bot
h circles cannot lie on different bisectors (that would require both $\Delta_i\g
e 0$, forcing $\Delta_1=\Delta_2=0$, the degenerate tangent case). So both circl
es lie on the **same** bisector.

For bisector 2, the product of roots of $(\star\star)$ is $117/t^2$; setting $=6
8$ gives $t^2=117/68>1$, contradicting $0<t<1$. Hence both circles lie on **bise
ctor 1**.

**Using the product of radii.** Equation $(\star)$ is monic in $r$, so its two r
oots $r_1,r_2$ (the two circles) satisfy
$$r_1 r_2 = 117\,t^2.$$
The problem gives $r_1 r_2 = 68$, so
$$117\,t^2 = 68 \;\Longrightarrow\; t^2 = \frac{68}{117}.$$

**Computing $m$.** With $t^2=68/117$:
$$1-t^2 = \frac{49}{117}, \qquad m = \frac{2t}{1-t^2} = \frac{2t\cdot 117}{49} =
 \frac{234}{49}\,t.$$
Now $t = \sqrt{\tfrac{68}{117}} = \frac{\sqrt{68}}{\sqrt{117}} = \frac{2\sqrt{17
}}{3\sqrt{13}} = \frac{2\sqrt{221}}{39}$. Therefore
$$m = \frac{234}{49}\cdot\frac{2\sqrt{221}}{39} = \frac{468\sqrt{221}}{1911} = \
frac{12\sqrt{221}}{49},$$
since $\gcd(468,1911)=39$.

**Verification of geometry.** With $t^2=68/117$, $t\approx 0.762$, so $t^2+3t-1\
approx 1.87>0$, giving $\Delta_1>0$: two distinct real circles. Both roots are p
ositive (sum $=18t+12t^2>0$, product $=68>0$). The point $(9,6)$ lies in the int
ernal wedge: $9m\approx 32.7>6$. The two distinct circles both pass through $(9,
6)$ and are not tangent there (their centers, both on the bisector through the o
rigin, are not collinear with $(9,6)$), so they intersect in exactly two points.
 ✓

**Final form.** $m = \dfrac{12\sqrt{221}}{49}$, where $221=13\cdot 17$ is square
free, and $\gcd(12,49)=1$. Thus $a=12,\; b=221,\; c=49$, and

$$a+b+c = 12+221+49 = \boxed{282}.$$
