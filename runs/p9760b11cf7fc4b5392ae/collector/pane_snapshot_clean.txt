## Solution

Let
$$F: \mathbb{R}^{n+1} \to \mathbb{R}, \qquad F(x^1,\ldots,x^n,x^{n+1}) = (x^1)^2
 + \cdots + (x^n)^2 - (x^{n+1})^2.$$

Then $M_a = F^{-1}(a)$.

**Step 1. Regular values.** Compute the gradient:
$$\nabla F = (2x^1, \ldots, 2x^n, -2x^{n+1}).$$

This vanishes if and only if $x^1 = \cdots = x^n = x^{n+1} = 0$, i.e. only at th
e origin. Since $F(0) = 0$, the **only critical value** of $F$ is $0$. Consequen
tly every $a \neq 0$ is a regular value.

By the **Preimage Theorem** (regular value theorem), for every $a \neq 0$ the le
vel set $M_a = F^{-1}(a)$ is a smooth embedded submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$
 of codimension $1$, hence of dimension $n$.

**Step 2. The case $a = 0$ is not a submanifold.** Here
$$M_0 = \Big\{(x^1,\ldots,x^{n+1}) : (x^1)^2 + \cdots + (x^n)^2 = (x^{n+1})^2\Bi
g\}$$
is the light cone, with vertex at the origin. We show the origin is a singular p
oint.

Suppose, for contradiction, that $M_0$ is a $d$-dimensional $C^1$ submanifold ne
ar $0$ (necessarily $d = n$ since $M_0$ is a hypersurface away from $0$). Inters
ect $M_0$ with a small sphere $S^n_\varepsilon = \{\|x\| = \varepsilon\}$. On $M
_0$ we have $\sum_{i=1}^n (x^i)^2 = (x^{n+1})^2$; combined with $\sum_{i=1}^n (x
^i)^2 + (x^{n+1})^2 = \varepsilon^2$ this gives
$$(x^{n+1})^2 = \varepsilon^2/2, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^n (x^i)^2 = \varepsilon^2/2.$
$

Hence the **link** $L_\varepsilon := M_0 \cap S^n_\varepsilon$ consists of two d
isjoint copies of $S^{n-1}_{\varepsilon/\sqrt{2}}$ (one with $x^{n+1} = +\vareps
ilon/\sqrt{2}$, one with $x^{n+1} = -\varepsilon/\sqrt{2}$).

If $M_0$ were a smooth $n$-manifold near $0$, then for small $\varepsilon$ the l
ink $L_\varepsilon$ would be diffeomorphic to $S^{n-1}$ (the link of a smooth $n
$-manifold point). But:

- For $n \geq 2$: $S^{n-1}$ is **connected**, whereas $L_\varepsilon$ is **two d
isjoint copies** of $S^{n-1}$, hence disconnected. Contradiction.
- For $n = 1$: $S^0$ has **two** points, whereas $L_\varepsilon$ has **four** po
ints ($x^{n+1} = \pm\varepsilon/\sqrt{2}$, $x^1 = \pm\varepsilon/\sqrt{2}$). Con
tradiction.

(Indeed $M_0$ is the cone over $L_\varepsilon$; a cone over two copies of $S^{n-
1}$ is a topological manifold at the vertex only if two copies of $S^{n-1}$ equa
l $S^{n-1}$, which never holds.)

Therefore $M_0$ is **not** a submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ at the origin.

**Conclusion.** The set $M_a$ is a (smooth, $n$-dimensional) submanifold of $\ma
thbb{R}^{n+1}$ if and only if

$$\boxed{a \neq 0}.$$
