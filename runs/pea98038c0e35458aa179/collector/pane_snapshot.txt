## Proof

Place square $ABCD$ with coordinates $A = (0,0)$, $B = (s,0)$, $C = (s,s)$, $D =
 (0,s)$, where $s$ is the side length. The midpoint of $AB$ is $M = (s/2, 0)$.

Line $l$ passes through $M$ and intersects segment $BC$ (the segment $x = s$, $0
 \le y \le s$) at some point $(s, t)$ with $0 \le t \le s$. The direction vector
 of $l$ is $(s/2,\, t)$, so

$$|l| = \sqrt{(s/2)^2 + t^2} = \sqrt{s^2/4 + t^2}.$$

The distance from a point $P$ to $l$ is $\dfrac{|(P - M) \times (s/2,\, t)|}{|l|
}$, where $\times$ denotes the 2-D cross product.

**Distance from $A$:** $A - M = (-s/2,\, 0)$, so
$$(A - M) \times (s/2,\, t) = (-s/2)(t) - (0)(s/2) = -\frac{st}{2}.$$
Thus $d(A, l) = \dfrac{st/2}{\sqrt{s^2/4 + t^2}} = 4. \quad (1)$

**Distance from $C$:** $C - M = (s/2,\, s)$, so
$$(C - M) \times (s/2,\, t) = (s/2)(t) - (s)(s/2) = \frac{st - s^2}{2} = -\frac{
s(s-t)}{2}.$$
Since $d(C,l) = 7 \neq 0$, we have $t < s$, so $|{st - s^2}/{2}| = s(s-t)/2$. Th
us
$$d(C, l) = \frac{s(s-t)/2}{\sqrt{s^2/4 + t^2}} = 7. \quad (2)$$

**Solving.** Dividing $(2)$ by $(1)$:
$$\frac{s - t}{t} = \frac{7}{4} \implies 4(s - t) = 7t \implies 4s = 11t \implie
s t = \frac{4s}{11}.$$

Substituting into $(1)$:
$$\frac{s \cdot (4s/11)/2}{\sqrt{s^2/4 + 16s^2/121}} = 4 \implies \frac{2s^2/11}
{s\sqrt{1/4 + 16/121}} = 4.$$

Compute the radical:
$$\frac{1}{4} + \frac{16}{121} = \frac{121 + 64}{484} = \frac{185}{484}, \qquad
\sqrt{\frac{185}{484}} = \frac{\sqrt{185}}{22}.$$

Therefore
$$\frac{2s/11}{\sqrt{185}/22} = 4 \implies \frac{2s}{11}\cdot\frac{22}{\sqrt{185
}} = 4 \implies \frac{4s}{\sqrt{185}} = 4 \implies s = \sqrt{185}.$$

**Verification.** With $s = \sqrt{185}$, $t = 4\sqrt{185}/11$:
- $s^2/4 + t^2 = 185/4 + 16\cdot 185/121 = 185\cdot\frac{185}{484} = \frac{185^2

- $d(C,l) = \frac{\sqrt{185}\cdot\sqrt{185}\cdot 7/(11\cdot 2)}{185/22} = \frac{

The area of square $ABCD$ is

$$\boxed{s^2 = 185}.$$
