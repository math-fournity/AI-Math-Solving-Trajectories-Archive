## Proof

**Setup.** Let $\boldsymbol{g}\sim CN(0,\boldsymbol{I}_N)$ and let $\boldsymbol{
A}=[\boldsymbol{a}_1,\dots,\boldsymbol{a}_M]$ with $\boldsymbol{a}_m^H\boldsymbo
l{a}_k=\delta_{mk}$ (orthonormal columns, requiring $M\le N$; we consider the re
gime where $N$ grows so that $M\to\infty$ is admissible). Define
$$
x=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\boldsymbol{g}^H\boldsymbol{a}_m.
$$

**Step 1 — Marginal distribution of each term.** For each $m$, set $z_m:=\boldsy
mbol{g}^H\boldsymbol{a}_m$. Since $\boldsymbol{g}$ is circularly symmetric compl
ex Gaussian,
$$
z_m\sim CN\!\bigl(0,\;\boldsymbol{a}_m^H\,\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{g}\boldsymbol{g
}^H]\,\boldsymbol{a}_m\bigr)=CN\!\bigl(0,\;\boldsymbol{a}_m^H\boldsymbol{a}_m\bi
gr)=CN(0,1),
$$
using $\|\boldsymbol{a}_m\|^2=1$.

**Step 2 — Joint distribution (independence).** The vector $\boldsymbol{z}=(z_1,
\dots,z_M)^T=\boldsymbol{A}^H\boldsymbol{g}$ is a linear transformation of $\bol
dsymbol{g}$, hence complex Gaussian with covariance
$$
\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{z}\boldsymbol{z}^H]=\boldsymbol{A}^H\,\mathbb{E}[\boldsym
bol{g}\boldsymbol{g}^H]\,\boldsymbol{A}=\boldsymbol{A}^H\boldsymbol{A}=\boldsymb
ol{I}_M,
$$
where the last equality uses orthonormality of the columns. Therefore the $z_m$
are **independent** $CN(0,1)$ random variables.

**Step 3 — Distribution of $x$ for finite $M$.** Since $x=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^
M z_m$ is a linear combination of independent complex Gaussians,
$$
x\sim CN\!\left(0,\;\frac{1}{M^2}\sum_{m=1}^M 1\right)=CN\!\left(0,\;\frac{1}{M}
\right).
$$

**Step 4 — Limit as $M\to\infty$.** The variance of $x$ is $\operatorname{Var}(x
)=1/M\to 0$. By Chebyshev's inequality, for any $\varepsilon>0$,
$$
\Pr\!\bigl(|x|>\varepsilon\bigr)\le\frac{\mathbb{E}[|x|^2]}{\varepsilon^2}=\frac
{1}{M\varepsilon^2}\xrightarrow[M\to\infty]{}0.
$$
Hence $x\to 0$ in probability, and therefore also in distribution.

**Conclusion.** As $M\to\infty$, the scalar $x$ converges in distribution to the
 degenerate distribution concentrated at $0$:
$$
\boxed{x\ \xrightarrow{\,d\,}\ 0\quad\text{as }M\to\infty,}
$$
i.e., $x$ converges to a deterministic constant $0$.
