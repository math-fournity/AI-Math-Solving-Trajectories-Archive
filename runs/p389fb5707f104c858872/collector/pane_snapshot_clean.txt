Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the Perron method solution
coincides with the unique $H^1(\Omega)$ solution for discontinuous boundary data
 $g = \chi_K$.

**Answer: Yes, the Perron solution coincides with the unique $H^1$ solution.**

**Proof:**

Let $u_P$ denote the Perron solution and $u_H$ the unique $H^1(\Omega)$ solution
.

**Preliminary observation.** The existence of a unique $H^1(\Omega)$ solution wi
th trace $g$ requires $g \in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)$, since on a Lipschitz doma
in the trace space of $H^1(\Omega)$ is precisely $H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)$. The
problem's assertion that the $H^1$ solution exists and is unique implicitly guar
antees $g \in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)$. We proceed under this assumption.

**Step 1: Both solutions are harmonic and bounded in $\Omega$.**

$u_P$ is harmonic by property (i). Since $g \in [0,1]$, the constant functions $
0$ and $1$ serve as a subharmonic minorant and superharmonic majorant respective
ly. By the Perron construction (which preserves bounds), $0 \le u_P \le 1$.

$u_H$ is a weak solution of $\Delta u = 0$; by Weyl's lemma, $u_H$ is smooth (he
nce harmonic) in $\Omega$. For boundedness: since $\operatorname{tr}(u_H) = g \l
e 1$, the function $v = (u_H - 1)^+ \in H^1_0(\Omega)$ (its trace is $(g-1)^+ =
0$). Testing the weak formulation with $v$:
$$0 = \int_\Omega \nabla u_H \cdot \nabla v\, dx = \int_\Omega |\nabla v|^2\, dx
,$$
so $v \equiv 0$ and $u_H \le 1$. Similarly, testing with $(u_H)^+$ gives $u_H \g
e 0$.

**Step 2: The Perron solution is the Poisson integral of $g$ with respect to har
monic measure.**

Since $\Omega$ has Lipschitz boundary, every boundary point is regular (each adm
its a barrier). For bounded Borel boundary data on a domain with regular boundar
y, the Perron solution coincides with the generalized Poisson integral:
$$u_P(x) = \int_{\partial\Omega} g(y)\, d\omega^x(y), \qquad x \in \Omega,$$
where $\omega^x$ is the harmonic measure at $x$. This is a standard result in cl
assical potential theory (the Perron solution is the unique bounded harmonic fun
ction attaining $g$ at every regular boundary point where $g$ is continuous, and
 the Poisson integral has the same property).

**Step 3: The $H^1$ solution is also the Poisson integral of $g$.**

On a Lipschitz domain, by the foundational results of Dahlberg (1977) and Vercho
ta (1984), the Poisson integral defines a bounded linear operator
$$P: H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega) \to H^1(\Omega).$$
Dahlberg proved that harmonic measure $\omega$ and surface measure $\sigma$ on $
\partial\Omega$ are mutually absolutely continuous with $d\omega/d\sigma \in L^p
(\partial\Omega)$ for some $p > 1$. Verchota used this to show that the Poisson
integral maps $H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)$ boundedly onto $H^1(\Omega)$, with $\ope
ratorname{tr}(Pg) = g$ for all $g \in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)$.

Since $g \in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)$, the function $Pg$ is harmonic in $\Omega$
, belongs to $H^1(\Omega)$, and satisfies $\operatorname{tr}(Pg) = g$. By the un
iqueness of the $H^1$ Dirichlet problem (the solution is unique because the diff
erence of two solutions lies in $H^1_0(\Omega)$ and is harmonic, hence zero), we
 conclude $u_H = Pg$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 2 and 3:
$$u_P(x) = \int_{\partial\Omega} g(y)\, d\omega^x(y) = u_H(x), \qquad \forall\,
x \in \Omega.$$

Therefore, the function produced by the Perron method coincides with the unique
$H^1(\Omega)$ solution. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the hypothesis.** The assumption $g \in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)$ is
essential for the argument. For $n \ge 2$, the characteristic function $\chi_K$
of a proper compact subset $K \subsetneq \partial\Omega$ with nonempty relative
interior generally does **not** belong to $H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)$, because the
 $H^{1/2}$ seminorm
$$\iint_{\partial\Omega \times \partial\Omega} \frac{|\chi_K(x) - \chi_K(y)|^2}{
|x-y|^{n}}\, d\sigma(x)\, d\sigma(y)$$
diverges (the integrand equals $1$ on $\partial K \times (\partial\Omega \setmin
us \overline{K})$-type regions, and the singularity along $\partial K$ is non-in
tegrable at the $H^{1/2}$ threshold). In such cases the standard $H^1$ solution
with trace $g$ does not exist, and the question would be ill-posed. The problem'
s assertion that the $H^1$ solution exists implicitly ensures $g \in H^{1/2}(\pa
rtial\Omega)$, under which the Perron solution—always well-defined for bounded B
orel data on a regular domain—belongs to $H^1(\Omega)$ and equals $u_H$ by uniqu
eness.
