Looking at this problem, I need to determine the sign of the quadratic form with
 kernel $\frac{1}{|x-y|^2}$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$.

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $g(x) = |x|^{-2}$, interpreted as a tempered distribution on $\ma
thbb{R}^3$. The integral can be written as a convolution quadratic form:

$$I_f = \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} f(x)\,(g * f)(x)\,dx.$$

**Fourier transform of the kernel.** We use the convention $\hat{f}(\xi) = \int_
{\mathbb{R}^n} f(x)\,e^{-2\pi i\, x\cdot\xi}\,dx$. A classical result on Riesz p
otentials (see, e.g., Stein, *Singular Integrals*, or Grafakos, *Classical Fouri
er Analysis*) states that for $0 < \alpha < n$,

$$\widehat{|x|^{-(n-\alpha)}}(\xi) = C_{n,\alpha}\,|\xi|^{-\alpha}, \qquad C_{n,
\alpha} > 0.$$

Here $n=3$ and $|x|^{-2} = |x|^{-(3-1)}$, so $\alpha = 1$ (which satisfies $0 <
1 < 3$). Therefore:

$$\hat{g}(\xi) = C\,|\xi|^{-1}, \qquad C = C_{3,1} > 0.$$

This is a real, non-negative (in fact positive for $\xi \neq 0$) function.

**Applying Plancherel.** Since $f$ is real-valued, $\bar{f} = f$. Using Plancher
el's theorem and the convolution theorem $\widehat{g*f} = \hat{g}\cdot\hat{f}$:

$$I_f = \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} f(x)\,(g*f)(x)\,dx = \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} \hat{f}(\xi
)\,\overline{\hat{g}(\xi)\,\hat{f}(\xi)}\,d\xi = \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} \overline{\
hat{g}(\xi)}\,|\hat{f}(\xi)|^2\,d\xi.$$

Since $\hat{g}(\xi) = C\,|\xi|^{-1}$ is real and positive for $\xi \neq 0$:

$$\boxed{I_f = C\int_{\mathbb{R}^3} \frac{|\hat{f}(\xi)|^2}{|\xi|}\,d\xi.}$$

**Convergence.** Since $f \in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^3)$, its Fourier transform
$\hat{f}$ is also Schwartz, so $|\hat{f}(\xi)|^2$ decays faster than any polynom
ial. Near $\xi = 0$, $|\hat{f}(\xi)|^2$ is bounded, and in spherical coordinates
:

$$\int_{|\xi|<1} \frac{|\hat{f}(\xi)|^2}{|\xi|}\,d\xi \leq \|\hat{f}\|_\infty^2
\int_{|\xi|<1} |\xi|^{-1}\,d\xi = \|\hat{f}\|_\infty^2 \cdot 4\pi\int_0^1 r\,dr
= 2\pi\,\|\hat{f}\|_\infty^2 < \infty.$$

At infinity, the rapid decay of $|\hat{f}|^2$ ensures integrability. So $I_f$ is
 well-defined and finite.

**Sign.** The integrand $\frac{|\hat{f}(\xi)|^2}{|\xi|}$ is non-negative everywh
ere (and strictly positive on a set of positive measure whenever $f \not\equiv 0
$, since then $|\hat{f}|^2 > 0$ on an open set, and $|\xi|^{-1} > 0$ for $\xi \n
eq 0$). With $C > 0$, we conclude:

$$I_f \geq 0 \quad \text{for all } f \in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^3),$$

with equality if and only if $f \equiv 0$.

**Conclusion.** Yes, it is true that $I_f \geq 0$ for every real Schwartz functi
on $f$. The kernel $|x-y|^{-2}$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$ defines a positive semi-defini
te quadratic form, as revealed by its Fourier transform $\hat{g}(\xi) = C\,|\xi|
^{-1}$ being a positive function.
