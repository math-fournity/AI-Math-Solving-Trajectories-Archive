## Theorem

Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ be a bounded domain (open, connected, bounded)
. Then $L^2(\Omega)$, equipped with the Lebesgue measure, admits a countable ort
honormal basis.

## Proof

The argument has two parts: (A) $L^2(\Omega)$ is a separable Hilbert space; (B)
every separable Hilbert space has a countable orthonormal basis.

### Part A. $L^2(\Omega)$ is a separable Hilbert space

**Hilbert space structure.** With inner product
$$\langle f, g\rangle = \int_\Omega f(x)\overline{g(x)}\,dx,$$
$L^2(\Omega)$ is complete (Riesz–Fischer theorem), hence a Hilbert space.

**Separability.** We exhibit a countable dense subset. Let $\mathcal{S}$ be the
set of all *rational step functions*
$$\varphi = \sum_{k=1}^{N} q_k\,\chi_{R_k\cap\Omega},$$
where $N\in\mathbb{N}$, $q_k\in\mathbb{Q}+\mathrm{i}\mathbb{Q}$, and each $R_k$
is a closed axis-aligned rectangle in $\mathbb{R}^n$ with rational vertices. The
 collection of such rectangles is countable, the rational scalars are countable,
 and finite sums of countable sets are countable, so $\mathcal{S}$ is countable.

We claim $\mathcal{S}$ is dense in $L^2(\Omega)$.

1. **Simple functions are dense.** Every $f\in L^2(\Omega)$ is the $L^2$-limit o
f simple functions $\sum c_k\chi_{E_k}$ with $E_k\subseteq\Omega$ measurable (st
andard approximation by truncation and level-set decomposition).

2. **Measure-theoretic approximation of $\chi_E$.** By regularity of the Lebesgu
e measure, for every measurable $E\subseteq\Omega$ and every $\varepsilon>0$ the
re exists a compact $K\subseteq E$ and an open $U\supseteq E$ with $|U\setminus
K|<\varepsilon$. Cover $K$ by finitely many rational rectangles $R_1,\dots,R_m$
contained in $U$; then $\bigcup R_k$ approximates $E$ in measure, hence $\chi_{\
bigcup R_k}\to\chi_E$ in $L^2$. Replacing each $c_k$ by a nearby rational $q_k$
gives an element of $\mathcal{S}$ arbitrarily close to the original simple funct
ion.

Combining steps 1 and 2, $\mathcal{S}$ is dense in $L^2(\Omega)$. Hence $L^2(\Om
ega)$ is separable.

### Part B. A separable Hilbert space has a countable orthonormal basis

Let $\{f_1, f_2, f_3,\dots\}$ be a countable dense subset of $L^2(\Omega)$ (obta
ined from Part A). Apply the **Gram–Schmidt process**:

- Set $e_1 = f_1/\|f_1\|$ (discard $f_1$ if it is zero).
- Having constructed orthonormal $e_1,\dots,e_m$, take the next $f_j$ not in $\o
peratorname{span}\{e_1,\dots,e_m\}$, set
$$g = f_j - \sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle f_j, e_i\rangle\,e_i,\qquad e_{m+1}=g/\|g\|.$$

This produces a (finite or countably infinite) orthonormal sequence $\{e_k\}$.

**Completeness of the system.** By construction each $f_j$ lies in $\overline{\o
peratorname{span}\{e_k\}}$. Since $\{f_j\}$ is dense in $L^2(\Omega)$,
$$\overline{\operatorname{span}\{e_k\}} = L^2(\Omega).$$
Thus $\{e_k\}$ is a *complete* orthonormal system, i.e. an orthonormal basis: ev
ery $f\in L^2(\Omega)$ admits the convergent expansion
$$f = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle f, e_k\rangle\,e_k,\qquad \|f\|^2=\sum_{k=1}^{\
infty}|\langle f,e_k\rangle|^2$$
(Parseval's identity).

### Conclusion

Since $L^2(\Omega)$ is a separable Hilbert space, it possesses a countable ortho
normal basis, obtained concretely by applying Gram–Schmidt to any countable dens
e subset (such as the rational step functions constructed above). Therefore **ye
s**, it is possible to construct an orthonormal basis for $L^2(\Omega)$ when $\O
mega$ is a bounded domain in $\mathbb{R}^n$.
