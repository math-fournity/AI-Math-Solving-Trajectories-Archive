## Proof

**Setup.** We have a Poisson point process $\Phi$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$ with intensi
ty measure $\mu(S) = \lambda \cdot \text{Vol}(S)$, where $\text{Vol}(\cdot)$ den
otes 3-dimensional Lebesgue measure. For any measurable set $S$, the number of p
oints $N(S) = |\Phi \cap S|$ is a Poisson random variable with parameter $\mu(S)
 = \lambda\,\text{Vol}(S)$.

**Key observation.** A line $L$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is a 1-dimensional affine subs
pace. Its 3-dimensional Lebesgue measure (volume) is zero:
$$\text{Vol}(L) = 0.$$

**Step 1: Fixed line.** For any fixed (deterministic) line $L_0 \subset \mathbb{
R}^3$, the number of Poisson points lying on $L_0$ is
$$N(L_0) \sim \text{Poisson}\!\big(\lambda \cdot \text{Vol}(L_0)\big) = \text{Po
isson}(\lambda \cdot 0) = \text{Poisson}(0).$$
A Poisson(0) random variable is almost surely $0$, so
$$\mathbb{P}\big(N(L_0) \geq 1\big) = 1 - e^{-0} \cdot \frac{0^0}{0!} = 1 - 1 =
0.$$

**Step 2: Random line.** The line $L$ is chosen randomly (uniformly over all dir
ections through the origin), independently of the Poisson process $\Phi$. Condit
ioning on the line and applying the law of total probability:
$$\mathbb{P}\big(N(L) \geq 1\big) = \mathbb{E}_{L}\!\Big[\mathbb{P}\big(N(L) \ge
q 1 \;\big|\; L\big)\Big] = \mathbb{E}_{L}[0] = 0.$$

The last equality holds because, by Step 1, the conditional probability is $0$ f
or **every** realization of $L$ (regardless of its direction), since every line
through the origin has 3-dimensional volume $0$.

**Step 3: Independence of $\lambda$.** Note that the argument does not depend on
 the value of $\lambda$, as long as $\lambda$ is finite: $\lambda \cdot 0 = 0$ f
or all $\lambda \in [0, \infty)$. Whether the expected count in volume $V$ is $V
$ (i.e., $\lambda = 1$) or $\lambda V$ for general $\lambda$, the conclusion is
identical.

**Conclusion.** The probability that the random line $L$ intersects at least one
 point of the Poisson distribution is

$$\boxed{0}.$$

This reflects the general principle that a Poisson point process with intensity
absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure places no points on any f
ixed lower-dimensional manifold (here, a 1-dimensional line in $\mathbb{R}^3$),
almost surely.

### PROOF COMPLETE
