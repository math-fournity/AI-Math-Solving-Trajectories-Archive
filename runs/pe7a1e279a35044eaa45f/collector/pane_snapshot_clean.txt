Looking at this problem, I need to find $b_n$, the smallest base where: for ever
y integer $N$ and every $m \in [2,n]$, if $m$ divides the digit sum $S_{b_n}(N)$
, then $m$ divides $N$.

## Key Lemma: The property holds for $m$ iff $m \mid (b-1)$

**Sufficiency:** If $m \mid (b-1)$, then $b \equiv 1 \pmod{m}$, so $b^k \equiv 1
 \pmod{m}$ for all $k$. Thus $N = \sum a_k b^k \equiv \sum a_k = S_b(N) \pmod{m}
$, giving $m \mid S_b(N) \Rightarrow m \mid N$. ✓

**Necessity:** Suppose $m \nmid (b-1)$. I construct a counterexample $N$ with $S
_b(N) = m$ but $m \nmid N$.

**Case 1: $\gcd(b,m) > 1$.** Let $p \mid \gcd(b,m)$. Take $N = 1 + b + b^2 + \cd
ots + b^{m-1}$ (all digits 1, digit sum $m$). Since $p \mid b$, we get $N \equiv
 1 \pmod{p}$, so $p \nmid N$, hence $m \nmid N$. Counterexample. ✓

**Case 2: $\gcd(b,m) = 1$, $b \geq 3$.** Take $N_1 = \frac{b^m-1}{b-1}$ (digit s
um $m$). If $m \nmid N_1$, done. If $m \mid N_1$, take $N' = N_1 + b^j(b-1)$ for
 some $j < m-1$ (moves a 1 from position $j$ to position $j+1$, making digit 2 —
 valid since $b \geq 3$). Digit sum stays $m$, but $N' \equiv b^j(b-1) \pmod{m}$
. Since $\gcd(b,m)=1$ and $m \nmid (b-1)$, we get $m \nmid N'$. Counterexample.
✓

**Case 3: $b = 2$, $m$ odd.** Take $N = 2^m - 1$ (digit sum $m$). If $m \mid (2^
m - 1)$, let $p$ be the smallest prime factor of $m$. Then $\text{ord}_p(2) \mid
 m$ and $\text{ord}_p(2) \mid (p-1)$. All prime factors of $m$ are $\geq p$, whi
le all prime factors of $p-1$ are $< p$, so $\gcd(\text{ord}_p(2), m) = 1$, forc
ing $\text{ord}_p(2) = 1$, i.e., $2 \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$ — contradiction. So $m \n
mid (2^m - 1)$. Counterexample. ✓

**Case 4: $b = 2$, $m$ even.** Then $2 \mid m$ and $2 \mid b$, so $\gcd(b,m) > 1
$ — reduces to Case 1. ✓

This exhausts all cases. **The property holds for $m$ if and only if $m \mid (b-
1)$.**

## Computing $b_n$

$b_n$ is the smallest $b$ with $m \mid (b-1)$ for all $m \in [2,n]$, i.e., $\tex
t{lcm}(2,3,\ldots,n) \mid (b-1)$.

$$b_n = \text{lcm}(2,3,\ldots,n) + 1$$

## Computing the ratio $b_{36}/b_{25}$

**For $n = 25$:** Highest prime powers $\leq 25$: $2^4=16,\; 3^2=9,\; 5^2=25,\;
7,\; 11,\; 13,\; 17,\; 19,\; 23$.

$$L_{25} = 16 \times 9 \times 25 \times 7 \times 11 \times 13 \times 17 \times 1
9 \times 23 = 26{,}771{,}144{,}400$$

**For $n = 36$:** Highest prime powers $\leq 36$: $2^5=32,\; 3^3=27,\; 5^2=25,\;
 7,\; 11,\; 13,\; 17,\; 19,\; 23,\; 29,\; 31$.

$$\frac{L_{36}}{L_{25}} = \frac{32}{16} \cdot \frac{27}{9} \cdot 29 \cdot 31 = 2
 \cdot 3 \cdot 899 = 5394$$

So $L_{36} = 5394 \cdot L_{25}$.

## Final computation

$$\frac{b_{36}}{b_{25}} = \frac{5394 \cdot L_{25} + 1}{L_{25} + 1} = \frac{5394(
L_{25}+1) - 5394 + 1}{L_{25}+1} = 5394 - \frac{5393}{26{,}771{,}144{,}401}$$

This equals $5394 - 2.01 \times 10^{-7} \approx 5393.9999998$.

The nearest integer is $\boxed{5394}$.
