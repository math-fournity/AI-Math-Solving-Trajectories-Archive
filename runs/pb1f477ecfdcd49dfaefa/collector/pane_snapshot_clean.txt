Proof

Setup. Let $g: B \to A$ with $g \circ f_i = \mathrm{id}_A$ for $i=1,2$. Each
$f_i$ is injective and $g$ is surjective. For a section $f$ of $g$, define: $
$N(f) = {g': B \to A \mid g' \circ f = \mathrm{id}_A}.$$

Step 1: Identify $N(f)$ with a function space.

The condition $g' \circ f = \mathrm{id}A$ means $g'(f(a)) = a$ for every $a \in
A$. This pins down $g'$ completely on the subset $f(A) \subseteq B$ (namely $g'|
{f(A)} = f^{-1}$, which is well-defined since $f$ is injective). On the
complement $B \setminus f(A)$, the function $g'$ is completely free: it can be
any map $B \setminus f(A) \to A$.

This gives a bijection: $$N(f) ;\cong; A^{,B \setminus f(A)}.$$ Explicitly: $g'
\mapsto g'|{B \setminus f(A)}$, with inverse $h \mapsto g'$ where $g'(f(a))=a$
and $g'|{B\setminus f(A)} = h$.

Step 2: Show $|B \setminus f(A)|$ is independent of the section $f$.

Decompose $B$ into fibers of $g$: $$B = \bigsqcup_{a \in A} g^{-1}(a).$$ For any
section $f$, we have $f(a) \in g^{-1}(a)$, so: $$B \setminus f(A) = \bigsqcup_{a
\in A} \bigl(g^{-1}(a) \setminus {f(a)}\bigr).$$

The key observation: $|g^{-1}(a) \setminus {f(a)}|$ depends only on $|g^{-1}(a)|
$, not on which element $f(a)$ is chosen. Indeed:

  • If $|g^{-1}(a)| = n < \aleph_0$ (finite, $n \geq 1$ since $g$ is
  surjective), then $|g^{-1}(a) \setminus {f(a)}| = n - 1$, regardless of which
  element is removed.
  • If $|g^{-1}(a)| \geq \aleph_0$ (infinite), then removing a single point does
  not change the cardinality: $|g^{-1}(a) \setminus {f(a)}| = |g^{-1}(a)|$.

Define $c_a := |g^{-1}(a) \setminus {f(a)}|$, which is well-defined (independent
of $f$) by the above. Then: $$|B \setminus f(A)| = \sum_{a \in A} c_a,$$ where
the right-hand side is the cardinality of a disjoint union, determined entirely
by the multiset of cardinals ${c_a : a \in A}$, which in turn is determined by
the fiber sizes ${|g^{-1}(a)| : a \in A}$ — all independent of $f$.

Therefore $|B \setminus f_1(A)| = |B \setminus f_2(A)|$ for any two sections
$f_1, f_2$.

Step 3: Conclude.

From Steps 1 and 2: $$|N(f_1)| = |A|^{|B \setminus f_1(A)|} = |A|^{|B \setminus
f_2(A)|} = |N(f_2)|.$$

Since $N(f_1)$ and $N(f_2)$ have the same cardinality, there exists a bijection
between them. $\blacksquare$
