The generalized Kronecker delta tensor $\delta^{i_1 \ldots i_n}_{j_1 \ldots j_n}
$ is defined as the determinant of an $n \times n$ matrix whose entries are ordi
nary Kronecker deltas. Its determinantal (array) form is:

$$
\delta^{i_1 i_2 \cdots i_n}_{j_1 j_2 \cdots j_n}
\;=\;
\begin{vmatrix}
\delta^{i_1}_{j_1} & \delta^{i_1}_{j_2} & \cdots & \delta^{i_1}_{j_n} \\
\delta^{i_2}_{j_1} & \delta^{i_2}_{j_2} & \cdots & \delta^{i_2}_{j_n} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
\delta^{i_n}_{j_1} & \delta^{i_n}_{j_2} & \cdots & \delta^{i_n}_{j_n}
\end{vmatrix},
$$

where each entry $\delta^{i_a}_{j_b}$ is the ordinary Kronecker delta, equal to
$1$ if $i_a = j_b$ and $0$ otherwise, and the vertical bars denote the determina
nt of the enclosed $n \times n$ array.

**Derivation / justification.**

The generalized Kronecker delta is the unique totally antisymmetric rank-$(n,n)$
 tensor that equals $+1$ when $(i_1,\ldots,i_n) = (j_1,\ldots,j_n)$ is an even p
ermutation of a fixed ordering, $-1$ for an odd permutation, and $0$ whenever an
y two upper (or lower) indices coincide. The determinant of the array $(\delta^{
i_a}_{j_b})_{a,b=1}^n$ has exactly these properties:

1. **Antisymmetry in the upper indices.** Swapping two upper indices $i_a \leftr
ightarrow i_b$ swaps two rows of the matrix, which changes the sign of the deter
minant.

2. **Antisymmetry in the lower indices.** Swapping two lower indices $j_a \leftr
ightarrow j_b$ swaps two columns of the matrix, which changes the sign of the de
terminant.

3. **Value on the diagonal.** When $(i_1,\ldots,i_n) = (j_1,\ldots,j_n)$, the ma
trix becomes the identity matrix, whose determinant is $+1$.

4. **Vanishing when indices repeat.** If two upper indices coincide, two rows ar
e identical and the determinant is $0$; likewise for repeated lower indices (two
 identical columns).

Expanding the determinant by the Leibniz formula gives the equivalent summation
form

$$
\delta^{i_1 \ldots i_n}_{j_1 \ldots j_n}
= \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\,
\delta^{i_1}_{j_{\sigma(1)}}\,\delta^{i_2}_{j_{\sigma(2)}} \cdots \delta^{i_n}_{
j_{\sigma(n)}},
$$

which is precisely the determinant of the array displayed above. This confirms t
hat the determinantal array form correctly represents the generalized Kronecker
delta in terms of ordinary Kronecker deltas.
