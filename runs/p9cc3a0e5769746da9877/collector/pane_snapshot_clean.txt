## Proof

**Yes, such a field exists.**

### Construction

Let $F_0 = \mathbb{Q}(\mu_\infty) \subset \mathbb{C}$, the field obtained by adj
oining all roots of unity to $\mathbb{Q}$. Inductively define
$$F_{n+1} = F_n\!\left(\left\{a^{1/p} : a \in F_n^*,\; p \neq 5 \text{ prime}\ri
ght\}\right),$$
and set $F = \bigcup_{n \geq 0} F_n$.

### $F^{*p} = F^*$ for every prime $p \neq 5$

For any $x \in F^*$, we have $x \in F_n^*$ for some $n$, so $x^{1/p} \in F_{n+1}
 \subseteq F^*$. Thus every element of $F^*$ is a $p$-th power. $\checkmark$

### $F^{*5} \neq F^*$

We show $2^{1/5} \notin F$, so $2 \in F^*$ is not a fifth power.

**Key Claim.** Every finite subextension $K/F_0$ with $K \subseteq F$ satisfies
$5 \nmid :F_0]$.

*Proof of Claim.* Since $F_0$ contains all roots of unity, each $F_n$ does as we
ll. For any $a \in F_n^*$ and prime $p \neq 5$, the extension $F_n(a^{1/p})/F_n$
 is a Kummer extension of degree $1$ or $p$ (since $\zeta_p \in F_n$). Any finit
e subextension of $F_{n+1}/F_n$ lies inside a compositum of finitely many such e
xtensions, so its degree over $F_n$ is a product of primes $\neq 5$. Composing t
he tower, any finite subextension of $F/F_0$ has degree coprime to $5$. $\square
$

**$2$ is not a fifth power in $F_0$.** Suppose $2 = x^5$ with $x \in F_0 = \bigc
up_n \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$. Then $x \in \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ for some $n$, giving
 $\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/5}) \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$. But $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\
mathbb{Q}$ is abelian, so every subfield is Galois over $\mathbb{Q}$. Meanwhile,
 $\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/5})/\mathbb{Q}$ is **not** Galois: the polynomial $t^5 - 2$ is
 irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ (Eisenstein at $2$), and $\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/5}) \su
bset \mathbb{R}$ does not contain $\zeta_5$, so it cannot contain all conjugates
 $2^{1/5}\zeta_5^k$. This is a contradiction.

Since $\zeta_5 \in F_0$ and $2 \notin (F_0^*)^5$, Kummer theory gives $[F_0(2^{1
/5}) : F_0] = 5$.

**Conclusion.** If $2^{1/5} \in F$, then $F_0(2^{1/5}) \subseteq K$ for some fin
ite extension $K/F_0$ with $K \subseteq F$. This forces $5 \mid :F_0]$, contra
dicting the Key Claim. Therefore $2^{1/5} \notin F$, so $2 \notin (F^*)^5$, and
$F^{*5} \neq F^*$. $\blacksquare$
