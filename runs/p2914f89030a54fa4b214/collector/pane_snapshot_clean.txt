**No**, it does not follow. Here is a counterexample.

---

Let $X = [0,1]$ with Lebesgue measure $m$. Define the **typewriter sequence** of
 intervals $\{I_n\}$ as follows:

- Stage $k=1$: one interval $I_1 = [0,1]$.
- Stage $k=2$: two intervals $I_2 = [0,\tfrac12]$, $I_3 = [\tfrac12, 1]$.
- Stage $k$: $2^{k-1}$ intervals of length $1/2^{k-1}$ partitioning $[0,1]$:
$$I_{2^{k-1}},\; I_{2^{k-1}+1},\; \dots,\; I_{2^k - 1}.$$

Concretely, at stage $k$ the intervals are $\left[\frac{j}{2^{k-1}},\,\frac{j+1}
{2^{k-1}}\right]$ for $j = 0, 1, \dots, 2^{k-1}-1$, enumerated in order.

Now set
$$f_n = \mathbf{1}_{I_n}, \qquad n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$$

Each $f_n$ is a nonnegative measurable function on $X$.

---

**Step 1: $\displaystyle\int_X \sqrt{f_n}\,dm \to 0$.**

Since $f_n = \mathbf{1}_{I_n}$, we have $\sqrt{f_n} = \mathbf{1}_{I_n}$, so
$$\int_X \sqrt{f_n}\,dm = m(I_n) = \frac{1}{2^{k-1}},$$
where $k$ is the stage to which $n$ belongs. As $n \to \infty$, the stage $k \to
 \infty$, hence
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_X \sqrt{f_n}\,dm = \lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k-1}} =
0.$$

---

**Step 2: $f_n \not\to 0$ a.e. (in fact, $f_n \not\to 0$ at *any* point).**

Fix any $x \in [0,1]$. At every stage $k$, the $2^{k-1}$ intervals of that stage
 partition $[0,1]$, so $x$ lies in exactly one of them. Therefore, for each stag
e $k$, there exists an index $n_k$ (with $2^{k-1} \le n_k \le 2^k - 1$) such tha
t $x \in I_{n_k}$, i.e.,
$$f_{n_k}(x) = 1.$$

This gives an infinite subsequence $\{n_k\}_{k=1}^\infty$ with $n_k \to \infty$
and $f_{n_k}(x) = 1$ for all $k$. Hence $f_n(x)$ does not converge to $0$:
$$\limsup_{n\to\infty} f_n(x) = 1 \ne 0.$$

Since this holds for **every** $x \in [0,1]$, the set where $f_n \not\to 0$ is a
ll of $[0,1]$, which has measure $1$. In particular,
$$m\bigl(\{x \in X : f_n(x) \not\to 0\}\bigr) = 1 \ne 0.$$

---

**Conclusion.**

We have constructed a sequence of nonnegative functions $\{f_n\}$ on $X = [0,1]$
 satisfying
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_X \sqrt{f_n}\,dm = 0,$$
yet $f_n$ fails to converge to $0$ at **every** point of $X$. Therefore the impl
ication is **false**: the condition $\int_X \sqrt{f_n}\,dm \to 0$ does **not** i
mply $f_n \to 0$ almost everywhere.
