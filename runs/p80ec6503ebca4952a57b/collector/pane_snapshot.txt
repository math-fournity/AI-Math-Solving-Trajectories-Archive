**Proof.**

Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ complex matrix with singular values $\sigma_1 \geq \s
igma_2 \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_n \geq 0$. Then $A^*A$ (positive semidefinite) ha
s eigenvalues $\sigma_1^2, \ldots, \sigma_n^2$, and:

$$\det(I - A^*A) = \prod_{i=1}^n (1 - \sigma_i^2), \qquad \|A\|_{\text{op}} = \s
igma_1.$$

---

**Forward direction (holds for all $n \geq 1$):** If $\|A\|_{\text{op}} \leq 1$,
 then $\sigma_i \leq 1$ for all $i$, so each factor $(1-\sigma_i^2) \geq 0$, hen
ce $\det(I - A^*A) \geq 0$. By contrapositive:

$$\det(I - A^*A) < 0 \implies \|A\|_{\text{op}} > 1.$$

---

**Reverse direction ($n = 1$):** For $n=1$, $A = [a]$, so $\det(I - A^*A) = 1 -
|a|^2$ and $\|A\|_{\text{op}} = |a|$. Thus:

$$\|A\|_{\text{op}} > 1 \iff |a| > 1 \iff 1 - |a|^2 < 0 \iff \det(I - A^*A) < 0.
$$

So the equivalence holds for $n = 1$.

---

**Reverse direction fails for $n \geq 2$:** Consider $A = \operatorname{diag}(2,
\, 2,\, \tfrac{1}{2},\, \ldots,\, \tfrac{1}{2})$ (with $n-2$ entries equal to $\
frac{1}{2}$). Then:

- $\|A\|_{\text{op}} = 2 > 1$.
- $A^*A = \operatorname{diag}(4,\, 4,\, \tfrac{1}{4},\, \ldots,\, \tfrac{1}{4})$
.
- $I - A^*A = \operatorname{diag}(-3,\, -3,\, \tfrac{3}{4},\, \ldots,\, \tfrac{3
}{4})$.
- $\det(I - A^*A) = (-3)^2 \cdot \left(\tfrac{3}{4}\right)^{n-2} = 9 \cdot \left
(\tfrac{3}{4}\right)^{n-2} > 0$.

So $\|A\|_{\text{op}} > 1$ but $\det(I - A^*A) > 0$, showing the reverse implica
tion fails for all $n \geq 2$.

---

**Conclusion.** The condition $\det(I - A^*A) < 0$ is equivalent to $\|A\|_{\tex
t{op}} > 1$ if and only if $n = 1$. For $n \geq 2$, one can have $\|A\|_{\text{o
p}} > 1$ with $\det(I - A^*A) \geq 0$ (an even number of singular values exceedi
ng 1 makes the determinant non-negative).

$$\boxed{n = 1}$$
