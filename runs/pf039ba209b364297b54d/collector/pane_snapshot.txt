## Proof

**The statement is true.**

Let $A$ be a $C^*$-algebra and let $a \in A$ be self-adjoint, i.e. $a = a^*$.

**Step 1: Reduce to a commutative $C^*$-subalgebra.**

Let $C^*(a, 1)$ denote the unital $C^*$-subalgebra of $A$ generated by $a$ (and
the identity if $A$ is unital; if not, we work in the unitization $\tilde A$, wh
ich does not affect the conclusion since $a \in A$ and the constructed elements
will lie in $C^*(a) \subseteq A$). Because $a$ is self-adjoint, $C^*(a,1)$ is ab
elian: it is the norm-closure of the algebra of polynomials in $a$, and polynomi
als in a single self-adjoint element commute.

**Step 2: Apply the Gelfand representation.**

By the Gelfand–Naimark theorem for commutative $C^*$-algebras, $C^*(a,1) \cong C
(\sigma(a))$, where $\sigma(a)$ is the spectrum of $a$. Since $a$ is self-adjoin
t, $\sigma(a) \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, and $\sigma(a)$ is compact. Under this isom
orphism, $a$ corresponds to the identity function
$$
\mathrm{id} : \sigma(a) \to \mathbb{R}, \qquad \mathrm{id}(t) = t.
$$
Moreover, the isomorphism is a $*$-isomorphism, hence it preserves addition, mul
tiplication, scalar multiplication, the involution, and the order structure (pos
itivity).

**Step 3: Construct the positive and negative parts as functions.**

Define two continuous functions on $\sigma(a) \subseteq \mathbb{R}$:
$$
f_+(t) = \max(t, 0), \qquad f_-(t) = \max(-t, 0).
$$
Both are continuous (they are compositions of continuous functions), and both ar
e non-negative valued, so they correspond to **positive** elements of $C^*(a,1)
\subseteq A$.

We verify the three required properties pointwise:

1. **Decomposition.** For every $t \in \sigma(a)$:
   - If $t \ge 0$: $f_+(t) - f_-(t) = t - 0 = t$.
   - If $t < 0$: $f_+(t) - f_-(t) = 0 - (-t) = t$.

   Hence $f_+ - f_- = \mathrm{id}$, and applying the isomorphism gives $a = a_+
- a_-$.

2. **Orthogonality.** For every $t \in \sigma(a)$:
   - If $t \ge 0$: $f_+(t) \cdot f_-(t) = t \cdot 0 = 0$.
   - If $t < 0$: $f_+(t) \cdot f_-(t) = 0 \cdot (-t) = 0$.

   Hence $f_+ \cdot f_- = 0$ in $C(\sigma(a))$. Since $C(\sigma(a))$ is abelian,
 $f_- \cdot f_+ = 0$ as well. Applying the isomorphism yields
   $$
   a_+ a_- = a_- a_+ = 0.
   $$

3. **Positivity.** As noted, $f_+ \ge 0$ and $f_- \ge 0$ pointwise, which is pre
cisely the characterization of positive elements in $C(\sigma(a))$. Therefore $a
_+ \ge 0$ and $a_- \ge 0$ in $A$.

**Conclusion.** Setting $a_+$ and $a_-$ to be the Gelfand transforms of $f_+$ an
d $f_-$ respectively, we obtain positive elements in $A$ satisfying
$$
a = a_+ - a_-, \qquad a_+ a_- = a_- a_+ = 0.
$$
This is the **Jordan decomposition** of a self-adjoint element in a $C^*$-algebr
a, and it always exists. $\blacksquare$
