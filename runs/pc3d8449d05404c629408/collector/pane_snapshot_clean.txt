## Proof

- On objects: $F(X) = X$.
- On morphisms: a function $f: X \to Y$ is sent to its graph $R_f \subseteq X \t
imes Y$, where $(x, y) \in R_f \iff f(x) = y$.

We must show: if $R_f$ is an isomorphism in $\mathbf{Rel}$, then $f$ is an isomo
rphism (bijection) in $\mathbf{Set}$.

**Isomorphisms in Rel.** Recall that in $\mathbf{Rel}$, the identity on $X$ is t
he diagonal relation $\Delta_X = \{(x,x) : x \in X\}$, and composition is relati
onal composition:
$$(S \circ R) = \{(x, z) : \exists\, y,\; (x,y) \in R \text{ and } (y,z) \in S\}
.$$
A relation $R: X \to Y$ is an isomorphism iff there exists $S: Y \to X$ with $S
\circ R = \Delta_X$ and $R \circ S = \Delta_Y$.

**Assume $R_f$ is an isomorphism.** Let $S: Y \to X$ be its inverse, so:

1. $S \circ R_f = \Delta_X$, i.e., $\{(x,z) : (f(x), z) \in S\} = \{(x,x) : x \i
n X\}$.
2. $R_f \circ S = \Delta_Y$, i.e., $\{(y,w) : \exists\, x,\; (y,x) \in S,\; f(x)
 = w\} = \{(y,y) : y \in Y\}$.

**$f$ is injective.** Suppose $f(x_1) = f(x_2) = y$. From condition (1), applied
 to $x_1$: $(f(x_1), x_1) \in S$, i.e., $(y, x_1) \in S$. Applied to $x_2$: $(y,
 x_2) \in S$. But condition (1) also says that for each $x$, the set $\{z : (f(x
), z) \in S\}$ equals $\{x\}$ — it is a singleton. Since $(y, x_1) \in S$ and $(
y, x_2) \in S$ with $y = f(x_1) = f(x_2)$, uniqueness forces $x_1 = x_2$. Hence
$f$ is injective.

**$f$ is surjective.** Let $y \in Y$. By condition (2), $(y, y) \in R_f \circ S$
, so there exists $x \in X$ with $(y, x) \in S$ and $f(x) = y$. Thus $y$ lies in
 the image of $f$. Since $y$ was arbitrary, $f$ is surjective.

**Conclusion.** $f$ is both injective and surjective, hence a bijection, i.e., a
n isomorphism in $\mathbf{Set}$. Therefore the graph functor $F: \mathbf{Set} \t
o \mathbf{Rel}$ is conservative (it reflects isomorphisms). $\blacksquare$
