## Solution

**Setup.** Place the pavement ℓ as the x-axis. Since each tank is tangent to ℓ,
the centers are at heights equal to their radii:
$$A=(x_A,\,a),\quad B=(x_B,\,b),\quad C=(x_C,\,c).$$

For two circles of radii $r_1,r_2$ both tangent to ℓ and externally tangent to e
ach other, the distance between centers equals $r_1+r_2$, while the vertical sep
aration is $|r_1-r_2|$. Hence the horizontal separation satisfies
$$(\Delta x)^2=(r_1+r_2)^2-(r_1-r_2)^2=4r_1r_2,\quad\text{so}\quad |\Delta x|=2\
sqrt{r_1r_2}.$$

**Parametrization.** Set $t=\sqrt{a/c},\; s=\sqrt{b/c}$ (both $>1$ since $c$ is
smallest) and place $C$ at $x_C=0$. Then $x_A=\pm 2\sqrt{ac}= \pm 2tc\cdot\sqrt{
c}\cdot\ldots$ — more cleanly, in units where $c=1$: $x_A=\pm 2t$, $x_B=\pm 2s$,
 and the constraint $|x_A-x_B|=2\sqrt{ab}=2ts$ must hold.

- **Opposite sides** ($x_A=2t,\;x_B=-2s$): $2t+2s=2ts$, giving $t+s=ts$, i.e. $(
t-1)(s-1)=1$. ✓ feasible for $t,s>1$.
- **Same side** ($x_A=2t,\;x_B=2s$): $|t-s|=ts$. But for $t,s>1$, $|t-s|<\max(t,
s)<ts$, so this is **impossible**.

Thus only the opposite-sides configuration applies, with constraint $\boxed{(t-1
)(s-1)=1}$.

**Angle at C.** The side lengths are $|CA|=a+c$, $|CB|=b+c$, $|AB|=a+b$. By the
law of cosines at $C$:
$$\cos\gamma=\frac{(a+c)^2+(b+c)^2-(a+b)^2}{2(a+c)(b+c)}=\frac{c(a+b+c)-ab}{(a+c
)(b+c)}.$$

Setting $a=t^2c,\;b=s^2c$ and dividing by $c^2$:
$$\cos\gamma=\frac{t^2+s^2+1-t^2s^2}{(t^2+1)(s^2+1)}.$$

Using $ts=t+s$ (so $t^2s^2=(t+s)^2=t^2+2ts+s^2$):
$$\text{numerator}=t^2+s^2+1-(t^2+2ts+s^2)=1-2ts.$$

**Optimization.** Parametrize $(t-1)(s-1)=1$ by $t-1=r,\;s-1=1/r$, so $t=1+r,\;s
=(r+1)/r$ and $ts=(r+1)^2/r$. Then
$$\cos\gamma=\frac{1-2(r+1)^2/r}{\big((1+r)^2+1\big)\big((r+1)^2/r^2+1\big)}.$$

By symmetry $r\leftrightarrow 1/r$ (swapping $a\leftrightarrow b$ leaves $\gamma
$ unchanged), the unique interior critical point is at $r=1$ (i.e. $a=b=4c$). Ch
ecking:
- $r=1$: $\cos\gamma=-7/25\approx -0.28$.
- $r=2$: $\cos\gamma=-16/65\approx -0.246$ (less negative, smaller $\gamma$).
- $r\to\infty$: $\cos\gamma\to 0^-$ (so $\gamma\to 90°$).

Hence $r=1$ gives the **minimum** of $\cos\gamma$, i.e. the **maximum** of $\gam
ma$.

**Verification at $r=1$ ($a=b=4c$).** Tangency check: $\sqrt{ab}/(\sqrt a+\sqrt
b)=4c/(4\sqrt c)=\sqrt c$. ✓
Sides: $|CA|=|CB|=5c$, $|AB|=8c$.
$$\cos\gamma=\frac{25+25-64}{2\cdot 25}=\frac{-14}{50}=-\frac{7}{25}.\;\checkmar
k$$

**Extracting $k$.** With $\gamma_{\max}=2\arcsin(k)$:
$$\cos\gamma_{\max}=1-2\sin^2(\gamma_{\max}/2)=1-2k^2=-\frac{7}{25}.$$
$$2k^2=1+\frac{7}{25}=\frac{32}{25}\implies k^2=\frac{16}{25}\implies k=\frac{4}
{5}.$$

$$100k=100\cdot\frac{4}{5}=\boxed{80}.$$
