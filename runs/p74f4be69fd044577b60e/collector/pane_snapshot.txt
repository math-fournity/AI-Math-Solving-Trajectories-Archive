**Answer: Yes.** Every 3-regular (cubic) graph with no bridge has a perfect matc
hing. This is **Petersen's Theorem** (1891).

**Proof.** We use Tutte's theorem: a graph $G$ has a perfect matching if and onl
y if for every subset $S\subseteq V(G)$,
$$o(G-S)\le |S|,$$
where $o(H)$ denotes the number of odd components (components with an odd number
 of vertices) of $H$.

Let $G$ be a cubic graph with no bridge, and let $S\subseteq V(G)$ be arbitrary.
 Let the components of $G-S$ be $C_1,\dots,C_k$, and let $d_i$ be the number of
edges between $C_i$ and $S$.

**Step 1: parity of $d_i$.** Every vertex of $G$ has degree $3$, so summing degr
ees over vertices of $C_i$:
$$3|V(C_i)| = 2|E(C_i)| + d_i.$$
Hence $d_i \equiv |V(C_i)| \pmod 2$. In particular, if $C_i$ is an odd component
, then $d_i$ is odd.

**Step 2: lower bound on $d_i$ for odd components.** Suppose $C_i$ is an odd com
ponent.

- If $d_i=0$, then $C_i$ is a component of $G$ itself. Being cubic, $3|V(C_i)|=2
|E(C_i)|$ forces $|V(C_i)|$ to be even — contradicting that $C_i$ is odd. So $d_
i\neq 0$.
- If $d_i=1$, the unique edge joining $C_i$ to $S$ is a bridge of $G$ (its remov
al isolates $C_i$), contradicting the bridgeless hypothesis. So $d_i\neq 1$.

Since $d_i$ is odd and $d_i\ge 2$, we conclude $d_i\ge 3$ for every odd componen
t $C_i$.

**Step 3: counting.** Let $q=o(G-S)$ be the number of odd components. Each contr
ibutes at least $3$ edges to the cut $(S,\,V(G)\setminus S)$, so
$$3q \;\le\; \sum_{i=1}^{k} d_i \;=\; \bigl|\text{edges between }S\text{ and }G-
S\bigr|.$$
On the other hand, every vertex of $S$ has degree $3$, so the number of edges in
cident to $S$ is at most $3|S|$ (at most, since some edges may have both endpoin
ts in $S$). Therefore
$$3q \le 3|S| \quad\Longrightarrow\quad q\le |S|.$$

**Step 4: conclusion.** We have shown $o(G-S)\le |S|$ for every $S\subseteq V(G)
$. By Tutte's theorem, $G$ has a perfect matching. $\blacksquare$
