## Solution

We solve Laplace's equation on the unit square with the given boundary data. Sin
ce the boundary values on the vertical sides vanish, we expand in a sine series
in $x$.

**Separation of variables.** Seek a solution of the form
$$u(x,y)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n(y)\sin(n\pi x).$$
Substituting into $u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0$ gives $b_n''(y)-(n\pi)^2 b_n(y)=0$, whose ge
neral solution we write as
$$b_n(y)=A_n\sinh(n\pi y)+C_n\sinh\!\bigl(n\pi(1-y)\bigr).$$
This form is convenient because $\sinh(n\pi y)$ vanishes at $y=0$ and $\sinh(n\p
i(1-y))$ vanishes at $y=1$.

**Boundary data at $y=0$ and $y=1$.** We need the sine-series coefficients of th
e bottom and top boundary functions.

For $f(x)=1-x$ on $(0,1)$:
$$\int_0^1(1-x)\sin(n\pi x)\,dx=\int_0^1\sin(n\pi x)\,dx-\int_0^1 x\sin(n\pi x)\
,dx.$$
Using
$$\int_0^1\sin(n\pi x)\,dx=\frac{1-(-1)^n}{n\pi},\qquad \int_0^1 x\sin(n\pi x)\,
dx=-\frac{(-1)^n}{n\pi},$$
we obtain
$$\int_0^1(1-x)\sin(n\pi x)\,dx=\frac{1-(-1)^n}{n\pi}+\frac{(-1)^n}{n\pi}=\frac{
1}{n\pi}.$$
Hence $b_n(0)=\dfrac{2}{n\pi}$ for every $n\ge 1$.

For $g(x)=x$ on $(0,1)$:
$$b_n(1)=2\int_0^1 x\sin(n\pi x)\,dx=2\!\left(-\frac{(-1)^n}{n\pi}\right)=\frac{
2(-1)^{n+1}}{n\pi}.$$

**Determining the constants.** From $b_n(0)=C_n\sinh(n\pi)=\dfrac{2}{n\pi}$ we g
et
$$C_n=\frac{2}{n\pi\sinh(n\pi)}.$$
From $b_n(1)=A_n\sinh(n\pi)=\dfrac{2(-1)^{n+1}}{n\pi}$ we get
$$A_n=\frac{2(-1)^{n+1}}{n\pi\sinh(n\pi)}.$$

**The solution.** Therefore
$$\boxed{\,u(x,y)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{n\pi\sinh(n\pi)}\Bigl[(-1)^{n+1}\s
inh(n\pi y)+\sinh\!\bigl(n\pi(1-y)\bigr)\Bigr]\sin(n\pi x)\,}.$$

**Verification of the boundary conditions.**

- *Bottom edge* $y=0$: $\sinh(0)=0$ and $\sinh(n\pi)$ survives, so
$$u(x,0)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{n\pi}\sin(n\pi x)=1-x,$$
which is the standard sine expansion of $1-x$ on $(0,1)$ (coefficients $2/(n\pi)
$ computed above). ✓

- *Top edge* $y=1$: $\sinh(n\pi(1-y))\to 0$ and $\sinh(n\pi)$ survives, giving
$$u(x,1)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2(-1)^{n+1}}{n\pi}\sin(n\pi x)=x,$$
the sine expansion of $x$ on $(0,1)$. ✓

- *Left edge* $x=0$: every $\sin(n\pi\cdot 0)=0$, so $u(0,y)=0$. ✓

- *Right edge* $x=1$: every $\sin(n\pi)=0$, so $u(1,y)=0$. ✓

**Convergence and harmonicity.** For each $n$, the term $\sin(n\pi x)\sinh(n\pi
y)$ and $\sin(n\pi x)\sinh(n\pi(1-y))$ are harmonic functions. The coefficients
decay like $1/(n\sinh(n\pi))\sim e^{-n\pi}/n$, so the series and its termwise de
rivatives of every order converge uniformly on compact subsets of the open squar
e. Thus $u$ is smooth and harmonic in the interior, and the series converges con
tinuously up to each boundary edge to the prescribed boundary values. By the max
imum principle this is the unique bounded solution.
