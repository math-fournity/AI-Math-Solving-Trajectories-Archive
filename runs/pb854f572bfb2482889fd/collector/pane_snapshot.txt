## Proof

Let $G = \mathbb{Z}/p = \langle g \mid g^p = 1 \rangle$ and $R = \mathbb{F}_p[G]
$ where $\mathbb{F}_p = \mathbb{Z}/p$. The augmentation map $\varepsilon: R \to
\mathbb{F}_p$ is defined by $\varepsilon\!\left(\sum_{i=0}^{p-1} a_i g^i\right)
= \sum_{i=0}^{p-1} a_i$, and the augmentation ideal is $I = \ker \varepsilon$.

**Step 1: Identify the ring structure.** The map $\mathbb{F}_p[x] \to R$ sending
 $x \mapsto g$ is surjective with kernel $(x^p - 1)$. Since we are in characteri
stic $p$,

$$x^p - 1 = (x-1)^p$$

by the Frobenius endomorphism (freshman's dream). Setting $t = g - 1 = x - 1 \pm
od{x^p - 1}$, we obtain a ring isomorphism

$$R \cong \mathbb{F}_p[t]/(t^p).$$

As an $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector space, $R$ has basis $\{1, t, t^2, \dots, t^{p-1}\}$
, so $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} R = p$.

**Step 2: Identify $I$ and $I^n$.** The augmentation ideal $I = \ker\varepsilon$
 is generated by $\{g^i - 1 : 1 \le i \le p-1\}$, and since $g^i - 1 = (g-1)(1 +
 g + \cdots + g^{i-1})$, it is in fact the principal ideal $I = (g-1) = (t)$. Un
der the isomorphism above,

$$I = (t) \subset \mathbb{F}_p[t]/(t^p), \qquad I^n = (t^n).$$

**Step 3: Compute $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p}(I^n)$.** The ideal $(t^n)$ in $\mathbb{F}
_p[t]/(t^p)$ consists of all multiples of $t^n$, i.e., elements of the form $t^n
 \cdot f(t)$ with $f(t) \in \mathbb{F}_p[t]/(t^p)$. A basis is given by

$$\{t^n,\; t^{n+1},\; \dots,\; t^{p-1}\},$$

since $t^p = 0$ and these powers are linearly independent (being part of the sta
ndard basis of $R$). The number of basis elements is

$$p - 1 - n + 1 = p - n.$$

**Verification of edge cases.**
- $n=1$: $\dim I = p-1$, matching $\dim \ker\varepsilon = \dim R - \dim \mathbb{
F}_p = p - 1$. ✓
- $n = p-1$: $\dim I^{p-1} = 1$, the ideal $(t^{p-1})$ being one-dimensional. ✓
- $n = p$ (formally): $\dim I^p = 0$, since $(t^p) = 0$. ✓

**Conclusion.** For $1 \le n \le p-1$,

$$\boxed{\dim_{\mathbb{Z}/p}\, I^n = p - n.}$$
