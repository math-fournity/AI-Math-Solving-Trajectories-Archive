## Answer

**No.** A continuous bijection between two complete metric spaces need not be a
homeomorphism. Here is a counterexample.

### Counterexample

Let
- $X = \mathbb{R}$ equipped with the **discrete metric** $d_X(x,y) = \begin{case
s}0 & x=y \\ 1 & x\neq y\end{cases}$,
- $Y = \mathbb{R}$ equipped with the **usual metric** $d_Y(x,y) = |x-y|$,

and let $f: X \to Y$ be the identity map $f(x) = x$.

### Both spaces are complete

- **$Y$ is complete:** This is the standard fact that $\mathbb{R}$ with the usua
l metric is complete.
- **$X$ is complete:** Let $(x_n)$ be a Cauchy sequence in $X$. Taking $\varepsi
lon = \tfrac{1}{2}$, there exists $N$ such that for all $m, n \geq N$, $d_X(x_m,
 x_n) < \tfrac{1}{2}$, which forces $d_X(x_m, x_n) = 0$, i.e. $x_m = x_n$. Thus
$(x_n)$ is eventually constant, hence convergent. So $X$ is complete.

### $f$ is a continuous bijection

- **Bijective:** The identity map is clearly a bijection.
- **Continuous:** The topology on $X$ induced by the discrete metric is the disc
rete topology, in which *every* subset is open. Therefore, for any open set $U \
subseteq Y$, the preimage $f^{-1}(U) = U$ is open in $X$. Hence $f$ is continuou
s.

### $f$ is not a homeomorphism

A bijection is a homeomorphism if and only if its inverse is continuous. Conside
r $f^{-1}: Y \to X$, also the identity map. The singleton $\{0\}$ is open in $X$
 (since $X$ has the discrete topology). However, its preimage under $f^{-1}$ is
$\{0\}$, which is **not** open in $Y$ (with the usual topology, since no open in
terval around $0$ is contained in $\{0\}$). Therefore $f^{-1}$ is not continuous
, and $f$ is **not** a homeomorphism.

### Conclusion

The implication fails: completeness of the metric spaces does not substitute for
 compactness. (Recall that a continuous bijection from a *compact* space onto a
*Hausdorff* space is always a homeomorphism; the counterexample above exploits t
he failure of compactness — $X$ with the discrete metric is not compact, being a
n infinite discrete space.)
