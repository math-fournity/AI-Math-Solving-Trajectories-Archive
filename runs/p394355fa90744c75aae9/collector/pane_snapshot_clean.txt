## Solution

**Setup.** Expand $(1-\tan x)(1+\tan x) = 1 - \tan^2 x$ and use $\sec^2 x = 1 +
\tan^2 x$. Setting $t = \tan^2 x \geq 0$, the equation becomes:

$$f(t) = (1-t)(1+t) + 2^t = 1 - t^2 + 2^t = 0, \quad t \geq 0.$$

**Finding roots of $f(t) = 2^t + 1 - t^2$.**

Evaluate at integer points:
- $f(0) = 1 + 1 - 0 = 2 > 0$
- $f(1) = 2 + 1 - 1 = 2 > 0$
- $f(2) = 4 + 1 - 4 = 1 > 0$
- $f(3) = 8 + 1 - 9 = 0$ — **root at $t = 3$**
- $f(4) = 16 + 1 - 16 = 1 > 0$

**Analyzing the shape of $f$.** Compute $f'(t) = 2^t \ln 2 - 2t$:
- $f'(0) = \ln 2 \approx 0.693 > 0$
- $f'(1) = 2\ln 2 - 2 \approx -0.614 < 0$
- $f'(3) = 8\ln 2 - 6 \approx -0.455 < 0$
- $f'(4) = 16\ln 2 - 8 \approx 3.09 > 0$

So $f'$ has exactly two zeros: one in $(0,1)$ (giving a local maximum of $f$) an
d one in $(3,4)$ (giving a local minimum of $f$). Since $f''(t) = 2^t(\ln 2)^2 -
 2$ changes sign exactly once, $f'$ has at most two zeros — confirming these are
 the only critical points.

**Behavior:** $f(0) = 2 > 0$, $f$ rises to a local max (still positive), then de
creases strictly through $f(3) = 0$ (since $f'(3) < 0$, $f$ crosses from positiv
e to negative), reaches a **negative** local minimum in $(3, 3.5)$, then increas
es to $+\infty$ (as $2^t$ dominates $t^2$).

Verification: $f(3.2) = 2^{3.2} + 1 - 10.24 \approx 9.19 + 1 - 10.24 = -0.05 < 0
$, and $f(3.5) = 2^{3.5} + 1 - 12.25 \approx 11.31 + 1 - 12.25 = 0.06 > 0$.

So $f$ crosses zero exactly **twice** for $t \geq 0$: once at $t = 3$ (descendin
g) and once at some $t_0 \in (3.2, 3.5)$ (ascending). On $[0, 3)$, $f$ is strict
ly positive (it only decreases from its local max and $f(2) = 1 > 0$, $f(3) = 0$
), so no earlier roots exist.

**Counting values of $x$.** For each root $t > 0$ of $f(t) = 0$, the equation $\
tan^2 x = t$ gives $\tan x = \sqrt{t}$ or $\tan x = -\sqrt{t}$. Since $\tan: (-\
pi/2, \pi/2) \to \mathbb{R}$ is a bijection, each value of $\tan x$ yields exact
ly one $x$ in the interval.

- $t = 3$: $\tan x = \sqrt{3} \Rightarrow x = \pi/3$; $\tan x = -\sqrt{3} \Right
arrow x = -\pi/3$. (2 values)
- $t = t_0$: $\tan x = \sqrt{t_0}$ and $\tan x = -\sqrt{t_0}$, each giving one $
x$. (2 values)

**Total:** $2 + 2 = \boxed{4}$ values of $x$.
