## Answer

**Yes, it is possible for $F$ to fail to be a local contraction near $x_0$ even
when the spectral radius of $JF(x_0)$ is strictly less than $1$.**

### Key observation

A map $F$ is a *local contraction* near $x_0$ (with respect to a chosen norm $\|
\cdot\|$) if there exist a neighborhood $U$ of $x_0$ and a constant $L<1$ such t
hat
$$\|F(x)-F(y)\|\le L\|x-y\|,\qquad \forall x,y\in U.$$
When $F$ is $C^1$, the relevant quantity controlling the local Lipschitz constan
t is the **operator norm** $\|JF(x)\|$ (the induced matrix norm), not the **spec
tral radius** $\rho(JF(x))$. In general,
$$\rho(A)\le \|A\|$$
for any induced norm, and the inequality can be *strict* — even arbitrarily so.
The spectral radius equals the operator norm only for special classes of matrice
s (e.g. normal matrices under the Euclidean norm). So a small spectral radius do
es **not** force a small operator norm, and hence does not force a contraction.

### Explicit counterexample

Work in $\mathbb{R}^2$ with the Euclidean norm, and take $x_0=(0,0)$. Define
$$F(x,y)=(2y,\,0).$$
The Jacobian is constant:
$$JF(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\[2pt]0&0\end{pmatrix}.$$
This matrix is nilpotent, so both eigenvalues are $0$:
$$\rho\!\bigl(JF(0,0)\bigr)=0<1.$$

Yet $F$ is **not** a local contraction near the origin. Indeed, for any $\vareps
ilon>0$ take $p=(0,\varepsilon)$ and $q=(0,0)$. Then
$$\|F(p)-F(q)\|=\|(2\varepsilon,0)\|=2\varepsilon,\qquad \|p-q\|=\varepsilon,$$
so
$$\frac{\|F(p)-F(q)\|}{\|p-q\|}=2>1.$$
Since such pairs $(p,q)$ exist in *every* neighborhood of the origin, no neighbo
rhood $U$ admits a Lipschitz constant $L<1$. Hence $F$ fails to be a local contr
action near $x_0$, despite $\rho(JF(x_0))=0<1$.

### Conceptual summary

The spectral radius governs the **asymptotic** behavior of iterates $A^k$ (via $
\rho(A)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|A^k\|^{1/k}$), which is why it controls local converg
ence of fixed-point iteration (the Ostrowski theorem). But a *contraction* is a
**one-step** condition, governed by the operator norm $\|A\|$, which can be made
 arbitrarily large while keeping $\rho(A)=0$ — as the nilpotent Jordan block abo
ve shows. The two notions coincide only when the Jacobian is normal (or more gen
erally when $\|A\|=\rho(A)$ for the chosen norm), and the problem statement impo
ses no such symmetry hypothesis.

Therefore the answer is **yes**: a small spectral radius of the Jacobian at $x_0
$ does not by itself guarantee that $F$ is a local contraction near $x_0$.
