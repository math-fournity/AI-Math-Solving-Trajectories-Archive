# Proof

## Setup and notation

Let $E$ be the elliptic curve (smooth projective curve of genus 1 over $\mathbb{
k}$ with distinguished point $O$), equipped with the Weierstrass coordinate func
tions $x, y \in \mathbb{k}(E)$. Let $C \subset \mathbb{P}^2$ be the projective c
losure of the affine zero locus of
$$f(X,Y) = (Y^2 + a_1 XY + a_3 Y) - (X^3 + a_2 X^2 + a_4 X + a_6).$$

The homogenization is
$$F(X,Y,Z) = Y^2 Z + a_1 XYZ + a_3 YZ^2 - X^3 - a_2 X^2 Z - a_4 XZ^2 - a_6 Z^3.$
$

The unique point of $C$ with $Z = 0$ is $[0:1:0]$, which we identify with $O$.

---

## Part 1: $\varphi : E \setminus \{O\} \to C$ is a morphism

On the open set $E \setminus \{O\}$, the functions $x$ and $y$ are regular (they
 are elements of $\mathbb{k}(E)$ whose only possible poles are at $O$). The map
$$\varphi(P) = [x(P) : y(P) : 1]$$
is given by the triple of regular functions $(x, y, 1)$ on $E \setminus \{O\}$.
Since these do not simultaneously vanish — the third coordinate is the constant
$1 \neq 0$ — this defines a morphism $E \setminus \{O\} \to \mathbb{P}^2$ by the
 standard criterion for maps to projective space given by regular functions.

The image lies in $C$: for every $P \in E \setminus \{O\}$, the pair $(x(P), y(P
))$ satisfies the Weierstrass equation $f(x(P), y(P)) = 0$, so $[x(P):y(P):1]$ s
atisfies $F = 0$. Hence $\varphi : E \setminus \{O\} \to C$ is a morphism. $\squ
are$

---

## Part 2: $\varphi$ extends to a morphism $\Phi : E \to C$

It remains to extend $\varphi$ across $O$. We work in the affine chart $Y \neq 0
$ of $\mathbb{P}^2$, setting $Y = 1$ with local coordinates $u = X/Y$ and $w = Z
/Y$. In this chart the curve $C$ is given by
$$g(u, w) = w + a_1 u w + a_3 w^2 - u^3 - a_2 u^2 w - a_4 u w^2 - a_6 w^3 = 0.$$

The point $O = [0:1:0]$ corresponds to $(u, w) = (0, 0)$.

**Local structure at $O$.** Since $\frac{\partial g}{\partial w}\big|_{(0,0)} =
1 \neq 0$, the implicit function theorem gives $w$ as an analytic function of $u
$ near the origin. Solving order by order:
$$w = u^3 + \text{(higher order terms in } u\text{)}.$$
Thus $u$ is a uniformizer (local parameter of order $1$) at $O$, and $w$ has ord
er $3$ at $O$.

**Pole orders of $x$ and $y$ at $O$.** On $E \setminus \{O\} \cap \{y \neq 0\}$
(i.e., where $Y \neq 0$):
$$x = \frac{X}{Z} = \frac{u}{w}, \qquad y = \frac{Y}{Z} = \frac{1}{w}.$$
Therefore:
$$\operatorname{ord}_O(x) = \operatorname{ord}_O(u) - \operatorname{ord}_O(w) =
1 - 3 = -2, \qquad \operatorname{ord}_O(y) = 0 - 3 = -3.$$
So $x$ has a pole of order $2$ and $y$ has a pole of order $3$ at $O$.

**The extension.** On $E \setminus \{O\} \cap \{y \neq 0\}$, rewrite $\varphi$ i
n the $Y \neq 0$ chart:
$$\varphi(P) = [x(P) : y(P) : 1] = [x(P)/y(P) : 1 : 1/y(P)].$$
The map is thus given by the pair of functions
$$\left(\frac{x}{y},\; \frac{1}{y}\right) = (u,\; w).$$
Their orders at $O$ are:
$$\operatorname{ord}_O(x/y) = -2 - (-3) = 1, \qquad \operatorname{ord}_O(1/y) =
0 - (-3) = 3.$$
Both are non-negative, so $x/y$ and $1/y$ are **regular at $O$** (and vanish the
re). Since they are also regular on $E \setminus \{O\} \cap \{y \neq 0\}$, the p
air $(x/y,\; 1/y)$ defines a morphism from the open neighborhood $\{y \neq 0\} \
cup \{O\}$ of $O$ into the chart $Y \neq 0$ of $C$.

At $O$ itself, both $x/y$ and $1/y$ vanish, so the extended map sends
$$\Phi(O) = [0 : 1 : 0],$$
which is the point at infinity on $C$.

This morphism agrees with $\varphi$ on the overlap $(E \setminus \{O\}) \cap \{y
 \neq 0\}$. By the gluing lemma for morphisms, $\varphi$ and this local extensio
n paste together to give a morphism
$$\Phi : E \longrightarrow C$$
extending $\varphi$. $\square$

---

**Summary.** On $E \setminus \{O\}$, $\varphi = [x:y:1]$ is a morphism because i
t is given by regular functions with non-vanishing third coordinate. At $O$, rew
riting in the chart $Y \neq 0$ as $[x/y : 1 : 1/y] = [u : 1 : w]$ reveals that $
x/y$ (order $1$) and $1/y$ (order $3$) are both regular at $O$, so the map exten
ds smoothly, sending $O \mapsto [0:1:0]$.
