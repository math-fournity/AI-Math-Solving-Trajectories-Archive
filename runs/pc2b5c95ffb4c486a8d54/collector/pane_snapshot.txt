## Proof

Let $X$ be a CW-complex with $q$-skeleton $X^q$ and $(q-1)$-skeleton $X^{q-1}$.
Let $\{e_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in A}$ be the collection of $q$-cells of $X$, each eq
uipped with a characteristic map
$$\chi^{e_\alpha} : (D^q, S^{q-1}) \longrightarrow (X^q, X^{q-1}).$$
Define $Y = X^q / X^{q-1}$ with basepoint $y_0 = X^{q-1}/X^{q-1}$, and let $p :
(X^q, X^{q-1}) \to (Y, y_0)$ be the quotient projection. We must show that the f
amily $\{p_*\,\chi_*^{e_\alpha}[D^q]\}_{\alpha \in A}$ is a basis of the free ab
elian group $H_q(Y, y_0)$.

### Step 1. $Y$ is a wedge of $q$-spheres, one per $q$-cell.

The $q$-skeleton is built from $X^{q-1}$ by attaching the $q$-cells $\{e_\alpha\
}$ via their attaching maps $\varphi_\alpha : S^{q-1} \to X^{q-1}$. Each charact
eristic map $\chi^{e_\alpha}$ sends $\operatorname{int}(D^q)$ homeomorphically o
nto $e_\alpha$ and sends $S^{q-1}$ into $X^{q-1}$. Upon collapsing all of $X^{q-
1}$ to the single point $y_0$, the boundary $S^{q-1}$ of each cell is identified
 to $y_0$, so each $q$-cell becomes a copy of $D^q/S^{q-1} \cong S^q$. Distinct
cells meet only at $y_0$ (their interiors are disjoint in $X^q$ and remain disjo
int in the quotient). Hence
$$Y \;=\; X^q/X^{q-1} \;\cong\; \bigvee_{\alpha \in A} S^q_\alpha,$$
a wedge (one-point union) of $|A|$ copies of $S^q$.

### Step 2. $H_q(Y, y_0) \cong \bigoplus_{\alpha \in A} \mathbb{Z}$.

For $q \geq 1$, the long exact sequence of the pair $(Y, y_0)$ gives
$$\cdots \to H_q(y_0) \to H_q(Y) \to H_q(Y, y_0) \to H_{q-1}(y_0) \to \cdots$$
Since $H_q(y_0) = 0$ and $H_{q-1}(y_0) = 0$ for $q \geq 2$ (and for $q = 1$ the
map $H_0(y_0) \to H_0(Y)$ is injective, so the connecting map is zero), we obtai
n
$$H_q(Y, y_0) \;\cong\; \widetilde{H}_q(Y).$$
For a wedge of spaces, the reduced homology splits as a direct sum:
$$\widetilde{H}_q\!\left(\bigvee_{\alpha} S^q_\alpha\right) \;\cong\; \bigoplus_
{\alpha} \widetilde{H}_q(S^q_\alpha) \;\cong\; \bigoplus_{\alpha \in A} \mathbb{
Z}.$$
Thus $H_q(Y, y_0)$ is free abelian of rank $|A|$, with one generator per $q$-cel
l.

### Step 3. Each $p_*\,\chi_*^{e_\alpha}[D^q]$ is the generator of the $\alpha$-
th summand.

Here $[D^q]$ denotes the relative fundamental class $[D^q, S^{q-1}] \in H_q(D^q,
 S^{q-1}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. Under the identification $Y \cong \bigvee_\beta S^q
_\beta$, the composition $p \circ \chi^{e_\alpha}$ factors as
$$(D^q, S^{q-1}) \xrightarrow{\;\pi_\alpha\;} (S^q_\alpha, y_0) \xrightarrow{\;i
_\alpha\;} \left(\bigvee_{\beta} S^q_\beta,\, y_0\right),$$
where:

- $\pi_\alpha : D^q \to D^q/S^{q-1} \cong S^q_\alpha$ is the quotient map collap
sing the boundary to the basepoint. This is the standard identification, and $(\
pi_\alpha)_*[D^q, S^{q-1}]$ is the fundamental class $[S^q_\alpha] \in H_q(S^q_\
alpha, y_0) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, i.e., a generator.

- $i_\alpha : S^q_\alpha \hookrightarrow \bigvee_\beta S^q_\beta$ is the inclusi
on of the $\alpha$-th wedge summand. On $H_q$, the inclusion of a wedge summand
induces the canonical injection $\mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow \bigoplus_\beta \mat
hbb{Z}$ into the $\alpha$-th direct summand (sending $1 \mapsto e_\alpha$, the s
tandard basis vector).

Therefore
$$(p \circ \chi^{e_\alpha})_*[D^q] \;=\; (i_\alpha)_*\,(\pi_\alpha)_*[D^q] \;=\;
 (i_\alpha)_*[S^q_\alpha],$$
which is precisely the standard generator of the $\alpha$-th copy of $\mathbb{Z}
$ in $\bigoplus_\beta \mathbb{Z} \cong H_q(Y, y_0)$.

### Step 4. The family forms a basis.

As $\alpha$ ranges over all $q$-cells, the elements $p_*\,\chi_*^{e_\alpha}[D^q]
$ are exactly the standard basis vectors of the direct sum $\bigoplus_{\alpha \i
n A} \mathbb{Z}$, one generator per summand. A direct sum of copies of $\mathbb{
Z}$ is by definition free abelian on these generators. Hence $\{p_*\,\chi_*^{e_\
alpha}[D^q]\}_{\alpha \in A}$ is a basis for the free abelian group $H_q(Y, y_0)
$. $\blacksquare$
