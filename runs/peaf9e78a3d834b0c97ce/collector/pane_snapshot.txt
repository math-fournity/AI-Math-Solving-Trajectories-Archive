## Proof

**Setup.** We have two independent sequences $\{X_i\}$ and $\{Y_i\}$ with $X_i \
sim \text{Bernoulli}(\lambda/n)$ and $Y_i \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda/n)$, and
$$T_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n^2} X_i, \qquad S_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n^2} Y_i.$$

**Distribution of the sums.** Since the $X_i$ are i.i.d. Bernoulli,
$$T_n \sim \text{Binomial}\!\left(n^2,\, \tfrac{\lambda}{n}\right), \qquad \math
bb{E}[T_n] = n^2\cdot\tfrac{\lambda}{n}=n\lambda, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(T_n)
=n^2\cdot\tfrac{\lambda}{n}\!\left(1-\tfrac{\lambda}{n}\right)=n\lambda\!\left(1
-\tfrac{\lambda}{n}\right).$$
Since the $Y_i$ are i.i.d. Poisson and Poisson variables are stable under summat
ion,
$$S_n \sim \text{Poisson}(n\lambda), \qquad \mathbb{E}[S_n]=n\lambda, \qquad \op
eratorname{Var}(S_n)=n\lambda.$$

**Convergence in probability of the normalized sums.** Consider
$$\frac{T_n}{n\lambda}: \qquad \operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac{T_n}{n\lambda}\ri
ght)=\frac{n\lambda(1-\lambda/n)}{(n\lambda)^2}=\frac{1-\lambda/n}{n\lambda}\xri
ghtarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.$$
By Chebyshev's inequality (or directly, since the variance of the normalized sum
 vanishes and its mean is $1$),
$$\frac{T_n}{n\lambda}\xrightarrow{P}1.$$
Likewise,
$$\frac{S_n}{n\lambda}: \qquad \operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac{S_n}{n\lambda}\ri
ght)=\frac{n\lambda}{(n\lambda)^2}=\frac{1}{n\lambda}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0
,$$
so
$$\frac{S_n}{n\lambda}\xrightarrow{P}1.$$

**Ratio.** By the continuous mapping theorem (the function $g(u,v)=u/v$ is conti
nuous on a neighborhood of $(1,1)$, and $S_n>0$ with probability tending to $1$
since $\mathbb{P}(S_n=0)=e^{-n\lambda}\to 0$),
$$\frac{T_n}{S_n}=\frac{T_n/(n\lambda)}{S_n/(n\lambda)}\xrightarrow{P}\frac{1}{1
}=1.$$

Convergence in probability to a constant implies convergence in distribution to
that constant. Therefore the limiting distribution of $\dfrac{T_n}{S_n}$ is **de
generate (a point mass) at $1$**.
