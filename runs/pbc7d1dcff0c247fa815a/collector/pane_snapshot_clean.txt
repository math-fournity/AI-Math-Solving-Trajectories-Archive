## Theorem (`upper_bound`)

For every fixed `k ≥ 2`, `g_k(n) ≪_k log n`. Concretely,

$$g_k(n) \;\le\; \frac{k}{\ln 2}\,\ln n + k \qquad (n \ge 1).$$

### Proof

Recall
$$g_k(n)=\max\Bigl\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_i-n\;:\;a_1,\dots,a_k\in\mathbb N,\;\prod_{i
=1}^{k}a_i!\;\big|\;n!\Bigr\}.$$

Fix a feasible tuple $(a_1,\dots,a_k)$ with $\prod a_i!\mid n!$ and write $E:=\s
um_i a_i-n$.

**Step 1 — Each $a_i\le n$.**
Since $\prod a_i!\mid n!$ and all quantities are positive, $\prod a_i!\le n!$. I
f some $a_j>n$ then $a_j!=(n+1)(n+2)\cdots a_j\cdot n!\ge (n+1)\,n!>n!$, contrad
icting $\prod a_i!\le n!$. Hence $a_i\le n$ for all $i$.

**Step 2 — Legendre's formula.**
For a prime $p$ let $v_p(m!)$ be the $p$-adic valuation and $s_p(m)$ the digit s
um of $m$ in base $p$. Legendre's formula gives
$$v_p(m!)=\frac{m-s_p(m)}{p-1}.$$

The divisibility $\prod a_i!\mid n!$ is equivalent to $v_p\!\bigl(\prod a_i!\big
r)\le v_p(n!)$ for every prime $p$, i.e.
$$\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{a_i-s_p(a_i)}{p-1}\;\le\;\frac{n-s_p(n)}{p-1}.$$

Multiplying by $p-1>0$ and rearranging,
$$\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_i-n\;\le\;\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_p(a_i)-s_p(n).\tag{$\star$}$$

**Step 3 — Specialise to $p=2$ and bound.**
For $p=2$ each binary digit is $0$ or $1$, so $s_2(m)\le\lfloor\log_2 m\rfloor+1
$ (the number of binary digits). By Step 1, $a_i\le n$, hence
$$s_2(a_i)\le\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor+1\le\log_2 n+1.$$
Also $s_2(n)\ge 1$ for $n\ge1$. Applying $(\star)$ with $p=2$:
$$E\;\le\;\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_2(a_i)-s_2(n)\;\le\;k(\log_2 n+1)-1\;\le\;k\log_2 n+k\
;=\;\frac{k}{\ln 2}\ln n+k.$$

This bound holds for every feasible tuple, so taking the supremum,
$$g_k(n)\le\frac{k}{\ln 2}\ln n+k.$$

Since $k$ is fixed, this is $g_k(n)\ll_k\log n$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

## Remarks on the open parts

- **Part i** asks whether $\sum_{n\le x}g_k(n)\sim c_k\,x\log x$. The upper boun
d above only gives $\sum_{n\le x}g_k(n)\ll_k x\log x$; the existence of an asymp
totic with a definite constant $c_k$ is the open content.
- **Part ii** asks for an *almost-all* pointwise asymptotic $g_k(n)=c_k\log x+o(
\log x)$ for $n<x$, a stronger average-case statement also left open by Erdős–Gr
aham.
- **`g_pos`** is the (already supplied) witness $a=(n,1,0,\dots,0)$, giving $\pr
od a_i!=n!\mid n!$ and $\sum a_i-n=1>0$.

The provable mathematical content — the Erdős–Graham upper bound — is establishe
d above.
