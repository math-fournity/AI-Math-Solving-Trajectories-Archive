## Proof

We compute $S = \sum_{k=1}^{p-1} \left\{\dfrac{k^2}{p}\right\}$.

**Step 1: Reduce to residues.** Write $k^2 = p\left\lfloor \dfrac{k^2}{p}\right\
rfloor + r_k$, where $r_k$ is the least nonnegative residue of $k^2 \pmod p$. Si
nce $1 \le k \le p-1$ and $p$ is prime, $p \nmid k^2$, so $1 \le r_k \le p-1$. T
hen

$$\left\{\frac{k^2}{p}\right\} = \frac{k^2}{p} - \left\lfloor\frac{k^2}{p}\right
\rfloor = \frac{k^2}{p} - \frac{k^2 - r_k}{p} = \frac{r_k}{p}.$$

Summing,

$$S = \sum_{k=1}^{p-1} \frac{r_k}{p} = \frac{1}{p}\sum_{k=1}^{p-1} r_k.$$

**Step 2: Evaluate $\sum_{k=1}^{p-1} r_k$.** As $k$ ranges over $1, 2, \dots, p-
1$, the value $r_k = k^2 \bmod p$ runs over the nonzero quadratic residues modul
o $p$, each appearing exactly twice (since $k^2 \equiv (p-k)^2 \pmod p$, and the
se are the only collisions). There are $(p-1)/2$ distinct quadratic residues, so

$$\sum_{k=1}^{p-1} r_k = 2\sum_{\substack{a \in \{1,\dots,p-1\}\\ a \text{ QR}}}
 a.$$

**Step 3: Sum of quadratic residues.** Let $g$ be a primitive root mod $p$. The
quadratic residues are $g^2, g^4, \dots, g^{p-1}$, i.e. $\{g^{2j} : 1 \le j \le
(p-1)/2\}$. Their sum is

$$\sum_{j=1}^{(p-1)/2} g^{2j} = g^2\cdot\frac{g^{p-1}-1}{g^2-1}.$$

Since $g^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod p$, this is $\equiv 0 \pmod p$ (the denominator $g
^2 - 1 \not\equiv 0 \pmod p$ since $g^2 \not\equiv 1$, as $g$ has order $p-1 > 2
$). Thus the sum of quadratic residues is divisible by $p$. Being a sum of $(p-1
)/2$ distinct integers from $\{1,\dots,p-1\}$, it lies between $1$ and $\dfrac{(
p-1)p}{2}$, so the only multiple of $p$ in range that fits is $\dfrac{p(p-1)}{4}
$ (using that the total sum $1+2+\cdots+(p-1) = \frac{p(p-1)}{2}$ splits equally
 between residues and non-residues — for $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, $-1$ is a quadrat
ic residue, so negation permutes the residue set, confirming the residue sum equ
als the non-residue sum). Hence

$$\sum_{\text{QR } a} a = \frac{p(p-1)}{4}.$$

**Step 4: Conclude.**

$$\sum_{k=1}^{p-1} r_k = 2 \cdot \frac{p(p-1)}{4} = \frac{p(p-1)}{2},$$

$$S = \frac{1}{p}\cdot\frac{p(p-1)}{2} = \frac{p-1}{2}.$$

**Verification ($p=5$):** $\{1/5\}+\{4/5\}+\{9/5\}+\{16/5\} = \tfrac15+\tfrac45+
\tfrac45+\tfrac15 = 2 = \tfrac{5-1}{2}.$ ✓

The total sum of the residual overheads is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{p-1}{2}}.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
