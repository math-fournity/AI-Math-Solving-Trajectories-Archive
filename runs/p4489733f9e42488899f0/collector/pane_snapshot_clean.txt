# Puiseux Expansion of an Algebraic Function Near a Singularity

## Setup

Let $P(z, w) \in \mathbb{C}[z, w]$ be a polynomial in two variables, and let $f(
z)$ be a branch of the algebraic function defined by $P(z, f(z)) = 0$. Let $\zet
a \in \mathbb{C}$ be a singularity of $f$ (a point where $f$ fails to be holomor
phic — either a branch point or a pole-type singularity of some branch).

## The Newton–Puiseux Theorem

The classical **Newton–Puiseux theorem** states that every branch of an algebrai
c function admits, near any point $\zeta \in \mathbb{C}$ (including $\zeta = \in
fty$ via the change $z \mapsto 1/z$), a convergent expansion in fractional power
s of $(z - \zeta)$. Concretely, for a branch $f(z)$ near $\zeta$, there exist:

- an integer $q \geq 1$, called the **ramification index** (or **branching order
**),
- an integer $N_0 \in \mathbb{Z}$ (the lowest exponent, possibly negative, givin
g the leading/polar term),
- a sequence of complex coefficients $\{a_n\}_{n \geq N_0}$ with $a_{N_0} \neq 0
$,

such that

$$
\boxed{\; f(z) \;=\; \sum_{n=N_0}^{\infty} a_n\,(z-\zeta)^{\,n/q} \;}
$$

converges in a punctured disk $0 < |z - \zeta| < R$ for some $R > 0$, on a suita
ble local branch (after choosing a branch of $(z-\zeta)^{1/q}$).

Equivalently, setting $t = (z - \zeta)^{1/q}$ (a local uniformizing parameter),
the branch becomes a **meromorphic** (in fact Laurent) function of $t$:

$$
f(\zeta + t^q) \;=\; \sum_{n=N_0}^{\infty} a_n\, t^n,
$$

which is a convergent Laurent series in $t$. This is the precise sense in which
the singularity is "resolved" by passing to the $q$-fold ramified cover $t \maps
to \zeta + t^q$.

## Key Parameters

| Parameter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Singularity $\zeta$** | The point in $\mathbb{C}$ (or $\infty$) near which t
he expansion is taken. |
| **Ramification index $q \geq 1$** | The denominator of the fractional powers;
the order of the cyclic covering $t \mapsto \zeta + t^q$ needed to uniformize th
e branch. When $q = 1$ the point is **not** a branch point (the singularity is a
t worst a pole or removable). When $q > 1$, $\zeta$ is a **branch point** of ord
er $q-1$. |
| **Leading exponent $N_0/q$** | The smallest power appearing. If $N_0 < 0$, the
 branch has a **pole** of order $-N_0/q$ at $\zeta$ (on the uniformized cover).
If $N_0 \geq 0$, the branch is bounded (algebraic, hence removable after uniform
ization). |
| **Coefficients $\{a_n\}$** | The complex coefficients of the expansion, with $
a_{N_0} \neq 0$. They are determined by $P$ via the Newton polygon construction.
 |
| **Radius of convergence $R$** | Determined by the distance from $\zeta$ to the
 nearest other singularity of the algebraic function. |

## How $q$ and the Exponents Are Determined: the Newton Polygon

The values of $q$, $N_0$, and the successive exponents are read off from the **N
ewton polygon** of $P$ at $\zeta$. Writing

$$
P(z, w) = \sum_{(i,j)} c_{ij}\,(z-\zeta)^i\, w^j,
$$

one plots the points $(i, j)$ with $c_{ij} \neq 0$ in the $(i,j)$-plane, takes t
he lower-left convex hull (the Newton polygon), and each **edge** of slope $-p/q
'$ (in lowest terms) of this polygon corresponds to a family of branches whose l
eading exponent is $-p/q'$. The ramification index $q$ of a given branch is the
least common multiple of the denominators $q'$ of the successive edges traversed
 in the Newton–Puiseux algorithm. The coefficients $a_n$ are then determined rec
ursively by substituting the series into $P(z, f(z)) = 0$ and matching powers.

## Summary of the Form

A branch $f(z)$ of an algebraic function $P(z, f) = 0$ near a singularity $\zeta
$ has the form of a **convergent Puiseux series**

$$
f(z) = \sum_{n=N_0}^{\infty} a_n\,(z-\zeta)^{n/q},
$$

characterized by:

1. the **singularity** $\zeta$,
2. the **ramification index** $q \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}$ (denominator of the fr
actional powers; $q=1$ means no branching),
3. the **leading exponent** $N_0/q \in \mathbb{Q}$ (with $N_0 \in \mathbb{Z}$; n
egative $\Rightarrow$ pole, non-negative $\Rightarrow$ bounded/algebraic value),
4. the **coefficient sequence** $\{a_n\}_{n \geq N_0} \subset \mathbb{C}$ with $
a_{N_0} \neq 0$,
5. the **radius of convergence** $R > 0$ (distance to the next singularity),

with $q$ and the exponents determined by the **Newton polygon** of $P$ at $\zeta
$, and the series converging on the punctured disk $0 < |z-\zeta| < R$ after a c
hoice of branch of $(z-\zeta)^{1/q}$.
