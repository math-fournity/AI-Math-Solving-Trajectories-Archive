## Proof

**Notation.** Let $D$ be the closed disc of radius $1$ with center $O$. We call
a pair of points a *long pair* if their distance is $>1$, and a *short pair* oth
erwise. We seek the maximum number of long pairs.

---

### Step 1: Key Geometric Lemma

**Lemma.** If $P, Q \in D$ and $\angle POQ \le 60°$, then $PQ \le 1$.

*Proof.* Let $a = OP,\ b = OQ,\ \theta = \angle POQ \le 60°$. By the law of cosi
nes:
$$PQ^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos\theta \le a^2 + b^2 - ab,$$
since $\cos\theta \ge \tfrac{1}{2}$. The function $f(a,b) = a^2 + b^2 - ab$ has
Hessian $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2\end{pmatrix}$, which is positive defin
ite, so $f$ is convex on $[0,1]^2$. Its maximum is attained at a corner: $f(0,0)
=0$, $f(1,0)=f(0,1)=f(1,1)=1$. Thus $PQ^2 \le 1$, i.e., $PQ \le 1$. $\square$

**Corollary.** If $PQ > 1$, then $\angle POQ > 60°$.

---

### Step 2: The Long-Pair Graph is $K_6$-free

Define the **long-pair graph** $G$ on the $n$ points: vertices are the points, e
dges are the long pairs. We claim $G$ contains no $K_6$ (complete graph on $6$ v
ertices).

*Proof.* Suppose $6$ points $P_1, \ldots, P_6 \in D$ are pairwise at distance $>
 1$. By the Corollary, every pair satisfies $\angle P_i O P_j > 60°$. Consider t
he $6$ rays $OP_1, \ldots, OP_6$ (if some $P_i = O$, then $OP_i = 0 \le 1$ for a
ll $j$, contradicting $P_iP_j > 1$, so no point is the center). Sort the angles
of these rays around $O$; the $6$ consecutive angular gaps sum to $360°$, so som
e gap $\le 60°$. The two points bounding this gap have $\angle P_iOP_j \le 60°$,
 hence $P_iP_j \le 1$ by the Lemma — contradiction. $\square$

### Step 3: Upper Bound via Turán's Theorem

By **Turán's theorem**, a $K_6$-free graph on $n$ vertices has at most $e(T(n,5)
)$ edges, where $T(n,5)$ is the complete $5$-partite Turán graph with parts as e
qual as possible.

Write $n = 5q + r$ with $0 \le r \le 4$. The part sizes are $q+1$ ($r$ times) an
d $q$ ($5-r$ times), so:
$$e(T(n,5)) = \frac{n^2 - \bigl[r(q+1)^2 + (5-r)q^2\bigr]}{2} = 10q^2 + 4rq + \f
rac{r(r-1)}{2}.$$

One verifies that this equals $\left\lfloor \dfrac{2n^2}{5} \right\rfloor$, sinc
e $\frac{2n^2}{5} = e(T(n,5)) + \frac{r(5-r)}{10}$ and $0 \le \frac{r(5-r)}{10}
< 1$.

### Step 4: Construction Achieving the Bound

Place $5$ clusters of points on the unit circle at angles $0°, 72°, 144°, 216°,
288°$, with cluster sizes $q+1$ ($r$ times) and $q$ ($5-r$ times). Within each c
luster, place points within an arc of width $\varepsilon$ (for sufficiently smal
l $\varepsilon > 0$).

- **Intra-cluster pairs:** distance $\approx 0 < 1$ — all short. ✓
- **Inter-cluster pairs:** the minimum angular separation between clusters is $7
2° - 2\varepsilon$. For $\varepsilon < 6°$, this exceeds $60°$, giving distance
$> 2\sin(30°) = 1$ — all long. ✓

The long-pair graph is exactly $T(n,5)$, achieving $e(T(n,5)) = \left\lfloor \fr
ac{2n^2}{5} \right\rfloor$ long pairs.

### Conclusion

The maximum number of segments of length greater than $1$ determined by $n$ poin
ts in a closed disc of radius $1$ is:

$$\boxed{\left\lfloor \frac{2n^2}{5} \right\rfloor}$$
