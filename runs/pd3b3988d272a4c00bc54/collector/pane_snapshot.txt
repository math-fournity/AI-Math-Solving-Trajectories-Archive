## Proof

We find the $k$-th derivative of $\Gamma(x) = \int_0^\infty t^{x-1} e^{-t}\,dt$
for $x \in (0,\infty)$ by differentiating under the integral sign.

**Step 1: Formal differentiation.** Write $t^{x-1} = e^{(x-1)\ln t}$. Then
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\, t^{x-1} = e^{(x-1)\ln t}\ln t = t^{x-1}\ln t.$$
By induction, for every $k \geq 0$,
$$\frac{\partial^k}{\partial x^k}\, t^{x-1} = t^{x-1}(\ln t)^k. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2: Justification via dominated convergence.** Fix $x_0 \in (0,\infty)$ an
d choose $a,b$ with $0 < a < x_0 < b$. We show differentiation under the integra
l is valid on $[a,b]$, which suffices.

For $x \in [a,b]$, the $k$-th partial derivative of the integrand is $t^{x-1}(\l
n t)^k e^{-t}$. We dominate it by an integrable function independent of $x$:

- **For $t \geq 1$:** since $x-1 \leq b-1$, we have $t^{x-1} \leq t^{b-1}$, so
$$|t^{x-1}(\ln t)^k e^{-t}| \leq t^{b-1}(\ln t)^k e^{-t} \leq C\, t^{b} e^{-t/2}
,$$
where the last bound holds because $(\ln t)^k$ grows slower than any power of $t
$, so $t^{b-1}(\ln t)^k e^{-t} \leq C t^{b} e^{-t/2}$ for some constant $C$ (abs
orbing $(\ln t)^k e^{-t/2} \leq C'$). The function $t^b e^{-t/2}$ is integrable
on $[1,\infty)$.

- **For $0 < t < 1$:** since $x-1 \geq a-1 > -1$, we have $t^{x-1} \leq t^{a-1}$
, so
$$|t^{x-1}(\ln t)^k e^{-t}| \leq t^{a-1}|\ln t|^k e^{-t} \leq t^{a-1}|\ln t|^k.$
$
Near $t=0$, $|\ln t|^k$ grows slower than $t^{-\varepsilon}$ for any $\varepsilo
n > 0$. Choosing $\varepsilon < a$, the function $t^{a-1}|\ln t|^k \leq t^{a-1-\
varepsilon}$ for $t$ small enough, and $a-1-\varepsilon > -1$, so this is integr
able on $(0,1]$.

Combining both regions, there exists $g \in L^1(0,\infty)$ with $|t^{x-1}(\ln t)
^k e^{-t}| \leq g(t)$ for all $x \in [a,b]$. The same domination applies to all
lower-order derivatives $\frac{\partial^j}{\partial x^j}(t^{x-1}e^{-t}) = t^{x-1
}(\ln t)^j e^{-t}$, $j = 0, \dots, k$, since $|\ln t|^j \leq \max(1, |\ln t|^k)$
.

By the Leibniz integral rule (justified by the dominated convergence theorem app
lied iteratively), $\Gamma$ is $k$-times differentiable on $[a,b]$, hence at $x_
0$, and the derivatives pass under the integral:

$$\Gamma^{(k)}(x) = \int_0^\infty \frac{\partial^k}{\partial x^k}\!\left(t^{x-1}
e^{-t}\right) dt = \int_0^\infty t^{x-1}(\ln t)^k e^{-t}\,dt. \tag{2}$$

**Step 3: Conclusion.** Since $x_0 \in (0,\infty)$ was arbitrary, formula (2) ho
lds for all $x \in (0,\infty)$.

$$\boxed{\;\Gamma^{(k)}(x) = \int_0^\infty t^{x-1}\,(\ln t)^k\, e^{-t}\, dt, \qq
uad x \in (0,\infty).\;}$$
