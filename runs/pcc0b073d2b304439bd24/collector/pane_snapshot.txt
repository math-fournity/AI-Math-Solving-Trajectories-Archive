## Proof

Since $S$ is open in the subspace topology on $A$, there exists an open set $V \
subset X$ such that
$$S = V \cap A.$$

**Key fact (local connectedness).** In a locally connected space, every connecte
d component of an open set is itself open. Indeed, let $W \subset X$ be open and
 let $C$ be a component of $W$. For any $x \in C$, local connectedness gives a c
onnected open neighborhood $N_x$ of $x$ with $N_x \subset W$ (shrink a connected
 neighborhood to lie inside $W$). Since $N_x$ is connected and meets $C$ (at $x$
), it lies in the component $C$. Thus $C = \bigcup_{x \in C} N_x$ is open.

**Applying this.** Consider the open set $V \subset X$. Since $X$ is locally con
nected, every component of $V$ is open in $X$.

Now $S \subset V$ (because $S = V \cap A \subset V$) and $S$ is connected. The c
onnected components of $V$ partition $V$ into pairwise-separated clopen pieces,
so a connected subset of $V$ must lie entirely within a single component. Concre
tely: pick any $s \in S$ and let $C$ be the component of $V$ containing $s$; the
n $C \cup S$ is connected (both contain $s$), and by maximality of $C$ among con
nected subsets of $V$, we get $S \subset C$.

**The candidate.** Set $U := C$, the component of $V$ containing $S$. Then:

- $U$ is **connected** (it is a connected component).
- $U$ is **open in $X$** (by the key fact above, using local connectedness of $X
$).
- $U \cap A = S$: Since $U = C \subset V$, we have $U \cap A \subset V \cap A =
S$. Conversely, $S \subset C = U$ and $S \subset A$, so $S \subset U \cap A$. He
nce $U \cap A = S$.

Thus $U$ is a connected, open subset of $X$ with $S = U \cap A$. $\blacksquare$
