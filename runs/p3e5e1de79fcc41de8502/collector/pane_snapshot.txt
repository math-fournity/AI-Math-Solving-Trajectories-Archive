## Setup and notation

Let $Y_1 = X + Z_1$, $Y_2 = X + Z_2$, and $W = X + Z_2 = Y_2$. Write $\mu = \mat
hbb{E}[X]$, $\sigma^2 = \operatorname{Var}(X) = 1 - \mu^2$ (since $\mathbb{E}[X^
2]=1$).

## Step 1 — Reduction via the law of total variance

By the law of total variance,
$$\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Var}(Y_1\mid Y_2)] = \operatorname{Var}(Y_1) - \opera
torname{Var}(\mathbb{E}[Y_1\mid Y_2]).$$

**First term.** $\operatorname{Var}(Y_1) = \operatorname{Var}(X)+\operatorname{V
ar}(Z_1) = \sigma^2 + 1$.

**Conditional mean.** Since $Z_1$ is independent of $(X,Z_2)$,
$$\mathbb{E}[Y_1\mid Y_2] = \mathbb{E}[X\mid X+Z_2] + \mathbb{E}[Z_1\mid X+Z_2]
= \mathbb{E}[X\mid W] + 0.$$

So $\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb{E}[Y_1\mid Y_2]) = \operatorname{Var}(\mathbb{E}[
X\mid W])$, and
$$\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Var}(Y_1\mid Y_2)] = \sigma^2 + 1 - \operatorname{Var
}(\mathbb{E}[X\mid W]).$$

Decomposing $\mathbb{E}[(\mathbb{E}[X\mid W])^2] = \operatorname{Var}(\mathbb{E}
[X\mid W]) + \mu^2$, and using $\sigma^2+\mu^2 = \mathbb{E}[X^2]=1$:
$$\boxed{\;\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Var}(Y_1\mid Y_2)] = 2 - \mathbb{E}\!\left[(
\mathbb{E}[X\mid W])^2\right].\;}$$

Thus maximizing the target is equivalent to **minimizing** $\mathbb{E}[(\mathbb{
E}[X\mid W])^2]$.

## Step 2 — A lower bound on $\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb{E}[X\mid W])$ via the l
inear predictor

The conditional expectation $\mathbb{E}[X\mid W]$ is the $L^2$-optimal predictor
 of $X$ from $W$, so it is at least as good as the **best linear** predictor
$$L(W) = \mu + \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X,W)}{\operatorname{Var}(W)}(W-\mu).$$

Here $\operatorname{Cov}(X,W)=\operatorname{Cov}(X,X+Z_2)=\sigma^2$ and $\operat
orname{Var}(W)=\sigma^2+1$, so
$$L(W) = \mu + \frac{\sigma^2}{\sigma^2+1}(W-\mu),\qquad \operatorname{Var}(L(W)
) = \frac{\sigma^4}{\sigma^2+1}.$$

Since $\mathbb{E}[X\mid W]$ dominates $L(W)$ in $L^2$,
$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[(X-\mathbb{E}[X\mid W])^2\bigr] \le \mathbb{E}\bigl[(X-L(W))^2
\bigr].$$

The left side is $\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Var}(X\mid W)] = \sigma^2 - \operator
name{Var}(\mathbb{E}[X\mid W])$; the right side is $\sigma^2 - \operatorname{Var
}(L(W)) = \sigma^2 - \frac{\sigma^4}{\sigma^2+1} = \frac{\sigma^2}{\sigma^2+1}$.
 Therefore
$$\sigma^2 - \operatorname{Var}(\mathbb{E}[X\mid W]) \le \frac{\sigma^2}{\sigma^
2+1}\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \operatorname{Var}(\mathbb{E}[X\mid W]) \ge \frac{\s
igma^4}{\sigma^2+1}.$$

## Step 3 — Upper bound on the target

Combining,
$$\mathbb{E}[(\mathbb{E}[X\mid W])^2] = \operatorname{Var}(\mathbb{E}[X\mid W])
+ \mu^2 \ge \frac{\sigma^4}{\sigma^2+1} + \mu^2.$$

With $\sigma^2 = 1-\mu^2$ and $t = \mu^2 \in [0,1]$:
$$\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Var}(Y_1\mid Y_2)] \le 2 - t - \frac{(1-t)^2}{2-t} \;
=:\; f(t).$$

Compute $f'(t)$: writing $(1-t)(3-t) = (2-t)^2 - 1$,
$$f'(t) = -1 + \frac{(1-t)(3-t)}{(2-t)^2} = -1 + 1 - \frac{1}{(2-t)^2} = -\frac{
1}{(2-t)^2} < 0.$$

So $f$ is **strictly decreasing** on $[0,1]$, attaining its maximum at $t=0$:
$$f(0) = 2 - 0 - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2}.$$

Hence
$$\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Var}(X+Z_1\mid X+Z_2)] \le \frac{3}{2}.$$

## Step 4 — Equality is attained when $X\sim N(0,1)$

Take $X\sim N(0,1)$, independent of $Z_1,Z_2$. Then $(Y_1,Y_2)$ is jointly Gauss
ian, so the conditional expectation is exactly linear:
$$\mathbb{E}[Y_1\mid Y_2] = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_2)}{\operatorname{Var
}(Y_2)}\,Y_2 = \frac{1}{2}\,Y_2,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\mathbb{E}[Y_1\mid Y_2
]) = \tfrac14\operatorname{Var}(Y_2)=\tfrac14\cdot 2 = \tfrac12.$$

Therefore
$$\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Var}(Y_1\mid Y_2)] = \operatorname{Var}(Y_1) - \tfrac
12 = 2 - \tfrac12 = \frac{3}{2}.$$

The bound is tight.

## Conclusion

The maximum value of $\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Var}(X+Z_1\mid X+Z_2)]$ over all
distributions of $X$ with $\mathbb{E}[X^2]=1$ is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{3}{2}},$$

achieved when $X\sim N(0,1)$.
