Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $a(x, u_m, \xi) \to a(x, u,
 \xi)$ in $L^1(\Omega)$ under the given hypotheses, with $\xi$ fixed.

**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

**Step 1: Uniform boundedness of $\{u_m\}$.**

Since $u_m \to u$ in $L^\infty(\Omega)$, we have $\|u_m - u\|_{L^\infty} \to 0$.
 In particular, for all $m$ sufficiently large, $\|u_m - u\|_\infty \leq 1$, so
$$|u_m(x)| \leq |u(x)| + 1 \leq \|u\|_\infty + 1 =: M$$
a.e. for all large $m$. Adjusting $M$ to cover the finitely many remaining terms
, there exists $M > 0$ such that $\|u_m\|_{L^\infty} \leq M$ for all $m$.

**Step 2: An $L^1$ dominating function.**

Fix $\xi \in \mathbb{R}^n$. By the growth condition (3), for a.e. $x \in \Omega$
 and every $m$:
$$|a(x, u_m(x), \xi)| \leq k(x) + \beta\bigl(|u_m(x)|^{p-1} + |\xi|^{p-1}\bigr)
\leq k(x) + \beta\bigl(M^{p-1} + |\xi|^{p-1}\bigr).$$

Define
$$g(x) := k(x) + \beta\bigl(M^{p-1} + |\xi|^{p-1}\bigr).$$

Since $\Omega$ is bounded ($|\Omega|<\infty$) and $k \in L^{p'}(\Omega)$ with $p
' > 1$, Hölder's inequality gives $k \in L^1(\Omega)$:
$$\int_\Omega |k|\,dx \leq \|k\|_{L^{p'}}\,|\Omega|^{1/p} < \infty.$$
The constant $\beta(M^{p-1}+|\xi|^{p-1})$ is integrable over the bounded set $\O
mega$. Hence $g \in L^1(\Omega)$, and
$$|a(x, u_m(x), \xi)| \leq g(x) \quad \text{a.e. for all } m.$$

**Step 3: Pointwise convergence a.e.**

Since $a$ is a Carathéodory function, condition (1) gives that $a(x, \cdot, \xi)
$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$ for a.e. $x \in \Omega$. The uniform convergence
 $u_m \to u$ implies $u_m(x) \to u(x)$ for a.e. $x$ (in fact, uniformly). Theref
ore, for a.e. $x \in \Omega$:
$$a(x, u_m(x), \xi) \to a(x, u(x), \xi).$$

(Measurability of $x \mapsto a(x, u_m(x), \xi)$ follows from the Carathéodory pr
operty and measurability of $u_m$.)

**Step 4: Dominated convergence.**

We have established:
- $a(x, u_m(x), \xi) \to a(x, u(x), \xi)$ a.e.,
- $|a(x, u_m(x), \xi)| \leq g(x)$ with $g \in L^1(\Omega)$.

By the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem:
$$\int_\Omega |a(x, u_m(x), \xi) - a(x, u(x), \xi)|\,dx \to 0,$$
i.e., $a(x, u_m, \xi) \to a(x, u, \xi)$ in $L^1(\Omega)$. $\blacksquare$
