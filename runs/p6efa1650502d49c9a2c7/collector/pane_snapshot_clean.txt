## Proof

We determine the universal cover of the punctured plane $X = \mathbb{R}^2 \setmi
nus \{0\}$.

### Step 1: Identify the fundamental group

Identify $\mathbb{R}^2 \cong \mathbb{C}$. The map
$$r: X \to S^1, \quad z \mapsto \frac{z}{|z|}$$
is a (strong) deformation retraction, with homotopy $H(z,t) = (1-t)z + t\,\frac{
z}{|z|}$. Hence $X \simeq S^1$, and
$$\pi_1(X) \cong \pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}.$$

### Step 2: Construct the covering map

Define
$$p: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}, \qquad p(t) = \big(e^{t}\cos t
,\; e^{t}\sin t\big).$$
Equivalently, viewing the codomain as $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$, $p(t) = e^{(1+
i)t}$.

**$p$ is continuous:** it is a composition of continuous functions.

**$p$ is surjective.** Given any $w \in \mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$, write $w = \r
ho\, e^{i\theta}$ with $\rho > 0$ and $\theta \in \mathbb{R}$. Set $t = \ln\rho$
 (so $e^t = \rho$) and choose the representative $\theta = \ln\rho + 2\pi k$ for
 the appropriate $k$... more directly: we need $e^t\cos t = \rho\cos\theta$ and
$e^t\sin t = \rho\sin\theta$, i.e. $t = \theta + 2\pi k$ and $e^t = \rho$, i.e.
$t = \ln\rho$. These are compatible only if $\theta \equiv \ln\rho \pmod{2\pi}$.


Let me use a cleaner construction. Define instead
$$p: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}, \qquad p(t) = e^{2\pi i t}\cdot e^
{t} = e^{t + 2\pi i t}.$$
Actually, the cleanest standard construction separates the radial and angular pa
rts. Define
$$p: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}, \qquad p(t) = e^{t}\cdot e^{2\pi i
 t}.$$
Given $w = \rho e^{i\varphi}$ with $\rho > 0$, $\varphi \in [0,2\pi)$: we need $
e^t = \rho$ (so $t = \ln\rho$) and $e^{2\pi i t} = e^{i\varphi}$ (so $t \equiv \
varphi/(2\pi) \pmod 1$). These two conditions on $t$ are independent constraints
 and generally incompatible.

The correct approach: the universal cover should use **two** real parameters for
 the radial direction won't work since $\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{0\}$ is 2-dimensi
onal. Let me reconsider — the universal cover of $S^1$ is $\mathbb{R}$, and $X \
simeq S^1$ but $X$ is not homeomorphic to $S^1$. The universal cover of $X$ must
 be a 2-manifold.

### Step 3: Correct construction via the universal cover of $S^1$

Since $X \simeq S^1$ and $\pi_1(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}$, the universal cover $\wideti
lde{X}$ is a simply connected 2-manifold with a free, properly discontinuous $\m
athbb{Z}$-action such that $\widetilde{X}/\mathbb{Z} \cong X$.

Use polar coordinates: $X \cong (0,\infty) \times S^1$ via $z \mapsto (|z|, z/|z
|)$. The universal cover of $S^1$ is $\exp: \mathbb{R} \to S^1$, $\theta \mapsto
 e^{i\theta}$. Since $(0,\infty)$ is already simply connected, the universal cov
er of the product is the product of the universal covers:
$$\widetilde{X} = (0,\infty) \times \mathbb{R},$$
with covering map
$$p: (0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R} \to (0,\infty)\times S^1 \cong \mathbb{R}^2\setm
inus\{0\}, \qquad p(r,\theta) = \big(r,\; e^{i\theta}\big) \;\longleftrightarrow
\; r\,e^{i\theta}.$$

### Step 4: Verify the covering properties

- **Continuity and surjectivity:** $p(r,\theta) = r\,e^{i\theta}$ is continuous
and clearly surjective onto $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$ (every nonzero complex nu
mber has a modulus $r>0$ and argument $\theta$).

- **Local homeomorphism:** For any $(r_0,\theta_0)$, pick an open interval $I \s
ubset \mathbb{R}$ of length $< 2\pi$ containing $\theta_0$. Then $p$ restricted
to $(0,\infty)\times I$ is a homeomorphism onto its image (an open sector), sinc
e on $I$ the map $\theta\mapsto e^{i\theta}$ is injective and open. Thus every p
oint has an evenly covered neighborhood.

- **Simply connected domain:** $(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}$ is homeomorphic to $
\mathbb{R}^2$ (via $(r,\theta)\mapsto(\ln r, \theta)$), hence simply connected.

### Step 5: Uniqueness

Since $X$ is path-connected, locally path-connected, and semi-locally simply con
nected (given), the universal covering space exists and is unique up to homeomor
phism (over $X$). Therefore the universal cover is determined up to isomorphism.

### Conclusion

The universal cover of $\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{0\}$ is **$(0,\infty)\times\mathb
b{R}$**, which is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^2$, with covering map
$$p(r,\theta) = r\,e^{i\theta} \in \mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\} \cong \mathbb{R}^2\s
etminus\{0\}.$$
The group of deck transformations is $\mathbb{Z}$, generated by $(r,\theta)\maps
to(r,\theta+2\pi)$, consistent with $\pi_1(\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{0\})\cong\math
bb{Z}$.
