The statement is true. Here is the proof.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Setup. Let $K$ be a number field, $\mathcal{O}_K$ its ring of integers, $v$ a
non-archimedean discrete valuation on $K$, and $\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}K$
the nonzero prime ideal corresponding to $v$. Let $$\mathcal{O}{K,v}
=(\mathcal{O}K){\mathfrak{p}}$$ be the localization of $\mathcal{O}K$ at $
\mathfrak{p}$; this is a DVR. Denote by $\widehat{\mathcal{O}{K,v}}$ its
completion with respect to the $\mathfrak{p}$-adic topology, and by $$
\varprojlim_n \mathcal{O}_K/\mathfrak{p}^n$$ the inverse limit of the natural
quotient system.

We prove $\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{K,v}}\cong\varprojlim_n\mathcal{O}_K/
\mathfrak{p}^n$.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 1. The completion of a DVR is the inverse limit of its truncations.

The $\mathfrak{p}$-adic completion of $\mathcal{O}{K,v}$ is, by definition/
construction, $$\widehat{\mathcal{O}{K,v}}=\varprojlim_n \mathcal{O}{K,v}/
\mathfrak{p}^n\mathcal{O}{K,v}.$$ This is the standard description of the
completion of a local ring with respect to its maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}
=\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{O}{K,v}$: the $\mathfrak{m}$-adic completion is the
inverse limit of the quotients $\mathcal{O}{K,v}/\mathfrak{m}^n$, and $
\mathfrak{m}^n=\mathfrak{p}^n\mathcal{O}_{K,v}$.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 2. Localization does not change the quotients: $\mathcal{O}K/\mathfrak{p}
^n\cong\mathcal{O}{K,v}/\mathfrak{p}^n\mathcal{O}_{K,v}$.

Consider the natural map $$\varphi_n:\mathcal{O}K/\mathfrak{p}
^n\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}{K,v}/\mathfrak{p}^n\mathcal{O}{K,v},\qquad a+
\mathfrak{p}^n\mapsto a/1+\mathfrak{p}^n\mathcal{O}{K,v}.$$

Surjectivity. Every element of $\mathcal{O}{K,v}$ is of the form $a/s$ with
$a\in\mathcal{O}K$ and $s\in\mathcal{O}K\setminus\mathfrak{p}$. Since
$s\notin\mathfrak{p}$, the image $\bar{s}$ of $s$ in the local ring $\mathcal{O}
{K,v}/\mathfrak{p}^n\mathcal{O}{K,v}$ (whose maximal ideal is $
\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{O}{K,v}/\mathfrak{p}^n\mathcal{O}{K,v}$) is a unit:
$s\notin\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{O}{K,v}$, so $\bar{s}$ lies outside the unique
maximal ideal and is invertible. Hence $a/s\equiv a\cdot
s^{-1}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}^n\mathcal{O}_{K,v}}$, and there exists $t\in\mathcal{O}
K$ with $st\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{p}^n}$ (the inverse of $s$ mod $\mathfrak{p}
^n$ can be lifted from $\mathcal{O}K/\mathfrak{p}^n$ since $s$ is a unit there).
Therefore $a/s\equiv at\pmod{\mathfrak{p}^n\mathcal{O}{K,v}}$, and $
\varphi_n(at+\mathfrak{p}^n)=a/s+\mathfrak{p}^n\mathcal{O}{K,v}$. So $\varphi_n$
is surjective.

Injectivity. Suppose $a\in\mathcal{O}K$ maps to $0$, i.e. $a/1\in\mathfrak{p}
^n\mathcal{O}{K,v}$. Then $a/1=b/s$ for some $b\in\mathfrak{p}^n$,
$s\in\mathcal{O}_K\setminus\mathfrak{p}$, meaning there exists $t\in\mathcal{O}
_K\setminus\mathfrak{p}$ with $t(sa-b)=0$, i.e. $tsa=tb\in\mathfrak{p}^n$. Since
$t,s\notin\mathfrak{p}$ and $\mathfrak{p}$ is prime (so $\mathfrak{p}^n$ is $
\mathfrak{p}$-primary in the Dedekind domain $\mathcal{O}_K$),
$ts\notin\mathfrak{p}$ implies $ts$ is a unit modulo $\mathfrak{p}^n$, hence
$a\in\mathfrak{p}^n$. So $\ker\varphi_n=\mathfrak{p}^n/\mathfrak{p}^n=0$.

Thus $\varphi_n$ is an isomorphism for every $n\geq 1$.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 3. The isomorphisms are compatible with the inverse systems.

The maps $\varphi_n$ commute with the natural reduction maps $\mathcal{O}K/
\mathfrak{p}^{n+1}\to\mathcal{O}K/\mathfrak{p}^n$ and $\mathcal{O}{K,v}/
\mathfrak{p}^{n+1}\mathcal{O}{K,v}\to\mathcal{O}{K,v}/\mathfrak{p}^n\mathcal{O}
{K,v}$, since both are induced by the identity on representatives. Therefore the
${\varphi_n}$ form an isomorphism of inverse systems, which induces an
isomorphism on inverse limits: $$\varprojlim_n\mathcal{O}K/\mathfrak{p}^n;\cong;
\varprojlim_n\mathcal{O}{K,v}/\mathfrak{p}^n\mathcal{O}_{K,v}.$$

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 4. Conclusion.

Combining Steps 1 and 3: $$\widehat{\mathcal{O}{K,v}}=\varprojlim_n\mathcal{O}
{K,v}/\mathfrak{p}^n\mathcal{O}_{K,v}\cong\varprojlim_n\mathcal{O}_K/
\mathfrak{p}^n.$$

Hence the statement is true: the completion of the localization $
\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{K,v}}$ is isomorphic to $\varprojlim_n\mathcal{O}_K/
\mathfrak{p}^n$. $\blacksquare$

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Remark. The essential point is that localizing a Dedekind domain at a prime $
\mathfrak{p}$ does not change any of the quotients $\mathcal{O}_K/\mathfrak{p}
^n$, because elements outside $\mathfrak{p}$ are already units modulo $
\mathfrak{p}^n$. Thus the inverse limit — which sees only these quotients and
their transition maps — is unchanged by localization, and equals the $
\mathfrak{p}$-adic completion of the localized DVR.
