**Answer: Yes.** This is the Continuous Mapping Theorem. Below is a complete pro
of.

---

**Theorem (Continuous Mapping Theorem).** Let $X_n, X$ be random variables takin
g values in a metric space $(S, d)$ (e.g. $\mathbb{R}^k$). Suppose $X_n \xrighta
rrow{d} X$ and let $f : S \to T$ be a continuous map into a metric space $(T, \r
ho)$. Then $f(X_n) \xrightarrow{d} f(X)$.

**Proof.**

We use **Skorokhod's representation theorem**, which says: if $X_n \xrightarrow{
d} X$, then there exists a common probability space $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \math
bb{P})$ carrying random variables $Y_n, Y$ such that

1. $Y_n \stackrel{d}{=} X_n$ for every $n$ (same distribution),
2. $Y \stackrel{d}{=} X$,
3. $Y_n \to Y$ $\mathbb{P}$-almost surely.

(When $S = \mathbb{R}$, this is constructed explicitly on $([0,1], \mathcal{B},
\lambda)$ via the quantile functions $Y_n(u) = F_n^{-1}(u)$, $Y(u) = F^{-1}(u)$,
 where $F_n^{-1}(u) = \inf\{x : F_n(x) \ge u\}$; the a.s. convergence $F_n^{-1}(
u) \to F^{-1}(u)$ at every continuity point $u$ of $F^{-1}$ gives the result. Th
e general metric-space version is the Skorokhod–Dudley embedding.)

Now consider the composed random variables $f(Y_n)$ and $f(Y)$ on this common pr
obability space. Since $f$ is continuous and $Y_n(\omega) \to Y(\omega)$ for $\m
athbb{P}$-almost every $\omega$, continuity of $f$ gives

$$f(Y_n(\omega)) \to f(Y(\omega)) \quad \text{for } \mathbb{P}\text{-a.e. } \ome
ga.$$

Hence $f(Y_n) \to f(Y)$ almost surely. Almost sure convergence implies convergen
ce in distribution, so

$$f(Y_n) \xrightarrow{d} f(Y). \tag{$\ast$}$$

But $f(Y_n) \stackrel{d}{=} f(X_n)$ (because $Y_n \stackrel{d}{=} X_n$ and apply
ing the same measurable function $f$ preserves equality in distribution) and $f(
Y) \stackrel{d}{=} f(X)$. Therefore ($\ast$) is exactly the statement

$$f(X_n) \xrightarrow{d} f(X). \qquad \blacksquare$$

---

**Remarks.**

- Continuity of $f$ is used only to turn a.s. convergence $Y_n \to Y$ into a.s.
convergence $f(Y_n) \to f(Y)$. The result in fact holds under the weaker hypothe
sis that $f$ is continuous $\mathbb{P}_X$-a.s. (i.e. its discontinuity set $D_f$
 satisfies $\mathbb{P}(X \in D_f) = 0$); the same Skorokhod argument works becau
se $Y \stackrel{d}{=} X$ implies $\mathbb{P}(Y \in D_f) = 0$, so on the a.s. set
 where $Y_n \to Y$ *and* $Y \notin D_f$, continuity at the limit point yields $f
(Y_n) \to f(Y)$.
- The measurability of $f$ (needed for $f(X_n)$, $f(X)$ to be random variables)
is automatic for continuous $f$ between Polish spaces, since continuous maps are
 Borel measurable.
