## Step 1: Distribution of $Y_k = 1/X_k$

Since $X_k$ has density $f_X(x) = \frac{1}{2}$ on $[-1,1]$, the transformation $
Y = 1/X$ gives:

$$f_Y(y) = f_X(1/y)\cdot\frac{1}{y^2} = \frac{1}{2y^2}, \quad |y| \geq 1$$

This is a **symmetric heavy-tailed distribution**. The mean does not exist since
 $\int_1^\infty y \cdot \frac{1}{2y^2}\,dy = \frac{1}{2}\int_1^\infty \frac{dy}{
y} = \infty$.

## Step 2: Tail behavior

$$P(|Y| > t) = 2\int_t^\infty \frac{1}{2y^2}\,dy = \frac{1}{t}, \quad t \geq 1$$

So the tail is exactly $\frac{1}{t}$, giving tail coefficient $c = 1$ and tail i

## Step 3: Generalized CLT (Domain of attraction of stable law)

Since $Y_k$ is symmetric with $P(|Y|>t) \sim c \cdot t^{-\alpha}$ where $\alpha
= 1$ and $c = 1$, by the generalized central limit theorem, $Y_k$ lies in the do
main of attraction of a **symmetric $\alpha$-stable law** with $\alpha = 1$.

The normalization is $a_n = n^{1/\alpha} = n$ (confirmed: $n \cdot P(|Y|>n) = n
\cdot \frac{1}{n} = 1 \to \text{const}$).

The limit characteristic function for a symmetric $\alpha$-stable law with tail
coefficient $c$ is:

$$\varphi(t) = \exp\!\left(-\sigma^\alpha |t|^\alpha\right), \quad \sigma^\alpha
 = \frac{c\,\pi}{2\,\Gamma(\alpha)\sin(\pi\alpha/2)}$$

For $\alpha = 1$, $c = 1$: $\sigma = \frac{\pi}{2\cdot 1 \cdot 1} = \frac{\pi}{2

Therefore:

$$\frac{S_n}{n} \xrightarrow{d} Z, \quad \text{where } \varphi_Z(t) = e^{-(\pi/2
