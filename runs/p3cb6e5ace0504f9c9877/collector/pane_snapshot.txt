## Solution

We parametrize the upper semicircle $S_r$ by $z = re^{it}$, $t \in [0,\pi]$, so
$dz = ire^{it}\,dt$. The integral becomes

$$I(r) = \int_{S_r} \frac{e^{iz}-1}{z^2}\,dz = \int_0^\pi \frac{e^{ire^{it}}-1}{
r^2 e^{2it}}\,ire^{it}\,dt = \frac{i}{r}\int_0^\pi \frac{e^{ire^{it}}-1}{e^{it}}
\,dt.$$

**Laurent expansion of the integrand near $z = 0$.** Since

$$e^{iz} - 1 = iz - \frac{z^2}{2} + O(z^3),$$

we have

$$\frac{e^{iz}-1}{z^2} = \frac{i}{z} - \frac{1}{2} + O(z).$$

**Integrating term by term over $S_r$.**

**Leading term.** The $1/z$ term gives the dominant contribution:

$$\int_{S_r} \frac{i}{z}\,dz = i\int_0^\pi \frac{1}{re^{it}}\,ire^{it}\,dt = i\c
dot i\int_0^\pi dt = -\pi.$$

This is independent of $r$.

**Constant term.** The $-1/2$ term integrates over the arc:

$$-\frac{1}{2}\int_{S_r} dz = -\frac{1}{2}\bigl[z\bigr]_{z=r}^{z=-r} = -\frac{1}
{2}(-r - r) = r \xrightarrow{r\to 0} 0.$$

**Remainder.** The $O(z)$ term contributes $O(r^2)\cdot \pi = O(r^2) \to 0$, sin
ce the arc length is $\pi r$ and the integrand is $O(r)$, giving $O(r^2)$.

**Combining.**

$$I(r) = -\pi + r + O(r^2) \xrightarrow{r \to 0} -\pi.$$

**Verification via residue.** The integrand has a simple pole at $z = 0$ with re
sidue $\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{e^{iz}-1}{z^2} = i$. A full counterclockwis
e loop would give $2\pi i \cdot i = -2\pi$. The upper semicircle $S_r$ (traverse
d counterclockwise from $r$ to $-r$) captures exactly half of this contribution
from the pole, namely $-\pi$, while the regular part vanishes as $r \to 0$. This
 confirms the result.

$$\boxed{\lim_{r\to 0}\int_{S_r}\frac{e^{iz}-1}{z^2}\,dz = -\pi}$$
