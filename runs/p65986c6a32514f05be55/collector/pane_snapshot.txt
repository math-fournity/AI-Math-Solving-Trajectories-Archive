## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, such a finite group exists. Specifically, the cyclic group $\mat
hbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ for any prime $p \geq 2$ has irreducible representations tha
t cannot be induced from any irreducible representation of any proper subgroup.

**Key Fact (Dimension Formula):** If $H \leq G$ and $\sigma$ is a representation
 of $H$, then
$$\dim(\mathrm{Ind}_H^G(\sigma)) = [G : H] \cdot \dim(\sigma).$$

**Setup:** Let $G = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ with $p$ prime. Since $p$ is prime,
the **only** proper subgroup of $G$ is the trivial subgroup $H = \{e\}$, with in
dex $[G : H] = p$.

**Irreducible representations of $G$:** Since $G$ is abelian, all irreducible re
presentations are 1-dimensional. There are exactly $p$ of them, given by the cha
racters
$$\chi_k(\bar{g}) = e^{2\pi i k / p}, \quad k = 0, 1, \dots, p-1,$$
each of dimension 1.

**Irreducible representations of $H = \{e\}$:** The trivial group has only one i
rreducible representation: the trivial representation $\mathbf{1}$, of dimension
 1.

**Inducing from the only proper subgroup:** The only possible induced representa
tion from a proper subgroup is:
$$\mathrm{Ind}_{\{e\}}^{G}(\mathbf{1}) = \text{the (left) regular representation
 of } G,$$
which has dimension $p \cdot 1 = p$.

For $p \geq 2$, the regular representation has dimension $p > 1$, so it is **red
ucible** (it decomposes as the direct sum of all $p$ irreducible representations
, each appearing once). In particular, it is not equal to any single irreducible
 representation $\chi_k$.

**Conclusion:** For any irreducible representation $\chi_k$ of $G = \mathbb{Z}/p
\mathbb{Z}$ (with $p \geq 2$ prime), there is no proper subgroup $H < G$ and no
irreducible representation $\sigma$ of $H$ such that $\mathrm{Ind}_H^G(\sigma) \
cong \chi_k$. This is because:

1. The only proper subgroup is $\{e\}$.
2. The only irreducible representation of $\{e\}$ is $\mathbf{1}$.
3. $\mathrm{Ind}_{\{e\}}^{G}(\mathbf{1})$ is the regular representation of dimen
sion $p \geq 2$, which is reducible and hence not isomorphic to any 1-dimensiona
l irreducible $\chi_k$.

Therefore, **there exists a finite group with an irreducible representation that
 cannot be induced from any irreducible representation of any proper subgroup**,
 and $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ (for any prime $p \geq 2$) is such an example. $\b
lacksquare$
