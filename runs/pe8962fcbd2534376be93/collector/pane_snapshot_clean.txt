## Proof

Let $P(x) = \prod_{j=1}^n (x-a_j)$, a monic polynomial of degree $n$. We seek th
e smallest $c$ such that $\max_{[0,2]}|P| \le c^n \max_{[0,1]}|P|$ for all $n\ge
 1$ and all real $a_j$. Note $\max_{[0,1]}|P|>0$ since a nonzero polynomial cann
ot vanish on an interval.

**Step 1: Reduction to Chebyshev's extremal theorem.**

Map $[0,1]\to[-1,1]$ via $t=2x-1$, so $[0,2]\to[-1,3]$. Set $Q(t)=P\!\bigl(\tfra
c{t+1}{2}\bigr)$, degree $n$. Then $\max_{[0,1]}|P|=\max_{[-1,1]}|Q|$ and $\max_
{[0,2]}|P|=\max_{[-1,3]}|Q|$.

> **Lemma (Chebyshev).** If $Q$ has degree $\le n$ and $|Q(t)|\le 1$ on $[-1,1]$
, then $|Q(t)|\le |T_n(t)|$ for $|t|\ge 1$, where $T_n(t)=\cos(n\arccos t)$.

*Proof.* Suppose $Q(t_0)>T_n(t_0)$ for some $t_0>1$ (other cases analogous). Let
 $S=T_n-Q$, $\deg S\le n$, $S(t_0)<0$. At $t_k=\cos(k\pi/n)$, $k=0,\dots,n$: $T_
n(t_k)=(-1)^k$, so $S(t_k)=(-1)^k-Q(t_k)$ has sign $(-1)^k$ (nonneg for even $k$
, nonpos for odd $k$). This forces $\ge n$ zeros in $[-1,1]$. Since $S(1)=1-Q(1)
\ge 0$ and $S(t_0)<0$, there is an additional zero in $(1,t_0]$, giving $n+1$ ze
ros—contradicting $\deg S\le n$. (Degenerate cases follow by a limiting argument
.) $\square$

**Step 2: Upper bound — $\|P\|_{[0,2]}\le T_n(3)\,\|P\|_{[0,1]}$.**

Let $M=\max_{[-1,1]}|Q|>0$ and $\tilde Q=Q/M$, so $|\tilde Q|\le 1$ on $[-1,1]$.

- $t\in[-1,1]$: $|Q(t)|\le M\le T_n(3)\,M$ since $T_n(3)\ge T_n(1)=1$.
- $t\in[1,3]$: $|Q(t)|\le M|T_n(t)|\le M\,T_n(3)$, since $|T_n(t)|$ is increasin
g for $t\ge 1$ ($T_n'=nU_{n-1}>0$ for $t>1$).

Hence $\max_{[-1,3]}|Q|\le T_n(3)\,M$, i.e. $\max_{[0,2]}|P|\le T_n(3)\max_{[0,1
]}|P|$. $\tag{$\ast$}$

**Step 3: $T_n(3)\le (3+2\sqrt2)^n$.**

For $t\ge 1$, $T_n(t)=\cosh(n\operatorname{arccosh}t)$. With $\alpha=\operatorna
me{arccosh}3=\ln(3+2\sqrt2)$:
$$T_n(3)=\frac{e^{n\alpha}+e^{-n\alpha}}{2}=\frac{(3+2\sqrt2)^n+(3-2\sqrt2)^n}{2
}<(3+2\sqrt2)^n,$$
since $0<3-2\sqrt2<3+2\sqrt2$. Combining with $(\ast)$: $\max_{[0,2]}|P|\le(3+2\
sqrt2)^n\max_{[0,1]}|P|$, so $c=3+2\sqrt2$ is feasible.

**Step 4: Lower bound — $c\ge 3+2\sqrt2$.**

Take the monic Chebyshev polynomial on $[0,1]$:
$$P(x)=\frac{T_n(2x-1)}{2^{2n-1}},$$
monic of degree $n$ (since $T_n(2x-1)$ has leading coefficient $2^{2n-1}$). Then
:
- $\max_{[0,1]}|P|=2^{1-2n}$ (equioscillation on $[-1,1]$),
- $\max_{[0,2]}|P|=|P(2)|=\frac{T_n(3)}{2^{2n-1}}$ (by monotonicity of $|T_n|$ f
or $t\ge 1$),

so the ratio equals $T_n(3)$ exactly. The inequality requires $T_n(3)\le c^n$, i
.e. $c\ge T_n(3)^{1/n}$.

Now $T_n(3)^{1/n}=\bigl(\frac{(3+2\sqrt2)^n+(3-2\sqrt2)^n}{2}\bigr)^{1/n}\to 3+2
\sqrt2$ as $n\to\infty$, since $\bigl(\frac{1+r^n}{2}\bigr)^{1/n}\to 1$ for $r=\
frac{3-2\sqrt2}{3+2\sqrt2}\in(0,1)$. Moreover $T_n(3)^{1/n}$ is increasing in $n
$ (as $\frac{d}{dn}\frac{\ln\cosh(n\alpha)}{n}=\frac{n\alpha\tanh(n\alpha)-\ln\c
osh(n\alpha)}{n^2}>0$), so $\sup_n T_n(3)^{1/n}=3+2\sqrt2$.

For any $c<3+2\sqrt2$, sufficiently large $n$ gives $T_n(3)^{1/n}>c$, and the Ch
ebyshev polynomial violates the inequality. Hence $c\ge 3+2\sqrt2$.

**Conclusion.** Steps 3 and 4 together yield

$$\boxed{c = 3+2\sqrt{2}}.$$
