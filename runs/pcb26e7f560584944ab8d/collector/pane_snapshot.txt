## Derivation of Second-Order Covariant Derivatives

### Setup

Let $A_i$ be a covariant vector field on a manifold with connection $\Gamma^l_{i
j}$ (assumed symmetric: $\Gamma^l_{ij}=\Gamma^l_{ji}$). We use the notation $A_{
i,j} := \partial_j A_i$ for partial derivatives and semicolons for covariant der
ivatives.

### First covariant derivative

For a covariant vector:
$$A_{i;j} = A_{i,j} - \Gamma^l_{ij}\,A_l \tag{1}$$

### Second covariant derivative $A_{i;jk}$

Since $A_{i;j}$ is a $(0,2)$-tensor, its covariant derivative w.r.t. $k$ is:
$$A_{i;jk} = \partial_k(A_{i;j}) - \Gamma^l_{ik}\,A_{l;j} - \Gamma^l_{jk}\,A_{i;
l} \tag{2}$$

Substituting (1) into (2) and expanding each piece:

$$A_{i;jk} = \partial_k\!\left(A_{i,j} - \Gamma^l_{ij}A_l\right) - \Gamma^l_{ik}
\!\left(A_{l,j} - \Gamma^m_{lj}A_m\right) - \Gamma^l_{jk}\!\left(A_{i,l} - \Gamm
a^m_{il}A_m\right)$$

$$\boxed{A_{i;jk} = A_{i,jk} - (\partial_k\Gamma^l_{ij})A_l - \Gamma^l_{ij}A_{l,
k} - \Gamma^l_{ik}A_{l,j} + \Gamma^l_{ik}\Gamma^m_{lj}A_m - \Gamma^l_{jk}A_{i,l}
 + \Gamma^l_{jk}\Gamma^m_{il}A_m} \tag{3}$$

### Second covariant derivative $A_{i;kj}$

Swapping $j \leftrightarrow k$ in the entire derivation:

$$A_{i;kj} = \partial_j(A_{i;k}) - \Gamma^l_{ij}\,A_{l;k} - \Gamma^l_{kj}\,A_{i;
l} \tag{4}$$

Expanding:

$$A_{i;kj} = \partial_j\!\left(A_{i,k} - \Gamma^l_{ik}A_l\right) - \Gamma^l_{ij}
\!\left(A_{l,k} - \Gamma^m_{lk}A_m\right) - \Gamma^l_{kj}\!\left(A_{i,l} - \Gamm
a^m_{il}A_m\right)$$

$$\boxed{A_{i;kj} = A_{i,kj} - (\partial_j\Gamma^l_{ik})A_l - \Gamma^l_{ik}A_{l,
j} - \Gamma^l_{ij}A_{l,k} + \Gamma^l_{ij}\Gamma^m_{lk}A_m - \Gamma^l_{kj}A_{i,l}
 + \Gamma^l_{kj}\Gamma^m_{il}A_m} \tag{5}$$

### Verification that $A_{i;jk} \neq A_{i;kj}$

Subtract (5) from (3). The following terms cancel identically:

- $A_{i,jk} = A_{i,kj}$ — **partial derivatives commute**
- $\Gamma^l_{ij}A_{l,k}$ — appears in both with the same sign
- $\Gamma^l_{ik}A_{l,j}$ — appears in both with the same sign
- $\Gamma^l_{jk}A_{i,l} = \Gamma^l_{kj}A_{i,l}$ — **connection is symmetric**, s
o these cancel
- $\Gamma^l_{jk}\Gamma^m_{il}A_m = \Gamma^l_{kj}\Gamma^m_{il}A_m$ — symmetric co
nnection, these cancel

The surviving terms give:

$$A_{i;jk} - A_{i;kj} = \left[(\partial_j\Gamma^l_{ik}) - (\partial_k\Gamma^l_{i
j}) + \Gamma^m_{ik}\Gamma^l_{mj} - \Gamma^m_{ij}\Gamma^l_{mk}\right] A_l \tag{6}
$$

(In the product terms I relabeled dummy indices $l \leftrightarrow m$ to factor
out $A_l$.)

The bracket is precisely the **Riemann–Christoffel curvature tensor**:

$$R^l_{\;ijk} = \partial_j\Gamma^l_{ik} - \partial_k\Gamma^l_{ij} + \Gamma^m_{ik
}\Gamma^l_{mj} - \Gamma^m_{ij}\Gamma^l_{mk} \tag{7}$$

Therefore:

$$\boxed{A_{i;jk} - A_{i;kj} = R^l_{\;ijk}\,A_l} \tag{8}$$

This is the **Ricci identity** for a covariant vector.

### Why the non-commutativity occurs

Equation (8) shows that the failure of covariant differentiation to commute is *
*not** a failure of partial differentiation (which commutes: $A_{i,jk}=A_{i,kj}$
), but rather a consequence of the **connection** $\Gamma^l_{ij}$:

1. **The connection is position-dependent.** When we take $\partial_k$ of $A_{i;
j}$, the derivative acts not only on $A_i$ but also on the Christoffel symbols $
\Gamma^l_{ij}$ inside $A_{i;j}$. The terms $-(\partial_k\Gamma^l_{ij})A_l$ and $
-(\partial_j\Gamma^l_{ik})A_l$ survive the subtraction because $\partial_k\Gamma
^l_{ij}\neq\partial_j\Gamma^l_{ik}$ in general.

2. **The connection is non-commutative as an operator.** Each covariant derivati
ve introduces correction terms $\Gamma\cdot A$. When two covariant derivatives a
ct in different orders, the "correction of the correction" terms (the $\Gamma\Ga
mma$ products) combine differently, yielding $\Gamma^m_{ik}\Gamma^l_{mj} - \Gamm
a^m_{ij}\Gamma^l_{mk}$.

3. **Geometric meaning — curvature.** The combination of these two effects is ex
actly the curvature tensor $R^l_{\;ijk}$. Physically, parallel-transporting a ve
ctor around an infinitesimal loop (first along $j$ then $k$, versus first along
$k$ then $j$) returns a different vector whenever the manifold is curved. The co
mmutator of covariant derivatives measures precisely this path-dependence.

4. **Flat-space limit.** In flat space with Cartesian (or any inertial) coordina
tes, $\Gamma^l_{ij}\equiv 0$, hence $R^l_{\;ijk}\equiv 0$, and $A_{i;jk}=A_{i;kj
}$. Even in flat space with curvilinear coordinates (where $\Gamma\neq 0$ but $R
=0$), the identity (8) still gives equality because the curvature tensor vanishe
s identically — the $\partial\Gamma$ and $\Gamma\Gamma$ terms cancel each other.
 Thus **non-commutativity occurs if and only if the manifold has non-zero curvat
ure**.

Gamma^l_{jk}\Gamma^m_{il}A_m$ |

The non-commutativity is governed entirely by the Riemann curvature tensor and v
