**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

We show that the supporting hyperplane theorem (together with basic compactness
facts in $\mathbb{R}^n$) implies that every closed convex set is the intersectio
n of closed halfspaces. Since the closure of a convex set is closed and convex,
the result follows.

---

**Step 1: The supporting hyperplane theorem implies the strict separation theore
m.**

We prove: if $D \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is a nonempty closed convex set and $y \
notin D$, then there exists a closed halfspace $H$ with $D \subseteq H$ and $y \
notin H$.

Since $D$ is closed and $y \notin D$, the distance $d = \operatorname{dist}(y, D
) > 0$. The function $x \mapsto \|y - x\|$ is continuous and attains its minimum
 on the compact set $D \cap \overline{B}(y, d+1)$ (nonempty by definition of $d$
, compact by Heine–Borel). Let $z \in D$ be a minimizer, so $\|y - z\| = d > 0$.

Since $z \in D$ while $y \notin D$, the point $z$ is a boundary point of $D$ (if
 $z$ were interior, a point on the segment from $z$ toward $y$ would still lie i
n $D$ and be closer to $y$, contradicting minimality).

Set $a = y - z \neq 0$. We prove that the hyperplane $\{x : a^T x = a^T z\}$ sup
ports $D$ at $z$:

For any $x \in D$ and $t \in (0, 1]$, convexity gives $z_t = (1-t)z + tx \in D$.
 By minimality of $z$:
$$\|y - z_t\|^2 \geq \|y - z\|^2 = \|a\|^2.$$
Expanding:
$$\|a - t(x - z)\|^2 = \|a\|^2 - 2t\, a^T(x - z) + t^2\|x - z\|^2 \geq \|a\|^2.$
$
Dividing by $t > 0$ and letting $t \to 0^+$:
$$a^T(x - z) \leq 0 \quad \text{for all } x \in D.$$

This is exactly the **supporting hyperplane condition**: the hyperplane through
$z$ with normal $a$ supports $D$, with $D \subseteq \{x : a^T x \leq a^T z\}$. M
oreover:
$$a^T y = a^T z + \|a\|^2 = a^T z + d^2 > a^T z.$$

So the closed halfspace $H = \{x : a^T x \leq a^T z\}$ contains $D$ but **not**
$y$. This is strict separation. $\square$

**Remark on the role of the supporting hyperplane theorem.** The inequality $a^T
(x - z) \leq 0$ for all $x \in D$ is precisely the conclusion of the supporting
hyperplane theorem at the boundary point $z$. The projection provides the correc
t normal direction $a = y - z$, ensuring the supporting hyperplane strictly sepa
rates $y$ from $D$. Thus the supporting hyperplane theorem, combined with the ex
istence of a nearest point (a basic compactness fact in $\mathbb{R}^n$), yields
strict separation.

---

**Step 2: Every closed convex set is the intersection of halfspaces.**

Let $D$ be a nonempty closed convex set. Let $\mathcal{H} = \{H : H \text{ is a
closed halfspace},\; D \subseteq H\}$ and $S = \bigcap_{H \in \mathcal{H}} H$. C
learly $D \subseteq S$.

We show $S \subseteq D$. Let $y \notin D$. By Step 1, there exists $H \in \mathc
al{H}$ with $y \notin H$, so $y \notin S$. Therefore $S \subseteq D$, giving $D
= S$.

(Edge cases: $D = \emptyset$ is the intersection of two disjoint parallel halfsp
aces; $D = \mathbb{R}^n$ is the intersection of the empty family.)

---

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

Let $C$ be any convex set. Then $\overline{C}$ is closed and convex. By Step 2,
$\overline{C} = \bigcap \{H : H \text{ closed halfspace},\; \overline{C} \subset
eq H\}$. Since halfspaces are closed, a halfspace contains $C$ if and only if it
 contains $\overline{C}$. Therefore:

$$\overline{C} = \bigcap \{H : H \text{ is a closed halfspace},\; C \subseteq H\
}.$$

This shows that the supporting hyperplane theorem implies that the closure of an
y convex set can be expressed as the intersection of halfspaces. $\blacksquare$
