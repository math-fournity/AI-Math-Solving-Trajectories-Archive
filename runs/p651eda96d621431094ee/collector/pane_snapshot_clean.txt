## Proof

**Setup.** Let $G = A_5$, a non-abelian finite simple group of order 60. We seek
 the smallest natural number $n$ such that there exist $g_1, g_2, \ldots, g_{n+1
} \in G$ with
$$x = g_1\, x\, g_2\, x\, g_3 \cdots g_n\, x\, g_{n+1} \quad \text{for all } x \
in G.$$

**Lower bound: $n \geq 1$.** For $n = 0$, the expression reduces to $f(x) = g_1$
, a constant function. The identity map is not constant (since $|A_5| = 60 > 1$)
, so $n = 0$ is impossible.

**Upper bound: $n = 1$ suffices.** Take $n = 1$ with $g_1 = e$ and $g_2 = e$ (th
e identity element). Then
$$g_1\, x\, g_2 = e \cdot x \cdot e = x \quad \text{for all } x \in G.$$
This is exactly the identity map.

**Conclusion.** Since $n = 0$ is impossible and $n = 1$ is achievable, the small
est such natural number is $n = 1$.
