Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there exists a metric space
 $(X, d)$ such that for any two distinct points $x, y \in X$ and any radii $R >
r \geq 1$, both $B(x, R) = X$ and $B(y, r) = X$.

**Answer: Yes, such a metric space exists.**

**Proof.**

Consider the metric space $X = \{a, b\}$ with the metric defined by $d(a, a) = d
(b, b) = 0$ and $d(a, b) = d(b, a) = \tfrac{1}{2}$.

This is a valid metric space (non-negativity, symmetry, and the triangle inequal
ity are all easily verified; the only distinct pair has distance $\frac{1}{2}$,
and $\frac{1}{2} \leq \frac{1}{2} + 0$ etc.).

Now take any two distinct points — the only choice is $\{a, b\}$ — and any radii
 $R > r \geq 1$.

**Ball $B(a, R)$:** We have $B(a, R) = \{x \in X : d(a, x) < R\}$. Since $d(a, a
) = 0 < R$ and $d(a, b) = \tfrac{1}{2} < 1 \leq R$, both points belong to $B(a,
R)$. Hence $B(a, R) = X$.

**Ball $B(b, r)$:** We have $B(b, r) = \{x \in X : d(b, x) < r\}$. Since $d(b, b
) = 0 < r$ and $d(b, a) = \tfrac{1}{2} < 1 \leq r$, both points belong to $B(b,
r)$. Hence $B(b, r) = X$.

Both open balls equal the entire space, as required. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The essential observation is that the condition "$B(p, \rho) = X$ fo
r every point $p$ and every $\rho \geq 1$" is equivalent to requiring the diamet
er of $X$ to be strictly less than $1$ (i.e., $d(x,y) < 1$ for all $x, y \in X$)
. Any metric space with at least two points and diameter $< 1$ satisfies the req
uirement — for instance, scaling any metric space with $\geq 2$ points by a fact
or $< 1/\text{diam}$.
