## Simplification

Let $S = \sqrt{2020}$. Since $a^2+b^2+c^2 = S^2$, I can factor:

$$a^4 - b^4 = (a^2-b^2)(a^2+b^2) = (a^2-b^2)(S^2-c^2) = (a^2-b^2)(S-c)(S+c)$$

Dividing by $S-c$:

$$\frac{a^4-b^4}{S-c} = (a^2-b^2)(S+c)$$

Applying this to all three terms:

$$E = (a^2-b^2)(S+c) + (b^2-c^2)(S+a) + (c^2-a^2)(S+b)$$

Splitting the $S$ terms:

$$E = S\underbrace{[(a^2-b^2)+(b^2-c^2)+(c^2-a^2)]}_{=\,0} + \underbrace{c(a^2-b
^2)+a(b^2-c^2)+b(c^2-a^2)}_{\text{key term}}$$

The $S$ terms cancel completely (no irrationality remains). Expanding the key te
rm:

$$E = a^2(c-b) + b^2(a-c) + c^2(b-a) = (a-b)(b-c)(c-a)$$

(verified by expanding $(a-b)(b-c)(c-a) = a^2(c-b)+b^2(a-c)+c^2(b-a)$).

## Finding the Maximum

Since $(a-b)(b-c)(c-a)$ is antisymmetric under transpositions, the maximum equal
s the maximum of $|(a-b)(b-c)(c-a)|$.

**Parity constraint:** $2020 \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$. Since squares are $\equiv 0$ or
 $1 \pmod{4}$, and the sum of three such values is $\equiv 0 \pmod{4}$ only if a
ll are $0$, we need $a,b,c$ all even. Setting $a=2a', b=2b', c=2c'$:

$$a'^2+b'^2+c'^2 = 505, \qquad |(a-b)(b-c)(c-a)| = 8|(a'-b')(b'-c')(c'-a')|$$

**Representations of 505 as sum of three positive squares:** Checking all $x$ fr
om 1 to 22, I test whether $505-x^2$ is a sum of two positive squares (using the
 criterion: every prime $p\equiv 3\pmod{4}$ must appear to even power, and at le
ast one prime $\equiv 1\pmod{4}$ must be present for positivity).

The only solution is:
$$505 = 18^2 + 10^2 + 9^2 = 324 + 100 + 81$$

All other values of $x$ yield either a number with a prime $\equiv 3\pmod{4}$ to
 odd power (impossible), or a perfect square $n^2+0^2$ (excluded since all varia

**Optimizing signs:** The magnitudes are $\{18, 20, 36\}$ (after multiplying by
