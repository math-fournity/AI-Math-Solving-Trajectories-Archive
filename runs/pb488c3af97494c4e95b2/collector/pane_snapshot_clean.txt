## Proof

Equip $E = C^0[0,1]$ with the supremum norm $\|f\|_\infty = \sup_{x\in[0,1]}|f(x
)|$. The operator $T(f)=f\circ g$ is linear and bounded with $\|T\|\le 1$, since
 $\|Tf\|_\infty = \|f\circ g\|_\infty \le \|f\|_\infty$.

**Claim.** $T$ is compact **if and only if** $g$ is constant.

---

### ($\Longleftarrow$) $g$ constant $\Rightarrow$ $T$ compact

If $g\equiv c$, then $T(f)(x)=f(c)$ for every $x$, so $T(f)$ is the constant fun
ction with value $f(c)$. Hence $T$ maps $E$ into the one-dimensional subspace of
 constant functions. A bounded operator with finite-dimensional range is compact
. $\checkmark$

---

### ($\Longrightarrow$) $T$ compact $\Rightarrow$ $g$ constant

Let $B=\{f\in E:\|f\|_\infty\le 1\}$ be the closed unit ball. Since $T$ is compa
ct, $T(B)=\{f\circ g:f\in B\}$ is relatively compact in $E$. By the **Arzelà–Asc
oli theorem**, $T(B)$ is equicontinuous.

We show $g$ is locally constant at every point of $[0,1]$.

Fix $x_0\in[0,1]$ and take $\varepsilon=1$. By equicontinuity of $T(B)$ at $x_0$
, there exists $\delta>0$ such that for all $x\in[0,1]$ with $|x-x_0|<\delta$ an
d all $f\in B$:
$$|f(g(x))-f(g(x_0))|<1.$$

We claim $g(x)=g(x_0)$ for every such $x$. Suppose for contradiction that $g(x)\
neq g(x_0)$ for some $x$ with $|x-x_0|<\delta$. Set $a=g(x)$, $b=g(x_0)$; then $
a\neq b$ and $a,b\in[0,1]$. Assume WLOG $a<b$. Define $h:[0,1]\to[-1,1]$ by
$$h(t)=\begin{cases}1 & t\le a,\\[2pt] 1-2\dfrac{t-a}{b-a} & a\le t\le b,\\[2pt]
 -1 & t\ge b.\end{cases}$$
Then $h$ is continuous, $\|h\|_\infty=1$ (so $h\in B$), $h(a)=1$, $h(b)=-1$, giv
ing
$$|h(g(x))-h(g(x_0))|=|1-(-1)|=2>1,$$
contradicting the equicontinuity estimate. Hence $g(x)=g(x_0)$ for all $x$ with
$|x-x_0|<\delta$; i.e., $g$ is locally constant at $x_0$.

Since $x_0$ was arbitrary, $g$ is locally constant at every point of $[0,1]$. Th
e interval $[0,1]$ is connected and $g$ is continuous, so a locally constant $g$
 must be **globally constant**. $\checkmark$

---

### Conclusion

The composition operator $T(f)=f\circ g$ on $C^0[0,1]$ (with the sup norm) is co
mpact **if and only if** $g:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ is a constant map. Equivalently, $T$
is compact exactly when it has rank $1$ (collapsing every $f$ to the constant fu
nction $x\mapsto f(c)$ for some fixed $c\in[0,1]$). Any non-constant continuous
$g$ yields a non-compact $T$, because the unit ball's image fails to be equicont
inuous at every point where $g$ is not locally constant — and such points must e
xist by connectedness of $[0,1]$.
