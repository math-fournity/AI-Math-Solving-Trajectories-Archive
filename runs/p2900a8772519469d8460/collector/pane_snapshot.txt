## Setup and Key Observation

When team $P$ (home) plays team $Q$ (road), $P$ wears $h_P$ and $Q$ wears $h_Q$
if $h_Q \neq h_P$, else $Q$ wears $r_Q$. The two colors on the field are always
distinct. Denote the color pair of game $(P,Q)$ as $\pi(P,Q) = \{h_P, g(P,Q)\}$
where $g(P,Q) \neq h_P$.

**Reformulation:** The condition "4 jerseys use ≥ 3 colors" for two games with 4
 distinct teams is equivalent to: *no two games with 4 distinct teams have the s
ame color pair* (since each game contributes 2 distinct colors, two games sharin
g the same pair use exactly 2 colors total).

**Road jerseys are only worn within a group.** $Q$ wears its road jersey iff $h_
Q = h_P$, i.e., $P$ and $Q$ share the same home color. So road jerseys appear on
ly in within-group games.

## Structural Lemma: At Most One Group Has Size ≥ 2

Partition teams into groups $G_c$ by home color $c$.

**Claim:** At most one group has size ≥ 2.

*Proof.* Suppose $|G_c| \geq 2$ and $|G_d| \geq 2$ with $c \neq d$. Pick $P_1 \n
eq P_2 \in G_c$ and $Q_1 \neq Q_2 \in G_d$. In game $(P_1, Q_1)$: $P_1$ wears $c
$, $Q_1$ wears $d$ (since $d \neq c$). Colors $= \{c, d\}$. In game $(P_2, Q_2)$
: same colors $\{c, d\}$. The 4 teams are distinct, but only 2 colors appear. Co
ntradiction. $\square$

## Lower Bound: At Least $n-1$ Colors

Let $k$ = number of distinct home colors. By the lemma, either all groups are si
ngletons ($k = n$, using $\geq n$ colors) or exactly one group $G_c$ has size $m
 \geq 2$ and the rest are singletons ($k = n - m + 1$).

**Case $m \geq 4$:** For any two distinct $Q_1, Q_2 \in G_c$, pick $P_1, P_2 \in
 G_c$ distinct from $Q_1, Q_2$ and each other (possible since $m \geq 4$). Games
 $(P_1, Q_1)$ and $(P_2, Q_2)$ have 4 distinct teams, both with pairs $\{c, r_{Q
_1}\}$ and $\{c, r_{Q_2}\}$. So $r_{Q_1} \neq r_{Q_2}$: **all $m$ road colors in
 $G_c$ are distinct**. Additionally, for any singleton $j$ and $Q \in G_c$: with
in-game $(P, Q)$ (with $P \in G_c \setminus \{Q\}$, $P$ distinct from $Q$ and fr
om some third team) and cross-game against singleton $j$ give 4 distinct teams w
ith pairs $\{c, r_Q\}$ and $\{c, d_j\}$, forcing $r_Q \neq d_j$. Also $r_Q \neq
c$. So all $m$ road colors are new. Total $\geq k + m = (n-m+1) + m = n+1$.

**Case $m = 3$:** $k = n - 2$. For any $Q \in G_c$ and singleton $j$: within-gam
e $(P, Q)$ (with $P \in G_c \setminus \{Q\}$) and cross-game $(e, j)$ (with $e \
in G_c \setminus \{P, Q\}$) give 4 distinct teams with pairs $\{c, r_Q\}$ and $\
{c, d_j\}$, forcing $r_Q \neq d_j$. Also $r_Q \neq c$. So each road color avoids
 all $k$ home colors, requiring $\geq 1$ new color. Total $\geq k + 1 = n - 1$.

**Case $m = 2$:** $k = n - 1$. Road colors $r_a, r_b \neq c$ but face no other c
onstraint (any cross-game vs within-game pair shares a team, so no 4-distinct-te
am conflict). Road colors can reuse existing home colors. Total $= k = n - 1$.

**Case all singletons ($m = 1$, $k = n$):** Total $\geq n$.

In every case, total $\geq n - 1$.

## Upper Bound: Construction with $n - 1$ Colors

Take $m = 2$: $G_c = \{1, 2\}$ with home color $c$ and road color $r_1 = r_2 = d
_3$ (reusing team 3's home color). Singletons $\{3, 4, \ldots, n\}$ with distinc
t home colors $d_3, d_4, \ldots, d_n$ (all $\neq c$, all $\neq$ each other), and
 road colors $r_j = c$ for $j \geq 3$ (valid since $d_j \neq c$).

**Total colors:** $\{c, d_3, d_4, \ldots, d_n\}$, which is $n - 1$ colors.

**Verification:** The color pairs that arise are:
- Within $G_c$: $(1,2)$ and $(2,1)$ give $\{c, d_3\}$.
- Cross $G_c$ vs singleton $j$: $(1,j), (2,j), (j,1), (j,2)$ all give $\{c, d_j\
}$.
- Cross singleton $j$ vs singleton $\ell$: $(j,\ell)$ gives $\{d_j, d_\ell\}$.

All pairs $\{c, d_j\}$ are distinct (since $d_j$ are distinct), and all pairs $\
{d_j, d_\ell\}$ are distinct (since all $d$'s are distinct).

For pair $\{c, d_j\}$: the games producing it involve only teams from $\{1, 2, j
\}$ (3 teams). Any two such games share a team by pigeonhole, so no two have 4 d
istinct teams.

For pair $\{d_j, d_\ell\}$: only game $(j, \ell)$ and $(\ell, j)$ produce it, in
volving just 2 teams.

For pairs $\{c, d_3\}$: games $(1,2), (2,1), (1,3), (2,3), (3,1), (3,2)$ — all i
nvolve teams from $\{1,2,3\}$, any two share a team.

No two games with 4 distinct teams share a color pair. The condition is satisfie
d.

## Conclusion

The lower bound is $n - 1$ and the upper bound is $n - 1$.

The least number of colors the $n$ teams' $2n$ jerseys may use is $\boxed{n - 1}
