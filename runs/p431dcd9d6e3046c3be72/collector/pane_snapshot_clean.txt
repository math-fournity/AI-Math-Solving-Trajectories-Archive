## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, such a function exists.

**Key fact:** $|\mathbb{R}| = |\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}| = \mathfrak{c}$ (th
e cardinality of the continuum). This is a standard result: $\mathfrak{c} = 2^{\
aleph_0}$, and $2^{\aleph_0} \cdot 2^{\aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_0 + \aleph_0} = 2^{\
aleph_0} = \mathfrak{c}$.

**Construction (via cardinality):**

Since $|\mathbb{R}| = |\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}|$, there exists a bijection
$$\phi: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}.$$

Define $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ by
$$f(x) = \pi_1(\phi(x)),$$
where $\pi_1: \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is the projection ont
o the first coordinate, i.e., $\pi_1(a, b) = a$.

**Verification that every fiber is uncountable:**

Fix any $y \in \mathbb{R}$. The fiber is:
$$f^{-1}(y) = \{x \in \mathbb{R} : f(x) = y\} = \{x \in \mathbb{R} : \pi_1(\phi(
x)) = y\} = \phi^{-1}\big(\{y\} \times \mathbb{R}\big).$$

Since $\phi$ is a bijection, it preserves cardinalities, so:
$$\left|f^{-1}(y)\right| = \left|\{y\} \times \mathbb{R}\right| = |\mathbb{R}| =
 \mathfrak{c}.$$

Since $\mathfrak{c} = 2^{\aleph_0} > \aleph_0$, each fiber $f^{-1}(y)$ is **unco
untable**.

This holds for every $y \in \mathbb{R}$, so $f$ satisfies the required property.
 $\blacksquare$
