Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest possible $M$ over all valid
 $m$ (having a prime factor $p > \sqrt{2m}+1$) and all valid sets $T$.

## Proof

**Step 1: The prime $p$ divides $m$ exactly once.**

Since $p \mid m$, write $m = pk$. The condition $p > \sqrt{2m}+1 = \sqrt{2pk}+1$
 gives $(p-1)^2 > 2pk = 2m$, so $p^2 > 2m \geq m$, meaning $p^2 \nmid m$. Theref
ore $p \| m$ (exactly one factor of $p$).

**Step 2: Lower bound $M \geq m + p$ for any valid $m$.**

Since $p \| m$, the prime $p$ appears to an **odd** power (exactly 1) in $m$. Fo
r the product of all elements of $T$ to be a perfect square, $p$ must appear to
an even total power. Since $m$ is the smallest element and all elements are dist
inct, every other element is $> m$. So some other element $a \in T$ with $a > m$
 must be divisible by $p$ to an odd power — in particular, $p \mid a$.

Since $m = pk$, the smallest multiple of $p$ strictly greater than $m$ is $p(k+1
) = m + p$. I verify this multiple has $p$ to an odd power: from $p^2 > 2pk$ we
get $p > 2k$, so $k < p/2$, hence $k+1 \leq \lceil p/2 \rceil < p$ (for $p \geq
3$), so $p \nmid (k+1)$, giving $p \| (m+p)$. ✓

Therefore $a \geq m+p$, so $M = \max(T) \geq m + p$.

**Step 3: Minimize $m + p = p(k+1)$ over all valid $m = pk$.**

We need $p$ prime, $k \geq 1$, and $p > \sqrt{2pk} + 1$.

- **$k=1$** ($m = p$): condition $p > \sqrt{2p}+1$.
  - $p=2$: $2 > 3$? No. $p=3$: $3 > \sqrt{6}+1 \approx 3.45$? No. $p=5$: $5 > \s
qrt{10}+1 \approx 4.16$? **Yes** → $m+p = 10$.
  - $p=7$: Yes → $m+p = 14$. (Larger.)

- **$k=2$** ($m = 2p$): condition $p > 2\sqrt{p}+1$.
  - $p=5$: $5 > 2\sqrt{5}+1 \approx 5.47$? No. $p=7$: $7 > 2\sqrt{7}+1 \approx 6
.29$? **Yes** → $m+p = 21$.

- **$k=3$** ($m = 3p$): condition $p > \sqrt{6p}+1$.
  - $p=7$: $7 > \sqrt{42}+1 \approx 7.48$? No. $p=11$: Yes → $m+p = 44$.

For $k \geq 2$, $m + p = p(k+1) \geq 7 \cdot 3 = 21 > 10$. For $k = 1$, the mini
mum is $p = 5$ giving $m + p = 10$.

**The global minimum of $m + p$ is $10$**, achieved at $m = 5$, $p = 5$.

**Step 4: $M = 10$ is achievable.**

Take $m = 5$ (valid: $5 > \sqrt{10}+1 \approx 4.16$ ✓) and $T = \{5, 8, 10\}$.

- $m = 5$ is the smallest element ✓
- $M = 10$ is the largest element ✓
- All elements are distinct ✓
- Product: $5 \cdot 8 \cdot 10 = 400 = 20^2$ is a perfect square ✓

**Step 5: No valid $m$ yields $M < 10$.**

From Step 2, $M \geq m + p \geq 10$ for every valid $m$ (Step 3). Combined with
Step 4, the minimum is exactly $10$.

$$\boxed{M = 10}$$
