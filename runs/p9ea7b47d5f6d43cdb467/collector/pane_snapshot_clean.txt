## Proof

We prove $A(m,s,t)=A(m,t,s)$ by showing the generating functions in $n$ coincide
.

**Step 1: A formula for the generating function.** Fix positive integers $k,r$.
A tuple counted by $A(n,k,r)$ is a partition $x_1\ge \cdots \ge x_k\ge 0$ of $n$
 with $x_1-x_k\le r$. Let $q=x_k\ge 0$ be the minimum part and write $x_i=q+a_i$
, so
$$a_1\ge a_2\ge \cdots \ge a_{k-1}\ge a_k=0,\qquad a_1=x_1-x_k\le r,\qquad \sum
a_i = n-kq.$$
Thus $(a_1,\dots,a_{k-1})$ is a partition of $n-kq$ into at most $k-1$ parts, ea
ch of size at most $r$. Conversely any such partition together with any $q\ge 0$
 reconstructs a unique tuple. Hence
$$A(n,k,r)=\sum_{q\ge 0} p(n-kq;\,\le k{-}1\text{ parts},\,\le r),$$
where $p(N;\le a\text{ parts},\le b)$ denotes the number of partitions of $N$ fi
tting inside an $a\times b$ rectangle.

**Step 2: Gaussian binomial.** It is standard (via conjugation of Ferrers diagra
ms inside a rectangle) that
$$\sum_{N\ge 0} p(N;\,\le a\text{ parts},\,\le b)\,x^N=\binom{a+b}{a}_x,$$
the Gaussian binomial coefficient, defined by
$$\binom{N}{K}_x=\prod_{i=1}^{K}\frac{1-x^{N-K+i}}{1-x^i}=\frac{(1-x^{N})(1-x^{N
-1})\cdots(1-x^{N-K+1})}{(1-x)(1-x^2)\cdots(1-x^K)},$$
which satisfies the symmetry $\binom{N}{K}_x=\binom{N}{N-K}_x$.

Therefore the generating function of $A(\cdot,k,r)$ is
$$F_{k,r}(x):=\sum_{n\ge 0}A(n,k,r)x^n=\frac{1}{1-x^k}\binom{r+k-1}{k-1}_x.$$

**Step 3: Symmetry $F_{s,t}=F_{t,s}$.** We must show
$$\frac{1}{1-x^s}\binom{s+t-1}{s-1}_x=\frac{1}{1-x^t}\binom{s+t-1}{t-1}_x.$$
By the symmetry $\binom{N}{K}_x=\binom{N}{N-K}_x$ with $N=s+t-1$:
$$\binom{s+t-1}{s-1}_x=\binom{s+t-1}{t}_x,\qquad \binom{s+t-1}{t-1}_x=\binom{s+t
-1}{s}_x.$$
Using the product formula,
$$\binom{s+t-1}{t}_x=\frac{\prod_{i=s}^{s+t-1}(1-x^i)}{\prod_{i=1}^{t}(1-x^i)},\
qquad \binom{s+t-1}{s}_x=\frac{\prod_{i=t}^{s+t-1}(1-x^i)}{\prod_{i=1}^{s}(1-x^i
)}.$$
Hence
$$\frac{1}{1-x^s}\binom{s+t-1}{t}_x=\frac{\prod_{i=s+1}^{s+t-1}(1-x^i)}{\prod_{i
=1}^{t}(1-x^i)},\qquad \frac{1}{1-x^t}\binom{s+t-1}{s}_x=\frac{\prod_{i=t+1}^{s+
t-1}(1-x^i)}{\prod_{i=1}^{s}(1-x^i)}.$$
To see these are equal, cross-multiply: the identity is equivalent to
$$\prod_{i=s+1}^{s+t-1}(1-x^i)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{s}(1-x^i)=\prod_{i=t+1}^{s+t-1}(
1-x^i)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{t}(1-x^i).$$
Both sides equal $\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{s+t-1}(1-x^i)$: the left side is $\p
rod_{i=1}^{s}\cdot\prod_{i=s+1}^{s+t-1}$ and the right side is $\prod_{i=1}^{t}\
cdot\prod_{i=t+1}^{s+t-1}$, each a partition of the index set $\{1,2,\dots,s+t-1
\}$ into two consecutive blocks. Thus $F_{s,t}(x)=F_{t,s}(x)$ as formal power se
ries.

**Step 4: Conclusion.** Since the generating functions agree, their coefficients
 agree: for every $n\ge 0$,
$$A(n,s,t)=A(n,t,s).$$
In particular for every positive integer $m$, $A(m,s,t)=A(m,t,s)$. $\blacksquare
$
