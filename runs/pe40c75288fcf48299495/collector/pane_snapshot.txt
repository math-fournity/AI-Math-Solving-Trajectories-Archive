## Proof

Let $S_n = \xi_1 + \cdots + \xi_n$. By definition,
$$X_t = \inf\{n \ge 1 : S_n > t\},$$
so $X_t$ is the first index at which the partial sums exceed $t$. Equivalently,
$$S_{X_t - 1} \le t < S_{X_t}. \tag{1}$$

**Step 1: $X_t \to \infty$ a.s. as $t \to \infty$.**

Since $\xi_i \ge 0$ and $E[\xi_i] = 1/\lambda < \infty$, the strong law of large
 numbers gives
$$\frac{S_n}{n} \to \frac{1}{\lambda} \quad \text{a.s.},$$
hence $S_n \to \infty$ a.s. For any fixed $N$, $S_N$ is finite a.s., so for all
$t > S_N$ we have $X_t > N$. Thus $X_t \to \infty$ a.s.

**Step 2: Apply SLLN along the random subsequence $n = X_t$ and $n = X_t - 1$.**

Since $X_t \to \infty$ a.s. and $S_n/n \to 1/\lambda$ a.s., by the standard fact
 that a convergent sequence remains convergent along any subsequence tending to
infinity,
$$\frac{S_{X_t}}{X_t} \to \frac{1}{\lambda}, \qquad \frac{S_{X_t-1}}{X_t - 1} \t
o \frac{1}{\lambda} \quad \text{a.s. as } t \to \infty. \tag{2}$$

**Step 3: Sandwich $X_t/t$.**

From the right inequality in (1), $t < S_{X_t}$, so
$$\frac{X_t}{t} > \frac{X_t}{S_{X_t}} = \frac{1}{S_{X_t}/X_t}.$$
Taking $\liminf_{t\to\infty}$ and using (2):
$$\liminf_{t\to\infty} \frac{X_t}{t} \ge \frac{1}{\lim_{t\to\infty} S_{X_t}/X_t}
 = \lambda. \tag{3}$$

From the left inequality in (1), $S_{X_t - 1} \le t$, so
$$\frac{X_t - 1}{t} \le \frac{X_t - 1}{S_{X_t-1}} = \frac{1}{S_{X_t-1}/(X_t-1)}.
$$
Taking $\limsup_{t\to\infty}$ and using (2):
$$\limsup_{t\to\infty} \frac{X_t - 1}{t} \le \lambda.$$
Since $\frac{X_t}{t} = \frac{X_t - 1}{t} + \frac{1}{t}$ and $1/t \to 0$,
$$\limsup_{t\to\infty} \frac{X_t}{t} \le \lambda. \tag{4}$$

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Combining (3) and (4):
$$\lambda \le \liminf_{t\to\infty} \frac{X_t}{t} \le \limsup_{t\to\infty} \frac{
X_t}{t} \le \lambda,$$
so the limit exists and
$$\frac{X_t}{t} \to \lambda \quad \text{a.s. as } t \to \infty.$$

**Interpretation.** $X_t$ counts the number of i.i.d. $\mathrm{Exp}(\lambda)$ ar
rivals needed to exceed level $t$; this is the Poisson process $N(t)$ counting a
rrivals up to time $t$ (in fact $X_t = N(t) + 1$ where $N(t)$ is a Poisson proce
ss with rate $\lambda$). The result $\lim X_t/t = \lambda$ a.s. is the a.s. vers
ion of the elementary renewal theorem, consistent with $N(t)/t \to \lambda$ a.s.

The almost sure limit is $\boxed{\lambda}$.
