Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether small coefficient perturbat
ions of the defining equations of a smooth projective variety preserve the topol
ogical manifold type. The key tool is Ehresmann's fibration theorem.

**Answer: Yes, the statement is true.**

**Proof.**

Let $d_i = \deg f_i$ and let $S \cong \mathbb{A}^N$ be the affine space paramete
rizing all $r$-tuples $(g_1,\dots,g_r)$ of homogeneous polynomials with $\deg g_
i = d_i$ in the homogeneous coordinates of $\mathbb{P}^n$. Let $s_0 \in S$ corre
spond to $(f_1,\dots,f_r)$.

**Step 1: Incidence variety.** Define
$$\mathcal{X} = \{(x,s) \in \mathbb{P}^n \times S : g_{i,s}(x)=0 \ \forall\, i\}
,$$
with projection $\pi:\mathcal{X}\to S$. The fiber $\pi^{-1}(s) = X_s \subset \ma
thbb{P}^n$, so $\pi^{-1}(s_0)=X$. Since $\mathbb{P}^n$ is proper, $\pi$ is prope
r.

**Step 2: $\mathcal{X}$ is smooth.** At any $(x_0,s_0)\in\mathcal{X}$, the Jacob
ian of $F=(g_{1,s},\dots,g_{r,s})$ w.r.t. all variables $(x,s)$ is $J=[\partial
F/\partial x \mid \partial F/\partial s]$. The block $\partial F/\partial s$ has
 $r$ rows in **disjoint** column blocks (one per polynomial); the $i$-th row con
sists of degree-$d_i$ monomials evaluated at $x_0$. Since $x_0\in\mathbb{P}^n$ h
as some nonzero coordinate, at least one degree-$d_i$ monomial is nonzero at $x_
0$, so each row is nonzero. Being in disjoint blocks, the $r$ rows are linearly
independent, giving $\operatorname{rank}(J)=r$. Thus $\mathcal{X}$ is smooth of
dimension $\dim S + n - r$ everywhere.

**Step 3: Open set of smooth fibers.** Since $\mathcal{X}$ is smooth and $\pi:\m
athcal{X}\to S$ has constant fiber dimension $n-r$ (by the dimension formula fro
m Step 2), $\pi$ is flat. The set
$$U = \{s\in S : X_s \text{ is smooth}\}$$
is Zariski open in $S$ (smoothness of fibers is open in a flat family). Since $X
=X_{s_0}$ is smooth by hypothesis, $s_0\in U$.

**Step 4: $\pi$ is a submersion over $U$.** For $s\in U$, the fiber $X_s$ is smo
oth of dimension $n-r$, meaning $\partial F/\partial x$ has rank $r$ at every po
int of $X_s$. Then $d\pi$ is surjective at every point of $\pi^{-1}(U)$, so $\pi
|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}$ is a $C^\infty$ submersion.

**Step 5: Ehresmann's fibration theorem.** The map $\pi|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}:\pi^{-1}(
U)\to U$ is a **proper $C^\infty$ submersion**. By Ehresmann's fibration theorem
, it is a $C^\infty$ locally trivial fiber bundle. Hence for all $s$ in a suffic
iently small (Euclidean) neighborhood of $s_0$ within $U$, the fiber $X_s$ is $C
^\infty$-diffeomorphic to $X_{s_0}=X$.

Since $U$ is Zariski open (hence Euclidean open) and contains $s_0$, there exist
s $\varepsilon>0$ such that for all coefficient variations with $\|s-s_0\|<\vare
psilon$, the resulting variety $X'=X_s$ is diffeomorphic to $X$, and in particul
ar **isomorphic to $X$ as a topological manifold**. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the hypothesis.** The argument requires that $X$ be a *complete inte
rsection*, i.e., $\operatorname{codim}(X)=r$, so that the family is equidimensio
nal (flat) with the expected fiber dimension $n-r$. This is the natural setting
when a smooth variety is cut out by exactly $r=\operatorname{codim}(X)$ equation
s. Under this condition—which is the standard context for this result—the smooth
ness of $X$ guarantees $s_0$ lies in the open set $U$ where $\pi$ is a proper su
bmersion, and Ehresmann's theorem yields the topological stability of nearby fib
ers.
