## Solution

**Interpreting the expression.** The quantity to compute is
$$\det\!\bigl(\operatorname{adj}(A^{-1}\,B^{-1}\,2B^{T})\bigr).$$

Let $M = A^{-1}\,B^{-1}\,2B^{T} = 2\,A^{-1}\,B^{-1}\,B^{T}$.

---

**Step 1: Reduce using the adjugate identity.**

For an $n\times n$ matrix, $\det(\operatorname{adj}(M)) = \det(M)^{n-1}$. Here $
n=3$, so
$$\det(\operatorname{adj}(M)) = \det(M)^{2}.$$

---

**Step 2: Compute $\det(M)$.**

$$\det(M) = \det(2\,A^{-1}\,B^{-1}\,B^{T}) = 2^{3}\,\det(A^{-1})\,\det(B^{-1})\,
\det(B^{T}).$$

Since $\det(B^{T}) = \det(B)$ and $\det(B^{-1}) = \dfrac{1}{\det(B)}$, the $B$-d
ependent factors cancel:
$$\det(B^{-1})\,\det(B^{T}) = \frac{1}{\det(B)}\cdot\det(B) = 1.$$

Therefore
$$\det(M) = 8\cdot\det(A^{-1}) = \frac{8}{\det(A)}.$$

(The constraint $AA^{T}A + 3B^{-1}=0$ guarantees $B$ exists and is invertible, b
ut its specific value is irrelevant here since the $B$-factors cancel.)

---

**Step 3: Compute $\det(A)$.**

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & 4\\ 3 & 6 & 9\\ 1 & 6 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$$

Expanding along the first row:
$$\det(A) = 1\,(6\cdot4 - 9\cdot6) - 3\,(3\cdot4 - 9\cdot1) + 4\,(3\cdot6 - 6\cd
ot1)$$
$$= 1\,(24-54) - 3\,(12-9) + 4\,(18-6)$$
$$= -30 - 9 + 48 = 9.$$

So $\det(A) = 9 \neq 0$, confirming $A$ (and hence $B$) is invertible.

---

**Step 4: Final computation.**

$$\det(M) = \frac{8}{9}, \qquad \det(\operatorname{adj}(M)) = \left(\frac{8}{9}\
right)^{2} = \frac{64}{81}.$$

---

**Answer:**
$$\boxed{\dfrac{64}{81}}$$
