No. A counterexample exists.

**Claim:** There exists a group $G$ and a non-identity automorphism $\varphi\in\
operatorname{Aut}(G)$ such that $\varphi$ fixes every maximal subgroup of $G$ (e
ven pointwise).

**Proof.** Let $G=\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}=\{\bar 0,\bar 1,\bar 2,\bar 3\}$, the c
yclic group of order $4$.

The subgroups of $G$ are $\{\bar 0\}$, $\{\bar 0,\bar 2\}$, and $G$ itself. The
only maximal (proper) subgroup is $M=\{\bar 0,\bar 2\}=2\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$,
 which has index $2$.

Define $\varphi:G\to G$ by $\varphi(\bar x)=-\bar x=3\bar x\pmod 4$. Since $\gcd
(3,4)=1$, this is an automorphism of $G$. It is non-trivial, since $\varphi(\bar
 1)=\bar 3\neq\bar 1$.

Now check the action on the unique maximal subgroup:
$$\varphi(M)=\{\varphi(\bar 0),\varphi(\bar 2)\}=\{\bar 0,\,-\bar 2\}=\{\bar 0,\
bar 2\}=M.$$
In fact $\varphi$ fixes $M$ **pointwise**: $\varphi(\bar 0)=\bar 0$ and $\varphi
(\bar 2)=-\bar 2\equiv\bar 2\pmod 4$.

Thus $\varphi$ fixes every maximal subgroup of $G$ (pointwise, hence also setwis
e), yet $\varphi\neq\operatorname{id}_G$.

Therefore, it is **not** true in general that an automorphism fixing all maximal
 subgroups must be the identity. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** This generalizes: for $G=\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$ with $k\ge 2$, th
e unique maximal subgroup is $p\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$, and every automorphism
 $x\mapsto ux$ ($u$ a unit) fixes it setwise; those with $u\equiv 1\pmod p$ fix
it pointwise, yet are non-identity whenever $u\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^k}$.
