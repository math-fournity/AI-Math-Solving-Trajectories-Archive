## Proof

The **Weierstrass product representation** of the Gamma function is

$$\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)} = z\,e^{\gamma z}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{z}{n}\r
ight)e^{-z/n},$$

where $\gamma$ denotes the Euler–Mascheroni constant.

---

### Step 1. Take the logarithm

Taking $\ln$ of both sides (valid for $z>0$, and by analytic continuation elsewh
ere off the poles):

$$-\ln\Gamma(z) = \ln z + \gamma z + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\ln\!\left(1+\frac
{z}{n}\right) - \frac{z}{n}\right].$$

The series converges absolutely and uniformly on compact sets because each summa
nd is $O(z^2/n^2)$.

---

### Step 2. Differentiate term-by-term

Differentiating with respect to $z$ (justified by the uniform convergence above)
:

$$-\frac{\Gamma'(z)}{\Gamma(z)} = \frac{1}{z} + \gamma + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lef
t[\frac{1}{n+z} - \frac{1}{n}\right].$$

Hence the **digamma function** admits the representation

$$\frac{\Gamma'(z)}{\Gamma(z)} = -\frac{1}{z} - \gamma - \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lef
t[\frac{1}{n+z} - \frac{1}{n}\right].$$

---

### Step 3. Evaluate at $z=1$

Since $\Gamma(1)=1$, we have $\Gamma'(1)/\Gamma(1)=\Gamma'(1)$. Substituting $z=
1$:

$$\Gamma'(1) = -1 - \gamma - \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{n+1} - \frac{1}{n
}\right].$$

The remaining series **telescopes**. Writing the partial sums with $N$ terms:

$$\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left[\frac{1}{n+1} - \frac{1}{n}\right] = \left(\frac{1}{2}+\fr
ac{1}{3}+\cdots+\frac{1}{N+1}\right) - \left(1+\frac{1}{2}+\cdots+\frac{1}{N}\ri
ght) = \frac{1}{N+1} - 1.$$

Taking $N\to\infty$:

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{n+1} - \frac{1}{n}\right] = -1.$$

---

### Step 4. Conclude

Substituting back:

$$\Gamma'(1) = -1 - \gamma - (-1) = -\gamma.$$

Therefore the **alternative expression** for the derivative of the Gamma functio
n at $z=1$, derived from the Weierstrass product, is

$$\boxed{\;\Gamma'(1) = -\gamma = -\lim_{N\to\infty}\!\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{
1}{n} - \ln N\right)\;}$$

where the second equality is the defining limit of the Euler–Mascheroni constant
. This connects the logarithmic derivative of the Weierstrass product directly t
o the classical harmonic-number limit definition of $\gamma$.
