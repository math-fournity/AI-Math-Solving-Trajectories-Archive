## Proof

We evaluate
$$I(a) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{iax}}{x^2+1}\,dx, \qquad a\in\mathbb{R
},$$
by contour integration, treating the cases $a>0$, $a<0$, and $a=0$ separately.

---

### Case 1: $a > 0$

Consider the meromorphic function
$$f(z) = \frac{e^{iaz}}{z^2+1} = \frac{e^{iaz}}{(z-i)(z+i)}.$$
Its poles are at $z = i$ and $z = -i$, both simple.

Let $C_R$ be the closed contour consisting of the segment $[-R,R]$ on the real a
xis followed by the upper semicircle $\Gamma_R = \{Re^{i\theta}: 0\le\theta\le\p
i\}$, traversed counterclockwise. For $R>1$, the only pole enclosed is $z=i$.

**Residue at $z=i$:**
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=i} f(z) = \lim_{z\to i}(z-i)\frac{e^{iaz}}{(z-i)(z+i)} =
 \frac{e^{ia\cdot i}}{2i} = \frac{e^{-a}}{2i}.$$

By the residue theorem,
$$\oint_{C_R} f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i\cdot \frac{e^{-a}}{2i} = \pi e^{-a}.$$

**Estimate on $\Gamma_R$:** For $z = Re^{i\theta}$ with $\theta\in[0,\pi]$, we h
ave $\operatorname{Im}z = R\sin\theta\ge 0$, so
$$|e^{iaz}| = e^{-a\,\operatorname{Im}z} = e^{-aR\sin\theta}\le 1 \quad (\text{s
ince } a>0).$$
Hence
$$\left|\int_{\Gamma_R} f(z)\,dz\right| \le \int_0^{\pi} \frac{e^{-aR\sin\theta}
}{R^2-1}\,R\,d\theta \le \frac{\pi R}{R^2-1}\xrightarrow[R\to\infty]{}0.$$

Therefore
$$I(a) = \lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{-R}^{R} f(x)\,dx = \pi e^{-a}, \qquad a>0.$$

---

### Case 2: $a < 0$

Now $a<0$, so $e^{iaz}$ decays in the **lower** half-plane. Take the contour con
sisting of $[-R,R]$ followed by the **lower** semicircle $\Gamma_R^- = \{Re^{-i\
theta}:0\le\theta\le\pi\}$, traversed **clockwise**. The enclosed pole is $z=-i$
.

**Residue at $z=-i$:**
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=-i} f(z) = \frac{e^{ia(-i)}}{-2i} = \frac{e^{a}}{-2i}.$$

Since the contour is clockwise, the residue theorem gives a minus sign:
$$\oint_{C_R^-} f(z)\,dz = -2\pi i\cdot \frac{e^{a}}{-2i} = \pi e^{a}.$$

**Estimate on $\Gamma_R^-$:** For $z=Re^{i\theta}$ with $\theta\in[\pi,2\pi]$, $
\operatorname{Im}z = R\sin\theta\le 0$, so $-a\,\operatorname{Im}z = |a|\,|\oper
atorname{Im}z|\ge 0$ and $|e^{iaz}| = e^{-a\operatorname{Im}z}\le 1$. The same b
ound $\frac{\pi R}{R^2-1}\to 0$ applies.

Thus
$$I(a) = \pi e^{a} = \pi e^{-|a|}, \qquad a<0.$$

---

### Case 3: $a = 0$

$$I(0) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x^2+1} = \Big[\arctan x\Big]_{-\infty}
^{\infty} = \pi = \pi e^{0} = \pi e^{-|a|}.$$

---

### Conclusion

Combining all three cases,
$$\boxed{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{iax}}{x^2+1}\,dx = \pi\, e^{-|a|}, \qq
uad a\in\mathbb{R}.}$$
