## 2. Separation of Variables — Spatial Eigenvalue Problem

Set $u(x,t)=X(x)\,T(t)$. Substituting into

$$u_{tt}=c^{2}\,u_{xx}-\gamma\,u_{t},\qquad c=\tfrac14,\;\gamma=\tfrac15,$$

and dividing by $XT$:

$$\frac{T''+\gamma T'}{c^{2}T}=\frac{X''}{X}=-\mu^{2}.$$

**Spatial ODE and boundary conditions:**

$$X''+\mu^{2}X=0,\qquad X'(0)-X(0)=0,\qquad X(1)=0.$$

General solution $X=A\cos\mu x+B\sin\mu x$.

- $X'(0)-X(0)=B\mu-A=0\;\Longrightarrow\;B=A/\mu$.
- $X(1)=A\cos\mu+\frac{A}{\mu}\sin\mu=0\;\Longrightarrow\;\mu\cos\mu+\sin\mu=0$.

**Eigenvalue equation:**

$$\boxed{\tan\mu=-\mu.}$$

$\mu=0$ gives only the trivial solution (check: $X''=0$, $X=Ax+B$, $A=B$, $X(1)=
2A=0$), so $\mu=0$ is **not** an eigenvalue. The positive roots $\mu_n$ ($n=1,2,
3,\dots$) lie in the intervals $\bigl((n-\tfrac12)\pi,\;n\pi\bigr)$:

| $n$ | $\mu_n$ (approx) | $\cos\mu_n$ (approx) | $\omega_n$ (approx) |
|-----|-------------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| 1   | 2.0288            | −0.444               | 0.497                |
| 2   | 4.9132            |  0.198               | 1.224                |
| 3   | 7.9787            | −0.125               | 1.992                |
| 4   | 11.0855           |  0.090               | 2.771                |
| 5   | 14.2074           | −0.070               | 3.551                |

For large $n$: $\;\mu_n\approx\bigl(n-\tfrac12\bigr)\pi+\dfrac{1}{(n-\tfrac12)\p
i}$.

**Unnormalized eigenfunctions:**

$$\psi_n(x)=\cos\mu_n x+\frac{1}{\mu_n}\sin\mu_n x.$$

---

## 3. Orthonormalization

Compute $N_n^2=\int_0^1\psi_n^2\,dx$. Using $\int_0^1\cos^2=\tfrac12+\frac{\sin2
\mu}{4\mu}$, $\int_0^1\sin^2=\tfrac12-\frac{\sin2\mu}{4\mu}$, $\int_0^1\cos\sin=
\frac{\sin^2\mu}{2\mu}$, and the eigenvalue relation $\sin\mu=-\mu\cos\mu$ (so $
\sin2\mu=-2\mu\cos^2\mu$, $\sin^2\mu=\mu^2\cos^2\mu$):

$$N_n^2=\frac{(1+\cos^2\mu_n)(\mu_n^2+1)}{2\mu_n^2}.$$

**Orthonormal eigenfunctions:**

$$\boxed{\varphi_n(x)=\frac{\cos\mu_n x+\frac{1}{\mu_n}\sin\mu_n x}{N_n},\qquad
N_n=\sqrt{\frac{(1+\cos^2\mu_n)(\mu_n^2+1)}{2\mu_n^2}}.}$$

One verifies $\int_0^1\varphi_m\varphi_n\,dx=\delta_{mn}$.

---

## 4. Temporal Equation — Damped Oscillation

$$T_n''+\gamma\,T_n'+c^2\mu_n^2\,T_n=0,\qquad \gamma=\tfrac15,\;c=\tfrac14.$$

Characteristic roots: $r=-\frac{\gamma}{2}\pm\sqrt{\frac{\gamma^2}{4}-c^2\mu_n^2
}$.

Since $\mu_1\approx2.03>\frac{\gamma}{2c}=\frac{2}{5}$, **every** mode is underd
amped. Define

$$\alpha=\frac{\gamma}{2}=\frac{1}{10},\qquad \omega_n=\sqrt{c^2\mu_n^2-\frac{\g
amma^2}{4}}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_n^2}{16}-\frac{1}{100}}.$$

$$T_n(t)=e^{-\alpha t}\bigl[a_n\cos\omega_n t+b_n\sin\omega_n t\bigr].$$

---

## 5. General Solution

$$\boxed{u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-t/10}\Bigl[A_n\cos\omega_n t+B_n\sin\omeg
a_n t\Bigr]\,\varphi_n(x),}$$

with coefficients determined by the initial data:

$$A_n=\int_0^1 f(x)\,\varphi_n(x)\,dx,\qquad -\alpha A_n+\omega_n B_n=\int_0^1 g
(x)\,\varphi_n(x)\,dx\equiv G_n,$$

i.e.,

$$\boxed{A_n=\frac{I_n(f)}{N_n},\qquad B_n=\frac{G_n+\frac{1}{10}A_n}{\omega_n}=
\frac{I_n(g)+\frac{1}{10}I_n(f)}{N_n\,\omega_n},}$$

where $I_n(h)=\int_0^1 h(x)\,\psi_n(x)\,dx$.

---

## 6. Useful Integrals (using $\sin\mu_n=-\mu_n\cos\mu_n$)

Let $c_n=\cos\mu_n$.

$$J_0\equiv\int_0^1\psi_n\,dx=-c_n+\frac{1-c_n}{\mu_n^2}.$$

$$J_1\equiv\int_0^1 x\,\psi_n\,dx=-c_n\!\left(1+\frac{1}{\mu_n^2}\right)-\frac{1
}{\mu_n^2}.$$

$$J_2\equiv\int_0^1 x^2\,\psi_n\,dx=-c_n+\frac{c_n}{\mu_n^2}+\frac{2(c_n-1)}{\mu
_n^4}.$$

---

## 7. Case 1: $f_1(x)=1,\;g_1(x)=0$

$$I_n(f_1)=J_0=-c_n+\frac{1-c_n}{\mu_n^2}.$$

$$\boxed{A_n^{(1)}=\frac{-c_n+\frac{1-c_n}{\mu_n^2}}{N_n},\qquad B_n^{(1)}=\frac
{1}{10}\,\frac{A_n^{(1)}}{\omega_n}.}$$

$$u_1(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-t/10}\,A_n^{(1)}\!\left[\cos\omega_n t+\frac{1
}{10\omega_n}\sin\omega_n t\right]\varphi_n(x).$$

*Physical behavior:* The constant initial displacement $f_1=1$ does **not** sati
sfy the right BC $u(1)=0$, so all modes are excited. The solution is a superposi
tion of damped oscillations decaying as $e^{-t/10}$; the bar relaxes toward equi
librium $u\equiv0$.

---

## 8. Case 2: $f_2(x)=1-x,\;g_2(x)=1$

$$I_n(f_2)=\int_0^1(1-x)\psi_n\,dx=\frac{1-c_n}{\mu_n^2}+\frac{1}{\mu_n^2}-\frac
{\sin\mu_n}{\mu_n^3}=\frac{1-c_n}{\mu_n^2}+\frac{1}{\mu_n^2}+\frac{c_n}{\mu_n^2}
=\frac{2}{\mu_n^2}.$$

(The last step uses $\sin\mu_n=-\mu_n c_n$.) This is a remarkably clean result.

$$I_n(g_2)=I_n(f_1)=J_0=-c_n+\frac{1-c_n}{\mu_n^2}.$$

$$\boxed{A_n^{(2)}=\frac{2/\mu_n^2}{N_n},\qquad B_n^{(2)}=\frac{-c_n+\frac{1-c_n
}{\mu_n^2}+\frac{2}{10\,\mu_n^2}}{N_n\,\omega_n}=\frac{-c_n+\frac{6/5-c_n}{\mu_n
^2}}{N_n\,\omega_n}.}$$

$$u_2(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-t/10}\left[A_n^{(2)}\cos\omega_n t+B_n^{(2)}\s
in\omega_n t\right]\varphi_n(x).$$

*Physical behavior:* $f_2=1-x$ satisfies $u(1)=0$ but not the left Robin conditi
on ($f_2'(0)-f_2(0)=-1\neq0$). The nonzero initial velocity $g_2=1$ injects addi
tional energy. Both displacement and velocity contribute to every mode. The solu
tion oscillates and decays.

---

## 9. Case 3: $f_3(x)=-\frac{2x^2}{3}+\frac{x}{3}+\frac{1}{3},\;g_3(x)=0$

**Verification that $f_3$ satisfies both BCs:**

$$f_3'(x)=-\frac{4x}{3}+\frac{1}{3},\qquad f_3'(0)-f_3(0)=\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{3
}=0\;\checkmark,\qquad f_3(1)=-\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{3}=0\;\checkmark
.$$

$$I_n(f_3)=-\frac{2}{3}J_2+\frac{1}{3}J_1+\frac{1}{3}J_0.$$

Substituting and collecting (the $c_n$ and $1/\mu_n^2$ terms cancel exactly):

$$I_n(f_3)=\frac{4}{3}\!\left[\frac{1-c_n}{\mu_n^4}-\frac{c_n}{\mu_n^2}\right]=\
frac{4}{3}\cdot\frac{1-c_n-\mu_n^2 c_n}{\mu_n^4}.$$

$$\boxed{A_n^{(3)}=\frac{4}{3}\cdot\frac{1-c_n-\mu_n^2 c_n}{\mu_n^4\,N_n},\qquad
 B_n^{(3)}=\frac{1}{10}\,\frac{A_n^{(3)}}{\omega_n}.}$$

$$u_3(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-t/10}\,A_n^{(3)}\!\left[\cos\omega_n t+\frac{1
}{10\omega_n}\sin\omega_n t\right]\varphi_n(x).$$

*Physical behavior:* Since $f_3$ satisfies both boundary conditions exactly, the
 initial profile is "compatible." The coefficients $A_n^{(3)}$ decay faster ($\s
im 1/\mu_n^4$ for large $n$, since $c_n\to0$) than in Cases 1–2, so the series c
onverges rapidly and the solution is dominated by the first few modes. The motio
n is a gentle damped oscillation returning to rest.

## 11. Animated Solution Description ($0<t<5$)

For all three cases the solution has the form $u=\sum e^{-t/10}[\cdots]\varphi_n
(x)$, so the envelope decays as $e^{-t/10}$. At $t=5$ the envelope is $e^{-1/2}\
approx0.607$, meaning roughly 39% of the initial energy has been dissipated.

- **Case 1** ($f=1$, $g=0$): The flat initial profile (violating $u(1)=0$) excit
es many modes. The right end is pinned at zero from $t=0^+$, creating a sharp tr
ansient. The wave oscillates at the fundamental frequency $\omega_1\approx0.497$
 (period $\approx12.6$), so within $0<t<5$ less than half a fundamental cycle co
mpletes; higher modes produce visible ripples that die out first. The profile sm
oothly relaxes toward $u\equiv0$.

- **Case 2** ($f=1-x$, $g=1$): The linear ramp satisfying the right BC but not t
he left Robin condition, combined with uniform initial velocity, excites all mod
es with $A_n^{(2)}\propto1/\mu_n^2$. The bar oscillates more vigorously than Cas
e 1 due to the added kinetic energy from $g_2=1$. Superposed damped sinusoids at
 frequencies $\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3,\dots$ create a traveling-wave-like pat
tern that gradually settles.

- **Case 3** ($f_3$ polynomial, $g=0$): The BC-compatible initial profile yields
 coefficients $\propto1/\mu_n^4$, so the series is dominated by $\mu_1$ ($\omega
_1\approx0.497$). The motion is nearly a single-mode damped sinusoid: the parabo
lic initial shape oscillates gently and decays, with minimal higher-mode content
. This is the smoothest of the three animations.
