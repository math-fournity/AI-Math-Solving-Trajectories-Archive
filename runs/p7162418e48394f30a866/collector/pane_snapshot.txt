## Proof

**Claim.** Let $M$ be a smooth manifold equipped with a Riemannian metric $g$, a
nd let $d_M$ be the induced Riemannian distance
$$d_M(p,q)=\inf\bigl\{L(\gamma):\gamma\text{ a piecewise-smooth curve from }p\te
xt{ to }q\bigr\},\qquad L(\gamma)=\int_a^b\sqrt{g_{\gamma(t)}(\dot\gamma(t),\dot
\gamma(t))}\,dt.$$
Then the metric topology $\tau_{d_M}$ on $M$ coincides with the manifold topolog
y $\tau_{\text{man}}$.

We prove both inclusions by working in a single coordinate chart and comparing $
d_M$ against the Euclidean distance in coordinates.

---

### Local comparison lemma

Let $(U,\varphi)$ be a coordinate chart centered at $p\in M$, and write $g_{ij}$
 for the components of $g$ in these coordinates. Since $g$ is a smooth section o
f $S^2T^*M$, each $g_{ij}$ is smooth, hence continuous, and $g_{ij}(x)$ is symme
tric positive-definite for every $x$.

Choose $\rho>0$ small enough that the closed coordinate ball $\overline{B_\rho^{
\text{e}}(0)}=\{x:\|x\|\le\rho\}\subseteq\varphi(U)$ is compact. By compactness
and continuity, the eigenvalues of $(g_{ij}(x))$ on $\overline{B_\rho^{\text{e}}
(0)}$ are bounded above and below:
$$0<c\le\lambda_{\min}(x)\le\lambda_{\max}(x)\le C\qquad\forall\,x\in\overline{B
_\rho^{\text{e}}(0)},$$
for constants $c,C$ with $0<c\le C<\infty$. Consequently, for every tangent vect
or $v$ at a point of $\overline{B_\rho^{\text{e}}(0)}$,
$$c\,\|v\|_{\text{e}}^2\;\le\;g(v,v)\;\le\;C\,\|v\|_{\text{e}}^2,\tag{$\star$}$$
where $\|\cdot\|_{\text{e}}$ is the coordinate Euclidean norm.

### Inclusion $\tau_{\text{man}}\subseteq\tau_{d_M}$

Let $W\in\tau_{\text{man}}$ and $p\in W$. Pick a coordinate chart $(U,\varphi)$
about $p$ and $\rho>0$ as above with the coordinate ball $B_\rho^{\text{e}}(p)\s
ubseteq W$. Let $c>0$ be the lower bound from $(\star)$.

We show $B_{d_M}\!\bigl(p,\sqrt{c}\,\rho\bigr)\subseteq W$. Take $q$ with $d_M(p
,q)<\sqrt{c}\,\rho$, and let $\gamma$ be any piecewise-smooth curve from $p$ to
$q$ with $L(\gamma)<\sqrt{c}\,\rho$.

- If $\gamma$ stays inside $B_\rho^{\text{e}}(p)$ throughout, then by the left i
nequality in $(\star)$,
$$L(\gamma)\ge\sqrt{c}\;L_{\text{e}}(\gamma)\ge\sqrt{c}\,\| \varphi(q)-\varphi(p
)\|,$$
so $\|\varphi(q)-\varphi(p)\|<\rho$, i.e. $q\in B_\rho^{\text{e}}(p)\subseteq W$
.

- If $\gamma$ exits $B_\rho^{\text{e}}(p)$, then some sub-arc of $\gamma$ travel
s from $p$ to the coordinate sphere $\partial B_\rho^{\text{e}}(p)$ while stayin
g in $\overline{B_\rho^{\text{e}}(p)}$, so by $(\star)$ its length is at least $
\sqrt{c}\,\rho$. Hence $L(\gamma)\ge\sqrt{c}\,\rho$, contradicting $L(\gamma)<\s
qrt{c}\,\rho$.

Thus every such $\gamma$ stays in $B_\rho^{\text{e}}(p)$, giving $q\in W$. There
fore
$$B_{d_M}\!\bigl(p,\sqrt{c}\,\rho\bigr)\subseteq W,$$
so $W$ is $d_M$-open. Hence $\tau_{\text{man}}\subseteq\tau_{d_M}$.

### Inclusion $\tau_{d_M}\subseteq\tau_{\text{man}}$

Let $B_{d_M}(p,\varepsilon)$ be a $d_M$-ball and $p\in B_{d_M}(p,\varepsilon)$.
Choose a chart $(U,\varphi)$ about $p$ and $\rho>0$ as above, with upper bound $
C$ from $(\star)$.

For any $q\in B_\rho^{\text{e}}(p)$, the coordinate straight-line segment $\sigm
a(t)=\varphi(p)+t(\varphi(q)-\varphi(p))$, $t\in[0,1]$, is a smooth curve from $
p$ to $q$ lying in $B_\rho^{\text{e}}(p)$. By the right inequality in $(\star)$,
$$L(\sigma)\le\sqrt{C}\,\|\varphi(q)-\varphi(p)\|.$$
Hence
$$d_M(p,q)\le L(\sigma)\le\sqrt{C}\,\|\varphi(q)-\varphi(p)\|.$$
Therefore
$$B_{\text{e}}\!\bigl(p,\,\varepsilon/\sqrt{C}\bigr)\;\subseteq\;B_{d_M}(p,\vare
psilon),$$
and (shrinking $\rho$ if necessary so $\varepsilon/\sqrt{C}\le\rho$) the left-ha
nd side is a coordinate ball, hence manifold-open. So every $d_M$-ball contains
a manifold-open neighborhood of each of its points, i.e. $B_{d_M}(p,\varepsilon)
\in\tau_{\text{man}}$. Hence $\tau_{d_M}\subseteq\tau_{\text{man}}$.

---

### Conclusion

Both inclusions hold:
$$\tau_{\text{man}}\subseteq\tau_{d_M}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{d_M}\subseteq\ta
u_{\text{man}},$$
so
$$\boxed{\;\tau_{d_M}=\tau_{\text{man}}\;}.$$

The topology induced by the Riemannian distance $d_M$ is exactly the manifold's
inherent topology. The essential reason is that on any compact coordinate patch
the smooth metric tensor is uniformly equivalent to the Euclidean metric (bounds
 $c\le g\le C$), which makes Riemannian lengths and Euclidean coordinate lengths
 comparable; this comparability forces the two families of balls to interleave,
and hence the two topologies to coincide.
