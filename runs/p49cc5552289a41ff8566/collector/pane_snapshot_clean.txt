## Proof

We use the Hardy–Ramanujan asymptotic formula for the partition function:

$$p(n) \sim \frac{1}{4n\sqrt{3}} \exp\!\left(\pi\sqrt{\frac{2n}{3}}\right) \quad
 \text{as } n \to \infty.$$

More precisely, there exists a function $\varepsilon(n) \to 0$ such that

$$p(n) = \frac{1}{4n\sqrt{3}} \exp\!\left(\pi\sqrt{\frac{2n}{3}}\right)(1 + \var
epsilon(n)).$$

We analyze the ratio $p(n)/p(n-5)$ by splitting it into three factors: the prefa
ctor ratio, the exponential ratio, and the error ratio.

**Factor 1 (prefactor).**

$$\frac{1/(4n\sqrt{3})}{1/(4(n-5)\sqrt{3})} = \frac{n-5}{n} = 1 - \frac{5}{n} \x
rightarrow{n\to\infty} 1.$$

**Factor 2 (exponential).** The ratio of exponentials is

$$\exp\!\left(\pi\sqrt{\tfrac{2n}{3}} - \pi\sqrt{\tfrac{2(n-5)}{3}}\right) = \ex
p\!\left(\pi\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}}\left(\sqrt{n} - \sqrt{n-5}\right)\right).$$

Rationalizing,

$$\sqrt{n} - \sqrt{n-5} = \frac{5}{\sqrt{n} + \sqrt{n-5}}.$$

Since $\sqrt{n} + \sqrt{n-5} \sim 2\sqrt{n}$, we get

$$\sqrt{n} - \sqrt{n-5} = \frac{5}{2\sqrt{n}}(1 + o(1)) \xrightarrow{n\to\infty}
 0.$$

Therefore the exponent $\pi\sqrt{2/3}\,(\sqrt{n}-\sqrt{n-5}) \to 0$, and by cont
inuity of $\exp$,

$$\exp\!\left(\pi\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}}\left(\sqrt{n} - \sqrt{n-5}\right)\right) \x
rightarrow{n\to\infty} e^{0} = 1.$$

**Factor 3 (error term).** Since $\varepsilon(n) \to 0$ and $\varepsilon(n-5) \t
o 0$,

$$\frac{1 + \varepsilon(n)}{1 + \varepsilon(n-5)} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} \frac
{1}{1} = 1.$$

**Combining.** The three factors each tend to $1$, so

$$\frac{p(n)}{p(n-5)} = \underbrace{\frac{n-5}{n}}_{\to\, 1} \cdot \underbrace{\
exp\!\left(\pi\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}}\left(\sqrt{n}-\sqrt{n-5}\right)\right)}_{\to\,
 1} \cdot \underbrace{\frac{1+\varepsilon(n)}{1+\varepsilon(n-5)}}_{\to\, 1} \xr
ightarrow{n\to\infty} 1.$$

**Remark on the choice of $5$.** The argument only used that $n - (n-5) = 5$ is
a *fixed* constant. For any fixed $k$, $\sqrt{n} - \sqrt{n-k} = k/(\sqrt{n}+\sqr
t{n-k}) \to 0$, so the same proof shows $\lim_{n\to\infty} p(n)/p(n-k) = 1$ for
every fixed $k$. The specific value $k=5$ plays no special role.

**Conclusion.** The limit $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{p(n)}{p(n-5)}$ ex
ists and equals $1$.
