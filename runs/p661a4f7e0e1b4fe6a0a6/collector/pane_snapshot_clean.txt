## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. If every commutative square in $C$ is a pullback square, then ev
ery morphism in $C$ is invertible (i.e., $C$ is a groupoid).

**Proof.** Let $f: A \to B$ be an arbitrary morphism in $C$. We show $f$ is an i
somorphism.

Consider the square

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
A & \xrightarrow{\;f\;} & B \\
{\scriptstyle f}\downarrow & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle \mathrm{id}_B} \\
B & \xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{id}_B\;} & B
\end{array}
$$

This square commutes, since both composites $A \to B$ equal $f$:
$$
\mathrm{id}_B \circ f = f = \mathrm{id}_B \circ f.
$$

By hypothesis, every commutative square is a pullback, so this square is a pullb
ack. Concretely, $A$ (with projections $f: A \to B$ on the left and $f: A \to B$
 on the top) is the pullback of the cospan

$$
B \xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}_B} B \xleftarrow{\mathrm{id}_B} B.
$$

We now exploit the universal property of this pullback.

**Step 1 — a right inverse of $f$.** Take the test object $Q = B$ with maps $q_1
 = q_2 = \mathrm{id}_B : B \to B$. The compatibility condition $\mathrm{id}_B \c
irc q_1 = \mathrm{id}_B \circ q_2$ holds trivially. The universal property of th
e pullback $A$ yields a unique morphism $u: B \to A$ such that

$$
f \circ u = q_1 = \mathrm{id}_B. \tag{1}
$$

So $u$ is a right inverse of $f$.

**Step 2 — $u$ is also a left inverse.** Now take the test object $Q = A$ with $
q_1 = q_2 = f : A \to B$. Compatibility $\mathrm{id}_B \circ f = \mathrm{id}_B \
circ f$ holds. The universal property yields a *unique* morphism $v: A \to A$ wi
th $f \circ v = f$.

Both $v = \mathrm{id}_A$ and $v = u \circ f$ satisfy this equation:
- $f \circ \mathrm{id}_A = f$. ✓
- $f \circ (u \circ f) = (f \circ u) \circ f = \mathrm{id}_B \circ f = f$, using
 (1). ✓

By uniqueness,
$$
u \circ f = \mathrm{id}_A. \tag{2}
$$

**Conclusion.** From (1) and (2), $f \circ u = \mathrm{id}_B$ and $u \circ f = \
mathrm{id}_A$, so $f$ is an isomorphism with inverse $u$.

Since $f: A \to B$ was an arbitrary morphism, every morphism in $C$ is invertibl
e. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The argument uses only the single, always-available commutative squa
re built from $f$ and $\mathrm{id}_B$; no additional morphisms out of $B$ are re
quired, so the result holds unconditionally (it is not vacuous even for objects
with minimal endomorphism structure).
