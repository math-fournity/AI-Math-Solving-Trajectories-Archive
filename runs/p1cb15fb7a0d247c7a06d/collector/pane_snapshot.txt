Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum number of "strange segments"
 where $B$ is closest to $A$ and $A$ is farthest from $B$.

## Proof

**Setup.** For each point $P$, let $c(P)$ denote the unique closest point to $P$
, and $f(P)$ the unique farthest point from $P$ (uniqueness follows from all dis
tances being distinct). Segment $AB$ is strange iff $c(A)=B$ and $f(B)=A$.

**Upper bound: at most 1 strange segment.**

Suppose for contradiction that two distinct strange segments exist.

**Case 1: They share an endpoint $X$.**

- If $X$ is the closest-source in both: $c(X)=Y$ and $c(X)=Z$ forces $Y=Z$, not
distinct.
- If $X$ is the farthest-source in both: $f(X)=Y$ and $f(X)=Z$ forces $Y=Z$, not
 distinct.
- If $X$ is closest-source in one and farthest-source in the other: WLOG $AB$ st
range ($c(A)=B$, $f(B)=A$) and $CA$ strange ($c(C)=A$, $f(A)=C$). Then:
  - $c(A)=B$ gives $|AB|<|AC|$
  - $c(C)=A$ gives $|AC|<|BC|$
  - $f(B)=A$ gives $|AB|>|BC|$

  Combining: $|AC|<|BC|<|AB|<|AC|$, contradiction.

  The symmetric sub-case ($B$ shared as farthest-source then closest-source) giv
es $|BD|<|AB|<|AD|<|BD|$, also a contradiction.

**Case 2: They are vertex-disjoint.**

Let $A_1B_1$ and $A_2B_2$ be strange with all four points distinct. Using $|B_1A
_2|=|A_2B_1|$ and $|A_1B_2|=|B_2A_1|$:

- From $A_1B_1$ strange: $|A_1B_1|>|B_1A_2|$ (A₁ farthest from B₁) and $|A_1B_1|
<|A_1B_2|$ (B₁ closest to A₁).
- From $A_2B_2$ strange: $|A_2B_2|<|A_2B_1|$ (B₂ closest to A₂) and $|A_2B_2|>|B
_2A_1|$ (A₂ farthest from B₂).

**Chain 1:** $|A_2B_2|<|A_2B_1|=|B_1A_2|<|A_1B_1|$, so $|A_2B_2|<|A_1B_1|$.

**Chain 2:** $|A_1B_1|<|A_1B_2|=|B_2A_1|<|A_2B_2|$, so $|A_1B_1|<|A_2B_2|$.

This gives $|A_2B_2|<|A_1B_1|<|A_2B_2|$, a contradiction.

All cases yield contradictions, so **at most 1 strange segment** can exist.

**Lower bound: 1 strange segment is achievable for all $n\geq 2$.**

For $n=2$: the single segment is trivially strange (each point is both closest a
nd farthest to the other).

For $n\geq 3$: Place $B=(0,0)$ and $A=(1,0)$. The region $R=\{P\in\mathbb{R}^2:|
BP|<1 \text{ and } |AP|>1\}$ is non-empty (it contains points near $(-\tfrac{1}{
2},0)$, since $|B(-\tfrac{1}{2},0)|=\tfrac{1}{2}<1$ and $|A(-\tfrac{1}{2},0)|=\t
frac{3}{2}>1$). Place the remaining $n-2$ points generically in $R$; the conditi
ons of distinct pairwise distances and no three collinear are open and dense, so
 such a placement exists.

Then $|AB|=1$, while $|AC_i|>1$ for all $i$ (so $B$ is closest to $A$) and $|BC_
i|<1$ for all $i$ (so $A$ is farthest from $B$). Hence $AB$ is strange.

**Conclusion.** The largest possible number of strange segments is $\boxed{1}$ f
or all $n\geq 2$.
