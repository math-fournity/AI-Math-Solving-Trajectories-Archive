## Proof

t $0 \le c_n \le H_{n-1}$ (the $(n{-}1)$-th harmonic number), so $c_n = O(\log n
)$. The series in question is $\sum_{n=2}^\infty c_n \,\frac{\sin(na)}{n}$.

We consider two cases.

### Case 1: $a \in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}$

Then $\sin(na) = 0$ for every positive integer $n$, so every term of the series
is $0$. The series converges trivially (to $0$).

### Case 2: $a \notin 2\pi\mathbb{Z}$

We apply **Dirichlet's test**: if $(a_n)$ has bounded partial sums and $(b_n)$ i
s a sequence that is eventually monotonically decreasing with $b_n \to 0$, then
$\sum a_n b_n$ converges.

Set $a_n = \sin(na)$ and $b_n = \frac{c_n}{n}$.

**Bounded partial sums of $\sin(na)$.** For $N \ge 1$,
$$\sum_{n=1}^{N} \sin(na) = \frac{\sin\!\left(\frac{Na}{2}\right)\sin\!\left(\fr
ac{(N+1)a}{2}\right)}{\sin(a/2)},$$
which is bounded in absolute value by $\frac{1}{|\sin(a/2)|}$, a finite constant
 since $a \notin 2\pi\mathbb{Z}$ implies $\sin(a/2) \ne 0$.

**$b_n \to 0$.** Since $c_n \le H_{n-1} \sim \log n$, we have
$$0 \le b_n = \frac{c_n}{n} \le \frac{H_{n-1}}{n} \sim \frac{\log n}{n} \to 0.$$

**$b_n$ is eventually monotonically decreasing.** We show $b_n \ge b_{n+1}$ for
all $n \ge 2$. Since $c_n$ is non-decreasing and $c_{n+1} \le c_n + \frac{1}{n}$
 (each step adds at most $\frac{1}{n}$), we have:
$$b_{n+1} = \frac{c_{n+1}}{n+1} \le \frac{c_n + \frac{1}{n}}{n+1}.$$
We need $\frac{c_n}{n} \ge \frac{c_n + \frac{1}{n}}{n+1}$, which is equivalent t
o
$$(n+1)\,c_n \ge n\,c_n + 1 \iff c_n \ge 1.$$
Since $c_2 = 1$ and $c_n$ is non-decreasing, we have $c_n \ge 1$ for all $n \ge
2$. Hence $b_n \ge b_{n+1}$ for all $n \ge 2$. ✓

All conditions of Dirichlet's test are satisfied, so $\sum_{n=2}^\infty c_n\,\fr
ac{\sin(na)}{n}$ converges.

### Conclusion

The series converges for **every** $a \in \mathbb{R}$.

- When $a \in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}$, convergence is trivial (all terms vanish).
- When $a \notin 2\pi\mathbb{Z}$, convergence follows from Dirichlet's test, sin
ce the partial sums of $\sin(na)$ are bounded and $\frac{c_n}{n}$ decreases mono
