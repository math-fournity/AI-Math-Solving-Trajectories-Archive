## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** Weak convergence of $u_n$ in $L^p(\Omega)$ together with weak c
onvergence of $\nabla u_n$ in $L^p(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)$ implies weak convergenc
e of $u_n$ in $W^{1,p}(\Omega)$.

**Setup.** Equip the product $L^p(\Omega)\times L^p(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)$ with t
he norm
$$\|(f,g)\|=\|f\|_{L^p}+\|g\|_{L^p},$$
which is exactly the norm induced on $W^{1,p}(\Omega)$ via $\|u\|_{W^{1,p}}=\|u\
|_{L^p}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^p}$. The map
$$T:W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to L^p(\Omega)\times L^p(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N),\qquad T(u)=(u
,\nabla u)$$
is then an **isometric embedding**: $\|T(u)\|=\|u\|_{W^{1,p}}$.

**Step 1: $T(W^{1,p})$ is a closed subspace.** Since $W^{1,p}(\Omega)$ is a Bana
ch space and $T$ is a linear isometry, the image $T(W^{1,p})$ is complete, hence
 closed in $L^p\times L^p(\mathbb{R}^N)$.

**Step 2: Identify the dual of the product.** The dual of $(L^p\times L^p(\mathb
b{R}^N),\,\|\cdot\|_1\text{-sum})$ is $(L^q\times L^q(\mathbb{R}^N),\,\max\text{
-norm})$, where $\frac1p+\frac1q=1$, with the pairing
$$\big\langle(\varphi,\Psi),\,(f,g)\big\rangle=\int_\Omega f\,\varphi\,dx+\int_\
Omega g\cdot\Psi\,dx,\qquad \varphi\in L^q(\Omega),\;\Psi\in L^q(\Omega,\mathbb{
R}^N).$$

**Step 3: Hahn–Banach representation of $(W^{1,p})^*$.** Let $\ell\in (W^{1,p}(\
Omega))^*$. Then $\ell\circ T^{-1}$ is a continuous linear functional on the clo
sed subspace $T(W^{1,p})\subset L^p\times L^p(\mathbb{R}^N)$. By the Hahn–Banach
 theorem, it extends to a continuous linear functional on the whole product, so
by Step 2 there exist $\varphi\in L^q(\Omega)$ and $\Psi\in L^q(\Omega,\mathbb{R
}^N)$ such that
$$\ell(u)=\int_\Omega u\,\varphi\,dx+\int_\Omega \nabla u\cdot\Psi\,dx\qquad\for
all\,u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega). \tag{$\star$}$$

**Step 4: Verify weak convergence.** Assume $u_n\rightharpoonup u$ in $L^p(\Omeg
a)$ and $\nabla u_n\rightharpoonup \nabla u$ in $L^p(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)$. For
any $\ell\in(W^{1,p})^*$, using the representation $(\star)$ with $\varphi\in L^
q$ and $\Psi\in L^q(\mathbb{R}^N)$:
$$\ell(u_n)=\int_\Omega u_n\,\varphi\,dx+\int_\Omega \nabla u_n\cdot\Psi\,dx\;\l
ongrightarrow\;\int_\Omega u\,\varphi\,dx+\int_\Omega \nabla u\cdot\Psi\,dx=\ell
(u),$$
where the first integral converges by weak convergence $u_n\rightharpoonup u$ in
 $L^p$ (test function $\varphi\in L^q$), and the second by weak convergence $\na
bla u_n\rightharpoonup \nabla u$ in $L^p(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)$ (test function $\
Psi\in L^q(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)$).

Since $\ell(u_n)\to\ell(u)$ for **every** $\ell\in(W^{1,p}(\Omega))^*$, we concl
ude
$$u_n\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{weakly in }W^{1,p}(\Omega).$$

**Remark.** The essential point is that $T$ is an isometry, so the $W^{1,p}$-top
ology coincides with the subspace topology inherited from $L^p\times L^p(\mathbb
{R}^N)$. Hahn–Banach then guarantees that *every* $W^{1,p}$-continuous functiona
l is realized by a pair $(\varphi,\Psi)\in L^q\times L^q(\mathbb{R}^N)$ acting o
n the two components separately. Hence testing against all functionals in $(W^{1
,p})^*$ reduces to testing $u_n$ against all $L^q$ functions and $\nabla u_n$ ag
ainst all $L^q(\mathbb{R}^N)$ vector fields — precisely the two given weak conve
rgence hypotheses.
