## Answer

**Yes**, a simply connected Riemannian 2-manifold with everywhere negative curva
ture must be diffeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^2$. Here is the justification.

---

**Proof.**

Let $M$ be a simply connected Riemannian 2-manifold (without boundary) whose Gau
ssian curvature $K$ satisfies $K < 0$ everywhere.

**Step 1: $M$ is second countable.** Since $M$ carries a Riemannian metric, it a
dmits partitions of unity, hence is paracompact. A connected (simply connected i
mplies connected) paracompact locally Euclidean Hausdorff space is $\sigma$-comp
act, and a $\sigma$-compact locally Euclidean Hausdorff manifold is second count
able. Thus $M$ falls under the standard classification of surfaces.

**Step 2: The compact case is excluded by Gauss–Bonnet.** By the classification
of compact surfaces, the only simply connected compact 2-manifold (without bound
ary) is $S^2$. The Gauss–Bonnet theorem gives
$$\int_M K\, dA = 2\pi\,\chi(M).$$
For $M \cong S^2$, $\chi(S^2) = 2$, so $\int_M K\, dA = 4\pi > 0$. But $K < 0$ e
verywhere forces $\int_M K\, dA < 0$, a contradiction. Hence $M$ is **not compac
t**.

**Step 3: The non-compact case is $\mathbb{R}^2$.** By the classification of (se
cond countable) non-compact surfaces — equivalently, by the classical theorem th
at every simply connected non-compact 2-manifold without boundary is homeomorphi
c (indeed diffeomorphic) to $\mathbb{R}^2$ — the only simply connected non-compa
ct 2-manifold is $\mathbb{R}^2$ itself. (A genus-0 orientable surface with more
than one end, or with isolated planar ends, acquires nontrivial $\pi_1$ from loo
ps encircling those ends; simply connectedness forces a single end and no such l
oops, yielding exactly $\mathbb{R}^2$.)

**Conclusion.** $M$ is non-compact and simply connected, hence diffeomorphic to
$\mathbb{R}^2$. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** If $M$ were additionally assumed *complete*, the Cartan–Hadamard the
orem would directly imply that the exponential map $\exp_p : T_p M \to M$ is a d
iffeomorphism, giving $M \cong \mathbb{R}^2$ without invoking the surface classi
fication. The point of the argument above is that **completeness is not needed**
: the topological classification of surfaces together with Gauss–Bonnet already
forces the conclusion. The role of negative curvature is solely to rule out the
compact simply connected surface $S^2$ (via Gauss–Bonnet), after which the class
ification leaves $\mathbb{R}^2$ as the only possibility.
