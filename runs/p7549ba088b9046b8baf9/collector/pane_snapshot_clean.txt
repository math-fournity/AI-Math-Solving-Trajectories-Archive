Looking at this problem, I need to analyze whether subsets of non-consecutive co
lumns from the infinite Vandermonde matrix span the full space.

**Solution.**

Let $k_1 < k_2 < \cdots < k_n$ be non-negative integers (column indices). The $n
 \times n$ submatrix has $(i,j)$-entry $x_i^{k_j}$, with determinant $D(k_1,\ldo
ts,k_n)$.

**Generalized Vandermonde decomposition.** Since $k_1 < \cdots < k_n$ are non-ne
gative integers, $k_j \geq j-1$. Define the partition $\lambda = (\lambda_1,\ldo
ts,\lambda_n)$ by $\lambda_i = k_{n+1-i} - (n-i)$. Then $\lambda_1 \geq \cdots \
geq \lambda_n \geq 0$, and by the classical formula:

$$D(k_1,\ldots,k_n) = s_\lambda(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \cdot \prod_{i<j}(x_j - x_i)$$

where $s_\lambda$ is the Schur polynomial. Since the $x_i$ are distinct, the Van
dermonde factor $\prod_{i<j}(x_j - x_i) \neq 0$, so:

$$D \neq 0 \iff s_\lambda(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \neq 0.$$

**Non-consecutive condition.** The columns are non-consecutive iff $k_{j+1} - k_
j \geq 2$ for all $j$. This translates to $\lambda_i - \lambda_{i+1} \geq 1$, i.
e., $\lambda$ is a **strict partition** ($\lambda_1 > \lambda_2 > \cdots > \lamb
da_n \geq 0$).

---

**Part 1: Not every non-consecutive subset spans (in general).**

The Schur polynomial $s_\lambda$ is a nonzero polynomial with positive integer c
oefficients, but it can vanish at specific points.

*Counterexample.* Take $n=2$, $x_1 = 1$, $x_2 = -1$, columns $\{0, 2\}$. The sub
matrix is:
$$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$
with determinant $0$. Here $\lambda = (1,0)$ and $s_{(1,0)}(x_1,x_2) = x_1 + x_2
 = 0$.

So **not every** non-consecutive subset necessarily spans.

---

**Part 2: If all $x_i > 0$, then every subset of $n$ columns spans.**

When $x_1,\ldots,x_n > 0$, every monomial in $s_\lambda(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ is stri
ctly positive (Schur polynomials have non-negative integer coefficients with at
least one positive term). Thus $s_\lambda > 0$ and $D = s_\lambda \cdot V \neq 0
$ for **any** choice of $n$ columns, consecutive or not.

---

**Part 3: For any distinct $x_i$, there always exists a non-consecutive subset t
hat spans.**

*Proof.* Consider columns $\{0, 2, 4, \ldots, 2(n-1)\}$. The submatrix is $(x_i^
{2j})$, the Vandermonde in $y_i = x_i^2$, with determinant $\prod_{i<j}(x_j^2 -
x_i^2)$.

**Case 1:** All $x_i^2$ distinct. The determinant is nonzero; these non-consecut
ive columns span. ✓

**Case 2:** Some $x_i^2 = x_j^2$ ($i \neq j$), i.e., $x_i = -x_j$. Since the $x_
i$ are distinct, each value of $x_i^2$ is attained by at most 2 indices. Partiti
on $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ into groups $G_1,\ldots,G_m$ by equal $x_i^2$ values; each g
roup has size 1 or 2, and $m \geq \lceil n/2 \rceil$.

The even columns $\{0, 2, \ldots, 2(m-1)\}$ form the Vandermonde in $x_i^2$ with
 $m$ distinct values, so they are linearly independent and span an $m$-dimension
al subspace $E$.

For each group $G_s = \{i,j\}$ of size 2 (with $x_i = a_s$, $x_j = -a_s$, $a_s \
neq 0$), an odd column $c_{2k+1}$ satisfies $c_{2k+1}(i) = a_s^{2k+1}$ and $c_{2
k+1}(j) = -a_s^{2k+1}$, which is anti-symmetric — hence not in $E$.

Choose odd columns $\{2m+1, 2m+3, \ldots, 2m+1+2(n-m-1)\}$. These are non-consec
utive with each other (gaps of 2) and with the even columns (gap of 3 between $2
(m-1)$ and $2m+1$).

The projections of these odd columns onto $\mathbb{C}^n / E$ form a matrix with
$(s,t)$-entry $a_s^{2m+1+2t} = a_s^{2m+1} \cdot (a_s^2)^t$. This equals $\operat
orname{diag}(a_s^{2m+1}) \cdot V(a_s^2)$, a product of an invertible diagonal ma
trix (since $a_s \neq 0$) and a Vandermonde in the distinct values $a_s^2$ (inve
rtible). Hence the projections are linearly independent.

Therefore the $m$ even columns and $n-m$ odd columns are linearly independent (a
ny linear relation projects to a relation among the odd columns, forcing all odd
 coefficients to zero, then all even coefficients to zero), giving $n$ non-conse
cutive columns that span. ✓

---

**Summary.**
- **Every** non-consecutive subset spans: **No** in general (counterexample: $n=
2$, $x_1=1, x_2=-1$, columns $\{0,2\}$). **Yes** if all $x_i > 0$ (Schur polynom
ials are strictly positive).
- **There exists** a non-consecutive subset that spans: **Yes**, always, for any
 distinct $x_1,\ldots,x_n$.
