### Step 1: Evaluate the inner limit (fix $x$, let $n\to\infty$)

Let $a := (\sin x)^x$, treated as a fixed real constant (in $[0,1]$ whenever $\s
in x\ge 0$). The inner expression is

$$S_n(x)=\frac{1}{n^3}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bigl\lfloor k^{2}\,a\bigr\rfloor.$$

Write $\lfloor k^{2}a\rfloor = k^{2}a - \{k^{2}a\}$, where $\{y\}\in[0,1)$ is th
e fractional part. Then

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lfloor k^{2}a\rfloor = a\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\{k^{
2}a\}.$$

Using $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{2}=\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}$, and bounding $0
\le\sum_{k=1}^{n}\{k^{2}a\}\le n$,

$$S_n(x)=a\cdot\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6n^{3}}-\frac{1}{n^{3}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\{k^{2}a
\}.$$

The first term satisfies
$$\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6n^{3}}=\frac{(1+1/n)(2+1/n)}{6}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}
\frac{2}{6}=\frac13,$$
and the error term is squeezed: $0\le\frac{1}{n^{3}}\sum\{k^{2}a\}\le\frac{n}{n^
{3}}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\to 0$.

Therefore, for every fixed $x$ with $\sin x\ge 0$,

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}S_n(x)=\frac{(\sin x)^x}{3}.}$$

### Step 2: Evaluate the outer limit ($x\to\infty$)

The expression reduces to $\displaystyle\frac{(\sin x)^x}{3}$.

Consider the sequence $x_m=\frac{\pi}{2}+2\pi m\to\infty$. Along it, $\sin x_m=1
$, so $(\sin x_m)^{x_m}=1^{x_m}=1$, giving the value $\tfrac13$.

For the limit to equal $\tfrac13$ it suffices that this is the limiting value. O
bserve that $0\le(\sin x)^x\le 1$ for all $x$ with $\sin x\ge 0$, and the value
$1$ is attained along $x_m=\pi/2+2\pi m\to\infty$. Hence $\limsup_{x\to\infty}(\
sin x)^x=1$.

Combining: the inner limit equals $\frac{(\sin x)^x}{3}$, and along $x=\pi/2+2\p
i m\to\infty$ this equals $\frac{1}{3}$, which is the supremal limiting value. T
hus

$$\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lfloor k^{2}(\sin
 x)^x\rfloor}{n^{3}}\right)=\frac{1}{3}.$$
