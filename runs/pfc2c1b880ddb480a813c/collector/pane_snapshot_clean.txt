## Proof

We determine the transcendence degree of $\mathbb{C}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, denoted
 $\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{C}$.

**Upper bound.** The field $\mathbb{C}$ has cardinality $|\mathbb{C}| = 2^{\alep
h_0}$. Any transcendence basis $T$ of $\mathbb{C}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is algebrai
cally independent over $\mathbb{Q}$, and $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraic over $\mathbb
{Q}(T)$. The field $\mathbb{Q}(T)$ consists of rational functions in the element
s of $T$ with rational coefficients. Since each such function involves only fini
tely many elements of $T$ and finitely many rational coefficients, and $\mathbb{
Q}$ is countable, we have

$$|\mathbb{Q}(T)| \leq \max(|T|, \aleph_0).$$

Because $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}(T)$, every element of $\mathb
b{C}$ is a root of a polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}(T)$, and each polynomial has on
ly finitely many roots. The number of polynomials over $\mathbb{Q}(T)$ is $\max(
|\mathbb{Q}(T)|, \aleph_0) = \max(|T|, \aleph_0)$, so

$$|\mathbb{C}| \leq \max(|T|, \aleph_0).$$

Since $|\mathbb{C}| = 2^{\aleph_0} > \aleph_0$, we must have $|T| \geq 2^{\aleph
_0}$. Combined with $|T| \leq |\mathbb{C}| = 2^{\aleph_0}$ (as $T \subseteq \mat
hbb{C}$), we obtain $|T| = 2^{\aleph_0}$.

**Lower bound (existence of a large algebraically independent set).** It remains
 to show that a transcendence basis of cardinality $2^{\aleph_0}$ exists. We use
 Zorn's lemma. Consider the collection $\mathcal{F}$ of all algebraically indepe
ndent subsets of $\mathbb{C}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, partially ordered by inclusion.
 Every chain in $\mathcal{F}$ has an upper bound (its union, which is algebraica
lly independent since algebraic independence is a finite condition: any finite s
ubset of the union lies in some element of the chain). By Zorn's lemma, $\mathca
l{F}$ has a maximal element $T$, which is by definition a transcendence basis.

We must verify $|T| = 2^{\aleph_0}$ and not smaller. Suppose for contradiction t
hat $|T| \leq \aleph_0$ (the only other possibility, since $|T|$ is uncountable
or countable). Then $\mathbb{Q}(T)$ has cardinality $\max(|T|, \aleph_0) = \alep
h_0$, and the algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}(T)$ within $\mathbb{C}$ also has
cardinality $\aleph_0$. But $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}(T)$ by ma
ximality of $T$, so $|\mathbb{C}| = \aleph_0$, contradicting $|\mathbb{C}| = 2^{
\aleph_0}$. Hence $|T|$ is uncountable, and by the upper bound argument, $|T| =
2^{\aleph_0}$.

**Conclusion.** Every transcendence basis of $\mathbb{C}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ has
the same cardinality (a standard theorem: any two transcendence bases of an exte
nsion have the same cardinality). Therefore

$$\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{C} = |T| = 2^{\aleph_0},$$

the cardinality of the continuum.
