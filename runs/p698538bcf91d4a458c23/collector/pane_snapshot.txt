## Proof

**Setup and structure of the rings.**

By the Artin–Wedderburn theorem, since $R$ is a left Artinian simple ring, there
 exists a positive integer $n_R$ such that
$$R \cong M_{n_R}(D(R)),$$
where $D(R)$ is the division ring associated to $R$. Likewise $S \cong M_{n_S}(D
(S))$ for some $n_S$.

Since $R$ is simple and left Artinian, it is semisimple; every left $R$-module i
s completely reducible. Up to isomorphism there is a **unique** simple left $R$-
module
$$V_R := D(R)^{n_R}\quad(\text{column vectors, }R\text{ acts on the left}),$$
and every left $R$-module is a direct sum of copies of $V_R$.

**Decomposition of $M$ and $N$.**

Set $k := \operatorname{length}(M) = \operatorname{length}(N)$. Since $M \neq 0$
 has finite length $k$ and is semisimple,
$$M \cong V_R^{\,k} = \underbrace{V_R \oplus \cdots \oplus V_R}_{k \text{ copies
}}.$$
By the identical argument over $S$,
$$N \cong V_S^{\,k}, \qquad V_S = D(S)^{n_S}.$$

**The submodule lattice of $V_R^{\,k}$.**

Let $E_R := \operatorname{End}_R(V_R)$. By Schur's lemma $E_R$ is a division rin
g. A direct computation shows that every $R$-endomorphism of $V_R = D(R)^{n_R}$
is right multiplication by an element of $D(R)$, so
$$E_R \cong D(R)^{\mathrm{op}}.$$

The simple module $V_R$ carries a natural right $E_R$-module structure. A standa
rd result in module theory (a consequence of the Morita equivalence between $R\t
ext{-Mod}$ and $E_R^{\mathrm{op}}\text{-Mod} = D(R)\text{-Mod}$, or equivalently
 of the Jacobson density theorem) gives:

> **Claim.** The lattice $L_R$ of $R$-submodules of $V_R^{\,k}$ is isomorphic to
 the lattice of right $E_R$-subspaces of the right $E_R$-vector space $E_R^{\,k}
$.

*Sketch of Claim.* The functor $V_R \otimes_{E_R} {-}\colon (E_R\text{-Mod}) \to
 (R\text{-Mod})$ is a Morita equivalence (since $V_R$ is a faithful, balanced ri
ght $E_R$-module that is a progenerator). Under this equivalence, the free right
 $E_R$-module $E_R^{\,k}$ corresponds to $V_R^{\,k}$, and right $E_R$-submodules
 of $E_R^{\,k}$ correspond bijectively to $R$-submodules of $V_R^{\,k}$, preserv
ing inclusion. $\square$

Since right $E_R$-modules are the same as left $D(R)$-modules (because $E_R \con
g D(R)^{\mathrm{op}}$), we obtain
$$L_R \;\cong\; \bigl\{\text{left } D(R)\text{-subspaces of } D(R)^k\bigr\}.$$

Applying the same reasoning to $S$:
$$L_S \;\cong\; \bigl\{\text{left } D(S)\text{-subspaces of } D(S)^k\bigr\},$$
where $L_S$ is the lattice of $S$-submodules of $N$.

**Comparison via $D(R) \cong D(S)$.**

We are given a ring isomorphism $\varphi\colon D(R) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} D(S)$
. Applying $\varphi$ componentwise defines a map
$$\Phi\colon D(R)^k \longrightarrow D(S)^k, \qquad (a_1,\dots,a_k) \mapsto (\var
phi(a_1),\dots,\varphi(a_k)).$$
If $W \subseteq D(R)^k$ is a left $D(R)$-subspace, then $\Phi(W)$ is a left $D(S
)$-subspace of $D(S)^k$: for $d \in D(R)$ and $w = (w_1,\dots,w_k) \in W$, we ha
ve $d\cdot w = (dw_1,\dots,dw_k) \in W$, so
$$\varphi(d)\cdot \Phi(w) = (\varphi(d)\varphi(w_1),\dots,\varphi(d)\varphi(w_k)
) = \varphi(dw_1,\dots,dw_k) = \Phi(d\cdot w) \in \Phi(W).$$
The map $W \mapsto \Phi(W)$ is a bijection (with inverse given by $\varphi^{-1}$
 componentwise) that preserves inclusion in both directions. Hence it is an **is
omorphism of ordered sets** (indeed, of lattices):
$$\bigl\{\text{left } D(R)\text{-subspaces of } D(R)^k\bigr\} \;\cong\; \bigl\{\
text{left } D(S)\text{-subspaces of } D(S)^k\bigr\}.$$

**Conclusion.**

Composing the isomorphisms,
$$L_R \;\cong\; \{\text{left } D(R)\text{-subspaces of } D(R)^k\} \;\cong\; \{\t
ext{left } D(S)\text{-subspaces of } D(S)^k\} \;\cong\; L_S.$$

Therefore the ordered set of $R$-submodules of $M$ is isomorphic to the ordered
set of $S$-submodules of $N$.

**Answer: Yes.** $\blacksquare$
