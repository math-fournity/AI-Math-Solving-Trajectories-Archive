## Proof

We seek the number of ordered quadruples $(a,b,c,d)$ with each digit in $\{1,2,\
dots,9\}$ satisfying
$$(2a-1)(2b-1)(2c-1)(2d-1) = 2abcd - 1.$$

**Key observation.** For digits $\geq 2$, the left-hand side grows much faster t
han the right-hand side, since $(2a-1) \geq \tfrac{3}{2}a$ for $a\geq 2$. This s
uggests solutions only occur when most digits equal $1$.

**Case 1: Three or four digits equal to 1.**

If $a=b=c=1$, the equation becomes
$$(1)(1)(1)(2d-1) = 2(1)(1)(1)(d) - 1 \implies 2d-1 = 2d-1,$$
which holds for **every** $d \in \{1,\dots,9\}$. By symmetry, the same works whe
never at least three of the four digits are $1$.

- All four equal to $1$: $(1,1,1,1)$ → **1 code**.
- Exactly three equal to $1$: choose the non-$1$ position (4 ways) and its value
 in $\{2,\dots,9\}$ (8 ways) → $4 \times 8 = $ **32 codes**.

**Case 2: Exactly two digits equal to 1.**

WLOG $a=b=1$, $c,d \geq 2$. The equation reduces to
$$(2c-1)(2d-1) = 2cd - 1 \implies 4cd - 2c - 2d + 1 = 2cd - 1 \implies (c-1)(d-1
) = 0.$$
This forces $c=1$ or $d=1$, contradicting $c,d\geq 2$. So **no solutions** with
exactly two $1$'s.

**Case 3: Exactly one digit equal to 1.**

WLOG $a=1$, $b,c,d \geq 2$. Define
$$g(b,c,d) = (2b-1)(2c-1)(2d-1) - 2bcd + 1.$$
At the minimum $(2,2,2)$: $g = 27 - 16 + 1 = 12 > 0$. The partial derivative
$$\frac{\partial g}{\partial b} = 2\bigl[(2c-1)(2d-1) - cd\bigr] = 2\bigl[3cd -
2c - 2d + 1\bigr].$$
For $c,d \geq 2$: $3cd - 2c - 2d + 1 \geq 12 - 4 - 4 + 1 = 5 > 0$, and this only
 increases with $c,d$. So $g$ is strictly increasing in each variable, hence $g
\geq 12 > 0$ for all $b,c,d\geq 2$. **No solutions.**

**Case 4: No digit equals 1 (all $\geq 2$).**

Define $h(a,b,c,d) = (2a-1)(2b-1)(2c-1)(2d-1) - 2abcd + 1$. At $(2,2,2,2)$: $h =
 81 - 32 + 1 = 50 > 0$. The partial derivative w.r.t. $a$ is
$$2\bigl[(2b-1)(2c-1)(2d-1) - bcd\bigr].$$
For $b,c,d\geq 2$: $(2b-1)(2c-1)(2d-1) \geq 27 > 8 = bcd$ (at minimum), and the
gap grows. So $h$ is strictly increasing in each variable, $h \geq 50 > 0$. **No
 solutions.**

**Conclusion.** The only valid codes are those with at least three digits equal
to $1$:
$$1 + 32 = \boxed{33}.$$
