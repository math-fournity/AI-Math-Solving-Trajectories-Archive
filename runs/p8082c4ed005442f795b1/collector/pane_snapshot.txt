## Solution

We analyze the integral
$$I(\alpha) = \int_0^{+\infty} \frac{1}{x^\alpha}\,\log\!\left(1+\frac{1}{x}\rig
ht)\arctan(x)\,dx$$
by splitting it at, say, $x=1$, and examining the behavior near $0^+$ and near $
+\infty$.

### Behavior near $x\to 0^+$

As $x\to 0^+$:
- $\arctan(x) \sim x$,
- $\log\!\left(1+\tfrac{1}{x}\right) = \log(1+x)-\log x \sim -\log x$ (since $\l
og(1+x)\to 0$),
- $\frac{1}{x^\alpha}=x^{-\alpha}$.

Hence the integrand satisfies
$$f(x) \sim x^{1-\alpha}(-\log x),\qquad x\to 0^+.$$

The comparison integral $\int_0^\varepsilon x^{1-\alpha}(-\log x)\,dx$ converges
 if and only if $1-\alpha>-1$, i.e. $\alpha<2$. At the boundary $\alpha=2$ it be
comes $\int_0^\varepsilon \frac{-\log x}{x}\,dx=\bigl[-\tfrac12(\log x)^2\bigr]_
0^\varepsilon=+\infty$, which diverges.

Therefore **convergence near $0$ requires $\alpha<2$**.

### Behavior near $x\to+\infty$

As $x\to+\infty$:
- $\log\!\left(1+\tfrac{1}{x}\right)\sim \tfrac{1}{x}$,
- $\arctan(x)\to \tfrac{\pi}{2}$,
- $\frac{1}{x^\alpha}=x^{-\alpha}$.

Hence
$$f(x)\sim \frac{\pi}{2}\,x^{-\alpha-1},\qquad x\to+\infty.$$

The integral $\int_M^{+\infty} x^{-\alpha-1}\,dx$ converges if and only if $\alp
ha+1>1$, i.e. $\alpha>0$. At $\alpha=0$ it becomes $\int_M^{+\infty}\frac{dx}{x}
=+\infty$, which diverges.

Therefore **convergence near $+\infty$ requires $\alpha>0$**.

### Combining

Both conditions must hold simultaneously:
$$\alpha>0 \quad\text{and}\quad \alpha<2.$$

For every $\alpha\in(0,2)$ the integral converges at both endpoints; for $\alpha
\le 0$ it diverges at $+\infty$, and for $\alpha\ge 2$ it diverges at $0$.

### Conclusion

The integral is convergent if and only if
$$\boxed{\alpha\in(0,2)}.$$
