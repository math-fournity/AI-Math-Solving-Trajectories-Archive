## Proof

Let $t$ be the time in minutes, $0 \le t < 720$ (a 12-hour cycle).

**Hand positions:**
- Minute hand angle: $\theta_m = 6t \pmod{360°}$ (it moves $360°/60 = 6°$ per mi
nute)
- Hour hand angle: $\theta_h = 0.5t \pmod{360°}$ (it moves $360°/720 = 0.5°$ per
 minute)

**Relative angle:** The minute hand gains on the hour hand at rate $6 - 0.5 = 5.
5°$ per minute. The signed angle between them is:
$$\theta_m - \theta_h = 5.5t \pmod{360°}$$

**Perpendicular condition:** The hands are perpendicular when the angle between
them is $90°$ or $270°$, i.e., when:
$$5.5t \equiv 90 \pmod{180}$$

This gives:
$$5.5t = 90 + 180k, \quad k \in \mathbb{Z}$$

Solving for $t$:
$$t = \frac{90 + 180k}{5.5} = \frac{180 + 360k}{11}$$

**Counting solutions in $[0, 720)$:**

We need $0 \le \frac{180 + 360k}{11} < 720$, which gives:
$$0 \le 180 + 360k < 7920$$
$$-180 \le 360k < 7740$$
$$-0.5 \le k < 21.5$$

So $k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots, 21$, yielding **22 distinct times**.

**Verification that all 22 times are distinct:** Since consecutive values of $t$
 differ by $\frac{360}{11} \approx 32.73$ minutes, all 22 times are distinct and
 fall within the 12-hour cycle.

**Explicit listing (first few):**

| $k$ | $t$ (min) | Time |
|-----|-----------|------|
| 0 | $180/11 \approx 16.36$ | 12:16:22 |
| 1 | $540/11 \approx 49.09$ | 12:49:05 |
| 2 | $900/11 \approx 81.82$ | 1:21:49 |
| 3 | $1260/11 \approx 114.55$ | 1:54:33 |
| 4 | $1620/11 \approx 147.27$ | 2:27:16 |
| 5 | $1800/11 \approx 163.64$ | 3:00:00 |
| ... | ... | ... |
| 21 | $7740/11 \approx 703.64$ | 11:43:38 |

**Intuitive check:** In 12 hours, the minute hand laps the hour hand 11 times (e
very $12/11$ hours). During each lap, the hands are perpendicular exactly twice
(once approaching, once separating). Thus $11 \times 2 = 22$ perpendicular confi
gurations.

## Answer

The hands of a precise clock are perpendicular to each other at **22** distinct
times in a 12-hour cycle.
