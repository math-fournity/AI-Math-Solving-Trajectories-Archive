only even powers, we can write $f(x) = g(x^2)$ where $g(y) = y^3 + ay^2 + by + c

If $g(y) = h(y)\,k(y)$ with $\deg h, \deg k \geq 1$ in $\mathbb{Q}[y]$, then $f(
x) = h(x^2)\,k(x^2)$, and since $\deg h, \deg k \geq 1$, both $h(x^2)$ and $k(x^
2)$ have degree $\geq 2$ in $x$, contradicting the irreducibility of $f$. $\squa

_1},\, \pm\sqrt{\alpha_2},\, \pm\sqrt{\alpha_3}$.

Let $L = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3)$ be the splitting field of $g$

{Q})$ is isomorphic to a transitive subgroup of $S_3$, so $|\operatorname{Gal}(L
/\mathbb{Q})| \in \{3, 6\}$, with maximum $6$ (i.e., $S_3$).

**Step 3: Upper bound via the wreath product.** The Galois group $G = \operatorn
ame{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ acts on the six roots of $f$, and this action is faithfu

ha_i}, -\sqrt{\alpha_i}\}$. Therefore $G$ embeds into the wreath product
$$G \hookrightarrow (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^3 \rtimes S_3 = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb
{Z} \wr S_3,$$

$$|G| = :\mathbb{Q}] = :L]\cdot[L:\mathbb{Q}] \leq 2^3 \cdot 6 = 48.$$
