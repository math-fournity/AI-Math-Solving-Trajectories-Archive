## Proof

Let $V = H^0(\mathbb{P}^3, \mathcal{O}(d))$ be the vector space of degree-$d$ ho
mogeneous forms in four variables, with $\dim V = \binom{d+3}{3}$. The space of
degree-$d$ surfaces is $\mathbb{P}(V) = \mathbb{P}^N$ where $N = \binom{d+3}{3}-
1$.

Let $G = \mathbb{G}(1,3)$ be the Grassmannian of lines in $\mathbb{P}^3$, with $
\dim G = 4$.

**Step 1: The incidence correspondence.** Define
$$I = \{(S, L) \in \mathbb{P}^N \times G : L \subset S\}.$$
Let $\pi_1: I \to \mathbb{P}^N$ and $\pi_2: I \to G$ be the projections. A surfa
ce $S$ contains a line iff $S \in \pi_1(I)$.

**Step 2: Fiber of $\pi_2$ over a fixed line.** Fix a line $L$. Choose coordinat
es so $L = \{x_2 = x_3 = 0\}$. The restriction map
$$\rho_L: V \to H^0(L, \mathcal{O}_L(d))$$
sends $F(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)$ to $F(x_0,x_1,0,0)$. This map is **surjective**: any
degree-$d$ form in $x_0, x_1$ is the restriction of itself (viewed as a form in
four variables). The target has dimension $d+1$ (degree-$d$ forms in two variabl
es). Hence
$$\dim \ker \rho_L = \binom{d+3}{3} - (d+1),$$
and the space of degree-$d$ surfaces containing $L$ is $\mathbb{P}(\ker \rho_L)$
, of projective dimension
$$\binom{d+3}{3} - (d+1) - 1 = N - (d+1).$$
So every fiber of $\pi_2$ is a projective space of dimension $N - d - 1$. Since
$\pi_2$ is a projective bundle over the irreducible variety $G$, the incidence v
ariety $I$ is irreducible and
$$\dim I = \dim G + (N - d - 1) = 4 + N - d - 1 = N + 3 - d.$$

**Step 3: Dimension comparison.** The image $\pi_1(I)$ is a closed irreducible s
ubset of $\mathbb{P}^N$ (since $I$ is projective), with
$$\dim \pi_1(I) \le \dim I = N + 3 - d.$$

For $d \ge 4$:
$$\dim \pi_1(I) \le N + 3 - d \le N - 1 < N = \dim \mathbb{P}^N.$$

Therefore $\pi_1(I)$ is a **proper closed subset** of $\mathbb{P}^N$. Its comple
ment is a nonempty (Zariski-open, hence dense) set of surfaces that contain no l
ine.

**Conclusion.** For every $d \ge 4$, the locus of degree-$d$ surfaces containing
 a line is a proper closed subset of the full parameter space $\mathbb{P}^N$. Co
nsequently, the **general** surface of degree $d \ge 4$ contains no line, and in
 particular such surfaces exist. $\blacksquare$
