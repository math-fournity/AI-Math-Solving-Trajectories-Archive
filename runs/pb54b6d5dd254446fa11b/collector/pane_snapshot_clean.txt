## Proof

**Step 1: Determine the possible rational canonical forms.**

Since $P$ is the unique irreducible factor of $\chi_A = P^3$, the minimal polyno
mial $\mu_A$ satisfies $P \mid \mu_A \mid P^3$, so $\mu_A \in \{P, P^2, P^3\}$.

The rational canonical form of $A$ is a direct sum of companion blocks $C(P^{a_1
}) \oplus \cdots \oplus C(P^{a_s})$ where $1 \le a_1 \le \cdots \le a_s$ and $\s
um a_i = 3$ (since $\deg \chi_A = 3q = q \sum a_i$). The sequence $(a_1, \ldots,
 a_s)$ is a partition of $3$ with $a_s = \deg(\mu_A)/q$. The three partitions of
 $3$ are:

| Partition | Invariant factors | $\mu_A$ |
|-----------|------------------|---------|
| $(3)$ | $P^3$ | $P^3$ |
| $(1,2)$ | $P, P^2$ | $P^2$ |
| $(1,1,1)$ | $P, P, P$ | $P$ |

Each is realizable (take the corresponding block-diagonal companion matrix), so
all three cases occur.

**Step 2: Compute $\dim C(A)$ for each case.**

View $V = \mathbb{Q}^{3q}$ as a $\mathbb{Q}[x]$-module via $A$. Then $C(A) = \op
eratorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}[x]}(V)$, and

$$V \cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^{s} \mathbb{Q}[x]/(P^{a_i}).$$

By the universal property of direct sums:

$$\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} C(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{s}\sum_{j=1}^{s} \dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \opera
torname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}[x]}\!\bigl(\mathbb{Q}[x]/(P^{a_j}),\; \mathbb{Q}[x]/(P^
{a_i})\bigr).$$

**Lemma.** $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}[x]}\!\bigl(\mathbb{
Q}[x]/(P^{a_j}),\; \mathbb{Q}[x]/(P^{a_i})\bigr) = q \cdot \min(a_i, a_j)$.

*Proof of Lemma.* A $\mathbb{Q}[x]$-module homomorphism $\varphi: \mathbb{Q}[x]/
(P^{a_j}) \to \mathbb{Q}[x]/(P^{a_i})$ is determined by $\varphi(1) = g \in \mat
hbb{Q}[x]/(P^{a_i})$ satisfying $P^{a_j} g \equiv 0 \pmod{P^{a_i}}$, i.e., $P^{a
_i} \mid P^{a_j} g$, i.e., $P^{\max(0,\, a_i - a_j)} \mid g$. Thus the valid $g$
 form the ideal $(P^{\max(0, a_i - a_j)})/(P^{a_i})$ inside $\mathbb{Q}[x]/(P^{a
_i})$. This ideal has $\mathbb{Q}$-dimension

$$q\bigl(a_i - \max(0, a_i - a_j)\bigr) = q \cdot \min(a_i, a_j). \quad \square$
$

Applying the lemma:

$$\dim C(A) = q \sum_{i=1}^{s}\sum_{j=1}^{s} \min(a_i, a_j).$$

**Case $(3)$:** $\sum_{i,j}\min(a_i,a_j) = \min(3,3) = 3$, so $\dim C(A) = 3q$.

**Case $(1,2)$:** $\sum_{i,j}\min(a_i,a_j) = 1+1+1+2 = 5$, so $\dim C(A) = 5q$.

**Case $(1,1,1)$:** All $\min(a_i,a_j) = 1$ with $3\times 3 = 9$ terms, so $\sum
 = 9$, giving $\dim C(A) = 9q$.

**Step 3: Verification with $q=1$.** When $q=1$, $P(x)=x-\lambda$ and $A$ has a
single eigenvalue $\lambda$. The three cases correspond to Jordan forms $J_3(\la
mbda)$, $J_1(\lambda)\oplus J_2(\lambda)$, and $\lambda I_3$, with commutant dim
ensions $3$, $5$, and $9$ respectively—matching $3q, 5q, 9q$. ✓

## Conclusion

The possible values of $\dim(C(A))$ are:

$$\boxed{3q, \quad 5q, \quad 9q}$$
