Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether commuting with $g_x$ (for s
ome $x \neq 0, 1$) forces $h$ to be a multiplication map $g_y$.

**Answer:** The implication holds **if and only if** $x$ generates the full exte
nsion $\mathbb{F}_{q^n}/\mathbb{F}_q$. In general (for arbitrary $x \neq 0,1$),
the answer is **NO**.

---

## Proof

Let $d = [\mathbb{F}_q(x) : \mathbb{F}_q]$, the degree of the minimal polynomial
 of $x$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$. Since $x \in \mathbb{F}_{q^n}$, we have $d \mid n$,
 and $\mathbb{F}_q(x) = \mathbb{F}_{q^d} \subseteq \mathbb{F}_{q^n}$.

### Step 1: Compute the centralizer of $g_x$

An $\mathbb{F}_q$-linear map $h \in \text{End}_{\mathbb{F}_q}(V)$ commutes with
$g_x$ (multiplication by $x$) if and only if $h$ is $\mathbb{F}_q[x]$-linear, i.
e., $h \in \text{End}_{\mathbb{F}_q[x]}(V)$.

Now $V = \mathbb{F}_{q^n}$ is a vector space over $\mathbb{F}_{q^d} = \mathbb{F}
_q(x)$ of dimension $n/d$. As an $\mathbb{F}_{q^d}$-module, $\mathbb{F}_{q^n}$ i
s free of rank $n/d$ (it is a field extension of $\mathbb{F}_{q^d}$ of degree $n
/d$). Therefore:

$$\text{End}_{\mathbb{F}_q[x]}(V) \cong \text{End}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^d}}(\mathbb{F}
_{q^n}) \cong M_{n/d}(\mathbb{F}_{q^d}).$$

The centralizer of $g_x$ in $GL(V)$ is thus $\cong GL_{n/d}(\mathbb{F}_{q^d})$.

### Step 2: Where do the multiplication maps sit?

The set $\{g_y : y \in \mathbb{F}_{q^n}\}$ is the image of $\mathbb{F}_{q^n}$ un
der the regular representation over $\mathbb{F}_{q^d}$: each $y \in \mathbb{F}_{
q^n}$ acts by $\mathbb{F}_{q^d}$-linear multiplication on $\mathbb{F}_{q^n} \con
g \mathbb{F}_{q^d}^{\,n/d}$, giving an element of $M_{n/d}(\mathbb{F}_{q^d})$. T
his is precisely the embedding $\mathbb{F}_{q^n} \hookrightarrow M_{n/d}(\mathbb
{F}_{q^d})$.

### Step 3: Dimension comparison

As $\mathbb{F}_q$-vector spaces:

- $\{g_y : y \in \mathbb{F}_{q^n}\} \cong \mathbb{F}_{q^n}$ has $\mathbb{F}_q$-d
imension **$n$**.
- $\text{End}_{\mathbb{F}_q[x]}(V) \cong M_{n/d}(\mathbb{F}_{q^d})$ has $\mathbb
{F}_q$-dimension $(n/d)^2 \cdot d = \mathbf{n^2/d}$.

### Step 4: Case analysis

**Case $d = n$ (i.e., $\mathbb{F}_q(x) = \mathbb{F}_{q^n}$, $x$ is a primitive e
lement):**

Here $n/d = 1$, so $\text{End}_{\mathbb{F}_q[x]}(V) \cong M_1(\mathbb{F}_{q^n})
\cong \mathbb{F}_{q^n}$. The centralizer equals $\{g_y : y \in \mathbb{F}_{q^n}\
}$ exactly. **YES**, the implication holds.

**Case $d < n$ (i.e., $x$ does not generate the full extension):**

Here $n^2/d = n \cdot (n/d) > n$ since $n/d > 1$. The centralizer is **strictly
larger** than $\{g_y\}$. **NO**, the implication fails.

### Step 5: Explicit counterexample ($d < n$)

Take $q = 2$, $n = 4$. Choose $x \in \mathbb{F}_{16}$ with minimal polynomial of
 degree $2$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$ (e.g., a root of $t^2 + t + 1$, so $x \in \mathb
b{F}_4 \subset \mathbb{F}_{16}$, $x \neq 0, 1$). Then $d = 2$, $n/d = 2$.

The centralizer of $g_x$ is $\text{End}_{\mathbb{F}_4}(\mathbb{F}_{16}) \cong M_
2(\mathbb{F}_4)$, which has $\mathbb{F}_2$-dimension $8$. The multiplication map
s $\{g_y : y \in \mathbb{F}_{16}\}$ form an $\mathbb{F}_2$-subspace of dimension
 $4$.

Concretely: $\mathbb{F}_{16} \cong \mathbb{F}_4^2$ as $\mathbb{F}_4$-vector spac
es. The map $h$ that swaps the two $\mathbb{F}_4$-coordinates (relative to some
$\mathbb{F}_4$-basis of $\mathbb{F}_{16}$) is $\mathbb{F}_4$-linear, hence commu
tes with $g_x$, but is **not** $\mathbb{F}_{16}$-linear (it is not a multiplicat
ion map), since $\{g_y\}$ corresponds exactly to the $\mathbb{F}_{16}$-linear (i
.e., $\mathbb{F}_{16}$-scalar) maps, which form a proper subspace.

### Conclusion

The implication "$h$ commutes with $g_x$ $\Rightarrow$ $h = g_y$" holds **if and
 only if** $[\mathbb{F}_q(x) : \mathbb{F}_q] = n$, i.e., $x$ generates $\mathbb{
F}_{q^n}$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$. For a general $x \neq 0, 1$ that does not generat
e the full extension, the centralizer is strictly larger than the set of multipl
ication maps, and the implication **fails**.
