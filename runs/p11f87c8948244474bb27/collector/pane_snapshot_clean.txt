## Solution

**Answer: No, it is not possible for $|S|$ to be less than $1+q$.**

### Proof

We work in the projective plane $\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{F}_q)$, which has $N = q^2
+q+1$ points. The group $G = \operatorname{PGL}(3,q)$ acts transitively on these
 points.

**Reformulation.** The condition $gS \cap S \neq \emptyset$ for every $g \in G$
means: for every $g \in G$, there exist $s_1, s_2 \in S$ (depending on $g$) such
 that $g \cdot s_1 = s_2$. Equivalently,

$$G = \bigcup_{(s_1, s_2) \in S \times S} G_{s_1 \to s_2},$$

where $G_{s_1 \to s_2} := \{g \in G : g \cdot s_1 = s_2\}$.

**Size of each piece.** Since $G$ acts transitively on the $N = q^2+q+1$ points,
 by the orbit–stabilizer theorem, the stabilizer $\operatorname{Stab}(s_1)$ of a
ny point $s_1$ has order $|G|/N$. The set $G_{s_1 \to s_2}$ is a coset of $\oper
atorname{Stab}(s_1)$, so:

$$|G_{s_1 \to s_2}| = \frac{|G|}{q^2+q+1} \quad \text{for every } (s_1, s_2) \in
 S \times S.$$

**Counting argument.** The union above has $|S|^2$ sets, each of size $|G|/(q^2+
q+1)$. For this union to cover all of $G$:

$$|G| \leq |S|^2 \cdot \frac{|G|}{q^2+q+1}.$$

Canceling $|G| > 0$:

$$|S|^2 \geq q^2 + q + 1.$$

**Conclusion.** Since $q \geq 2$ (as a prime power), we have $q^2 + q + 1 > q^2$
, hence:

$$|S| \geq \sqrt{q^2+q+1} > q.$$

Since $|S|$ is an integer, $|S| \geq q+1 = 1+q$.

**Tightness (for completeness).** The bound is achieved by taking $S$ to be a pr
ojective line, which has exactly $q+1$ points. Any homography maps a line to a l
ine, and any two lines in $\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{F}_q)$ intersect, so $gS \cap S
\neq \emptyset$ for all $g \in G$. $\blacksquare$
