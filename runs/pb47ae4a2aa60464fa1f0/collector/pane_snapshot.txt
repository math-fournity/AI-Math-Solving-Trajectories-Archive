## Proof

**Yes**, $V$ has a $T$-invariant subspace of dimension $r$.

**Step 1: $\ker P_1(T)\neq\{0\}$.**

View $V$ as an $F[x]$-module with $x$ acting as $T$. By the invariant factor dec
omposition,
$$V \cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^{s} F[x]/(q_i),\qquad q_1\mid q_2\mid\cdots\mid q_s =
P,$$
where $P$ is the minimal polynomial. Since $P_1\mid P = q_s$, consider in the su
mmand $F[x]/(q_s)$ the element represented by $q_s/P_1$. It is nonzero (its degr
ee is strictly less than $\deg q_s$, so it is not a multiple of $q_s$), yet
$$P_1\cdot (q_s/P_1) = q_s \equiv 0 \pmod{q_s}.$$
Hence this element lies in $\ker P_1(T)$ and is nonzero. So $\ker P_1(T)\neq\{0\
}$.

**Step 2: $\ker P_1(T)$ is $T$-invariant.**

If $v\in\ker P_1(T)$, then $P_1(T)(Tv)=T(P_1(T)v)=T(0)=0$, so $Tv\in\ker P_1(T)$
.

**Step 3: Build an $r$-dimensional $T$-invariant subspace.**

Pick any nonzero $v\in\ker P_1(T)$ and let
$$W = Z(v,T) = \operatorname{span}\{v, Tv, T^2v,\dots\}$$
be the $T$-cyclic subspace generated by $v$. It is $T$-invariant by construction
. The annihilator
$$\operatorname{Ann}(v)=\{f\in F[x]: f(T)v=0\}=(g)$$
is an ideal containing $P_1$ (since $P_1(T)v=0$) and not containing $1$ (since $
v\neq 0$). Thus $g\mid P_1$ and $g\neq 1$. Because $P_1$ is irreducible, $g=P_1$
 up to a unit. Therefore
$$\dim W = \deg g = \deg P_1 = r.$$

So $W$ is a $T$-invariant subspace of $V$ of dimension $r$.

(Remark: the argument works over any field; the characteristic-$0$ hypothesis is
 more than enough.)
