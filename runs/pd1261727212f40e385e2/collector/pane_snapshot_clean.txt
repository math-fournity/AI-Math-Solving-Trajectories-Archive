## Proof

**Theorem.** Every finite extension $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is complete with respect to
 the (extended) $p$-adic norm. In particular, every finite unramified extension
of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is complete.

---

### Step 1. The extended norm exists and is unique

Let $|\cdot|_p$ be the $p$-adic absolute value on $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and let $K/\ma
thbb{Q}_p$ be a finite extension of degree $n$. Define

$$|x|_K := |N_{K/\mathbb{Q}_p}(x)|_p^{1/n}, \qquad x \in K^\times, \qquad |0|_K
= 0.$$

This is a non-archimedean absolute value on $K$ extending $|\cdot|_p$ (one check
s $|xy|_K = |x|_K|y|_K$ and the ultrametric inequality using properties of the n
orm map and the non-archimedean nature of $|\cdot|_p$).

**Uniqueness:** Since $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is complete, the valuation $v_p$ extends un
iquely to $K$ (equivalently, the integral closure of $\mathbb{Z}_p$ in $K$ is a
DVR, and there is a unique prime above $p$). Hence $|\cdot|_K$ is the unique ext
ension of $|\cdot|_p$ to $K$.

---

### Step 2. All norms on a finite-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}_p$-vector space are eq
uivalent

We use the following standard lemma.

**Lemma.** Let $F$ be a field complete with respect to a non-trivial non-archime
dean absolute value $|\cdot|$, and let $V$ be a finite-dimensional $F$-vector sp
ace. Then any two norms $\|\cdot\|_1, \|\cdot\|_2$ on $V$ (satisfying $\|a v\|_i
 = |a|\,\|v\|_i$) are equivalent.

**Proof of Lemma.** Fix a basis $e_1, \dots, e_n$ of $V$ and define the sup-norm
 $\|v\|_\infty = \max_i |a_i|$ for $v = \sum a_i e_i$. It suffices to show any n
orm $\|\cdot\|$ is equivalent to $\|\cdot\|_\infty$.

The inequality $\|v\| \le C\,\|v\|_\infty$ for some constant $C$ follows from th
e ultrametric inequality.

For the reverse inequality, we use **local compactness**: $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is loca
lly compact, so the closed unit ball $B = \{v \in V : \|v\|_\infty \le 1\} = \pr
od_{i=1}^n \mathbb{Z}_p\, e_i$ is compact (as a product of compact sets $\mathbb
{Z}_p$).

The function $\|\cdot\|: B \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ is continuous with respect to
 the $\|\cdot\|_\infty$-topology (by the inequality $\|v\| \le C\|v\|_\infty$).
The set $S = \{v : \|v\|_\infty = 1\}$ is a closed subset of $B$, hence compact.
 Since $\|\cdot\|$ is a norm, $\|v\| > 0$ for all $v \in S$. By compactness, $\|
\cdot\|$ attains a minimum $m > 0$ on $S$:

$$\|v\| \ge m \quad \text{for all } v \text{ with } \|v\|_\infty = 1.$$

By homogeneity, $\|v\| \ge m\,\|v\|_\infty$ for all $v \in V$. This gives equiva
lence. $\square$

---

### Step 3. $K$ is complete

Choose a $\mathbb{Q}_p$-basis $e_1, \dots, e_n$ of $K$ and consider the sup-norm
 $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ as above. Since $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is complete, $(K, \|\cdot\|_\
infty)$ is complete: a $\|\cdot\|_\infty$-Cauchy sequence $\{x_k\}$ with $x_k =
\sum_i a_{k,i}\, e_i$ has each coordinate sequence $\{a_{k,i}\}_k$ Cauchy in $\m
athbb{Q}_p$, hence convergent to some $a_i \in \mathbb{Q}_p$, and $x_k \to \sum_
i a_i e_i \in K$.

By the Lemma, $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ and $|\cdot|_K$ are equivalent norms on the $\m
athbb{Q}_p$-vector space $K$. Equivalent norms define the same Cauchy sequences
and preserve completeness. Therefore $(K, |\cdot|_K)$ is complete. $\square$

---

### Conclusion

Every finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ — and in particular every finite **unra
mified** extension — is complete with respect to the (unique extension of the) $
p$-adic norm. The unramified hypothesis is not needed; completeness holds for al
l finite extensions, ramified or not.
