## Part (a): Weakening real-analytic to $C^1$

**Answer: Yes, the result is still valid.**

**Proof.** We must show:
$$\text{(Cauchy formula with } C^1 \text{ kernel)} \iff \left(\exists\, \{p_j\}
: p_j e_l + p_l e_j = 2\delta_{jl}\right)$$

**($\Leftarrow$) Sufficiency:** If there exist $\{p_j\}$ satisfying $p_j e_l + p
_l e_j = 2\delta_{jl}$, Ryan's construction produces a kernel $G(x,x_0)$ that is
 real-analytic. Since real-analyticity implies $C^1$, the Cauchy formula holds w
ith a $C^1$ kernel. This direction is immediate.

**($\Rightarrow$) Necessity:** Suppose the Cauchy formula
$$f(x_0) = \int_{\partial M} G(x, x_0) \sum_{j=1}^k (-1)^j e_j\, dx_j\, f(x)$$

**Step 1: $G$ is a fundamental solution.** By applying the Cauchy formula to sui

$$D_x G(x, x_0) = \delta(x - x_0) \cdot \mathbf{1}$$
where $\delta$ is the Dirac distribution and $\mathbf{1}$ is the identity in $A$

**Step 2: Fourier analysis.** Taking the Fourier transform of the fundamental so

$$\left(\sum_{j=1}^k i\, e_j\, \xi_j\right) \hat{G}(\xi, x_0) = e^{-i\xi \cdot x

so $\hat{G}(\xi, x_0) = e^{-i\xi \cdot x_0}\left(\sum_j i\, e_j\, \xi_j\right)^{
-1}$, provided the inverse exists in $A$.

**Step 3: The singularity structure forces the algebraic condition.** The Cauchy
 formula must reproduce $f(x_0)$ for *all* left analytic $f$ and *all* manifolds
 $M$. This requires $G$ to have a specific singularity at $x = x_0$ — namely, th
e singularity must be of order $|x - x_0|^{-(k-1)}$ (for $k \geq 3$) or $\log|x
- x_0|$ (for $k = 2$), corresponding to the fundamental solution of a first-orde

In Fourier space, this singularity order corresponds to $\hat{G}(\xi) \sim C/|\x
i|$ as $|\xi| \to \infty$, i.e., $\left(\sum_j e_j\, \xi_j\right)^{-1}$ must dec

The element $\sum_j e_j\, \xi_j \in A$ is linear in $\xi$. Its inverse in the fi
nite-dimensional subalgebra generated by $\{e_j\}$ is a rational function of $\x
i$. For this inverse to decay exactly as $1/|\xi|$, the "symbolic inverse" must
take the form:
$$\left(\sum_j e_j\, \xi_j\right)^{-1} = \frac{\sum_j p_j\, \xi_j}{|\xi|^2}$$
for some elements $\{p_j\} \subset A$. This is because:
- The denominator $|\xi|^2$ (degree 2) is the unique homogeneous polynomial of d
egree 2 that is radial and non-vanishing for $\xi \neq 0$.
- The numerator must be degree 1 in $\xi$ (to give overall $1/|\xi|$ decay), hen
ce linear: $\sum_j p_j \xi_j$.

The identity $\left(\sum_j e_j\, \xi_j\right)\left(\sum_l p_l\, \xi_l\right) = |
\xi|^2 \cdot \mathbf{1}$ expands to:
$$\sum_{j,l} e_j\, p_l\, \xi_j\, \xi_l = \sum_j \xi_j^2 \cdot \mathbf{1}$$

Comparing coefficients: the symmetric part gives $e_j p_l + e_l p_j = 2\delta_{j
l} \cdot \mathbf{1}$, and the antisymmetric part $e_j p_l - e_l p_j$ must vanish

$$p_j e_l + p_l e_j = 2\delta_{jl}$$

**Step 4: $C^1$ implies real-analytic (bootstrap).** Once the algebraic conditio

$\xi \neq 0$). By elliptic regularity with analytic coefficients, any $C^1$ solu

Therefore, **any $C^1$ kernel satisfying the Cauchy formula is automatically rea

## Part (b): Non-associative algebra

**Answer:** In a non-associative algebra, the analogous result is:

> The generalized Cauchy integral formula holds if and only if there exist eleme
nts $\{p_j\}_{j=1}^k$ in $A$ satisfying **both**:
> 1. $p_j e_l + p_l e_j = 2\delta_{jl}$ (the same algebraic relation), **and**
> 2. The **associator condition**: $[p_j, e_l, a] + [p_l, e_j, a] = 0$ for all $
a \in A$ and all $j, l$,
>
> where $[x, y, z] = (xy)z - x(yz)$ is the associator.

**Proof sketch.**

In a non-associative algebra, the product of three or more elements is bracketin
g-dependent. The proof of the Cauchy formula relies on a **Leibniz-type rule** f

$$D(G \cdot f) = (DG) \cdot f + G \cdot (Df)$$

Computing the left side:
$$D(G \cdot f) = \sum_j e_j \cdot \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}(G \cdot f) = \su
m_j e_j \cdot \left(\frac{\partial G}{\partial x_j} \cdot f + G \cdot \frac{\par
tial f}{\partial x_j}\right)$$

The right side is:
$$(DG) \cdot f + G \cdot (Df) = \sum_j (e_j \cdot \frac{\partial G}{\partial x_j
}) \cdot f + \sum_j G \cdot (e_j \cdot \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_j})$$

For these to be equal, we need:
1. $e_j \cdot \left(\frac{\partial G}{\partial x_j} \cdot f\right) = \left(e_j \

2. $e_j \cdot \left(G \cdot \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_j}\right) = G \cdot \le
ft(e_j \cdot \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_j}\right)$

Condition 1 requires the associator $[e_j, \cdot, \cdot]$ to vanish for the rele
vant elements. Condition 2 is a "shifted associativity" that can be rewritten us
ing the associator as:
$$[e_j, G, b] = (e_j \cdot G) \cdot b - G \cdot (e_j \cdot b) \quad \text{where
} b = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_j}$$

For the Leibniz rule to hold for **all** left analytic $f$ and **all** kernels $
G$ (which take values in the subalgebra generated by $\{p_j, e_j\}$), we need th

$$[p_j, e_l, a] + [p_l, e_j, a] = 0 \quad \forall\, a \in A, \quad \forall\, j,
l$$

**Why this specific condition?** The kernel $G$ is constructed from the elements

e Leibniz rule must hold when $G$ takes values in the span of $\{p_j\}$ and $f$
takes values in $A$. The antisymmetric pattern $[p_j, e_l, a] + [p_l, e_j, a] =
0$ mirrors the algebraic condition $p_j e_l + p_l e_j = 2\delta_{jl}$ and ensure
s that the "non-associative correction terms" cancel in the integration by parts

**Equivalence with a combined identity.** Combining conditions (1) and (2):
$$(p_j e_l + p_l e_j) a = p_j(e_l a) + p_l(e_j a) \quad \forall\, a \in A$$
which, using condition (1), becomes:
$$2\delta_{jl}\, a = p_j(e_l a) + p_l(e_j a) \quad \forall\, a \in A$$

This is a single unified condition that encodes both the algebraic relation and
the associator constraint.

**Reduction to the associative case.** In an associative algebra, all associator
s vanish identically: $[x,y,z] = 0$ for all $x,y,z$. Hence condition (2) is auto
matic, and the result reduces to Ryan's original theorem with only condition (1)

**Sufficiency ($\Leftarrow$).** Given conditions (1) and (2), the Leibniz rule $
D(G \cdot f) = (DG) \cdot f + G \cdot (Df)$ holds. Since $DG = 0$ (away from $x_
0$) and $Df = 0$ (left analytic), we get $D(G \cdot f) = 0$ away from $x_0$. App

**Necessity ($\Rightarrow$).** If the Cauchy formula holds, the same Fourier-ana
