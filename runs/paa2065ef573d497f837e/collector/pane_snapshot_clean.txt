## Solution

**Claim:** $\nu(F) = 0$ for every power series $F(x)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n x^n$
converging in a neighborhood of the origin.

**Proof.**

Since $F$ converges in a neighborhood of the origin, there exists $R>0$ such tha
t the series converges (absolutely) for all $|x|<R$. A power series converges un
iformly on every compact subset of its disk of convergence; consequently $F$ is
continuous — indeed real-analytic — on $(-R,R)$.

Pick any $\varepsilon$ with $0<\varepsilon<R$. On the closed interval $[-\vareps
ilon,\varepsilon]\subset(-R,R)$, the function $F$ is continuous, hence bounded:
there exists $M>0$ such that
$$|F(x)|\le M \qquad\text{for all }x\in[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon].$$

Now let $\delta>0$ be arbitrary. Then
$$|F(x)|^{\delta}\le M^{\delta}\qquad\text{on }[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon],$$
so
$$\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}|F(x)|^{\delta}\,dx \;\le\; 2\varepsilon\cdot
 M^{\delta} \;<\;\infty.$$

Thus for **every** $\delta>0$ there exists an $\varepsilon>0$ (any $\varepsilon<
R$ works) making the integral finite. Therefore
$$\bigl\{\delta>0:\exists\,\varepsilon>0\text{ with }\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varep
silon}|F|^{\delta}<\infty\bigr\}=(0,\infty),$$
and taking the infimum gives
$$\nu(F)=\inf(0,\infty)=0.$$

The value is independent of the particular power series: convergence in a neighb
orhood of the origin already forces $F$ to be bounded near $0$, so every positiv
e power $|F|^{\delta}$ is automatically locally integrable. $\blacksquare$
