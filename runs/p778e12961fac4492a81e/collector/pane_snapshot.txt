## Proof

We verify that $S = \{\delta_k : k = 0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \ldots\}$ is an algebraic
(Hamel) basis for $\ell_0$, the vector space of all two-sided sequences with onl
y finitely many non-zero entries. Here $\delta_k$ denotes the sequence with $(\d
elta_k)_j = 1$ if $j = k$ and $(\delta_k)_j = 0$ otherwise.

We must show two things: (i) $S$ is linearly independent, and (ii) $S$ spans $\e
ll_0$.

**Spanning.** Let $x = (x_j)_{j \in \mathbb{Z}} \in \ell_0$. By definition, the
set $\{j : x_j \neq 0\}$ is finite. For every index $j$, the sequence $x_j\,\del
ta_k$ evaluated at position $j$ equals $x_j$, and is zero elsewhere. Hence
$$
x = \sum_{j \in \mathbb{Z}} x_j\,\delta_j,
$$
where the sum is effectively finite (only finitely many $x_j$ are non-zero). Thu
s every $x \in \ell_0$ is a finite linear combination of elements of $S$, so $\o
peratorname{span}(S) = \ell_0$.

**Linear independence.** Suppose a finite linear combination equals the zero seq
uence:
$$
\sum_{k \in F} a_k\,\delta_k = 0,
$$
where $F \subset \mathbb{Z}$ is finite. Evaluating both sides at the position $j
 \in F$ gives
$$
a_j = \bigl(\textstyle\sum_{k \in F} a_k\,\delta_k\bigr)_j = 0.
$$
Since this holds for every $j \in F$, all coefficients are zero. Therefore $S$ i
s linearly independent.

**Conclusion.** $S$ is a linearly independent set that spans $\ell_0$, hence $S$
 is an algebraic basis for $\ell_0$. $\blacksquare$
