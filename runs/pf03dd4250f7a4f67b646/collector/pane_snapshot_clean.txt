**Answer: Yes, $I$ is projective.**

**Proof.**

**Step 1: A short exact sequence from the colon ideal.**

Define the $R$-module homomorphism
$$\varphi: R \longrightarrow (I+Rb)/I, \qquad \varphi(r) = rb + I.$$
This is well-defined since $rb \in I + Rb$ for every $r \in R$. It is surjective
: every element of $(I+Rb)/I$ has the form $a + rb + I$ with $a \in I$, which eq
uals $rb + I = \varphi(r)$. The kernel is
$$\ker\varphi = \{r \in R : rb \in I\} = (I:b).$$
Thus we obtain the short exact sequence
$$0 \longrightarrow (I:b) \xrightarrow{\;\iota\;} R \xrightarrow{\;\varphi\;} (I
+Rb)/I \longrightarrow 0. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2: $(I+Rb)/I$ is projective.**

By hypothesis, $(I:b)$ is projective. Therefore the short exact sequence (1) spl
its, giving
$$R \;\cong\; (I:b) \;\oplus\; (I+Rb)/I.$$
Hence $(I+Rb)/I$ is a direct summand of the free (hence projective) module $R$,
and is therefore projective.

**Step 3: The short exact sequence for $I$.**

There is a natural short exact sequence
$$0 \longrightarrow I \xrightarrow{\;j\;} I+Rb \xrightarrow{\;\pi\;} (I+Rb)/I \l
ongrightarrow 0, \tag{2}$$
where $j$ is the inclusion and $\pi$ is the canonical projection.

**Step 4: $I$ is projective.**

From Step 2, $(I+Rb)/I$ is projective, so the short exact sequence (2) splits. T
his yields
$$I + Rb \;\cong\; I \;\oplus\; (I+Rb)/I.$$
By hypothesis, $I + Rb$ is projective. Since $I$ is a direct summand of the proj
ective module $I + Rb$, it follows that $I$ is projective. $\blacksquare$

 holds for any commutative ring $R$. The condition $b \notin I$ ensures the quot
ient $(I+Rb)/I$ is nonzero, making the question nontrivial, but the proof goes t
