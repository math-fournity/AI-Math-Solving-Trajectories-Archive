## Proof

Let $A = I_n - V$, so that $V = I_n - A$ and $\lVert A \rVert_\infty \le \epsilo
n$.

**Step 1: Bound the spectrum of $A$.**

For any square matrix and any subordinate (induced) matrix norm, the spectral ra
dius is bounded by the norm:
$$\rho(A) \le \lVert A \rVert_\infty \le \epsilon.$$
Hence every eigenvalue $\mu$ of $A$ satisfies $|\mu| \le \epsilon$.

**Step 2: Relate the spectra of $V$ and $A$.**

Since $V = I_n - A$, the eigenvalues of $V$ are exactly $\{1 - \mu : \mu \in \si
gma(A)\}$. Thus every eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $V$ satisfies
$$|\lambda - 1| = |\mu| \le \epsilon.$$
So every eigenvalue of $V$ lies in the closed disk of radius $\epsilon$ centered
 at $1$ in $\mathbb{C}$, which gives
$$\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) \ge 1 - \epsilon$$
for every eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $V$. In particular, when $V$ has real eigenval
ues (e.g. $V$ Hermitian),
$$\lambda_{\min}(V) \ge 1 - \epsilon.$$

**Step 3: Tightness.**

Take $A = \epsilon\, e_1 e_1^*$, i.e. the matrix with $\epsilon$ in the $(1,1)$-
entry and $0$ elsewhere. Its infinity norm is
$$\lVert A \rVert_\infty = \max_i \sum_j |a_{ij}| = \epsilon \quad\text{(row 1 s
ums to } \epsilon\text{, all other rows sum to } 0\text{)}.$$
Then $V = I_n - A$ has eigenvalues $1 - \epsilon$ (eigenvector $e_1$) and $1$ wi
th multiplicity $n-1$. Therefore
$$\lambda_{\min}(V) = 1 - \epsilon,$$
which attains the bound exactly.

**Conclusion.**

The tight lower bound is
$$\boxed{\lambda_{\min}(V) \ge 1 - \epsilon.}$$
