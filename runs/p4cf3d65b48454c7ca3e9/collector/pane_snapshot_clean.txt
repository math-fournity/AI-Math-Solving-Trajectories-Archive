## Proof

Since $\mu$ is an isolated point of $\sigma(T)$, there exists a small positively
-oriented circle $\gamma$ around $\mu$ separating $\{\mu\}$ from $\sigma(T)\setm
inus\{\mu\}$. The **Riesz projection** is
$$P_\mu = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma (\lambda I - T)^{-1}\,d\lambda.$$

**Key facts (standard spectral theory):**

1. $P_\mu \neq 0$ since $\mu \in \sigma(T)$. Indeed, $\ell_2 = M_\mu \oplus N_\m
u$ where $M_\mu = \operatorname{ran}(P_\mu)$, $N_\mu = \ker(P_\mu)$, both $T$-in
variant, with $\sigma(T|_{M_\mu}) = \{\mu\}$ and $\sigma(T|_{N_\mu}) = \sigma(T)
\setminus\{\mu\}$.

2. $P_\mu = I$ if and only if $\sigma(T) = \{\mu\}$ (equivalently $N_\mu = \{0\}
$).

**Laurent expansion of the resolvent.** Near $\lambda = \mu$,
$$(\lambda I - T)^{-1} = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_k}{(\lambda-\mu)^k} + \text{
holomorphic part},$$
where
$$A_k = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma (\lambda-\mu)^{k-1}(\lambda I - T)^{-1}\,d\
lambda.$$

**Claim:** $A_k = (T - \mu I)^{k-1}P_\mu$ for all $k \geq 1$.

*Proof of claim.* By the Riesz functional calculus, $A_k = g(T)$ where $g(\lambd
a) = (\lambda-\mu)^{k-1}$ on a neighborhood of $\mu$ and $g = 0$ on a neighborho
od of $\sigma(T)\setminus\{\mu\}$. Since $g$ decomposes as $g|_{M_\mu}(\lambda)
= (\lambda-\mu)^{k-1}$ and $g|_{N_\mu} = 0$, we get
$$g(T) = (T-\mu I)^{k-1}\big|_{M_\mu} \oplus\, 0\big|_{N_\mu} = (T-\mu I)^{k-1}P
_\mu. \qquad\square$$

**Removable singularity criterion.** For $x \in \ell_2$, the function $R(x)(\lam
bda) = (\lambda I - T)^{-1}x$ has a removable singularity at $\mu$ if and only i
f the principal part vanishes, i.e., $A_k x = 0$ for all $k \geq 1$. Since $A_k
x = (T-\mu I)^{k-1}P_\mu x$, this holds for all $k \geq 1$ if and only if $P_\mu
 x = 0$ (the case $k=1$ is necessary; it is also sufficient since $A_k x = (T-\m
u I)^{k-1}\cdot 0 = 0$).

**Conclusion.** There exists $x \neq 0$ with $R(x)$ removable at $\mu$ if and on
ly if $\ker(P_\mu) \neq \{0\}$, i.e., $P_\mu \neq I$, i.e., $\sigma(T) \neq \{\m
u\}$.

- **If $\sigma(T) = \{\mu\}$:** $P_\mu = I$, so $P_\mu x = 0 \Rightarrow x = 0$.
 No nonzero $x$ works.

- **If $\sigma(T) \neq \{\mu\}$:** $N_\mu = \ker(P_\mu) \neq \{0\}$ (since $\sig
ma(T|_{N_\mu}) = \sigma(T)\setminus\{\mu\} \neq \emptyset$ forces $N_\mu \neq \{
0\}$). Any nonzero $x \in N_\mu$ yields $R(x)$ with a removable singularity at $
\mu$.

**Concrete example.** Let $T$ be the diagonal operator on $\ell_2$ with entries
$(1, \tfrac12, \tfrac13, \ldots)$. Then $\sigma(T) = \{0\}\cup\{1/n : n\geq 1\}$
, and $\mu = 1$ is isolated. The Riesz projection $P_1$ projects onto $\operator
name{span}\{e_1\}$, so $\ker(P_1) = \overline{\operatorname{span}}\{e_2, e_3, \l
dots\} \neq \{0\}$. For $x = e_2$, $R(x)(\lambda) = \frac{1}{\lambda - 1/2}\,e_2
$, which is analytic at $\lambda = 1$.

**Therefore, yes, it is possible** — precisely when the spectrum of $T$ contains
 points other than $\mu$. In that case, any nonzero $x$ in the kernel of the Rie
sz spectral projection $P_\mu$ gives a resolvent $R(x)$ with a removable singula
rity at $\mu$.
