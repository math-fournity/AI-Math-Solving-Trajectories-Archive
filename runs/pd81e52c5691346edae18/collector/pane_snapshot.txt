## Proof

We wish to evaluate
$$I_n := \int_{0}^{\infty} \exp\!\bigl(\cos^{n} x - x\bigr)\,dx = \int_{0}^{\inf
ty} e^{\cos^{n} x}\,e^{-x}\,dx.$$

**Step 1: Pointwise limit of the integrand.**

Consider $\cos^{n} x$ for $x \in [0,\infty)$.

- If $x = 2k\pi$ for some non-negative integer $k$, then $\cos x = 1$, so $\cos^
{n} x = 1$ for every $n$.
- For every other $x$, we have $|\cos x| < 1$, hence $|\cos x|^{n} \to 0$ as $n
\to \infty$. Whether $\cos x$ is positive or negative, $|\cos^{n} x| = |\cos x|^
{n} \to 0$, so $\cos^{n} x \to 0$.

The exceptional set $\{2k\pi : k = 0, 1, 2, \dots\}$ is countable, hence has Leb
esgue measure zero. Therefore, for almost every $x \in [0,\infty)$,
$$\cos^{n} x \to 0, \qquad \text{so} \qquad e^{\cos^{n} x - x} \to e^{0 - x} = e
^{-x}.$$

**Step 2: Dominating function.**

Since $\cos^{n} x \le 1$ for all $x$ and all $n$ (as $|\cos x| \le 1$ implies $\
cos^{n} x \le |\cos x|^{n} \le 1$ when $\cos^n x$ is considered — note $\cos^n x
 \le |\cos x|^n \le 1$), we have
$$e^{\cos^{n} x - x} \le e^{1 - x} = e \cdot e^{-x}.$$

The function $g(x) = e \cdot e^{-x}$ is integrable on $[0,\infty)$:
$$\int_{0}^{\infty} e \cdot e^{-x}\,dx = e.$$

**Step 3: Apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem.**

The sequence of measurable functions $f_n(x) = e^{\cos^{n} x - x}$ satisfies:
1. $f_n(x) \to e^{-x}$ pointwise a.e. on $[0,\infty)$,
2. $|f_n(x)| \le g(x) = e \cdot e^{-x}$ for all $n$ and all $x$, with $g \in L^{
1}([0,\infty))$.

By the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem,
$$\lim_{n \to \infty} I_n = \int_{0}^{\infty} \lim_{n \to \infty} e^{\cos^{n} x
- x}\,dx = \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-x}\,dx = \left[-e^{-x}\right]_{0}^{\infty} = 1.
$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\lim_{n \to \infty} \int_{0}^{\infty} \exp\!\bigl(\cos^{n} x - x\bigr)\
,dx = 1.}$$
