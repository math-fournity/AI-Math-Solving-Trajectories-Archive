Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the complement of the kerne

e abelian.

**Answer: No.**

## Proof

I will construct an explicit counterexample: a sharply 2-transitive Frobenius gr
oup with elementary abelian kernel whose complement is the quaternion group $Q_8
$ (non-abelian).

### Setup

Let $K = (\mathbb{Z}/3)^2$ (elementary abelian of order $9$, so $p=3$, $d=2$, $n
=9$). We seek a complement $H$ of order $n-1 = 8$ acting fixed-point-freely on $
K$.

### Constructing $Q_8 \hookrightarrow \mathrm{GL}(2,3)$

Define matrices over $\mathbb{F}_3$:
$$a = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 2 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad b = \begin{pmatrix} 1
 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

**Verification of the quaternion relations:**
- $a^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} = -I$, so $a^4 = I$ (order
 $4$).
- $b^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1+1 & 1+2 \\ 1+2 & 1+4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}
2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} = -I$ (order $4$).
- So $a^2 = b^2 = -I$. ✓
- $bab^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = a^3 = a^{-1}$. ✓ (c
omputed directly)

Thus $H = \langle a, b \rangle \cong Q_8 = \langle a, b \mid a^4 = 1,\ a^2 = b^2
,\ bab^{-1} = a^{-1}\rangle$, a subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}(2,3)$ of order $8$.

### Fixed-point-free action

The non-identity elements of $Q_8$ are $a, a^2, a^3, b, ab, a^2b, a^3b$. I compu
te the characteristic polynomial of each:

- **Elements of order 4** ($a, a^3, b, ab, a^2b, a^3b$): each has $\operatorname
{tr} = 0$ and $\det = 1$, so the characteristic polynomial is $x^2 + 1$.

  Over $\mathbb{F}_3$, $x^2 + 1$ is irreducible (its discriminant is $-4 \equiv
2 \pmod{3}$, and $2$ is not a square mod $3$). Hence these elements have **no ei
genvalues in $\mathbb{F}_3$**, in particular no eigenvalue $1$, so they fix no n
onzero vector.

- **The central element** $a^2 = b^2 = -I$: eigenvalue $-1 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$, n
o eigenvalue $1$. Fixed-point-free. ✓

Therefore $C_K(h) = \{0\}$ for every $h \in H \setminus \{1\}$.

### Sharp transitivity on nonzero vectors

Since $H$ acts fixed-point-freely (freely) on $K \setminus \{0\}$, every orbit h
as size $|H| = 8$. Since $|K \setminus \{0\}| = 9 - 1 = 8$, there is a single or
bit. So $H$ acts **sharply transitively** on $K \setminus \{0\}$.

### The Frobenius group

Define $G = K \rtimes H = (\mathbb{Z}/3)^2 \rtimes Q_8$.

- **$K$ is normal** in $G$: ✓ (semidirect product).
- **$C_K(h) = \{0\}$ for all $h \neq 1$**: ✓ (established above). So $G$ is a **
Frobenius group** with kernel $K$ and complement $H$.
- **$|G| = |K| \cdot |H| = 9 \cdot 8 = 72 = n(n-1)$** where $n = 9$. ✓
- **$G$ is 2-transitive**: $G$ acts on the $n = 9$ cosets of $H$. The point stab
ilizer $H$ has order $n - 1 = 8$ and acts sharply transitively on the remaining
$8$ points (equivalently, on $K \setminus \{0\}$). Hence $G$ is **sharply 2-tran
sitive**, in particular 2-transitive. ✓
- **$K = (\mathbb{Z}/3)^2$ is an elementary abelian $p$-group** ($p = 3$). ✓
- **$H \cong Q_8$ is non-abelian**. ✓

### Conclusion

The group $G = (\mathbb{Z}/3)^2 \rtimes Q_8$ is a 2-transitive Frobenius group w
ith $|G| = 9 \cdot 8 = n(n-1)$, whose kernel is the elementary abelian $3$-group
 $(\mathbb{Z}/3)^2$, and whose complement $Q_8$ is **non-abelian**.

The answer is **no** — the complement of the kernel is not always abelian.
