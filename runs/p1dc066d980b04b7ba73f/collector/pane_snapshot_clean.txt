## Solution

Bob's favorite number satisfies:
- Between 50 and X
- Multiple of 11
- Not a multiple of 2 (i.e., odd)
- Sum of digits is a multiple of 3

We are told the answer is **99**. We must determine X.

**Step 1: List odd multiples of 11 greater than 50.**

Multiples of 11: 55, 66, 77, 88, 99, 110, 121, 132, 143, 154, 165, 176, 187, 198
, 209, 220, 231, ...

Odd ones (not divisible by 2): 55, 77, 99, 121, 143, 187, 209, 231, ...

**Step 2: Check the digit-sum condition (sum of digits divisible by 3).**

- 55 → 5+5 = 10, not divisible by 3 ✗
- 77 → 7+7 = 14, not divisible by 3 ✗
- 99 → 9+9 = 18, divisible by 3 ✓
- 121 → 1+2+1 = 4, not divisible by 3 ✗
- 143 → 1+4+3 = 8, not divisible by 3 ✗
- 187 → 1+8+7 = 16, not divisible by 3 ✗
- 209 → 2+0+9 = 11, not divisible by 3 ✗
- 231 → 2+3+1 = 6, divisible by 3 ✓

**Step 3: Determine X.**

For 99 to be the **unique** answer, the range (50, X) must:
- Include 99, so X > 99, i.e., X ≥ 100.
- Exclude 231 (the next qualifying number), so X ≤ 230.

The natural and intended bound that makes this a clean problem is **X = 100**, g
iving the range "between 50 and 100." Within (50, 100), the odd multiples of 11
are 55, 77, 99 — and only 99 has a digit sum divisible by 3.

**Verification:** With X = 100, the only number between 50 and 100 that is an od
d multiple of 11 with digit sum divisible by 3 is **99**. ✓

The value of X is **100**.
