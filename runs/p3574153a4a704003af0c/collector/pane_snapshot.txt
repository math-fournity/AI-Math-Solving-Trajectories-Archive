Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the injectivity of $f \otim
es \mathrm{id}$ for all ideals $I$ forces $\mathrm{coker}(f)$ to be flat (i.e.,
$\mathrm{Tor}_1^A(\mathrm{coker}(f), T) = 0$ for all $T$).

**Answer: No.** Here is the justification with an explicit counterexample.

---

## Key Observation

The condition $f \otimes \mathrm{id}: M \otimes A/I \to N \otimes A/I$ injective
 for every ideal $I$ implies that $0 \to \mathrm{im}(f) \to N$ is a **pure** sub
module inclusion.

**Proof of this claim.** The map $f$ factors as $M \twoheadrightarrow \mathrm{im
}(f) \hookrightarrow N$. Tensoring with $A/I$, the map $M \otimes A/I \to \mathr
m{im}(f) \otimes A/I$ is surjective (right exactness of tensor). If the composit
ion $M \otimes A/I \to \mathrm{im}(f) \otimes A/I \to N \otimes A/I$ is injectiv
e and the first factor is surjective, then the second factor $\mathrm{im}(f) \ot
imes A/I \to N \otimes A/I$ must be injective. Since this holds for all ideals $
I$ (in particular all finitely generated $I$), the inclusion $0 \to \mathrm{im}(
f) \to N$ is pure. $\square$

However, **purity of one specific sequence does not imply flatness of the quotie
nt.** A module $F$ is flat if and only if *every* short exact sequence $0 \to M'
 \to N' \to F \to 0$ is pure. A single pure (even split) sequence ending in a no
n-flat module is entirely possible.

---

## Explicit Counterexample

Take $A = \mathbb{Z}$. Set
$$M = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \quad N = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}
/2\mathbb{Z}, \quad f(a) = (a, a).$$

**The cokernel.** We have $\mathrm{coker}(f) = (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^2 / \lan
gle(1,1)\rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, which is **not flat** over $\math
bb{Z}$ (it is a torsion module).

**Verification of the condition.** The sequence
$$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{f} (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^2 \to \m
athbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \to 0$$
is **split exact** (a complement to $\langle(1,1)\rangle$ is $\langle(1,0)\rangl
e$). Every split exact sequence is pure, so $f \otimes \mathrm{id}: M \otimes \m
athbb{Z}/I \to N \otimes \mathbb{Z}/I$ is injective for every ideal $I$ of $\mat
hbb{Z}$.

Concretely, the ideals of $\mathbb{Z}$ are $(n)$ for $n \geq 0$:
- **$n = 0$** ($I = 0$, $A/I = \mathbb{Z}$): $f \otimes \mathbb{Z}$ is $a \mapst
o (a,a)$ on $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, injective. ✓
- **$n$ odd**: $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} = 0$ and $
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^2 \otimes \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} = 0$; the zero map $0
\to 0$ is injective. ✓
- **$n$ even**: $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \cong \ma
thbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ and $(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^2 \otimes \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb
{Z} \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^2$; the map $a \mapsto (a,a)$ is injective. ✓

**Tor is nonzero.** Using the free resolution $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\cd
ot 2} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \to 0$ and tensoring with $T = \math
bb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$:
$$\mathrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},\; \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
= \ker\!\big(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\cdot 2} \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
\big) = \ker(0) = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \neq 0.$$

---

## Why the Implication Fails (Structural Explanation)

From the long exact Tor sequence associated to $0 \to \mathrm{im}(f) \to N \to \
mathrm{coker}(f) \to 0$:
$$\mathrm{Tor}_1(N, T) \to \mathrm{Tor}_1(\mathrm{coker}(f), T) \to \mathrm{im}(
f) \otimes T \to N \otimes T$$

Purity of $0 \to \mathrm{im}(f) \to N$ forces $\mathrm{im}(f) \otimes T \to N \o
times T$ to be injective, so the connecting map $\mathrm{Tor}_1(\mathrm{coker}(f
), T) \to \mathrm{im}(f) \otimes T$ is zero. This yields:
$$\mathrm{Tor}_1(\mathrm{coker}(f), T) = \mathrm{im}\!\big(\mathrm{Tor}_1(N, T)
\to \mathrm{Tor}_1(\mathrm{coker}(f), T)\big).$$

This can be **nonzero** whenever $N$ itself has torsion. In our example, $N = (\
mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^2$ has $\mathrm{Tor}_1(N, \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}) = (\mat
hbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^2 \neq 0$, and the surjection onto $\mathrm{Tor}_1(\mathrm{c
oker}(f), \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is nonzero.

The condition $f \otimes \mathrm{id}$ injective for all $I$ controls the **purit
y of the specific sequence** $0 \to \mathrm{im}(f) \to N$, but flatness of $\mat
hrm{coker}(f)$ would require **every** sequence ending in $\mathrm{coker}(f)$ to
 be pure — a strictly stronger requirement.

---

**Conclusion:** The implication does **not** hold. The condition that $f \otimes
 \mathrm{id}$ is injective for every ideal $I$ only guarantees that $0 \to \math
rm{im}(f) \to N$ is pure, which is insufficient to force $\mathrm{coker}(f)$ to
be flat.
