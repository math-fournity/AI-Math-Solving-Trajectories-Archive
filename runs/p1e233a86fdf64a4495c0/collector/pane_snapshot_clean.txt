## Solution

**The statement is TRUE.**

### Proof

Let $A_1, \dots, A_n$ be real $m \times m$ matrices (the argument is independent
 of size) satisfying
$$S_k := A_1^k + A_2^k + \cdots + A_n^k = 0 \quad \text{for all } k \in \mathbb{
N}_{>0}.$$

We work over $\mathbb{C}$ (complexifying each $A_i$), which does not affect nilp
otence: a real matrix is nilpotent over $\mathbb{R}$ iff it is nilpotent over $\
mathbb{C}$.

**Step 1: Power sums of eigenvalues vanish.**

Taking the trace of $S_k = 0$ gives
$$0 = \operatorname{tr}(S_k) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \operatorname{tr}(A_i^k) = \sum_{i
=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m} \lambda_{ij}^k,$$
where $\lambda_{i1},\dots,\lambda_{im}$ are the eigenvalues of $A_i$ (with algeb
raic multiplicity), using $\operatorname{tr}(A_i^k)=\sum_j \lambda_{ij}^k$.

Collect all $N := nm$ eigenvalues into one multiset $\{\mu_1,\dots,\mu_N\}$. The
n
$$\sum_{\ell=1}^{N} \mu_\ell^k = 0 \quad \text{for all } k \ge 1. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Step 2: All eigenvalues are zero.**

Let the *distinct nonzero* values among $\mu_1,\dots,\mu_N$ be $\nu_1,\dots,\nu_
r$ with respective multiplicities $c_1,\dots,c_r \ge 1$. Equation $(\ast)$ becom
es
$$\sum_{s=1}^{r} c_s\, \nu_s^k = 0 \quad \text{for all } k \ge 1.$$

Restricting to $k = 1, 2, \dots, r$ yields the linear system $V\mathbf{c} = \mat
hbf{0}$, where $V_{k,s} = \nu_s^k$. Factoring $\nu_s$ out of column $s$,
$$\det V = \left(\prod_{s=1}^{r} \nu_s\right) \cdot \det\!\big[\nu_s^{k-1}\big]_
{k,s=1}^{r} = \left(\prod_{s=1}^{r} \nu_s\right)\cdot \prod_{1\le a < b \le r}(\
nu_b - \nu_a).$$
This is nonzero: the $\nu_s$ are distinct and nonzero. Hence $V$ is invertible,
forcing $\mathbf{c} = \mathbf{0}$, i.e. $c_s = 0$ for all $s$—contradicting $c_s
 \ge 1$.

Therefore $r = 0$: there are **no** nonzero eigenvalues. Every $\mu_\ell = 0$, s
o every eigenvalue of every $A_i$ is $0$.

**Step 3: Conclude nilpotence.**

A matrix is nilpotent iff all its eigenvalues are zero (equivalently, its charac
teristic polynomial is $t^m$). Since every eigenvalue of each $A_i$ is $0$, each
 $A_i$ is nilpotent. $\blacksquare$
