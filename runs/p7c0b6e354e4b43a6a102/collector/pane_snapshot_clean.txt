## Solution

**Setup.** Let $\phi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ be a standard mollifier with
$\phi \geq 0$, $\text{supp}(\phi) \subset B(0,1)$, and $\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \phi
 = 1$. Define $\phi_\epsilon(x) = \epsilon^{-n}\phi(x/\epsilon)$ and $f_\epsilon
 = \phi_\epsilon * f$.

**Assumption.** $f \in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, i.e., $f \in L^1$ and its weak gr
adient $\nabla f \in L^1$.

**Claim.** $\|f_\epsilon - f\|_{L^1} = O(\epsilon)$, specifically
$$\|f_\epsilon - f\|_{L^1} \leq C\,\epsilon\,\|\nabla f\|_{L^1},$$
where $C = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \phi(z)|z|\,dz$ depends only on the mollifier.

---

### Proof

**Step 1: Rewrite the error using $\int \phi_\epsilon = 1$.**

Since $\int \phi_\epsilon(x-y)\,dy = \int \phi_\epsilon = 1$,

$$f_\epsilon(x) - f(x) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \phi_\epsilon(x-y)\bigl[f(y) - f(x)
\bigr]\,dy.$$

Substitute $z = (x-y)/\epsilon$, so $y = x - \epsilon z$, $dy = \epsilon^n dz$,
and $\phi_\epsilon(x-y)\,dy = \phi(z)\,dz$:

$$f_\epsilon(x) - f(x) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \phi(z)\bigl[f(x - \epsilon z) - f(
x)\bigr]\,dz.$$

**Step 2: Use the weak derivative via the fundamental theorem of calculus.**

Since $f \in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, for a.e. $x$ and every $z$,

$$f(x - \epsilon z) - f(x) = -\epsilon \int_0^1 \nabla f(x - t\epsilon z)\cdot z
\, dt.$$

This holds by the ACL characterization of Sobolev functions (applied along the l
ine segment from $x$ to $x - \epsilon z$).

**Step 3: Estimate pointwise.**

$$\bigl|f_\epsilon(x) - f(x)\bigr| \leq \epsilon \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \phi(z)\,|z
| \int_0^1 \bigl|\nabla f(x - t\epsilon z)\bigr|\,dt\,dz.$$

**Step 4: Integrate in $x$ and use Fubini + translation invariance of $L^1$.**

$$\|f_\epsilon - f\|_{L^1} \leq \epsilon \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \phi(z)\,|z| \int_0
^1 \underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \bigl|\nabla f(x - t\epsilon z)\bigr|\,dx}_{=
\,\|\nabla f\|_{L^1}}\,dt\,dz.$$

The inner integral equals $\|\nabla f\|_{L^1}$ by translation invariance of the
Lebesgue integral. Therefore:

$$\|f_\epsilon - f\|_{L^1} \leq \epsilon \,\|\nabla f\|_{L^1} \int_{\mathbb{R}^n
} \phi(z)\,|z|\,dz \cdot \int_0^1 dt = C\,\epsilon\,\|\nabla f\|_{L^1},$$

where $C = \int \phi(z)|z|\,dz < \infty$ (finite since $\phi$ has compact suppor
t and $|z|$ is bounded on $\text{supp}(\phi)$).

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\|f_\epsilon - f\|_{L^1} \leq C\,\epsilon\,\|\nabla f\|_{L^1} = O(\epsi
lon).}$$

The rate of convergence is **$O(\epsilon)$**.

---

**Remark on sharpness and generalization.** The rate $O(\epsilon)$ is sharp for
$f \in W^{1,1}$: one cannot in general do better without additional regularity.
More generally, if $f \in W^{k,1}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, iterating the argument (or usi
ng a Taylor expansion with integral remainder to order $k-1$) yields

$$\|f_\epsilon - f\|_{L^1} = O(\epsilon^k).$$

For $f \in L^1$ only (no weak differentiability), no polynomial rate is guarante
ed—the convergence $\|f_\epsilon - f\|_{L^1} \to 0$ holds but the rate is govern
ed by the $L^1$-modulus of continuity of $f$, which can be arbitrarily slow.
