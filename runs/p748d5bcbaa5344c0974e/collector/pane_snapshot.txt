## Solution

**Step 1: Identify the geometry.**

The surface $\Sigma$ is defined by two conditions:
- $(x-2y)^2 + (y-x)^2 = 1$ (a cylinder extruded in the $z$-direction, since $z$
does not appear)
- $0 \le x+y+2z \le 1$ (two parallel cutting planes)

Expanding the first condition: $2x^2 - 6xy + 5y^2 = 1$, which is an ellipse $C$
in the $xy$-plane, centered at the origin (no linear terms).

**Step 2: Parametrize the surface.**

Parametrize $\Sigma$ as $\mathbf{r}(t, z) = (x(t),\, y(t),\, z)$, where $(x(t),
y(t))$ traces $C$ and $z$ satisfies the plane constraint:
$$z_1(t) = -\frac{x(t)+y(t)}{2}, \qquad z_2(t) = \frac{1 - x(t) - y(t)}{2}.$$

The $z$-interval length is:
$$z_2 - z_1 = \frac{1 - x - y}{2} + \frac{x + y}{2} = \frac{1}{2},$$
which is **constant** for all points on $C$.

**Step 3: Compute the surface element.**

$$\mathbf{r}_t \times \mathbf{r}_z = (x'(t),\, y'(t),\, 0) \times (0,0,1) = (y'(
t),\, -x'(t),\, 0),$$
$$|\mathbf{r}_t \times \mathbf{r}_z| = \sqrt{(x'(t))^2 + (y'(t))^2} = ds,$$
where $ds$ is the arc-length element along $C$. Thus $dS = ds\, dz$.

**Step 4: Reduce to a line integral.**

$$\int_\Sigma x\, dS = \int_C \int_{z_1}^{z_2} x\, dz\, ds = \int_C x \cdot \fra
c{1}{2}\, ds = \frac{1}{2}\int_C x\, ds.$$

**Step 5: Evaluate $\int_C x\, ds$ by symmetry.**

The curve $C: 2x^2 - 6xy + 5y^2 = 1$ is **centrally symmetric**: if $(x,y) \in C
$, then $(-x,-y) \in C$ (all terms are quadratic). Under the map $(x,y) \to (-x,
-y)$:
- $x \to -x$ (odd),
- $ds \to ds$ (arc length is preserved).

Therefore:
$$\int_C x\, ds = \int_C (-x)\, ds = -\int_C x\, ds \implies \int_C x\, ds = 0.$
$

**Step 6: Conclusion.**

$$\int_\Sigma x\, dS = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0 = \boxed{0}.$$
