**Answer: Yes, $X$ is a discrete scheme.**

**Proof.**

Recall that a scheme is *discrete* iff its underlying topological space is discr
ete, i.e. every point is open (equivalently, every point is clopen). The hypothe
sis gives us that every *closed* point is open; it remains to show that **every
point of $X$ is closed**. Once this is established, the hypothesis immediately i
mplies every point is open, hence $X$ is discrete.

We use two standard facts about schemes locally of finite type over a field $k$:

1. **Jacobson property.** A scheme locally of finite type over a field is a *Jac
obson scheme*: every non-empty closed subset is the closure (within that subset)
 of its closed points. In particular, every non-empty closed subset of $X$ conta
ins a closed point of $X$. Equivalently, every point of $X$ admits a closed spec
ialization: for every $x\in X$, the closed subset $\overline{\{x\}}$ contains a
closed point of $X$.

2. **Irreducibility of point closures.** For any point $x$ of a scheme, $\overli
ne{\{x\}}$ is an irreducible closed subset.

Now let $x\in X$ be arbitrary. By fact (1), the irreducible closed subset $Z:=\o
verline{\{x\}}$ contains a closed point $y$ of $X$. By the hypothesis, $\{y\}$ i
s open in $X$; intersecting with $Z$, the singleton $\{y\}$ is open in $Z$ (subs
pace topology).

Since $y$ is closed in $X$, the singleton $\{y\}$ is also closed in $Z$. Its com
plement $Z\setminus\{y\}$ is closed in $Z$ (being the complement of an open set)
. Thus we have a decomposition
$$Z \;=\; \{y\}\;\cup\;(Z\setminus\{y\}),$$
where both summands are *closed* subsets of $Z$.

But $Z=\overline{\{x\}}$ is irreducible (fact (2)), and an irreducible space can
not be written as the union of two proper closed subsets. Hence one of the two s
ummands must equal $Z$:

- If $\{y\}=Z$, then $x\in Z=\{y\}$, so $x=y$; in particular $x$ is closed.
- If $Z\setminus\{y\}=Z$, then $y\notin Z$, contradicting the choice of $y\in Z$
.

Therefore $x=y$, and $x$ is a closed point.

Since $x\in X$ was arbitrary, **every point of $X$ is closed**. By the hypothesi
s, every (closed) point is also open. Hence every singleton $\{x\}$ is open, whi
ch is to say the underlying topological space of $X$ is discrete.

Therefore $X$ is a discrete scheme. $\blacksquare$
