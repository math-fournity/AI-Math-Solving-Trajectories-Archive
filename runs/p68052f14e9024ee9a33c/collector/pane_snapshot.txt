Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum number of students $m$ given
 the constraints on subjects taken.

## Setup and Notation

Let $S$ be the set of students ($|S|=m$) and $T$ the set of subjects. For studen
t $s$, let $A_s \subseteq T$ be the subjects taken by $s$. For subject $t$, let
$d_t = |\{s \in S : t \in A_s\}|$ be the number of students taking subject $t$.

The conditions translate to:
- $1 \le |A_s| \le p+1$ for all $s$,
- $d_t \le p$ for all $t$,
- $A_{s_1} \cap A_{s_2} \ne \emptyset$ for all distinct $s_1, s_2$.

## Upper Bound: $m \le p^2$

**Counting shared pairs.** Since every pair of students shares at least one subj
ect, the sum $\sum_{t \in T} \binom{d_t}{2}$ (which counts pairs of students sha
ring a subject, with multiplicity) satisfies:

$$\sum_{t \in T} \binom{d_t}{2} \ge \binom{m}{2}, \quad \text{i.e.,} \quad \sum_
{t} d_t(d_t - 1) \ge m(m-1). \tag{1}$$

**Bounding each term.** For every subject $t$ with $d_t \le p$:

$$d_t(d_t - 1) \le (p-1)\,d_t,$$

since $d_t - 1 \le p - 1$ (and this holds trivially when $d_t = 0$). Summing ove
r all subjects:

$$\sum_t d_t(d_t-1) \le (p-1)\sum_t d_t. \tag{2}$$

**Relating to student counts.** Let $D = \sum_t d_t = \sum_s |A_s|$. By conditio
n (i), $|A_s| \le p+1$, so:

$$D = \sum_s |A_s| \le m(p+1). \tag{3}$$

**Combining.** From (1), (2), and (3):

$$m(m-1) \le (p-1)\,D \le (p-1)\,m(p+1) = m(p^2 - 1).$$

Dividing by $m \ge 1$:

$$m - 1 \le p^2 - 1 \implies m \le p^2.$$

## Construction Achieving $m = p^2$

We use the **affine plane** $\mathrm{AG}(2, p)$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$, which exist
s since $p$ is prime.

- **Points:** The $p^2$ elements of $\mathbb{F}_p^2$ — these are our **students*
*.
- **Lines:** The $p(p+1)$ affine lines in $\mathbb{F}_p^2$ — these are our **sub
jects**.

The standard properties of $\mathrm{AG}(2,p)$ give:

1. **Each line contains exactly $p$ points** → each subject is taken by exactly
$p$ students. ✓ (condition (ii) with equality)
2. **Each point lies on exactly $p+1$ lines** → each student takes exactly $p+1$
 subjects. ✓ (condition (i) with equality)
3. **Any two distinct points lie on exactly one common line** → any two students
 share exactly one subject. ✓ (condition (iii))
4. **Every student takes at least one subject** (in fact $p+1 \ge 2$). ✓

All conditions are satisfied with $m = p^2$ students.

## Conclusion

The upper bound $m \le p^2$ is achieved by the affine plane construction, so the
 maximum possible value is:

$$\boxed{m = p^2}$$
