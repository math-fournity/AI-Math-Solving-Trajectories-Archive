## Proof

**Setup.** Let $a_{i,j}\in\{0,1\}$ denote whether plot $(i,j)$ is blue, for $1\l
e i,j\le 27$.

**Step 1: Structure from the $2\times 2$ constraint.**

The condition that every $2\times 2$ block has even sum means, over $\mathbb{F}_
2$:
$$a_{i,j}\oplus a_{i+1,j}\oplus a_{i,j+1}\oplus a_{i+1,j+1}=0\quad\text{for all
valid }i,j.$$
This is the classical condition that the matrix is a **sum of a row-vector and a
 column-vector** over $\mathbb{F}_2$: there exist binary sequences $r_1,\dots,r_
{27}$ and $c_1,\dots,c_{27}$ such that
$$a_{i,j}=r_i\oplus c_j\qquad\text{for all }i,j.$$
(One can take $r_i=a_{i,1}$ and $c_j=a_{1,j}\oplus a_{1,1}$; the $2\times2$ cond
ition guarantees consistency.)

**Step 2: Translate the $3\times 3$ constraint.**

For a $3\times 3$ block at rows $i,i+1,i+2$ and columns $j,j+1,j+2$, the number
of blue cells is
$$\sum_{p=0}^{2}\sum_{q=0}^{2}(r_{i+p}\oplus c_{j+q}).$$
Setting $R=r_i+r_{i+1}+r_{i+2}$ and $C=c_j+c_{j+1}+c_{j+2}$ (integer counts of 1
s), a direct count gives
$$\text{block sum}=3R+3C-2RC.$$
Modulo 2 this is $R+C\pmod 2$. The constraint requires the sum to be **odd**, so
$$R\not\equiv C\pmod 2\quad\text{for every row-triple }i\text{ and column-triple
 }j.$$

**Step 3: All triples have a fixed parity.**

If two row-triples had different parities, then fixing any column-triple $j$ wou
ld force $C$ to be both even and odd — a contradiction. Hence **all** row-triple
s share one parity $p_R$ and **all** column-triples share the opposite parity $p
_C$.

The condition "$r_i\oplus r_{i+1}\oplus r_{i+2}$ is constant" implies $r_i=r_{i+
3}$ for all $i$ (subtract consecutive equations). Since $27=9\times 3$, the sequ
ence $r$ is periodic with period 3, determined by $(r_1,r_2,r_3)$; likewise $c$
by $(c_1,c_2,c_3)$. Let
$$s_r=r_1+r_2+r_3,\qquad s_c=c_1+c_2+c_3,\qquad s_r,s_c\in\{0,1,2,3\}.$$
The common parities are $s_r\bmod 2$ and $s_c\bmod 2$, and they must differ:

- **Case A:** $s_r$ even, $s_c$ odd $\Rightarrow$ $s_r\in\{0,2\}$, $s_c\in\{1,3\
}$.
- **Case B:** $s_r$ odd, $s_c$ even $\Rightarrow$ $s_r\in\{1,3\}$, $s_c\in\{0,2\
}$.

**Step 4: Count blue cells.**

Each of $r_1,r_2,r_3$ appears in 9 positions, so $n_r=9s_r$ rows have $r_i=1$, a
nd $n_c=9s_c$ columns have $c_j=1$. A cell is blue iff exactly one of $r_i,c_j$
is 1, giving
$$\text{blue count}=n_r(27-n_c)+(27-n_r)n_c=27(n_r+n_c)-2n_r n_c=243(s_r+s_c)-16
2\,s_r s_c.$$

**Step 5: Exclude the all-blue configuration and maximize.**

All cells are blue iff all $r_i$ are equal, all $c_j$ are equal, and they differ
, i.e. $\{s_r,s_c\}=\{0,3\}$. This occurs only at $(s_r,s_c)=(0,3)$ in Case A an
d $(3,0)$ in Case B — both yield $729$ and are **excluded** by the requirement t
hat at least one plot is grey.

Evaluating all remaining cases:

| Case | $(s_r,s_c)$ | Blue count |
|------|-------------|------------|
| A | $(0,1)$ | $243$ |
| A | $(2,1)$ | $243\cdot3-162\cdot2=729-324=\mathbf{405}$ |
| A | $(2,3)$ | $243\cdot5-162\cdot6=1215-972=243$ |
| B | $(1,0)$ | $243$ |
| B | $(1,2)$ | $243\cdot3-162\cdot2=\mathbf{405}$ |
| B | $(3,2)$ | $243$ |

The maximum is $\mathbf{405}$, achieved for example by $(s_r,s_c)=(2,1)$: take $
(r_1,r_2,r_3)=(1,1,0)$ and $(c_1,c_2,c_3)=(1,0,0)$, extended periodically. Then
$n_r=18$, $n_c=9$, and the blue count is $27\cdot27-2\cdot18\cdot9=729-324=405$.
 One verifies: every $2\times2$ block has even sum (guaranteed by the $r_i\oplus
 c_j$ form), every $3\times3$ block has sum $3\cdot2+3\cdot1-2\cdot2\cdot1=5$ (o
dd), and not all cells are blue (e.g. $a_{i,j}=0$ whenever $r_i=1,c_j=1$).

**The maximum possible number of blue-panel plots is $\boxed{405}$.**
