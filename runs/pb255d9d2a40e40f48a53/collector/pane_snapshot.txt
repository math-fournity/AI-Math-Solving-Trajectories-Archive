## Solution

**Interpreting the problem:** The text "We have erasers" appears to have lost it
s count. Based on the standard version of this well-known problem, there are **3
 erasers**, 4 pencils, 2 notebooks, and 3 pens. Items of the same type are indis
tinguishable.

**Setup.** We have four types of stationery with counts:
- Erasers: 3
- Pencils: 4
- Notebooks: 2
- Pens: 3

We distribute **all** items between two persons (Person A and Person B), with th
e constraint that **each person receives at least one item**. Within each type,
items are identical, so a distribution is determined by how many items of each t
ype go to Person A (Person B receives the rest).

**Counting all distributions (no restriction).** For a type with $n$ identical i
tems, Person A can receive $0, 1, 2, \ldots, n$ of them — that is $n+1$ choices.
 Since the four types are independent, the total number of unrestricted distribu
tions is:

$$
(3+1)(4+1)(2+1)(3+1) = 4 \cdot 5 \cdot 3 \cdot 4 = 240.
$$

**Subtracting invalid distributions.** The only way a person receives **nothing*
* is if they receive 0 of every type — i.e., all items go to the other person. T
here is exactly **1** such distribution for "all to A" and **1** for "all to B",
 giving 2 invalid distributions.

**Final count:**

$$
240 - 2 = \boxed{238}.
$$

**Verification of the subtraction logic.** We must confirm there is no double-co
unting between the two excluded cases. The case "all items to A" and "all items
to B" are distinct (they require $n \geq 1$ for at least one type, which holds h
ere since every type has a positive count), and they are mutually exclusive. No
other distribution leaves a person empty-handed, since receiving even a single i
tem of any type already satisfies the "at least one" condition. Therefore exactl
y 2 distributions are removed, and the answer is **238**.
