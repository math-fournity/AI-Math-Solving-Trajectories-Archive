## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $v = u'$.

**Setup and notation.** Since $H$ is a Hilbert space, we identify $H^{**} \cong
H$, so the dual of $L^2(0,T;H^*)$ is $L^2(0,T;H)$. The weak convergences mean:

- $u_m \rightharpoonup u$ in $L^2(0,T;H)$: for every $f \in L^2(0,T;H)$,
$$\int_0^T (u_m(t), f(t))_H\, dt \;\longrightarrow\; \int_0^T (u(t), f(t))_H\, d
t.$$

- $u_m' \rightharpoonup v$ in $L^2(0,T;H^*)$: for every $g \in L^2(0,T;H)$,
$$\int_0^T \langle u_m'(t), g(t)\rangle_{H^*,H}\, dt \;\longrightarrow\; \int_0^
T \langle v(t), g(t)\rangle_{H^*,H}\, dt.$$

**Integration by parts for each $u_m$.** Since $u_m \in L^2(0,T;H)$ with $u_m' \
in L^2(0,T;H^*)$, the weak derivative satisfies, for every $\phi \in C_c^\infty(
0,T)$ and every $h \in H$:
$$\int_0^T \langle u_m'(t), h\rangle_{H^*,H}\,\phi(t)\, dt \;=\; -\int_0^T (u_m(
t), h)_H\,\phi'(t)\, dt. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Passing to the limit.** Fix $\phi \in C_c^\infty(0,T)$ and $h \in H$. Set $g(t
) = h\,\phi(t) \in L^2(0,T;H)$ and $f(t) = h\,\phi'(t) \in L^2(0,T;H)$.

- **Left-hand side of $(\ast)$:** By weak convergence $u_m' \rightharpoonup v$ i
n $L^2(0,T;H^*)$ tested against $g = h\phi$:
$$\int_0^T \langle u_m'(t), h\rangle\,\phi(t)\, dt \;\longrightarrow\; \int_0^T
\langle v(t), h\rangle\,\phi(t)\, dt.$$

- **Right-hand side of $(\ast)$:** By weak convergence $u_m \rightharpoonup u$ i
n $L^2(0,T;H)$ tested against $f = h\phi'$:
$$-\int_0^T (u_m(t), h)_H\,\phi'(t)\, dt \;\longrightarrow\; -\int_0^T (u(t), h)
_H\,\phi'(t)\, dt.$$

Equating the limits of both sides of $(\ast)$:
$$\int_0^T \langle v(t), h\rangle_{H^*,H}\,\phi(t)\, dt \;=\; -\int_0^T (u(t), h
)_H\,\phi'(t)\, dt \quad \forall\, \phi \in C_c^\infty(0,T),\; \forall\, h \in H
. \tag{$\ast\ast$}$$

**Conclusion.** The relation $(\ast\ast)$ is precisely the definition of $v$ bei
ng the weak (distributional) derivative of $u$ in the Bochner sense: $u' = v$ in
 $L^2(0,T;H^*)$. Hence

$$\boxed{v = u'.}$$
