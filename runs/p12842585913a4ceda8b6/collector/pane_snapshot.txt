## Proof

**Setup.** Five firms play a round-robin tournament: $\binom{5}{2}=10$ matches.
Each match is one of three types:
- **Decisive (W):** winner gets 5, loser gets 0. Total points: 5.
- **Draw with 0 breakthroughs (D₀):** both get 1. Total points: 2.
- **Draw with ≥1 breakthrough (D₁):** both get 2. Total points: 4.

Let $w, d_0, d_1$ be the counts, with $w+d_0+d_1=10$. The total points awarded i
s
$$P = 5w+2d_0+4d_1 = 5(10-d_0-d_1)+2d_0+4d_1 = 50-3d_0-d_1.$$

The five final scores are consecutive integers $n, n+1, n+2, n+3, n+4$, summing
to $5n+10$. Thus
$$5n+10 = 50-3d_0-d_1 \implies n = \frac{40-3d_0-d_1}{5},$$
requiring $3d_0+d_1 \equiv 0 \pmod{5}$.

We minimize total breakthroughs. Each D₁ match contributes ≥1 breakthrough, so t
he minimum total breakthroughs equals $d_1$ (setting each D₁ match to exactly 1
breakthrough). We seek the smallest feasible $d_1$.

**Each player plays 4 matches.** Player $i$'s score is $5w_i + d_{0,i} + 2d_{1,i
}$, where $w_i$ = decisive wins, $d_{0,i}$ = D₀ draws, $d_{1,i}$ = D₁ draws, and
 $w_i + d_{0,i} + d_{1,i} + \ell_i = 4$ ($\ell_i$ = decisive losses). Also $\sum
 w_i = w$, $\sum d_{0,i} = 2d_0$, $\sum d_{1,i} = 2d_1$, and crucially **$d_{1,i
} \le d_1$** (a player can't be in more D₁ matches than exist).

**$d_1 = 0$ is infeasible.** Then $3d_0 \equiv 0 \pmod 5$, so $d_0 \in \{0,5,10\
}$:
- $d_0=0$: all decisive; every score is a multiple of 5. Can't produce 5 consecu
tive integers.
- $d_0=5$: $n=5$, scores $\{5,6,7,8,9\}$, all with $w_i=1$. The score-9 player n
eeds $d_{0,i}=4$, but $w_i+d_{0,i}=1+4=5>4$ matches. Infeasible.
- $d_0=10$: all D₀ draws; every score is 4. Infeasible.

**$d_1 = 1$ is feasible.** Take $d_0=3, d_1=1, w=6$, giving $n=6$ and scores $\{
6,7,8,9,10\}$. The single D₁ match forces exactly two players to have $d_{1,i}=1
$. The following per-player decomposition works:

| Score | $(w_i, d_{0,i}, d_{1,i})$ | losses |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | (1, 1, 0) | 2 |
| 7 | (1, 2, 0) | 1 |
| 8 | (1, 1, 1) | 1 |
| 9 | (1, 2, 1) | 0 |
| 10 | (2, 0, 0) | 2 |

Sums: $w_i = 6$ ✓, $d_{0,i}=6=2\cdot3$ ✓, $d_{1,i}=2=2\cdot1$ ✓, losses $=6=w$ ✓
.

**Explicit construction.** Label firms A(6), B(7), C(8), D(9), E(10).
- **D₀ matches (1 pt each):** A–D, B–C, B–D.
- **D₁ match (2 pts each, 1 breakthrough):** C–D.
- **Decisive matches:** B beats A, A beats C, E beats A, E beats B, C beats E, D
 beats E.

**Verification:**
- A: D₀ vs D (+1), loss to B (0), win over C (5), loss to E (0) → **6** ✓
- B: D₀ vs C (+1), D₀ vs D (+1), win over A (5), loss to E (0) → **7** ✓
- C: D₀ vs B (+1), D₁ vs D (+2), loss to A (0), win over E (5) → **8** ✓
- D: D₀ vs A (+1), D₀ vs B (+1), D₁ vs C (+2), win over E (5) → **9** ✓
- E: win over A (5), win over B (5), loss to C (0), loss to D (0) → **10** ✓

Scores are $\{6,7,8,9,10\}$ — five consecutive integers — with exactly **1 break
through** (in the single D₁ match C–D).

Since $d_1=0$ is impossible and $d_1=1$ is achievable, the minimum total number
of breakthroughs is $\boxed{1}$.
