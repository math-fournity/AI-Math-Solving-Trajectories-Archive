Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every integer $n > 2$ has a
 coprime integer $a$ satisfying both $a^{n-1} \equiv 1 \pmod{n}$ and $n^{a-1} \e
quiv 1 \pmod{a}$.

**Answer: Yes**

## Proof

I construct an explicit $a$ for every $n > 2$, splitting into two cases by parit
y.

### Case 1: $n$ is even. Take $a = n + 1$.

- **Coprimality:** $\gcd(n+1, n) = 1$. ✓
- **First condition:** Since $n+1 \equiv 1 \pmod{n}$, we have $a^{n-1} = (n+1)^{
n-1} \equiv 1^{n-1} = 1 \pmod{n}$. ✓
- **Second condition:** Since $n \equiv -1 \pmod{n+1}$ and $n$ is even, $n^{a-1}
 = n^{n} \equiv (-1)^{n} = 1 \pmod{n+1} = 1 \pmod{a}$. ✓

### Case 2: $n$ is odd. Take $a = n - 1$.

- **Coprimality:** $\gcd(n-1, n) = 1$. ✓
- **First condition:** Since $n-1 \equiv -1 \pmod{n}$ and $n-1$ is even (as $n$
is odd), $a^{n-1} = (n-1)^{n-1} \equiv (-1)^{n-1} = 1 \pmod{n}$. ✓
- **Second condition:** Since $n \equiv 1 \pmod{n-1}$, we have $n^{a-1} = n^{n-2
} \equiv 1^{n-2} = 1 \pmod{n-1} = 1 \pmod{a}$. ✓

### Verification of nontriviality

For even $n > 2$: $a = n+1 \geq 4 > 1$. For odd $n > 2$: $a = n-1 \geq 2 > 1$ (w
ith equality at $n=3$, giving $a=2$). So $a$ is always a nontrivial integer grea
ter than 1.

### Inclusion of even pseudoprimes

In Case 2, $a = n-1$ is **even** (since $n$ is odd), so the construction natural
ly produces even pseudoprimes. For example:
- $n = 3$: $a = 2$ (even). Check: $2^2 = 4 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$ ✓; $3^1 = 3 \equiv
 1 \pmod{2}$ ✓.
- $n = 5$: $a = 4$ (even). Check: $4^4 = 256 \equiv 1 \pmod{5}$ ✓; $5^3 = 125 \e
quiv 1 \pmod{4}$ ✓.
- $n = 15$: $a = 14$ (even). Check: $14^{14} \equiv (-1)^{14} = 1 \pmod{15}$ ✓;
$15^{13} \equiv 1^{13} = 1 \pmod{14}$ ✓.

The construction also works regardless of whether $a$ is prime or composite. For
 instance, $n = 200$ (even) gives $a = 201 = 3 \times 67$ (composite), and $200^
{200} \equiv (-1)^{200} = 1 \pmod{201}$ ✓.

### Conclusion

For every integer $n > 2$:
- If $n$ is even, $a = n+1$ is a reciprocating pseudoprime coprime to $n$.
- If $n$ is odd, $a = n-1$ is a reciprocating pseudoprime coprime to $n$ (and is
 even).

Both conditions $a^{n-1} \equiv 1 \pmod{n}$ and $n^{a-1} \equiv 1 \pmod{a}$ hold
 in all cases, including when $a$ is even.
