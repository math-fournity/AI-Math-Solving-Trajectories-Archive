## Proof

### Step 1: Reformulation as $\mathbb{Z}/2$-equivariance

The condition $f(e^{-i\alpha}) = f(e^{i\alpha})^*$ is equivariance for the $\mat
hbb{Z}/2$-action $z \mapsto \bar{z}$ on $U(1)$ and $A \mapsto A^* = A^{-1}$ on $
U(n)$ (inversion is an involution on $U(n)$). The set $[U(1), U(n)]_*$ denotes e
quivariant homotopy classes of equivariant maps.

### Step 2: Reduction to paths with constrained endpoints

The $\mathbb{Z}/2$-action on $U(1)$ by conjugation has fixed points $\{+1, -1\}$
 and fundamental domain $[0,\pi]$ (parametrized by $\alpha \mapsto e^{i\alpha}$)
. An equivariant map $f$ is completely determined by the path
$$\gamma(\alpha) = f(e^{i\alpha}), \quad \alpha \in [0,\pi],$$
with the equivariance condition automatically extending $f$ to $[\pi, 2\pi]$ via
 $f(e^{-i\alpha}) = \gamma(\alpha)^{-1}$.

The fixed-point constraints at the endpoints require:
- $\gamma(0) = f(1) = f(1)^* = \gamma(0)^{-1}$, so $\gamma(0)^2 = I$.
- $\gamma(\pi) = f(-1) = f(-1)^* = \gamma(\pi)^{-1}$, so $\gamma(\pi)^2 = I$.

Thus equivariant maps correspond to paths $\gamma: [0,\pi] \to U(n)$ with both e
ndpoints in the **involution set** $I_n = \{A \in U(n) : A^2 = I\}$.

### Step 3: Structure of the involution set

Any involution $A \in U(n)$ with $A^2 = I$ is diagonalizable with eigenvalues $\
pm 1$. Such an $A$ is determined by its $+1$-eigenspace, a $k$-dimensional subsp
ace of $\mathbb{C}^n$ for some $0 \le k \le n$. Hence:
$$I_n \cong \bigsqcup_{k=0}^{n} \mathrm{Gr}(k, \mathbb{C}^n),$$
where $\mathrm{Gr}(k, \mathbb{C}^n) = U(n)/(U(k) \times U(n-k))$ is the Grassman
nian (the component $I_n^{(k)}$ of involutions with $k$ eigenvalues $+1$ and $n-
k$ eigenvalues $-1$).

### Step 4: Equivariant homotopy = path homotopy with free endpoints in $I_n$

An equivariant homotopy $F: U(1) \times [0,1] \to U(n)$ corresponds to $\Gamma:
[0,\pi] \times [0,1] \to U(n)$ with $\Gamma(0,t) \in I_n$ and $\Gamma(\pi,t) \in
 I_n$ for all $t$. Thus:
$$[U(1), U(n)]_* = \bigsqcup_{k,l=0}^{n} \left\{\text{homotopy classes of paths
in } U(n) \text{ from } I_n^{(k)} \text{ to } I_n^{(l)} \text{ with free endpoin
ts}\right\}.$$

### Step 5: Computing $\pi_1(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))$ and its image in $\pi_1(U(n))$

From the fibration $U(k) \times U(n-k) \hookrightarrow U(n) \to \mathrm{Gr}(k,n)
$, the long exact sequence gives:
$$\pi_1(U(k) \times U(n-k)) \xrightarrow{i_*} \pi_1(U(n)) \to \pi_1(\mathrm{Gr}(
k,n)) \to 0.$$

Here $\pi_1(U(k) \times U(n-k)) = \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}$, $\pi_1(U(n)) =
\mathbb{Z}$, and the block-diagonal inclusion induces $i_*(a,b) = a + b$, which
is **surjective**. Therefore:
$$\pi_1(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)) = 0 \quad \text{for all } 0 \le k \le n.$$
(For $k=0$ or $k=n$, $\mathrm{Gr}$ is a point, so this holds trivially.)

Consequently, the image of $\pi_1(I_n^{(k)}) \to \pi_1(U(n)) = \mathbb{Z}$ under
 the inclusion $I_n^{(k)} \hookrightarrow U(n)$ is $\{0\}$ for every $k$.

### Step 6: Classification for each $(k,l)$

Fix basepoints $A_k \in I_n^{(k)}$, $A_l \in I_n^{(l)}$. The set of homotopy cla
sses of paths from $A_k$ to $A_l$ in $U(n)$ with **fixed** endpoints is a torsor
 over $\pi_1(U(n)) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.

When endpoints are free to move within their components, we quotient by the acti
ons of $\pi_1(I_n^{(k)})$ and $\pi_1(I_n^{(l)})$ on this torsor (via pre- and po
st-concatenation of loops). Since both groups map trivially to $\pi_1(U(n))$, th
e quotient is:
$$\mathbb{Z} / (\{0\} + \{0\}) = \mathbb{Z}.$$

**Completeness of the invariant:** The winding number of a path $\gamma$ from $I
_n^{(k)}$ to $I_n^{(l)}$ is well-defined (close up using paths in the simply-con
nected Grassmannians; the simply-connectedness ensures independence of choices).
 Two paths with the same $(k,l)$ and same winding number are homotopic with free
 endpoints, since after moving endpoints to basepoints (which doesn't change the
 winding number, as $\pi_1(\mathrm{Gr}) = 0$), they become fixed-endpoint paths
differing by an element of $\pi_1(U(n)) = \mathbb{Z}$, and equal winding numbers
 mean this difference is zero.

### Step 7: Conclusion

Combining Steps 4 and 6:

$$[U(1), U(n)]_* = \bigsqcup_{k=0}^{n} \bigsqcup_{l=0}^{n} \mathbb{Z}$$

This is a disjoint union of $(n+1)^2$ copies of $\mathbb{Z}$, indexed by the pai
r $(k,l)$ recording the eigenvalue signatures of the involutions $f(1)$ and $f(-
1)$, with each copy parametrized by the winding number of the path in $U(n)$.

Equivalently, as a product of sets:
$$[U(1), U(n)]_* \cong \{0, 1, \ldots, n\} \times \{0, 1, \ldots, n\} \times \ma
thbb{Z}.$$
