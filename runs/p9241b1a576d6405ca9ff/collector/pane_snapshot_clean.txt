## Proof

Since $x_1, \dots, x_n, y$ are algebraically dependent over $F$, there exists a
nonzero polynomial $P \in F[T_1, \dots, T_n, S]$ with
$$P(x_1, \dots, x_n, y) = 0.$$

Write $P = \sum_{j=0}^{d} P_j(T_1, \dots, T_n)\, S^j$ with $P_j \in F[T_1, \dots
, T_n]$.

**Claim:** $P$ must involve $S$, i.e., $d \geq 1$ and $P_d \neq 0$.

If $d = 0$, then $P = P_0(T_1, \dots, T_n)$ is a nonzero polynomial in $T_1, \do
ts, T_n$ alone, and $P_0(x_1, \dots, x_n) = 0$ would be a nontrivial algebraic r
elation among $x_1, \dots, x_n$, contradicting their algebraic independence. Hen
ce $d \geq 1$ and $P_d \neq 0$.

Since $P_d$ is a nonzero polynomial in $F[T_1, \dots, T_n]$ and $x_1, \dots, x_n
$ are algebraically independent, $P_d(x_1, \dots, x_n) \neq 0$ in $R$.

**$y$ is algebraic over $K := F(x_1, \dots, x_n)$.** In the fraction field $K$,
we can divide the relation $P(x_1, \dots, x_n, y) = 0$ by $P_d(x_1, \dots, x_n)
\neq 0$ to obtain:
$$y^d + \frac{P_{d-1}(x_1, \dots, x_n)}{P_d(x_1, \dots, x_n)} y^{d-1} + \cdots +
 \frac{P_0(x_1, \dots, x_n)}{P_d(x_1, \dots, x_n)} = 0,$$
a monic polynomial relation for $y$ over $K$. Thus $y$ is algebraic over $K$.

**Every $r \in R$ is algebraic over $K$.** Since $R = F[x_1, \dots, x_n, y]$, ev
ery $r \in R$ is a polynomial in $y$ with coefficients in $F[x_1, \dots, x_n] \s
ubseteq K$, so $r \in K[y]$. As $y$ is algebraic over $K$, the extension $K[y]/K
$ is algebraic, hence every element of $K[y]$—in particular $r$—is algebraic ove
r $K$.

**Constructing the algebraic relation for $x_1, \dots, x_n, r$.** Since $r$ is a
lgebraic over $K = F(x_1, \dots, x_n)$, there exists a nonzero polynomial $Q(U)
= \sum_{i=0}^{m} c_i U^i \in K[U]$ with $Q(r) = 0$. Each coefficient $c_i \in K$
 is a ratio of polynomials: $c_i = a_i / b_i$ with $a_i, b_i \in F[T_1, \dots, T
_n]$, $b_i \neq 0$. Let $B = b_0 b_1 \cdots b_m \in F[T_1, \dots, T_n]$, which i
s nonzero. Multiplying $Q(r) = 0$ by $B(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ clears all denominator
s, yielding:
$$\sum_{i=0}^{m} A_i(x_1, \dots, x_n)\, r^i = 0,$$
where each $A_i \in F[T_1, \dots, T_n]$ and not all $A_i$ are zero (since $Q \ne
q 0$).

, U^i \in F[T_1, \dots, T_n, U]$. This is a nonzero polynomial (at least one $A_
i \neq 0$), and $\widetilde{Q}(x_1, \dots, x_n, r) = 0$.

Therefore $x_1, \dots, x_n, r$ are algebraically dependent over $F$. $\blacksqua
