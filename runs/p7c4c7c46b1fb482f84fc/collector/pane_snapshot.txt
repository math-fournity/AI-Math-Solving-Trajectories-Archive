## Proof

We show no such ring exists. Assume for contradiction that $R$ is a (unital) rin
g with an element $x$ satisfying $2x = 1$, and that $x$ is non-central.

**Key observation.** The element $2 = 1 + 1$ lies in the prime subring generated
 by $1$, so $2$ is central: $2r = r2$ for every $r \in R$.

Since $2x = 1$ and $2$ is central, $x$ is in fact the two-sided inverse of $2$:
$$x \cdot 2 = 2 \cdot x = 1.$$

**Claim.** $x$ commutes with every element of $R$.

Let $y \in R$ be arbitrary. Consider the two products $xy$ and $yx$. Using assoc
iativity and the centrality of $2$:

$$2(xy) = (2x)y = 1 \cdot y = y,$$

$$2(yx) = (yx)\cdot 2 = y(x \cdot 2) = y \cdot 1 = y.$$

(For the second equality chain we used that $2$ is central to rewrite $2(yx) = (
yx)2$, then associativity, then $x\cdot 2 = 1$.)

Hence $2(xy) = 2(yx)$. Multiplying both sides on the left by $x$ and using assoc
iativity together with $x \cdot 2 = 1$:

$$x\bigl(2(xy)\bigr) = (x \cdot 2)(xy) = 1 \cdot (xy) = xy,$$
$$x\bigl(2(yx)\bigr) = (x \cdot 2)(yx) = 1 \cdot (yx) = yx.$$

Since the left-hand arguments are equal, we conclude $xy = yx$.

As $y$ was arbitrary, $x$ is central — contradicting the assumption that $x$ is
non-central.

Therefore no ring with an element $x$ satisfying $2x = 1$ and $x$ non-central ex
ists. $\blacksquare$
