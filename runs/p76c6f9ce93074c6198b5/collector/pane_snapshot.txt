## Proof

Let $\alpha = \sqrt[3]{3}$ and $\beta = \sqrt{-3}$. We determine $\operatorname{
Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\beta)/\mathbb{Q})$.

**Step 1: Minimal polynomials and degrees.**

The minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is $f(x) = x^3 - 3$, which
is irreducible by Eisenstein's criterion at $p=3$. Hence $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\m
athbb{Q}] = 3$.

The minimal polynomial of $\beta$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is $g(x) = x^2 + 3$, so $[\m
athbb{Q}(\beta):\mathbb{Q}] = 2$.

Since $\gcd(3,2)=1$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)\cap\mathbb{Q}(\beta)$ has degree div
iding both $3$ and $2$, the intersection is $\mathbb{Q}$. Therefore
$$[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\beta):\mathbb{Q}] = 3 \cdot 2 = 6.$$

**Step 2: $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\beta)$ is the splitting field of $x^3-3$.**

Let $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3} = \frac{-1+\sqrt{-3}}{2}$. Then $\omega = \frac{-1+\b
eta}{2} \in \mathbb{Q}(\beta)$, so $\omega \in \mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\beta)$.

The three roots of $x^3 - 3$ are $\alpha,\ \alpha\omega,\ \alpha\omega^2$, all o
f which lie in $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\beta)$. Hence $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\beta)$ cont
ains the splitting field of $x^3-3$. Conversely, the splitting field contains $\
alpha$ and $\alpha\omega$, hence $\omega = (\alpha\omega)/\alpha$, hence $\beta
= 2\omega+1$. So the splitting field equals $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\beta)$.

Being a splitting field of a separable polynomial (char $0$), $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha
,\beta)/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois, and
$$|\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\beta)/\mathbb{Q})| = 6.$$

**Step 3: Structure of the Galois group.**

Any $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\beta)/\mathbb{Q})$ is dete
rmined by $\sigma(\alpha)$ and $\sigma(\omega)$:
- $\sigma(\alpha)$ must be a root of $x^3-3$: one of $\alpha, \alpha\omega, \alp
ha\omega^2$ (3 choices),
- $\sigma(\omega)$ must be a root of $x^2+x+1$ (the minimal polynomial of $\omeg
a$): one of $\omega, \omega^2$ (2 choices).

This gives $3 \times 2 = 6$ automorphisms, matching the degree, so every such ch
oice is realized. The Galois group embeds into $S_3$ (permutations of the three
roots of $x^3-3$) and has order $6 = |S_3|$, so
$$\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\beta)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_3.$$

**Step 4: Explicit generators confirming non-abelian structure.**

Define:
- $\sigma$: $\alpha \mapsto \alpha\omega$, $\omega \mapsto \omega$ (order $3$),
- $\tau$: $\alpha \mapsto \alpha$, $\omega \mapsto \omega^2$ (complex conjugatio
n, order $2$).

Then $\sigma$ and $\tau$ generate the group. Computing:
$$\tau\sigma(\alpha) = \tau(\alpha\omega) = \alpha\omega^2, \qquad \sigma\tau(\a
lpha) = \sigma(\alpha) = \alpha\omega.$$

Since $\alpha\omega^2 \neq \alpha\omega$, we have $\tau\sigma \neq \sigma\tau$,
so the group is non-abelian. The only non-abelian group of order $6$ is $S_3$, c
onfirming:

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{3},\sqrt{-3})/\mathbb{Q}) \cong
S_3}.$$
