# Solution

**Weaker hypothesis.** Replace "bounded near $P$" with the growth condition

$$
(z-P)\,f(z)\to 0 \quad\text{as } z\to P,
$$

equivalently $|f(z)|=o\!\bigl(1/|z-P|\bigr)$. This is strictly weaker in stateme
nt than boundedness: if $|f|\le M$ then $|(z-P)f(z)|\le M|z-P|\to 0$, so bounded
ness implies the condition, but the condition permits $f$ to be unbounded (it on
ly caps the blow-up rate at $o(1/|z-P|)$).

**Proof that the conclusion still holds.** Define $g(z):=(z-P)\,f(z)$ on the pun
ctured disc $D(P,r)\setminus\{P\}$. Then $g$ is holomorphic there and, by the hy
pothesis, $g(z)\to 0$ as $z\to P$. In particular $g$ is bounded near $P$, so by
the Riemann removable singularities theorem $g$ extends to a holomorphic functio
n $G$ on all of $D(P,r)$, with $G(P)=\lim_{z\to P}g(z)=0$.

Since $G$ is holomorphic on $D(P,r)$ and $G(P)=0$, the quotient

$$
H(z):=\frac{G(z)}{z-P}
$$

extends to a holomorphic function on $D(P,r)$ (the zero of $G$ at $P$ cancels th
e simple pole). On the punctured disc we have

$$
(z-P)\,f(z)=g(z)=G(z)=(z-P)\,H(z),
$$

so dividing by $(z-P)\neq 0$ gives $f(z)=H(z)$ for all $z\in D(P,r)\setminus\{P\
}$. Thus $H$ is the desired holomorphic extension of $f$ to $D(P,r)$.

**Why this is genuinely weaker.** The hypothesis $(z-P)f(z)\to 0$ controls only
the *rate* of growth, not the supremum. A function such as $f(z)=\log|z-P|$ is n
ot holomorphic, but the point stands that the condition allows $|f|$ to be unbou
nded in principle — one only needs $|f(z)|$ to grow slower than $1/|z-P|$. Bound
edness is the special case of zero growth; the argument above shows that any sub
-$1/|z-P|$ growth already forces the singularity to be removable, because multip
lying by $(z-P)$ tames $f$ down to a bounded (indeed vanishing) function to whic
h the classical theorem applies.
