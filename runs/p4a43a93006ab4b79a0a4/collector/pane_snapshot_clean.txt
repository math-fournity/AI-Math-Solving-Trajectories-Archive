## Proof

**Yes**, such a sequence exists. In fact, we can construct $(v_j)$ with the stro
nger bound $|v_j(x)| \leq 1 \leq 2$ a.e. The argument is a standard mollificatio
n-with-cutoff, and the key point is that both operations (convolution with a non
-negative mollifier, and multiplication by a cutoff valued in $[0,1]$) are *poin
twise bound-preserving*.

**Step 1: Zero extension.** Since $v \in H^1_0(\Omega)$, its extension by zero o
utside $\Omega$,
$$\tilde v(x) = \begin{cases} v(x), & x \in \Omega, \\ 0, & x \notin \Omega, \en
d{cases}$$
belongs to $H^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$. Moreover $|\tilde v(x)| \leq 1$ for a.e. $x \in
\mathbb{R}^n$ (the bound holds on $\Omega$ by hypothesis and is trivially $0 \le
q 1$ outside).

**Step 2: Mollification.** Let $\rho \in C^\infty_c(\mathbb{R}^n)$ be a standard
 mollifier: $\rho \geq 0$, $\operatorname{supp}\rho \subset B_1$, $\int \rho = 1
$, and set $\rho_\varepsilon(x) = \varepsilon^{-n}\rho(x/\varepsilon)$. Define
$$v_\varepsilon := \rho_\varepsilon * \tilde v \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n).$$
Since $\rho_\varepsilon \geq 0$ and $\int \rho_\varepsilon = 1$,
$$|v_\varepsilon(x)| = \left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \rho_\varepsilon(x-y)\,\tilde v
(y)\,dy\right| \leq \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \rho_\varepsilon(x-y)\,|\tilde v(y)|\,dy
 \leq \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \rho_\varepsilon(x-y)\,dy = 1.$$
So $|v_\varepsilon| \leq 1$ everywhere. By the standard property of mollificatio
n in $H^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$,
$$v_\varepsilon \to \tilde v \text{ in } H^1(\mathbb{R}^n) \quad \text{as } \var
epsilon \to 0,$$
hence $v_\varepsilon|_\Omega \to v$ in $H^1(\Omega)$.

**Step 3: Cutoff to recover compact support in $\Omega$.** The function $v_\vare
psilon$ is smooth but its support may extend slightly beyond $\overline{\Omega}$
 (by a distance $\leq \varepsilon$). To obtain a function in $C^\infty_c(\Omega)
$, multiply by a smooth cutoff. Since $\Omega$ is a bounded Lipschitz domain, fo
r each sufficiently small $\delta > 0$ there exists $\eta_\delta \in C^\infty_c(
\Omega)$ with $0 \leq \eta_\delta \leq 1$, $\eta_\delta \equiv 1$ on $\{x \in \O
mega : \operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega) > \delta\}$, and $\operatorname{sup
p}\eta_\delta \subset \Omega$. Standard estimates give
$$\|\nabla \eta_\delta\|_{L^\infty} \leq C/\delta, \qquad |\{x \in \Omega : \eta
_\delta(x) \neq 1\}| \to 0 \text{ as } \delta \to 0.$$

Set $w_{\varepsilon,\delta} := \eta_\delta \cdot v_\varepsilon$. Then $w_{\varep
silon,\delta} \in C^\infty_c(\Omega)$, and since $0 \leq \eta_\delta \leq 1$ and

$$|w_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x)| = \eta_\delta(x)\,|v_\varepsilon(x)| \leq 1 \leq 2
.$$

**Step 4: Convergence in $H^1(\Omega)$.** We estimate
$$\|w_{\varepsilon,\delta} - v\|_{H^1(\Omega)} \leq \|\eta_\delta v_\varepsilon
- \eta_\delta v\|_{H^1(\Omega)} + \|\eta_\delta v - v\|_{H^1(\Omega)}.$$
For the first term, using $0 \leq \eta_\delta \leq 1$:
$$\|\eta_\delta(v_\varepsilon - v)\|_{L^2} \leq \|v_\varepsilon - v\|_{L^2(\Omeg
a)}, \qquad \|\nabla(\eta_\delta(v_\varepsilon - v))\|_{L^2} \leq \|v_\varepsilo
n - v\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\|\nabla\eta_\delta\|_{L^\infty} + \|\nabla v_\varepsilon -
 \nabla v\|_{L^2}.$$
Choose $\delta = \delta(\varepsilon) \to 0$ slowly enough that $\varepsilon/\del
ta(\varepsilon) \to 0$ (e.g., $\delta = \sqrt{\varepsilon}$). Then $\|\nabla\eta
_\delta\|_{L^\infty}\,\|v_\varepsilon - v\|_{L^2} = O(\delta^{-1})\,o(1) = o(1)$
, and $\|\nabla v_\varepsilon - \nabla v\|_{L^2} \to 0$. For the second term, $\
eta_\delta v \to v$ in $H^1(\Omega)$ because $\eta_\delta \to 1$ a.e. boundedly
(dominated convergence in $L^2$ for the function term; for the gradient, $\nabla
(\eta_\delta v) = \eta_\delta \nabla v + v\nabla\eta_\delta$, the first part con

a\eta_\delta\|_{L^\infty}\|v\|_{L^2(\Omega \setminus \Omega_\delta)} \to 0$ sinc

minus\Omega_\delta|^{1/2} \to 0$ while $\delta^{-1}|\Omega\setminus\Omega_\delta
|^{1/2} \to 0$ for Lipschitz domains).

Combining, $w_{\varepsilon,\delta(\varepsilon)} \to v$ in $H^1(\Omega)$ as $\var
epsilon \to 0$.

**Step 5: Extracting the sequence.** Setting $v_j := w_{\varepsilon_j, \delta(\v
arepsilon_j)}$ for a sequence $\varepsilon_j \downarrow 0$ chosen so that $\|v_j

v_j \to v$ in $H^1(\Omega)$ and
$$|v_j(x)| \leq 1 \leq 2 \quad \text{for all } x \in \Omega.$$

**Conclusion.** Such a sequence exists; indeed the construction yields the sharp

 (convolution with a non-negative, unit-mass kernel) and multiplication by a $[0
,1]$-valued cutoff are pointwise bound-preserving operations, so the a.e. bound
$|v| \leq 1$ is inherited by the approximants. $\blacksquare$
