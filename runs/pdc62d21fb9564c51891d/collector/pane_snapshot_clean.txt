## Analysis

**Key distinction:** If $R$ were Noetherian, then by Krull's height theorem, $\d
im R \leq \mu(\mathfrak{m}_R)$ (the number of generators), so dimension would be
 finite. But we are only told $\mathfrak{m}_R$ is finitely generated — $R$ need
not be Noetherian. The question is whether a non-Noetherian local domain can hav
e a finitely generated maximal ideal yet infinite Krull dimension.

**Strategy:** Construct a valuation domain whose value group has a minimal posit
ive element (making the maximal ideal principal) but has infinite rank (making t
he Krull dimension infinite).

## Proof

The answer is **No**. We construct a counterexample.

**Step 1: A totally ordered abelian group of infinite rank.**

Let $H = \mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}}$ be the group of all integer sequences, equippe
d with the lexicographic order (comparing from the first coordinate). This is a
totally ordered abelian group. For each $k \geq 0$, define
$$C_k = \{(a_1, a_2, \ldots) \in \mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}} : a_1 = \cdots = a_k =
0\}.$$
Each $C_k$ is a convex subgroup: if $0 \leq (a_i) \leq (b_i)$ with $(b_i) \in C_
k$, then comparing lexicographically forces $a_1 = \cdots = a_k = 0$, so $(a_i)
\in C_k$. We have an infinite strictly descending chain
$$H = C_0 \supsetneq C_1 \supsetneq C_2 \supsetneq \cdots, \quad \bigcap_k C_k =
 \{0\},$$
so $H$ has infinite rank.

**Step 2: Adjoin a minimal positive element.**

Set $G = H \times \mathbb{Z}$ with lexicographic order: $(h, n) > 0$ iff $h > 0$
 in $H$, or $h = 0$ and $n > 0$. Then:

- **Minimal positive element:** $(0, 1)$ is positive. Any positive $(h, n)$ with
 $h > 0$ satisfies $(h,n) > (0,1)$. Any positive $(0, n)$ with $n > 0$ satisfies
 $(0,n) \geq (0,1)$. So $(0,1)$ is the unique minimal positive element of $G$.

- **Infinite rank:** The subgroup $H \times \{0\}$ is convex in $G$ (it is the k
ernel of the order-preserving projection $G \to \mathbb{Z}$, $(h,n) \mapsto n$).
 Since $H \times \{0\} \cong H$ has infinite rank, $G$ has infinite rank.

**Step 3: Construct the valuation domain.**

By the Krull–Kaplansky–Jaffard–Ohm theorem, for any totally ordered abelian grou
p $G$, there exists a field $K$ and a valuation $v: K^\times \to G$ with value g
roup exactly $G$ (e.g., take $K = k((t^G))$, the field of Hahn series with expon
ents in $G$ over a field $k$). The valuation ring
$$V = \{x \in K : v(x) \geq 0\} \cup \{0\}$$
is a local domain with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m} = \{x \in V : v(x) > 0\}$.

**Step 4: $V$ is integrally closed (hence "integral").**

Every valuation domain is integrally closed in its field of fractions. In partic
ular, $V$ is an integral domain.

**Step 5: $\mathfrak{m}$ is principal (hence finitely generated).**

Since $G$ has minimal positive element $(0,1)$, pick $\pi \in K$ with $v(\pi) =
(0,1)$. For any $x \in \mathfrak{m}$, we have $v(x) \geq (0,1) = v(\pi)$, so $v(
x/\pi) \geq 0$, hence $x/\pi \in V$, i.e., $x \in \pi V$. Thus $\mathfrak{m} = (
\pi)$ is principal.

**Step 6: $\dim V = \infty$.**

The prime ideals of a valuation domain are in inclusion-preserving bijection wit
h the convex subgroups of its value group (a classical result; see Bourbaki, *Co
mmutative Algebra*, Ch. VI, §4, no. 4, Proposition 5). The Krull dimension equal
s the rank of the value group, i.e., the supremum of lengths of chains of convex
 subgroups. Since $G$ has infinite rank, $\dim V = \infty$.

**Conclusion.**

The valuation domain $V$ is a local domain that is integrally closed, has a prin
cipal (hence finitely generated) maximal ideal, and has infinite Krull dimension
. Therefore, the Krull dimension of $R$ is **not** necessarily finite.
