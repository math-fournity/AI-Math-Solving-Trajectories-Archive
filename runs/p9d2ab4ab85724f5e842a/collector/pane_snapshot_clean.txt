## Solution

Let $I = (x^3 - y^6,\; xy - y^3) \subset \mathbb{C}[x,y]$. I claim
$$\sqrt{I} = (x - y^2).$$

**Step 1: Factor the generators.**
$$x^3 - y^6 = (x-y^2)(x^2+xy^2+y^4), \qquad xy - y^3 = y(x-y^2).$$
Set $g := x - y^2$ and $h := x^2+xy^2+y^4$. Both generators lie in $(g)$, so
$$I \subseteq (g) \implies \sqrt{I} \subseteq \sqrt{(g)} = (g),$$
where the last equality uses that $g$ is irreducible (linear in $x$), hence prim
e.

**Step 2: Compute $V(I)$ to confirm the candidate.** From $xy - y^3 = 0$: either
 $y=0$ or $x=y^2$. If $y=0$, then $x^3=0$ gives $x=0$, which lies on $x=y^2$. If
 $x=y^2$, then $x^3=y^6$ holds automatically. Hence $V(I)=V(x-y^2)$, and by the
Nullstellensatz $\sqrt{I}=I(V(I))=(x-y^2)$. It remains to give a direct algebrai
c proof that $g\in\sqrt{I}$.

**Step 3: Show $g^3 \in I$ (direct computation).** Work in $R=\mathbb{C}[x,y]/I$
. The relations $yg\in I$ and $gh\in I$ give, in $R$:
$$yg = 0, \qquad gh = 0. \tag{$\ast$}$$
Express $h$ in terms of $g$ by substituting $x = y^2+g$:
$$x^2 = y^4+2y^2g+g^2,\quad xy^2 = y^4+y^2g,$$
$$h = (y^4+2y^2g+g^2)+(y^4+y^2g)+y^4 = 3y^4+3y^2g+g^2.$$
Multiplying by $g$ and using $gh=0$:
$$0 = g\cdot h = 3y^4 g + 3y^2 g^2 + g^3.$$
From $yg=0$ in $R$ we get $y^2g = y(yg)=0$, hence $y^4g=0$ and $y^2g^2 = g(y^2g)
=0$. The equation collapses to
$$g^3 = 0 \quad\text{in } R,$$
i.e. $g^3 = (x-y^2)^3 \in I$.

**Step 4: Conclude.** We have $I\subseteq (g)$ and $g^3\in I$, so
$$(g) \subseteq \sqrt{I} \subseteq (g).$$
Therefore
$$\boxed{\sqrt{(x^3-y^6,\;xy-y^3)} = (x-y^2).}$$
