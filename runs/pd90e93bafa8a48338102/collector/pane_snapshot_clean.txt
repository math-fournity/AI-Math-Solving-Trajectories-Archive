## Solution

Let $X$ be a locally compact topological space and $\mathcal{F}$ the presheaf as
signing to each open $U \subseteq X$ the set $\mathcal{F}(U) = C_b(U,\mathbb{R})
$ of bounded continuous real-valued functions on $U$, with restriction maps give
n by ordinary restriction of functions.

**Goal.** Describe the associated sheaf $\mathcal{F}^+$.

### The stalk

For $x \in X$, the stalk $\mathcal{F}_x$ is the set of germs of bounded continuo
us functions at $x$: equivalence classes of pairs $(V, f)$ with $V$ a neighborho
od of $x$ and $f \in C_b(V,\mathbb{R})$, where $(V,f) \sim (W,g)$ iff $f = g$ on
 some neighborhood of $x$ contained in $V \cap W$. The germ $[f]_x$ determines t
he value $f(x) \in \mathbb{R}$, so there is a well-defined evaluation map $\oper
atorname{ev}_x : \mathcal{F}_x \to \mathbb{R}$, $[f]_x \mapsto f(x)$.

### The sheafification (espace étalé / Godement construction)

By the standard construction, $\mathcal{F}^+(U)$ consists of all functions
$$s : U \to \coprod_{x \in U} \mathcal{F}_x, \qquad s(x) \in \mathcal{F}_x,$$
that are *locally representable by sections of $\mathcal{F}$*: for every $x \in
U$ there exist an open neighborhood $V \subseteq U$ of $x$ and a section $f \in
\mathcal{F}(V) = C_b(V,\mathbb{R})$ such that
$$s(y) = [f]_y \quad \text{for all } y \in V.$$

Since the germ $[f]_y$ evaluates to $f(y)$, the condition $s(y) = [f]_y$ means p
recisely that $s$, viewed via evaluation as a function $U \to \mathbb{R}$, coinc
ides with $f$ on $V$. Therefore:

> $\mathcal{F}^+(U) = \big\{ s : U \to \mathbb{R} \;\big|\; s \text{ is continuo
us and locally bounded on } U \big\}.$

**Proof of the characterization.**
- ($\subseteq$) If $s \in \mathcal{F}^+(U)$, then locally $s = f$ with $f$ bound
ed and continuous, so $s$ is continuous (continuity is local) and locally bounde
d.
- ($\supseteq$) If $s : U \to \mathbb{R}$ is continuous and locally bounded, the
n for each $x \in U$ there is a neighborhood $V \ni x$ on which $s$ is bounded;
$s|_V$ is then continuous and bounded, i.e. $s|_V \in \mathcal{F}(V)$, and $s(y)
 = [s|_V]_y$ for all $y \in V$. Hence $s \in \mathcal{F}^+(U)$.

### Using local compactness: every continuous function is locally bounded

We now use the hypothesis that $X$ is locally compact. Let $s : U \to \mathbb{R}
$ be continuous and let $x \in U$. By local compactness, $x$ has a compact neigh
borhood $K$ (i.e. $K$ is compact and contains an open set $W$ with $x \in W \sub
seteq K$). Replacing $K$ by $K \cap U$ (still compact, still a neighborhood of $
x$ in $U$), we may assume $K \subseteq U$. Since $s$ is continuous, $s(K) \subse
teq \mathbb{R}$ is compact, hence bounded. Thus $s$ is bounded on the neighborho
od $K$ of $x$. As $x$ was arbitrary, $s$ is locally bounded.

Consequently the "locally bounded" condition is automatic for continuous functio
ns on a locally compact space, and the characterization above collapses to:

$$\boxed{\mathcal{F}^+(U) = C(U, \mathbb{R}) \quad \text{for every open } U \sub
seteq X,}$$

with restriction maps given by ordinary restriction of functions.

### Verification that this is a sheaf

The assignment $U \mapsto C(U,\mathbb{R})$ with ordinary restriction is the shea
f of continuous real-valued functions on $X$. It is indeed a sheaf: continuity i
s a local property, so a family of continuous functions $\{f_i \in C(U_i,\mathbb
{R})\}$ with $f_i|_{U_i \cap U_j} = f_j|_{U_i \cap U_j}$ glues uniquely to a con
tinuous function on $\bigcup U_i$. (Note the contrast with $\mathcal{F}$ itself:
 boundedness is *not* local, so $\mathcal{F}$ fails to be a sheaf in general — g
luing locally bounded functions can yield an unbounded function — and sheafifica
tion is needed precisely to repair this.)

### Conclusion

The associated sheaf $\mathcal{F}^+$ of the presheaf of bounded continuous funct
ions on a locally compact space $X$ is the **sheaf of all (not necessarily bound
ed) continuous real-valued functions** on $X$:

$$\mathcal{F}^+ = \mathscr{C}_X(-,\mathbb{R}), \qquad \mathcal{F}^+(U) = C(U,\ma
thbb{R}).$$

The key points are: (1) sheafification replaces "globally bounded" by "locally b
ounded" (the local version of the boundedness condition, which is what the gluin
g axiom requires), and (2) on a locally compact space every continuous function
is automatically locally bounded, since continuous functions are bounded on comp
act sets and every point has a compact neighborhood. Thus the local boundedness
condition imposes no additional restriction beyond continuity, and $\mathcal{F}^
+$ is exactly the sheaf of continuous functions.
