**Answer: Yes, $T$ is bounded.**

*Proof.* We assume "operator" means linear operator (the standard convention). S
uppose for contradiction that $T$ is unbounded.

**Construction of a weakly null sequence with unbounded image.** Since $T$ is un
bounded, for each $n\in\mathbb N$ there exists $v_n\in X$ with $\|v_n\|\le 1$ an
d $\|Tv_n\|\ge n^2$. Set $x_n = v_n/n$. Then
$$\|x_n\|\le \tfrac1n\to 0,$$
so $x_n\to 0_X$ in norm, hence $x_n\rightharpoonup 0_X$ weakly. By the hypothesi
s, $T x_n\rightharpoonup 0_Y$ weakly in $Y$. On the other hand
$$\|T x_n\|=\frac{\|T v_n\|}{n}\ge n\to\infty. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Weakly convergent sequences are norm-bounded (no completeness of $Y$ needed).*
* We recall why: if $y_n\rightharpoonup 0$ weakly in $Y$, then for every $g\in Y
^*$ we have $g(y_n)\to 0$, so $\sup_n|g(y_n)|<\infty$. Define $\Lambda_n\in Y^{*
*}$ by $\Lambda_n(g)=g(y_n)$. Then $\sup_n|\Lambda_n(g)|<\infty$ for each $g\in
Y^*$. Since $Y^*$ is *always* a Banach space (the dual of any normed space is co
mplete), the Uniform Boundedness Principle yields $\sup_n\|\Lambda_n\|<\infty$.
By Hahn–Banach the canonical embedding $Y\hookrightarrow Y^{**}$ is isometric, s
o $\|\Lambda_n\|=\|y_n\|$, giving $\sup_n\|y_n\|<\infty$.

**Contradiction.** Applying this to $y_n=T x_n$ (which is weakly null by hypothe
sis) gives $\sup_n\|T x_n\|<\infty$, contradicting $(\ast)$, where $\|T x_n\|\ge
 n\to\infty$.

Hence $T$ must be bounded. $\blacksquare$
