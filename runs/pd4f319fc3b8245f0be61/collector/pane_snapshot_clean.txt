Looking at this problem, I need to prove that all terms of the lower central ser
ies of a finitely generated nilpotent group are finitely generated. Let me work
through this carefully.

**Answer: Yes.** Every term $\gamma_i G$ of the lower central series of a finite
ly generated nilpotent group $G$ is finitely generated.

---

## Proof

We prove by induction on the nilpotency class $c$ (where $\gamma_{c+1} G = 1$ an
d $\gamma_c G \neq 1$, with $c = 0$ meaning $G = 1$) the following statement:

> **$(P_c)$**: If $G$ is finitely generated and nilpotent of class $c$, then $\g
amma_i G$ is finitely generated for every $i \geq 0$.

### Base case: $c = 0$

Then $G = 1$, and every $\gamma_i G = 1$ is finitely generated. ✓

### Inductive step

Assume $P_{c-1}$ holds for some $c \geq 1$. Let $G = \langle g_1, \dots, g_n \ra
ngle$ be nilpotent of class $c$, so $\gamma_{c+1} G = 1$ and $\gamma_c G \neq 1$
.

**Setup.** Set $\bar{G} = G / \gamma_c G$. Since $\gamma_c G$ is normal, $\bar{G
}$ is finitely generated (by the images of $g_1, \dots, g_n$) and nilpotent of c
lass $c - 1$ (since $\gamma_c(\bar{G}) = \gamma_c G \cdot \gamma_c G / \gamma_c
G = 1$).

By the standard identity $\gamma_i(G/N) = \gamma_i(G) \cdot N / N$ for any $N \t
rianglelefteq G$, we have:
$$\gamma_i(\bar{G}) = \gamma_i G / \gamma_c G \quad \text{for } i \leq c.$$

By the induction hypothesis $P_{c-1}$, every $\gamma_i(\bar{G})$ is finitely gen
erated, so **$\gamma_i G / \gamma_c G$ is finitely generated for all $i$**.

**Key Lemma: $\gamma_c G$ is finitely generated.**

Since $\gamma_{c+1} G = [\gamma_c G, G] = 1$, we have $\gamma_c G \subseteq Z(G)
$; in particular, $\gamma_c G$ is **central** (hence abelian).

Recall $\gamma_c G = [\gamma_{c-1} G,\, G]$, which is generated (as a group) by
all commutators $[x, g]$ with $x \in \gamma_{c-1} G$ and $g \in G$.

**Claim:** For any fixed $x \in \gamma_{c-1} G$, the map $\varphi_x : G \to \gam
ma_c G$ defined by $\varphi_x(g) = [x, g]$ is a **group homomorphism**.

*Proof of claim.* Since $[x, g] \in \gamma_c G \subseteq Z(G)$ for all $g$, the
commutator $[x, g]$ is central. Using the identity $[x, gh] = [x, h]\,[x, g]^h$
and the fact that $[x,g]^h = [x,g]$ (by centrality), we get $[x, gh] = [x, g]\,[
x, h]$. ✓

Since $G = \langle g_1, \dots, g_n \rangle$ and $\varphi_x$ is a homomorphism:
$$[x, G] = \langle [x, g_1], \dots, [x, g_n] \rangle.$$

Therefore:
$$\gamma_c G = \big\langle [x, g_i] : x \in \gamma_{c-1} G,\; i = 1, \dots, n \b
ig\rangle. \tag{$\ast$}$$

Now we reduce $x$ to finitely many values. By the induction hypothesis, $\gamma_
{c-1}(\bar{G}) = \gamma_{c-1} G / \gamma_c G$ is finitely generated. Let $\bar{x
}_1, \dots, \bar{x}_m$ be generators (cosets), with representatives $x_1, \dots,
 x_m \in \gamma_{c-1} G$. Then any $x \in \gamma_{c-1} G$ can be written as:
$$x = x_1^{a_1} \cdots x_m^{a_m} \cdot z, \quad a_j \in \mathbb{Z},\; z \in \gam
ma_c G.$$

Since $z \in \gamma_c G \subseteq Z(G)$, we have $[z, g_i] = 1$. Using the ident
ity $[ab, g] = [a, g]\,[b, g]$ (valid when $[a,g]$ is central) and $[x^a, g] = [
x, g]^a$ (valid when $[x, g]$ is central, proved by induction on $|a|$):

$$[x, g_i] = [x_1^{a_1} \cdots x_m^{a_m} \cdot z,\, g_i] = [x_1, g_i]^{a_1} \cdo
ts [x_m, g_i]^{a_m}.$$

Thus every $[x, g_i]$ lies in $\langle [x_j, g_i] : 1 \leq j \leq m \rangle$. Su
bstituting into $(\ast)$:

$$\boxed{\gamma_c G = \big\langle [x_j, g_i] : 1 \leq j \leq m,\; 1 \leq i \leq
n \big\rangle,}$$

which is generated by the **finite** set $\{[x_j, g_i]\}$. Hence **$\gamma_c G$
is finitely generated**. ∎ (Key Lemma)

**Completing the induction.** For each $i$, we have the short exact sequence of
groups:
$$1 \longrightarrow \gamma_c G \longrightarrow \gamma_i G \longrightarrow \gamma
_i G / \gamma_c G \longrightarrow 1.$$

Both $\gamma_c G$ (by the Key Lemma) and $\gamma_i G / \gamma_c G$ (by the induc
tion hypothesis) are finitely generated. Since an **extension of finitely genera
ted groups is finitely generated** (if $N = \langle a_1, \dots, a_p \rangle$ and
 $H/N = \langle b_1 N, \dots, b_q N \rangle$, then $H = \langle a_1, \dots, a_p,
 b_1, \dots, b_q \rangle$), we conclude that **$\gamma_i G$ is finitely generate
d for every $i$**.

This establishes $P_c$, completing the induction. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** In fact, a stronger result holds: every **subgroup** of a finitely g
enerated nilpotent group is finitely generated (i.e., finitely generated nilpote
nt groups are *Noetherian* / satisfy the maximal condition on subgroups). The ab
ove proof specializes to the lower central series terms, which is the question a
sked. The general result follows from the same induction on nilpotency class: th
e center $Z(G)$ is finitely generated (by the same commutator-homomorphism argum
ent applied to $\gamma_c G \subseteq Z(G)$, combined with the fact that $G/\gamm
a_c G$ being polycyclic-by-induction lifts the Noetherian property), and then an
y subgroup $H \leq G$ is finitely generated because $HZ/Z \leq G/Z$ is finitely
generated (by induction) and $H \cap Z \leq Z$ is finitely generated (since $Z$
is finitely generated abelian).
