nly on $n$, where $c_k$ counts $k$-subsets in convex position with no other poin

**Proof.**

Let $P$ be our set of $n$ points in general position. A $k$-subset $S \subseteq
P$ is *good* if all points of $S$ lie on the convex hull of $S$ (convex position
) and no point of $P \setminus S$ lies inside $\operatorname{conv}(S)$.

**Step 1: Reformulation via inclusion–exclusion.**

For a subset $S \subseteq P$ in convex position with $|S| \geq 3$, let $I(S) = \
{p \in P \setminus S : p \in \operatorname{int}(\operatorname{conv}(S))\}$ be th
e set of points of $P$ strictly inside $\operatorname{conv}(S)$. Then $S$ is goo
d iff $I(S) = \emptyset$. By inclusion–exclusion:

$$[I(S) = \emptyset] = \sum_{T \subseteq I(S)} (-1)^{|T|}.$$

Therefore:

$$\sum_{k=3}^n (-1)^k c_k = \sum_{\substack{S \subseteq P,\; |S|\geq 3 \\ S \tex
t{ convex}}} (-1)^{|S|} \sum_{T \subseteq I(S)} (-1)^{|T|} = \sum_{\substack{S \
text{ convex} \\ |S| \geq 3}} \sum_{T \subseteq I(S)} (-1)^{|S|+|T|}.$$

**Step 2: A bijection.**

Consider a pair $(S, T)$ where $S \subseteq P$ is in convex position with $|S| \
geq 3$, and $T \subseteq I(S)$. Set $U = S \cup T$.

Since every point of $T$ lies strictly inside $\operatorname{conv}(S)$, the conv
ex hull of $U$ equals $\operatorname{conv}(S)$, and the vertices of $\operatorna
me{conv}(U)$ are exactly $S$. Thus:
- $V(U) := \{\text{vertices of } \operatorname{conv}(U)\} = S$,
- $\operatorname{int}(U) := U \setminus V(U) = T$.

Conversely, given any subset $U \subseteq P$ with $|U| \geq 3$ (so $|V(U)| \geq
3$ since no three points are collinear), setting $S = V(U)$ and $T = U \setminus
 V(U)$ recovers a valid pair: $S$ is in convex position, $|S| \geq 3$, and $T \s
ubseteq I(S)$ (the interior points of $U$ are exactly the points of $U$ inside $
\operatorname{conv}(V(U))$).

This gives a **bijection** between pairs $(S, T)$ and subsets $U \subseteq P$ wi
th $|U| \geq 3$.

**Step 3: Simplification.**

Under this bijection, $|S| + |T| = |V(U)| + |U \setminus V(U)| = |U|$. Therefore
:

$$\sum_{k=3}^n (-1)^k c_k = \sum_{\substack{U \subseteq P \\ |U| \geq 3}} (-1)^{
|U|} = \sum_{j=3}^{n} (-1)^j \binom{n}{j}.$$

**Step 4: Evaluate the binomial sum.**

Using $\sum_{j=0}^{n} (-1)^j \binom{n}{j} = (1-1)^n = 0$:

$$\sum_{j=3}^{n} (-1)^j \binom{n}{j} = 0 - \left[\binom{n}{0} - \binom{n}{1} + \
binom{n}{2}\right] = -\left(1 - n + \frac{n(n-1)}{2}\right) = n - 1 - \frac{n(n-
1)}{2}.$$

$$= \frac{2(n-1) - n(n-1)}{2} = \frac{(n-1)(2-n)}{2} = -\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2} = -
\binom{n-1}{2}.$$

This depends only on $n$. $\blacksquare$
