Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether surjectivity of the equaliz
er immersion $i : \text{eq}(f,g) \to X$ on topological spaces forces $f = g$ as
morphisms of schemes.

**Answer: No.** Surjectivity of $i$ does not imply $f = g$.

## Counterexample

Let $k$ be any field. Set:
- $Z = \operatorname{Spec}(k)$,
- $X = Y = \operatorname{Spec}(k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2))$ (the scheme of dual nu
mbers over $k$),
- $f = \operatorname{id}_X$ (the identity morphism),
- $g = $ the morphism corresponding to the $k$-algebra map $k[\epsilon]/(\epsilo
n^2) \to k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$ sending $\epsilon \mapsto 0$ (the reduction m
ap).

Both $f$ and $g$ are morphisms of schemes over $Z$ (they are $k$-morphisms), and
 $f \neq g$ since $\epsilon \neq 0$ in $k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$.

## Computing the equalizer

The equalizer of $f$ and $g$ (over $Z$) is the fiber product:
$$\operatorname{eq}(f,g) = X \times_{Y \times_Z Y} Y,$$
where $X \to Y \times_Z Y$ is the map $(f, g)$ and $Y \to Y \times_Z Y$ is the d
iagonal $\Delta_{Y/Z}$.

We have $Y \times_Z Y = \operatorname{Spec}\!\big(k[\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2]/(\ep
silon_1^2, \epsilon_2^2)\big)$. The relevant ring maps from $A = k[\epsilon_1, \
epsilon_2]/(\epsilon_1^2, \epsilon_2^2)$ are:

- **For $X$ (via $(f,g)$):** $\phi_X: A \to B = k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$, with
$\epsilon_1 \mapsto \epsilon$, $\epsilon_2 \mapsto 0$.
- **For $Y$ (via diagonal):** $\phi_Y: A \to C = k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$, with
 $\epsilon_1 \mapsto \epsilon$, $\epsilon_2 \mapsto \epsilon$.

The equalizer ring is $B \otimes_A C$. Denoting the generators $\epsilon_B = \ep
silon \otimes 1$ and $\epsilon_C = 1 \otimes \epsilon$, the relations from $A$ a
re:

1. From $\epsilon_1$: $\epsilon_B = \epsilon_C$.
2. From $\epsilon_2$: $\phi_X(\epsilon_2) = 0 = \phi_Y(\epsilon_2) = \epsilon_C$
, so $\epsilon_C = 0$.

Combining: $\epsilon_B = \epsilon_C = 0$. Therefore:
$$B \otimes_A C \cong k[\epsilon_B, \epsilon_C]/(\epsilon_B^2, \epsilon_C^2, \ep
silon_B - \epsilon_C, \epsilon_C) \cong k.$$

So $\operatorname{eq}(f,g) = \operatorname{Spec}(k)$.

## Surjectivity of $i$

The map $i : \operatorname{eq}(f,g) = \operatorname{Spec}(k) \to X = \operatorna
me{Spec}(k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2))$ corresponds on rings to the quotient $k[\eps
ilon]/(\epsilon^2) \to k$, $\epsilon \mapsto 0$.

Both $\operatorname{Spec}(k)$ and $\operatorname{Spec}(k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2))
$ have a **single point** (the unique prime ideal). The map $i$ sends the unique
 point to the unique point, hence $i$ is **surjective** on underlying topologica
l spaces.

## Conclusion

We have $i : \operatorname{eq}(f,g) \to X$ surjective, yet $f \neq g$ (the ident
ity $\epsilon \mapsto \epsilon$ differs from the reduction $\epsilon \mapsto 0$)
. Therefore, surjectivity of $i$ does **not** imply $f = g$.

**Reasoning:** Surjectivity of $i$ on topological spaces only ensures that $f$ a
nd $g$ agree on the level of points (i.e., $f(x) = g(x)$ for every $x \in X$ as
points of $Y$). It does not capture agreement on the scheme structure—specifical
ly, on nilpotent elements. The dual numbers provide a minimal example: the under
lying topological space is a single point, so any two morphisms automatically ag
ree on points, but they can differ on the nilpotent $\epsilon$.
