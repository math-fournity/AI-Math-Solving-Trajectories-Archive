# Proof

**Claim:** Yes. The function $f(x) = u(x,0)$ is real analytic on $D \cap \mathbb
{R}$.

---

## Step 1. $D \cap \mathbb{R}$ is open in $\mathbb{R}$

Since $D \subset \mathbb{C} \cong \mathbb{R}^2$ is open, for every point $(x_0,
0) \in D$ there exists $\varepsilon > 0$ with $B((x_0,0), \varepsilon) \subset D
$. In particular $(x_0 - \varepsilon,\, x_0 + \varepsilon) \subset D \cap \mathb
b{R}$, so $D \cap \mathbb{R}$ is an open subset of $\mathbb{R}$. Thus "real anal
ytic on $D \cap \mathbb{R}$" is well-defined (a local property on an open set).

## Step 2. Harmonic functions are locally the real part of a holomorphic functio
n

Let $z_0 = (x_0, y_0) \in D$. Choose $r > 0$ so that the disk $B(z_0, r) \subset
 D$. The disk is simply connected, and $u$ is harmonic on it. Define a harmonic
conjugate $v$ on $B(z_0,r)$ by

$$v(x,y) \;=\; \int_{\gamma}\!\left(-u_y\,dx + u_x\,dy\right),$$

where $\gamma$ is any path in $B(z_0,r)$ from a fixed basepoint to $(x,y)$. The
1-form $-u_y\,dx + u_x\,dy$ is closed because $u$ is harmonic ($u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0
\Rightarrow (-u_y)_y = (u_x)_x$), and simple connectivity of the disk guarantees
 path-independence, so $v$ is well-defined and single-valued. Then

$$F(z) = u(x,y) + i\,v(x,y)$$

is holomorphic on $B(z_0, r)$, with $u = \operatorname{Re} F$.

## Step 3. Holomorphic $\Rightarrow$ real analytic

A holomorphic function on a disk admits a convergent power series expansion

$$F(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n (z - z_0)^n, \qquad |z - z_0| < r.$$

Writing $z - z_0 = (x - x_0) + i(y - y_0)$ and expanding $(z-z_0)^n$ by the bino
mial theorem, the real part gives

$$u(x,y) = \operatorname{Re} F(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \operatorname{Re}\!\bigl
[a_n ((x-x_0)+i(y-y_0))^n\bigr].$$

This is a convergent power series in the two real variables $(x-x_0,\, y-y_0)$ o
n $B(z_0,r)$. Hence $u$ is **real analytic** as a function of two real variables
 at every point of $D$.

## Step 4. Restriction to the real axis preserves real analyticity

Fix $x_0 \in D \cap \mathbb{R}$, so $(x_0, 0) \in D$. By Step 3, on some disk $B
((x_0,0), r) \subset D$,

$$u(x,y) = \sum_{j,k \ge 0} c_{jk}\,(x-x_0)^j (y-0)^k,$$

with absolute convergence for $|x-x_0|^2 + y^2 < r^2$. Setting $y = 0$,

$$f(x) = u(x,0) = \sum_{j \ge 0} c_{j0}\,(x - x_0)^j,$$

and this one-variable power series converges for $|x - x_0| < r$ (a sub-converge
nt series of an absolutely convergent two-variable series). Therefore $f$ is rep
resented by a convergent power series in a neighborhood of every $x_0 \in D \cap
 \mathbb{R}$.

## Conclusion

Since $x_0 \in D \cap \mathbb{R}$ was arbitrary, $f(x) = u(x,0)$ is real analyti
c on all of $D \cap \mathbb{R}$.

**The answer is yes.** $\blacksquare$
