## Proof

We prove that **yes**, provided $V \neq 0$, there exists a non-zero vector $v \i
n V$ such that $\rho(X)v = 0$ for all $X \in \mathfrak{g}$. This is **Engel's th
eorem** (in its "existence of a common kernel vector" form).

We proceed by induction on $n = \dim \mathfrak{g}$.

**Base case** ($n = 1$). Let $\mathfrak{g} = \langle X \rangle$. By hypothesis $
\rho(X)$ is a nilpotent endomorphism of $V \neq 0$. A nilpotent endomorphism has
 non-trivial kernel, so there exists $0 \neq v \in V$ with $\rho(X)v = 0$. Since
 $\mathfrak{g}$ is spanned by $X$, this gives $\rho(Y)v = 0$ for all $Y \in \mat
hfrak{g}$.

**Inductive step.** Assume the result for all nilpotent Lie algebras of dimensio
n $< n$, and let $\dim \mathfrak{g} = n \geq 2$.

*Key claim.* There exists a codimension-$1$ ideal $\mathfrak{h} \trianglelefteq
\mathfrak{g}$.

Since $\mathfrak{g}$ is nilpotent, its center $Z(\mathfrak{g})$ is non-trivial:
$Z(\mathfrak{g}) \neq 0$. Pick any non-zero $Z \in Z(\mathfrak{g})$, and extend
it to a basis of $\mathfrak{g}$. Let $\mathfrak{h}$ be the span of $n-1$ of thes
e basis vectors, chosen to include $Z$; then $\dim \mathfrak{h} = n-1$. Because
$Z$ is central, $[\mathfrak{g}, Z] = 0 \subseteq \mathfrak{h}$, and for the rema
ining generators one checks $[\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{h}] \subseteq \mathfrak{h}
$ by noting $\mathfrak{g}$ is nilpotent (so $[\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}] \subse
tneq \mathfrak{g}$, and a suitable hyperplane through $Z$ containing $[\mathfrak
{g},\mathfrak{g}]$ when $\dim \mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}] \geq 2$,
or just $[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]$ itself when $\dim \mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{
g},\mathfrak{g}] = 1$). Concretely: since $\mathfrak{g}$ is nilpotent, $[\mathfr
ak{g},\mathfrak{g}] \subsetneq \mathfrak{g}$; choose a hyperplane $\mathfrak{h}$
 containing both $[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]$ and a non-zero central element $Z
$. Then $[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}] \subseteq [\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}] \subs
eteq \mathfrak{h}$, so $\mathfrak{h}$ is an ideal. Thus $\mathfrak{h}$ is a codi
mension-$1$ ideal.

Now $\mathfrak{h}$ is nilpotent (subalgebra of a nilpotent Lie algebra) with $\d
im \mathfrak{h} = n-1 < n$, and the restriction $\rho|_{\mathfrak{h}}$ still con
sists of nilpotent endomorphisms. By the **induction hypothesis**, the subspace
$$W := \{v \in V : \rho(H)v = 0 \text{ for all } H \in \mathfrak{h}\}$$
is non-zero: $W \neq 0$.

*Claim.* $W$ is stable under $\rho(\mathfrak{g})$, i.e., $\rho(X)W \subseteq W$
for every $X \in \mathfrak{g}$.

Indeed, for $v \in W$, $H \in \mathfrak{h}$, using $\mathfrak{h}$ is an ideal so
 $[X, H] \in \mathfrak{h}$:
$$\rho(H)\rho(X)v = \rho(X)\rho(H)v + \rho([H,X])v = \rho(X)\cdot 0 + \rho([H,X]
)v = 0 + 0 = 0,$$
since $[H,X] \in \mathfrak{h}$ and $v \in W$. Hence $\rho(X)v \in W$.

Choose $X_0 \in \mathfrak{g} \setminus \mathfrak{h}$, so $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfr
ak{h} \oplus \langle X_0 \rangle$. The endomorphism $\rho(X_0)$ restricts to a n
ilpotent endomorphism of $W$ (it maps $W$ to $W$ and is nilpotent on $V$, hence
on $W$). Since $W \neq 0$ and $\rho(X_0)|_W$ is nilpotent, its kernel on $W$ is
non-trivial: there exists $0 \neq v \in W$ with $\rho(X_0)v = 0$.

For this $v$:
- $\rho(H)v = 0$ for all $H \in \mathfrak{h}$ (since $v \in W$),
- $\rho(X_0)v = 0$.

Since $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{h} \oplus \langle X_0 \rangle$, by linearity $\r
ho(X)v = 0$ for all $X \in \mathfrak{g}$.

Thus $v \neq 0$ is annihilated by every $\rho(X)$, completing the induction. $\b
lacksquare$

**Conclusion.** Provided $V \neq 0$, such a non-zero vector $v$ always exists.
