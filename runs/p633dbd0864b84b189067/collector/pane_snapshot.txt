# Proof: Does $GL_5(\mathbb{R})$ have a subgroup of index 2?

**Yes.** $GL_5(\mathbb{R})$ has a subgroup of index 2.

## Construction

Consider the **determinant map**
$$\det : GL_5(\mathbb{R}) \to \mathbb{R}^* = \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\},$$
which is a group homomorphism from $GL_5(\mathbb{R})$ (under matrix multiplicati
on) to the multiplicative group of nonzero reals.

The sign function
$$\operatorname{sgn} : \mathbb{R}^* \to \{+1, -1\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},
$$
defined by $\operatorname{sgn}(x) = +1$ if $x > 0$ and $\operatorname{sgn}(x) =
-1$ if $x < 0$, is a surjective group homomorphism whose kernel is $\mathbb{R}_{
>0}$ (a subgroup of index 2 in $\mathbb{R}^*$).

Composing these, we obtain a homomorphism
$$\varphi = \operatorname{sgn} \circ \det : GL_5(\mathbb{R}) \to \{+1, -1\} \con
g \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.$$

## Surjectivity

The map $\varphi$ is **surjective**: the diagonal matrix $\operatorname{diag}(-1
, 1, 1, 1, 1)$ lies in $GL_5(\mathbb{R})$ and has determinant $-1 < 0$, so $\var
phi$ attains the value $-1$. The identity matrix has determinant $1 > 0$, so $\v
arphi$ attains $+1$. Thus $\varphi$ is onto.

## The index-2 subgroup

The kernel of $\varphi$ is
$$\ker(\varphi) = \{A \in GL_5(\mathbb{R}) : \det(A) > 0\} =: GL_5^+(\mathbb{R})
,$$
the subgroup of invertible $5 \times 5$ real matrices with **positive determinan
t**.

By the first isomorphism theorem,
$$GL_5(\mathbb{R}) \,/\, GL_5^+(\mathbb{R}) \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},$$
so $GL_5^+(\mathbb{R})$ is a subgroup of $GL_5(\mathbb{R})$ of index 2.

## Verification that $GL_5^+(\mathbb{R})$ is indeed a subgroup

- **Closure**: If $\det(A) > 0$ and $\det(B) > 0$, then $\det(AB) = \det(A)\det(
B) > 0$.
- **Identity**: $\det(I_5) = 1 > 0$.
- **Inverses**: If $\det(A) > 0$, then $\det(A^{-1}) = 1/\det(A) > 0$.

Hence $GL_5^+(\mathbb{R})$ is a valid subgroup, and it has exactly two cosets in
 $GL_5(\mathbb{R})$:
- $GL_5^+(\mathbb{R})$ itself (matrices with positive determinant),
- its complement (matrices with negative determinant).

Therefore $[GL_5(\mathbb{R}) : GL_5^+(\mathbb{R})] = 2$.

## Conclusion

$GL_5(\mathbb{R})$ **does** have a subgroup of index 2, namely $GL_5^+(\mathbb{R
}) = \{A \in GL_5(\mathbb{R}) : \det A > 0\}$, realized as the kernel of the sur
jective homomorphism $\operatorname{sgn} \circ \det : GL_5(\mathbb{R}) \to \math
bb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. $\blacksquare$
