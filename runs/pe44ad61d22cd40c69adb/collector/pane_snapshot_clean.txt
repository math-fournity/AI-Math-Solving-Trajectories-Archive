## Solution

**Claim:** The only connected graph of order 5 in which the distance between eve
ry two distinct vertices is odd is the complete graph $K_5$.

### Proof

Let $G$ be a connected graph on 5 vertices such that $d(u,v)$ is odd for every p
air of distinct vertices $u, v$.

**Key step.** Suppose for contradiction that $G$ is not complete. Then there exi
st vertices $u, v$ with $d(u,v) \geq 2$. Since $d(u,v)$ must be odd, we have $d(
u,v) \geq 3$.

Let $u = x_0, x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_k = v$ be a shortest path from $u$ to $v$, whe
re $k = d(u,v) \geq 3$. A standard property of shortest paths is that every subp
ath is itself a shortest path. In particular, the subpath $x_0, x_1, x_2$ is a s
hortest path from $u$ to $x_2$, so:

$$d(u, x_2) = 2.$$

But $2$ is **even**, which contradicts our assumption that every pairwise distan
ce in $G$ is odd.

**Conclusion of contradiction.** The assumption that $G$ is not complete is fals
e. Therefore $d(u,v) = 1$ for every pair of distinct vertices, meaning every pai
r of vertices is adjacent.

**Verification.** The complete graph $K_5$ is connected, and the distance betwee
n every two distinct vertices is $1$, which is odd. ✓

**Uniqueness.** Since the argument above applies to *any* connected graph (of an
y order) with the all-odd-distances property — not just order 5 — and forces the
 graph to be complete, $K_5$ is the **unique** connected graph of order 5 satisf
ying the condition.

### The graph

$K_5$: five vertices, every pair joined by an edge (10 edges total). This is the
 only connected graph of order 5 in which the distance between every two distinc
t vertices is odd.
