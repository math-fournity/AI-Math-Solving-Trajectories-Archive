### Setup

Let $f(x)=ax^3+bx^2+cx+d$ with $a,b,c,d\in\mathbb Z$, and let $y_i=f(i)$ for $i=
0,1,2,3$. Each $y_i\in\{0,1,\dots,20\}$. Since a polynomial of degree $\le 3$ is
 uniquely determined by its values at four points, counting valid functions equa
ls counting valid 4-tuples $(y_0,y_1,y_2,y_3)$ that yield integer coefficients.

### Expressing coefficients via finite differences

Define the forward differences
$$C=y_1-y_0,\quad B=y_2-2y_1+y_0,\quad A=y_3-3y_2+3y_1-y_0.$$
In Newton form, $f(x)=y_0+\binom{x}{1}C+\binom{x}{2}B+\binom{x}{3}A$. Expanding
the binomial coefficients gives:
$$a=\frac{A}{6},\qquad b=\frac{B-A}{2},\qquad c=C-\frac{B}{2}+\frac{A}{3},\qquad
 d=y_0.$$

### Integrality conditions

- $d=y_0\in\mathbb Z$: automatic.
- $a\in\mathbb Z\iff 6\mid A$.
- $b\in\mathbb Z\iff 2\mid(B-A)$. With $6\mid A$ (so $A$ even), this becomes $2\
mid B$.
- $c\in\mathbb Z\iff 6\mid(2A-3B)$. With $6\mid A$, we have $2A\equiv0\pmod6$, s
o this becomes $6\mid3B$, i.e. $2\mid B$.

**Both extra conditions reduce to $6\mid A$ and $2\mid B$.** One checks converse
ly that these are sufficient: $6\mid A\Rightarrow A/3,A/6\in\mathbb Z$; $2\mid B
\Rightarrow B/2\in\mathbb Z$ and $B-A$ even. Hence:

$$\boxed{6\mid A\ \text{and}\ 2\mid B}$$

are exactly the integrality conditions. Since $B=y_2-2y_1+y_0\equiv y_2+y_0\pmod
2$, the condition $2\mid B$ is $y_0\equiv y_2\pmod2$.

### Counting

We count
$$\sum_{\substack{y_0,y_1,y_2\in\{0,\dots,20\}\\ y_0\equiv y_2\pmod2}} N\!\big((
3(y_2-y_1)+y_0)\bmod 6\big),$$
where $N(r)=\#\{y_3\in\{0,\dots,20\}:y_3\equiv r\pmod6\}$. Over $\{0,\dots,20\}$
 (21 values): $N(0)=N(1)=N(2)=4$ and $N(3)=N(4)=N(5)=3$.

**Key reduction.** Since $3y_2-3y_1\pmod6$ equals $0$ when $y_1\equiv y_2\pmod2$
 and $3$ otherwise, the residue $r=(3(y_2-y_1)+y_0)\bmod6$ depends only on $\alp
ha:=y_0\bmod6$ and the parity match of $y_1,y_2$:
$$r=\alpha+\delta\pmod6,\quad \delta=\begin{cases}0&y_1\equiv y_2\pmod2\\3&\text
{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$

Group by $\alpha=y_0\bmod6$ and $\beta=y_2\bmod6$ (requiring same parity). Let $
c_\alpha=\#\{y_0\in\{0,\dots,20\}:y_0\equiv\alpha\pmod6\}$:

| $\alpha$ | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $c_\alpha$ | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 3 |

For fixed $\alpha$ (with parity $p$), the bracket $g(\alpha)=\#\{y_1\text{ parit
y }p\}\cdot N(\alpha)+\#\{y_1\text{ parity }1{-}p\}\cdot N(\alpha{+}3)$:

- **Even $\alpha\in\{0,2,4\}$**: $y_1$ even count $=11$, odd $=10$.
  - $g(0)=11\cdot4+10\cdot3=74$
  - $g(2)=11\cdot4+10\cdot3=74$
  - $g(4)=11\cdot3+10\cdot4=73$

- **Odd $\alpha\in\{1,3,5\}$**: $y_1$ odd count $=10$, even $=11$.
  - $g(1)=10\cdot4+11\cdot3=73$
  - $g(3)=10\cdot3+11\cdot4=74$
  - $g(5)=10\cdot3+11\cdot4=74$

The total factors as $\bigl(\sum_{\text{even }\alpha}c_\alpha g(\alpha)\bigr)\bi
gl(\sum_{\text{even }\beta}c_\beta\bigr)+\bigl(\sum_{\text{odd }\alpha}c_\alpha
g(\alpha)\bigr)\bigl(\sum_{\text{odd }\beta}c_\beta\bigr)$.

**Even part:** $\sum_\beta c_\beta=4+4+3=11$ (number of even $y_2$); $\sum_\alph
a c_\alpha g(\alpha)=4\cdot74+4\cdot74+3\cdot73=296+296+219=811$. Product $=811\
cdot11=8921$.

**Odd part:** $\sum_\beta c_\beta=4+3+3=10$ (number of odd $y_2$); $\sum_\alpha
c_\alpha g(\alpha)=4\cdot73+3\cdot74+3\cdot74=292+222+222=736$. Product $=736\cd
ot10=7360$.

$$\text{Total}=8921+7360=\boxed{16281}.$$
