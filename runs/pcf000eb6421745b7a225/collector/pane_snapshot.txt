Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a subspace $V \subseteq H^s
(\mathbb{R}^n)$ satisfying $\|f\|_{H^s} \leq C\|f\|_{H^t}$ for all $f \in V$ (wi
th $s > t$) must be finite-dimensional.

**Answer: No, $V$ is not necessarily finite-dimensional.**

## Proof

I construct an explicit infinite-dimensional subspace satisfying the hypothesis.

**Construction.** Fix any $R > 0$ and define
$$V = \bigl\{f \in H^s(\mathbb{R}^n) : \operatorname{supp}(\widehat{f}) \subsete
q \overline{B(0,R)}\bigr\},$$
where $\widehat{f}$ denotes the Fourier transform of $f$.

**Step 1: $V$ is a linear subspace of $H^s(\mathbb{R}^n)$.**

If $f, g \in V$ and $\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{R}$, then $\widehat{\alpha f + \b
eta g} = \alpha\widehat{f} + \beta\widehat{g}$ is supported in $\overline{B(0,R)
}$, so $\alpha f + \beta g \in V$. Moreover, every $f \in V$ belongs to $H^s$ si
nce $\widehat{f}$ is compactly supported and in $L^2$, giving $(1+|\xi|^2)^{s/2}
\widehat{f} \in L^2$.

**Step 2: $V$ is infinite-dimensional.**

The Fourier transform $\mathcal{F}: L^2(\mathbb{R}^n) \to L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is
an isomorphism (up to a constant). Its restriction gives a bijection from $V$ on
to $L^2\bigl(\overline{B(0,R)}\bigr)$ (the space of $L^2$ functions supported in
 $\overline{B(0,R)}$). Since $L^2\bigl(\overline{B(0,R)}\bigr)$ is infinite-dime
nsional for any $R > 0$ (it contains, e.g., the functions $e^{ik\cdot\xi}$ restr
icted to the ball for infinitely many distinct $k$, or simply a countable family
 of disjointly supported bump functions), $V$ is infinite-dimensional.

**Step 3: The norm inequality holds.**

For any $f \in V$, using $\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{f}) \subseteq \overline{B
(0,R)}$:
$$\|f\|_{H^s}^2 = \int_{\overline{B(0,R)}} (1+|\xi|^2)^s\,|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^2\,
d\xi.$$

Since $|\xi| \leq R$ on the support, we have $(1+|\xi|^2)^{s-t} \leq (1+R^2)^{s-
t}$, so:
$$(1+|\xi|^2)^s = (1+|\xi|^2)^{s-t}\cdot(1+|\xi|^2)^t \leq (1+R^2)^{s-t}\cdot(1+
|\xi|^2)^t.$$

Therefore:
$$\|f\|_{H^s}^2 \leq (1+R^2)^{s-t}\int_{\overline{B(0,R)}}(1+|\xi|^2)^t\,|\wideh
at{f}(\xi)|^2\,d\xi = (1+R^2)^{s-t}\,\|f\|_{H^t}^2.$$

Setting $C = (1+R^2)^{(s-t)/2}$, we obtain $\|f\|_{H^s} \leq C\,\|f\|_{H^t}$ for
 all $f \in V$.

**Conclusion.** The subspace $V$ is infinite-dimensional and satisfies the requi
red norm inequality with $C = (1+R^2)^{(s-t)/2}$. Hence $V$ is **not** necessari
ly finite-dimensional.

**Remark.** The key difference from the bounded-domain case is that the Rellich–
Kondrachov compactness theorem (which would force finite-dimensionality) fails o
n $\mathbb{R}^n$: the embedding $H^s(\mathbb{R}^n) \hookrightarrow H^t(\mathbb{R
}^n)$ is continuous but **not** compact. The counterexample exploits this by con
centrating Fourier support in a fixed ball, where the weights $(1+|\xi|^2)^s$ an
d $(1+|\xi|^2)^t$ are comparable—yielding norm equivalence on an infinite-dimens
ional space.
