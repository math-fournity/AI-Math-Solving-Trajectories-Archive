## Solution

**Claim.** The polynomial
$$f(x) = (x^2 - 2)(x^2 - 3)(x^2 - 6) \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$$
has a root in every finite field $\mathbb{F}_p$ ($p$ prime) but has no root in $
\mathbb{Z}$.

---

### No integer root

An integer root $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ would require one of $n^2 = 2$, $n^2 = 3$, or
 $n^2 = 6$. None of $2, 3, 6$ is a perfect square, so no such integer exists. He
nce $f$ has **no root in $\mathbb{Z}$**.

---

### Root in every $\mathbb{F}_p$

We must show that for every prime $p$, at least one of $2, 3, 6$ is a quadratic
residue modulo $p$ (so that the corresponding factor $x^2 - a$ vanishes at some
$x \in \mathbb{F}_p$).

**Case $p = 2$.** Then $x^2 - 2 \equiv x^2 \pmod{2}$, which has root $x = 0$.

**Case $p = 3$.** Then $x^2 - 3 \equiv x^2 \pmod{3}$, which has root $x = 0$.

**Case $p \geq 5$.** Consider the Legendre symbols $\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)$, $
\left(\frac{3}{p}\right)$, $\left(\frac{6}{p}\right)$. Since $6 = 2 \cdot 3$, by
 complete multiplicativity,
$$\left(\frac{6}{p}\right) = \left(\frac{2}{p}\right)\left(\frac{3}{p}\right).$$
Therefore
$$\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)\left(\frac{3}{p}\right)\left(\frac{6}{p}\right) = \le
ft(\frac{2}{p}\right)\left(\frac{3}{p}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)\left(
\frac{3}{p}\right) = \left(\frac{2}{p}\right)^2\left(\frac{3}{p}\right)^2 = 1.$$
Each Legendre symbol is $\pm 1$, and their product is $1$, so an **even** number
 of them equal $-1$. In particular they cannot all be $-1$; at least one of $\le
ft(\frac{2}{p}\right), \left(\frac{3}{p}\right), \left(\frac{6}{p}\right)$ equal
s $+1$.

Thus at least one of $2, 3, 6$ is a quadratic residue mod $p$, so the correspond
ing factor $x^2 - a$ has a root in $\mathbb{F}_p$, and consequently $f$ has a ro
ot in $\mathbb{F}_p$.

---

### Conclusion

The polynomial $f(x) = (x^2-2)(x^2-3)(x^2-6) \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ has a root in $\
mathbb{F}_p$ for every prime $p$, yet has no root in $\mathbb{Z}$, as required.
