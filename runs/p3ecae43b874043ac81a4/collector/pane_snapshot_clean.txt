## Proof

**Answer: Yes.**

We construct an explicit continuous map $f \colon T^2 \to \mathbb{R}P^2$ that is
 not null-homotopic, by ensuring it induces a nontrivial homomorphism on fundame
ntal groups.

**Setup.** Recall:
- $\pi_1(T^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$, with generators corresponding to the two circ
le factors of $T^2 = S^1 \times S^1$.
- $\pi_1(\mathbb{R}P^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.
- A standard fact: if two maps are homotopic, they induce the same homomorphism
on $\pi_1$; a constant map induces the trivial homomorphism. Hence **any map ind
ucing a nontrivial $\pi_1$-homomorphism is not null-homotopic.**

**The construction.** Realize $\mathbb{R}P^1$ as the 1-skeleton of $\mathbb{R}P^
2$ (a projective line). The standard CW-structure of $\mathbb{R}P^2$ attaches a
2-cell to $\mathbb{R}P^1 \cong S^1$ via a degree-2 map, giving the presentation
$\pi_1(\mathbb{R}P^2) = \langle x \mid x^2 = 1\rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{
Z}$. The inclusion
$$\iota \colon \mathbb{R}P^1 \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}P^2$$
induces the surjection $\iota_* \colon \pi_1(\mathbb{R}P^1)\cong\mathbb{Z} \to \
pi_1(\mathbb{R}P^2)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, sending the generator $1 \mapst
o \bar{1}$.

Now define
$$f \colon T^2 = S^1 \times S^1 \xrightarrow{\;p_1\;} S^1 \cong \mathbb{R}P^1 \x
rightarrow{\;\iota\;} \mathbb{R}P^2,$$
where $p_1(z,w)=z$ is projection onto the first factor and the middle $\cong$ is
 the canonical homeomorphism $S^1 \cong \mathbb{R}P^1$.

**Induced map on $\pi_1$.** The projection $p_1$ induces $p_{1*}\colon \mathbb{Z
}^2 \to \mathbb{Z}$, $(a,b)\mapsto a$. Composing with $\iota_*$,
$$f_* \colon \mathbb{Z}^2 \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \qquad (a,b) \mapsto a \bm
od 2.$$
This homomorphism is **nontrivial**: for instance, $f_*(1,0) = \bar{1} \neq \bar
{0}$.

**Conclusion.** Since $f_*$ is nontrivial, $f$ cannot be homotopic to a constant
 map (which would induce the trivial homomorphism). Therefore $f$ is a continuou
s map from the torus to the real projective plane that is not homotopic to a con
stant map.
