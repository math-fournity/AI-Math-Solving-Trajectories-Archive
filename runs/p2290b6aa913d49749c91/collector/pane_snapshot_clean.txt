## Solution

**Setup.** We solve the heat equation $u_t = u_{xx}$ on $[0,\pi]$ with $u(0,t)=u
(\pi,t)=0$ and $u(x,0)=f(x)=(\pi-2x)/4$ via separation of variables:

$$u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n\, e^{-n^2 t}\sin(nx),\qquad b_n=\frac{2}{\pi}\i
nt_0^\pi f(x)\sin(nx)\,dx.$$

**Computing the coefficients.**

$$b_n=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^\pi(\pi-2x)\sin(nx)\,dx.$$

Using $\int_0^\pi\sin(nx)\,dx=\frac{1-(-1)^n}{n}$ and $\int_0^\pi x\sin(nx)\,dx=
-\frac{\pi(-1)^n}{n}$:

$$\int_0^\pi(\pi-2x)\sin(nx)\,dx=\pi\cdot\frac{1-(-1)^n}{n}-2\cdot\!\left(-\frac
{\pi(-1)^n}{n}\right)=\frac{\pi\,[1+(-1)^n]}{n}.$$

Hence $b_n=\frac{1+(-1)^n}{2n}$: **all odd coefficients vanish**, and for $n=2k$
,

$$b_{2k}=\frac{1}{2k}.$$

So

$$u(x,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2k}\,e^{-4k^2 t}\sin(2kx).$$

**Applying Parseval.** Since $\{\sin(nx)\}$ is orthogonal on $[0,\pi]$ with $\in
t_0^\pi\sin^2(nx)\,dx=\pi/2$,

$$A^2=\int_0^\pi|u(x,t)-f(x)|^2\,dx=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_n^2\bigl(1
-e^{-n^2 t}\bigr)^2=\frac{\pi}{8}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^2}\bigl(1-e^{-4k^
2 t}\bigr)^2.$$

**Asymptotics of the sum.** Set $s=4t$ and

$$S(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^2}\bigl(1-e^{-sk^2}\bigr)^2.$$

Write $\frac{1}{k^2}(1-e^{-sk^2})^2 = s\,h(k\sqrt{s})$ where $h(u)=\frac{(1-e^{-
u^2})^2}{u^2}$. Then

$$S(s)=\sqrt{s}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}h(k\sqrt{s})\,\sqrt{s}\;\xrightarrow{s\to 0^+}
\;\sqrt{s}\int_0^\infty h(u)\,du = C\sqrt{s},$$

by the Riemann-sum approximation (the function $h$ is continuous, integrable, an
d $h(u)\sim u^2$ near $0$, $h(u)\sim 1/u^2$ at $\infty$).

**Evaluating the constant.** Integrate by parts with $dv=du/u^2$, $v=-1/u$:

$$C=\int_0^\infty\frac{(1-e^{-u^2})^2}{u^2}\,du=\Bigl[-\frac{(1-e^{-u^2})^2}{u}\
Bigr]_0^\infty+\int_0^\infty\frac{4u\,e^{-u^2}(1-e^{-u^2})}{u}\,du$$

$$=4\int_0^\infty\bigl(e^{-u^2}-e^{-2u^2}\bigr)\,du=4\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}-
\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\sqrt{2}}\Bigr)=\sqrt{\pi}\,(2-\sqrt{2}).$$

**Final estimate.** Therefore, as $t\to 0^+$,

$$A^2\sim\frac{\pi}{8}\,C\sqrt{4t}=\frac{\pi}{8}\cdot\sqrt{\pi}\,(2-\sqrt{2})\cd
ot 2\sqrt{t}=\frac{\pi^{3/2}(2-\sqrt{2})}{4}\,\sqrt{t},$$

and

$$\boxed{A\;\sim\;\frac{\pi^{3/4}}{2}\sqrt{2-\sqrt{2}}\;\,t^{1/4}\quad(t\to 0^+)
.}$$

**Conclusion.** The solution $u(x,t)$ converges to $f(x)$ in the mean (in $L^2(0
,\pi)$) as $t\to 0^+$ with order $t^{1/4}$:

$$\left(\int_0^\pi|u(x,t)-f(x)|^2\,dx\right)^{1/2}=O(t^{1/4}),\qquad t\to 0^+.$$

The slow rate $t^{1/4}$ (rather than the $t^{1/2}$ one gets for smoother data) r
eflects the fact that $f$ is piecewise smooth with jump-type boundary incompatib
ility ($f(0)=\pi/4\neq 0$, $f(\pi)=-\pi/4\neq 0$), so the sine coefficients deca
y only as $b_{2k}\sim 1/k$, producing the $\sqrt{s}$ behavior in the spectral su
m.
