# Proof

**Answer: Yes, such an injective envelope exists.**

## Setup and Key Facts

Let $A$ be an artinian ring. We recall the following standard facts:

- **(F1)** Over a noetherian ring (in particular, an artinian ring), every injec
tive module decomposes as a direct sum of indecomposable injective modules (Matl
is–Papp theorem).
- **(F2)** Over a noetherian ring, every indecomposable injective module has a *
*local endomorphism ring** (Matlis).
- **(F3)** A finite direct sum of injective modules is injective.
- **(F4)** A direct summand of an injective module is injective.
- **(F5)** (Azumaya's uniqueness theorem) If a module $M$ admits two decompositi
ons into direct sums of modules with local endomorphism rings, then the decompos
itions are equivalent: the summands are pairwise isomorphic up to a bijection of
 indices.

## Step 1: $E(X)$ embeds into $M = \bigoplus_{j=1}^n I_j$

By (F3), $M$ is injective. Let $\iota: X \hookrightarrow E(X)$ be the injective
envelope. Since $M$ is injective and $f: X \to M$ is a monomorphism, there exist
s a map $\varphi: E(X) \to M$ extending $f$, i.e., $\varphi \circ \iota = f$.

**Claim:** $\varphi$ is a monomorphism. Suppose $\varphi(e) = 0$ for some $e \in
 E(X)$. If $e \neq 0$, then since $\iota: X \hookrightarrow E(X)$ is an **essent
ial** extension, the nonzero submodule $Ae \subseteq E(X)$ intersects $X$ nontri
vially: there exists $a \in A$ with $0 \neq ae \in X$. Then:
$$0 = \varphi(ae) = a\,\varphi(e) = 0,$$
but also $\varphi(ae) = f(ae)$ (since $ae \in X$). Since $f$ is a monomorphism,
$f(ae) = 0 \Rightarrow ae = 0$, contradicting $ae \neq 0$. Hence $e = 0$, and $\
varphi$ is injective. $\square$

## Step 2: $E(X)$ is a direct summand of $M$

Since $E(X)$ is injective (F4 applies to the identity), the monomorphism $\varph
i: E(X) \hookrightarrow M$ **splits**: there exists $K \subseteq M$ such that
$$M = \varphi(E(X)) \oplus K.$$

## Step 3: Decomposition into indecomposable injectives

Since $A$ is artinian (hence noetherian), by (F1):
- $E(X) \cong \bigoplus_{t=1}^{m} J_t$, where each $J_t$ is indecomposable injec
tive.
- $K$ is injective (being a direct summand of the injective $M$, by (F4)), so $K
 \cong \bigoplus_{s=1}^{n-m} K_s$, where each $K_s$ is indecomposable injective.

Thus we have two decompositions of $M$:
$$M = \bigoplus_{j=1}^{n} I_j = \left(\bigoplus_{t=1}^{m} J_t\right) \oplus \lef
t(\bigoplus_{s=1}^{n-m} K_s\right).$$

## Step 4: Apply Azumaya's uniqueness theorem

By (F2), each indecomposable injective over the artinian ring $A$ has a local en
domorphism ring. Therefore, by (F5) (Azumaya's theorem), the two decompositions
are equivalent: there exists a bijection $\sigma: \{1, \ldots, n\} \to \{1, \ldo
ts, n\}$ such that
$$I_{\sigma(t)} \cong J_t \quad (t = 1, \ldots, m), \qquad I_{\sigma(m+s)} \cong
 K_s \quad (s = 1, \ldots, n-m).$$

Setting $S = \{\sigma(1), \ldots, \sigma(m)\} \subseteq \{1, \ldots, n\}$, we ob
tain:
$$E(X) \cong \bigoplus_{t=1}^{m} J_t \cong \bigoplus_{j \in S} I_j.$$

## Step 5: Construct the injective envelope $g: X \to \bigoplus_{j \in S} I_j$

Let $\psi: E(X) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \bigoplus_{j \in S} I_j$ be the isomorphi
sm from Step 4, and let $\iota: X \hookrightarrow E(X)$ be the injective envelop
e inclusion. Define:
$$g = \psi \circ \iota : X \longrightarrow \bigoplus_{j \in S} I_j.$$

- **$g$ is a monomorphism:** Both $\psi$ and $\iota$ are injective.
- **$\bigoplus_{j \in S} I_j$ is injective:** It is a finite direct sum of injec
tives (F3).
- **$g$ is an essential extension:** Let $N \neq 0$ be a submodule of $\bigoplus
_{j \in S} I_j$. Then $\psi^{-1}(N) \neq 0$ is a submodule of $E(X)$. Since $\io
ta$ is essential, $\psi^{-1}(N) \cap \iota(X) \neq 0$, so $N \cap g(X) = \psi(\p
si^{-1}(N) \cap \iota(X)) \neq 0$.

Therefore $g: X \to \bigoplus_{j \in S} I_j$ is an injective envelope of $X$, wh
ere $S = \{j_1, \ldots, j_m\} \subset \{1, \ldots, n\}$.

## Conclusion

**Yes**, there exists an injective envelope $g: X \to \bigoplus_{t=1}^{m} I_{j_t
}$ with $\{j_1, \ldots, j_m\} \subset \{1, \ldots, n\}$. The justification is:

1. The injective envelope $E(X)$ embeds into $M = \bigoplus I_j$ (by injectivity
 of $M$ and essentiality of $X \subseteq E(X)$).
2. This embedding splits, making $E(X)$ a direct summand of $M$.
3. Over the artinian (hence noetherian) ring $A$, both $E(X)$ and its complement

4. By the Azumaya (Krull–Schmidt) uniqueness theorem, $E(X)$ is isomorphic to a
direct sum of a **subcollection** of $\{I_1, \ldots, I_n\}$.
5. Transporting the essential extension $X \hookrightarrow E(X)$ through this is
omorphism yields the desired injective envelope $g: X \to \bigoplus_{t=1}^{m} I_
{j_t}$.
