## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. If $E$ is a sheaf on an algebraic surface $S$ over $\mathbb{C}$
with $\dim(\operatorname{Supp}(E))=0$, then $H^{i}(S,E)=0$ for all $i\geq 1$.

*Setup.* In the context of sheaves on an algebraic surface, "$E$ is a zero-dimen
sional sheaf" means $E$ is a coherent (or at least quasi-coherent) sheaf whose s
upport is a closed subset of dimension $0$. Since $S$ is Noetherian, $Z:=\operat
orname{Supp}(E)$ is a Noetherian topological space of dimension $0$, hence a **f
inite discrete** space (a $0$-dimensional Noetherian topological space has only
finitely many points, each open).

*Step 1 — Reduce cohomology from $S$ to $Z$.* Let $i:Z\hookrightarrow S$ be the
inclusion. Because every stalk $E_{x}$ with $x\notin Z$ is zero, the canonical m
ap
$$E \;\longrightarrow\; i_{*}\,i^{-1}E$$
is an isomorphism of sheaves on $S$ (both sides have stalk $E_{x}$ on $Z$ and st
alk $0$ off $Z$). The map $i$ is a closed immersion, so $i_{*}$ is **exact**; co
nsequently $R^{q}i_{*}=0$ for $q\geq 1$. The Leray spectral sequence
$$H^{p}(S,\,R^{q}i_{*}(i^{-1}E))\;\Rightarrow\;H^{p+q}(Z,\,i^{-1}E)$$
therefore degenerates and gives, for every $i$,
$$H^{i}(S,E)=H^{i}(S,\,i_{*}i^{-1}E)=H^{i}(Z,\,i^{-1}E).$$

*Step 2 — Cohomology on a finite discrete space vanishes in degree $\geq 1$.* A
finite discrete space has the property that **every** sheaf of abelian groups on
 it is flasque (flabby): any open $U\subseteq Z$ is a union of points, and a sec
tion over $U$ extends to all of $Z$ by assigning arbitrary stalk values at the f
initely many points outside $U$. Flasque sheaves are acyclic for global sections
, so
$$H^{i}(Z,\,G)=0 \quad\text{for all } i\geq 1 \text{ and every sheaf } G \text{
on } Z.$$

*Step 3 — Conclusion.* Taking $G=i^{-1}E$ in Step 2 and combining with Step 1,
$$H^{i}(S,E)=H^{i}(Z,\,i^{-1}E)=0 \quad\text{for all } i\geq 1.$$

*Remark.* This is the $d=0$ case of Grothendieck's vanishing theorem: on a Noeth
erian topological space of dimension $n$, $H^{i}(X,\mathcal{F})=0$ for all $i>n$
 and all sheaves of abelian groups $\mathcal{F}$. Applying it to $Z$ (dimension
$0$) yields $H^{i}(Z,i^{-1}E)=0$ for $i>0$. The argument above does not require
coherence beyond guaranteeing that the support is closed; it works for any sheaf
 of abelian groups whose support is a closed $0$-dimensional subset of $S$.
