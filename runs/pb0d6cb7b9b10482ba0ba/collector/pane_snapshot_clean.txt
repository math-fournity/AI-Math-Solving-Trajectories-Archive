## Proof

**Setup.** We have $n$ drones, each completing 15 simulations (earning 0 or 1 cr
edit per simulation). Let $s_i$ denote drone $i$'s total score ($0 \le s_i \le 1
5$). The condition states that every subset of 12 drones has combined score $\ge
 36$. The conclusion states that some simulation has $\ge 3$ successful drones.

**We prove $n = 13$ is sufficient via the contrapositive.**

Suppose, for contradiction, that **no** simulation was completed by 3 or more dr
ones — that is, every simulation was completed by **at most 2** drones. Then the
 total number of successful (drone, simulation) pairs across the entire fleet is
 at most:
$$15 \times 2 = 30.$$
So $\sum_{i=1}^{13} s_i \le 30$.

Order the scores: $s_1 \le s_2 \le \cdots \le s_{13}$. The 12 drones with the **
smallest** scores have combined total $s_1 + s_2 + \cdots + s_{12}$.

**Averaging argument.** Since $s_1, s_2, \ldots, s_{12}$ are the 12 smallest val
ues, their average cannot exceed the overall average:
$$\frac{s_1 + s_2 + \cdots + s_{12}}{12} \le \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{13} s_i}{13} \le
\frac{30}{13}.$$

Therefore:
$$s_1 + s_2 + \cdots + s_{12} \le \frac{12 \times 30}{13} = \frac{360}{13} \appr
ox 27.7.$$

Since the sum is an integer:
$$s_1 + s_2 + \cdots + s_{12} \le 27 < 36.$$

This means the 12 drones with the smallest scores have a combined total of at mo
st 27, which is **strictly less than 36**. This directly contradicts the conditi
on that *every* group of 12 drones has a combined total of at least 36.

**Contrapositive conclusion.** The assumption that every simulation has at most
2 successful drones leads to a contradiction with the condition. Therefore, when
ever the condition holds for $n = 13$, there must exist at least one simulation
completed by $\ge 3$ drones.

**Minimality.** The problem requires $n > 12$. Since $n$ is a positive integer,
the smallest value satisfying $n > 12$ is $n = 13$. We have shown $n = 13$ suffi
ces, so it is the smallest possible value.

**Non-vacuity check.** The condition is satisfiable for $n = 13$: for instance,
if all 13 drones each complete exactly 3 simulations (score = 3), then every 12-
drone subgroup has combined total $12 \times 3 = 36 \ge 36$, and indeed each sim
ulation has $\ge 3$ successful drones by pigeonhole ($39$ total successes across
 $15$ simulations).

$$\boxed{n = 13}$$
