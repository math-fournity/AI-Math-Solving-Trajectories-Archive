## Solution

**Preliminary observation.** The integrand $\dfrac{e^{-bx^2}\cos(mx)}{x}$ is an
**odd** function of $x$ (the numerator $e^{-bx^2}\cos(mx)$ is even, $1/x$ is odd
). Hence, taken literally as a Cauchy principal value,

$$\operatorname{P.V.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-bx^2}\cos(mx)}{x}\,dx =
0.$$

The instruction to use the **error function** signals that the intended integran
d is $\sin(mx)$ (the classic result). I solve that version, which is the nontriv
ial one tied to the Fourier transform and $\operatorname{erf}$.

---

### Evaluate $\displaystyle I(m)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-bx^2}\sin(mx)}
{x}\,dx$, $\,b>0$.

Since $\sin(mx)/x$ is even and $e^{-bx^2}$ is even, the integrand is even, so

$$I(m)=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-bx^2}\sin(mx)}{x}\,dx, \qquad I(0)=0.$$

**Step 1 — Differentiate under the integral sign (Fourier cosine transform).**

$$I'(m)=2\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-bx^2}\cos(mx)\,dx.$$

The integral $\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-bx^2}\cos(mx)\,dx$ is the **Fourier cosine tr
ansform** of the Gaussian $e^{-bx^2}$. Completing the square,

$$-bx^{2}+imx=-b\!\left(x-\frac{im}{2b}\right)^{2}-\frac{m^{2}}{4b},$$

so the full Fourier transform gives

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-bx^{2}}e^{imx}\,dx=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{b}}\,e^{-m^{2}/
(4b)}.$$

Taking the real part and halving (even integrand):

$$\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-bx^{2}}\cos(mx)\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{b}}\,e^{-
m^{2}/(4b)}.$$

Therefore

$$I'(m)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{b}}\,e^{-m^{2}/(4b)}.$$

**Step 2 — Integrate in $m$ and express via the error function.**

$$I(m)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{b}}\int_{0}^{m}e^{-t^{2}/(4b)}\,dt.$$

Substitute $u=\dfrac{t}{2\sqrt{b}}$, $\,dt=2\sqrt{b}\,du$:

$$I(m)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{b}}\cdot 2\sqrt{b}\int_{0}^{m/(2\sqrt{b})}e^{-u^{2}}\,du
=2\sqrt{\pi}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\operatorname{erf}\!\left(\frac{m}{2\sqrt{b
}}\right).$$

Thus

$$\boxed{\,I(m)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-bx^{2}}\sin(mx)}{x}\,dx=\pi\,\o
peratorname{erf}\!\left(\frac{m}{2\sqrt{b}}\right)\,}.$$

**Consistency checks.**
- $m=0$: $\operatorname{erf}(0)=0$, so $I(0)=0$. ✓
- $m\to\infty$: $\operatorname{erf}\to 1$, so $I\to\pi$, recovering the Dirichle
t integral $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(mx)}{x}\,dx=\pi$. ✓
- $b\to 0^{+}$ (Gaussian → 1): same limit $\pi$. ✓

### Conclusion

- For the integral **as written** with $\cos(mx)$: the Cauchy principal value is
 $\boxed{0}$ (odd integrand).
- For the **intended** integral with $\sin(mx)$ (the one naturally involving the
 Fourier transform and error function):

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-bx^{2}}\sin(mx)}{x}\,dx=\pi\,\operatorname{er
f}\!\left(\frac{m}{2\sqrt{b}}\right),\qquad b>0.$$
