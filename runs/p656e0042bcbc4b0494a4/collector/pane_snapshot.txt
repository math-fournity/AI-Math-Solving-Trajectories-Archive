## Proof

We determine for which $p \in [1,\infty)$ the function
$$f(x) = \frac{1}{|x|(1+|x|)^d}, \quad x \neq 0,\qquad f(0)=0$$
belongs to $L^p(\mathbb{R}^d)$.

**Reduction to a one-dimensional integral.** Passing to polar coordinates $x = r
\theta$ with $r=|x|>0$ and $\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$, the volume element is $d
x = \omega_{d-1}\, r^{d-1}\,dr$, where $\omega_{d-1}=|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|$. Since
$f$ is radial,

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}^d} |f(x)|^p\,dx \;=\; \omega_{d-1}\int_0^\infty \frac{r^{d-1}
}{\bigl(r(1+r)^d\bigr)^p}\,dr \;=\; \omega_{d-1}\int_0^\infty r^{d-1-p}(1+r)^{-d
p}\,dr.$$

So $f\in L^p$ iff $I_p := \int_0^\infty r^{d-1-p}(1+r)^{-dp}\,dr < \infty$. We s
plit $I_p = \int_0^1 + \int_1^\infty$ and analyze each piece.

**Behavior near $r=0$.** For $0<r\le 1$, $(1+r)^{-dp}\to 1$, so the integrand is
 comparable to $r^{d-1-p}$. Thus
$$\int_0^1 r^{d-1-p}(1+r)^{-dp}\,dr < \infty \iff \int_0^1 r^{d-1-p}\,dr < \inft
y \iff d-1-p > -1 \iff p < d.$$

**Behavior near $r=\infty$.** For $r\ge 1$, $(1+r)^{-dp}\sim r^{-dp}$, so the in
tegrand is comparable to $r^{d-1-p-dp} = r^{d-1-p(d+1)}$. Thus
$$\int_1^\infty r^{d-1-p}(1+r)^{-dp}\,dr < \infty \iff d-1-p(d+1) < -1 \iff p >
\frac{d}{d+1}.$$

**Combining.** Both pieces are finite simultaneously iff
$$\frac{d}{d+1} < p < d.$$

Intersecting with the required range $p\in[1,\infty)$:

- Since $\frac{d}{d+1}<1$ for every $d\ge 1$, the lower constraint $\frac{d}{d+1
}<p$ is automatically satisfied once $p\ge 1$ (with $p=1$ allowed since $\frac{d
}{d+1}<1$).
- The upper constraint $p<d$ is strict: at $p=d$ the integral diverges logarithm
ically at the origin.

Hence, for $d\ge 2$ (so that $d>1$ and the interval $[1,d)$ is nonempty),
$$\boxed{\,f\in L^p(\mathbb{R}^d) \iff p\in[1,\,d)\,}.$$

For $d=1$, the condition $\frac{1}{2}<p<1$ is disjoint from $[1,\infty)$, so **n
o** $p\in[1,\infty)$ gives $f\in L^p(\mathbb{R})$.

**Summary.** For $d\ge 2$, $f\in L^p(\mathbb{R}^d)$ exactly when $1\le p < d$. T
he singularity at the origin forces $p<d$, and the decay at infinity (rate $|x|^
{-(d+1)}$) forces $p>\frac{d}{d+1}$, which is weaker than $p\ge 1$.
