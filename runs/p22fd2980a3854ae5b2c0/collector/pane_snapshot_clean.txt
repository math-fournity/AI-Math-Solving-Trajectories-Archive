### Setup and notation

Let $G=(X,A)$ be a strict digraph with integer weights $l:A\to\mathbb{N}^*$. Con
struct $H=(Y,B)$ by replacing each arc $a=(s,t)\in A$ with a directed path $s\to
 p_1^{(a)}\to p_2^{(a)}\to\cdots\to p_{l(a)-1}^{(a)}\to t$ of length $l(a)$, all
 internal vertices being pairwise distinct across different arcs. The **main ver
tices** of $H$ are the vertices of $G$; the internal vertices $p_j^{(a)}$ are no
n-main. By construction, for any two main vertices $u,v$:

$$d_H(u,v)=d_G(u,v).$$

The "section 6.2 algorithm" is BFS (breadth-first search) for computing distance
s in an unweighted digraph from a source $s_0$: vertices are dequeued in FIFO or
der, which is non-decreasing order of distance from $s_0$.

---

### Observation 1 — Order in which main vertices of $H$ are processed

BFS processes every vertex of $H$ in non-decreasing order of its distance from $
s_0$. Restricting attention to main vertices, they are processed in non-decreasi
ng order of $d_H(s_0,v)=d_G(s_0,v)$. This is **exactly the minimum-label order**
 of Dijkstra's algorithm: the main vertex settled next is the one whose (final)
distance from $s_0$ in $G$ is smallest among all not-yet-settled main vertices.

---

### Observation 2 — Internal vertices are trivial chains

Consider an arc $a=(s,t)\in A$ of weight $l(a)$. In $H$ it becomes a path $s\to
p_1\to\cdots\to p_{l(a)-1}\to t$. Because the paths are internally disjoint, eac
h internal vertex $p_j$ has **exactly one in-neighbor** ($p_{j-1}$, or $s$ if $j
=1$) and **exactly one out-neighbor** ($p_{j+1}$, or $t$ if $j=l(a)-1$) in $H$.

Therefore, when BFS reaches $p_j$ at distance $d(s)+j$, processing $p_j$ does on
ly one thing: it enqueues $p_{j+1}$ (or $t$) at distance $d(s)+j+1$. No branchin
g, no merging, no alternative paths pass through $p_j$. The entire chain $p_1,\d
ots,p_{l(a)-1}$ is a **pure relay**: it forwards the distance counter from $s$ t
o $t$, incrementing by $1$ at each step, over $l(a)$ steps total.

Hence the chain can be **compressed**: instead of creating $l(a)-1$ internal ver
tices and processing them one by one, we directly record that $t$ is reachable f
rom $s$ at cost $d(s)+l(a)$. The quantity $l(a)$ replaces the chain.

---

### Observation 3 — Extracting the algorithm on $G$

Run BFS on $H$ but **skip the explicit processing of internal vertices**, tracki
ng only the main vertices. At any moment, the main vertices split into:

- **Settled**: already dequeued by BFS; their final distances $d(\cdot)$ are kno
wn.
- **Unsettled**: not yet reached (or reached but not yet dequeued).

For an unsettled main vertex $t$, BFS will reach it at distance

$$\lambda(t)=\min_{\substack{(s,t)\in A\\ s\text{ settled}}} d(s)+l(s,t),$$

because the only way to reach $t$ in $H$ is via a path whose last arc originates
 from a settled main vertex $s$, and the internal chain contributes exactly $l(s
,t)$. The value $\lambda(t)$ is precisely the **Dijkstra label** of $t$.

The next main vertex BFS processes is the unsettled one with smallest $\lambda$
(Observation 1). When it is processed, new arcs from it become "activated," and
the labels of its out-neighbors are updated.

This yields the following algorithm on $G$:

1. **Initialize.** Set $d(s_0)=0$, mark $s_0$ settled. For every arc $(s_0,u)\in
 A$, set $\lambda(u)=l(s_0,u)$. All other labels are $+\infty$.
2. **Select.** Among all unsettled vertices, pick $v$ with minimum $\lambda(v)$.
 Set $d(v)=\lambda(v)$, mark $v$ settled.
3. **Update.** For every arc $(v,u)\in A$ with $u$ unsettled, set $\lambda(u)\le
ftarrow\min\bigl(\lambda(u),\,d(v)+l(v,u)\bigr)$.
4. **Repeat** steps 2–3 until all vertices are settled.

This is **Dijkstra's algorithm**.

---

### Why it is correct (rediscovery of the correctness argument)

BFS on an unweighted graph computes exact shortest distances: a vertex is first
reached along a shortest path, and once dequeued its distance is final. Applied
to $H$, this gives exact $d_G$-distances on main vertices. The compression of in
ternal chains does not lose any information, because:

- **Non-negative increments.** All weights $l(a)>0$, so distances along any path
 in $H$ strictly increase. Therefore a main vertex, once reached in BFS, cannot
be reached later at a smaller distance — its label is final when it becomes the
minimum. This is the same monotonicity that guarantees Dijkstra's correctness.
- **No shortcuts through internal vertices.** Internal vertices have degree 2 (o
ne in, one out) and belong to a single chain, so no path through them can provid
e a shortcut bypassing the arc weight. The only effect of the chain is to add ex
actly $l(a)$ to the distance.

Thus the minimum-label selection (step 2) faithfully mirrors the BFS processing
order, and the label update (step 3) faithfully mirrors the traversal of interna
l chains. Dijkstra's algorithm on $G$ is exactly BFS on $H$ with the trivial int
ernal chains compressed into arc weights.

---

### Conclusion

By applying the unweighted BFS distance algorithm to the expanded graph $H$ and
observing that (a) main vertices are processed in non-decreasing order of their
$G$-distance from $s_0$, and (b) the internal vertices form trivial non-branchin
g chains that merely relay the distance counter by the arc weight, we recover Di
jkstra's algorithm on $G$: maintain a label $\lambda(v)=\min_{s\text{ settled}}(
d(s)+l(s,v))$ for each unsettled vertex, repeatedly settle the minimum-label ver
tex, and update its out-neighbors' labels. The minimum-label rule is the direct
translation of BFS's level-order processing of main vertices in $H$.
