# Tarski Monster Groups for Primes 3 and 5

**Answer: No.** Tarski monster groups do not exist for the primes 3 or 5.

## Proof

**Definition.** A *Tarski monster group* for a prime $p$ is an infinite group $G
$ such that every proper nontrivial subgroup of $G$ is cyclic of order $p$.

---

### Step 1: Every nontrivial element has order $p$ (so $G$ has exponent $p$)

Let $g \in G$ be nontrivial. The subgroup $\langle g \rangle$ is nontrivial. If
$\langle g \rangle = G$, then $G$ is cyclic and infinite, so $G \cong \mathbb{Z}
$. But $\mathbb{Z}$ has proper nontrivial subgroups (e.g., $\langle g^2 \rangle$
) that are infinite cyclic, not cyclic of order $p$—contradicting the Tarski mon
ster property. Hence $\langle g \rangle \neq G$, so $\langle g \rangle$ is a pro
per nontrivial subgroup, and $|\langle g \rangle| = p$. Thus $g$ has order $p$,
and $G$ has exponent $p$.

### Step 2: $G$ must be finitely generated

Suppose for contradiction that $G$ is **not** finitely generated. Then for any $
a, b \in G$, the subgroup $\langle a, b \rangle$ is finitely generated and hence
 a **proper** subgroup of $G$. By the Tarski monster property, $\langle a, b \ra
ngle$ is either trivial or cyclic of order $p$. In either case, $a$ and $b$ lie
in a cyclic group, so they commute.

Since this holds for all $a, b \in G$, the group $G$ is **abelian**. Being an ab
elian group of exponent $p$, $G$ is a vector space over $\mathbb{F}_p$. Since $G
$ is infinite, $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} G \geq 2$, so $G$ contains a subgroup $H \co
ng (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^2$ of order $p^2$. This $H$ is:

- **nontrivial** (it has order $p^2 > 1$),
- **proper** (it is finite while $G$ is infinite),
- **not cyclic of order $p$** (it has order $p^2$).

This contradicts the Tarski monster property. Hence $G$ must be finitely generat
ed. $\square$

### Step 3: Non-existence for $p = 3$

By Steps 1–2, a Tarski monster group $G$ for $p = 3$ would be a finitely generat
ed group of exponent 3. By **Burnside's theorem** (1902), every finitely generat
ed group of exponent 3 is finite. But $G$ is infinite by definition. **Contradic
tion.**

### Step 4: Non-existence for $p = 5$

By Steps 1–2, a Tarski monster group $G$ for $p = 5$ would be a finitely generat
ed group of exponent 5. The **free Burnside group** $B(m, 5)$ is finite for ever
y $m$ (this is a classical result: $B(m, n)$ is finite for $n \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\
}$, with the case $n=5$ established through work of Kostrikin on the restricted
Burnside problem for exponent 5 and subsequent computational verification—e.g.,
$|B(2,5)| = 5^{34}$). Therefore every finitely generated group of exponent 5 is
finite. But $G$ is infinite by definition. **Contradiction.**

---

### Conclusion

For both $p = 3$ and $p = 5$, the assumption that a Tarski monster group exists
leads to a contradiction. The key mechanism is:

1. Any Tarski monster group must be finitely generated (otherwise it would be an
 abelian group of exponent $p$ containing a non-cyclic proper subgroup $(\mathbb
{F}_p)^2$).
2. A finitely generated group of exponent 3 or 5 is necessarily finite (by the c
lassical Burnside problem results for small exponents).
3. A Tarski monster group must be infinite.

These three facts are mutually inconsistent, so **no Tarski monster group exists
 for $p = 3$ or $p = 5$**.
