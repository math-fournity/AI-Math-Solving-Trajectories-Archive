## Proof

Let the projections $P', Q', R'$ on the $x$-axis be equidistant with $|P'Q'| = |
Q'R'| = s$. Writing the $x$-coordinates of $P, Q, R$ as $t,\; t+s,\; t+2s$ for s
ome real $t$, the three points on $y = x^2$ are

$$P = (t,\, t^2),\quad Q = (t+s,\,(t+s)^2),\quad R = (t+2s,\,(t+2s)^2).$$

The area of $\triangle PQR$ is

$$\mathcal{A} = \tfrac12\bigl|\det(\overrightarrow{PQ},\,\overrightarrow{PR})\bi
gr|.$$

Compute the two edge vectors:

$$\overrightarrow{PQ} = Q - P = \bigl(s,\; (t+s)^2 - t^2\bigr) = \bigl(s,\; 2ts
+ s^2\bigr),$$

$$\overrightarrow{PR} = R - P = \bigl(2s,\; (t+2s)^2 - t^2\bigr) = \bigl(2s,\; 4
ts + 4s^2\bigr).$$

The determinant is

$$\det = s\cdot(4ts + 4s^2) - (2s)\cdot(2ts + s^2) = 4ts^2 + 4s^3 - 4ts^2 - 2s^3
 = 2s^3.$$

Therefore

$$\mathcal{A} = \tfrac12\,|2s^3| = s^3.$$

The result is independent of $t$, so the area is the same for every position of
the equidistant triple along the parabola.

$$\boxed{\mathcal{A} = s^3}$$
