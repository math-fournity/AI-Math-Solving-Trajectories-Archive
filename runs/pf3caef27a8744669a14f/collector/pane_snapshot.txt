## Solution

### 1. Identification and Convergence

The series is the **Riemann zeta function at $s=3$**:

$$\zeta(3) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^3}.$$

**Convergence** follows from the integral test. Since $f(x) = 1/x^3$ is positive
, continuous, and decreasing on $[1,\infty)$,

$$\int_1^{\infty} \frac{dx}{x^3} = \left[-\frac{1}{2x^2}\right]_1^{\infty} = \fr
ac{1}{2} < \infty,$$

the series converges. More precisely, the $p$-series $\sum 1/n^p$ converges if a
nd only if $p > 1$; here $p = 3 > 1$.

### 2. No Elementary Closed Form Exists

Unlike the even-integer cases, where Euler's formula gives

$$\zeta(2k) = \frac{(-1)^{k+1}(2\pi)^{2k}\, B_{2k}}{2\,(2k)!},$$

yielding $\zeta(2) = \pi^2/6$, $\zeta(4) = \pi^4/90$, etc., **no closed-form exp
ression for $\zeta(3)$ in terms of elementary constants ($\pi$, $e$, logarithms,
 algebraic numbers) is known**. This is one of the most famous open problems in
mathematics.

What **is** known:

- **Apéry (1978)** proved that $\zeta(3)$ is **irrational**, via the rapidly con
verging series

$$\zeta(3) = \frac{5}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n^3 \binom{2n}{n}}
.$$

- It is **not known** whether $\zeta(3)$ is transcendental, or whether it is a r
ational multiple of $\pi^3$.

### 3. Integral Representation

From the general identity (valid for $\operatorname{Re}(s)>1$)

$$\zeta(s)\,\Gamma(s) = \int_0^{\infty} \frac{t^{s-1}}{e^t - 1}\,dt,$$

setting $s=3$ and using $\Gamma(3) = 2! = 2$:

$$\boxed{\zeta(3) = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\infty} \frac{t^2}{e^t - 1}\,dt.}$$

*Derivation of the general identity*: Expand $(e^t - 1)^{-1} = \sum_{n=1}^{\inft
y} e^{-nt}$ (geometric series, valid for $t > 0$), then

$$\int_0^{\infty} t^{s-1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} e^{-nt}\,dt = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\i
nt_0^{\infty} t^{s-1}e^{-nt}\,dt = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s)}{n^s} = \G
amma(s)\,\zeta(s),$$

where the interchange of sum and integral is justified by Tonelli's theorem (all
 terms positive).

### 4. Numerical Value with Rigorous Error Bound

Define the partial sum $S_N = \sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{1}{n^3}$ and the remainder $R
_N = \sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^3}$. By comparison with the integral,

$$\int_{N+1}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x^3} < R_N < \int_{N}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x^3},$$

i.e.,

$$\frac{1}{2(N+1)^2} < R_N < \frac{1}{2N^2}.$$

Computing $S_{10}$:

| $n$ | $1/n^3$ |
|-----|---------|
| 1 | 1.000000 |
| 2 | 0.125000 |
| 3 | 0.037037 |
| 4 | 0.015625 |
| 5 | 0.008000 |
| 6 | 0.004630 |
| 7 | 0.002915 |
| 8 | 0.001953 |
| 9 | 0.001372 |
| 10 | 0.001000 |

$$S_{10} = 1.197532\ldots, \qquad R_{10} < \frac{1}{200} = 0.005.$$

Using Apéry's rapidly converging series (each term shrinks by roughly $1/4$), on
e obtains the value to arbitrary precision:

$$\zeta(3) = 1.20205690315959428540\ldots$$

### 5. Conclusion

The series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 1/n^3$ equals **Apéry's constant** $\zeta(3)$, w
hich has no known closed form in terms of elementary constants. Its value is

$$\boxed{\zeta(3) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^3} = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\infty
}\frac{t^2}{e^t-1}\,dt = \frac{5}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n^3\bin
om{2n}{n}} \approx 1.2020569031595942854\ldots}$$

It was proven irrational by Roger Apéry in 1978.
