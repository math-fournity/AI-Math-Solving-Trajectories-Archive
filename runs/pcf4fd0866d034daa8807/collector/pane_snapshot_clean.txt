Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a non-DVR valuation ring $R
$ of $L(X,Y)$ must exclude $A = L(Y)[X]$.

**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

Suppose for contradiction that $R$ is a (proper) valuation ring of $K = L(X,Y)$
that is not a DVR, yet $A = L(Y)[X] \subseteq R$.

**Step 1: The valuation is trivial on $L(Y)$.**

Since $A \subseteq R$, in particular $L(Y) \subseteq R$. For any $a \in L(Y)^*$,
 both $a \in R$ and $a^{-1} \in L(Y) \subseteq R$, so $a$ is a unit in $R$. Thus
 the valuation $v$ associated to $R$ satisfies $v(a) = 0$ for all $a \in L(Y)^*$
, i.e., $v$ is trivial on $L(Y)$.

**Step 2: $K/L(Y)$ is a function field of one variable.**

$K = L(Y)(X)$ is a purely transcendental extension of $L(Y)$ of transcendence de
gree 1 (since $X$ is transcendental over $L(Y)$). Thus $K/L(Y)$ is a function fi
eld of one variable, with associated complete nonsingular curve $C = \mathbb{P}^
1_{L(Y)}$.

**Step 3: Every non-trivial valuation of a one-variable function field (trivial
on the base) is a DVR.**

We use the valuative criterion of properness. Since $C = \mathbb{P}^1_{L(Y)}$ is
 proper over $L(Y)$, and $R$ is a valuation ring of $K$ containing $L(Y)$, there
 exists a unique center $\xi \in C$ for $R$: a local homomorphism $\mathcal{O}_{
C,\xi} \to R$ with $\mathfrak{m}_R \cap \mathcal{O}_{C,\xi} = \mathfrak{m}_\xi$.

- **If $\xi$ is the generic point**, then $\mathcal{O}_{C,\xi} = K$, so $R = K$
and $v$ is the trivial valuation. But $R$ is a proper valuation ring ($R \neq K$
), so this case is excluded.

- **If $\xi$ is a closed point**, then $\mathcal{O}_{C,\xi}$ is a DVR (since $C$
 is a nonsingular curve). We claim $R = \mathcal{O}_{C,\xi}$. Indeed, suppose th
ere exists $x \in R \setminus \mathcal{O}_{C,\xi}$. Since $\mathcal{O}_{C,\xi}$
is a valuation ring, $x^{-1} \in \mathcal{O}_{C,\xi}$. Moreover $x^{-1} \in \mat
hfrak{m}_\xi$ (for if $x^{-1}$ were a unit, then $x = (x^{-1})^{-1} \in \mathcal
{O}_{C,\xi}$, contradiction). Since $R$ dominates $\mathcal{O}_{C,\xi}$, we have
 $\mathfrak{m}_\xi \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_R$, so $x^{-1} \in \mathfrak{m}_R$. Bu
t then $1 = x \cdot x^{-1} \in \mathfrak{m}_R$, a contradiction. Thus $R = \math
cal{O}_{C,\xi}$, which is a DVR.

**Step 4: Contradiction.**

Since $R$ is a proper valuation ring of $K$ (so $v$ is non-trivial), Step 3 impl
ies $R$ is a DVR. This contradicts the assumption that $R$ is not a DVR.

Therefore $A \not\subseteq R$. $\blacksquare$
