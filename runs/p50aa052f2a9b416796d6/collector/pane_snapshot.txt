## Answer

The number of different ways to (totally) order ℚ is **2^ℵ₀** (the cardinality o
f the continuum).

## Proof

We count the total (linear) order relations that can be placed on ℚ.

**Upper bound.** A total order on ℚ is a binary relation, hence a subset of ℚ ×
ℚ. Since ℚ is countable, ℚ × ℚ is countable, so the collection of all subsets of
 ℚ × ℚ has cardinality 2^ℵ₀. The set of total orders is a subcollection, so it h
as cardinality at most 2^ℵ₀.

**Lower bound.** We exhibit 2^ℵ₀ distinct total orders. Since ℚ is countably inf
inite, it is in bijection with ℕ × {0, 1}; it suffices to construct 2^ℵ₀ distinc
t total orders on ℕ × {0, 1}.

For each subset S ⊆ ℕ, define a relation <_S on ℕ × {0, 1} by:

- If m ≠ n: (m, i) <_S (n, j) ⟺ m < n (in the usual order on ℕ).
- If m = n: (n, 0) <_S (n, 1) ⟺ n ∈ S, and (n, 1) <_S (n, 0) ⟺ n ∉ S.

This is a total order: it is the lexicographic order on ℕ × {0,1} where the orde
r within each pair {(n,0), (n,1)} is determined by membership of n in S. Trichot
omy, transitivity, and totality are immediate from the construction (the first c
oordinate gives a well-order on ℕ, and within each fiber the two elements are co
mpared by the S-determined rule).

**Distinctness.** If S ≠ S′, pick n ∈ S △ S′. If n ∈ S \ S′, then under <_S we h
ave (n, 0) <_S (n, 1), while under <_{S′} we have (n, 1) <_{S′} (n, 0). Hence <_
S ≠ <_{S′}. The map S ↦ <_S is therefore injective.

Since there are 2^ℵ₀ subsets of ℕ, we obtain 2^ℵ₀ distinct total orders on ℕ × {
0, 1}, and transporting each through a fixed bijection ℕ × {0,1} → ℚ gives 2^ℵ₀
distinct total orders on ℚ.

**Conclusion.** The number of total orders on ℚ is bounded above and below by 2^
ℵ₀, so it equals 2^ℵ₀, the cardinality of the continuum.
