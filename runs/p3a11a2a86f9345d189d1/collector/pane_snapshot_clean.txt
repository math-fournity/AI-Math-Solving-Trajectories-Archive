## Proof

### 1. Two-sided estimate of $\|q_k\alpha\|$

Write the complete quotient $\alpha_{k+1}^*:=[a_{k+1};a_{k+2},\dots]$, so $a_{k+
1}<\alpha_{k+1}^*<a_{k+1}+1$. The standard identity

$$\alpha=\frac{p_k\,\alpha_{k+1}^*+p_{k-1}}{q_k\,\alpha_{k+1}^*+q_{k-1}}$$

gives, using $p_kq_{k-1}-p_{k-1}q_k=(-1)^{k-1}$,

$$\alpha-\frac{p_k}{q_k}=\frac{(-1)^k}{q_k\bigl(q_k\,\alpha_{k+1}^*+q_{k-1}\bigr
)}.$$

Multiplying by $q_k$ and taking absolute value,

$$\|q_k\alpha\|=\frac{1}{q_k\,\alpha_{k+1}^*+q_{k-1}}.$$

Since $q_k\,a_{k+1}+q_{k-1}=q_{k+1}$ and $a_{k+1}<\alpha_{k+1}^*<a_{k+1}+1$,

$$q_{k+1}<q_k\,\alpha_{k+1}^*+q_{k-1}<q_{k+1}+q_k,$$

hence

$$\frac{1}{q_{k+1}+q_k}<\|q_k\alpha\|<\frac{1}{q_{k+1}}.\tag{1}$$

## Consequences (the "decay rate" read off from (3)–(4))

The decay is **not** a power law in general; it is a staircase constant on each
interval $[q_k,q_{k+1})$ and dropping like $1/q_{k+1}$.

1. **Dirichlet bound (sharp form).** Since $q_{k+1}>n$, (3) gives $a_n<1/q_{k+1}
\le 1/(n+1)$. So $a_n\le 1/(n+1)$ for all $n$, recovering Dirichlet with the pre
cise location of the drop.

2. **Badly approximable $\alpha$ (bounded partial quotients).** If $a_j\le M$ fo
r all $j$, then $q_{k+1}\le(M+1)q_k\le(M+1)n$, and (3) gives $a_n\ge 1/((M+2)n)$
. Hence
   $$a_n=\Theta(1/n).$$
   Conversely, if partial quotients are unbounded, $\liminf_{n\to\infty} n\,a_n=
0$.

3. **Almost every $\alpha$ (Khinchin–Lévy).** For a.e. $\alpha$, $q_k^{1/k}\to e
^{\pi^2/(12\log 2)}$ (Lévy's constant $L$), so $q_{k+1}\approx q_k^{1+o(1)}$ on
the exponential scale and $a_n$ is roughly $n^{-1+o(1)}$ along the generic scale
, but with arbitrarily deep dips at the convergent denominators where $a_{q_k}\a
symp 1/q_{k+1}$ can be far smaller than $1/q_k$ whenever a large partial quotien
t $a_{k+1}$ occurs (which happens infinitely often for a.e. $\alpha$).

4. **Liouville numbers.** If $q_{k+1}$ grows faster than any power of $q_k$ (e.g
. $a_{k+1}$ super-exponential), then $a_n$ decays faster than any negative power
 of $n$ along the subsequence $n=q_k$.

5. **Quadratic irrationals.** Periodic continued fraction ⇒ bounded partial quot
ients ⇒ $a_n=\Theta(1/n)$, and in fact $a_n\,q_{k+1}$ oscillates in a compact su
binterval of $(1/2,1)$ determined by the periodic complete quotients.
