## Proof

**Setup.** Place $A$ at the origin with the line of centers along the $x$-axis.
Let the smaller circle have center $(r,0)$ and radius $r$, and the larger circle
 have center $(R,0)$ and radius $R$, with $R > r > 0$. Then:
$$\text{small circle: } x^2+y^2=2rx, \qquad \text{large circle: } x^2+y^2=2Rx,$$
both passing through $A=(0,0)$.

Let $C=(x_c,y_c)\neq A$ be on the small circle, so
$$x_c^2+y_c^2=2r\,x_c. \tag{1}$$

---

**Finding $P$.** The line $AC$ is $\{tC:t\in\mathbb{R}\}$. Substituting into the
 large circle equation:
$$t^2(x_c^2+y_c^2)=2Rt\,x_c \implies t=0\ (\text{point }A)\ \text{or}\ t=\frac{2
Rx_c}{x_c^2+y_c^2}\stackrel{(1)}{=}\frac{R}{r}.$$
Hence $P=\frac{R}{r}\,C$, the homothetic image of $C$ about $A$.

---

**The tangent at $C$ and points $D,E$.** The tangent to the small circle at $C$
is
$$(x_c-r)x+y_c\,y=r\,x_c. \tag{2}$$
Parametrize it as $(x,y)=(x_c-s\,y_c,\; y_c+s(x_c-r))$. Substituting into $x^2+y
^2=2Rx$ and simplifying using (1) and $y_c^2+(x_c-r)^2=r^2$ yields:
$$r^2 s^2+2(R-r)y_c\,s-2(R-r)x_c=0. \tag{3}$$
The two roots $s_D,s_E$ give $D$ and $E$.

---

**Key computation: $PE^2=PA\cdot PC$.** Set $k=\tfrac{R-r}{r}$. Then
$$P-E=\bigl(k\,x_c+s_E y_c,\; k\,y_c-s_E(x_c-r)\bigr),$$
so
$$PE^2=k^2(x_c^2+y_c^2)+2kr\,s_E y_c+r^2 s_E^2=2k^2 r\,x_c+2kr\,s_E y_c+r^2 s_E^
2. \tag{4}$$
From (3): $r^2 s_E^2=2(R-r)(x_c-y_c s_E)=2kr(x_c-y_c s_E)$. Substituting into (4
):
$$PE^2=2k^2 r\,x_c+2kr\,s_E y_c+2kr\,x_c-2kr\,y_c s_E=2kr\,x_c(k+1)=\frac{2R(R-r
)}{r}\,x_c. \tag{5}$$

On the other hand,
$$PA=\tfrac{R}{r}|C|,\quad PC=\tfrac{R-r}{r}|C|,\quad |C|^2=2r\,x_c,$$
so
$$PA\cdot PC=\frac{R(R-r)}{r^2}\cdot 2r\,x_c=\frac{2R(R-r)}{r}\,x_c. \tag{6}$$

Comparing (5) and (6):
$$\boxed{PE^2=PA\cdot PC.}$$

---

**Conclusion.** The circle through $A,C,E$ meets line $PAC$ at $A$ and $C$, so t
he power of $P$ with respect to this circle is $PA\cdot PC$. Since $E$ lies on t
his circle and $PE^2$ equals the power of $P$, the line $PE$ is tangent to the c
ircle $(A,C,E)$ at $E$. $\blacksquare$
