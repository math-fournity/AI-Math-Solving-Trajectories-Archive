## Solution

We evaluate
$$I=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{x\,\ln^{2}(\cos^{2}x)}{\sin^{2}x}\,dx.$$

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains only the integral to evalu
ate — no solutions, hints, or formalization notes. Proceeding.

---

### Step 1. Integration by parts

Use $\dfrac{1}{\sin^{2}x}=-\dfrac{d}{dx}\cot x$, i.e. set $dv=\dfrac{dx}{\sin^{2
}x}$, $v=-\cot x$, and $u=x\ln^{2}(\cos^{2}x)$. Since
$$\frac{d}{dx}\ln^{2}(\cos^{2}x)=2\ln(\cos^{2}x)\cdot\frac{-2\sin x}{\cos x}=-4\
tan x\,\ln(\cos^{2}x),$$
we get
$$du=\bigl[\ln^{2}(\cos^{2}x)-4x\tan x\,\ln(\cos^{2}x)\bigr]dx.$$

The boundary term $[-x\ln^{2}(\cos^{2}x)\cot x]_{0}^{\pi/2}$ vanishes: as $x\to(
\pi/2)^{-}$, $\cot x\sim(\pi/2-x)$ while $\ln^{2}(\cos^{2}x)\sim 4\ln^{2}(\pi/2-
x)$, so the product $\to 0$; as $x\to 0^{+}$, $\ln(\cos^{2}x)\to 0$. Hence
$$I=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cot x\,\ln^{2}(\cos^{2}x)\,dx-4\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\,\ln(\cos^
{2}x)\,dx = A-4B.$$

---

### Step 2. Evaluate $A=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cot x\,\ln^{2}(\cos^{2}x)\
,dx$

Since $\ln^{2}(\cos^{2}x)=4\ln^{2}(\cos x)$, write $A=4A'$ with $A'=\int_{0}^{\p
i/2}\cot x\,\ln^{2}(\cos x)\,dx$. Substitute $t=\cos x$, $dt=-\sin x\,dx$:
$$\cot x\,dx=\frac{\cos x}{\sin x}\cdot\frac{-dt}{\sin x}=\frac{-t\,dt}{1-t^{2}}
,\qquad x:0\to\pi/2\Rightarrow t:1\to 0.$$
Thus
$$A'=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t\,\ln^{2}t}{1-t^{2}}\,dt.$$
Expanding $\frac{1}{1-t^{2}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{2n}$ and using $\int_{0}^{1}t
^{a}\ln^{2}t\,dt=\frac{2}{(a+1)^{3}}$:
$$A'=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}t^{2n+1}\ln^{2}t\,dt=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\fra
c{2}{(2n+2)^{3}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4(n+1)^{3}}=\frac{1}{4}\zeta(3).$$
Therefore
$$A=4A'=\zeta(3).$$

---

### Step 3. Evaluate $B=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\,\ln(\cos^{2}x)\,dx=2P$,
where $P=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\ln(\cos x)\,dx$

Use the Fourier series (valid for $|x|<\pi/2$)
$$\ln\cos x=-\ln 2+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k}\cos(2kx).$$
Then
$$P=-\ln 2\cdot\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k}\int_{0}
^{\pi/2}x\cos(2kx)\,dx.$$
Compute
$$\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x\cos(2kx)\,dx=\Bigl[\frac{x\sin(2kx)}{2k}\Bigr]_{0}^{\pi/2}+\
Bigl[\frac{\cos(2kx)}{4k^{2}}\Bigr]_{0}^{\pi/2}=\frac{\cos(k\pi)-1}{4k^{2}}=\fra
c{(-1)^{k}-1}{4k^{2}}.$$
Hence
$$P=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}\ln 2+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}\bigl(
(-1)^{k}-1\bigr)}{k^{3}}.$$
The factor $(-1)^{k-1}((-1)^{k}-1)=-1-(-1)^{k-1}$ equals $0$ for even $k$ and $-
2$ for odd $k$, so
$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}((-1)^{k}-1)}{k^{3}}=-2\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\
frac{1}{(2m+1)^{3}}=-2\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{8}\Bigr)\zeta(3)=-\frac{7}{4}\zeta(3).$$
Therefore
$$P=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}\ln 2-\frac{7}{16}\zeta(3),\qquad B=2P=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\
ln 2-\frac{7}{8}\zeta(3).$$

---

### Step 4. Combine

$$I=A-4B=\zeta(3)-4\Bigl(-\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\ln 2-\frac{7}{8}\zeta(3)\Bigr)=\zeta
(3)+\pi^{2}\ln 2+\frac{7}{2}\zeta(3).$$

$$\boxed{\,I=\pi^{2}\ln 2+\frac{9}{2}\,\zeta(3)\,}$$
