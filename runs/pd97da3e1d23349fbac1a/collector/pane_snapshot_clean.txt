## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, the resulting object is a surface.

**Setup.** Let $S$ be a surface (a 2-manifold without boundary, though the argum
ent works even if $S$ already has boundary). Remove the interiors of two embedde
d closed discs $D_1, D_2 \subset S$, obtaining $M = S \setminus (\operatorname{i
nt} D_1 \cup \operatorname{int} D_2)$. This is a surface with two boundary circl
es $C_1 = \partial D_1$ and $C_2 = \partial D_2$. Now glue $C_1$ to $C_2$ via a
homeomorphism $\varphi: C_1 \to C_2$, forming the quotient space
$$X = M / (x \sim \varphi(x)).$$

We must show every point of $X$ has a neighborhood homeomorphic to an open disc
in $\mathbb{R}^2$.

**Case 1: Points away from the glued curve.** A point $[p] \in X$ coming from $p
 \in \operatorname{int} M$ (the interior of $M$, i.e., not on either boundary ci
rcle) has an open disc neighborhood in $M$ that does not meet either boundary. T
his neighborhood survives unchanged in the quotient $X$, so $[p]$ has an open di
sc neighborhood. ✓

**Case 2: Points on the glued curve.** Let $[p] \in X$ be the equivalence class
of $p_1 \in C_1$ (and $p_2 = \varphi(p_1) \in C_2$). Since $M$ is a surface with
 boundary, $p_1$ has a neighborhood $U_1$ in $M$ homeomorphic to a half-disc
$$\{(x,y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 : x^2 + y^2 < 1,\ y \geq 0\},$$
with $p_1$ corresponding to the origin and $C_1 \cap U_1$ corresponding to the d
iameter $\{(x,0) : |x| < 1\}$. Likewise $p_2$ has a half-disc neighborhood $U_2$
 with $C_2 \cap U_2$ corresponding to a diameter.

After gluing, the image of $U_1 \cup U_2$ in $X$ is the union of two half-discs
identified along their diameters (via $\varphi$). This union is homeomorphic to
a full open disc: concretely, the two half-discs $\{y \geq 0\}$ and $\{y \leq 0\
}$ glued along $\{y = 0\}$ give $\{x^2 + y^2 < 1\}$, an open disc in $\mathbb{R}
^2$. The specific homeomorphism $\varphi$ used to identify the diameters only af
fects *how* the two halves are matched, but any homeomorphism of an interval to
an interval still yields a space homeomorphic to a disc (one can construct an ex
plicit homeomorphism by adjusting coordinates). ✓

**Conclusion.** Every point of $X$ — whether in the interior or on the glued cur
ve — has a neighborhood homeomorphic to an open disc in $\mathbb{R}^2$. Therefor
e $X$ is a surface. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The gluing may change the global topology (e.g., gluing two boundary
 circles of a cylinder can yield either a torus or a Klein bottle, depending on
the orientation of $\varphi$), but in *both* cases the result is a surface. The
local structure is always a disc; only orientability and global topology are aff
ected by the choice of $\varphi$.
