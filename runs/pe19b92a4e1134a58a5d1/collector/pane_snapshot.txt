## Theorem

**Let $(M, g)$ be a closed Riemannian manifold with strictly negative sectional
curvature, and let $p \in M$. Then every element of the fundamental group $\pi_1
(M, p)$ is represented by a unique unit-speed geodesic loop based at $p$. (For t
he identity element, the unique representative is the constant loop of length $0
$.)**

This theorem exists and is a classical consequence of the **Cartan–Hadamard theo
rem**. Below is the complete proof.

## Proof

### Step 1: The universal cover is a Hadamard manifold

Let $\pi: \widetilde{M} \to M$ be the universal Riemannian covering, with the pu
llback metric. Since $M$ is closed, it is compact and complete; by the Hopf–Rino
w theorem, completeness lifts to $\widetilde{M}$, so $\widetilde{M}$ is complete
. The manifold $\widetilde{M}$ is simply connected by definition, and the sectio
nal curvature of $\widetilde{M}$ equals that of $M$, hence is strictly negative
(in particular, nonpositive).

By the **Cartan–Hadamard theorem**, a complete, simply connected Riemannian mani
fold with nonpositive sectional curvature is a **Hadamard manifold**. The key co
nsequence we need is:

> **(Hadamard property)** In a Hadamard manifold, any two points are joined by a
 **unique** geodesic segment (up to reparametrization), and $\exp_p$ is a diffeo
morphism for every $p$.

### Step 2: Identification of $\pi_1(M, p)$ with the deck group

Fix a lift $\tilde{p} \in \widetilde{M}$ with $\pi(\tilde{p}) = p$. The fundamen
tal group $\pi_1(M, p)$ acts on $\widetilde{M}$ by deck transformations, which a
re isometries of $\widetilde{M}$. The choice of $\tilde{p}$ fixes an isomorphism
$$\Phi: \pi_1(M, p) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \operatorname{Deck}(\widetilde{M}/M),
 \qquad \gamma \mapsto D_\gamma,$$
where $D_\gamma$ is the unique deck transformation sending $\tilde{p}$ to the en
dpoint of the lift of any loop representing $\gamma$ starting at $\tilde{p}$.

### Step 3: Construction of the geodesic loop

For each $\gamma \in \pi_1(M, p)$, consider the two points $\tilde{p}$ and $D_\g
amma(\tilde{p})$ in $\widetilde{M}$. By the Hadamard property, there is a **uniq
ue** unit-speed geodesic segment
$$\tilde{\sigma}_\gamma : [0, L_\gamma] \to \widetilde{M}, \qquad \tilde{\sigma}
_\gamma(0) = \tilde{p}, \quad \tilde{\sigma}_\gamma(L_\gamma) = D_\gamma(\tilde{
p}),$$
where $L_\gamma = d_{\widetilde{M}}(\tilde{p}, D_\gamma(\tilde{p}))$.

Define $\sigma_\gamma := \pi \circ \tilde{\sigma}_\gamma : [0, L_\gamma] \to M$.
 Since $\pi$ is a local isometry and $\tilde{\sigma}_\gamma$ is a geodesic, $\si
gma_\gamma$ is a geodesic in $M$ (on $(0, L_\gamma)$). Moreover:
$$\sigma_\gamma(0) = \pi(\tilde{p}) = p, \qquad \sigma_\gamma(L_\gamma) = \pi(D_
\gamma(\tilde{p})) = \pi(\tilde{p}) = p.$$
So $\sigma_\gamma$ is a **geodesic loop based at $p$**, parametrized by unit spe
ed.

**It represents $\gamma$**: The lift of $\sigma_\gamma$ starting at $\tilde{p}$
is $\tilde{\sigma}_\gamma$, which ends at $D_\gamma(\tilde{p})$. By the definiti
on of the isomorphism $\Phi$, a loop based at $p$ whose lift from $\tilde{p}$ en
ds at $D_\gamma(\tilde{p})$ represents the class $\gamma$. Hence $\sigma_\gamma$
 represents $\gamma$.

**Existence** is thus established for every $\gamma \in \pi_1(M, p)$.

### Step 4: Uniqueness

Suppose $\sigma: [0, L] \to M$ is any unit-speed geodesic loop based at $p$ repr
esenting $\gamma \in \pi_1(M, p)$. Lift $\sigma$ to $\widetilde{M}$ starting at
$\tilde{p}$; call the lift $\tilde{\sigma}: [0, L] \to \widetilde{M}$. Since $\p
i$ is a local isometry, $\tilde{\sigma}$ is a geodesic in $\widetilde{M}$ (on $(
0, L)$). Since $\sigma$ represents $\gamma$, the endpoint satisfies:
$$\tilde{\sigma}(L) = D_\gamma(\tilde{p}).$$
So $\tilde{\sigma}$ is a geodesic segment in $\widetilde{M}$ from $\tilde{p}$ to
 $D_\gamma(\tilde{p})$, parametrized by unit speed, hence of length $L = d_{\wid
etilde{M}}(\tilde{p}, D_\gamma(\tilde{p})) = L_\gamma$.

By the **Hadamard property** (uniqueness of geodesics between two points in a Ha
damard manifold), $\tilde{\sigma} = \tilde{\sigma}_\gamma$ on $[0, L_\gamma]$. T
herefore:
$$\sigma = \pi \circ \tilde{\sigma} = \pi \circ \tilde{\sigma}_\gamma = \sigma_\
gamma.$$
This proves uniqueness: the geodesic loop representing $\gamma$ is uniquely dete
rmined.

### Step 5: The identity element

For $\gamma = e$ (the identity), $D_e = \operatorname{Id}$, so $D_e(\tilde{p}) =
 \tilde{p}$. The unique geodesic from $\tilde{p}$ to itself is the constant geod
esic of length $0$. Its projection is the constant loop at $p$, which is the uni
que geodesic loop representing the identity. (It is a geodesic of zero length; t
he "unit speed" condition is vacuous for a zero-length curve, or one simply excl

### Step 6: Conclusion

For every non-trivial $\gamma \in \pi_1(M, p)$, there exists **exactly one** uni

geodesic in the Hadamard manifold $\widetilde{M}$ from $\tilde{p}$ to $D_\gamma(
\tilde{p})$. The identity element is represented uniquely by the constant loop.
