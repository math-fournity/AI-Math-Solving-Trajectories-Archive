## Proof

ts, a_{10}$ with $\max(a_i) = M$, $\sum a_i \geq 10$, and $\sum a_i^2 \geq 100$.

Equivalently, $M$ is the smallest value such that the maximum of $\sum a_i^2$ ov

$$\mathcal{F}_M = \{a \in \mathbb{R}^{10} : \tsum a_i \geq 10,\; a_i \le M \;\fo
rall i\}$$
is at least $100$.

**The feasible region is compact.** Each $a_i$ is bounded above by $M$ and bound
ed below: since the other nine terms sum to at most $9M$, we need $a_i \ge 10 -
9M$ to maintain $\sum a_i \ge 10$. So $\mathcal{F}_M$ is a bounded polyhedron (c
ompact and convex).

**Vertices of $\mathcal{F}_M$.** The region is defined by 11 inequality constrai
nts ($a_i \le M$ for $i=1,\dots,10$, and $\sum a_i \ge 10$). A vertex in $\mathb
b{R}^{10}$ requires 10 active constraints. There are two types:

- **Type A:** $\sum a_i = 10$ active and 9 of the $a_i \le M$ active. Then 9 val
ues equal $M$ and the remaining value is $10 - 9M$ (requires $10 - 9M \le M$, i.
e., $M \ge 1$). Sum of squares: $9M^2 + (10-9M)^2 = 90M^2 - 180M + 100$.

- **Type B:** All 10 constraints $a_i \le M$ active, so all $a_i = M$, with $\su
m a_i = 10M \ge 10$ (requires $M \ge 1$). Sum of squares: $10M^2$.

**Maximum is at a vertex.** Since $f(a) = \sum a_i^2$ is convex and $\mathcal{F}
_M$ is a compact convex polyhedron, the maximum of $f$ over $\mathcal{F}_M$ is a
ttained at a vertex. Thus:
$$\max_{\mathcal{F}_M} \sum a_i^2 = \max\!\big(10M^2,\; 90M^2 - 180M + 100\big).
$$

**Finding the threshold.** We need this maximum $\ge 100$.

- **Type B:** $10M^2 \ge 100 \implies M \ge \sqrt{10} \approx 3.16$.

- **Type A:** $90M^2 - 180M + 100 \ge 100 \implies 90M(M-2) \ge 0 \implies M \ge
 2$ (for $M > 0$).

The Type A condition gives the smaller threshold: $M = 2$.

**For $M < 2$ (with $M \ge 1$):** Both vertex values are strictly less than $100

- $10M^2 < 10 \cdot 4 = 40 < 100$.
- $90M^2 - 180M + 100 = 100 + 90M(M-2) < 100$ since $M < 2$.

Since the maximum over $\mathcal{F}_M$ is attained at a vertex and both vertex t

**For $M = 2$:** Take $a_1 = \cdots = a_9 = 2$, $a_{10} = 10 - 18 = -8$.
- $\sum a_i = 18 - 8 = 10 \ge 10$. ✓
- $\sum a_i^2 = 9 \cdot 4 + 64 = 36 + 64 = 100 \ge 100$. ✓
- $\max(a_i) = 2$. ✓

**Conclusion.** The smallest possible value is:

$$\boxed{M = 2}$$
