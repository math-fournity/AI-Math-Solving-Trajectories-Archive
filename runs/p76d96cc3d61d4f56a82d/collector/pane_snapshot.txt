Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the condition "$T_n(x_n) \t
o T(x)$ for every convergent $x_n \to x$" forces norm convergence $T_n \to T$.

**Answer: No.** The condition does not imply norm convergence.

## Counterexample

Let $X = \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$ with standard orthonormal basis $\{e_n\}$. Define
$$T = 0, \qquad T_n(x) = \langle x, e_n\rangle\, e_n \quad (x \in \ell^2).$$

Each $T_n$ is the rank-one projection onto $\operatorname{span}(e_n)$, with $\|T
_n\| = 1$.

**Norm convergence fails.** Since $\|T_n - T\| = \|T_n\| = 1$ for every $n$, the
 sequence $(T_n)$ does not converge to $T$ in norm.

**The given property holds.** Let $x_k \to x$ in $\ell^2$ (using $k$ for the seq
uence index to avoid confusion with the operator index). We must show $T_k(x_k)
\to 0 = T(x)$.

Writing $x_k = (x_k^{(1)}, x_k^{(2)}, \dots)$, we have
$$T_k(x_k) = x_k^{(k)}\, e_k, \qquad \|T_k(x_k)\|_{\ell^2} = |x_k^{(k)}|.$$

Decompose the $k$-th coordinate:
$$x_k^{(k)} = \bigl(x_k - x\bigr)^{(k)} + x^{(k)}.$$

- **First term:** $\bigl|\bigl(x_k - x\bigr)^{(k)}\bigr| \le \|x_k - x\|_{\ell^2
} \to 0$ as $k \to \infty$.
- **Second term:** $|x^{(k)}| \to 0$ as $k \to \infty$, since $x \in \ell^2$ imp
lies its coordinates tend to $0$.

Therefore $|x_k^{(k)}| \to 0$, hence $\|T_k(x_k)\| = |x_k^{(k)}| \to 0$, i.e., $
T_k(x_k) \to 0 = T(x)$.

## Why the condition is weaker than norm convergence

The key observation is that for any convergent sequence $x_n \to x$,
$$\|T_n(x_n) - T(x)\| \le \underbrace{\|(T_n - T)x\|}_{\to 0 \text{ (strong conv
ergence)}} + \underbrace{\|T_n\|\cdot\|x_n - x\|}_{\to 0 \text{ (uniform bounded
ness } \times \text{ convergence)}}.$$

The condition forces strong convergence $T_n \to T$ (take $x_n \equiv x$), which
 by the Uniform Boundedness Principle gives $\sup_n \|T_n\| < \infty$. But the "
bad directions" along which $\|T_n - T\|$ stays large (here, $e_n$) are precisel
y the directions that any *fixed* convergent sequence can only sample with vanis
hing weight — the coordinate $x_k^{(k)}$ is squeezed both by $\|x_k - x\| \to 0$
 and by $x^{(k)} \to 0$. Thus the condition is strictly intermediate between str
ong and norm operator convergence.
