Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the positive Weyl chamber $
A$ (defined by coroots) is contained in the set $B$ (defined by coweights).

## Proof

Let $\Delta = \{\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n\}$ be the simple roots, $\Delta^\vee =
 \{\alpha_1^\vee, \dots, \alpha_n^\vee\}$ the coroots, and $\hat{\Delta}^\vee =
\{\varpi_1^\vee, \dots, \varpi_n^\vee\}$ the coweights (dual basis to $\Delta$,
so $\langle \alpha_i, \varpi_j^\vee \rangle = \delta_{ij}$).

**Step 1: Coordinate representation.** Since $\Delta$ is a basis of $\mathfrak{a
}_0^*$, any $v \in \mathfrak{a}_0^*$ decomposes as $v = \sum_{i=1}^n v_i \,\alph
a_i$ where $v_i = \langle v, \varpi_i^\vee \rangle$. Thus:
$$B = \{v \in \mathfrak{a}_0^* : v_i > 0 \text{ for all } i\}.$$

**Step 2: Express the coroot conditions via the Cartan matrix.** Each coroot $\a
lpha_j^\vee$ expands in the coweight basis as $\alpha_j^\vee = \sum_i \langle \a
lpha_i, \alpha_j^\vee\rangle\, \varpi_i^\vee = \sum_i C_{ij}\,\varpi_i^\vee$, wh
ere $C = (C_{ij})$ is the **Cartan matrix**. Therefore:
$$\langle v, \alpha_j^\vee \rangle = \sum_{i=1}^n C_{ij}\, v_i.$$
So $A = \{v : Cv > 0\}$ (componentwise), and $B = \{v : v > 0\}$.

**Step 3: Key property — $C^{-1} \geq 0$.** The Cartan matrix of a finite root s
ystem is a **nonsingular M-matrix**: it has diagonal entries $C_{ii}=2 > 0$, off
-diagonal entries $C_{ij} \leq 0$, and is positive definite (since the root syst
em is finite). A classical result on M-matrices states that a nonsingular M-matr
ix has an **entrywise non-negative inverse**: $C^{-1} \geq 0$.

*(Brief justification: $C = 2I - B$ with $B \geq 0$ and $\rho(B) < 2$, so $C^{-1
} = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(B/2)^k \geq 0$.)*

**Step 4: $A \subseteq B$.** Let $v \in A$, so $w := Cv > 0$ (all components pos
itive). Then:
$$v = C^{-1} w.$$
Since $C^{-1} \geq 0$ and $w > 0$, each component satisfies:
$$v_i = \sum_j (C^{-1})_{ij}\, w_j.$$
Every term is $\geq 0$, and at least one term is $> 0$: indeed, since $C^{-1}$ i
s invertible, no row of $C^{-1}$ is identically zero, so there exists $j$ with $
(C^{-1})_{ij} > 0$, giving $v_i \geq (C^{-1})_{ij}\, w_j > 0$.

Therefore $v_i > 0$ for all $i$, i.e., $v \in B$.

**Conclusion:** $A \subseteq B$. $\blacksquare$
