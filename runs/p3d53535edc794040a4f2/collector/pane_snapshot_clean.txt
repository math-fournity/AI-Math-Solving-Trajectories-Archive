## Continuous Maps Between Topological Manifolds

**Setup.** Let $M$ be an $m$-dimensional topological manifold and $N$ an $n$-dim
ensional topological manifold. A *chart* on $M$ is a pair $(U, \varphi)$ where $
U \subseteq M$ is open and $\varphi: U \to \varphi(U) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$ is
 a homeomorphism onto an open subset $\varphi(U)$ of $\mathbb{R}^m$. Charts on $
N$ are defined analogously. An *atlas* is a collection of charts whose domains c
over the manifold.

**Definition (continuous map).** A map $f: M \to N$ is *continuous* if for every
 point $p \in M$ and every chart $(V, \psi)$ on $N$ with $f(p) \in V$, there exi
sts a chart $(U, \varphi)$ on $M$ with $p \in U$ and $f(U) \subseteq V$, such th
at the **coordinate representation**

$$\psi \circ f \circ \varphi^{-1}: \varphi(U) \longrightarrow \psi(V)$$

is continuous as a map between open subsets of Euclidean space.

**Why local continuity gives global continuity.** We must show this definition i
s equivalent to $f$ being continuous in the ordinary topological sense (preimage
s of open sets are open).

- ($\Rightarrow$) Suppose every coordinate representation $\psi \circ f \circ \v
arphi^{-1}$ is continuous. Let $W \subseteq N$ be open; we show $f^{-1}(W)$ is o
pen in $M$. Take $p \in f^{-1}(W)$, so $f(p) \in W$. Choose a chart $(V, \psi)$
on $N$ with $f(p) \in V \subseteq W$ (possible because chart domains form a basi
s for the topology, or at least cover $W$ by intersecting with $W$). By hypothes
is there is a chart $(U, \varphi)$ on $M$ with $p \in U$, $f(U) \subseteq V$, an
d $\psi \circ f \circ \varphi^{-1}$ continuous. Then
$$\varphi\bigl(U \cap f^{-1}(W)\bigr) = \varphi\bigl(U \cap f^{-1}(V \cap W)\big
r) = (\psi \circ f \circ \varphi^{-1})^{-1}(\psi(V \cap W)),$$
which is open in $\varphi(U)$ because $\psi(V \cap W)$ is open in $\psi(V)$ and
$\psi \circ f \circ \varphi^{-1}$ is continuous. Since $\varphi$ is a homeomorph
ism, $U \cap f^{-1}(W)$ is open in $U$, hence open in $M$. Every point $p \in f^
{-1}(W)$ has an open neighborhood contained in $f^{-1}(W)$, so $f^{-1}(W)$ is op
en.

- ($\Leftarrow$) If $f$ is topologically continuous, then for any charts $(U, \v
arphi)$, $(V, \psi)$ with $f(U) \subseteq V$, the map $\psi \circ f \circ \varph
i^{-1} = \psi \circ f|_{U} \circ \varphi^{-1}$ is a composition of three continu
ous maps: $\varphi^{-1}$ (homeomorphism, continuous), $f|_U$ (restriction of a c
ontinuous map), and $\psi$ (homeomorphism, continuous). Hence every coordinate r
epresentation is continuous.

The key point: **charts are homeomorphisms**, so they preserve open sets exactly
. Continuity is a purely topological (open-set) property, and homeomorphisms tra
nsport it faithfully between the manifold and Euclidean space. The manifold topo
logy is *defined* to be the unique topology making all charts homeomorphisms, so

## Smooth Maps Between Smooth Manifolds

**Setup.** Let $M, N$ be *smooth* manifolds of dimensions $m, n$. This means eac
h is equipped with a *maximal smooth atlas*: an atlas where any two charts $(U,
\varphi)$, $(U', \varphi')$ have $C^\infty$-compatible **transition maps**
$$\varphi' \circ \varphi^{-1}: \varphi(U \cap U') \longrightarrow \varphi'(U \ca
p U'),$$
i.e., these are smooth (infinitely differentiable) maps between open subsets of
Euclidean space, and the atlas is maximal with this property.

**Definition (smooth map).** A map $f: M \to N$ is *smooth* (of class $C^\infty$
) if:

1. $f$ is continuous, and
2. for every $p \in M$ there exist a chart $(U, \varphi)$ on $M$ with $p \in U$
and a chart $(V, \psi)$ on $N$ with $f(U) \subseteq V$, such that the coordinate
 representation
$$\hat{f} := \psi \circ f \circ \varphi^{-1}: \varphi(U) \longrightarrow \psi(V)
 \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$$
is smooth ($C^\infty$) as a map between open subsets of Euclidean space.

**Well-definedness — independence of chart choice.** This is the crucial point t
hat makes the definition coherent. Suppose $(U_1, \varphi_1)$ and $(U_2, \varphi
_2)$ are two charts on $M$ and $(V_1, \psi_1)$, $(V_2, \psi_2)$ two charts on $N
$, both satisfying $f(U_i) \subseteq V_i$. On the overlap, the two coordinate re
presentations are related by
$$\hat{f}_2 = \psi_2 \circ f \circ \varphi_2^{-1} = (\psi_2 \circ \psi_1^{-1}) \
circ (\psi_1 \circ f \circ \varphi_1^{-1}) \circ (\varphi_1 \circ \varphi_2^{-1}
).$$
The outer two maps are **transition maps** of the smooth atlases, hence $C^\inft
y$ by the definition of a smooth manifold. The middle map $\hat{f}_1$ is $C^\inf
ty$ by assumption. A composition of $C^\infty$ maps is $C^\infty$ (the chain rul
e), so $\hat{f}_2$ is $C^\infty$. Thus smoothness of the coordinate representati
on is **independent of which charts are used** — it is a property of $f$ itself,
 not of the coordinate description.

**Why local smoothness gives a global smooth map.** The argument has three layer
s:

1. **Local-to-global via covering.** Smoothness is defined pointwise: at each $p
$ we require *some* chart pair to give a smooth coordinate representation. The d
omains of these charts form an open cover $\{U_\alpha\}$ of $M$. On each $U_\alp
ha$ the map is smooth in coordinates. Because smoothness is a *local* property (
a function is $C^\infty$ on an open set iff it is $C^\infty$ in a neighborhood o
f every point), and the chart domains cover $M$, the map is "smooth everywhere"
in a globally consistent sense.

2. **Consistency across overlaps via transition maps.** The potential obstructio
n — that two different coordinate descriptions might disagree on what "smooth" m
eans — is removed by the compatibility axiom of the smooth atlas. On any overlap
 $U_\alpha \cap U_\beta$, the two coordinate representations differ by pre- and
post-composition with smooth transition maps (as shown above). Since transition
maps are $C^\infty$ by the manifold's defining structure, smoothness in one char
t automatically implies smoothness in every overlapping chart. **The atlas compa
tibility condition is exactly the glue that makes local smoothness patch togethe
r into a global notion.**

3. **Equivalence with a global atlas check.** In fact, one can show: $f$ is smoo
th in the sense above if and only if for *every* pair of charts $(U, \varphi)$,
$(V, \psi)$ from the maximal atlases with $f(U) \subseteq V$, the coordinate rep
resentation $\psi \circ f \circ \varphi^{-1}$ is $C^\infty$. The forward directi
on uses the transition-map argument above (any chart in the maximal atlas is com
patible with the one used in the definition). The reverse direction is trivial.
This shows the definition does not depend on a *choice* of atlas — any smooth at
las generating the same smooth structure gives the same class of smooth maps.

**Summary of the mechanism.** For both continuity and smoothness, the principle
is the same:

- **Charts are homeomorphisms** (topological case) or **smooth-compatible homeom
orphisms** (smooth case), so they faithfully transport the relevant structure (o
pen sets / $C^\infty$ structure) between the manifold and Euclidean space.
- The relevant property (continuity / smoothness) is **local**: it holds globall
y iff it holds in a neighborhood of every point.
- Chart domains **cover** the manifold, so checking the property in charts cover
s every point.
- In the smooth case, the **transition-map compatibility axiom** ensures that th
e local checks in different charts are mutually consistent, so the locally-verif
ied property patches into a single well-defined global property of $f$.

This is why "continuous / smooth in local coordinates" is not merely *equivalent
* to the global property — it is the *definition* of the global property, made c
oherent by the chart compatibility structure of the manifold.
