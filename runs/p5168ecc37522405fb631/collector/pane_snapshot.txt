# Evaluation of $I = \int_0^1 \log \Gamma(x) \cos(2\pi n x)\, dx$

## Setup: Fourier decomposition via the reflection formula

We use the **Euler reflection formula**
$$\Gamma(x)\,\Gamma(1-x) = \frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi x)}, \qquad 0 < x < 1,$$
which, upon taking logarithms, gives
$$\log\Gamma(x) + \log\Gamma(1-x) = \log\pi - \log\sin(\pi x). \tag{1}$$

The classical Fourier series (valid for $0 < x < 1$)
$$\log\sin(\pi x) = -\log 2 - \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{\cos(2\pi k x)}{k} \tag{
2}$$
substituted into (1) yields
$$\log\Gamma(x) + \log\Gamma(1-x) = \log(2\pi) + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{\cos(
2\pi k x)}{k}. \tag{3}$$

## Even and odd parts about $x = \tfrac{1}{2}$

Decompose $\log\Gamma(x) = f(x) + g(x)$ where
$$f(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\log\Gamma(x) + \log\Gamma(1-x)\bigr], \qquad g(x) =
\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\log\Gamma(x) - \log\Gamma(1-x)\bigr].$$

By construction $f(1-x) = f(x)$ (even about $\tfrac12$) and $g(1-x) = -g(x)$ (od
d about $\tfrac12$).

From (3) the even part is explicitly
$$f(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}\log(2\pi) + \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{\cos(2\p
i k x)}{k}. \tag{4}$$

## The odd part contributes no cosine coefficients

Using $\cos(2\pi n(1-x)) = \cos(2\pi n x)$ and $\sin(2\pi n(1-x)) = -\sin(2\pi n
 x)$, the condition $g(1-x) = -g(x)$ forces every cosine coefficient of $g$ to v
anish. Hence **all cosine Fourier coefficients of $\log\Gamma(x)$ come from $f(x
)$ alone**.

## Reading off the coefficient

Write the Fourier series on $[0,1]$ in the standard convention
$$\log\Gamma(x) = a_0 + \sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\bigl[a_m\cos(2\pi m x) + b_m\sin(2\p
i m x)\bigr],$$
with $a_m = 2\int_0^1 \log\Gamma(x)\cos(2\pi m x)\,dx$.

Comparing (4) with this convention, the cosine coefficient at frequency $m = n$
is
$$a_n = \frac{1}{2n}.$$

Therefore
$$I = \int_0^1 \log\Gamma(x)\cos(2\pi n x)\,dx = \frac{a_n}{2} = \frac{1}{2}\cdo
t\frac{1}{2n} = \boxed{\dfrac{1}{4n}}.$$

## Verification (consistency checks)

1. **$n = 0$ limit.** The formula is for $n \geq 1$; the separate classical resu
lt $\int_0^1 \log\Gamma(x)\,dx = \tfrac12\log(2\pi)$ agrees with $a_0 = \tfrac12
\log(2\pi)$ from (4). ✓

2. **Sign.** Near $x = 0^+$, $\log\Gamma(x) \to +\infty$ while $\cos(2\pi n x) \
to 1$, so the integral should be positive; $\frac{1}{4n} > 0$. ✓

3. **Reflection consistency.** Adding the cosine coefficients of $\log\Gamma(x)$
 and $\log\Gamma(1-x)$: since $\log\Gamma(1-x)$ has the same cosine coefficients
 (cosine is even about $\tfrac12$), the sum gives $2a_n = \frac{1}{n}$, matching
 the coefficient $\frac{1}{n}$ in (3). ✓

The integral evaluates to $\dfrac{1}{4n}$ for every positive integer $n$.
