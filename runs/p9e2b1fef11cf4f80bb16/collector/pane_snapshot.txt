Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the subgroup generated by t
wo "bicyclic" permutations (products of two disjoint cycles partitioning $\{1,\l
dots,n\}$) is always $S_n$ under the given conditions.

**Answer: No.** The subgroup is not necessarily $S_n$. I provide a counterexampl
e.

## Counterexample: $n = 5$, $t = k = 1$

Let $A = \{1\}$, $B = \{2,3,4,5\}$, $C = \{5\}$, $D = \{1,2,3,4\}$.

The two permutations are:
$$\sigma = (1)(2,3,5,4) = (2,3,5,4), \qquad \tau = (5)(1,2,4,3) = (1,2,4,3).$$

**Verification of conditions:**
- $A \cup B = \{1,2,3,4,5\}$, $A \cap B = \emptyset$ ✓
- $C \cup D = \{1,2,3,4,5\}$, $C \cap D = \emptyset$ ✓
- $A = \{1\} \neq C = \{5\}$ ✓
- $A = \{1\} \neq D = \{1,2,3,4\}$ ✓ (different cardinalities)

## Proof that $\langle \sigma, \tau \rangle \neq S_5$

Identify $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$ with $\mathbb{F}_5 = \{0,1,2,3,4\}$ via $i \mapsto i-1$
. Under this identification:

- $\sigma: x \mapsto 2x \pmod{5}$, fixing $0$ (i.e., element $1$).
  *Check:* $1\mapsto 2,\; 2\mapsto 4,\; 4\mapsto 3,\; 3\mapsto 1,\; 0\mapsto 0$,
 giving the cycle $(1,2,4,3)$ in $\mathbb{F}_5$, which is $(2,3,5,4)$ in origina
l labeling. ✓

- $\tau: x \mapsto 2x+1 \pmod{5}$, fixing $4$ (i.e., element $5$).
  *Check:* $0\mapsto 1,\; 1\mapsto 3,\; 3\mapsto 2,\; 2\mapsto 0,\; 4\mapsto 4$,
 giving the cycle $(0,1,3,2)$ in $\mathbb{F}_5$, which is $(1,2,4,3)$ in origina
l labeling. ✓

Both $\sigma$ and $\tau$ are elements of the **affine group**:
$$\operatorname{AGL}(1,5) = \{x \mapsto ax + b \pmod{5} : a \in \mathbb{F}_5^*,\
; b \in \mathbb{F}_5\},$$
which has order $4 \times 5 = 20$.

Since $\operatorname{AGL}(1,5)$ is a subgroup of $S_5$, we have $\langle \sigma,
 \tau \rangle \subseteq \operatorname{AGL}(1,5)$.

**The generated group equals all of $\operatorname{AGL}(1,5)$:** Compute
$$\sigma^{-1}\tau: x \mapsto 2^{-1}(2x+1) = x + 3 \pmod{5}.$$
This is a translation by $3$. Since $\gcd(3,5)=1$, the translation $x \mapsto x+
3$ generates the entire translation subgroup $\{x \mapsto x+b : b \in \mathbb{F}
_5\}$ of order $5$. Together with $\sigma: x \mapsto 2x$ (which has order $4$ an
d acts nontrivially on the translations by conjugation), the group $\langle \sig
ma, \tau \rangle$ is the semidirect product $\mathbb{F}_5 \rtimes \langle 2 \ran
gle$ of order $5 \times 4 = 20$.

Therefore:
$$\langle \sigma, \tau \rangle = \operatorname{AGL}(1,5), \qquad |\operatorname{
AGL}(1,5)| = 20 < 120 = |S_5|.$$

## Conclusion

The subgroup generated by $(a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_t)(b_1, b_2, \ldots, b_{n-t})$ a
nd $(c_1, c_2, \ldots, c_k)(d_1, d_2, \ldots, d_{n-k})$ is **not necessarily** $
S_n$. The counterexample above ($n=5$, $t=k=1$, with the two permutations being
4-cycles lying in the affine group $\operatorname{AGL}(1,5)$) satisfies all the
hypotheses ($n$ odd, $1 \leq t,k < n$, $A \neq C$, $A \neq D$) yet generates a p
roper subgroup of $S_5$ of order 20.
