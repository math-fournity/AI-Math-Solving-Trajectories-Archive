## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** The Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem (DCT) can be used to
 conclude that $\int_{\mathbb{R}} f_{m,n}(x)\,dx \to 0$ as $m,n \to +\infty$.

The key observation is that the standard DCT is stated for a single-index sequen
ce, whereas here we have a double-index family. We reduce the double-index situa
tion to the single-index one by a subsequence argument.

---

### Setup and interpretation

The hypothesis "$f_{m,n}(x) \to 0$ as $m,n \to +\infty$ for a.e. $x$" is the joi
nt (Pringsheim) limit: for a.e. $x$, for every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exist $M
, N \in \mathbb{N}$ such that for all $m \ge M$ and $n \ge N$,
$$|f_{m,n}(x)| < \varepsilon.$$

We are also given $|f_{m,n}(x)| \le g(x)$ for all $m, n$, with $g \in L^1(\mathb
b{R})$.

### Reduction to a single index

**Claim.** For any sequence of pairs $(m_k, n_k)_{k \ge 1}$ with $m_k \to +\inft
y$ and $n_k \to +\infty$, the single-index sequence $h_k := f_{m_k, n_k}$ satisf
ies $h_k(x) \to 0$ for a.e. $x$.

*Proof of claim.* Fix an $x$ at which the joint convergence holds and fix $\vare
psilon > 0$. Choose $M, N$ as above. Since $m_k \to \infty$ and $n_k \to \infty$
, there exists $K$ such that for all $k \ge K$, $m_k \ge M$ and $n_k \ge N$. Hen
ce $|h_k(x)| = |f_{m_k,n_k}(x)| < \varepsilon$ for all $k \ge K$. ∎

Moreover, $|h_k(x)| \le g(x)$ for all $k$, with $g \in L^1$.

### Applying DCT along an arbitrary subsequence

Let $(m_k, n_k)$ be any sequence of pairs with $m_k, n_k \to +\infty$, and set $
h_k = f_{m_k, n_k}$. By the claim, $h_k \to 0$ a.e., and $|h_k| \le g \in L^1$.
By the standard (single-index) Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem,
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} h_k(x)\,dx \;\longrightarrow\; 0 \quad\text{as } k \to \inft
y.$$

### Concluding the joint limit

We now show $\int_{\mathbb{R}} f_{m,n}(x)\,dx \to 0$ as $m, n \to +\infty$. Supp
ose, for contradiction, that this fails. Then there exists $\varepsilon_0 > 0$ a
nd a sequence of pairs $(m_k, n_k)$ with $m_k \to \infty$, $n_k \to \infty$ such
 that
$$\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}} f_{m_k, n_k}(x)\,dx\right| \ge \varepsilon_0 \quad\tex
t{for all } k.$$

But applying DCT to $h_k = f_{m_k, n_k}$ (as above) yields $\int_{\mathbb{R}} h_
k \to 0$, contradicting the inequality $|\int h_k| \ge \varepsilon_0 > 0$.

Therefore no such $\varepsilon_0$ exists, and we conclude
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} f_{m,n}(x)\,dx \;\longrightarrow\; 0 \quad\text{as } m, n \t
o +\infty.$$

### Justification summary

The DCT applies because:
1. The joint convergence $f_{m,n} \to 0$ a.e. implies convergence along every su
bsequence $(m_k, n_k)$ with both indices diverging (the reduction step).
2. The uniform dominating bound $|f_{m,n}| \le g \in L^1$ is preserved under thi
s reduction.
3. The standard single-index DCT then gives convergence of the integrals along e
very such subsequence, which is equivalent to the joint convergence of $\int f_{
m,n} \to 0$.
