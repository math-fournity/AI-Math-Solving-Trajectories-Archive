The statement is **false**. Below is a counterexample.

**Interpretation.** The exponent $1/p - 1 = (1-p)/p$ is negative for $p > 1$, so
 $u_n^{1/p-1} = u_n^{(1-p)/p}$ requires $u_n > 0$ a.e. We show the claimed impli
cation fails already for $p = 2$.

---

**Counterexample ($p = 2$, $\Omega = [0,1]$ with Lebesgue measure).**

For $p = 2$ the exponent is $1/2 - 1 = -1/2$, so $u_n^{1/p-1} = u_n^{-1/2}$ and
$u^{1/p-1} = u^{-1/2}$. Note
$$\|u_n^{-1/2} - u^{-1/2}\|_{L^2}^2 = \int_0^1 \bigl|u_n^{-1} - u^{-1}\bigr|\,dx
.$$

Define
$$u(x) = 1, \qquad u_n(x) = \begin{cases} 1/n, & x \in [0,\, 1/n],\\ 1, & x \in
(1/n,\, 1]. \end{cases}$$

**Step 1: $u_n \to u$ in $L^2$.**
$$\|u_n - u\|_{L^2}^2 = \int_0^{1/n}\!\left|\tfrac{1}{n}-1\right|^2 dx = \frac{1
}{n}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^2 \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{} 0.$$

**Step 2: $u_n^{-1/2},\, u^{-1/2} \in L^2$ for every $n$.**
$$\|u_n^{-1/2}\|_{L^2}^2 = \int_0^{1/n} n\,dx + \int_{1/n}^{1} 1\,dx = 1 + \left
(1 - \tfrac{1}{n}\right) = 2 - \tfrac{1}{n} < \infty,$$
$$\|u^{-1/2}\|_{L^2}^2 = \int_0^1 1\,dx = 1 < \infty.$$

**Step 3: $u_n^{-1/2} \not\to u^{-1/2}$ in $L^2$.**
$$\|u_n^{-1/2} - u^{-1/2}\|_{L^2}^2 = \int_0^{1/n}\!\left|\sqrt{n} - 1\right|^2
dx = \frac{1}{n}\bigl(\sqrt{n}-1\bigr)^2 = 1 - \frac{2}{\sqrt{n}} + \frac{1}{n}
\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{} 1 \neq 0.$$

Thus $u_n^{-1/2}$ does **not** converge to $u^{-1/2}$ in $L^2$.

---

**Why it fails.** The map $t \mapsto t^{\,1/p-1}$ (with negative exponent for $p
>1$) is not uniformly continuous on $(0,\infty)$; it blows up near $t = 0$. Conv
ergence in $L^p$ allows $u_n$ to get arbitrarily close to $0$ on sets of small b
ut non-negligible measure, and the negative power amplifies these excisions enou
gh to break $L^p$-convergence of the transformed sequence. The hypotheses $u_n^{
1/p-1}, u^{1/p-1} \in L^p$ only guarantee integrability of each term separately,
 with no uniform control relating the two.

**Conclusion.** The statement is **false**: there exist $p$ (e.g. $p=2$) and a m
easure space $\Omega$ (e.g. $[0,1]$) for which $u_n \to u$ in $L^p$ and all $u_n
^{1/p-1}, u^{1/p-1} \in L^p$, yet $u_n^{1/p-1} \not\to u^{1/p-1}$ in $L^p$.
