## Proof

We compute
$$I = \int_0^1 \frac{\log x\,\log(1-x)}{x}\,dx.$$

**Step 1: Series expansion.** For $x \in (0,1)$, the Mercator series gives
$$\log(1-x) = -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n},$$
so that
$$\frac{\log(1-x)}{x} = -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^{n-1}}{n}.$$

**Step 2: Justify termwise integration.** On $(0,1)$ we have $\log x < 0$ and $\
log(1-x) < 0$, hence the integrand $\frac{\log x\,\log(1-x)}{x} \ge 0$. Moreover
 each term
$$-\frac{x^{n-1}}{n}\log x \ge 0$$
on $(0,1)$. The partial sums $\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(-\frac{x^{n-1}}{n}\log x\right
)$ are non-negative and increase pointwise to $\frac{\log x\,\log(1-x)}{x}$. By
the Monotone Convergence Theorem (equivalently Tonelli's theorem for non-negativ
e functions), we may interchange sum and integral:
$$I = -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n}\int_0^1 x^{n-1}\log x\,dx.$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the inner integral.** For $a > 0$,
$$\int_0^1 x^{a-1}\,dx = \frac{1}{a}.$$
Differentiating both sides with respect to $a$ (valid since the integral converg
es uniformly for $a \ge \varepsilon > 0$):
$$\int_0^1 x^{a-1}\log x\,dx = -\frac{1}{a^2}.$$
Setting $a = n$:
$$\int_0^1 x^{n-1}\log x\,dx = -\frac{1}{n^2}.$$

**Step 4: Assemble the result.** Substituting back,
$$I = -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\left(-\frac{1}{n^2}\right) = \sum_{n=1}^{\
infty}\frac{1}{n^3} = \zeta(3).$$

**Conclusion.** The integral equals
$$\int_0^1 \frac{\log x\,\log(1-x)}{x}\,dx = \zeta(3),$$
which is indeed Apéry's constant.
