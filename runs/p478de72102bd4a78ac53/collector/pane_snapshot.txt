## Solution

Let $f(x) = bx + a(1-x) = a + (b-a)x$, which is linear and satisfies $f(0)=a$, $
f(1)=b$, and $f(x)>0$ on $[0,1]$ since $0<a<b$.

Define
$$I(t) = \int_0^1 f(x)^t\,dx, \qquad L = \lim_{t\to 0} I(t)^{1/t}.$$

**Step 1: Reduce to a logarithmic integral.**

Since $f(x)^t \to 1$ as $t\to 0$, we have $I(t)\to 1$, so $\log I(t)\to 0$. Thus
$$\log L = \lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\log I(t)}{t},$$
a $0/0$ form. By L'Hôpital's rule (justified since $I$ is differentiable near $t
=0$ by dominated convergence, as $f$ is bounded away from $0$):
$$\log L = \frac{I'(0)}{I(0)} = I'(0),$$
because $I(0)=\int_0^1 1\,dx = 1$.

Differentiating under the integral sign:
$$I'(t) = \int_0^1 f(x)^t \log f(x)\,dx \implies I'(0) = \int_0^1 \log f(x)\,dx
= \int_0^1 \log\bigl(a+(b-a)x\bigr)\,dx.$$

**Step 2: Evaluate the integral.**

Substitute $u = a+(b-a)x$, $du = (b-a)\,dx$:
$$\int_0^1 \log\bigl(a+(b-a)x\bigr)\,dx = \frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b \log u\,du = \fr
ac{1}{b-a}\Bigl[u\log u - u\Bigr]_a^b.$$
$$= \frac{1}{b-a}\Bigl[(b\log b - b) - (a\log a - a)\Bigr] = \frac{b\log b - a\l
og a}{b-a} - 1.$$

**Step 3: Exponentiate.**

$$\log L = \frac{b\log b - a\log a}{b-a} - 1 = \frac{1}{b-a}\log\!\left(\frac{b^
b}{a^a}\right) - 1.$$

Therefore
$$L = \exp\!\left(\frac{1}{b-a}\log\!\left(\frac{b^b}{a^a}\right) - 1\right) = \
frac{1}{e}\left(\frac{b^b}{a^a}\right)^{\!1/(b-a)}.$$

**Sanity check.** Let $a\to b$ (set $b=a+h$, $h\to 0$). Then $(b^b/a^a)^{1/(b-a)
} \to e$ (since $\log L\to \log a$, recovering $L\to a$, the limit of the geomet
ric/arithmetic means of the constant $a$). Indeed $\frac{b\log b - a\log a}{b-a}
\to \log a + 1$, giving $\log L\to \log a$, $L\to a$. Consistent.

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \lim_{t \to 0} \left( \int_{0}^{1}(bx + a(1-x))^t dx \rig
ht)^{\frac{1}{t}} = \frac{1}{e}\left(\frac{b^b}{a^a}\right)^{\!1/(b-a)}}$$
