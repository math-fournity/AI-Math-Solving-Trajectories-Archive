## Proof

**Setup.** Let $C \subset \mathbb{P}^2$ be a nonsingular plane curve of degree $
d$ over $\mathbb{C}$. We use the Plücker formulas relating a plane curve and its
 dual $C^*$.

**Notation.**
- $d, d^*$: degrees of $C$ and $C^*$.
- $\delta, \kappa$: numbers of nodes and cusps of $C$.
- $\delta^*, \kappa^*$: numbers of nodes and cusps of $C^*$ (equivalently, the n
umbers of bitangents and flexes of $C$).

Since $C$ is nonsingular, $\delta = 0$ and $\kappa = 0$.

**The Plücker formulas** (for a plane curve and its dual):
1. $d^* = d(d-1) - 2\delta - 3\kappa$
2. $\kappa^* = 3d(d-2) - 6\delta - 8\kappa$
3. $d = d^*(d^*-1) - 2\delta^* - 3\kappa^*$

Formula (3) is formula (1) applied to the dual curve: the degree of the "dual of
 the dual" is $d$, and the nodes/cusps of $C^*$ correspond to bitangents/flexes
of $C$.

**Applying to the nonsingular case** ($\delta = \kappa = 0$):

From (1): $d^* = d(d-1)$.

From (2): $\kappa^* = 3d(d-2)$ (the classical count of flexes).

From (3), solving for $\delta^*$:
$$2\delta^* = d^*(d^*-1) - d - 3\kappa^*.$$

Substituting:
$$2\delta^* = d(d-1)\bigl[d(d-1)-1\bigr] - d - 9d(d-2).$$

Factor out $d$:
$$2\delta^* = d\Bigl[(d-1)(d^2-d-1) - 1 - 9(d-2)\Bigr].$$

Compute $(d-1)(d^2-d-1) = d^3 - 2d^2 + 1$, so the bracket becomes
$$d^3 - 2d^2 + 1 - 1 - 9d + 18 = d^3 - 2d^2 - 9d + 18 = (d-2)(d^2 - 9).$$

Therefore
$$2\delta^* = d(d-2)(d^2 - 9),$$
and
$$\boxed{\delta^* = \tfrac{1}{2}\,d(d-2)(d^2-9) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,d(d-2)(d-3)(d+3)
.}$$

**Verification.** For $d = 4$ (nonsingular quartic):
$$\delta^* = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 4 \cdot 2 \cdot (16-9) = 28,$$
recovering the classical result that a smooth plane quartic has 28 bitangents. ✓

For $d = 3$ (nonsingular cubic): $\delta^* = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 3\cdot 1\cdot 0 =
 0$, consistent with the fact that a smooth cubic has no bitangents. ✓

**Conclusion.** The Plücker formula gives the number of bitangents of a nonsingu
lar plane curve of degree $d$ as

$$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}\,d(d-2)(d^2-9).}$$
