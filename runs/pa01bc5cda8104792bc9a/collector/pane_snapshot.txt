## Theorem (Approximate Differentiability of Sobolev Functions)

**Statement.** Let $u \in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^n)$. Then $u$ is *ap
proximately differentiable* at $\mathcal{L}^n$-almost every $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$
, and the approximate derivative equals the weak gradient $\nabla u(x)$. Concret
ely, for a.e. $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and every $z \in \mathbb{R}^n$:

$$\operatorname*{ap\text{-}lim}_{t \to 0^+} \frac{u(x + tz) - u(x)}{t} \;=\; \na
bla u(x) \cdot z.$$

Equivalently, for every $\varepsilon > 0$:

$$\lim_{s \to 0^+} \frac{\mathcal{L}^1\!\Bigl(\bigl\{t \in (0,s) : \bigl|\tfrac{
u(x+tz)-u(x)}{t} - \nabla u(x)\cdot z\bigr| > \varepsilon\bigr\}\Bigr)}{s} = 0.$
$

Setting $t = 1/k$, this gives the stated form:

$$\boxed{\operatorname*{ap\text{-}lim}_{k \to \infty} k\Bigl[u\Bigl(x + \tfrac{z
}{k}\Bigr) - u(x)\Bigr] = \nabla u(x)\cdot z \quad \text{for a.e. } x,\ \forall\
, z.}$$

Moreover, for $\mathcal{L}^{2n}$-a.e. $(x,z) \in \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}^
n$ the convergence is *actual pointwise convergence* (not merely approximate).

---

## Explanation

### 1. Why approximate differentiability is the right notion

A function $u \in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ need not have a continuo
us representative (when $n \geq 2$), so classical pointwise differentiability a.
e. is too much to ask. The correct substitute is **approximate differentiability
**: the linear approximation $u(x) + \nabla u(x)\cdot(y-x)$ is correct on a set
of *density 1* near $x$, even if it fails on a sparse set of points. This is the
 natural generalization of Rademacher's theorem (Lipschitz $\Rightarrow$ differe
ntiable a.e.) to the Sobolev setting.

### 2. Key ingredients

**(a) Lebesgue differentiation theorem.** For $f \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{
R}^n)$, a.e. $x$ is a *Lebesgue point*:
$$\frac{1}{|B(x,\rho)|}\int_{B(x,\rho)} |f(y)-f(x)|\,dy \;\longrightarrow\; 0 \q
uad (\rho \to 0).$$
Apply this to $u$ and to each component of $\nabla u = (\partial_1 u, \dots, \pa
rtial_n u)$. The set of $x$ that are simultaneous Lebesgue points of $u$ and all
 $\partial_i u$ has full measure.

**(b) ACL characterization (absolutely continuous on lines).** $u \in W^{1,1}_{\
mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ iff $u$ has a representative that is locally absolut
ely continuous on a.e. line parallel to each coordinate axis, with classical par
tial derivatives agreeing a.e. with the weak derivatives. By Fubini, the same ho
lds for lines in *any* fixed direction $z$: for a.e. line $\ell$ in direction $z
$, $u|_\ell$ is locally absolutely continuous.

**(c) Fundamental theorem of calculus on lines.** Where $u$ is absolutely contin
uous on the segment from $x$ to $x+y/k$:
$$u\Bigl(x+\frac{y}{k}\Bigr) - u(x) = \int_0^1 \nabla u\Bigl(x+\frac{ty}{k}\Bigr
)\cdot\frac{y}{k}\,dt.$$

### 3. Proof of approximate differentiability (the $L^1$ blow-up argument)

Fix $x$ a simultaneous Lebesgue point of $u$ and $\nabla u$. Define the **blow-u
p** (rescaled difference):
$$v_k(y) := k\bigl[u(x + y/k) - u(x)\bigr], \qquad y \in B(0,r).$$

Using (c):
$$v_k(y) - \nabla u(x)\cdot y = \int_0^1 \bigl[\nabla u(x + ty/k) - \nabla u(x)\
bigr]\cdot y\,dt.$$

Take the $L^1(B(0,r))$ norm:
$$\int_{B(0,r)} |v_k(y) - \nabla u(x)\cdot y|\,dy \;\leq\; r\int_0^1\int_{B(0,r)
} |\nabla u(x+ty/k)-\nabla u(x)|\,dy\,dt.$$

Change variables $w = x + ty/k$ (so $dy = (k/t)^n\,dw$, domain $B(x,\,tr/k)$):
$$\int_{B(0,r)}|\nabla u(x+ty/k)-\nabla u(x)|\,dy = \Bigl(\frac{k}{t}\Bigr)^n\!\
int_{B(x,\,tr/k)}|\nabla u(w)-\nabla u(x)|\,dw.$$

Since $|B(x,tr/k)| = \omega_n(tr/k)^n$ and $(k/t)^n\,|B(x,tr/k)| = \omega_n r^n
= |B(0,r)|$, we can write:
$$= |B(0,r)|\;\cdot\;\frac{1}{|B(x,tr/k)|}\int_{B(x,tr/k)}|\nabla u(w)-\nabla u(
x)|\,dw \;=:\; |B(0,r)|\;\phi\!\Bigl(\frac{tr}{k}\Bigr),$$

where $\phi(\rho) \to 0$ as $\rho \to 0$ because $x$ is a Lebesgue point of $\na
bla u$, and $\phi$ is bounded near $0$. Therefore:

$$\int_{B(0,r)} |v_k(y) - \nabla u(x)\cdot y|\,dy \;\leq\; r\,|B(0,r)|\int_0^1 \
phi(tr/k)\,dt \;\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\; 0$$

by dominated convergence ($\phi$ is bounded, $t \in [0,1]$). **Thus $v_k \to \na
bla u(x)\cdot y$ in $L^1(B(0,r))$**, hence in measure on $B(0,r)$.

Convergence in measure of $v_k$ to the linear function $\nabla u(x)\cdot y$ on e
very ball $B(0,r)$ is *exactly* the definition of approximate differentiability
of $u$ at $x$ with derivative $\nabla u(x)$. Restricting to any ray $y = tz$ (vi
a Fubini / 1D approximate limits) yields:

$$\operatorname*{ap\text{-}lim}_{t\to 0^+} \frac{u(x+tz)-u(x)}{t} = \nabla u(x)\
cdot z \qquad \forall\, z \in \mathbb{R}^n,$$

and setting $t = 1/k$ gives the stated discrete form.

### 4. Pointwise convergence for a.e. $(x,z)$

For a **fixed** direction $z \neq 0$, by the ACL characterization and Fubini, fo
r a.e. $x$ the map $t \mapsto u(x+tz)$ is locally absolutely continuous near $t=
0$. At such $x$, the classical derivative exists:

$$\lim_{t\to 0^+}\frac{u(x+tz)-u(x)}{t} = \frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}u(x+tz) = \nab
la u(x)\cdot z,$$

so in particular $\lim_{k\to\infty} k[u(x+z/k)-u(x)] = \nabla u(x)\cdot z$ as an
 **ordinary** limit. The exceptional set in $x$ depends on $z$, but since it has
 measure zero for each $z$, Fubini gives $\mathcal{L}^{2n}$-a.e. $(x,z) \in \mat
hbb{R}^n\times\mathbb{R}^n$ enjoys genuine pointwise convergence.

### 5. Summary

| Convergence mode | Quantifier | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Approximate** limit | a.e. $x$, **every** $z$ | $L^1$ blow-up + Lebesgue poi
nts |
| **Pointwise** limit | a.e. $(x,z)$ jointly | ACL + Fubini |

The weak gradient $\nabla u(x)$ is recovered as the approximate (and, on a full-
measure set of directions, classical) directional derivative in every direction,
 and the difference quotient $k[u(x+z/k)-u(x)]$ converges to $\nabla u(x)\cdot z
$.
