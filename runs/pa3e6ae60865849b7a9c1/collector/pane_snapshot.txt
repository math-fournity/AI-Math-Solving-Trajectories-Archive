## Proof

Let $V$ be the vector space of infinite sequences $(a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots)$ with
 only finitely many nonzero terms (i.e., finitely-supported sequences), equipped
 with the standard inner product
$$\langle a, b\rangle = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} a_i\,\overline{b_i},$$
which is a finite sum since $a, b$ have finite support.

### The example: the right-shift operator

Define $T: V \to V$ by
$$T(a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots) = (0, a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots).$$
This is the **right-shift** (insertion of a leading zero). It maps finitely-supp
orted sequences to finitely-supported sequences, so $T \in L(V)$.

### Step 1: $T$ admits an adjoint, and $T^*$ is the left-shift

For $a, b \in V$,
$$\langle Ta, b\rangle = 0\cdot\overline{b_1} + a_1\overline{b_2} + a_2\overline
{b_3} + \cdots = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} a_i\,\overline{b_{i+1}}.$$
Define $S: V \to V$ (the **left-shift**) by
$$S(b_1, b_2, b_3, \ldots) = (b_2, b_3, b_4, \ldots).$$
Then
$$\langle a, Sb\rangle = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} a_i\,\overline{(Sb)_i} = \sum_{i=1}
^{\infty} a_i\,\overline{b_{i+1}} = \langle Ta, b\rangle.$$
Hence $T^* = S$ exists, i.e., $T^*(b_1, b_2, \ldots) = (b_2, b_3, \ldots)$. **Co
ndition 1 holds.**

### Step 2: $T^*T = I$

For any $a = (a_1, a_2, \ldots) \in V$:
$$Ta = (0, a_1, a_2, \ldots), \qquad T^*(Ta) = (a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots) = a.$$
So $T^*T = I$. **Condition 2 holds.**

### Step 3: $TT^*$ is a projection and $TT^* \neq I$

For any $b = (b_1, b_2, \ldots) \in V$:
$$T^*b = (b_2, b_3, \ldots), \qquad T(T^*b) = (0, b_2, b_3, \ldots).$$
So $TT^*$ is the operator $P$ that replaces the first coordinate by $0$ and keep
s the rest:
$$P(b_1, b_2, b_3, \ldots) = (0, b_2, b_3, \ldots).$$

**Idempotence:** $P(Pb) = P(0, b_2, b_3, \ldots) = (0, b_2, b_3, \ldots) = Pb$,
so $(TT^*)^2 = TT^*$.

**Not the identity:** For $e_1 = (1, 0, 0, \ldots) \in V$, $P e_1 = (0, 0, \ldot
s) \neq e_1$, so $TT^* \neq I$.

Thus $TT^*$ is a projection but not the identity. **Condition 3 holds.**

### Why the left-shift does NOT satisfy the conditions

Let $L$ be the left-shift, $L(a_1, a_2, \ldots) = (a_2, a_3, \ldots)$. By the sa
me computation as above, $L^* = T$ (the right-shift). Then:
- $L^*L = TL$: $La = (a_2, a_3, \ldots)$, then $T(La) = (0, a_2, a_3, \ldots) \n
eq a$. So $L^*L \neq I$. **Condition 2 fails.**
- $LL^* = LT$: $Tb = (0, b_1, b_2, \ldots)$, then $L(Tb) = (b_1, b_2, \ldots) =
b$. So $LL^* = I$.

The left-shift satisfies the "mirror" conditions ($LL^* = I$, $L^*L$ a non-ident
ity projection), which is the opposite of what is required. Therefore the left-s
hift does **not** satisfy the three stated conditions; its adjoint, the **right-
shift**, does.

### Conclusion

The right-shift operator $T(a_1, a_2, \ldots) = (0, a_1, a_2, \ldots)$ on $V$ sa
tisfies all three requirements: it has an adjoint (the left-shift), $T^*T = I$,
and $TT^*$ is the projection onto the subspace of sequences with vanishing first
 coordinate, which is not the identity. The left-shift operator proposed in the
problem does not satisfy the conditions (it fails condition 2, instead satisfyin
g $LL^* = I$); its adjoint, the right-shift, is the correct example.
