## Proof

**Claim:** The extension $K(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n)/K(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_r)$ is a
finite (algebraic) extension.

**Key definitions.** Recall that a set $T = \{x_1, \dots, x_r\}$ is a *transcend
ental basis* of an extension $L/K$ if and only if:

1. **Algebraic independence:** $x_1, \dots, x_r$ are algebraically independent o
ver $K$ (i.e., no nonzero polynomial $f \in K[X_1, \dots, X_r]$ vanishes on $(x_
1, \dots, x_r)$), and

2. **Algebraicity:** $L$ is algebraic over $K(T) = K(x_1, \dots, x_r)$.

By hypothesis, $\{x_1, \dots, x_r\}$ is a transcendental basis of $K(x_1, \dots,
 x_n)$ over $K$. Applying condition (2) with $L = K(x_1, \dots, x_n)$, we conclu
de that $K(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ is **algebraic** over $K(x_1, \dots, x_r)$.

**From algebraic to finite.** It remains to show the extension is not merely alg
ebraic but *finite*. Since $L = K(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ is generated over $K(x_1, \d
ots, x_r)$ by adjoining the finitely many elements $x_{r+1}, x_{r+2}, \dots, x_n
$, and each $x_i$ (for $i > r$) is algebraic over $K(x_1, \dots, x_r)$, we have
the tower

$$K(x_1, \dots, x_r) \;\subseteq\; K(x_1, \dots, x_r, x_{r+1}) \;\subseteq\; \cd
ots \;\subseteq\; K(x_1, \dots, x_n).$$

Each step adjoins a single algebraic element, so each step is a finite extension
:

$$\big(x_1, \dots, x_i) : K(x_1, \dots, x_{i-1})\big] < \infty, \qquad i = r+1
, \dots, n.$$

By the tower law (multiplicativity of degrees in towers),

$$\big(x_1, \dots, x_n) : K(x_1, \dots, x_r)\big] = \prod_{i=r+1}^{n} \big(x
_1, \dots, x_i) : K(x_1, \dots, x_{i-1})\big] < \infty.$$

This is a finite product of finite positive integers, hence finite.

**Conclusion.** The extension $K(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n)/K(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_r)$
is algebraic (by the definition of transcendental basis) and finitely generated,
 therefore **finite**. $\blacksquare$
