Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum spillage $M$ over all permut
ations of $[1,2,\ldots,10]$ and count how many permutations achieve it.

## Setup

For a permutation $(a_1, \ldots, a_{10})$, define $P_k = a_1 a_2 \cdots a_k$ and
 $S = \sum_{k=1}^{10} \lfloor P_k/100 \rfloor$.

Since $P_{10} = 10! = 3628800$ is fixed, I write $P_k = 10!/Q_k$ where $Q_k = a_
{k+1} \cdots a_{10}$ (product of the last $10-k$ elements, with $Q_{10}=1$). The
n $s(P_k) = \lfloor 10!/(100\,Q_k) \rfloor$.

## Minimizing each term

Since $\lfloor 10!/(100\,Q_k)\rfloor$ is non-increasing in $Q_k$, minimizing eac
h term requires **maximizing** each $Q_k$. The product of the last $10-k$ elemen
ts is maximized when those elements are the $10-k$ **largest** elements. The uni
que permutation achieving this for **all** $k$ simultaneously is the sorted perm
utation $[1,2,\ldots,10]$, giving $Q_k^* = 10!/k!$.

Computing the minimum spillage:

| $k$ | $Q_k^*$ | $P_k$ | $\lfloor P_k/100\rfloor$ |
|-----|---------|-------|--------------------------|
| 1–4 | large | 1, 2, 6, 24 | 0, 0, 0, 0 |
| 5 | 30240 | 120 | 1 |
| 6 | 5040 | 720 | 7 |
| 7 | 720 | 5040 | 50 |
| 8 | 90 | 40320 | 403 |
| 9 | 10 | 362880 | 3628 |
| 10 | 1 | 3628800 | 36288 |

$$M = 0+0+0+0+1+7+50+403+3628+36288 = 40377.$$

## Counting permutations achieving $M$

Since the sorted permutation minimizes **every** term simultaneously, any other
permutation has each term $\geq$ the sorted value. For the sum to equal $M$, **e
very** term must equal its minimum. This gives constraints on each $Q_k$.

**Fixed tail:** The constraints for $k=7,8,9,10$ force $a_7=7, a_8=8, a_9=9, a_{
10}=10$ (the ranges for $Q_k$ are so tight that only one value works each time).

**Free prefix:** Positions 1–6 are a permutation of $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$. The remai
ning constraints are:

- **$k=5$:** $\lfloor 720/(100\,a_6)\rfloor = 1$ requires $100 \le 720/a_6 < 200
$, giving $a_6 \in \{4,5,6\}$.
- **$k=4$:** $\lfloor 720/(100\,a_5 a_6)\rfloor = 0$ requires $a_5 \cdot a_6 \ge
 8$. Since $a_6 \ge 4$, this fails only when $a_5 = 1$ (giving $a_5 a_6 \le 6 <
8$). So $a_5 \neq 1$.
- **$k=3$:** Requires $a_4 \cdot a_5 \cdot a_6 \ge 8$. Since $a_4 \ge 1$ and $a_
5 a_6 \ge 8$, this is automatic. ✓
- **$k=2$:** Requires $a_3 \cdot a_4 \cdot a_5 \cdot a_6 \ge 8$. Automatic since
 $a_5 a_6 \ge 8$. ✓
- **$k=1$:** $P_1 = a_1 \le 6 < 100$. Automatic. ✓

## Counting

The constraints reduce to: $a_6 \in \{4,5,6\}$, $a_5 \in \{2,3,4,5,6\}\setminus\
{a_6\}$ (4 choices), and $(a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4)$ is any permutation of the remaining
 4 elements ($4! = 24$ ways).

**Per value of $a_6$:** $4 \times 24 = 96$ permutations.

**Total:** $3 \times 96 = \boxed{288}$.
