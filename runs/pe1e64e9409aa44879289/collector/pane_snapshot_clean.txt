## Solution

**Setting up the scaling relationship.**

The problem states there is an "internal scaling logic" — a constant $k$ — such
that the processed output equals $k$ times the amount of data actually fed into
the processor. The amount fed in is (fraction of batch) × (batch size in GB).

**Determining the constant from the calibration case.**

From the legacy calibration:
$$\text{output} = k \cdot \left(\frac{1}{9} \times 60\right) = 5$$

$$k \cdot \frac{60}{9} = 5 \implies k = \frac{5 \times 9}{60} = \frac{45}{60} =
\frac{3}{4}$$

So the internal scaling constant is $k = \tfrac{3}{4}$.

**Applying the same scaling to the new case.**

For $\tfrac{1}{20}$ of an 80 GB batch:
$$\text{amount fed} = \frac{1}{20} \times 80 = 4 \text{ GB}$$

$$\text{output} = k \cdot 4 = \frac{3}{4} \times 4 = 3$$

**Verification.** Check the calibration case with $k = \tfrac{3}{4}$:
$$\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{60}{9} = \frac{180}{36} = 5 \checkmark$$

The scaling is consistent.

**Answer:** The processed output is **3 units**.
