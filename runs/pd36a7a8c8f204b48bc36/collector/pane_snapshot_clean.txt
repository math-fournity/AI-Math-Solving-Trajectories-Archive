## Solution

**Answer: 11**

### Upper bound

Consider the seven numbers $\{1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13\}$. These are **pairwise rel
atively prime**: $1$ is coprime to everything, and $2,3,5,7,11,13$ are distinct
primes (hence pairwise coprime).

In a conspiratorial set $S$, no three elements can be pairwise coprime. Therefor
e $S$ can contain **at most two** elements from any collection of pairwise-copri
me numbers (three would themselves form a forbidden triple). Applying this to th
e seven-element pairwise-coprime set above:

$$|S \cap \{1,2,3,5,7,11,13\}| \le 2,$$

so at least $7 - 2 = 5$ of these seven numbers are **excluded** from $S$. Since
$S \subseteq \{1,2,\dots,16\}$,

$$|S| \le 16 - 5 = 11.$$

### Construction achieving 11

Take
$$S = \{2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 15, 16\}.$$

Partition $S$ into:
- **Evens** $E = \{2,4,6,8,10,12,14,16\}$ — every pair shares the factor $2$.
- **Odds** $O = \{3, 9, 15\}$ — every pair shares the factor $3$ (since $3\mid 9
$, $3\mid 15$, $3\mid 3$).

Now consider any three elements of $S$:

- **Three evens**: all share factor $2$, so not pairwise coprime.
- **Two evens + one odd**: the two evens share factor $2$, so not pairwise copri
me.
- **One even + two odds**: the two odds share factor $3$, so not pairwise coprim
e.
- **Three odds** $\{3,9,15\}$: every pair shares factor $3$, so not pairwise cop
rime.

In every case at least one pair shares a common factor $> 1$, so no three elemen
ts of $S$ are pairwise relatively prime. Thus $S$ is conspiratorial, and $|S| =
11$.

### Conclusion

The upper bound is $11$ and it is achieved by the construction above. The larges
t number of elements in any conspiratorial subset of $\{1, 2, \dots, 16\}$ is

$$\boxed{11}.$$
