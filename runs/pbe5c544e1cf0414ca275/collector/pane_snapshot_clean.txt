## Analysis

This is **Erdős Problem 972**, formally declared in the source as `@[category re
search open]`. The question asks:

> Let α > 1 be irrational. Are there infinitely many primes p such that ⌊pα⌋ is
also prime?

This is a genuine open problem in number theory. The conjecture is that for ever
y irrational α > 1, the set of primes p with ⌊pα⌋ also prime is infinite, but no
 proof is known.

### Why this is beyond reach

This problem sits at the intersection of two notoriously difficult areas:

1. **Distribution of primes in sparse sequences.** Even the weaker question — wh
ether ⌊nα⌋ is prime for infinitely many integers n (not necessarily prime n) — i
s already a deep result. Vinogradov-type methods and the work of Vinogradov, Rhi
n, and others establish that for irrational α with bounded partial quotients (or
 sufficiently good Diophantine approximation lower bounds), the sequence ⌊nα⌋ co
ntains infinitely many primes. This uses the Hardy–Littlewood circle method and
requires strong equidistribution of α modulo 1.

2. **Primality of ⌊pα⌋ with p itself prime.** This imposes the additional constr
aint that the index p must be prime. One needs to count primes p ≤ X such that ⌊
pα⌋ is prime. Heuristically, the expected count up to X is on the order of
$$\sum_{\substack{p \leq X \\ p \text{ prime}}} \frac{1}{\log(\alpha p)} \approx
 \frac{X}{(\log X)^2},$$
by the prime number theorem and a Bateman–Horn–style heuristic, which diverges.
So the conjectured answer is **yes**, the set is infinite.

However, turning this heuristic into a proof requires:
- Strong equidistribution of pα mod 1 for primes p (available via Vinogradov's t
heorem on exponential sums over primes, given good Diophantine properties of α),
 **and**
- A transference from "⌊pα⌋ hits every residue class / interval" to "⌊pα⌋ is pri
me," which is exactly the kind of prime-in-linear-sequence problem that the circ
le method handles only with great difficulty, and only when the linear form has
suitable structure.

The key obstruction: ⌊pα⌋ is **not** a linear form in p; it is a floor of a line
ar form, so the primality condition is on a sequence that is only piecewise-line
ar. The standard Vinogradov three-primes / circle method machinery does not dire
ctly apply to "⌊pα⌋ is prime" because the target value is not a fixed linear com
bination — it depends on the fractional part of pα in a way that prevents the us
ual major-arc/minor-arc decomposition from isolating primality.

For **specific** α (e.g., α = √2, or α with bounded partial quotients), partial
results or conditional results under generalized Hardy–Littlewood conjectures ex
ist, but the **unconditional, for all irrational α > 1** statement is open. In p
articular:
- No unconditional proof is known for any single explicit irrational α > 1 that
⌊pα⌋ is prime for infinitely many primes p. (Contrast: it is known for infinitel
y many *integers* n, not necessarily prime, for α with good Diophantine properti
es.)
- The "for all irrational α > 1" quantifier makes the problem strictly harder, a
s one must also handle Liouville-type α with extremely good rational approximati
ons, where equidistribution of pα mod 1 over primes can be very weak.

### Conclusion

This is an open research problem. The conjectured answer is **yes** (the set is
infinite for every irrational α > 1), supported by Bateman–Horn heuristics, but
no proof exists. I cannot produce a proof of an open problem.
