# IMO 2006 N3 — Proof

Let $D(n) = \sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\lfloor \tfrac{n}{k}\right\rfloor$, so $f(n)=D(n)
/n$. The key identity is

$$D(n)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\#\{m\le n:k\mid m\}=\sum_{m=1}^{n}d(m),$$

where $d(m)$ denotes the number of positive divisors of $m$ (each pair $(k,m)$ w
ith $k\mid m$, $m\le n$ is counted once on each side). Hence $D(n+1)=D(n)+d(n+1)
$, and

$$f(n+1)-f(n)=\frac{D(n)+d(n+1)}{n+1}-\frac{D(n)}{n}=\frac{n\,d(n+1)-D(n)}{n(n+1
)}=\frac{d(n+1)-f(n)}{n+1}.$$

Therefore
$$f(n+1)>f(n)\iff d(n+1)>f(n),\qquad f(n+1)<f(n)\iff d(n+1)<f(n).$$

We will use the standard bounds
$$H_n-1\;\le\; f(n)\;\le\; H_n,\qquad H_n:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tfrac1j,$$
which follow from $\frac{n}{k}-1<\lfloor n/k\rfloor\le \frac{n}{k}$, summed over
 $k=1,\dots,n$.

---

## Part 1: $f(n+1)>f(n)$ infinitely often

Take $n=2^{m}-1$ for $m\ge 1$, so $n+1=2^{m}$ and $d(n+1)=m+1$. Using the upper
bound and $\log(2^m-1)<m\log 2$,
$$f(n)\le H_n \le 1+\log n < 1+m\log 2.$$
Since $\log 2<1$, for every $m\ge 1$,
$$d(n+1)=m+1>1+m\log 2>f(n).$$
Thus $f(2^{m})>f(2^{m}-1)$ for every $m\ge 1$, giving infinitely many $n$ with $
f(n+1)>f(n)$.

---

## Part 2: $f(n+1)<f(n)$ infinitely often

Take $n=p-1$ where $p$ is a prime with $p\ge 13$ (so $n\ge 12$). Then $d(n+1)=d(
p)=2$. Using the lower bound,
$$f(n)\ge H_n-1.$$
For $n\ge 11$, $H_n\ge H_{11}=\tfrac{83711}{27720}>3$, so $f(n)>2=d(n+1)$. Hence
$$f(p)<f(p-1)$$
for every prime $p\ge 13$. By Euclid's theorem there are infinitely many such pr
imes, giving infinitely many $n$ with $f(n+1)<f(n)$.

---

Both parts are established. $\blacksquare$
