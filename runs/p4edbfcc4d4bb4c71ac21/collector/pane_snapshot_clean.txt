**Theorem.** A closed, smooth surface with a point of positive Gaussian curvatur
e can be isometrically immersed into ℝ³. Moreover, possessing a point of positiv
e Gaussian curvature is a necessary condition for a closed surface to admit a sm
ooth (C²) isometric immersion into ℝ³.

**Proof.**

We establish necessity and sufficiency separately.

---

**Part I — Necessity (any closed surface smoothly immersed in ℝ³ has a point of
positive curvature).**

Let *f : M → ℝ³* be a smooth isometric immersion of a closed (compact, without b
oundary) surface *M*. For a unit vector *v ∈ S²*, define the height function

$$h_v(x) = \langle f(x),\, v \rangle.$$

By Sard's theorem, for almost every *v*, the function *h_v* is a Morse function.
 Since *M* is compact, *h_v* attains its maximum at some point *p ∈ M*. At *p*,
the differential vanishes: *dh_v|_p = 0*, which means the tangent plane *df(T_pM
)* is perpendicular to *v*.

The Hessian of *h_v* at *p* is given by the second fundamental form:

$$D^2 h_v\big|_p(w, w) = \langle \mathrm{II}_p(w, w),\, v \rangle, \quad w \in T
_p M.$$

Since *p* is a (non-degenerate) maximum, *D²h_v|_p* is negative definite. Choose
 the unit normal *N* at *p* with ⟨N, v⟩ > 0. Then the shape operator *S = −dN* s
atisfies ⟨S(w), w⟩ > 0 for all nonzero *w ∈ T_pM*, i.e., *S* is positive definit
e. Both principal curvatures satisfy *κ₁(p) > 0* and *κ₂(p) > 0*, hence:

$$K(p) = \kappa_1(p)\,\kappa_2(p) > 0. \qquad \square$$

**Part II — Sufficiency (a closed surface with a point of positive curvature can
 be isometrically immersed in ℝ³).**

Let *(M, g)* be a closed smooth Riemannian surface with *K(p) > 0* for some *p ∈
 M*.

**Step 1 (Constructing a short immersion).** Every closed orientable 2-manifold
admits a smooth embedding *e : M ↪ ℝ³*. The pullback metric *ḡ = e\*g_{Euc}* is
smooth and positive definite on *M*. Since *g* is also positive definite and *M*
 is compact, we may choose *λ > 0* sufficiently small so that

$$\lambda^2 \bar{g} < g \quad \text{(pointwise, i.e., } g - \lambda^2 \bar{g} \t
ext{ is positive definite)}.$$

The map *f₀ = λ · e : M → ℝ³* is then a **strictly short** smooth immersion:

$$f_0^* g_{\mathrm{Euc}} = \lambda^2 \bar{g} < g.$$

**Step 2 (Applying the Nash–Kuiper theorem).** The **Nash–Kuiper theorem** (Nash
 1954, extended by Kuiper 1955) states:

> *If (Mᵐ, g) is a compact Riemannian manifold and f : M → ℝⁿ is a strictly shor
t C¹ immersion with n ≥ m + 1, then for every ε > 0 there exists a C¹ isometric
immersion f_ε : (M, g) → ℝⁿ with ‖f_ε − f‖_{C⁰} < ε.*

Here *m = 2* and *n = 3 = m + 1*, so the codimension condition is satisfied. App
lying the theorem to *f₀*, we obtain a **C¹ isometric immersion** *f : (M, g) →
ℝ³*.

Thus *(M, g)* admits an isometric immersion into ℝ³.  □

**Part III — Sharpness of the positive curvature condition.**

The condition *K > 0* at some point is **sharp**: a closed surface with *K ≤ 0*
everywhere cannot be smoothly (C²) isometrically immersed in ℝ³, since Part I sh
ows any such immersion must have a point with *K > 0*.

A concrete example: the **flat torus** *(T², g_flat)* with *K ≡ 0* has no point
of positive curvature. By the necessity argument, it admits no C² isometric imme
rsion into ℝ³. (It does admit a C¹ isometric immersion by Nash–Kuiper, illustrat
ing the dramatic regularity gap: C¹ isometric immersions exist with no curvature
 obstruction, while C² immersions are constrained by the Gauss equation and the
height-function argument.)
