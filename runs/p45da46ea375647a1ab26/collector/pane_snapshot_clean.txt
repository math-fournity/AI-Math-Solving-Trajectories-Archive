## Proof

We determine the torsion subgroup of $E: y^2 = x^3 + x$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Step 1: The 2-torsion.**
Points of order 2 satisfy $y=0$, i.e. $x^3+x = x(x^2+1)=0$. Over $\mathbb{Q}$, t
he only root is $x=0$ (since $x^2+1=0$ has no rational solution). Hence
$$E[2](\mathbb{Q}) = \{O,\,(0,0)\},$$
so $(0,0)$ is a rational point of order 2. In particular $|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{
tors}}|\geq 2$.

**Step 2: Bounding the torsion via reduction modulo primes.**
The discriminant of $x^3+x$ is $-4$, so the only prime of bad reduction is $2$.
For any odd prime $p$, the reduction map injects $E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}\ho
okrightarrow E(\mathbb{F}_p)$, so $|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}|\mid \#E(\mathbb
{F}_p)$.

- **Mod 3:** $y^2 = x^3+x$ over $\mathbb{F}_3$. The quadratic residues mod 3 are
 $\{0,1\}$.
  - $x=0:\; y^2=0 \Rightarrow (0,0)$.
  - $x=1:\; y^2=2$, not a square.
  - $x=2:\; y^2=8+2\equiv 1 \Rightarrow (2,1),(2,2)$.
  Together with $O$: $\#E(\mathbb{F}_3)=4$.

- **Mod 5:** $y^2=x^3+x$ over $\mathbb{F}_5$. The quadratic residues mod 5 are $
\{0,1,4\}$.
  - $x=0:\; y^2=0 \Rightarrow (0,0)$.
  - $x=1:\; y^2=2$, not a square.
  - $x=2:\; y^2=10\equiv 0 \Rightarrow (2,0)$.
  - $x=3:\; y^2=30\equiv 0 \Rightarrow (3,0)$.
  - $x=4:\; y^2=68\equiv 3$, not a square.
  Together with $O$: $\#E(\mathbb{F}_5)=4$.

Therefore
$$|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}|\;\Big|\;\gcd\bigl(\#E(\mathbb{F}_3),\#E(\mathbb{
F}_5)\bigr)=\gcd(4,4)=4.$$
Combined with Step 1, $|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}|\in\{2,4\}$.

**Step 3: Ruling out a point of order 4.**
A point $P=(x,y)$ of order 4 satisfies $2P=(0,0)$, in particular the $x$-coordin
ate of $2P$ is $0$. The duplication formula gives
$$x(2P)=\left(\frac{3x^2+1}{2y}\right)^2-2x.$$
Setting $x(2P)=0$ and using $y^2=x^3+x=x(x^2+1)$:
$$\left(\frac{3x^2+1}{2y}\right)^2 = 2x \;\Longrightarrow\; (3x^2+1)^2 = 8x\,y^2
 = 8x^2(x^2+1).$$
Expanding,
$$9x^4+6x^2+1 = 8x^4+8x^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; x^4-2x^2+1=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; (
x^2-1)^2=0,$$
so $x=\pm 1$. But $x=1$ gives $y^2=2$ and $x=-1$ gives $y^2=-2$, neither of whic
h is a rational square. Hence there is no rational point $P$ with $2P=(0,0)$, so
 $E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}$ has no element of order 4.

**Conclusion.**
Since $|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}|\in\{2,4\}$ and there is no element of order
 4, we must have
$$E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}} = E[2](\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.$$

The order of the torsion subgroup is $\boxed{2}$.
