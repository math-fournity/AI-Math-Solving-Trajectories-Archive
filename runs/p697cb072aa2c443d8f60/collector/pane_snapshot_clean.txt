## Analyzing the interior of $A$

The set $A = \{(z,w) \in \mathbb{C}^2 : p(z,w) = 0\}$ is the zero locus of the p
olynomial $p(z,w)$.

**Claim: $A$ has empty interior.**

Since each $a_i(z)$ is a non-constant polynomial in $z$, each $a_i$ is a nonzero
 polynomial. Therefore $p(z,w) = \sum_{i=0}^k a_i(z) w^k$ is a **nonzero** polyn
omial in the two variables $(z,w)$. A fundamental fact from complex analysis/alg
ebraic geometry is:

> The zero set of a nonzero polynomial (equivalently, a nonzero holomorphic func
tion) on $\mathbb{C}^n$ has empty interior.

*Proof of this fact:* If $A$ had nonempty interior, then $p$ would vanish on an
open subset of $\mathbb{C}^2$. By the identity theorem for holomorphic functions
 (applied iteratively: fix $z$ and view $p$ as a polynomial in $w$, or use the f
ull multivariable identity theorem), $p \equiv 0$, contradicting that $p$ is non
zero.

Hence **$A$ has empty interior**, eliminating options C and D.

## Analyzing boundedness of $A$

**Claim: $A$ is unbounded.**

Write $a_i(z) = c_i z^{d_i} + \text{(lower order terms)}$ with $c_i \neq 0$ and
$d_i \geq 1$ (since each $a_i$ is non-constant). Let $d = \max_{0 \leq i \leq k}
 d_i$ and let $I = \{i : d_i = d\}$.

Factor out $z^d$:

$$p(z,w) = z^d \left[\sum_{i \in I} c_i w^i + \sum_{i \notin I} c_i z^{d_i - d}
w^i (1 + O(1/z))\right].$$

Since $d_i - d < 0$ for $i \notin I$, the second sum tends to $0$ as $|z| \to \i
nfty$ (for $w$ in any bounded set). Thus:

$$\frac{p(z,w)}{z^d} \longrightarrow Q(w) := \sum_{i \in I} c_i w^i \quad \text{
as } |z| \to \infty,$$

uniformly on compact subsets of the $w$-plane.

The polynomial $Q(w) = \sum_{i \in I} c_i w^i$ is **nonzero** (since $I \neq \em
ptyset$ and each $c_i \neq 0$). By the fundamental theorem of algebra, $Q$ has a
t least one root $w_0 \in \mathbb{C}$.

Since $Q(w_0) = 0$ and $Q \not\equiv 0$, the root $w_0$ is isolated: there exist
s $\epsilon > 0$ such that $Q$ has no other zeros in $|w - w_0| \leq \epsilon$ a
nd $|Q(w)| > 0$ on $|w - w_0| = \epsilon$.

For $|z|$ sufficiently large, $|p(z,w)/z^d - Q(w)| < \min_{|w-w_0|=\epsilon} |Q(
w)|$ on the circle $|w - w_0| = \epsilon$. By **Rouché's theorem**, $p(z,w)/z^d$
 (as a function of $w$) has a zero inside $|w - w_0| < \epsilon$.

Therefore, for every sufficiently large $|z|$, there exists $w(z)$ with $|w(z) -
 w_0| < \epsilon$ such that $p(z, w(z)) = 0$, i.e., $(z, w(z)) \in A$.

Since $|z|$ can be taken arbitrarily large, **$A$ is unbounded**.

## Verification with a simple example

Take $p(z,w) = z + zw = z(1+w)$. Here $a_0(z) = z$, $a_1(z) = z$, both non-const
ant, $k=1$. Then $A = \{z = 0\} \cup \{w = -1\}$, which is unbounded (both compo
nents are unbounded) with empty interior. ✓

## Conclusion

$A$ is **unbounded** with **empty interior**.

The answer is **B**.
