## Proof

We work over the finite field $\mathbb{F}_p$ (integers modulo a prime $p$). Let

$$G = \mathrm{GL}(2, p) = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} :
 a,b,c,d \in \mathbb{F}_p,\; ad - bc \neq 0 \right\},$$

and

$$H = \mathrm{SL}(2, p) = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \
in G : ad - bc = 1 \right\}.$$

We determine $|H|$ by counting, then confirm it via the determinant homomorphism
.

---

### Step 1. Counting $|G| = |\mathrm{GL}(2,p)|$

A matrix in $\mathrm{GL}(2,p)$ is determined by two linearly independent rows (e
quivalently columns).

- **First row** $(a, b)$: any nonzero vector in $\mathbb{F}_p^2$. There are $p^2
 - 1$ choices.
- **Second row** $(c, d)$: any vector not in the span of the first row. The span
 of a nonzero vector has $p$ elements, so there are $p^2 - p$ choices.

Hence

$$|G| = (p^2 - 1)(p^2 - p) = p(p-1)^2(p+1).$$

---

### Step 2. The determinant is a surjective homomorphism onto $\mathbb{F}_p^\tim
es$

The map

$$\det : G \to \mathbb{F}_p^\times, \qquad h \mapsto \det(h)$$

is a group homomorphism (since $\det(h_1 h_2) = \det(h_1)\det(h_2)$), with kerne
l exactly

$$\ker(\det) = \{h \in G : \det(h) = 1\} = H.$$

It is **surjective**: for any $r \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$, the diagonal matrix $
\begin{pmatrix} r & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ has determinant $r$ and lies in $G
$. Therefore $|\mathbb{F}_p^\times| = p - 1$ divides $|G|$, and by the first iso
morphism theorem

$$G / H \cong \mathbb{F}_p^\times,$$

so

$$|H| = \frac{|G|}{|\mathbb{F}_p^\times|} = \frac{p(p-1)^2(p+1)}{p-1} = p(p-1)(p
+1) = p(p^2 - 1).$$

---

### Step 3. Direct enumeration of the equivalence classes $(a,b,c,d)$ with $ad -
 bc = 1$

We count the solutions directly to confirm. We split by whether $a = 0$ or $a \n
eq 0$.

**Case 1: $a \neq 0$.** Then $a$ can be any of the $p - 1$ nonzero elements. Onc
e $a$ is fixed, $b$ and $c$ are arbitrary ($p$ choices each), and $d$ is uniquel
y determined by $d = a^{-1}(1 + bc)$. This gives

$$(p-1) \cdot p \cdot p = p^2(p-1) \text{ matrices}.$$

**Case 2: $a = 0$.** The condition becomes $-bc = 1$, i.e. $bc = -1$. Since $-1
\neq 0$, both $b$ and $c$ must be nonzero. For each of the $p - 1$ choices of $b
$, $c$ is uniquely determined as $c = -b^{-1}$, and $d$ is arbitrary ($p$ choice
s). This gives

$$(p-1) \cdot p = p(p-1) \text{ matrices}.$$

**Total:**

$$|H| = p^2(p-1) + p(p-1) = p(p-1)(p + 1) = p(p^2 - 1).$$

This agrees with the homomorphism computation.

---

### Step 4. Summary of the distinct equivalence-class combinations

The $p(p^2-1)$ elements of $H = \mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{F}_p)$ partition into two
 families:

| Family | Condition | Count |
|---|---|---|
| $a \neq 0$ | $a \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$, $b, c \in \mathbb{F}_p$ free, $d = a
^{-1}(1+bc)$ | $p^2(p-1)$ |
| $a = 0$ | $b \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$, $c = -b^{-1}$, $d \in \mathbb{F}_p$ fre
e | $p(p-1)$ |

Together these exhaust all solutions of $ad - bc = 1$ in $\mathbb{F}_p$.

---

### Conclusion

The order of the subgroup $H = \mathrm{SL}(2, p)$ is

$$\boxed{|H| = p(p^2 - 1) = p(p-1)(p+1)}.$$
