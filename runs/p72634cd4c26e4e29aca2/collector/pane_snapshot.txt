**Answer: True.**

**Claim.** Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ be a probability space, $\mat
hcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{F}$ a sub-$\sigma$-algebra, and $\{X_n\}$ a uniformly i
ntegrable family of integrable random variables. Then $\{\mathbb{E}[X_n\mid\math
cal{G}]\}_{n}$ is uniformly integrable.

---

### Proof

Set $Y_n:=\mathbb{E}[\,|X_n|\mid\mathcal{G}\,]$. By conditional Jensen,
$$|\mathbb{E}[X_n\mid\mathcal{G}]|\;\le\;\mathbb{E}[\,|X_n|\mid\mathcal{G}\,]=Y_
n\qquad\text{a.s.}$$
So it suffices to prove $\{Y_n\}$ is UI; a non-negative family dominated a.s. by
 a UI family is itself UI (verified at the end).

**Step 1 — $L^1$-boundedness.** Since $\{X_n\}$ is UI it is $L^1$-bounded; let $
C:=\sup_n\mathbb{E}|X_n|<\infty$. Then
$$\sup_n\mathbb{E}\,Y_n=\sup_n\mathbb{E}|X_n|=C<\infty.$$

**Step 2 — The key estimate.** Because $\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_n>M\}}$ is $\mathcal{G}$
-measurable, we may pull it through the conditional expectation:
$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[Y_n\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_n>M\}}\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathbb{E}\b
igl[|X_n|\mid\mathcal{G}\bigr]\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_n>M\}}\Bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[|X_
n|\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_n>M\}}\bigr].$$
Split $|X_n|$ at a level $K>0$:
$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[|X_n|\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_n>M\}}\bigr]\;\le\;K\,\mathbb{P}(Y_n>M)
\;+\;\mathbb{E}\bigl[|X_n|\,\mathbf{1}_{\{|X_n|>K\}}\bigr].$$
By Markov and Step 1, $\mathbb{P}(Y_n>M)\le \mathbb{E}Y_n/M\le C/M$, so
$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[Y_n\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_n>M\}}\bigr]\;\le\;\frac{KC}{M}\;+\;\sup_
j\mathbb{E}\bigl[|X_j|\,\mathbf{1}_{\{|X_j|>K\}}\bigr].$$
Taking the supremum over $n$:
$$\sup_n\mathbb{E}\bigl[Y_n\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_n>M\}}\bigr]\;\le\;\frac{KC}{M}\;+\
;\sup_j\mathbb{E}\bigl[|X_j|\,\mathbf{1}_{\{|X_j|>K\}}\bigr].\tag{$\ast$}$$

**Step 3 — Letting thresholds go to infinity.** Fix $\varepsilon>0$.

- By uniform integrability of $\{X_n\}$, choose $K$ large enough that
$$\sup_j\mathbb{E}\bigl[|X_j|\,\mathbf{1}_{\{|X_j|>K\}}\bigr]<\tfrac{\varepsilon
}{2}.$$
- With this $K$ fixed, choose $M>\dfrac{2KC}{\varepsilon}$ so that $\dfrac{KC}{M
}<\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2}$.

Then $(\ast)$ gives $\sup_n\mathbb{E}\bigl[Y_n\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_n>M\}}\bigr]<\va
repsilon$. Since $\varepsilon>0$ was arbitrary,
$$\lim_{M\to\infty}\sup_n\mathbb{E}\bigl[Y_n\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_n>M\}}\bigr]=0.$$
Hence $\{Y_n\}$ is uniformly integrable.

**Step 4 — Domination transfers UI.** Let $Z_n:=|\mathbb{E}[X_n\mid\mathcal{G}]|
\le Y_n$ a.s. Then $\{Z_n>M\}\subseteq\{Y_n>M\}$, so
$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[Z_n\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Z_n>M\}}\bigr]\le\mathbb{E}\bigl[Y_n\,\math
bf{1}_{\{Y_n>M\}}\bigr],$$
and taking suprema and the limit $M\to\infty$ shows $\{Z_n\}$, i.e. $\{\mathbb{E
}[X_n\mid\mathcal{G}]\}$, is uniformly integrable. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** The argument uses only (i) Jensen, (ii) the $\mathcal{G}$-measurabil
ity of $\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_n>M\}}$ to pull the indicator inside the conditional exp
ectation, (iii) Markov's inequality, and (iv) the UI hypothesis on $\{X_n\}$. No
 property of $\mathcal{G}$ beyond being a sub-$\sigma$-algebra is required, so t
he result holds for **arbitrary** $\mathcal{G}$.
