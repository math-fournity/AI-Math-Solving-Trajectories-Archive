## Proof

We show that $\Pr[S_n \geq \alpha\,\Sigma_n^2 \text{ infinitely often}] = 0$.

The key tool is **Kolmogorov's Law of the Iterated Logarithm (LIL)** for indepen
dent, non-identically distributed, bounded random variables.

### Kolmogorov's LIL (bounded case)

**Theorem.** Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots$ be independent random variables with $\mathb
b{E}[X_i]=0$, $|X_i| \leq B$ for all $i$, and $s_n^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n \operatornam
e{Var}(X_i) \to \infty$. If $B = o\!\left(s_n / \sqrt{\log\log s_n}\right)$, the
n

$$\limsup_{n\to\infty} \frac{S_n}{\sqrt{2\, s_n^2 \log\log s_n^2}} = 1 \quad \te
xt{a.s.}$$

### Verification of hypotheses

In our setting:

- **Zero mean:** $\mathbb{E}[X_i] = \frac{1}{2}\sigma_i + \frac{1}{2}(-\sigma_i)
 = 0$. ✓
- **Boundedness:** $|X_i| = \sigma_i \leq B$ by condition (a). ✓
- **Variance divergence:** $s_n^2 = \Sigma_n^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n \sigma_i^2$. By co
ndition (b), $\sigma_i > A > 0$ for infinitely many $i$, so $\Sigma_n^2 \geq A^2
 \cdot |\{i \leq n : \sigma_i > A\}| \to \infty$. ✓
- **Kolmogorov condition:** $B \big/ \!\left(\Sigma_n / \sqrt{\log\log \Sigma_n}
\right) = B\sqrt{\log\log \Sigma_n}\,/\,\Sigma_n \to 0$ since $\Sigma_n \to \inf
ty$. ✓

All conditions are satisfied, so the LIL applies.

### Applying the LIL

By the LIL, for almost every $\omega$:

$$\limsup_{n\to\infty} \frac{S_n(\omega)}{\sqrt{2\,\Sigma_n^2 \log\log \Sigma_n^
2}} = 1.$$

Fix $\epsilon > 0$ (say $\epsilon = 1$). Then for a.e. $\omega$, there exists $N
_1(\omega)$ such that for all $n \geq N_1(\omega)$:

$$S_n(\omega) \leq (1+\epsilon)\sqrt{2\,\Sigma_n^2 \log\log \Sigma_n^2}.$$

### Growth-rate comparison

We claim that for all sufficiently large $n$ (deterministically):

$$(1+\epsilon)\sqrt{2\,\Sigma_n^2 \log\log \Sigma_n^2} < \alpha\,\Sigma_n^2.$$

Dividing both sides by $\Sigma_n > 0$, this is equivalent to:

$$(1+\epsilon)\sqrt{2\log\log \Sigma_n^2} < \alpha\,\Sigma_n.$$

Since $\Sigma_n \to \infty$, the right-hand side $\alpha\,\Sigma_n \to \infty$,
while the left-hand side grows as $\sqrt{\log\log \Sigma_n^2} = o(\Sigma_n)$. Th
erefore there exists a deterministic $N_2$ such that for all $n \geq N_2$:

$$(1+\epsilon)\sqrt{2\log\log \Sigma_n^2} < \alpha\,\Sigma_n.$$

### Conclusion

For almost every $\omega$, for all $n \geq \max(N_1(\omega),\, N_2)$:

$$S_n(\omega) \leq (1+\epsilon)\sqrt{2\,\Sigma_n^2 \log\log \Sigma_n^2} < \alpha
\,\Sigma_n^2.$$

Hence the event $\{S_n \geq \alpha\,\Sigma_n^2\}$ occurs only finitely many time
s almost surely. Therefore:

$$\boxed{\Pr\!\left[S_n \geq \alpha\,(\Sigma_n)^2 \;\text{infinitely often}\righ
t] = 0.}$$

**Remark on the role of $\alpha$.** The argument works for any $\alpha > 0$ (not
 just $\alpha < 1$): the threshold $\alpha\,\Sigma_n^2$ grows as $\Sigma_n^2$, w
hile the LIL bounds $S_n$ by $O(\Sigma_n\sqrt{\log\log \Sigma_n^2})$, which is $
o(\Sigma_n^2)$. The upper bound $\alpha < 1$ is not needed for the result; any f
ixed $\alpha > 0$ suffices.
