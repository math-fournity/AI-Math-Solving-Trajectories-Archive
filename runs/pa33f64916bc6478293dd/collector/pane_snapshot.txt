# Solution

**Answer: No.** The fibers of a surjective polynomial submersion $\mathbb{C}^n \
to \mathbb{C}$ need not all be homeomorphic.

## Counterexample

Consider the polynomial map
$$f : \mathbb{C}^2 \to \mathbb{C}, \qquad f(x,y) = x + x^2 y.$$

### Step 1: $f$ is a submersion

The partial derivatives are
$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 1 + 2xy, \qquad \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}
= x^2.$$

For both to vanish simultaneously we need $x^2 = 0$, hence $x = 0$, but then $\f
rac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 1 \neq 0$. So $\nabla f$ never vanishes, and $f$ i
s a submersion.

### Step 2: $f$ is surjective

For any $t \in \mathbb{C}$, the point $(t, 0)$ satisfies $f(t,0) = t$. Hence $f$
 is surjective.

### Step 3: The fiber $f^{-1}(0)$ is disconnected

We solve $x + x^2 y = 0$, i.e., $x(1 + xy) = 0$. This gives two cases:

- **$x = 0$**: the entire $y$-axis $\{(0, y) : y \in \mathbb{C}\} \cong \mathbb{
C}$.
- **$xy = -1$**: the hyperbola $\{(x, -1/x) : x \in \mathbb{C}^*\} \cong \mathbb
{C}^*$.

These two components are disjoint (the first has $x = 0$, the second has $x \neq
 0$). Therefore
$$f^{-1}(0) \;=\; \mathbb{C} \;\sqcup\; \mathbb{C}^*,$$
which is **disconnected** (two connected components).

### Step 4: The fiber $f^{-1}(t)$ for $t \neq 0$ is connected

For $t \neq 0$, the equation $x(1 + xy) = t$ forces $x \neq 0$ (since $t \neq 0$
). Then $y = (t - x)/x^2$, and the fiber is
$$f^{-1}(t) = \left\{\left(x,\, \frac{t-x}{x^2}\right) : x \in \mathbb{C}^*\righ
t\} \;\cong\; \mathbb{C}^*,$$
which is **connected** (a single irreducible component).

### Step 5: Conclusion

The fiber $f^{-1}(0)$ is disconnected while every fiber $f^{-1}(t)$ with $t \neq
 0$ is connected. A disconnected space cannot be homeomorphic to a connected one
. Therefore the fibers of this surjective polynomial submersion are **not** all
homeomorphic.

**Remark.** The obstruction here is at infinity: $f$ has no critical points, but
 $0$ is an *atypical value at infinity*. The generic fiber $\mathbb{C}^*$ "split
s" into $\mathbb{C} \sqcup \mathbb{C}^*$ at the special value, exhibiting non-tr
ivial monodromy-at-infinity. This shows that the Ehresmann-type fibration theore
m fails for polynomial submersions without a properness/coercivity condition (su
ch as the Broughton condition $\|\nabla f\| \to \infty$ as $\|x\| \to \infty$).
