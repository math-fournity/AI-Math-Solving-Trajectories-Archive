## Proof

**Yes**, such continuous curves always exist.

**Step 1 (Gram–Schmidt reduction).** Since $v_1(t),\dots,v_k(t)$ are linearly in
dependent for every $t\in I$, we apply the Gram–Schmidt process pointwise. This
uses only continuous operations (inner products, norms, subtractions, division b
y nonzero norms), so we obtain continuous curves $e_1,\dots,e_k:I\to\mathbb{R}^n
$ with $e_1(t),\dots,e_k(t)$ orthonormal for each $t$.

**Step 2 (Fibration setup).** Let $V_k(\mathbb{R}^n)$ be the Stiefel manifold of
 ordered orthonormal $k$-frames and $O(n)$ the orthogonal group. The map
$$\pi:O(n)\to V_k(\mathbb{R}^n),\qquad A\mapsto\text{(first }k\text{ columns of
}A)$$
is a fiber bundle with fiber $O(n-k)$. Since $V_k(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is a compact ma
nifold (hence paracompact Hausdorff), $\pi$ is a Serre fibration: it satisfies t
he homotopy lifting property for CW complexes.

The orthonormal frame $(e_1,\dots,e_k)$ defines a continuous map $f:I\to V_k(\ma
thbb{R}^n)$.

**Step 3 (Lifting via contractibility of $I$).** The interval $I=(a,b)$ is conve
x, hence contractible. Fix $t_0\in I$ and choose $A_0\in O(n)$ with $\pi(A_0)=f(
t_0)$ (i.e., the first $k$ columns of $A_0$ are $e_1(t_0),\dots,e_k(t_0)$).

Define a homotopy $H:I\times[0,1]\to V_k(\mathbb{R}^n)$ by
$$H(t,s)=f\bigl((1-s)\,t_0+s\,t\bigr).$$
This is well-defined since $I$ is convex, and satisfies $H(t,0)=f(t_0)$ (constan
t) and $H(t,1)=f(t)$.

The constant map $\widetilde{H}_0(t)=A_0$ lifts $H(\cdot,0)=f(t_0)$. By the homo
topy lifting property of the Serre fibration $\pi$ (applicable since $I\times[0,
1]$ is a CW complex), there exists a continuous lift $\widetilde{H}:I\times[0,1]
\to O(n)$ with $\pi\circ\widetilde{H}=H$ and $\widetilde{H}(t,0)=A_0$.

**Step 4 (Conclusion).** Set $\widetilde{f}(t)=\widetilde{H}(t,1)$. Then $\widet
ilde{f}:I\to O(n)$ is continuous and $\pi(\widetilde{f}(t))=f(t)$, so the first
$k$ columns of $\widetilde{f}(t)$ are $e_1(t),\dots,e_k(t)$. Let $e_{k+1}(t),\do
ts,e_n(t)$ be the remaining $n-k$ columns. These are continuous curves, and for
each $t$, the vectors $e_1(t),\dots,e_n(t)$ form an orthonormal basis of $\mathb
b{R}^n$.

Since $e_1(t),\dots,e_k(t)$ span the same subspace as $v_1(t),\dots,v_k(t)$ at e
ach $t$, the collection
$$v_1(t),\dots,v_k(t),\,e_{k+1}(t),\dots,e_n(t)$$
is a basis of $\mathbb{R}^n$ for every $t\in I$. Taking $v_i=e_i$ for $i=k+1,\do
ts,n$ gives the desired extension. $\blacksquare$
