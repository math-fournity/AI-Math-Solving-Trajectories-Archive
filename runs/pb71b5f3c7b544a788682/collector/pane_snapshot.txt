## Solution

We are given
$$a_p = \sum_{k=0}^{p}(-1)^k\binom{p}{k}\frac{1}{(k+2)(k+4)}.$$

**Step 1: Evaluate $a_p$ in closed form.**

Using partial fractions on the denominator:
$$\frac{1}{(k+2)(k+4)} = \frac{1}{2}\!\left(\frac{1}{k+2}-\frac{1}{k+4}\right).$
$

We use the standard identity (obtained via the Beta integral $\int_0^1 x^{m-1}(1
-x)^p\,dx$):
$$\sum_{k=0}^{p}(-1)^k\binom{p}{k}\frac{1}{k+m}=\frac{(m-1)!\,p!}{(p+m)!}.$$

Applying with $m=2$ and $m=4$:
$$\sum_{k=0}^{p}(-1)^k\binom{p}{k}\frac{1}{k+2}=\frac{1!\,p!}{(p+2)!}=\frac{1}{(
p+1)(p+2)},$$
$$\sum_{k=0}^{p}(-1)^k\binom{p}{k}\frac{1}{k+4}=\frac{3!\,p!}{(p+4)!}=\frac{6}{(
p+1)(p+2)(p+3)(p+4)}.$$

Therefore
$$a_p=\frac{1}{2}\!\left(\frac{1}{(p+1)(p+2)}-\frac{6}{(p+1)(p+2)(p+3)(p+4)}\rig
ht)=\frac{1}{2(p+1)(p+2)}\cdot\frac{(p+3)(p+4)-6}{(p+3)(p+4)}.$$

Since $(p+3)(p+4)-6=p^2+7p+6=(p+1)(p+6)$, we get
$$a_p=\frac{p+6}{2(p+2)(p+3)(p+4)}.$$

*Checks:* $a_0=\frac{6}{2\cdot2\cdot3\cdot4}=\frac18=\frac{1}{2\cdot4}$ ✓; $a_1=
\frac{7}{2\cdot3\cdot4\cdot5}=\frac{7}{120}=\frac18-\frac{1}{15}$ ✓.

**Step 2: Partial fractions of $a_p$.**

Decomposing $\frac{p+6}{(p+2)(p+3)(p+4)}$:
$$\frac{p+6}{(p+2)(p+3)(p+4)}=\frac{2}{p+2}-\frac{3}{p+3}+\frac{1}{p+4},$$
verified by $p=-2\Rightarrow A=2$, $p=-3\Rightarrow B=-3$, $p=-4\Rightarrow C=1$
.

Thus
$$a_p=\frac{1}{2}\!\left(\frac{2}{p+2}-\frac{3}{p+3}+\frac{1}{p+4}\right).$$

**Step 3: Sum the series.**

Let $S_n=\sum_{p=0}^{n}a_p=\frac12\,T_n$ where
$$T_n=2\!\sum_{p=0}^{n}\frac{1}{p+2}-3\!\sum_{p=0}^{n}\frac{1}{p+3}+\sum_{p=0}^{
n}\frac{1}{p+4}.$$

Writing in terms of harmonic numbers $H_m$:
$$T_n=2(H_{n+2}-1)-3\!\left(H_{n+3}-\tfrac32\right)+\!\left(H_{n+4}-\tfrac{11}{6
}\right)=2H_{n+2}-3H_{n+3}+H_{n+4}+\tfrac23.$$

For the harmonic part, using $H_{n+3}=H_{n+2}+\frac{1}{n+3}$ and $H_{n+4}=H_{n+3
}+\frac{1}{n+4}$:
$$2H_{n+2}-3H_{n+3}+H_{n+4}=-H_{n+2}+H_{n+3}-\frac{3}{n+3}+\frac{1}{n+4}=\frac{1
}{n+3}-\frac{3}{n+3}+\frac{1}{n+4}=-\frac{2}{n+3}+\frac{1}{n+4}.$$

As $n\to\infty$, this tends to $0$, so
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}T_n=\frac{2}{3},\qquad \lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{p=0}^{n}a_p=\fr
ac12\cdot\frac{2}{3}=\boxed{\frac{1}{3}}.$$
