Looking at this problem, I need to find the idempotent elements of $R = \mathbb{
Z}[i] \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}[i]$.

## Setting Up the Ring Structure

Since $\mathbb{Z}[i] \cong \mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^2+1)$, the tensor product is:
$$R \cong \mathbb{Z}[x,y]/(x^2+1,\, y^2+1) \cong \mathbb{Z}[i][y]/(y^2+1)$$

where $x$ is the first copy of $i$ and $y$ is the second.

## Fiber Product Decomposition

Over $\mathbb{Z}[i]$, factor $y^2+1 = (y-i)(y+i)$. Set $I = (y-i)$, $J = (y+i)$
in $\mathbb{Z}[i][y]$.

**Key fact:** $\mathbb{Z}[i][y]$ is a UFD, and $y-i$, $y+i$ are distinct irreduc
ibles (since $i \neq -i$ in $\mathbb{Z}[i]$, differing by $2i$ which is not a un
it). So:
$$I \cap J = (y-i)(y+i) = (y^2+1) = IJ$$

Since $I \cap J = IJ$, the natural map $R \to R/I \times R/J$ is **injective**,
with:
$$R \hookrightarrow R/I \times R/J \cong \mathbb{Z}[i] \times \mathbb{Z}[i]$$

The image is $\ker(\psi)$ where $\psi: \mathbb{Z}[i] \times \mathbb{Z}[i] \to R/
(I+J)$ sends $(a,b) \mapsto a - b$.

Computing $I + J = (y-i,\, y+i) = (y-i,\, 2i) = (y-i,\, 2)$ (since $i$ is a unit
), so:
$$R/(I+J) \cong \mathbb{Z}[i]/(2)$$

Therefore:
$$R \cong \{(a, b) \in \mathbb{Z}[i] \times \mathbb{Z}[i] : a \equiv b \pmod{2}\
}$$

**Verification of the image:** An element $f(y) = \alpha + \beta y \in R$ (with
$\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{Z}[i]$, using $y^2=-1$) maps to $(\alpha + \beta i,\;
 \alpha - \beta i)$. The difference is $2\beta i \in (2)$, confirming $a \equiv
b \pmod{2}$. Conversely, given $(u,v)$ with $u \equiv v \pmod 2$, set $\alpha =
(u+v)/2$, $\beta = (u-v)/(2i)$; both lie in $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ since $u-v \in (2) =
 (2i)$ (as $i$ is a unit). In $\mathbb{Z}[i]/(2)$ we have $2=0$ so $v = -v$, mea
ning $u \equiv v \pmod 2$ iff $u \equiv -v \pmod 2$, so both conditions coincide
. The image is exactly the congruence condition above.

## Counting Idempotents

Since $R$ injects into $\mathbb{Z}[i] \times \mathbb{Z}[i]$, every idempotent of
 $R$ maps to an idempotent of $\mathbb{Z}[i] \times \mathbb{Z}[i]$ lying in the
image.

**Idempotents of $\mathbb{Z}[i]$:** Since $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ is an integral domain,
 the only idempotents are $0$ and $1$.

**Idempotents of $\mathbb{Z}[i] \times \mathbb{Z}[i]$:** These are $(0,0),\; (1,
0),\; (0,1),\; (1,1)$.

**Filtering by the congruence condition $a \equiv b \pmod{2}$:**

| $(a,b)$ | $a - b$ | $\in (2)$? |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| $(0,0)$ | $0$ | ✓ |
| $(1,1)$ | $0$ | ✓ |
| $(1,0)$ | $1$ | ✗ ($1 \notin (2)$) |
| $(0,1)$ | $-1$ | ✗ ($-1 \notin (2)$) |

Only $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$ survive — these correspond to $0$ and $1$ in $R$.

## Conclusion

The ring $\mathbb{Z}[i] \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}[i]$ has exactly **2** id
empotent elements.
