## Proof

Let $t = \cot^2 x$. As $x \to 0$, we have $\cot x \to \infty$, so $t \to \infty$
. Also $\tan^2 x = \dfrac{1}{\cot^2 x} = \dfrac{1}{t}$.

The inner limit becomes:
$$\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{\left(1^t+2^t+3^t+\cdots+n^t\right)^{1/t}}.$$

**Evaluating the inner limit.** Factor out $n^t$ from the sum:
$$1^t+2^t+\cdots+n^t = n^t\left[\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^t+\left(\frac{2}{n}\rig
ht)^t+\cdots+\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)^t+1\right].$$

Denote the bracketed sum as $S(t) = \sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)^t$. S
ince $0 < \frac{k}{n} < 1$ for $1 \le k \le n-1$, each term $\left(\frac{k}{n}\r
ight)^t \to 0$ as $t\to\infty$, while the last term ($k=n$) equals $1$. Therefor
e:
$$\lim_{t\to\infty} S(t) = 1.$$

Now:
$$\left(1^t+2^t+\cdots+n^t\right)^{1/t} = n\cdot S(t)^{1/t}.$$

Since $S(t) \to 1 > 0$, we have $S(t)^{1/t} \to 1$ as $t\to\infty$ (because $\ln
 S(t) \to 0$ and $\frac{\ln S(t)}{t}\to 0$). Hence:
$$\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(1^t+2^t+\cdots+n^t\right)^{1/t} = n\cdot 1 = n.$$

This is the well-known fact that the $\ell^p$-norm of the vector $(1,2,\ldots,n)
$ converges to the $\ell^\infty$-norm $n$ as $p\to\infty$.

Therefore the inner limit is:
$$\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{1}{\left(1^{\cot^2 x}+2^{\cot^2 x}+\cdots+n^{\cot^2 x}\righ
t)^{\tan^2 x}} = \frac{1}{n}.$$

**Evaluating the outer limit.** Now:
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n} = 0.$$
