## Solution

**Step 1: Compute the divergence of F.**

Given $F(x,y,z) = (2x+y-z,\; x-2y+z,\; x+y-2z)$,

$$\nabla \cdot F = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(2x+y-z) + \frac{\partial}{\partia
l y}(x-2y+z) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(x+y-2z) = 2 - 2 - 2 = -2.$$

**Step 2: Apply the Divergence Theorem.**

The surface $S:\; |x+y-z| + |x-2y+z| + |x+y-2z| = 2$ is closed (it bounds a boun
ded region $V$). By the Divergence Theorem, the outward flux is

$$\iint_S F \cdot n\, dS = \iiint_V (\nabla \cdot F)\, dV = -2 \cdot \operatorna
me{Vol}(V).$$

**Step 3: Compute the enclosed volume via a linear change of variables.**

Set
$$u = x+y-z,\quad v = x-2y+z,\quad w = x+y-2z.$$

The region $V$ becomes $\Omega = \{(u,v,w): |u|+|v|+|w| \le 2\}$, a regular octa
hedron. The transformation is linear with matrix

$$M = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & -1 \\ 1 & -2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Its determinant is

$$\det M = 1\cdot((-2)(-2)-1\cdot1) - 1\cdot(1\cdot(-2)-1\cdot1) + (-1)\cdot(1\c
dot1-(-2)\cdot1)$$
$$= 1\cdot 3 - 1\cdot(-3) - 1\cdot 3 = 3 + 3 - 3 = 3.$$

The octahedron $|u|+|v|+|w|\le a$ has volume $\frac{4}{3}a^3$. With $a=2$:

$$\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega) = \tfrac{4}{3}\cdot 8 = \tfrac{32}{3}.$$

Converting back to $(x,y,z)$-space using $dV_{xyz} = \frac{1}{|\det M|}\,dV_{uvw
} = \frac{1}{3}\,dV_{uvw}$:

$$\operatorname{Vol}(V) = \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{32}{3} = \frac{32}{9}.$$

**Step 4: Compute the flux.**

$$\iint_S F \cdot n\, dS = -2 \cdot \frac{32}{9} = -\frac{64}{9}.$$

The flux of $F$ through the surface (with outward orientation) is $\boxed{-\dfra
c{64}{9}}$.
