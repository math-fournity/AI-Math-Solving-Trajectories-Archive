**Remark on the statement.** As written, the claim "there are exactly two groups
 of order $pq$" is **false** without an additional hypothesis. For example, $15
= 3 \cdot 5$ with $3 < 5$ and $3 \nmid (5-1)=4$; there is only **one** group of
order 15 (the cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}_{15}$). The correct theorem is:

> **Theorem.** Let $p < q$ be distinct primes. If $p \mid (q-1)$, there are exac
tly two groups of order $pq$ up to isomorphism; if $p \nmid (q-1)$, there is exa
ctly one.

I prove the full classification below; the "exactly two" case is the case $p \mi
d (q-1)$.

---

## Proof

Let $p < q$ be distinct primes and let $G$ be a group of order $pq$.

### Step 1. The Sylow $q$-subgroup is normal

Let $n_q$ be the number of Sylow $q$-subgroups. By the Sylow theorems:
$$n_q \equiv 1 \pmod{q}, \qquad n_q \mid p.$$
Since $p < q$, the only divisor of $p$ that is $\equiv 1 \pmod{q}$ is $1$ (as $p
 < q$ forces $n_q = 1$). So $n_q = 1$: the Sylow $q$-subgroup $Q \cong \mathbb{Z
}_q$ is **normal** in $G$.

### Step 2. $G$ is a semidirect product

Let $P$ be a Sylow $p$-subgroup, $P \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$. Since $Q \trianglelefte
q G$ and $Q \cap P = \{e\}$ (orders are coprime) and $|QP| = |Q||P|/|Q\cap P| =
qp = |G|$, we have
$$G = Q \rtimes_\varphi P$$
for some homomorphism $\varphi : P \to \operatorname{Aut}(Q)$.

### Step 3. Classify the homomorphisms $\varphi$

We have $\operatorname{Aut}(Q) \cong \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_q) \cong \mat
hbb{Z}_q^\times \cong \mathbb{Z}_{q-1}$, a cyclic group of order $q - 1$.

A homomorphism $\varphi : \mathbb{Z}_p \to \mathbb{Z}_{q-1}$ is determined by th
e image of a generator of $\mathbb{Z}_p$, which must be an element of $\mathbb{Z
}_{q-1}$ whose order divides $p$. Since $p$ is prime, the image is either:
- **trivial** (the identity), giving the **direct product** $\mathbb{Z}_q \times
 \mathbb{Z}_p \cong \mathbb{Z}_{pq}$ (cyclic, since $\gcd(p,q)=1$), or
- **of order $p$**, which exists **if and only if** $p \mid (q-1)$.

### Step 4. The case $p \nmid (q-1)$: exactly one group

If $p \nmid (q-1)$, then $\mathbb{Z}_{q-1}$ has no element of order $p$, so the
only homomorphism is trivial. Hence $G \cong \mathbb{Z}_q \times \mathbb{Z}_p \c
ong \mathbb{Z}_{pq}$. **There is exactly one group of order $pq$.**

### Step 5. The case $p \mid (q-1)$: non-trivial semidirect products exist

If $p \mid (q-1)$, then $\mathbb{Z}_{q-1}$ has a unique subgroup of order $p$, g
enerated by some element $\sigma$ of order $p$. A non-trivial $\varphi$ sends a
generator of $P$ to an element of order $p$ in $\operatorname{Aut}(Q)$, i.e., to
 $\sigma^k$ for $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, p-1\}$. This gives a non-abelian group
$$G_k = \langle a, b \mid a^q = b^p = e,\; b a b^{-1} = a^{\sigma^k} \rangle,$$
where $a$ generates $Q$ and $b$ generates $P$.

### Step 6. All non-trivial semidirect products are isomorphic

We show $G_k \cong G_1$ for every $k \in \{1, \dots, p-1\}$.

Two semidirect products $\mathbb{Z}_q \rtimes_{\varphi} \mathbb{Z}_p$ and $\math
bb{Z}_q \rtimes_{\varphi'} \mathbb{Z}_p$ are isomorphic if there exist automorph
isms $\alpha \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ and $\beta \in \operatorname{
Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_q)$ such that
$$\varphi' = \beta \circ \varphi \circ \alpha^{-1} \circ \beta^{-1}.$$
Since $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_q) \cong \mathbb{Z}_{q-1}$ is **abelian**,
conjugation by $\beta$ is trivial, so the condition reduces to $\varphi' = \varp
hi \circ \alpha^{-1}$ for some $\alpha \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$.

Now $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_p) \cong \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ acts on the set
 of non-trivial homomorphisms $\mathbb{Z}_p \to \mathbb{Z}_{q-1}$ by precomposit
ion. The non-trivial homomorphisms correspond to the $p - 1$ generators $\sigma,
 \sigma^2, \dots, \sigma^{p-1}$ of the unique subgroup $\langle \sigma \rangle \
leq \mathbb{Z}_{q-1}$ of order $p$. The automorphism $\alpha \in \operatorname{A
ut}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ sending the generator $b \mapsto b^m$ (for $\gcd(m,p)=1$) tra
nsforms $\varphi$ (which sends $b \mapsto \sigma^k$) into $\varphi'$ (which send
s $b \mapsto \sigma^{km}$). As $m$ ranges over $\{1, \dots, p-1\}$, the exponent
 $km$ ranges over all of $\{1, \dots, p-1\}$ (mod $p$). Hence $\operatorname{Aut
}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ acts **transitively** on the non-trivial homomorphisms.

Therefore all $p - 1$ non-trivial semidirect products are isomorphic to a single
 non-abelian group $G^*$.

### Step 7. The two groups are non-isomorphic

The direct product $\mathbb{Z}_{pq}$ is abelian; the non-trivial semidirect prod
uct $G^*$ is non-abelian (since $\varphi$ is non-trivial, $bab^{-1} = a^{\sigma}
 \neq a$). So they are not isomorphic.

### Conclusion

- **If $p \nmid (q-1)$**: exactly **one** group of order $pq$, namely $\mathbb{Z
}_{pq}$.
- **If $p \mid (q-1)$**: exactly **two** groups of order $pq$, namely $\mathbb{Z
}_{pq}$ and the non-abelian semidirect product $\mathbb{Z}_q \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_
p$.

In particular, **there are exactly two groups of order $pq$ (up to isomorphism)
if and only if $p \mid (q-1)$** (with $p < q$). $\blacksquare$
