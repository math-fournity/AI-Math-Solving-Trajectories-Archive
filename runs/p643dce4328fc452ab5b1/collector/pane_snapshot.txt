## Solution

We evaluate
$$I = \int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-(x+y)}}{x+y}\,dx\,dy.$$

**Change of variables.** Let
$$s = x+y, \qquad t = x,$$
so that $x = t$ and $y = s - t$. The Jacobian is
$$\left|\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(s,t)}\right| = \left|\det\begin{pmatrix} 0
 & 1 \\ 1 & -1\end{pmatrix}\right| = |0\cdot(-1) - 1\cdot 1| = 1.$$

**Region.** The original domain is $x \ge 0,\; y \ge 0$, i.e. $t \ge 0$ and $s -
 t \ge 0$, which gives $0 \le t \le s$ with $s \ge 0$.

**Transformed integral.**
$$I = \int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{s} \frac{e^{-s}}{s}\,dt\,ds = \int_{0}^{\in
fty} \frac{e^{-s}}{s}\left(\int_{0}^{s} dt\right)ds = \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{e^
{-s}}{s}\cdot s\,ds = \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-s}\,ds.$$

**Convergence justification.** The inner $t$-integration removes the apparent si
ngularity at $s=0$: the integrand $\frac{e^{-s}}{s}\cdot s = e^{-s}$ is bounded
near $s=0$, and decays exponentially as $s\to\infty$. Equivalently, the original
 integrand $\frac{e^{-(x+y)}}{x+y}$ is locally integrable near the origin (in po
lar coordinates $x=r\cos\theta, y=r\sin\theta$ the measure contributes an $r$ th
at cancels the $1/r$-type singularity), so Fubini–Tonelli applies to the non-neg
ative integrand and the change of variables is legitimate.

**Final evaluation.**
$$I = \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-s}\,ds = \left[-e^{-s}\right]_{0}^{\infty} = 0 - (-1
) = 1.$$

$$\boxed{I = 1}$$
