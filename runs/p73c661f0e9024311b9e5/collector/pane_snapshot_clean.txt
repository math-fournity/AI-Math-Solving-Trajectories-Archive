## Solution

**Answer:** The one-point compactification of $S^n \times \mathbb{R}$ is the wed
ge sum
$$S^{n+1} \vee S^1,$$
equivalently, $S^{n+1}$ with two distinct points identified.

---

### Step 1. $S^n \times \mathbb{R} \cong S^{n+1}\setminus\{N,S\}$

View $S^{n+1}\subset \mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times \mathbb{R}$ as $\{(u,s): \|u\|^2+s^2
=1\}$, with north pole $N=(0,1)$ and south pole $S=(0,-1)$. For any point with $
s\in(-1,1)$ we have $u\neq 0$, so the map
$$\varphi: S^{n+1}\setminus\{N,S\}\longrightarrow S^n\times(-1,1),\qquad (u,s)\m
apsto \Big(\frac{u}{\|u\|},\, s\Big)$$
is a homeomorphism (its inverse sends $(v,t)\mapsto (\sqrt{1-t^2}\,v,\, t)$). Si
nce $(-1,1)\cong\mathbb{R}$,
$$S^n\times\mathbb{R}\;\cong\;S^{n+1}\setminus\{N,S\}.$$

### Step 2. The one-point compactification is $S^{n+1}/\{N\sim S\}$

$S^n\times\mathbb{R}$ is locally compact, Hausdorff, and non-compact (product of
 compact $S^n$ with non-compact $\mathbb{R}$), so its one-point compactification
 $X^+=X\cup\{\infty\}$ is a compact Hausdorff space.

Let $Y=S^{n+1}/\{N\sim S\}$ be the quotient of $S^{n+1}$ collapsing $\{N,S\}$ to
 a single point $\ast$ (Hausdorff, since we collapse a compact subset of a compa
ct Hausdorff space). Define
$$f: X^+ \longrightarrow Y,\qquad f(x)=[x]\;\;(x\in X),\quad f(\infty)=\ast.$$
This is a bijection. It is continuous at points of $X$ (where it is just the inc
lusion $S^{n+1}\setminus\{N,S\}\hookrightarrow S^{n+1}$ followed by the quotient
 map). For continuity at $\infty$: a neighborhood of $\ast$ in $Y$ is the image
under the quotient map of $U_N\cup U_S$, where $U_N, U_S$ are neighborhoods of $
N, S$ in $S^{n+1}$. Its preimage under $f$ is
$$\{\infty\}\cup\bigl((U_N\cup U_S)\setminus\{N,S\}\bigr)=\{\infty\}\cup\bigl(X\
setminus K\bigr),$$
where $K=S^{n+1}\setminus(U_N\cup U_S)$ is compact in $S^{n+1}$, disjoint from $
\{N,S\}$, hence compact in $X$. This is exactly a basic neighborhood of $\infty$
 in the one-point compactification. So $f$ is continuous, hence a homeomorphism
(compact $\to$ Hausdorff). Therefore
$$\bigl(S^n\times\mathbb{R}\bigr)^+ \;\cong\; S^{n+1}/\{N\sim S\}.$$

### Step 3. $S^{n+1}/\{N\sim S\}\cong S^{n+1}\vee S^1$

This is the standard fact that identifying two points of a sphere of dimension $
\geq 2$ yields the wedge of that sphere with a circle. Here is the argument.

Let $\gamma$ be an embedded arc in $S^{n+1}$ from $N$ to $S$ (e.g. a meridian).
A closed tubular neighborhood $T$ of $\gamma$ is a closed $(n+1)$-ball ($\gamma$
 is a 1-disk, its normal bundle is trivial $\mathbb{R}^n$, so $T\cong D^{n+1}$),
 with $\gamma$ a diameter and $N,S\in\partial T$. The complement $B=S^{n+1}\setm
inus\operatorname{int}T$ is also a closed $(n+1)$-ball, with $\partial B=\partia
l T=S^n$.

In the quotient $S^{n+1}/\{N\sim S\}$, the arc $\gamma$ (whose endpoints are ide
ntified) becomes a circle $\bar\gamma\cong S^1$. The ball $T$ with its two bound
ary points $N,S$ identified deformation-retracts (as a based space) onto $\bar\g
amma$ together with a collar of its boundary; concretely $T/\{N\sim S\}\cong D^{
n+1}\vee S^1$, where the $D^{n+1}$ factor is a ball whose boundary is $\partial
T/\{N\sim S\}$ and the $S^1$ factor is $\bar\gamma$, wedged at $\ast=[N]=[S]$. G
luing the complementary ball $B$ back along this boundary attaches an $(n+1)$-ba
ll to the $D^{n+1}$ factor along their common $S^n$-boundary, producing $S^{n+1}
$, while leaving the $S^1$ factor attached at the single point $\ast$. Thus
$$S^{n+1}/\{N\sim S\}\;\cong\;S^{n+1}\vee S^1.$$

*(Consistency check via homology.)* The cofibration $S^0=\{N,S\}\hookrightarrow
S^{n+1}\to S^{n+1}/\{N\sim S\}$ gives, for $n\geq 1$,
$$\widetilde H_k\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}&k=1,\\ \mathbb{Z}&k=n+1,\\ 0&\text{
otherwise,}\end{cases}$$
matching $\widetilde H_*(S^{n+1}\vee S^1)$. For $n=0$, $S^0\times\mathbb{R}=\mat
hbb{R}\sqcup\mathbb{R}$, whose one-point compactification is two circles sharing
 the added point, i.e. $S^1\vee S^1=S^1\vee S^1$, agreeing with $S^{0+1}\vee S^1
$.

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{\bigl(S^n\times\mathbb{R}\bigr)^+\;\cong\;S^{n+1}\vee S^1}$$
