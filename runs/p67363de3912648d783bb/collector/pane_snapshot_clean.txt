## Solution

We evaluate $S = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{f(n)}{n(n+1)}$, where $f(n)$ is the nu
mber of 1s in the binary representation of $n$.

**Step 1: Integral representation.** Since
$$\frac{1}{n(n+1)} = \frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{n+1} = \int_0^1 x^{n-1}(1-x)\,dx,$$
we get
$$S = \int_0^1 (1-x)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} f(n)\,x^{n-1}\,dx.$$

**Step 2: Generating function of $f(n)$.** Write $f(n)=\sum_{k\ge 0} b_k(n)$, wh
ere $b_k(n)$ is the $k$-th bit of $n$. Then
$$\sum_{n\ge 0} b_k(n)\,x^n = \sum_{j\ge 0}\sum_{r=2^k}^{2^{k+1}-1} x^{j\cdot 2^
{k+1}+r} = \frac{x^{2^k}(1-x^{2^k})}{(1-x)(1-x^{2^{k+1}})} = \frac{x^{2^k}}{(1-x
)(1+x^{2^k})}.$$
Summing over $k$ (and using $f(0)=0$):
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} f(n)\,x^{n-1} = \frac{1}{x(1-x)}\sum_{k\ge 0}\frac{x^{2^k}
}{1+x^{2^k}}.$$

**Step 3: Substitute.** The factor $(1-x)$ cancels:
$$S = \int_0^1 \frac{1}{x}\sum_{k\ge 0}\frac{x^{2^k}}{1+x^{2^k}}\,dx = \sum_{k\g
e 0}\int_0^1 \frac{x^{2^k-1}}{1+x^{2^k}}\,dx.$$
(The interchange is justified by Tonelli's theorem, since all terms are non-nega
tive.)

**Step 4: Evaluate each integral.** With $u=x^{2^k}$, $du = 2^k x^{2^k-1}dx$:
$$\int_0^1 \frac{x^{2^k-1}}{1+x^{2^k}}\,dx = \frac{1}{2^k}\int_0^1\frac{du}{1+u}
 = \frac{\ln 2}{2^k}.$$

**Step 5: Sum.**
$$S = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln 2}{2^k} = \ln 2\cdot\frac{1}{1-1/2} = 2\ln 2.
$$

$$\boxed{S = 2\ln 2}$$
