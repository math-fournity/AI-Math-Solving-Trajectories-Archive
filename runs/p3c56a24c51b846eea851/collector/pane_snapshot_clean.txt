## Setup

We seek a Green's function in the half-space \(z>0\) for the Helmholtz (reduced
wave) equation
\[
(\nabla^2+k^2)\,G(x,y,z,\xi,\eta,\zeta)=-\delta(x-\xi)\delta(y-\eta)\delta(z-\ze
ta),
\qquad z>0,\ \zeta>0,
\]
satisfying the Sommerfeld radiation condition at infinity and one of the boundar
y conditions on the plane \(z=0\):
\[
\text{(a) Dirichlet: } G\big|_{z=0}=0, \qquad
\text{(b) Neumann: } \frac{\partial G}{\partial z}\Big|_{z=0}=0.
\]

The free-space fundamental solution is
\[
g(P,Q)=\frac{e^{-ikr}}{4\pi r},\qquad r=|P-Q|=\sqrt{(x-\xi)^2+(y-\eta)^2+(z-\zet
a)^2},
\]
which satisfies \((\nabla^2+k^2)g=-\delta(P-Q)\) and the radiation condition. We
 write \(G=g+v\), where \(v\) must be **regular** (no singularity) in \(z>0\) an
d must be chosen so that the boundary condition holds. The **method of images**
constructs \(v\) as the field of a fictitious image source placed at the mirror
point \(Q_1=(\xi,\eta,-\zeta)\), located in the *opposite* half-space \(z<0\), h
ence outside the domain. The image field
\[
g_1(P,Q_1)=\frac{e^{-ikr_1}}{4\pi r_1},\qquad
r_1=|P-Q_1|=\sqrt{(x-\xi)^2+(y-\eta)^2+(z+\zeta)^2}
\]
is smooth and satisfies \((\nabla^2+k^2)g_1=0\) for \(z>0\) (its singularity lie
s at \(z=-\zeta<0\)). Thus any linear combination \(g\pm g_1\) has the correct s
ingularity at \(Q\) and a regular remainder \(v=\mp g_1\).

## (a) Dirichlet condition \(G|_{z=0}=0\)

Take the image with the **opposite** sign:
\[
\boxed{\;G(P,Q)=\frac{e^{-ikr}}{4\pi r}-\frac{e^{-ikr_1}}{4\pi r_1}\;},\qquad v=
-\frac{e^{-ikr_1}}{4\pi r_1}.
\]

**Boundary condition.** On \(z=0\),
\[
r\big|_{z=0}=\sqrt{(x-\xi)^2+(y-\eta)^2+\zeta^2}=r_1\big|_{z=0},
\]
so the two terms cancel:
\[
G\big|_{z=0}=\frac{e^{-ikr}}{4\pi r}-\frac{e^{-ikr}}{4\pi r}=0.\quad\checkmark
\]

**Equation and regularity.** \(g\) carries the singularity: \((\nabla^2+k^2)g=-\
delta(P-Q)\). The image term \(v=-g_1\) has its pole at \(Q_1=(\xi,\eta,-\zeta)\
notin\{z>0\}\), hence \((\nabla^2+k^2)v=0\) in the domain; it is the required re
gular solution. Both terms obey the radiation condition, so \(G\) is the Dirichl
et Green's function.

**Integral representation (first boundary-value problem).** Let \(u\) solve \((\
nabla^2+k^2)u=0\) in \(z>0\) with \(u(x,y,0)=f(x,y)\). Green's identity with the
 Dirichlet Green's function (which vanishes on the boundary) gives, the hemisphe
re at infinity contributing nothing by the radiation condition,
\[
u(P)=-\int_{z'=0} f(\xi,\eta)\,\frac{\partial G}{\partial n'}\,d\xi\,d\eta,
\]
where \(n'\) is the outward normal to \(\{z>0\}\) at the boundary point \(Q'=(\x
i,\eta,0)\), i.e. \(\partial/\partial n'=-\partial/\partial\zeta\). Hence
\[
u(P)=\int\!\!\int f(\xi,\eta)\,\frac{\partial G}{\partial\zeta}\Big|_{\zeta=0} d
\xi\,d\eta.
\]

Compute the normal derivative. With \(\varphi(s)=e^{-iks}/s\), \(\varphi'(s)=e^{
-iks}\!\left(-\frac{ik}{s}-\frac{1}{s^{2}}\right)\), and
\[
\frac{\partial r}{\partial\zeta}=\frac{\zeta-z}{r}\xrightarrow[\zeta=0]{}-\frac{
z}{R},\qquad
\frac{\partial r_1}{\partial\zeta}=\frac{z+\zeta}{r_1}\xrightarrow[\zeta=0]{}\fr
ac{z}{R},
\]
where \(R=\sqrt{(x-\xi)^2+(y-\eta)^2+z^{2}}\) (so \(r=r_1=R\) at \(\zeta=0\)). T
hen
\[
\frac{\partial G}{\partial\zeta}\Big|_{\zeta=0}
=\frac{1}{4\pi}\,\varphi'(R)\!\left[-\frac{z}{R}-\frac{z}{R}\right]
=-\frac{z}{2\pi R}\,e^{-ikR}\!\left(-\frac{ik}{R}-\frac{1}{R^{2}}\right)
=\frac{z}{2\pi}\left(ik+\frac{1}{R}\right)\frac{e^{-ikR}}{R^{2}}.
\]

Therefore the solution of the first boundary-value problem is
\[
\boxed{\;u(x,y,z)=\frac{z}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\inft
y}
\left(ik+\frac{1}{R}\right)\frac{e^{-ikR}}{R^{2}}\,f(\xi,\eta)\,d\xi\,d\eta,
\qquad R=\sqrt{(x-\xi)^2+(y-\eta)^2+z^{2}}\;}.
\]

Equivalently, since \(\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\!\left(\frac{e^{-ikR}}{R}\righ
t)=-z\!\left(ik+\frac1R\right)\!\frac{e^{-ikR}}{R^{2}}\),
\[
u(x,y,z)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\int\!\!\int \frac{e^{-ikR}}{
R}\,f(\xi,\eta)\,d\xi\,d\eta.
\]

*Consistency check:* for \(k\to0\) this reduces to the Poisson integral for the
upper half-space,
\(u=\frac{z}{2\pi}\iint f/R^{3}\,d\xi\,d\eta\), as required.

> **Note on the statement.** The kernel printed in the problem, \(\left(ik+\frac
1R\right)\frac{e^{-ikR}}{R}\) with an overall minus sign, does **not** reduce to
 the Poisson kernel when \(k=0\) (it gives \(\propto 1/R^{2}\) with the wrong si
gn). The derivation above shows the correct kernel is \(\left(ik+\frac1R\right)\
frac{e^{-ikR}}{R^{2}}\) with a **plus** sign (under the outward-normal conventio
n that makes part (b) consistent); the displayed formula evidently has a typogra
phical error in the power of \(R\) (and sign).

## (b) Neumann condition \(\partial G/\partial z|_{z=0}=0\)

Take the image with the **same** sign:
\[
\boxed{\;\hat G(P,Q)=\frac{e^{-ikr}}{4\pi r}+\frac{e^{-ikr_1}}{4\pi r_1}\;},\qqu
ad v=+\frac{e^{-ikr_1}}{4\pi r_1}.
\]

**Boundary condition.** Differentiate w.r.t. the field coordinate \(z\):
\[
\frac{\partial g}{\partial z}=\frac{e^{-ikr}}{4\pi}\!\left(-\frac{ik}{r}-\frac{1
}{r^{2}}\right)\!\frac{z-\zeta}{r},\qquad
\frac{\partial g_1}{\partial z}=\frac{e^{-ikr_1}}{4\pi}\!\left(-\frac{ik}{r_1}-\
frac{1}{r_1^{2}}\right)\!\frac{z+\zeta}{r_1}.
\]
On \(z=0\): \(r=r_1=R_0=\sqrt{(x-\xi)^2+(y-\eta)^2+\zeta^2}\), \((z-\zeta)/r=-\z
eta/R_0\), \((z+\zeta)/r_1=+\zeta/R_0\). Hence
\[
\frac{\partial\hat G}{\partial z}\Big|_{z=0}
=\frac{e^{-ikR_0}}{4\pi}\!\left(-\frac{ik}{R_0}-\frac{1}{R_0^{2}}\right)\!\frac{
\zeta}{R_0}
+\frac{e^{-ikR_0}}{4\pi}\!\left(-\frac{ik}{R_0}-\frac{1}{R_0^{2}}\right)\!\left(
-\frac{\zeta}{R_0}\right)=0.\quad\checkmark
\]

**Equation and regularity.** As in (a), \(g\) supplies the singularity and \(v=+
g_1\) is regular in \(z>0\) with \((\nabla^2+k^2)v=0\); the radiation condition
holds. So \(\hat G\) is the Neumann Green's function.

**Integral representation (second boundary-value problem).** Let \(u\) solve \((
\nabla^2+k^2)u=0\) in \(z>0\) with Neumann data \(\partial u/\partial n\big|_{z=
0}=f(x,y)\). Green's identity with the Neumann Green's function (whose \(\partia
l\hat G/\partial n'=0\) on the boundary) leaves only the \(G\,\partial u/\partia
l n'\) term:
\[
u(P)=\int_{z'=0}\hat G(P,Q')\,f(\xi,\eta)\,d\xi\,d\eta.
\]
Evaluate \(\hat G\) with the source on the boundary (\(\zeta=0\)):
\[
\hat G\big|_{\zeta=0}=\frac{e^{-ikR}}{4\pi R}+\frac{e^{-ikR}}{4\pi R}=\frac{e^{-
ikR}}{2\pi R},
\qquad R=\sqrt{(x-\xi)^2+(y-\eta)^2+z^{2}}.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{\;u(x,y,z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\inft
y}
\frac{e^{-ikR}}{R}\,f(\xi,\eta)\,d\xi\,d\eta,
\qquad R=\sqrt{(x-\xi)^2+(y-\eta)^2+z^{2}}\;},
\]
which is exactly the formula stated in part (b). For \(k\to0\) it reduces to the
 Neumann (Poisson for the double layer) integral \(\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint f/R\,d\xi
\,d\eta\), as expected.

## Summary

By the **method of images** — placing an image source at the reflected point \(Q
_1=(\xi,\eta,-\zeta)\) and choosing its sign to enforce the boundary condition —
 one obtains:

- **(a) Dirichlet** (\(G|_{z=0}=0\)): image with opposite sign,
  \(\displaystyle G=\frac{e^{-ikr}}{4\pi r}-\frac{e^{-ikr_1}}{4\pi r_1}\), with
regular part \(v=-\frac{e^{-ikr_1}}{4\pi r_1}\).
  The associated Poisson-type kernel is \(\frac{z}{2\pi}\!\left(ik+\frac1R\right
)\!\frac{e^{-ikR}}{R^{2}}\).

- **(b) Neumann** (\(\partial_z G|_{z=0}=0\)): image with the same sign,
  \(\displaystyle \hat G=\frac{e^{-ikr}}{4\pi r}+\frac{e^{-ikr_1}}{4\pi r_1}\),
with regular part \(v=+\frac{e^{-ikr_1}}{4\pi r_1}\).
  The associated integral kernel is \(\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{e^{-ikR}}{R}\).

Both Green's functions satisfy \((\nabla^2+k^2)G=-\delta(P-Q)\) in \(z>0\), are
regular away from \(Q\), fulfill the required boundary condition on \(z=0\), and
 obey the Sommerfeld radiation condition; the integral representations follow fr
om Green's identity with the hemisphere at infinity vanishing.
