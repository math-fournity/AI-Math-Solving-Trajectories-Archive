## Solution

We evaluate $I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos x}{4x^2-\pi^2}\,dx$.

**Step 1: Identify the singularities.**

The denominator $4x^2 - \pi^2 = 4(x-\tfrac{\pi}{2})(x+\tfrac{\pi}{2})$ vanishes
at $x = \pm\tfrac{\pi}{2}$. However, since $\cos(\tfrac{\pi}{2}) = \cos(-\tfrac{
\pi}{2}) = 0$, the singularities are **removable**. For instance, near $x = \tfr
ac{\pi}{2}$:
$$\cos x = -\sin(x-\tfrac{\pi}{2}) \sim -(x-\tfrac{\pi}{2}), \qquad 4x^2-\pi^2 \
sim 4\pi(x-\tfrac{\pi}{2}),$$
so the ratio tends to $-\tfrac{1}{4\pi}$. Thus $I$ is a well-defined (conditiona
lly convergent) improper integral, and equals its own Cauchy principal value.

**Step 2: Partial fraction decomposition.**

$$\frac{1}{4x^2-\pi^2} = \frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{(x-\frac{\pi}{2})(x+\frac{\pi}
{2})} = \frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\frac{1}{x-\frac{\pi}{2}} - \frac{1}{x+\frac{\pi}{2}
}\right).$$

Hence
$$I = \frac{1}{4\pi}\left[\operatorname{p.v.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos
 x}{x-\frac{\pi}{2}}\,dx - \operatorname{p.v.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\co
s x}{x+\frac{\pi}{2}}\,dx\right].$$

(The p.v. interpretation is needed for each piece individually, even though the
combined integrand is regular; linearity of p.v. justifies the split.)

**Step 3: A standard contour integral.**

For real $a$, consider $f(z) = \frac{e^{iz}}{z-a}$. Integrate over the contour c
onsisting of $[-R, a-\varepsilon]$, a small semicircle of radius $\varepsilon$ i
n the **upper** half-plane around $a$, $[a+\varepsilon, R]$, and the large semic
ircle of radius $R$ in the upper half-plane. By Jordan's lemma the large arc van
ishes as $R\to\infty$; no poles lie inside the contour. The small arc (traversed
 from $\theta=\pi$ to $\theta=0$) contributes
$$i\int_{\pi}^{0} e^{ia}\,d\theta = -i\pi e^{ia}.$$
Since the total contour integral is $0$:
$$\operatorname{p.v.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{ix}}{x-a}\,dx = i\pi e^{i
a}.$$

Taking the real part:
$$\operatorname{p.v.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos x}{x-a}\,dx = \operator
name{Re}\!\left(i\pi e^{ia}\right) = \operatorname{Re}\!\left(i\pi(\cos a + i\si
n a)\right) = -\pi\sin a.$$

**Step 4: Apply to each piece.**

- For $a = \tfrac{\pi}{2}$: $\displaystyle\operatorname{p.v.}\!\int\frac{\cos x}
{x-\frac{\pi}{2}}\,dx = -\pi\sin\tfrac{\pi}{2} = -\pi$.
- For $a = -\tfrac{\pi}{2}$: $\displaystyle\operatorname{p.v.}\!\int\frac{\cos x
}{x+\frac{\pi}{2}}\,dx = -\pi\sin\!\left(-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right) = \pi$.

**Step 5: Combine.**

$$I = \frac{1}{4\pi}\bigl[(-\pi) - (\pi)\bigr] = \frac{1}{4\pi}(-2\pi) = -\frac{
1}{2}.$$

**Verification (cross-check via $e^{ix}$).** Computing directly:
$$\operatorname{p.v.}\!\int\frac{e^{ix}}{4x^2-\pi^2}\,dx = \frac{1}{4\pi}\left[i
\pi e^{i\pi/2} - i\pi e^{-i\pi/2}\right] = \frac{i}{4}\cdot 2i\sin\tfrac{\pi}{2}

which is purely real, confirming $I = -\tfrac{1}{2}$.

$$\boxed{I = -\dfrac{1}{2}}$$
