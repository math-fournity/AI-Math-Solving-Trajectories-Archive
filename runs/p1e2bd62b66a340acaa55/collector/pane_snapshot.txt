Looking at this problem, I need to find all integers $a \in [0, 400]$ such that
the equation has exactly 6 distinct real solutions.

## Setup

Let $t = x^2 \geq 0$ and $n = \lfloor t \rfloor$ (non-negative integer), so $t \
in [n, n+1)$. Set $s = \sin\frac{\pi a}{200} \in [-1, 1]$.

The equation $x^4 - 6x^2 + 4 = s - 2\lfloor x^2 \rfloor$ becomes:
$$f(t) = s - 2n, \quad \text{where } f(t) = t^2 - 6t + 4 = (t-3)^2 - 5$$

Each valid $t > 0$ yields $x = \pm\sqrt{t}$ (2 solutions); $t = 0$ yields 1 solu
tion. Since $f(0) = 4 = s$ requires $s = 4 \notin [-1,1]$, $t = 0$ never occurs.
 So **6 distinct $x$ values ⟺ exactly 3 valid $t > 0$ values**.

## Finding valid $a$

**Case $s = 1$**: $\sin\frac{\pi a}{200} = 1$ → $\frac{\pi a}{200} = \frac{\pi}{
2}$ → $a = 100$.

**Case $s \in (-1, 0]$**: $\sin\theta \in (-1, 0]$ for $\theta \in \{0\} \cup [\

- $\theta = 0$ → $a = 0$
- $\theta \in [\pi, 2\pi]$ → $a \in [200, 400]$

So valid $a$ from this case: $\{0\} \cup \{200, 201, \ldots, 299, 301, \ldots, 4
