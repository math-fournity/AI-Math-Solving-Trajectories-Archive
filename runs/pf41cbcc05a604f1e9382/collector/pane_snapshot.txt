## Proof

the measure spaces are not sigma-finite, $C \in \Sigma \otimes \Gamma$, and $T$

### Setup

Let $X = Y = [0,1]$, let $\Sigma = \Gamma = \mathcal{P}([0,1])$ (the full power
set), and let $\mu = \nu$ be the **counting measure** on $[0,1]$.

**The spaces are not sigma-finite.** If $[0,1] = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n$ wit
h each $\mu(A_n) < \infty$, then each $A_n$ is finite, so $\bigcup_n A_n$ is cou
ntable — contradicting the uncountability of $[0,1]$.

Let $C = \{(0,0)\} = \{0\} \times \{0\}$. This is a measurable rectangle, so $C
\in \Sigma \otimes \Gamma$.

### The operator $T$ is well-defined, linear, and bounded

For $f \in L_1(\mu)$, we have $\|f\|_{L_1(\mu)} = \sum_{x \in [0,1]} |f(x)| < \i
nfty$, which forces $f$ to be nonzero on at most countably many points. Computin
g:

$$(Tf)(y) = \int_X 1_C(x,y)\, f(x)\, d\mu(x) = \sum_{x \in [0,1]} \mathbf{1}_{\{
(0,0)\}}(x,y)\, f(x) = f(0)\cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{0\}}(y).$$

Then

$$\|Tf\|_{L_1(\nu)} = \sum_{y \in [0,1]} |f(0)|\cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{0\}}(y) = |f(
0)| \le \sum_{x \in [0,1]} |f(x)| = \|f\|_{L_1(\mu)}.$$

So $T$ is bounded with $\|T\| \le 1$. (Equality holds: taking $f = \mathbf{1}_{\
{0\}}$ gives $\|f\|_1 = 1$ and $\|Tf\|_1 = 1$.) Linearity is immediate from line
arity of the integral.

### The product measure of $C$ is finite

The product of two counting measures is the counting measure on the product spac
e. Therefore

$$\mu \otimes \nu\,(C) = \mu \otimes \nu\big(\{(0,0)\}\big) = 1.$$
