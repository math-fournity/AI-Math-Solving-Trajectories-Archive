## Proof

**Step 1: Identify a ruling (straight line) on the hyperboloid containing both p
oints.**

The hyperboloid of one sheet $S: x^2+y^2-z^2=1$ is a doubly ruled surface. One f
amily of rulings is
$$\ell_\theta(t) = (\cos\theta - t\sin\theta,\; \sin\theta + t\cos\theta,\; t),
\qquad \theta,t\in\mathbb{R}.$$
Indeed $|\ell_\theta(t)|_{xy}^2 = \cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta + t^2(\sin^2\theta+\
cos^2\theta) = 1+t^2 = z^2+1$, so $\ell_\theta(t)\in S$ for all $t$.

**Step 2: Find the ruling through $P_1$ and $P_2$.**

For $P_1=(\sqrt2,0,-1)$: set $t=-1$, giving $x=\cos\theta+\sin\theta=\sqrt2$, $y
=\sin\theta-\cos\theta=0$. The second equation gives $\sin\theta=\cos\theta$, i.
e. $\theta=\pi/4$; then $x=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}+\frac{\sqrt2}{2}=\sqrt2$. ✓

So the ruling $\theta=\pi/4$ is
$$\ell(t) = \Big(\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}(1-t),\;\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}(1+t),\;t\Big).$$
Check endpoints:
- $\ell(-1)=(\sqrt2,0,-1)=P_1$. ✓
- $\ell(+1)=(0,\sqrt2,1)=P_2$. ✓

So $P_1$ and $P_2$ lie on the **same ruling** (a straight line segment in $\math
bb{R}^3$ lying entirely on $S$).

**Step 3: Compute its length.**

$\ell'(t)=\big(-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\,\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\,1\big)$, so $|\ell'(t)|=
\sqrt{\tfrac12+\tfrac12+1}=\sqrt2$. Hence
$$\operatorname{Length}(\ell)=\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt2\,dt = 2\sqrt2.$$

**Step 4: This is the global minimum.**

For *any* piecewise-$C^1$ curve $\gamma$ on $S$ joining $P_1$ to $P_2$, its leng
th is at least the Euclidean chord distance:
$$\operatorname{Length}(\gamma)\;\geq\;|P_1-P_2|.$$
Computing the chord:
$$|P_1-P_2|^2 = (\sqrt2-0)^2+(0-\sqrt2)^2+(-1-1)^2 = 2+2+4 = 8, \qquad |P_1-P_2|
=2\sqrt2.$$

Since the ruling $\ell$ is itself a straight line segment in $\mathbb{R}^3$ join
ing $P_1$ and $P_2$, its length equals the chord distance $2\sqrt2$, **achieving
** the lower bound. Therefore no curve on $S$ between $P_1$ and $P_2$ can be sho
rter.

**Conclusion.** The minimum path length on $S$ between $P_1(\sqrt2,0,-1)$ and $P
_2(0,\sqrt2,1)$ is

$$\boxed{2\sqrt2}.$$
