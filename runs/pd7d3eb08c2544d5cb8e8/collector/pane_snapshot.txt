## Proof

p))$ satisfies the requirements.

**Step 1: Order of $SL(2, p)$.**

$SL(2, p)$ is the group of $2 \times 2$ matrices over $\mathbb{F}_p$ with determ
inant 1. To count its elements: the first column can be any nonzero vector $(a,
b)^T \in \mathbb{F}_p^2 \setminus \{0\}$, giving $p^2 - 1$ choices. The second c
olumn $(c, d)^T$ must satisfy $ad - bc = 1$. For each fixed first column, this i
s a single affine-linear condition on $(c, d)$, which has exactly $p$ solutions.
 Hence:

$$|SL(2, p)| = (p^2 - 1) \cdot p = p(p^2 - 1).$$

This matches the required order of $G$.

**Step 2: The center $Z(SL(2, p))$.**

A matrix $A \in SL(2, p)$ is central iff it commutes with every element of $SL(2
, p)$. The center of $GL(2, p)$ consists of scalar matrices $\lambda I$ for $\la
mbda \in \mathbb{F}_p^*$. Intersecting with $SL(2, p)$: we need $\det(\lambda I)
 = \lambda^2 = 1$, so $\lambda = \pm 1$.

- **For $p$ odd:** $\lambda = 1$ and $\lambda = -1$ are distinct, so $Z(SL(2, p)
) = \{I, -I\} \cong C_2$, which has order 2.
- **For $p = 2$:** $-1 = 1$ in $\mathbb{F}_2$, so $Z(SL(2, 2)) = \{I\}$, which i
s trivial.

**Step 3: The quotient $SL(2, p) / Z(SL(2, p)) \cong PSL(2, p)$.**

By definition, $PSL(2, p) = SL(2, p) / Z(SL(2, p))$. This is the standard defini

**Step 4: Normality of $N$.**

The center of any group is always a normal subgroup (it is fixed by conjugation
by every element). So $N = Z(SL(2, p)) \trianglelefteq G$.

For every prime $p$, taking $G = SL(2, p)$ and $N = Z(SL(2, p))$ gives:
- $|G| = p(p^2 - 1)$,
- $N \trianglelefteq G$,

Therefore, **yes**, such a finite group exists. $\blacksquare$
