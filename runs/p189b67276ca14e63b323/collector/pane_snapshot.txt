Looking at this problem, I need to find $\lim_{n\to\infty} I_n(r)$ where $I_n(r)
$ is the integral of $\prod f(x_k)$ over the ball of radius $r$ in $\mathbb{R}^n
$.

**Interpretation.** Since $f \ge 0$ and $\int f = 1$, the function $f$ is a prob
ability density. The integral $I_n(r)$ equals
$$I_n(r) = P\!\left(X_1^2 + X_2^2 + \cdots + X_n^2 \le r^2\right),$$
where $X_1, X_2, \dots$ are i.i.d. random variables with density $f$.

**Claim:** $I_n(r) \to 0$ for every fixed $r \ge 0$.

---

**Proof.**

Set $Y_k = X_k^2$ and $S_n = \sum_{k=1}^n Y_k$. We must show $P(S_n \le r^2) \to
 0$.

**Step 1: Reduction to a tail bound.**

If $S_n \le r^2$ then $|X_k| \le r$ for every $k$. Let
$$p := P(|X_1| \le r) = \int_{-r}^{r} f(x)\,dx.$$

**Case A: $p < 1$.** Then
$$I_n(r) = P(S_n \le r^2) \le P(|X_k|\le r,\ \forall k) = p^n \xrightarrow{n\to\
infty} 0.$$

**Case B: $p = 1$, i.e., $f$ is supported on $[-r, r]$.** Here the trivial bound
 gives $p^n = 1$, so we need a finer argument.

**Step 2: The case $f$ supported on $[-r,r]$.**

Since $f$ is continuous, non-negative, and $\int f = 1$, the second moment
$$\mu := E[X_1^2] = \int_{-r}^{r} x^2\, f(x)\,dx$$
satisfies $\mu > 0$. Indeed, $\mu = 0$ would force $x^2 f(x) = 0$ for all $x$, h
ence $f(x)=0$ for $x \ne 0$; by continuity $f(0)=0$ as well, contradicting $\int
 f = 1$.

Moreover $\mu \le r^2 < \infty$ (since $|x| \le r$ on the support), so the Weak
Law of Large Numbers applies:
$$\frac{S_n}{n} \xrightarrow{P} \mu > 0.$$

Fix $\varepsilon = \mu/2 > 0$. By the WLLN,
$$P\!\left(\frac{S_n}{n} \le \frac{\mu}{2}\right) \to 0.$$

For all sufficiently large $n$ (namely $n > 2r^2/\mu$), we have $r^2/n < \mu/2$,
 so
$$I_n(r) = P(S_n \le r^2) = P\!\left(\frac{S_n}{n} \le \frac{r^2}{n}\right) \le
P\!\left(\frac{S_n}{n} \le \frac{\mu}{2}\right) \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.$$

**Step 3: The case $E[X_1^2] = \infty$ (not covered by Case B).**

If $p < 1$ we are already done by Case A. If $p = 1$ then $f$ is supported on $[
-r,r]$, forcing $E[X_1^2] \le r^2 < \infty$, so this sub-case cannot arise. Henc
e Step 2 covers everything with $p=1$.

---

**Conclusion.** In every case, $I_n(r) \to 0$.

The intuition is clear: $S_n/n \to E[X_1^2] > 0$ (when finite) or grows even fas
ter (when infinite), so $S_n$ grows linearly (or faster) in $n$, while $r^2$ is
fixed. The probability of landing in a fixed-radius ball vanishes as the dimensi
on grows — a manifestation of concentration of measure in high dimensions.

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} I_n(r) = 0}$$
