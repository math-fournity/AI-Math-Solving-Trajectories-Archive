## Proof

We prove that the curvature operator $C: Q \rightsquigarrow L \otimes \Lambda^2
T^*B$ is universal among first-order gauge natural operators from $Q$ to gauge n
atural bundles of order $(2,0)$: every such operator factors uniquely through $C
$.

### Step 1 (Key Lemma): The curvature is the complete first-order gauge invarian
t of a connection

**Lemma.** Let $P \to B$ be a principal $G$-bundle, $x \in B$, and $\Gamma_1, \G
amma_2$ two connections on $P$. There exists a gauge transformation $g: B \to G$
 with $g(x) = e$ such that $j^1_x(g \cdot \Gamma_1) = j^1_x \Gamma_2$ if and onl
y if $C(\Gamma_1)(x) = C(\Gamma_2)(x)$.

**Proof of Lemma.** The forward direction is immediate: if $j^1_x(g \cdot \Gamma
_1) = j^1_x \Gamma_2$ with $g(x) = e$, then
$$C(\Gamma_2)(x) = C(g \cdot \Gamma_1)(x) = \operatorname{Ad}_{g(x)^{-1}} C(\Gam
ma_1)(x) = C(\Gamma_1)(x).$$

For the reverse direction, assume $C(\Gamma_1)(x) = C(\Gamma_2)(x)$. Working in
a local trivialization, let $\omega_1, \omega_2$ be the connection 1-forms. We p
roceed in two stages.

**Stage 1: Equalize the values.** If $\omega_1(x) \neq \omega_2(x)$, choose a ga
uge transformation $g_0$ with $g_0(x) = e$ and $(g_0^{-1}dg_0)|_x = \omega_2(x)
- \omega_1(x)$. This is possible since $g^{-1}dg|_x$ can be any $\mathfrak{g}$-v
alued 1-form when $g(x) = e$. Then
$$(g_0 \cdot \omega_1)(x) = \omega_1(x) + (\omega_2(x) - \omega_1(x)) = \omega_2
(x),$$
and $C(g_0 \cdot \Gamma_1)(x) = C(\Gamma_1)(x) = C(\Gamma_2)(x)$ since $g_0(x) =
 e$. Replace $\Gamma_1$ by $g_0 \cdot \Gamma_1$.

**Stage 2: Equalize the 1-jets.** Now $\omega_1(x) = \omega_2(x) =: \omega_0$. T
he curvatures at $x$ are:
$$C(\Gamma_k)(x) = d\omega_k(x) + \tfrac{1}{2}[\omega_0 \wedge \omega_0], \quad
k = 1, 2.$$
The equality $C(\Gamma_1)(x) = C(\Gamma_2)(x)$ gives $d\omega_1(x) = d\omega_2(x
)$, i.e., the antisymmetric parts of the first derivatives agree:
$$\partial_i\omega_{1,j}(x) - \partial_j\omega_{1,i}(x) = \partial_i\omega_{2,j}
(x) - \partial_j\omega_{2,i}(x).$$

The difference $\Delta_{ij} := \partial_i\omega_{2,j}(x) - \partial_i\omega_{1,j
}(x)$ is therefore symmetric: $\Delta_{ij} = \Delta_{ji}$.

Now choose a gauge transformation $g$ with $g(x) = e$, $(g^{-1}dg)|_x = 0$ (pres
erving the value), and second-order term $g = \exp\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}h_{ij}\, x
^i x^j + O(x^3)\bigr)$ where $h_{ij} = h_{ji} \in \mathfrak{g}$ is chosen so tha
t $h_{ij} = \Delta_{ij}$. Then:
$$\partial_j(g \cdot \omega_1)_i\,(x) = \partial_j\omega_{1,i}(x) + h_{ij} = \pa
rtial_j\omega_{1,i}(x) + \Delta_{ij} = \partial_j\omega_{2,i}(x),$$
where we used that $\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\omega_1 = \omega_1$ to first orde
r at $x$ (since $g(x) = e$ and $(g^{-1}dg)|_x = 0$). Thus $j^1_x(g \cdot \Gamma_
1) = j^1_x \Gamma_2$. $\square$

### Step 2: Construction of $\bar{A}$

Since $A: Q \rightsquigarrow F$ is a first-order gauge natural operator, $A(\Gam
ma)(x)$ depends only on $j^1_x \Gamma$ and is equivariant under gauge transforma
tions.

We define $\bar{A}: L \otimes \Lambda^2 T^*B \to F$ fiberwise. For $\Omega \in (
L \otimes \Lambda^2 T^*B)_x$, choose a connection $\Gamma$ with $C(\Gamma)(x) =
\Omega$. Such a connection exists: in a local trivialization with $\omega(x) = 0
$, set $\omega = \frac{1}{2}\Omega_{ij}\, x^i\, dx^j$, giving $d\omega(x) = \Ome
ga$ and $C(\Gamma)(x) = \Omega$. Define:
$$\bar{A}(\Omega) := A(\Gamma)(x).$$

**Well-definedness.** Suppose $C(\Gamma_1)(x) = C(\Gamma_2)(x) = \Omega$. By the
 Lemma, there exists a gauge transformation $g$ with $g(x) = e$ and $j^1_x(g \cd
ot \Gamma_1) = j^1_x \Gamma_2$. Since $A$ is first-order:
$$A(\Gamma_2)(x) = A(g \cdot \Gamma_1)(x).$$
Since $A$ is gauge natural:
$$A(g \cdot \Gamma_1)(x) = F(g)\bigl(A(\Gamma_1)(x)\bigr).$$
Because $F$ is a gauge natural bundle of order $(2,0)$—order $0$ in the bundle d
irection—the action of $g$ on $F_x$ depends only on $g(x) = e$, hence is trivial
. Therefore:
$$A(\Gamma_2)(x) = A(\Gamma_1)(x),$$
and $\bar{A}$ is well-defined.

### Step 3: $\bar{A}$ is a natural transformation

Let $\Phi: P \to P'$ be a principal bundle automorphism covering $\phi: B \to B'
$. For $\Omega \in (L \otimes \Lambda^2 T^*B)_x$, choose $\Gamma$ with $C(\Gamma
)(x) = \Omega$. Then $\Phi \cdot \Omega = C(\Phi \cdot \Gamma)(\phi(x))$, so:
$$\bar{A}_{P'}(\Phi \cdot \Omega) = A(\Phi \cdot \Gamma)(\phi(x)) = \Phi \cdot A
(\Gamma)(x) = \Phi \cdot \bar{A}_P(\Omega),$$
where the second equality uses gauge naturality of $A$. Hence $\bar{A}$ is a gau
ge natural transformation.

### Step 4: The factorization $A = \bar{A} \circ C$

For any connection $\Gamma$ and any $x \in B$:
$$(\bar{A} \circ C)(\Gamma)(x) = \bar{A}\bigl(C(\Gamma)(x)\bigr) = A(\Gamma)(x),
$$
by the definition of $\bar{A}$. Hence $A = \bar{A} \circ C$.

### Step 5: Uniqueness

Suppose $\bar{A}_1, \bar{A}_2: L \otimes \Lambda^2 T^*B \to F$ are gauge natural
 transformations with $\bar{A}_1 \circ C = \bar{A}_2 \circ C = A$. For any $\Ome
ga \in (L \otimes \Lambda^2 T^*B)_x$, by the surjectivity of $C$ on fibers (Step
 2), there exists $\Gamma$ with $C(\Gamma)(x) = \Omega$. Then:
$$\bar{A}_1(\Omega) = \bar{A}_1\bigl(C(\Gamma)(x)\bigr) = A(\Gamma)(x) = \bar{A}
_2\bigl(C(\Gamma)(x)\bigr) = \bar{A}_2(\Omega).$$
Hence $\bar{A}_1 = \bar{A}_2$. $\square$

### Conclusion

The curvature operator $C: Q \rightsquigarrow L \otimes \Lambda^2 T^*B$ is the *
*universal** first-order gauge natural operator on connections with values in ga
uge natural bundles of order $(2,0)$. The key mechanism is:

1. **The curvature completely determines the gauge orbit of the 1-jet of a conne
ction** (Lemma): the antisymmetric part of the first derivative (the curvature)
is the only gauge-invariant first-order information, while the symmetric part ca
n be absorbed by a gauge transformation with $g(x) = e$ and vanishing first deri
vative.

2. **The order $(2,0)$ condition** (specifically, order $0$ in the bundle direct
ion) ensures that gauge transformations fixing $x$ with $g(x) = e$ act trivially
 on $F_x$, so that $A(\Gamma)(x)$ descends to a well-defined function of $C(\Gam
ma)(x)$ alone.

3. **Surjectivity of $C$ on fibers** guarantees uniqueness of the factorization.
