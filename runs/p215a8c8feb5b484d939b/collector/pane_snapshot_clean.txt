**Answer: Yes, such a function exists.**

**Proof.**

Suppose $f = u + iv$ is analytic on the unit disk $\mathbb{D}$ and $u_x = 0$ on
$\mathbb{D}$. Since $f$ is analytic, $u$ and $v$ satisfy the Cauchy–Riemann equa
tions
$$u_x = v_y, \qquad u_y = -v_x.$$

From $u_x = 0$ we immediately get $v_y = u_x = 0$, so $v$ depends only on $x$: $
v = v(x)$. Likewise $u_x = 0$ means $u$ depends only on $y$: $u = u(y)$.

The second Cauchy–Riemann equation gives
$$u'(y) = u_y = -v_x = -v'(x).$$
The left-hand side is a function of $y$ alone, while the right-hand side is a fu
nction of $x$ alone. Hence both sides must equal a common real constant $c$:
$$u'(y) = c, \qquad v'(x) = -c.$$

Integrating,
$$u(y) = cy + d, \qquad v(x) = -cx + e,$$
for real constants $c, d, e$. Therefore
$$f(z) = u + iv = (cy + d) + i(-cx + e) = (d + ie) + c(y - ix).$$

Since $y - ix = -i(x + iy) = -iz$, we obtain
$$f(z) = (d + ie) - ic\,z.$$

Thus every analytic function on $\mathbb{D}$ with $u_x = 0$ is of the form $f(z)
 = \alpha + \beta z$ with $\alpha = d+ie \in \mathbb{C}$ and $\beta = -ic \in i\
mathbb{R}$ purely imaginary. In particular, taking $c = 1$, $d = e = 0$ gives
$$f(z) = -iz.$$

For this function, $f(z) = -i(x+iy) = y - ix$, so $u(x,y) = y$ and $u_x = \parti
al y/\partial x = 0$ everywhere on $\mathbb{D}$, yet $f$ is clearly not constant
 (e.g., $f(0) = 0$ but $f(1/2) = -i/2 \neq 0$).

Hence such a non-constant analytic function exists, and the complete family is $
f(z) = \alpha + \beta z$ with $\alpha \in \mathbb{C}$ and $\beta \in i\mathbb{R}
\setminus\{0\}$. $\blacksquare$
