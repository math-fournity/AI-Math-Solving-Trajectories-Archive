## Proof

**Setup.** Let $\varphi \in \mathcal{L}(V, \mathbf{F})$ be a nonzero linear func
tional on the (infinite-dimensional) vector space $V$ over $\mathbf{F}$. We esta
blish an isomorphism
$$V/\ker(\varphi) \;\cong\; \mathbf{F}.$$

(The case $\varphi = 0$ gives $\ker(\varphi)=V$ and $V/V=\{0\}$, which is not is
omorphic to $\mathbf{F}$; the meaningful statement is for $\varphi\neq 0$, which
 we assume throughout.)

---

**Step 1. Define the canonical map.**

Let $\pi: V \to V/\ker(\varphi)$ be the natural quotient map, $\pi(v) = v + \ker
(\varphi)$. Define
$$\widetilde{\varphi}: V/\ker(\varphi) \longrightarrow \mathbf{F}, \qquad \widet
ilde{\varphi}\bigl(v + \ker(\varphi)\bigr) := \varphi(v).$$

**Step 2. $\widetilde{\varphi}$ is well-defined.**

Suppose $v + \ker(\varphi) = v' + \ker(\varphi)$. Then $v - v' \in \ker(\varphi)
$, so $\varphi(v - v') = 0$, hence $\varphi(v) = \varphi(v')$. Thus $\widetilde{
\varphi}$ does not depend on the choice of coset representative.

**Step 3. $\widetilde{\varphi}$ is linear.**

For $v, w \in V$ and $\alpha \in \mathbf{F}$:
- $\widetilde{\varphi}\bigl((v+\ker\varphi) + (w+\ker\varphi)\bigr) = \widetilde
{\varphi}\bigl((v+w)+\ker\varphi\bigr) = \varphi(v+w) = \varphi(v)+\varphi(w) =
\widetilde{\varphi}(v+\ker\varphi)+\widetilde{\varphi}(w+\ker\varphi).$
- $\widetilde{\varphi}\bigl(\alpha(v+\ker\varphi)\bigr) = \widetilde{\varphi}\bi
gl(\alpha v+\ker\varphi\bigr) = \varphi(\alpha v) = \alpha\,\varphi(v) = \alpha\
,\widetilde{\varphi}(v+\ker\varphi).$

**Step 4. $\widetilde{\varphi}$ is injective.**

If $\widetilde{\varphi}(v + \ker\varphi) = 0$, then $\varphi(v) = 0$, so $v \in
\ker(\varphi)$, i.e. $v + \ker\varphi = 0 + \ker\varphi$, the zero coset. Hence
$\ker(\widetilde{\varphi}) = \{0\}$, so $\widetilde{\varphi}$ is injective.

**Step 5. $\widetilde{\varphi}$ is surjective.**

Since $\varphi \neq 0$, there exists $v_0 \in V$ with $\varphi(v_0) \neq 0$. The
 image $\operatorname{im}(\varphi)$ is a subspace of $\mathbf{F}$ (viewed as a $
1$-dimensional vector space over itself). Because $\varphi(v_0) \neq 0$, $\opera
torname{im}(\varphi)$ is a **nonzero** subspace of the one-dimensional space $\m
athbf{F}$. The only nonzero subspace of a one-dimensional space is the whole spa
ce, so
$$\operatorname{im}(\varphi) = \mathbf{F}.$$

Equivalently, for every $c \in \mathbf{F}$, setting $\alpha = c / \varphi(v_0) \
in \mathbf{F}$, we have $\varphi(\alpha v_0) = \alpha\,\varphi(v_0) = c$, so $c
= \widetilde{\varphi}(\alpha v_0 + \ker\varphi)$. Thus $\widetilde{\varphi}$ is
surjective.

**Step 6. Conclusion.**

$\widetilde{\varphi}: V/\ker(\varphi) \to \mathbf{F}$ is a well-defined linear m
ap that is both injective and surjective, hence an isomorphism of $\mathbf{F}$-v
ector spaces:
$$\boxed{V/\ker(\varphi) \;\cong\; \mathbf{F}.}$$
