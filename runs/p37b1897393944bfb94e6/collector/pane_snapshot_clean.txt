Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a normal family with a set
of pointwise-convergence points having a limit point must converge uniformly on
compact subsets.

**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

Recall that a family $\{f_n\}$ of holomorphic functions on $V$ is *normal* (in t
he sense of Montel) if every subsequence has a further subsequence converging un
iformly on compact subsets of $V$ to a holomorphic function, or to $\infty$ (uni
formly on compact subsets in the spherical metric).

Let $S = \{z \in V : \lim_{n\to\infty} f_n(z) \text{ exists (as a finite complex
 number)}\}$. By hypothesis, $S$ has a limit point $z_0 \in V$. In particular, $
S \neq \emptyset$.

**Step 1: Extract a subsequential limit $g$.**

Since $\{f_n\}$ is normal, there exists a subsequence $\{f_{n_k}\}$ converging u
niformly on compact subsets of $V$ to some limit $g$, where $g$ is either holomo
rphic on $V$ or $g \equiv \infty$.

**Step 2: Rule out $g \equiv \infty$.**

If $g \equiv \infty$, then for every $z \in V$, $f_{n_k}(z) \to \infty$. Pick an
y $z \in S$ (which is nonempty). Then $f_n(z) \to L_z$ finite, so the subsequenc
e $f_{n_k}(z) \to L_z$ finite—contradicting $f_{n_k}(z) \to \infty$. Hence $g$ i
s holomorphic on $V$.

**Step 3: Identify $g$ on $S$.**

For $z \in S$, the full sequence $f_n(z) \to L_z$, so every subsequence converge
s to $L_z$. In particular $f_{n_k}(z) \to L_z$. But also $f_{n_k}(z) \to g(z)$.
Thus $g(z) = L_z$ for all $z \in S$.

**Step 4: Show the entire sequence converges to $g$.**

Suppose for contradiction that $f_n \not\to g$ uniformly on compact subsets. The
n there exist a compact set $K \subset V$, $\varepsilon > 0$, and a subsequence
$\{f_{m_j}\}$ such that
$$\sup_{z \in K} |f_{m_j}(z) - g(z)| \geq \varepsilon \quad \text{for all } j.$$

By normality, $\{f_{m_j}\}$ has a further subsequence $\{f_{m_{j_\ell}}\}$ conve
rging uniformly on compact subsets to some $h$, holomorphic or $\equiv \infty$.

- If $h \equiv \infty$: at any $z \in S$, $f_{m_{j_\ell}}(z) \to \infty$, but $f
_{m_{j_\ell}}(z) \to L_z$ finite—contradiction. So $h$ is holomorphic.
- For $z \in S$: $f_{m_{j_\ell}}(z) \to L_z = g(z)$, and also $f_{m_{j_\ell}}(z)
 \to h(z)$. Thus $h(z) = g(z)$ for all $z \in S$.

Since $g$ and $h$ are holomorphic on the connected open set $V$ and agree on $S$
, which has a limit point in $V$, the **identity theorem** gives $h \equiv g$ on
 $V$.

But then $f_{m_{j_\ell}} \to g$ uniformly on $K$, contradicting $\sup_K |f_{m_{j
_\ell}} - g| \geq \varepsilon$ for all $\ell$.

**Conclusion.** The contradiction shows $f_n \to g$ uniformly on every compact s
ubset of $V$. Setting $f = g$ (which is holomorphic, hence $f: V \to \mathbb{C}$
), the claim holds. $\blacksquare$
