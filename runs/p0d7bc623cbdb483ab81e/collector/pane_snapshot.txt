## Proof: O(log n)-Approximation for Metric ATSP via Randomized Rounding

### Setup

Let $G=(V,A)$ be a complete directed graph on $n=|V|$ vertices with non-negative
 costs $c_{ij}\geq 0$ satisfying the triangle inequality: $c_{ij}\leq c_{ik}+c_{
kj}$ for all $i,j,k$. The goal is to find a minimum-cost Hamiltonian directed cy
cle (tour). Let $\mathrm{OPT}$ denote the optimal tour cost.

### Step 1: The Held–Karp LP Relaxation

Solve the following LP (the Held–Karp / subtour-elimination relaxation):

$$\min \sum_{(i,j)\in A} c_{ij}\,x_{ij}$$

subject to

$$\sum_{j\neq i} x_{ij}=1 \quad\forall i\in V \qquad\text{(out-degree)}$$

$$\sum_{j\neq i} x_{ji}=1 \quad\forall i\in V \qquad\text{(in-degree)}$$

$$\sum_{i\in S,\,j\notin S} x_{ij}\geq 1 \quad\forall\,\emptyset\neq S\subsetneq
 V \qquad\text{(subtour elimination)}$$

$$x_{ij}\geq 0.$$

Let $x^*$ be an optimal solution with cost $\mathrm{LP}^*$. Since every tour is
feasible for this LP, $\mathrm{LP}^*\leq \mathrm{OPT}$.

### Step 2: Decomposition into Cycle Covers

The polytope defined by the degree constraints and $x\geq 0$ (dropping the subto
ur-elimination constraints) is the **cycle-cover polytope** (equivalently, the a
ssignment polytope of a bipartite graph with parts $V^{\text{out}}$ and $V^{\tex
t{in}}$). This polytope is **integral**: its vertices are exactly the cycle cove
rs (collections of vertex-disjoint directed cycles covering all vertices).

Since $x^*$ satisfies the degree constraints, $x^*$ lies in this integral polyto
pe. By Carathéodory's theorem, we can decompose:

$$x^*=\sum_{k=1}^{t}\lambda_k\,C_k,\qquad \lambda_k\geq 0,\;\sum_k\lambda_k=1,$$

where each $C_k$ is a cycle cover (an integral vertex of the polytope) and $t$ i
s polynomially bounded. Moreover,

$$\sum_k \lambda_k\,\mathrm{cost}(C_k)=\mathrm{cost}(x^*)=\mathrm{LP}^*\leq\math
rm{OPT}.$$

### Step 3: Randomized Rounding — Sampling a Cycle Cover

**Sampling step.** Draw a cycle cover $C$ from the distribution $\{C_k,\lambda_k
\}$, i.e., select $C_k$ with probability $\lambda_k$. Then

$$\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{cost}(C)]=\sum_k\lambda_k\,\mathrm{cost}(C_k)=\mathrm{LP}^*
\leq\mathrm{OPT}.$$

### Step 4: Recursive Patching via Contraction

If the sampled cycle cover $C$ is a single Hamiltonian cycle, it is a tour and w
e are done. Otherwise, $C$ consists of $m\geq 2$ vertex-disjoint cycles $C^{(1)}
,\dots,C^{(m)}$.

**Contraction.** Contract each cycle $C^{(s)}$ into a single supernode $s$. Defi
ne the induced metric on supernodes:

$$d(s,s')=\min_{i\in C^{(s)},\,j\in C^{(s')}} c_{ij}.$$

Because the original costs satisfy the triangle inequality, $d$ is a valid metri
c on the supernodes (non-negative, symmetric in the sense of directed triangle i
nequality, and satisfies the directed triangle inequality).

**Key size reduction.** Every directed cycle in a cycle cover contains at least
$2$ vertices (there are no self-loops $(i,i)$). Therefore $m\leq n/2$: the contr
acted instance has at most half as many vertices.

**Recurse.** Solve the Held–Karp LP on the contracted instance, decompose, sampl
e, and recurse.

### Step 5: Combining the Tours (Cost Accounting)

Suppose the recursive call on the contracted instance returns a tour $T'$ on the
 $m$ supernodes. We expand $T'$ into a tour on the original $n$ vertices as foll
ows:

- When $T'$ visits supernode $s$ (coming from supernode $p$ and going to superno
de $q$), enter cycle $C^{(s)}$ at the vertex $\arg\min_{i\in C^{(s)}} c_{\text{e
xit}(p),i}$ and traverse the entire cycle $C^{(s)}$, then exit toward $q$.

By the triangle inequality and the definition of $d(s,s')$ as a minimum arc cost
, the connection cost between consecutive cycles is at most $d(s,s')$, which is
exactly the arc cost in the contracted tour $T'$. Therefore:

$$\mathrm{cost}(\text{expanded tour})=\sum_{s=1}^{m}\mathrm{cost}(C^{(s)})+\math
rm{cost}(T').$$

Unrolling the recursion to the base case (a single supernode, cost $0$), the **t
otal cost of the final tour** equals the **sum of the costs of the cycle covers
sampled at every recursion level**:

$$\mathrm{cost}(\text{final tour})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\mathrm{cost}(C_\ell),$$

where $C_\ell$ is the cycle cover sampled at level $\ell$ and $L$ is the number
of levels.

### Step 6: Bounding the Number of Levels

At level $0$ there are $n$ vertices. Each contraction reduces the vertex count b
y a factor of at least $2$ (since $m\leq n/2$). After $\ell$ levels, the instanc
e has at most $n/2^\ell$ vertices. The recursion terminates when a single cycle
(tour) is obtained, which happens once the vertex count reaches $1$. Hence

$$L\leq\lceil\log_2 n\rceil.$$

### Step 7: Bounding the Expected Cost at Each Level

**Claim.** At every recursion level $\ell$, the LP optimum of the current (contr
acted) instance is at most $\mathrm{OPT}$.

*Proof of claim.* Take the optimal tour of the **original** instance (cost $\mat
hrm{OPT}$). After contracting the cycles of any cycle cover, this tour induces a
 closed walk on the supernodes that visits every supernode. By shortcutting repe
ated visits (using the triangle inequality), we obtain a proper tour on the supe
rnodes of cost $\leq\mathrm{OPT}$. Since the LP optimum is a lower bound on any
tour, $\mathrm{LP}^*_\ell\leq\mathrm{OPT}$. $\square$

Therefore, at each level $\ell$, the expected cost of the sampled cycle cover sa
tisfies

$$\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{cost}(C_\ell)\big]\leq\mathrm{LP}^*_\ell\leq\mathrm{OPT
}.$$

This holds **conditionally** on any history of previous levels (the claim applie
s to whatever contracted instance arises), so by the tower property of condition
al expectation:

$$\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{cost}(C_\ell)\big]\leq\mathrm{OPT}\quad\text{for every
}\ell=0,\dots,L-1.$$

### Step 8: Final Bound

Combining Steps 5–7 by linearity of expectation:

$$\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{cost}(\text{final tour})\big]=\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\mathb
b{E}\big[\mathrm{cost}(C_\ell)\big]\leq L\cdot\mathrm{OPT}\leq\lceil\log_2 n\rce
il\cdot\mathrm{OPT}=O(\log n)\cdot\mathrm{OPT}.$$

### Step 9: High-Probability Amplification (Optional)

By Markov's inequality, $\Pr\big[\mathrm{cost}>2\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\cdot\mathrm
{OPT}\big]\leq\tfrac{1}{2}$. Running the entire randomized algorithm independent
ly $O(\log(1/\delta))$ times and keeping the best tour yields, with probability
at least $1-\delta$, a tour of cost at most $O(\log n)\cdot\mathrm{OPT}$.

### Step 10: Derandomization

Replacing the sampling step by selecting the **minimum-cost** cycle cover from t
he decomposition (which costs $\leq\mathrm{LP}^*\leq\mathrm{OPT}$ deterministica
lly) yields the deterministic Frieze–Galbiati–Maffioli algorithm with the same $
O(\log n)$ guarantee.

### Summary of the Algorithm

1. **Solve** the Held–Karp LP; obtain optimal fractional solution $x^*$.
2. **Decompose** $x^*$ into a convex combination of cycle covers $\{C_k,\lambda_
k\}$ (possible because the degree-constrained polytope is integral).
3. **Sample** a cycle cover $C$ from this distribution. Its expected cost is $\l
eq\mathrm{OPT}$.
4. **Contract** each cycle of $C$ to a supernode; the number of supernodes is $\
leq n/2$.
5. **Recurse** on the contracted metric instance.
6. **Combine** using the triangle inequality; total cost $=$ sum of cycle-cover
costs across all levels.
7. **Bound**: at most $\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$ levels, each costing $\leq\mathrm{O
PT}$ in expectation, giving $\mathbb{E}[\text{cost}]\leq O(\log n)\cdot\mathrm{O
PT}$.

This is an $O(\log n)$-approximation algorithm for the metric asymmetric TSP via
 randomized rounding of the Held–Karp LP relaxation. $\blacksquare$
