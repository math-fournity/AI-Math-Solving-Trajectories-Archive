## Analysis

**The stated facts:**
1. Finding a PNE in a Congestion game is PLS-complete (Fabrikant, Papadimitriou,
 Talwar, 2004).

ring the same potential function (Monderer & Shapley, 1996).

**The claimed implication:** Therefore, finding a PNE in an exact Potential game

## Proof that the implication does NOT follow

**Key principle — direction of PLS reductions.** To prove that problem $B$ is PL
S-hard, one needs a polynomial-time PLS-reduction **from** a known PLS-complete
problem $A$ **to** $B$:
$$A \preceq_{\text{PLS}} B$$
This means: every instance of $A$ can be transformed (in poly-time) into an inst
ance of $B$, and solutions of $B$ can be mapped back to solutions of $A$, preser
ving local optimality. This establishes that $B$ is **at least as hard as** $A$.

**What the stated mapping establishes.** The mapping "every exact Potential game
 $\to$ equivalent Congestion game" is a transformation:
$$\text{exact Potential game} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{Congestion game}$$

This is a reduction **from** exact Potential games **to** Congestion games:
$$\text{find-PNE(exact Potential)} \;\preceq\; \text{find-PNE(Congestion)}$$

This establishes that finding a PNE in Congestion games is **at least as hard as
** finding a PNE in exact Potential games — i.e., it gives an **upper bound** on
 the complexity of exact Potential games (they are no harder than Congestion gam
es), **not** a lower bound.

**The direction required for the conclusion.** To conclude that finding a PNE in
 exact Potential games is PLS-hard, one would need the **reverse** reduction:
$$\text{find-PNE(Congestion)} \;\preceq\; \text{find-PNE(exact Potential)}$$
i.e., a mapping **from** Congestion games **to** exact Potential games. The stat
ed implication provides the opposite direction, so it **cannot** establish PLS-h
ardness of exact Potential games.

**Why the conclusion is nonetheless true (but for a different reason).** The con
clusion that finding a PNE in exact Potential games is PLS-complete is actually
correct, but it follows from the **reverse inclusion**, not from the stated mapp
ing:

- Every Congestion game **is** an exact Potential game (Rosenthal's potential, 1
973). Congestion games form a **subclass** of exact Potential games.
- Therefore the identity map serves as a trivial polynomial-time PLS-reduction f
rom "find-PNE(Congestion)" to "find-PNE(exact Potential)": a Congestion game ins
tance is already a valid exact Potential game instance, strategy profiles coinci
de, and PNE coincide.
- PLS-membership holds because the potential function of a Congestion game (Rose
nthal's sum of resource costs) is computable in polynomial time from the compact
 input representation, and the neighborhood (unilateral deviations) is poly-time
 enumerable. The same compact representation serves as input to the exact Potent
ial game problem.

Thus: PLS-hardness (via the identity reduction from the subclass) + PLS-membersh
ip = PLS-completeness. But this argument relies on Congestion $\subseteq$ exact
Potential, **not** on the mapping exact Potential $\to$ Congestion stated in the
 question.

**Additional caveat on the stated mapping.** The Monderer–Shapley construction m
apping an arbitrary exact Potential game to a Congestion game may involve a blow
-up in the number of resources (potentially to the number of strategy profiles),
 which can be exponential in the number of players. Thus even the upper-bound di
rection (exact Potential $\preceq$ Congestion) may fail to be a **polynomial-tim
e** reduction under compact input representations, further weakening the stated
implication.

## Conclusion

**No.** The stated implication does **not** follow. The mapping "every exact Pot
ential game $\to$ Congestion game" establishes a reduction in the wrong directio
n for proving PLS-hardness of exact Potential games — it shows Congestion games
are at least as expressive (giving an upper bound on exact Potential game comple
xity), not a lower bound. The conclusion that finding a PNE in exact Potential g
ames is PLS-complete is true, but it follows from the **reverse** inclusion (eve
ry Congestion game is an exact Potential game via Rosenthal's potential), which
provides the correct reduction direction — not from the mapping stated in the qu
