## Proof of Erdős Problem 369 (Variant 2)

**Theorem.** For every $\varepsilon > 0$ and integer $k \ge 2$, for all sufficie
ntly large $n$, there exist $k$ consecutive integers in $[n/2,\, n]$, all of whi
ch are $n^\varepsilon$-smooth (every prime factor $\le n^\varepsilon$).

---

### Step 1. Reduction via LCM to simultaneous smoothness of linear forms

Set $L = \operatorname{lcm}(1, 2, \dots, k)$, which depends only on $k$. For eac
h $j \in \{1, \dots, k\}$, define the linear form

$$\ell_j(t) \;=\; \frac{L}{j}\, t \;+\; 1.$$

Since $L$ is divisible by $j$, the leading coefficient $L/j$ is a positive integ
er.

**Key observation.** If $t$ is a positive integer such that $\ell_j(t)$ is $y$-s
mooth for every $j$, then setting $a = L\,t$, the integers $a+1, a+2, \dots, a+k
$ are all $y$-smooth (provided $k \le y$).

*Proof.* We have $a + j = L\,t + j = j\!\left(\frac{L}{j}\,t + 1\right) = j \cdo
t \ell_j(t)$. The prime factors of $a+j$ are the union of prime factors of $j$ a
nd of $\ell_j(t)$. Since $j \le k$, every prime factor of $j$ is $\le k \le y$ (
for $y = n^\varepsilon$ and $n$ large). Since $\ell_j(t)$ is $y$-smooth by assum
ption, every prime factor of $a+j$ is $\le y$. $\square$

So it suffices to find $t$ in the interval

$$I_n \;=\; \Bigl[\,\lceil (n/2 - 1)/L \rceil,\;\; \lfloor (n-k)/L \rfloor\,\Big
r]$$

such that $\ell_j(t)$ is $n^\varepsilon$-smooth for all $j = 1, \dots, k$. The i
nterval $I_n$ has length $\approx n/(2L) \to \infty$.

For $t \in I_n$, each $\ell_j(t) \approx (L/j) \cdot n/L = n/j$, so the values a
re $\Theta(n)$. The smoothness threshold is $y = n^\varepsilon$, giving smoothne
ss parameter

$$u_j = \frac{\log \ell_j(t)}{\log y} \;\approx\; \frac{\log n}{\varepsilon \log
 n} = \frac{1}{\varepsilon},$$

a fixed constant. The Dickman function $\rho(1/\varepsilon) > 0$ for every $\var
epsilon > 0$.

### Step 2. Counting lemma (Balog–Wooley type)

We invoke the following result on simultaneous smooth values of linear forms, wh
ich is the core of the Balog–Wooley method [BaWo98] and its refinements [BFMW20]
.

**Lemma.** *Let $f_1, \dots, f_k$ be pairwise non-proportional linear forms with
 positive integer leading coefficients and no fixed prime divisor. For any $\del
ta > 0$, there exist constants $C = C(\delta, k, f_1, \dots, f_k) > 0$ and $T_0
= T_0(\delta, k, f_1, \dots, f_k)$ such that for all $T \ge T_0$:*

$$\#\bigl\{t \in [T,\, 2T] : f_j(t) \text{ is } T^\delta\text{-smooth for all }
j\bigr\} \;\ge\; C \cdot T.$$

*Proof sketch.* The argument combines sieve theory with the Balog–Wooley switchi
ng principle:

**(a) Sieve setup.** For each prime $p$ and form $f_j$, the congruence $f_j(t) \
equiv 0 \pmod{p}$ has at most one solution class (for $p$ not dividing the leadi
ng coefficient). Pairwise non-proportionality ensures that for $p$ large, the sy
stems $f_i(t) \equiv 0$ and $f_j(t) \equiv 0 \pmod{p}$ are independent for $i \n
e j$.

**(b) Level of distribution.** For linear forms, the large sieve provides a leve
l of distribution $D = T^{1 - o(1)}$, enabling accurate sieving up to level $D$.

**(c) Fundamental lemma of the $k$-dimensional sieve.** Applied with sifting par
ameter $z = T^\delta$ and level $D$, this gives

$$\#\{t \in [T, 2T] : \text{no prime } p > z \text{ divides any } f_j(t)\} = (1
+ o(1))\, V(z)\, T + O(T^{1-\delta'}),$$

where $V(z) = \prod_p \prod_{j=1}^k (1 - \omega_j(p)/p)$ is the product of local
 sieve densities and $\delta' > 0$.

**(d) Connection to the Dickman function.** By the standard correspondence betwe
en sieve densities and smooth-number theory, $V(z) \approx \prod_{j=1}^k \rho(u_
j)$ where $u_j = \log f_j(T) / \log z \approx 1/\delta$. Thus $V(z) \approx \rho
(1/\delta)^k > 0$.

**(e) Balog–Wooley switching.** The condition "no prime factor $> z$" is necessa
ry but not sufficient for $z$-smoothness (a number could have two prime factors
in $(z^{1/2}, z)$ whose product exceeds it). The switching principle decomposes
the count into:
- **Very smooth part:** all prime factors $\le z^{1/2}$, handled by the sieve wi
th parameter $z^{1/2}$.
- **One large prime part:** exactly one prime factor $p \in (z^{1/2}, z)$, with
$f_j(t)/p$ being $z^{1/2}$-smooth. This is handled by summing over such primes $
p$ and applying the sieve to the quotient.

This decomposition, iterated as needed, converts the "no large prime" condition
into the full smoothness condition, yielding the lower bound $\gg T$. $\square$

---

### Step 3. Verifying the hypotheses

The forms $\ell_j(t) = (L/j)\,t + 1$ satisfy all hypotheses of the Lemma:

- **Positive integer leading coefficients:** $L/j \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$. ✓
- **Pairwise non-proportional:** $\ell_i$ and $\ell_j$ are proportional iff $(L/
i)/(L/j) = 1/1$, i.e., $j = i$. So distinct forms are non-proportional. ✓
- **No fixed prime divisor:** A prime $p$ divides $\ell_j(t)$ for all $t$ iff $p
 \mid (L/j)$ and $p \mid 1$, which is impossible. ✓

---

### Step 4. Applying the counting lemma to all large $n$

Choose $\varepsilon'$ with $0 < \varepsilon' < \varepsilon$ (e.g., $\varepsilon'
 = \varepsilon/2$).

Let $T' = \lceil (n/2 - 1)/L \rceil \approx n/(2L)$ and $T'' = \lfloor (n-k)/L \
rfloor \approx n/L$. The interval $I_n = [T', T'']$ has $T''/T' \approx 2$.

Consider the sub-interval $[T',\, \tfrac{3}{2} T'] \subset I_n$ (contained in $I
_n$ since $\tfrac{3}{2} T' \approx \tfrac{3n}{4L} < \tfrac{n}{L} \approx T''$ fo
r large $n$). The counting lemma (adapted from dyadic $[T, 2T]$ to intervals $[T
, \tfrac{3}{2}T]$ by partitioning into $O(1)$ dyadic pieces) gives, for $T' \ge
T_0(\varepsilon', k, \ell_1, \dots, \ell_k)$:

$$\#\bigl\{t \in [T', \tfrac{3}{2} T'] : \ell_j(t) \text{ is } (T')^{\varepsilon
'}\text{-smooth for all } j\bigr\} \;\ge\; C \cdot T'.$$

Now we verify that $(T')^{\varepsilon'}$-smoothness implies $n^\varepsilon$-smoo
thness:

$$(T')^{\varepsilon'} \;\approx\; \left(\frac{n}{2L}\right)^{\varepsilon'} \;=\;
 n^{\varepsilon'} \cdot (2L)^{-\varepsilon'} \;\le\; n^{\varepsilon'} \;\le\; n^
{\varepsilon}$$

since $\varepsilon' < \varepsilon$ and $n \ge 1$. So every prime factor $\le (T'
)^{\varepsilon'}$ is also $\le n^\varepsilon$. ✓

Since $C \cdot T' \ge 1$ for $T' \ge 1/C$, and $T' \to \infty$ as $n \to \infty$
, there exists $N_0 = N_0(\varepsilon, k)$ such that for all $n \ge N_0$, at lea
st one such $t$ exists in $I_n$.

---

### Step 5. Constructing the consecutive smooth integers

For such $t$, set $a = L\,t$. Then:

1. **Lower bound:** $a + 1 = L\,t + 1 \ge L \cdot T' + 1 \ge L \cdot \frac{n/2 -
 1}{L} + 1 = n/2$. ✓

2. **Upper bound:** $a + k = L\,t + k \le L \cdot \tfrac{3}{2} T' + k \approx \t
frac{3n}{4} + k < n$ for $n$ large. ✓

3. **Smoothness:** For each $j = 1, \dots, k$:
$$a + j = j \cdot \ell_j(t).$$
   - Prime factors of $j$: all $\le j \le k \le n^\varepsilon$ for $n$ large. ✓
   - Prime factors of $\ell_j(t)$: all $\le (T')^{\varepsilon'} \le n^\varepsilo
n$ by the counting lemma. ✓

Therefore $a+1, a+2, \dots, a+k$ are $k$ consecutive $n^\varepsilon$-smooth inte
gers in $[n/2, n]$.

### Conclusion

We have shown: for every $\varepsilon > 0$ and $k \ge 2$, there exists $N_0(\var
epsilon, k)$ such that for all $n \ge N_0$, there exists $a \in \mathbb{N}$ with

$$n/2 \le a + 1, \quad a + k \le n, \quad \text{and} \quad \forall\, j < k,\; \f
orall\, p \in (a+1+j).\text{primeFactors},\; p \le n^\varepsilon.$$

In the language of the theorem, this is precisely:

$$\forall\, (\varepsilon : \mathbb{R}),\; 0 < \varepsilon \;\Rightarrow\; \foral
l\, (k : \mathbb{N}),\; 2 \le k \;\Rightarrow\; \forall^{\infty}\, n,\; \exists\
, a,\; \text{the above holds},$$

i.e., the property holds eventually in $n$ (in the Filter.atTop sense). Since $\
text{answer}(\text{True}) = \text{True}$, the equivalence $\text{answer}(\text{T
