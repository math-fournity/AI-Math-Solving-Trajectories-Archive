## Answer

**No.** The implication $N(I) \mid N(\langle a\rangle) \Rightarrow I \mid \langl
e a\rangle$ is **false** in general. The ideal norm only constrains a weighted s
um of valuations over each rational prime, not the individual valuations at each
 prime ideal. Here is an explicit counterexample.

---

### Counterexample

Let $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$, so $O_K = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ (since $-5 \eq
uiv 3 \pmod{4}$).

**Splitting of 3.** Since $\left(\frac{-5}{3}\right) = \left(\frac{1}{3}\right)
= 1$, the rational prime $3$ splits:
$$3\,O_K = \mathfrak{p}_1\,\mathfrak{p}_2, \qquad \mathfrak{p}_1 = \langle 3,\;
1+\sqrt{-5}\rangle, \quad \mathfrak{p}_2 = \langle 3,\; 1-\sqrt{-5}\rangle.$$
Both $\mathfrak{p}_1, \mathfrak{p}_2$ are distinct prime ideals of norm $3$ (res
idue degree $f = 1$).

**The ideal and element.** Set
$$I = \mathfrak{p}_1, \qquad a = 2 + \sqrt{-5} \in O_K.$$

**Norm computation.**
$$N(I) = N(\mathfrak{p}_1) = 3.$$
$$N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a) = (2+\sqrt{-5})(2-\sqrt{-5}) = 4+5 = 9, \qquad N(\langle a
\rangle) = |N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a)| = 9.$$
Thus $N(I) = 3 \mid 9 = N(\langle a\rangle)$. ✓

**Factorization of $\langle a\rangle$.** We determine which prime ideals divide
$\langle 2+\sqrt{-5}\rangle$ (which has norm $9 = 3^2$):

- $a \in \mathfrak{p}_2$: since $a + (1-\sqrt{-5}) = 3 \in \mathfrak{p}_2$, we h
ave $a = 3 - (1-\sqrt{-5}) \in \mathfrak{p}_2$. So $v_{\mathfrak{p}_2}(\langle a
\rangle) \geq 1$.
- $a \notin \mathfrak{p}_1$: if $a \in \mathfrak{p}_1$, then $a - (1+\sqrt{-5})
= 1 \in \mathfrak{p}_1$, contradicting $N(\mathfrak{p}_1)=3\neq 1$. So $v_{\math
frak{p}_1}(\langle a\rangle) = 0$.

Since $N(\langle a\rangle) = 3^{f_{\mathfrak{p}_1}\cdot 0 + f_{\mathfrak{p}_2}\c
dot v_{\mathfrak{p}_2}} = 3^{v_{\mathfrak{p}_2}} = 9$, we get $v_{\mathfrak{p}_2
} = 2$. Therefore:
$$\langle a\rangle = \mathfrak{p}_2^2.$$

**Does $I \mid \langle a\rangle$?** The divisibility $I \mid \langle a\rangle$ m
eans $\mathfrak{p}_1 \mid \mathfrak{p}_2^2$, i.e., $v_{\mathfrak{p}_1}(\mathfrak
{p}_2^2) \geq 1$. But $\mathfrak{p}_1$ and $\mathfrak{p}_2$ are **distinct** pri
me ideals, so $v_{\mathfrak{p}_1}(\mathfrak{p}_2^2) = 0$. Hence:
$$I \nmid \langle a\rangle. \quad ✗$$

---

### Why the implication fails — structural reason

The norm condition $N(I) \mid N(\langle a\rangle)$ translates to: for every rati
onal prime $p$,
$$\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\mid p} f_{\mathfrak{p}}\, v_{\mathfrak{p}}(I) \;\leq\; \sum
_{\mathfrak{p}\mid p} f_{\mathfrak{p}}\, v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\langle a\rangle).$$
This is a constraint on a **weighted sum** of valuations over all primes above $
p$. But ideal divisibility $I \mid \langle a\rangle$ requires the **pointwise**
condition $v_{\mathfrak{p}}(I) \leq v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\langle a\rangle)$ for **ea
ch** prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ individually.

When a rational prime $p$ splits into multiple prime ideals (here $3 = \mathfrak
{p}_1\mathfrak{p}_2$), the norm cannot distinguish *which* prime ideal carries t
he valuation. In our example, $I = \mathfrak{p}_1$ places all its $3$-adic mass
on $\mathfrak{p}_1$, while $\langle a\rangle = \mathfrak{p}_2^2$ places all its
mass on the *other* factor $\mathfrak{p}_2$. The norms match ($3 \mid 9$), but t
he ideals are coprime at $\mathfrak{p}_1$, so divisibility fails.
