## Proof

Let $C(X)$ denote the ring of continuous real-valued functions on $X$. For each
$\alpha \in X$, the **evaluation map** $\mathrm{ev}_\alpha : C(X) \to \mathbb{R}
$, $f \mapsto f(\alpha)$, is a surjective ring homomorphism, so its kernel $M_\a
lpha = \{f \in C(X) : f(\alpha) = 0\}$ is a maximal ideal with $C(X)/M_\alpha \c
ong \mathbb{R}$.

We prove the classical result (Gillman–Jerison): **For a completely regular (Tyc
honoff) space $X$, $X$ is compact if and only if every maximal ideal of $C(X)$ i
s of the form $M_\alpha$.** The non-compact direction gives the desired existenc
e.

### Key definitions

- A **zero-set** is $Z(f) = \{x \in X : f(x) = 0\}$ for some $f \in C(X)$. Zero-
sets are closed.
- A maximal ideal $M$ is **fixed** if $\bigcap_{f \in M} Z(f) \neq \emptyset$, a
nd **free** otherwise.
- If $M$ is fixed, pick $\alpha \in \bigcap_{f \in M} Z(f)$; then $M \subseteq M
_\alpha$, so by maximality $M = M_\alpha$. Conversely, each $M_\alpha$ is fixed.
 **Thus $M = M_\alpha$ for some $\alpha$ iff $M$ is fixed.**

So it suffices to show: **if $X$ is non-compact, a free maximal ideal exists.**

### Step 1: Compact $\Rightarrow$ every maximal ideal is fixed

Let $M$ be a maximal ideal and consider $\{Z(f) : f \in M\}$. This family has th
e **finite intersection property**: if $Z(f_1) \cap \cdots \cap Z(f_n) = \emptys
et$, then $g = f_1^2 + \cdots + f_n^2 \in M$ (since each $f_i \in M$) and $g(x)
> 0$ for all $x$, so $g$ is a unit — contradicting $M$ being proper. Since $X$ i
s compact and zero-sets are closed, $\bigcap_{f \in M} Z(f) \neq \emptyset$. Hen
ce $M$ is fixed. $\checkmark$

### Step 2: Non-compact (completely regular) $\Rightarrow$ a free maximal ideal
exists

Since $X$ is non-compact, there exists a family $\{F_i\}_{i \in I}$ of closed se
ts with the finite intersection property and $\bigcap_{i \in I} F_i = \emptyset$
 (contrapositive of compactness).

Since $X$ is **completely regular**, every closed set is an intersection of zero
-sets: for closed $F$ and $x \notin F$, there exists $f_x \in C(X)$ with $f_x|_F
 = 0$ and $f_x(x) = 1$, so $F = \bigcap_{x \notin F} Z(f_x)$.

Write each $F_i = \bigcap_{j \in J_i} Z_{ij}$ where each $Z_{ij}$ is a zero-set.
 Set $\mathcal{Z} = \{Z_{ij} : i \in I,\, j \in J_i\}$.

**$\mathcal{Z}$ has FIP:** For any finite subfamily $Z_{i_1 j_1}, \ldots, Z_{i_n
 j_n}$, since $Z_{ij} \supseteq F_i$:
$$\bigcap_{k=1}^n Z_{i_k j_k} \supseteq \bigcap_{k=1}^n F_{i_k} \neq \emptyset.$
$

**$\mathcal{Z}$ has empty intersection:**
$$\bigcap_{Z \in \mathcal{Z}} Z = \bigcap_{i \in I} \bigcap_{j \in J_i} Z_{ij} =
 \bigcap_{i \in I} F_i = \emptyset.$$

**Extend to a z-ultrafilter:** Order all families of zero-sets with FIP by inclu
sion. Every chain has an upper bound (its union, which still has FIP). By **Zorn
's Lemma**, $\mathcal{Z}$ extends to a maximal such family $\mathcal{U}$ — a **z
-ultrafilter**. Since $\mathcal{Z} \subseteq \mathcal{U}$:
$$\bigcap_{Z \in \mathcal{U}} Z \subseteq \bigcap_{Z \in \mathcal{Z}} Z = \empty
set,$$
so $\mathcal{U}$ is **free**.

**Construct the maximal ideal:** Define
$$M = \{f \in C(X) : Z(f) \in \mathcal{U}\}.$$

*$M$ is an ideal:*
- $0 \in M$ since $Z(0) = X \in \mathcal{U}$.
- If $f \in M$, $h \in C(X)$: $Z(fh) \supseteq Z(f) \in \mathcal{U}$, and $\math
cal{U}$ is upward-closed, so $Z(fh) \in \mathcal{U}$, giving $fh \in M$.
- If $f, g \in M$: $Z(f), Z(g) \in \mathcal{U}$, so $Z(f) \cap Z(g) \in \mathcal
{U}$. Since $Z(f+g) \supseteq Z(f) \cap Z(g)$, upward-closure gives $Z(f+g) \in
\mathcal{U}$, so $f+g \in M$.

*$M$ is maximal:* Suppose $M \subsetneq M'$ for some proper ideal $M'$. Pick $f
\in M' \setminus M$, so $Z(f) \notin \mathcal{U}$. By the z-ultrafilter property
, there exists $Z_0 \in \mathcal{U}$ with $Z(f) \cap Z_0 = \emptyset$. Choose $g
 \in C(X)$ with $Z(g) = Z_0$; then $g \in M \subseteq M'$. So $f, g \in M'$, hen
ce $f^2 + g^2 \in M'$. But $Z(f^2 + g^2) = Z(f) \cap Z(g) = \emptyset$, so $f^2
+ g^2$ is a unit — contradicting $M'$ being proper. Thus $M$ is maximal. $\check
mark$

*$M$ is free:* $\bigcap_{f \in M} Z(f) = \bigcap_{Z \in \mathcal{U}} Z = \emptys
et$, so $M \neq M_\alpha$ for any $\alpha \in X$. $\checkmark$

### Conclusion

For a non-compact completely regular space $X$, the maximal ideal $M$ constructe
d above is **not** of the form $M_\alpha$ for any $\alpha \in X$. Combined with
Step 1, we have the equivalence:

$$X \text{ is compact} \iff \text{every maximal ideal of } C(X) \text{ is of the
 form } M_\alpha.$$

**Answer: Yes**, such a maximal ideal exists (for non-compact completely regular
 spaces, which is the natural setting for $C(X)$ theory).

### Remark on general topological spaces

For an *arbitrary* non-compact topological space, the result can fail. **Counter
example:** Let $X$ be an infinite set with the particular point topology (open s
ets are $\emptyset$ and all sets containing a distinguished point $p$). Then $X$
 is non-compact (the cover $\{\{p,x\} : x \in X\}$ has no finite subcover), but
every continuous function $f: X \to \mathbb{R}$ is constant (since for any open
$U \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, $f^{-1}(U) \neq \emptyset \Rightarrow p \in f^{-1}(U)
\Rightarrow f(p) \in U$, forcing $f \equiv f(p)$). Thus $C(X) \cong \mathbb{R}$,
 the only maximal ideal is $\{0\} = M_\alpha$ for every $\alpha$, and no free ma
ximal ideal exists. The precise characterization for general spaces is: a free m
aximal ideal exists iff $X$ is not **z-compact** (every z-ultrafilter is fixed),
 and z-compactness coincides with compactness precisely for completely regular s
paces.
