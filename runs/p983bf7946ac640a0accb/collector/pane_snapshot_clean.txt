Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there exists a sequence of
number fields with degrees tending to infinity, where a fixed prime $p$ splits c
ompletely in each.

**Answer: Yes, such a sequence exists for every prime $p$.**

## Construction

For each $n \geq 1$, define $K_n = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\ell_1}, \ldots, \sqrt{\ell_
n})$, where $\ell_1, \ell_2, \ldots$ is a sequence of distinct primes chosen so
that $p$ splits completely in each $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\ell_i})$:

- **If $p$ is odd:** choose $\ell_i \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$.
- **If $p = 2$:** choose $\ell_i \equiv 1 \pmod{8}$.

By Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions, infinitely many suc
h primes exist in each case, so we can select $n$ distinct ones for any $n$.

## The degrees tend to infinity

Since $\ell_1, \ldots, \ell_n$ are distinct primes, their classes in $\mathbb{Q}
^*/(\mathbb{Q}^*)^2$ are $\mathbb{F}_2$-linearly independent. By Kummer theory (
applicable since $\mu_2 = \{\pm1\} \subset \mathbb{Q}$), the multiquadratic exte
nsion $K_n/\mathbb{Q}$ has Galois group $\operatorname{Gal}(K_n/\mathbb{Q}) \con
g (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^n$, so

$$_n : \mathbb{Q}] = 2^n \longrightarrow \infty.$$

## $p$ splits completely in each $\mathcal{O}_{K_n}$

Each $K_n/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois (compositum of quadratic, hence Galois, extensio
ns). For a Galois extension, $p$ splits completely iff $p$ is unramified and the
 Frobenius $\mathrm{Frob}_p$ is trivial.

**$p$ is unramified in $K_n$:** A prime unramified in each component of a compos
itum is unramified in the compositum (the inertia group at $p$ in the compositum
 injects into the product of inertia groups of the components).

- *$p$ odd:* The discriminant of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\ell_i})$ is $\ell_i$ or $4\e
ll_i$; since $p \neq 2$ and $p \neq \ell_i$, we have $p \nmid \operatorname{disc
}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\ell_i}))$, so $p$ is unramified in each $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\e
ll_i})$.
- *$p = 2$:* Since $\ell_i \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$, the discriminant of $\mathbb{Q}(\
sqrt{\ell_i})$ is $\ell_i$ (odd), so $2$ is unramified in each $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt
{\ell_i})$.

**The Frobenius at $p$ is trivial:** The Frobenius at $p$ in $\operatorname{Gal}
(K_n/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^n$ is determined by its restrict
ion to each $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\ell_i})/\mathbb{Q})$. In a qua
dratic extension $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ (with $p \nmid 2d$), the Frobenius at $p
$ is trivial iff $\left(\frac{d}{p}\right) = 1$, i.e., iff $p$ splits.

- *$p$ odd:* $\ell_i \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$ gives $\left(\frac{\ell_i}{p}\right) = \
left(\frac{1}{p}\right) = 1$, so $\mathrm{Frob}_p$ is trivial in each $\mathbb{Q
}(\sqrt{\ell_i})$.
- *$p = 2$:* $\ell_i \equiv 1 \pmod{8}$ gives $\left(\frac{\ell_i}{2}\right) = 1
$, so $\mathrm{Frob}_2$ is trivial in each $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\ell_i})$.

Since the Frobenius restricts to the identity on every component, it is the iden
tity in $\operatorname{Gal}(K_n/\mathbb{Q})$. Combined with $p$ being unramified
, this means $p$ splits completely in $\mathcal{O}_{K_n}$: we have $p\mathcal{O}
_{K_n} = \mathfrak{p}_1 \cdots \mathfrak{p}_{2^n}$ with all ramification indices
 and residue degrees equal to $1$.

## Conclusion

The sequence $(K_n)$ with $K_n = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\ell_1}, \ldots, \sqrt{\ell_n}
)$ satisfies $_n:\mathbb{Q}] = 2^n \to \infty$ and $p$ splits completely in ea
ch $\mathcal{O}_{K_n}$. Therefore, such a sequence exists for every prime $p$.
