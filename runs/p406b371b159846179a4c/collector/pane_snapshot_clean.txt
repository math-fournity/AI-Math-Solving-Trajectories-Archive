## Solution

**Setup.** Three fair 10-sided dice, faces labeled $0,1,\dots,9$. Roll two dice,
 record their sum $S = d_1 + d_2$. Then roll the third die $d_3$. We want $\Pr[S
 = d_3]$.

**Total outcomes.** Each die has 10 faces, so the total number of equally likely
 outcomes is
$$10 \times 10 \times 10 = 1000.$$

**Counting favorable outcomes.** Since $d_3 \in \{0,1,\dots,9\}$, we only need $
S \in \{0,1,\dots,9\}$ (sums $10$ through $18$ can never equal $d_3$).

For a fixed value $t \in \{0,1,\dots,9\}$, the number of ordered pairs $(d_1, d_
2)$ with $d_1 + d_2 = t$ is $t+1$ (namely $(0,t),(1,t-1),\dots,(t,0)$).

For each such pair, $d_3$ must equal $t$, giving exactly 1 choice for $d_3$. Hen
ce the number of favorable outcomes is
$$\sum_{t=0}^{9} (t+1) \cdot 1 = 1 + 2 + 3 + \cdots + 10 = \frac{10 \cdot 11}{2}
 = 55.$$

**Probability.**
$$\Pr[S = d_3] = \frac{55}{1000} = \boxed{\dfrac{11}{200}}.$$
