## Proof

Each of the three sensors (M, S, C) independently reports one of {Red, Purple, S
ilver}, each with probability 1/3. The total sample space has $3^3 = 27$ equally
 likely outcomes.

Let $A$ = "at least one Red" and $B$ = "at most one Purple." We seek
$$P(\text{exactly one Silver} \mid A \cap B) = \frac{|\text{exactly one Silver}
\cap A \cap B|}{|A \cap B|}.$$

### Counting $|A \cap B|$

Partition by the number of Purple reports (0 or 1, since $B$ forbids 2 or 3).

**Case $P = 0$ (no Purple):** Each sensor reports Red or Silver only — $2^3 = 8$
 outcomes. Impose $A$ (at least one Red): exclude the all-Silver outcome. This g
ives $8 - 1 = 7$ outcomes.

**Case $P = 1$ (exactly one Purple):** Choose which sensor is Purple: $3$ ways.
The other two sensors report Red or Silver: $2^2 = 4$ ways each, giving $3 \cdot
 4 = 12$ outcomes. Impose $A$: exclude outcomes where the other two are both Sil
ver — $3$ such outcomes (one per choice of Purple sensor). This gives $12 - 3 =
9$ outcomes.

So $|A \cap B| = 7 + 9 = 16$.

### Counting $|\text{exactly one Silver} \cap A \cap B|$

**Case $P = 0$, exactly one Silver:** No Purple, so each sensor is Red or Silver
. Exactly one Silver means one Silver and two Red: $\binom{3}{1} = 3$ outcomes.
All satisfy $A$ (they have two Reds). Contribution: $3$.

**Case $P = 1$, exactly one Silver:** Exactly one Purple, exactly one Silver, ex
actly one Red — all three distinct outcomes assigned to the three sensors. The n
umber of permutations of $(P, \text{Si}, R)$ across three sensors is $3! = 6$. A
ll satisfy $A$ (each has one Red). Contribution: $6$.

So $|\text{exactly one Silver} \cap A \cap B| = 3 + 6 = 9$.

### Final computation

$$P(\text{exactly one Silver} \mid A \cap B) = \frac{9}{16}.$$

Since $\gcd(9, 16) = 1$, we have $m = 9$ and $n = 16$, giving

$$100m + n = 100 \cdot 9 + 16 = 916.$$
