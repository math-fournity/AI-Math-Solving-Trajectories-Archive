**Answer: Yes, such a diffeomorphism exists.**

**Proof.**

Let $K \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ be compact with $C^2$ boundary $S$, and let $x \in
S$. Denote by $T$ the tangent line to $S$ at $x$. We construct a $C^2$ diffeomor
phism $f:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2$ with $f(x)=x$, $f(S)$ a $C^2$ curve, and $
f(S)$ coinciding with $T$ in a neighborhood of $x$.

**Step 1 (Normalization).** Let $R$ be the rigid motion (rotation + translation)
 sending $x$ to the origin $0$ and $T$ to the $x$-axis. Since $S$ is a $C^2$ cur
ve and $T$ is its tangent at $x$, in the coordinates given by $R$, the curve $R(
S)$ passes through $0$ with horizontal tangent. By the $C^2$ implicit function t
heorem, there exists $\delta>0$ such that near $0$, $R(S)$ is the graph of a $C^
2$ function:
$$R(S) \cap \{(u,v): |u|<\delta\} = \{(u, g(u)) : |u|<\delta\},$$
where $g:(-\delta,\delta)\to\mathbb{R}$ is $C^2$ with $g(0)=0$ and $g'(0)=0$.

**Step 2 (Bump extension).** Choose a smooth bump function $\varphi:\mathbb{R}\t
o\mathbb{R}$ with $\varphi\equiv 1$ on $(-\delta/3,\delta/3)$ and $\operatorname
{supp}\varphi\subset(-\delta/2,\delta/2)\subset(-\delta,\delta)$. Define
$$h(u) = \begin{cases} \varphi(u)\,g(u), & |u|<\delta,\\ 0, & |u|\ge\delta/2. \e
nd{cases}$$
These definitions agree on the overlap (where $\varphi=0$), so $h:\mathbb{R}\to\
mathbb{R}$ is well-defined and $C^2$ (product of $C^\infty$ and $C^2$ on $(-\del
ta,\delta)$, identically $0$ outside). Moreover $h$ has compact support, $h(0)=g
(0)=0$, and $h'(0)=\varphi'(0)g(0)+\varphi(0)g'(0)=0$.

**Step 3 (The flattening map).** Define $F:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2$ by
$$F(u,v) = (u,\; v - h(u)).$$

*Jacobian:* $DF(u,v) = \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-h'(u)&1\end{pmatrix}$, so $\det DF =
 1$ everywhere. Thus $F$ is a local $C^2$ diffeomorphism at every point.

*Global diffeomorphism:* $F$ is injective: $F(u_1,v_1)=F(u_2,v_2)$ implies $u_1=
u_2$ and $v_1-h(u_1)=v_2-h(u_2)$, hence $v_1=v_2$. $F$ is surjective: given $(a,
b)$, the point $(a,\,b+h(a))$ maps to $(a,b)$. Being a bijective local $C^2$ dif
feomorphism, $F$ is a global $C^2$ diffeomorphism.

*Fixes the origin:* $F(0,0)=(0,\,0-h(0))=(0,0)$. ✓

*Flattens the curve near $0$:* For $|u|<\delta/3$, we have $\varphi\equiv 1$, so
 $h(u)=g(u)$. Points on $R(S)$ near $0$ have the form $(u,g(u))$, and
$$F(u,g(u)) = (u,\; g(u)-g(u)) = (u,0),$$
which lies on the $x$-axis. Hence $F(R(S))$ coincides with the $x$-axis (i.e., $
R(T)$) in a neighborhood of $0$.

**Step 4 (Conjugate back).** Set
$$f = R^{-1}\circ F\circ R : \mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2.$$

- $f$ is a $C^2$ diffeomorphism (composition of $C^2$ diffeomorphisms).
- $f(x) = R^{-1}(F(R(x))) = R^{-1}(F(0)) = R^{-1}(0) = x$. ✓
- $f(S) = R^{-1}(F(R(S)))$ is a $C^2$ curve (image of a $C^2$ curve under a $C^2
$ diffeomorphism). ✓
- Near $x$: $R(S)$ near $0$ is $\{(u,g(u))\}$, $F$ maps it to $\{(u,0)\}$ (the $
x$-axis $=R(T)$), and $R^{-1}$ maps $R(T)$ back to $T$. So $f(S)$ coincides with
 $T$ in a neighborhood of $x$. ✓

All three required properties are satisfied. $\blacksquare$
