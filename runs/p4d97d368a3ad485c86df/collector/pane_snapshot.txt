## Proof

Let $s = x+y$ and $p = xy$. We are given $x+y = x^3+y^3 = x^5+y^5 = a$, so $s =
a$.

Recall the Newton-type identities:
- $x^3+y^3 = s^3 - 3ps$
- $x^5+y^5 = s^5 - 5ps^3 + 5p^2s$

**Case 1: $a = 0$.** Then $x+y=0$, and automatically $x^3+y^3=(x+y)(x^2-xy+y^2)=
0$ and $x^5+y^5=(x+y)(x^4-x^3y+x^2y^2-xy^3+y^4)=0$. So $a=0$ is attainable (e.g.
 $x=1,y=-1$).

**Case 2: $a \neq 0$.** From $x^3+y^3 = a$:
$$a^3 - 3pa = a \implies 3pa = a^3 - a \implies p = \frac{a^2-1}{3}.$$

From $x^5+y^5 = a$:
$$a^5 - 5pa^3 + 5p^2a = a.$$

Dividing by $a \neq 0$ and substituting $p = (a^2-1)/3$:
$$a^4 - \frac{5(a^2-1)a^2}{3} + \frac{5(a^2-1)^2}{9} = 1.$$

Multiplying by $9$ and setting $u = a^2$:
$$9u^2 - 15(u-1)u + 5(u-1)^2 = 9.$$
$$9u^2 - 15u^2 + 15u + 5u^2 - 10u + 5 = 9.$$
$$-u^2 + 5u - 4 = 0 \implies u^2 - 5u + 4 = 0 \implies (u-1)(u-4)=0.$$

So $a^2 = 1$ or $a^2 = 4$, giving $a \in \{-2, -1, 1, 2\}$.

**Reality check.** For $x,y$ to be real, the discriminant of $t^2 - st + p = 0$
must be non-negative: $s^2 - 4p \geq 0$.

- $a = 1$: $s=1, p=0$, discriminant $= 1 \geq 0$. Roots $1, 0$. ✓
- $a = -1$: $s=-1, p=0$, discriminant $= 1 \geq 0$. Roots $-1, 0$. ✓
- $a = 2$: $s=2, p=1$, discriminant $= 4-4 = 0$. Roots $1, 1$. ✓
- $a = -2$: $s=-2, p=1$, discriminant $= 4-4 = 0$. Roots $-1, -1$. ✓

All four values yield real $x, y$ and satisfy all three equations.

**Conclusion.** The possible values of $a$ are
$$\boxed{a \in \{-2,\, -1,\, 0,\, 1,\, 2\}}.$$
