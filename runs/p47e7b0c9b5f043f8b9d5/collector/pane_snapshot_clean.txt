## Fourier Series Expansion of the Dirac Delta Function on $(-\pi, \pi)$

### Setup

On the interval $-\pi < x < \pi$, a (sufficiently regular) function $f(x)$ admit
s the Fourier series

$$
f(x) \;=\; \frac{a_0}{2} \;+\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl[\,a_n \cos(nx) + b_n \si
n(nx)\,\bigr],
$$

with coefficients

$$
a_0 = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(x)\,dx, \qquad
a_n = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(x)\cos(nx)\,dx, \qquad
b_n = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(x)\sin(nx)\,dx.
$$

We take $f(x) = \delta(x)$, the Dirac delta distribution, whose support is at $x
=0$ (which lies inside $(-\pi,\pi)$). The defining sifting property we use is

$$
\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \delta(x)\,\varphi(x)\,dx \;=\; \varphi(0)
$$

for every test function $\varphi$ smooth near $0$.

### Computing the coefficients

**Constant term.** Taking $\varphi(x) = 1$,

$$
a_0 \;=\; \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\delta(x)\,dx \;=\; \frac{1}{\pi}\cdot 1
 \;=\; \frac{1}{\pi}.
$$

**Cosine coefficients.** Taking $\varphi(x) = \cos(nx)$,

$$
a_n \;=\; \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\delta(x)\cos(nx)\,dx \;=\; \frac{1}{\pi
}\cos(0) \;=\; \frac{1}{\pi}, \qquad n \ge 1.
$$

**Sine coefficients.** Taking $\varphi(x) = \sin(nx)$,

$$
b_n \;=\; \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\delta(x)\sin(nx)\,dx \;=\; \frac{1}{\pi
}\sin(0) \;=\; 0, \qquad n \ge 1.
$$

The sine coefficients vanish because $\sin(nx)$ is odd and $\delta(x)$ is even (
the integrand is odd, consistent with the sifting value $\sin 0 = 0$).

### The expansion

Substituting the coefficients,

$$
\boxed{\;\delta(x) \;=\; \frac{1}{2\pi} \;+\; \frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\c
os(nx), \qquad -\pi < x < \pi.\;}
$$

### Verification (distributional consistency)

To confirm the result is correct in the distributional sense, we check that the
partial sums

$$
S_N(x) \;=\; \frac{1}{2\pi} + \frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\cos(nx)
$$

converge to $\delta(x)$ as $N\to\infty$. Using the Dirichlet kernel identity

$$
\frac{1}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{N}\cos(nx) \;=\; \frac{\sin\!\bigl((N+\tfrac{1}{2})x\b
igr)}{2\sin(x/2)},
$$

we obtain

$$
S_N(x) \;=\; \frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{\sin\!\bigl((N+\tfrac{1}{2})x\bigr)}{\sin(
x/2)} \;=\; \frac{1}{\pi}\,D_N(x),
$$

where $D_N(x) = \frac{\sin((N+1/2)x)}{2\sin(x/2)}$ is the Dirichlet kernel. Two
standard properties of $D_N$ confirm the convergence:

1. **Normalization.** $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} D_N(x)\,dx = \pi$ for all
$N$, hence $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} S_N(x)\,dx = 1$, matching $\int \delt
a(x)\,dx = 1$.

2. **Concentration.** For any test function $\varphi$,
$$
\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} S_N(x)\,\varphi(x)\,dx \;=\; \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} D_
N(x)\,\varphi(x)\,dx \;\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}\; \varphi(0),
$$
since $D_N$ is an approximate identity (its mass concentrates at $x=0$ with widt
h $\sim 1/N$ and height $\sim N$). This is exactly the sifting property of $\del
ta(x)$.

Therefore $S_N \to \delta$ in the distributional sense, validating the expansion
.

### Interpretation

The result states that the Dirac delta is the superposition of a uniform "DC" co
mponent $\tfrac{1}{2\pi}$ and all cosine harmonics of equal amplitude $\tfrac{1}
{\pi}$. The sine harmonics are absent because $\delta(x)$ is even. Equivalently,
 using $\cos(nx) = \tfrac{1}{2}(e^{inx}+e^{-inx})$, the complex-form expansion i
s

$$
\delta(x) \;=\; \frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} e^{inx},
$$

with every Fourier mode (including $n=0$) carrying equal weight $\tfrac{1}{2\pi}
$ — reflecting the fact that the delta function contains all frequencies with un
iform strength.
