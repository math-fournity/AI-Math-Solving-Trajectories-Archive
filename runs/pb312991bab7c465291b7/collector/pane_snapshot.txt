## Proof

**Setup.** In the simple linear regression $y_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_i + \varep
silon_i$, the design matrix $\mathbf{X}$ is $n \times 2$ with $i$-th row $\tilde
{x}_i^\top = (1,\, x_i)$. Define

$$
\hat{Q}_{xx} = \frac{\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X}}{n} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \t
ilde{x}_i \tilde{x}_i^\top, \qquad Q_{xx} = \mathbb{E}[\tilde{x}_i \tilde{x}_i^\
top].
$$

We assume:
- **(A1)** $\{(x_i, \varepsilon_i)\}$ are i.i.d. with $\mathbb{E}[x_i^4] < \inft
y$ (finite fourth moment),
- **(A2)** $Q_{xx}$ is positive definite (hence invertible).

We are given that $\hat{Q}_{xx}^{-1} \stackrel{p}{\longrightarrow} Q_{xx}^{-1}$
and asked to verify whether the rate is $O_p(n^{-1/2})$.

---

**Step 1. $\hat{Q}_{xx} - Q_{xx} = O_p(n^{-1/2})$.**

Each entry of $\hat{Q}_{xx} - Q_{xx}$ is a sample mean of i.i.d. mean-zero rando
m variables of the form $\tilde{x}_{i,a}\tilde{x}_{i,b} - \mathbb{E}[\tilde{x}_{
i,a}\tilde{x}_{i,b}]$ for $a,b \in \{1,2\}$. By (A1), $\mathbb{E}[(\tilde{x}_{i,
a}\tilde{x}_{i,b})^2] < \infty$ (since $\tilde{x}_{i,a}\tilde{x}_{i,b}$ involves
 at most $x_i^2$, whose second moment $\mathbb{E}[x_i^4]$ is finite). Hence the
Lindeberg–Lévy CLT applies:

$$
\sqrt{n}\bigl(\hat{Q}_{xx} - Q_{xx}\bigr) \stackrel{d}{\longrightarrow} \mathcal
{N}(0,\, \Sigma),
$$

where $\Sigma = \operatorname{Var}(\tilde{x}_i \tilde{x}_i^\top)$ is a finite $4
\times 4$ covariance matrix. Convergence in distribution implies boundedness in
probability, so

$$
\hat{Q}_{xx} - Q_{xx} = O_p(n^{-1/2}). \tag{1}
$$

---

**Step 2. First-order expansion of the matrix inverse.**

Let $H = \hat{Q}_{xx} - Q_{xx}$. Since $Q_{xx}$ is invertible by (A2), for $\|H\
|$ sufficiently small the Neumann series gives

$$
(Q_{xx}+H)^{-1} = Q_{xx}^{-1} - Q_{xx}^{-1} H Q_{xx}^{-1} + Q_{xx}^{-1} H Q_{xx}
^{-1} H Q_{xx}^{-1} - \cdots
$$

so that

$$
\hat{Q}_{xx}^{-1} - Q_{xx}^{-1} = -Q_{xx}^{-1}(\hat{Q}_{xx}-Q_{xx})Q_{xx}^{-1} +
 R_n, \tag{2}
$$

where the remainder satisfies $\|R_n\| \le C\,\|\hat{Q}_{xx}-Q_{xx}\|^2$ for som
e constant $C$ depending only on $Q_{xx}^{-1}$.

---

**Step 3. Rate of the remainder.**

From (1), $\|\hat{Q}_{xx}-Q_{xx}\| = O_p(n^{-1/2})$, hence

$$
\|R_n\| \le C\,\|\hat{Q}_{xx}-Q_{xx}\|^2 = O_p(n^{-1}). \tag{3}
$$

---

**Step 4. Combine.**

The leading term in (2) is $-Q_{xx}^{-1}(\hat{Q}_{xx}-Q_{xx})Q_{xx}^{-1}$, which
 is a fixed linear map applied to an $O_p(n^{-1/2})$ quantity, so it is $O_p(n^{
-1/2})$. The remainder is $O_p(n^{-1})$, which is of smaller order. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\hat{Q}_{xx}^{-1} - Q_{xx}^{-1} = O_p(n^{-1/2})}. \tag{4}
$$

---

**Conclusion.** Under the standard regularity conditions (i.i.d. data, finite fo
urth moment of $x_i$, and invertibility of $Q_{xx}$), the convergence rate of $\
hat{Q}_{xx}^{-1}$ to $Q_{xx}^{-1}$ is indeed $O_p(n^{-1/2})$. The key mechanism
is:

1. The sample average $\hat{Q}_{xx}$ converges to $Q_{xx}$ at the CLT rate $O_p(
n^{-1/2})$.
2. Matrix inversion is continuously differentiable at any invertible matrix, so
the delta method / first-order Taylor expansion transmits the $O_p(n^{-1/2})$ ra
te to the inverse, with the nonlinear remainder being $O_p(n^{-1})$.

Thus the rate $O_p(n^{-1/2})$ is **verified**.
