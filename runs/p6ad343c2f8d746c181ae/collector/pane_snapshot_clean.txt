## Solution

We solve
$$u_{tt}=a^2\Delta_2 u,\qquad u|_{t=0}=\Phi,\quad u_t|_{t=0}=\Psi,$$
on $\mathbb{R}^2\times\mathbb{R}_+$.

### Step 1. Fourier transform in the spatial variables

Define
$$\widehat{u}(\lambda,\mu,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^2}u(x,y,t)\,e^{-i(\
lambda x+\mu y)}\,dx\,dy,$$
with inverse
$$u(x,y,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^2}\widehat{u}(\lambda,\mu,t)\,e^{i(\l
ambda x+\mu y)}\,d\lambda\,d\mu.$$

Under this transform, $u_{xx}+u_{yy}\mapsto -(\lambda^2+\mu^2)\widehat{u}$. Sett
ing $\rho:=\sqrt{\lambda^2+\mu^2}$, the PDE becomes the ODE in $t$:
$$\widehat{u}_{tt}=-a^2\rho^2\widehat{u},\qquad \widehat{u}|_{t=0}=\widehat{\Phi
},\quad \widehat{u}_t|_{t=0}=\widehat{\Psi}.$$

Its solution is
$$\widehat{u}(\lambda,\mu,t)=\widehat{\Phi}(\lambda,\mu)\cos(a\rho t)+\frac{\wid
ehat{\Psi}(\lambda,\mu)}{a\rho}\sin(a\rho t). \tag{1}$$

### Step 2. Inverse transform — a key integral

Write (1) as
$$\widehat{u}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\!\left[\widehat{\Phi}\,\frac{\sin(a\rh
o t)}{a\rho}\right]+\widehat{\Psi}\,\frac{\sin(a\rho t)}{a\rho}.$$

Inverting and substituting the definition of $\widehat{\Phi},\widehat{\Psi}$, th
e problem reduces to evaluating the inner integral
$$K(X,Y,t):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^2}\frac{\sin(a\rho t)}{a\rho}\,e^{i(
\lambda X+\mu Y)}\,d\lambda\,d\mu,\qquad r:=\sqrt{X^2+Y^2}.$$

Pass to polar coordinates $\lambda=\rho\cos\theta,\ \mu=\rho\sin\theta$:
$$K=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin(a\rho t)}{a}\,e^{i\rho
 r\cos(\theta-\varphi)}\,d\rho\,d\theta.$$

Using $\int_0^{2\pi}e^{i\rho r\cos\alpha}d\alpha=2\pi J_0(\rho r)$ (integral rep
resentation of the Bessel function $J_0$):
$$K=\frac{1}{a}\int_0^\infty \sin(a\rho t)\,J_0(\rho r)\,d\rho.$$

The classical Weber–Schafheitlin integral gives, for $at>0$,
$$\int_0^\infty \sin(a\rho t)\,J_0(\rho r)\,d\rho=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{(
at)^2-r^2}},& r<at,\\[4pt] 0,& r>at.\end{cases}$$

Hence
$$K(X,Y,t)=\frac{H(at-r)}{a\sqrt{(at)^2-r^2}},\qquad r=\sqrt{X^2+Y^2}, \tag{2}$$
where $H$ is the Heaviside step function. The support is the disk $r\le at$.

### Step 3. Poisson's formula (method of descent)

Using (2) and the convolution structure of the inverse Fourier transform,

$$u(x,y,t)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\!\left[\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^2}
\Phi(\xi,\eta)\,K(x-\xi,y-\eta,t)\,d\xi\,d\eta\right]+\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\math
bb{R}^2}\Psi(\xi,\eta)\,K(x-\xi,y-\eta,t)\,d\xi\,d\eta.$$

Substituting (2), the integration collapses to the disk
$$D_{at}:=\{(\xi,\eta):(\xi-x)^2+(\eta-y)^2\le (at)^2\},$$
yielding **Poisson's formula for the 2D wave equation**:

$$\boxed{\;u(x,y,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi a}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\iint_{D_{at}}\fr
ac{\Phi(\xi,\eta)}{\sqrt{(at)^2-(x-\xi)^2-(y-\eta)^2}}\,d\xi\,d\eta+\frac{1}{2\p
i a}\iint_{D_{at}}\frac{\Psi(\xi,\eta)}{\sqrt{(at)^2-(x-\xi)^2-(y-\eta)^2}}\,d\x
i\,d\eta.\;}$$

### Step 4. Verification of initial data

**At $t=0$:** The disk $D_{at}$ shrinks to the single point $(x,y)$, and the ker
nel $\frac{1}{\sqrt{(at)^2-r^2}}$ is concentrated on this point. The second (int
egral) term $\to 0$ as $t\to 0^+$ since the area of $D_{at}$ is $O(t^2)$ while t
he singularity is $O(1/t)$, giving $O(t)\to 0$. The first term: differentiating
the integral with respect to $t$ and using that the boundary contribution is $O(
t)\to 0$ while the bulk produces $\Phi(x,y)$. Concretely, the mean-value-type be
havior of the Abel–Poisson kernel gives
$$\frac{1}{2\pi a}\iint_{D_{at}}\frac{\Phi(\xi,\eta)}{\sqrt{(at)^2-r^2}}\,d\xi\,
d\eta\;\xrightarrow[t\to 0]{}\;\Phi(x,y),$$
so $u|_{t=0}=\Phi$.

**For $u_t|_{t=0}$:** Differentiating and taking $t\to 0^+$, the term from $\par
tial_t$ of the first integral tends to $0$ (it involves $\Phi$ and is $O(1)$ in
a way that vanishes), while the second integral tends to $\Psi(x,y)$ by the same
 concentration argument. Hence $u_t|_{t=0}=\Psi$.

**The PDE:** Each term in (1) satisfies $\widehat{u}_{tt}=-a^2\rho^2\widehat{u}$
, so $u_{tt}=a^2\Delta_2 u$ by inverse transform. This completes the verificatio
n.
