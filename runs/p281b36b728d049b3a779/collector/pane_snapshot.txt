**Answer: Yes, the statement is true.** Below is the proof.

---

## Part 1: $N = p = 1$, i.e. $W^{1,1}(I) \subset C^0(I)$

Let $I = (a,b)$ be a bounded interval and let $u \in W^{1,1}(I)$. By definition
$u \in L^1(I)$ and the weak derivative $u' \in L^1(I)$.

Pick any point $c \in (a,b)$ and define
$$
v(x) := \int_c^x u'(t)\,dt, \qquad x \in (a,b).
$$
Since $u' \in L^1(I)$, the function $v$ is **absolutely continuous** on $(a,b)$,
 hence continuous: $v \in C^0(I)$. By the Lebesgue differentiation theorem, $v'
= u'$ a.e.

Now consider $w := u - v$. Its weak derivative is $w' = u' - v' = 0$ a.e. On an
interval, a function with zero weak derivative is a.e. equal to a constant (this
 follows by mollification: $w * \rho_\varepsilon$ has derivative zero, hence is
constant, and $w * \rho_\varepsilon \to w$ in $L^1_{\text{loc}}$). Thus there ex
ists a constant $C$ with
$$
u = v + C \quad \text{a.e. on } I.
$$
The right-hand side $v + C$ is continuous. Therefore $u$ admits a continuous rep
resentative, and identifying $u$ with this representative,
$$
W^{1,1}(I) \subset C^0(I).
$$

## Part 2: $N = p \geq 2$, i.e. $W^{1,N}(\Omega) \not\subset C^0(\Omega)$

We exhibit an explicit counterexample. Let $\Omega = B(0,1/e) \subset \mathbb{R}
^N$ with $N \geq 2$, and define
$$
u(x) := \log\log\frac{1}{|x|}, \qquad x \neq 0,
$$
with $u(0)$ assigned any value (say $0$).

**Step 1: $u \in L^N(\Omega)$.** Using polar coordinates ($dx = r^{N-1}dr\,d\sig
ma$),
$$
\int_\Omega |u|^N\,dx = |S^{N-1}|\int_0^{1/e} \bigl(\log\log(1/r)\bigr)^N r^{N-1
}\,dr.
$$
Near $r = 0$, $\log\log(1/r)$ grows more slowly than any negative power of $r$,
while $r^{N-1}$ is integrable. Hence the integral is finite.

**Step 2: $\nabla u \in L^N(\Omega)$.** For $x \neq 0$,
$$
\nabla u(x) = \frac{d}{d|x|}\Bigl[\log\log(1/|x|)\Bigr]\cdot\frac{x}{|x|} = \fra
c{-1}{|x|\,\log(1/|x|)}\cdot\frac{x}{|x|},
$$
so
$$
|\nabla u(x)| = \frac{1}{|x|\,\log(1/|x|)}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\int_\Omega |\nabla u|^N\,dx = |S^{N-1}|\int_0^{1/e} \frac{1}{r^N\bigl(\log(1/r)
\bigr)^N}\,r^{N-1}\,dr = |S^{N-1}|\int_0^{1/e}\frac{dr}{r\,\bigl(\log(1/r)\bigr)
^N}.
$$
Substituting $s = \log(1/r)$, so $ds = -dr/r$, with $r = 1/e \Rightarrow s = 1$
and $r \to 0 \Rightarrow s \to +\infty$:
$$
\int_\Omega |\nabla u|^N\,dx = |S^{N-1}|\int_1^{\infty}\frac{ds}{s^N} = |S^{N-1}
|\cdot\frac{1}{N-1} < \infty \qquad (N \geq 2).
$$
Thus $u \in W^{1,N}(\Omega)$.

**Step 3: $u$ is not continuous.** As $|x| \to 0$,
$$
u(x) = \log\log(1/|x|) \to +\infty.
$$
So $u$ is **unbounded** in every neighborhood of the origin. A continuous functi
on on the bounded set $\overline{\Omega}$ is bounded; consequently $u$ cannot co
incide a.e. with any continuous function. Hence
$$
W^{1,N}(\Omega) \not\subset C^0(\Omega), \qquad N \geq 2.
$$

---

## Conclusion

The critical Sobolev embedding $W^{1,p} \hookrightarrow C^0$ at the threshold $p
 = N$ **succeeds in dimension $N = 1$** (because one-dimensional Sobolev functio
ns are absolutely continuous) but **fails in every dimension $N \geq 2$** (witne
ssed by the logarithmic singularity $\log\log(1/|x|)$). Therefore the asserted s
tatement is **true**:

$$
W^{1,1}(I) \subset C^0(I), \qquad \text{but} \qquad W^{1,N}(\Omega) \not\subset
C^0(\Omega) \text{ for } N = 2,3,4,\dots
$$
