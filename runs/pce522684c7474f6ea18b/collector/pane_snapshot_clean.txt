## Solution

We evaluate
$$I = \int_{S^2} \frac{dS}{\sqrt{(x-a)^2+y^2+z^2}}, \qquad a>1,$$
where $S^2 = \{(x,y,z): x^2+y^2+z^2=1\}$.

**Parametrization.** Use spherical coordinates with the $x$-axis as the polar ax
is:
$$x=\cos\theta,\quad y=\sin\theta\cos\phi,\quad z=\sin\theta\sin\phi,\qquad \the
ta\in[0,\pi],\;\phi\in[0,2\pi],$$
with surface element $dS = \sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi$.

**Simplify the integrand.** Since $x^2+y^2+z^2=1$ on $S^2$,
$$(x-a)^2+y^2+z^2 = (x^2+y^2+z^2)-2ax+a^2 = 1-2a\cos\theta+a^2.$$

Hence
$$I = \int_0^{2\pi}d\phi\int_0^\pi \frac{\sin\theta\,d\theta}{\sqrt{a^2-2a\cos\t
heta+1}} = 2\pi\int_0^\pi \frac{\sin\theta\,d\theta}{\sqrt{a^2-2a\cos\theta+1}}.
$$

**Substitution $u=\cos\theta$.** With $du=-\sin\theta\,d\theta$, and limits $\th
eta=0\Rightarrow u=1$, $\theta=\pi\Rightarrow u=-1$:
$$I = 2\pi\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{du}{\sqrt{a^2+1-2au}}.$$

**Evaluate the inner integral.** Set $v=a^2+1-2au$, $dv=-2a\,du$:
$$\int\frac{du}{\sqrt{a^2+1-2au}} = -\frac{1}{a}\sqrt{a^2+1-2au}.$$

Evaluating from $u=-1$ to $u=1$:
$$\left[-\frac{1}{a}\sqrt{a^2+1-2au}\right]_{-1}^{1} = -\frac{1}{a}\sqrt{a^2+1-2
a}+\frac{1}{a}\sqrt{a^2+1+2a}.$$

Since $a>1$,
$$\sqrt{a^2-2a+1}=\sqrt{(a-1)^2}=a-1,\qquad \sqrt{a^2+2a+1}=\sqrt{(a+1)^2}=a+1.$
$

Therefore
$$\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{du}{\sqrt{a^2+1-2au}} = -\frac{a-1}{a}+\frac{a+1}{a} = \fra
c{2}{a}.$$

**Final result.**
$$I = 2\pi\cdot\frac{2}{a} = \boxed{\dfrac{4\pi}{a}}.$$

*Remark.* This agrees with Newton's shell theorem: a uniform spherical shell of
radius $1$ and surface density $1$ (total "mass" $4\pi$) produces, at any exteri
or point distance $a$ from the center, the same potential as a point mass $4\pi$
 at the center, namely $4\pi/a$.
