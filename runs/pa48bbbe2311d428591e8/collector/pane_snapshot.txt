**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

Recall the order of growth of an entire function $f$ is

$$\rho_0 = \limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log\log M(r)}{\log r}, \qquad M(r):=\max_{
|z|=r}|f(z)|.$$

**Key claim.** For every $\rho < \rho_0$,

$$\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log M(r)}{r^\rho}=+\infty. \tag{$\ast$}$$

*Proof of claim.* Suppose for contradiction that $(\ast)$ fails, i.e. $\limsup_{
r\to\infty}\frac{\log M(r)}{r^\rho}<\infty$. Then there exist $C>0$ and $R>0$ su
ch that for all $r\ge R$,

$$\log M(r)\le Cr^\rho, \quad\text{i.e.,}\quad M(r)\le e^{Cr^\rho}.$$

Taking logs again (for $r$ large enough that $M(r)\ge 1$, which holds since $f$
is nonconstant entire—otherwise $\rho_0=0\le\rho$ trivially):

$$\log\log M(r)\le \log C+\rho\log r.$$

Hence

$$\rho_0=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log\log M(r)}{\log r}\le \rho,$$

contradicting $\rho<\rho_0$. $\square$

**Constructing the sequence.** By $(\ast)$, there exists a sequence $r_n\to\inft
y$ with

$$\frac{\log M(r_n)}{r_n^\rho}\to+\infty.$$

By the maximum modulus principle, for each $n$ there exists $z_n$ with $|z_n|=r_
n$ and $|f(z_n)|=M(r_n)$. Then $|z_n|=r_n\to\infty$ and

$$\frac{\log|f(z_n)|}{|z_n|^\rho}=\frac{\log M(r_n)}{r_n^\rho}\to+\infty. \tag{$
\ast\ast$}$$

**Verification.** Let $A,B>0$ be arbitrary. From $(\ast\ast)$, for all sufficien
tly large $n$,

$$\frac{\log|f(z_n)|}{|z_n|^\rho}>B+\frac{\log A}{|z_n|^\rho}.$$

Since $|z_n|^\rho\to\infty$, the term $\frac{\log A}{|z_n|^\rho}\to 0$, so for l
arge enough $n$,

$$\log|f(z_n)|>B|z_n|^\rho+\log A,$$

i.e.,

$$|f(z_n)|>Ae^{B|z_n|^\rho}.$$

Thus the inequality $|f(z_n)|\le Ae^{B|z_n|^\rho}$ fails for all sufficiently la
rge $n$. Since $A,B>0$ were arbitrary, the sequence $(z_n)$ satisfies the requir
ed property. $\blacksquare$
