**Statement:** Any linear subspace of a Banach space that contains the unit ball
 is the whole space.

**Answer: True.**

**Proof.**

Let $X$ be a Banach space with norm $\|\cdot\|$, and let $B = \{x \in X : \|x\|
\le 1\}$ be its closed unit ball. Let $M \subseteq X$ be a linear subspace such
that $B \subseteq M$. We claim $M = X$.

Let $x \in X$ be arbitrary. If $x = 0$, then $x \in M$ since $M$ is a linear sub
space (every subspace contains $0$).

If $x \neq 0$, set $u = \frac{x}{\|x\|}$. Then $\|u\| = 1 \le 1$, so $u \in B \s
ubseteq M$. Since $M$ is a linear subspace, it is closed under scalar multiplica
tion, and therefore
$$x = \|x\| \cdot u \in M.$$

Since $x \in X$ was arbitrary, $X \subseteq M$. The reverse inclusion $M \subset
eq X$ holds by hypothesis. Hence $M = X$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The argument uses only that $M$ is a linear subspace (closed under s
calar multiplication) and that $B \subseteq M$. Neither the completeness of $X$
nor the closedness of $M$ is required; the result holds for any normed space and
 any algebraic linear subspace containing the unit ball.
