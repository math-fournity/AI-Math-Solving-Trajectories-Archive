# Fundamental Group of the n-Sphere for $n \geq 2$

**Claim.** For $n \geq 2$, the fundamental group of $S^n$ is trivial:
$$\pi_1(S^n) \cong \{1\}.$$

## Proof

We use the Seifert–van Kampen theorem. Let $n \geq 2$ and write the sphere as th
e union of two open sets obtained by deleting one point each:

$$S^n = U \cup V, \qquad U = S^n \setminus \{N\}, \quad V = S^n \setminus \{S\},
$$

where $N$ and $S$ are the north and south poles.

**Step 1. $U$ and $V$ are contractible.**

By stereographic projection, $U = S^n \setminus \{N\}$ is homeomorphic to $\math
bb{R}^n$, and likewise $V = S^n \setminus \{S\}$ is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^
n$. Since $\mathbb{R}^n$ is contractible, both $U$ and $V$ are contractible. Hen
ce

$$\pi_1(U) \cong \pi_1(V) \cong \{1\}.$$

**Step 2. The intersection $U \cap V$ is path-connected for $n \geq 2$.**

We have
$$U \cap V = S^n \setminus \{N, S\}.$$

Under stereographic projection from $N$, this corresponds to $\mathbb{R}^n \setm
inus \{0\}$, which deformation retracts onto $S^{n-1}$. For $n \geq 2$, we have
$n - 1 \geq 1$, so $S^{n-1}$ is path-connected. Therefore $U \cap V$ is path-con
nected.

**Step 3. Apply Seifert–van Kampen.**

Choose a basepoint $x_0 \in U \cap V$. Since $U$ and $V$ are both simply connect
ed (each contractible), the Seifert–van Kampen theorem gives

$$\pi_1(S^n, x_0) \cong \pi_1(U, x_0) \;*_{\pi_1(U \cap V, x_0)}\; \pi_1(V, x_0)
 \cong \{1\} *_{\pi_1(U \cap V, x_0)} \{1\}.$$

The amalgamated free product of two trivial groups is trivial, regardless of the
 amalgamating subgroup. Concretely, every generator of the free product comes fr
om either $\pi_1(U)$ or $\pi_1(V)$, and both are trivial, so the resulting group
 is generated by the identity alone. Thus

$$\pi_1(S^n) \cong \{1\}.$$

**Remark on why $n \geq 2$ is necessary.** For $n = 1$, the intersection $U \cap
 V \cong \mathbb{R}^1 \setminus \{0\}$ has two path components, so Seifert–van K
ampen in the form above does not apply, and indeed $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$
. The argument above crucially uses the path-connectedness of $U \cap V$, which
holds exactly when $n \geq 2$.

$\blacksquare$
