**Answer: No, it does not follow in general.** Weak convergence to 0 does not im
ply norm convergence to 0 in an arbitrary (infinite-dimensional) Hilbert space.

---

### Counterexample

Let $H = \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$, the Hilbert space of square-summable sequences $x
= (x_1, x_2, \dots)$ with $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|x_k|^2 < \infty$, equipped with t
he standard inner product
$$\langle x, y\rangle = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} x_k\,\overline{y_k}.$$

Let $a_n := e_n$, the $n$-th standard basis vector ($1$ in position $n$, $0$ els
ewhere).

**Claim 1:** For every $x \in \ell^2$, $\langle x, a_n\rangle \to 0$.

For any $x = (x_k) \in \ell^2$, we have $\langle x, e_n\rangle = x_n$. Since $\s
um_{k}|x_k|^2 < \infty$, the general term of a convergent series must vanish: $|
x_k|^2 \to 0$, hence $x_k \to 0$. Therefore
$$\langle x, a_n\rangle = x_n \to 0 \quad \text{as } n\to\infty.$$

**Claim 2:** $a_n \not\to 0$ in norm.

We have $\|a_n\| = \|e_n\| = 1$ for every $n$. Hence $\|a_n - 0\| = 1 \not\to 0$
, so $a_n$ does not converge to $0$ in $H$.

---

### Conclusion

The hypothesis "$\forall x\in H,\ \langle x, a_n\rangle \to 0$" is precisely **w
eak convergence** of $a_n$ to $0$. The counterexample $a_n = e_n$ in $\ell^2$ sh
ows that weak convergence to $0$ does **not** force norm convergence to $0$. Thu
s the implication fails for arbitrary Hilbert spaces (it holds only in finite-di
mensional ones, where weak and norm topologies coincide).
