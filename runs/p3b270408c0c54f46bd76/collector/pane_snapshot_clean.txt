We evaluate $\int_c \frac{y^2}{2}\,dx + z\,dy + x\,dz$ where $c$ is the intersec
tion of the plane $x+z=1$ and the ellipsoid $x^2+2y^2+z^2=1$.

### Step 1: Apply Stokes' Theorem

Let $\mathbf{F} = \left(\tfrac{y^2}{2},\; z,\; x\right)$, so the line integral i
s $\int_c \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}$.

Compute the curl:
$$\nabla \times \mathbf{F} = \left(\frac{\partial x}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial
z}{\partial z},\;\; \frac{\partial (y^2/2)}{\partial z}-\frac{\partial x}{\parti
al x},\;\; \frac{\partial z}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial (y^2/2)}{\partial y}\rig
ht) = (-1,\;-1,\;-y).$$

Let $S$ be the portion of the plane $x+z=1$ bounded by $c$, with unit normal $\m
athbf{n} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1,0,1)$ (consistent with the counterclockwise orie
ntation of $c$ when viewed from the $+x,+z$ side). By Stokes' theorem:
$$\int_c \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_S (\nabla\times\mathbf{F})\cdot \ma
thbf{n}\, dS.$$

### Step 2: Compute the surface integrand

$$(\nabla\times\mathbf{F})\cdot\mathbf{n} = (-1,\,-1,\,-y)\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}
}(1,0,1) = \frac{-1-y}{\sqrt{2}}.$$

On the plane $z = 1-x$, the surface element is
$$dS = \sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\right)^2+\left(\frac{\partial
 z}{\partial y}\right)^2}\,dx\,dy = \sqrt{1+1+0}\,dx\,dy = \sqrt{2}\,dx\,dy.$$

Hence
$$\iint_S (\nabla\times\mathbf{F})\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dS = \iint_D \frac{-(1+y)}{\s
qrt{2}}\cdot\sqrt{2}\,dx\,dy = -\iint_D (1+y)\,dx\,dy,$$
where $D$ is the projection of $S$ onto the $xy$-plane.

### Step 3: Determine the domain $D$

Substitute $z = 1-x$ into the ellipsoid equation:
$$x^2 + 2y^2 + (1-x)^2 = 1 \;\Longrightarrow\; 2x^2 - 2x + 2y^2 = 0 \;\Longright
arrow\; \left(x-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 + y^2 = \tfrac{1}{4}.$$

So $D$ is the disk of radius $\tfrac{1}{2}$ centered at $\left(\tfrac{1}{2}, 0\r
ight)$.

### Step 4: Evaluate the double integral

$$-\iint_D (1+y)\,dx\,dy = -\iint_D 1\,dx\,dy - \iint_D y\,dx\,dy.$$

- $\displaystyle\iint_D 1\,dx\,dy = \text{Area}(D) = \pi\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right
)^2 = \frac{\pi}{4}.$
- $\displaystyle\iint_D y\,dx\,dy = 0$, since $D$ is symmetric about $y=0$.

Therefore:
$$\int_c \frac{y^2}{2}\,dx + z\,dy + x\,dz = -\frac{\pi}{4}.$$

(The opposite orientation of $c$ gives $+\frac{\pi}{4}$; with the standard Stoke
s orientation induced by the normal $(1,0,1)/\sqrt{2}$, the value is $-\dfrac{\p
