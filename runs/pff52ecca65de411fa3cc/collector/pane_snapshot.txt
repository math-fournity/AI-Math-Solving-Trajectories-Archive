## Proof

**Answer leak check:** The problem statement contains only the problem, no solut
ions, sketches, or leaked answers. Proceeding.

---

Define $S_n(x) = \sum_{i=0}^{n} x^i$ and $f_n(x) = \frac{1}{S_n(x)^2 + 1}$.

**Closed form.** For $x \neq 1$:
$$S_n(x) = \frac{1 - x^{n+1}}{1 - x}.$$

---

### Step 1: Pointwise limit

**Case $|x| < 1$:** Here $x^{n+1} \to 0$, so $S_n(x) \to \frac{1}{1-x}$, giving
$$f_n(x) \to \frac{1}{\frac{1}{(1-x)^2} + 1} = \frac{(1-x)^2}{1 + (1-x)^2}.$$

**Case $|x| > 1$:** Here $|x|^{n+1} \to \infty$, so $|S_n(x)| \to \infty$, givin
g $f_n(x) \to 0$.

**Case $x = 1$:** $S_n(1) = n+1 \to \infty$, so $f_n(1) \to 0$.

**Case $x = -1$:** $S_n(-1)$ oscillates (measure-zero point, irrelevant to the i
ntegral).

Thus the pointwise limit is
$$f(x) = \frac{(1-x)^2}{1+(1-x)^2}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{(-1,1)}(x).$$

---

### Step 2: Justifying the limit–integral interchange

Split the integral:
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_n(x)\,dx = \underbrace{\int_{-1}^{1} f_n(x)\,dx}_{I_
n} + \underbrace{\int_{|x|>1} f_n(x)\,dx}_{J_n}.$$

**Part $I_n$ (DCT on $(-1,1)$):** Since $f_n(x) \leq 1$ for all $x$ and $(-1,1)$
 has finite measure, the constant $1$ is an integrable dominator. By the Dominat
ed Convergence Theorem:
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} I_n = \int_{-1}^{1} \frac{(1-x)^2}{1+(1-x)^2}\,dx.$$

**Part $J_n$ (DCT on $\{|x|>1\}$):** We establish an integrable dominator. For $
|x|>1$ and $n \geq 1$, using the reverse triangle inequality $|x^{n+1}-1| \geq |
x|^{n+1}-1$ and $|x-1|\leq |x|+1$:
$$|S_n(x)| = \frac{|x^{n+1}-1|}{|x-1|} \geq \frac{|x|^{n+1}-1}{|x|+1}.$$
Since $n\geq 1$, we have $|x|^{n+1}\geq |x|^2$, so $|x|^{n+1}-1 \geq |x|^2-1 = (
|x|-1)(|x|+1)$, giving
$$|S_n(x)| \geq |x|-1.$$
Therefore
$$f_n(x) = \frac{1}{S_n(x)^2+1} \leq \frac{1}{(|x|-1)^2+1} =: g(x).$$
The dominator $g$ is integrable on $\{|x|>1\}$ since
$$\int_1^{\infty}\frac{dx}{(x-1)^2+1} = \int_0^{\infty}\frac{du}{u^2+1} = \frac{
\pi}{2} < \infty,$$
and similarly on $(-\infty,-1)$. Since $f_n \to 0$ pointwise on $\{|x|>1\}$, DCT
 gives
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} J_n = 0.$$

---

### Step 3: Computing the limit integral

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_n(x)\,dx = \int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(1-x)
^2}{1+(1-x)^2}\,dx.$$

Substitute $u = 1-x$, $du = -dx$; when $x=-1$, $u=2$; when $x=1$, $u=0$:

$$\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(1-x)^2}{1+(1-x)^2}\,dx = \int_0^2 \frac{u^2}{1+u^2}\,du =
\int_0^2\left(1 - \frac{1}{1+u^2}\right)du$$
$$= \Big[u - \arctan u\Big]_0^2 = 2 - \arctan(2).$$

---

### Result

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}x
^i\right)^2+1} = 2 - \arctan(2)}$$
