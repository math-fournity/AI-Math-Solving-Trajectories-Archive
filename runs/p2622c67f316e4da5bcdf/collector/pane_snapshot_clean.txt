Looking at the problem: We need to determine whether there exists an injective f
unction $f: C \to \mathbb{R}$ whose graph is dense in $C \times \mathbb{R}$, whe
re $C$ denotes the Cantor set.

**Answer: Yes, such a function exists.**

**Proof.**

The graph of $f$ is dense in $C \times \mathbb{R}$ if and only if for every none
mpty open $U \subseteq C$ and every nonempty open $V \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, ther
e exists $x \in U$ with $f(x) \in V$.

**Key properties of $C$:** The Cantor set is perfect (no isolated points), so ev
ery nonempty open subset of $C$ is uncountable. It is second-countable, with a c
ountable basis $\mathcal{B} = \{B_n : n \in \mathbb{N}\}$ of clopen cylinder set
s. It has cardinality $|C| = \mathfrak{c}$.

**Construction.** Let $\mathcal{J} = \{J_m : m \in \mathbb{N}\}$ be a countable
basis for $\mathbb{R}$ (open intervals with rational endpoints). Enumerate all p
airs $\{(B_n, J_m) : n, m \in \mathbb{N}\}$ as $\{(B_{n_k}, J_{m_k}) : k \in \ma
thbb{N}\}$.

We build a partial injection inductively:

**Step $k$:** We have chosen distinct points $x_1, \dots, x_{k-1} \in C$ and dis
tinct values $y_1, \dots, y_{k-1} \in \mathbb{R}$ with $f(x_i) = y_i$. Since $C$
 is perfect, $B_{n_k}$ is uncountable, so $B_{n_k} \setminus \{x_1, \dots, x_{k-
1}\} \neq \emptyset$; pick $x_k$ from this set. Since $J_{m_k}$ is a nonempty op
en interval, $J_{m_k} \setminus \{y_1, \dots, y_{k-1}\} \neq \emptyset$; pick $y
_k$ from this set. Set $f(x_k) = y_k$.

After all steps, $f$ is defined and injective on the countable set $D = \{x_k :
k \in \mathbb{N}\}$, and for every pair $(n, m)$, there exists $k$ with $(n_k, m
_k) = (n,m)$, ensuring $f(B_n) \cap J_m \neq \emptyset$.

**Extension to all of $C$:** Since $|C \setminus D| = \mathfrak{c}$ and $|\mathb
b{R} \setminus f(D)| = \mathfrak{c}$, there exists an injection $g: C \setminus
D \to \mathbb{R} \setminus f(D)$. Extend $f$ by setting $f(x) = g(x)$ for $x \in
 C \setminus D$. The resulting $f: C \to \mathbb{R}$ is injective (the partial f
unction on $D$ is injective, the extension is injective, and their ranges are di
sjoint).

**Density verification:** Let $U \subseteq C$ and $V \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be no
nempty open sets. Then $U \supseteq B_n$ and $V \supseteq J_m$ for some $n, m$.
By construction, $f(B_n) \cap J_m \neq \emptyset$, so $f(U) \cap V \neq \emptyse
t$. Therefore the graph $\{(x, f(x)) : x \in C\}$ intersects every nonempty basi
c open set in $C \times \mathbb{R}$, hence is dense in $C \times \mathbb{R}$.

**Remark on necessity of perfectness.** The construction relies crucially on $C$
 having no isolated points. If $C$ had an isolated point $p$, then $\{p\}$ would
 be open in $C$, and density would require $f(\{p\}) = \{f(p)\}$ to be dense in
$\mathbb{R}$, which is impossible for a singleton. The Cantor set, being perfect
, avoids this obstruction. $\blacksquare$
