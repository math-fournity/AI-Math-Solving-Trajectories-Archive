The problem asks whether commutativity of $G_{\bar{k}}$ over the algebraic
closure implies $G$ is commutative over $k$.

This is a standard fact in algebraic group theory. The commutator map is a
morphism, and commutativity can be checked over the algebraic closure
(faithfully flat descent, or simply because the commutator morphism being
trivial over $\bar{k}$ implies it's trivial over $k$ since $\bar{k}/k$ is
faithfully flat).

Let me write the proof.

Proof

Claim: Yes. If $G_{\bar{k}}$ is commutative, then $G$ is commutative over $k$.

Setup. Let $G$ be a smooth affine algebraic group over a field $k$, and let $
\bar{k}$ be an algebraic closure of $k$. Denote by $G_{\bar{k}} = G
\times_{\operatorname{Spec} k} \operatorname{Spec} \bar{k}$ the base change. We
are given that $G_{\bar{k}}$ is commutative, i.e., for all $\bar{k}$-algebras
$R$ and all $g, h \in G_{\bar{k}}(R)$, we have $gh = hg$.

Key observation: the commutator morphism. Consider the commutator morphism $$
c : G \times_k G \longrightarrow G, \qquad (g, h) \longmapsto g,h,g^{-1},h^{-1}.
$$ This is a morphism of $k$-schemes (it is built from the multiplication,
inverse, and identity morphisms defining the group scheme structure on $G$). The
group $G$ is commutative over $k$ if and only if $c$ is the trivial morphism,
i.e., $c$ factors through the identity section $e : \operatorname{Spec} k \to
G$.

Base change of the commutator. The formation of $c$ commutes with base change:
the commutator morphism of $G_{\bar{k}}$ is exactly $$ c_{\bar{k}} = c \times_k
\bar{k} : G_{\bar{k}} \times_{\bar{k}} G_{\bar{k}} \longrightarrow G_{\bar{k}}.
$$ Since $G_{\bar{k}}$ is commutative by hypothesis, $c_{\bar{k}}$ is the
trivial morphism (it sends every pair to the identity).

Faithfully flat descent. The extension $\bar{k}/k$ is faithfully flat (every
field extension is flat, and $\bar{k} \neq 0$ so it is faithfully flat). For
morphisms of schemes, we have the following standard descent principle:

│ If $f : X \to Y$ is a morphism of $k$-schemes and $f_{\bar{k}} = f \times_k
│ \bar{k}$ equals some morphism $g_{\bar{k}}$ (the base change of a $k$-morphism
│ $g$), then $f = g$.

Concretely, morphisms of schemes are determined locally by ring maps, and a ring
homomorphism $A \to B$ is determined by its base change $A \otimes_k \bar{k} \to
B \otimes_k \bar{k}$ because $\bar{k}$ is faithfully flat over $k$ (faithfully
flat modules reflect equality: $A \otimes_k \bar{k} \xrightarrow{\alpha \otimes
1, \beta \otimes 1} B \otimes_k \bar{k}$ being equal implies $\alpha = \beta$).

Applying descent. The trivial morphism $e_{\bar{k}} : G_{\bar{k}} \times
G_{\bar{k}} \to G_{\bar{k}}$ (constant at the identity) is the base change of
the trivial $k$-morphism $e \circ \pi : G \times_k G \to \operatorname{Spec} k
\xrightarrow{e} G$. Since $$ c_{\bar{k}} = (e \circ \pi)_{\bar{k}}, $$
faithfully flat descent yields $$ c = e \circ \pi. $$ That is, $c$ is the
trivial morphism over $k$.

Conclusion. The commutator morphism $c$ being trivial means that for every $k$-
algebra $R$ and all $g, h \in G(R)$, we have $g h g^{-1} h^{-1} = e$, i.e., $gh
= hg$. Therefore $G$ is commutative as a group scheme over $k$.

Remark on the smoothness hypothesis. The smoothness assumption is not needed for
the descent argument itself—faithfully flat descent of morphisms works for any
$k$-scheme. The smoothness is relevant in the broader context (e.g., for the
structure theory of algebraic groups, where one classifies smooth affine groups
via their base change to $\bar{k}$), but the implication "$G_{\bar{k}}$
commutative $\Rightarrow$ $G$ commutative" holds for any group scheme over $k$
by the same descent argument.
