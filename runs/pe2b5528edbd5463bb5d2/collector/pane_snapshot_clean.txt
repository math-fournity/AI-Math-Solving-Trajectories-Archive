Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum number of "long" heights in
a tetrahedron, where a height is long if it's ≥ every altitude of the opposite f
ace.

## Key Lemma

**Lemma:** For any edge of the tetrahedron, if the two heights from the two vert
ices *not* on that edge are both long, then the dihedral angle along that edge i
s π/2, and the two adjacent face areas are equal.

**Proof:** Consider edge CD, shared by faces BCD and ACD. Let α be the dihedral
angle along CD. Define:
- d_A = 2S_{ACD}/|CD| (altitude from A to CD in triangle ACD),
- d_B = 2S_{BCD}/|CD| (altitude from B to CD in triangle BCD).

Since A lies in face ACD at distance d_A from line CD, the height from A to plan
e BCD is **h_A = d_A·sin(α)**. Similarly, **h_B = d_B·sin(α)**.

If h_A is long, then h_A ≥ d_B (the altitude from B to CD is one of BCD's three
altitudes), giving:
$$S_{ACD}\cdot\sin\alpha \geq S_{BCD}$$

If h_B is long, then h_B ≥ d_A, giving:
$$S_{BCD}\cdot\sin\alpha \geq S_{ACD}$$

Multiplying: sin²α ≥ 1, so **sin α = 1, α = π/2**. Then both inequalities force
**S_{ACD} = S_{BCD}**. ∎

## Four long heights is impossible

If all 4 heights are long, then for **each of the 6 edges**, the two opposite he
ights are both long. By the lemma, **all 6 dihedral angles equal π/2**.

Consider face BCD. It is perpendicular to faces ABC (along BC), ABD (along BD),
and ACD (along CD). So the normal **n** to BCD must lie in all three other faces
:
- **n** ∈ plane ABC ∩ plane ABD = line AB,
- **n** ∈ plane ACD.

But line AB meets plane ACD only at point A (since B ∉ plane ACD in a non-degene
rate tetrahedron). No nonzero vector along AB lies in plane ACD. This contradict
s **n** ≠ **0**. ∎

## Three long heights is achievable

Take the **tri-rectangular tetrahedron** with A = (0,0,0), B = (1,0,0), C = (0,1
,0), D = (0,0,1). Volume V = 1/6.

**Face BCD** (opposite A): equilateral triangle, side √2, area √3/2.
- h_A = 3V/S_{BCD} = (1/2)/(√3/2) = 1/√3 ≈ 0.577.
- Max altitude of BCD = (√3/2)·√2 = √6/2 ≈ 1.225.
- h_A < √6/2 → **not long**.

**Face ACD** (opposite B): right triangle with legs 1, 1, area 1/2.
- h_B = (1/2)/(1/2) = 1.
- Altitudes: from A to CD = 1/√2, from C to AD = 1, from D to AC = 1. Max = 1.
- h_B = 1 ≥ 1 → **long** ✓

**Face ABD** (opposite C): by symmetry, h_C = 1, max altitude = 1 → **long** ✓

**Face ABC** (opposite D): by symmetry, h_D = 1, max altitude = 1 → **long** ✓

So **3 heights are long**.

## Conclusion

Since 4 long heights is impossible and 3 is achievable, the greatest number of l
ong heights is:

$$\boxed{3}$$
