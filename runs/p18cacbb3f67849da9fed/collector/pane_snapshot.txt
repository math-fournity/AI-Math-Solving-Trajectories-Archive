## Proof

Let $S \in \mathcal{D}'(\mathbb{R})$ be a distribution with compact support, and
 suppose $\langle S, x^n \rangle = 0$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ (i.e., for $n =
 0, 1, 2, \dots$).

**Step 1: Compact support gives finite order and an extension to $C^\infty$.**

Since $S$ has compact support, $S \in \mathcal{E}'(\mathbb{R})$. Three standard
facts about compactly supported distributions are used:

(a) $S$ has **finite order**: there is an integer $m \ge 0$ such that $S$ is of
order $\le m$.

(b) $S$ extends uniquely to a continuous linear functional on $\mathcal{E}(\math
bb{R}) = C^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ (with its Fréchet topology of uniform convergence
 of all derivatives on compact sets). Concretely, for $f \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R}
)$ one defines
$$\langle S, f\rangle := \langle S, \chi f\rangle,$$
where $\chi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ is any cutoff with $\chi \equiv 1$ on a
neighborhood of $\operatorname{supp} S$. This is independent of the choice of $\
chi$ (if $\tilde\chi$ is another such cutoff, then $(\chi - \tilde\chi)f$ vanish
es on a neighborhood of $\operatorname{supp} S$, so $S$ pairs to $0$ with it).

(c) **Continuity estimate.** There exist a compact interval $L \subset \mathbb{R
}$ (a compact neighborhood of $\operatorname{supp} S$), an integer $m \ge 0$, an
d a constant $C > 0$ such that for every $f \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R})$,
$$|\langle S, f\rangle| \;\le\; C \cdot \max_{0 \le j \le m}\; \sup_{x \in L} |f
^{(j)}(x)|. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Step 2: $S$ annihilates every polynomial.**

By hypothesis $\langle S, x^n\rangle = 0$ for all $n \ge 0$. By linearity, $\lan
gle S, p\rangle = 0$ for every polynomial $p$.

**Step 3: Polynomials are dense in $C^m(L)$.**

Enlarge $L$ if necessary and write $L = [a,b]$. A classical consequence of the W
eierstrass approximation theorem (for instance, via Bernstein polynomials $B_N(f
)$, which satisfy $B_N^{(j)}(f) \to f^{(j)}$ uniformly on $[a,b]$ for each fixed
 $j$) is:

> Polynomials are dense in $C^m([a,b])$ with respect to the norm $\|f\|_{C^m} =
\max_{0 \le j \le m} \sup_{x \in [a,b]} |f^{(j)}(x)|$.

**Step 4: $S$ annihilates every test function.**

Let $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ be arbitrary, regarded as an element of
 $C^\infty(\mathbb{R})$. By Step 3, there is a sequence of polynomials $\{p_k\}$
 with
$$\max_{0 \le j \le m}\; \sup_{x \in L} \bigl|p_k^{(j)}(x) - \varphi^{(j)}(x)\bi
gr| \;\longrightarrow\; 0 \quad \text{as } k \to \infty.$$

Apply the estimate $(\ast)$ to $f = \varphi - p_k \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R})$:
$$\bigl|\langle S, \varphi\rangle - \langle S, p_k\rangle\bigr| = \bigl|\langle
S, \varphi - p_k\rangle\bigr| \;\le\; C \cdot \max_{0 \le j \le m}\; \sup_{x \in
 L} \bigl|(\varphi - p_k)^{(j)}(x)\bigr| \;\longrightarrow\; 0.$$

By Step 2, $\langle S, p_k\rangle = 0$ for every $k$. Hence $\langle S, \varphi\
rangle = 0$.

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

Since $\langle S, \varphi\rangle = 0$ for every test function $\varphi \in C_c^\
infty(\mathbb{R})$, by the definition of equality of distributions we conclude $
S = 0$. $\blacksquare$
