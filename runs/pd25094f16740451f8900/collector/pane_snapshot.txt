## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** $Y$ is necessarily homeomorphic to a disjoint union of $p$ copi
es of $X$.

**Step 1: Each fiber has exactly $p$ points.**

The subgroup $\mathbb{Z}_p \leq \mathrm{Deck}(\pi)$ acts freely and transitively
 on every fiber $\pi^{-1}(x)$. For any group $G$ acting freely and transitively
on a set $S$, the map $G \to S$, $g \mapsto g \cdot s_0$ (for a fixed $s_0 \in S
$) is a bijection: surjective by transitivity, injective by freeness. Hence $|\p
i^{-1}(x)| = |\mathbb{Z}_p| = p$ for every $x \in X$. This is a $p$-sheeted cove
ring.

**Step 2: Connected components of $Y$ are covering spaces of $X$.**

Let $Y = \coprod_{\alpha \in A} Y_\alpha$ be the decomposition into connected co
mponents. Since the covering property is local, each restriction $\pi_\alpha :=
\pi|_{Y_\alpha} : Y_\alpha \to X$ is a covering map. We verify surjectivity: $\p
i(Y_\alpha)$ is open in $X$ (local homeomorphisms are open maps) and closed (if
$x \in \overline{\pi(Y_\alpha)}$, take an evenly covered connected neighborhood
$U$ of $x$; then $\pi^{-1}(U) = \coprod_{i=1}^p V_i$ with each $V_i$ connected,
each lying in a single component; since $U \cap \pi(Y_\alpha) \neq \emptyset$, s
ome $V_i \subset Y_\alpha$, giving $x \in U = \pi(V_i) \subset \pi(Y_\alpha)$).
As $X$ is connected and $\pi(Y_\alpha) \neq \emptyset$, we get $\pi(Y_\alpha) =
X$.

**Step 3: Each component is $n_\alpha$-sheeted, with $\sum n_\alpha = p$.**

For each $\alpha$, the cardinality $n_\alpha := |\pi^{-1}(x) \cap Y_\alpha|$ is
locally constant (on an evenly covered neighborhood, each sheet lies in one comp
onent) and hence constant on $X$ (connected). Since the fibers partition as $\pi
^{-1}(x) = \coprod_\alpha (\pi^{-1}(x) \cap Y_\alpha)$, we have $\sum_{\alpha \i
n A} n_\alpha = p$.

**Step 4: Primality forces $p$ components, each 1-sheeted.**

Since $Y$ is not connected, $|A| \geq 2$. Each $n_\alpha \geq 1$ (by surjectivit
y from Step 2). With $\sum n_\alpha = p$ and $p$ prime, the only partition of $p
$ into at least two parts each $\geq 1$ is $p = 1 + 1 + \cdots + 1$ ($p$ ones).
Thus $|A| = p$ and $n_\alpha = 1$ for every $\alpha$.

**Step 5: 1-sheeted coverings are homeomorphisms.**

A 1-sheeted covering $\pi_\alpha : Y_\alpha \to X$ is a bijection (each fiber is
 a singleton) and a local homeomorphism, hence a homeomorphism. So $Y_\alpha \co
ng X$ for each $\alpha$.

**Conclusion.** $Y = \coprod_{\alpha \in A} Y_\alpha \cong \coprod_{i=1}^{p} X$,
 a disjoint union of $p$ copies of $X$. $\blacksquare$
