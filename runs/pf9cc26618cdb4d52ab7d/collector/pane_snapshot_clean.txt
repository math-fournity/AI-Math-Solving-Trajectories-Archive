## Proof

Consider the group $\mathcal{G} = \mathrm{UT}(3, p)$ of $3 \times 3$ upper unitr
iangular matrices over the finite field $\mathbb{F}_p$, where $p$ is any prime.
Explicitly,

$$\mathcal{G} = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} 1 & a & b \\ 0 & 1 & c \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end
{pmatrix} : a, b, c \in \mathbb{F}_p \right\}.$$

This is a group of order $p^3$, hence a $p$-group.

**$\mathcal{G}$ is non-abelian.** Take $X = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 &
 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $Y = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \
\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Then

$$XY = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \neq \b
egin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = YX,$$

so $\mathcal{G}$ is non-abelian.

**The center $\mathbb{Z}(\mathcal{G})$.** A matrix $M = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & a &
b \\ 0 & 1 & c \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ is central iff it commutes with both
$X$ (having $a=1, c=0$) and $Y$ (having $a=0, c=1$). Computing $MX$ vs. $XM$ for
ces $c = 0$, and computing $MY$ vs. $YM$ forces $a = 0$. Thus

$$\mathbb{Z}(\mathcal{G}) = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & b \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0
& 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} : b \in \mathbb{F}_p \right\} \cong (\mathbb{F}_p, +) \con
g C_p,$$

which is elementary abelian (a one-dimensional vector space over $\mathbb{F}_p$)
.

**The commutator subgroup $\mathcal{G}'$.** For $X = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & a & b \
\ 0 & 1 & c \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $Y = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & a' & b' \\
0 & 1 & c' \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, a direct computation of the commutator $
[X, Y] = X^{-1}Y^{-1}XY$ yields

$$[X, Y] = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & ac' - a'c \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmat
rix}.$$

In particular, taking $X$ with $a = 1, c = 0$ and $Y$ with $a' = 0, c' = 1$ give
s $[X,Y]$ with $(1,3)$-entry $= 1$, so the commutator subgroup contains the elem
ent $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Since e
very commutator lies in $\mathbb{Z}(\mathcal{G})$ and the commutators generate a
 subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}(\mathcal{G}) \cong C_p$ that is non-trivial, we conclud
e

$$\mathcal{G}' = \mathbb{Z}(\mathcal{G}) \cong C_p.$$

**Metabelian.** Since $\mathcal{G}' \cong C_p$ is abelian, $\mathcal{G}$ is meta
belian.

**Summary.** $\mathcal{G} = \mathrm{UT}(3, p)$ is a non-abelian metabelian $p$-g
roup with $\mathcal{G}' = \mathbb{Z}(\mathcal{G}) \cong C_p$, which is non-trivi
al and elementary abelian. This satisfies all the required conditions. $\blacksq
uare$
