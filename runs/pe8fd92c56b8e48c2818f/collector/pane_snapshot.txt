## Proof

This is the finite Vitali covering lemma in the plane. We use a greedy selection
 argument.

**Setup.** Write the discs of $\mathcal F$ as $B_1,\dots,B_m$, ordered by non-in
creasing radius:
$$r_1 \ge r_2 \ge \cdots \ge r_m,$$
where $r_i$ is the radius of $B_i$ and $c_i$ its center.

**Greedy selection.** We construct the subcollection $D_1,\dots,D_n$ iteratively
.

- Let $D_1 = B_1$ (a disc of largest radius). Discard every $B_i$ that intersect
s $D_1$.
- Among the discs not yet discarded, let $D_2$ be one of largest radius. Discard
 every remaining disc that intersects $D_2$.
- Continue until no discs remain.

The selected discs $D_1,\dots,D_n$ are **pairwise disjoint**: when $D_j$ is chos
en, every disc intersecting it (including all later-selected $D_k$, $k>j$) is di
scarded, so no two selected discs can intersect.

**Key observation.** We claim every disc $B_i\in\mathcal F$ satisfies $B_i \subs
eteq 3D_j$ for some selected $D_j$.

Indeed, $B_i$ is either selected itself (in which case $B_i = D_j \subseteq 3D_j
$ trivially) or discarded at the step when some $D_j$ was chosen. In the latter
case, since $D_j$ was chosen as a disc of largest radius among all discs still r
emaining at that step (which included $B_i$, since $B_i$ had not yet been discar
ded), we have
$$\operatorname{rad}(D_j) \ge \operatorname{rad}(B_i).$$
Moreover $B_i$ was discarded at this step precisely because it intersects $D_j$.

Let $D_j$ have center $P$ and radius $R$, and $B_i$ have center $Q$ and radius $
r$, with $R\ge r$. Since the open discs $D_j$ and $B_i$ intersect, $|P-Q| < R+r$
. For any point $x\in B_i$,
$$|x-P| \le |x-Q| + |Q-P| < r + (R+r) = R + 2r \le R + 2R = 3R.$$
Thus $x\in 3D_j$. Since $x\in B_i$ was arbitrary, $B_i\subseteq 3D_j$.

**Conclusion.** Every $B_i\in\mathcal F$ lies inside $3D_j$ for some selected $D
_j$. Therefore
$$E \subseteq \bigcup_{B\in\mathcal F} B \subseteq \bigcup_{j=1}^{n} 3D_j,$$
with $D_1,\dots,D_n$ pairwise disjoint, as required. $\blacksquare$
