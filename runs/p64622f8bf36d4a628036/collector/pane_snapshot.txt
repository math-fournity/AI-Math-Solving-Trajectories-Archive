# Solution

**Claim:** Yes. The operation $\gamma_G(x,y) = x^{-1}\cdot y$ on the underlying
set $|G|$ uniquely determines the group $(|G|,\cdot,{}^{-1},e)$.

**Proof.**

We show that each of the three group operations — the identity $e$, the inverse
map ${}^{-1}$, and the multiplication $\cdot$ — can be recovered from $\gamma_G$
 alone by explicit formulas. Consequently, if two groups $G, H$ share the same u
nderlying set and the same $\gamma$-operation, they share all three operations a
nd are identical.

**Step 1: Recovering the identity.**

For every $x \in G$:
$$\gamma_G(x, x) = x^{-1}\cdot x = e.$$
Thus $e = \gamma_G(x,x)$, and this is independent of the choice of $x$ (it is th
e same element for all $x$). So $e$ is recovered.

**Step 2: Recovering the inverse.**

Once $e$ is known, for every $x \in G$:
$$\gamma_G(x, e) = x^{-1}\cdot e = x^{-1}.$$
So the inverse operation is $x^{-1} = \gamma_G(x, e)$.

**Step 3: Recovering the multiplication.**

For $x, y \in G$, using the inverse recovered in Step 2:
$$\gamma_G(x^{-1},\, y) = (x^{-1})^{-1}\cdot y = x\cdot y.$$
Substituting the formula from Step 2:
$$x \cdot y \;=\; \gamma_G\!\bigl(\gamma_G(x,\,e),\; y\bigr).$$

**Conclusion.**

All three operations are definable from $\gamma_G$:
$$e = \gamma_G(x,x), \qquad x^{-1} = \gamma_G(x, e), \qquad x\cdot y = \gamma_G(
\gamma_G(x, e),\, y).$$

Therefore the map $G \mapsto G'' = (|G|, \gamma_G)$ is injective up to equality
of groups on the same underlying set: if $G'' = H''$ (same set, same $\gamma$-op
eration), then $G$ and $H$ have the same identity, the same inverse, and the sam
e multiplication, hence $G = H$. $\blacksquare$
