## Christoffel Symbols for Polar Coordinates

**Setup.** Let $x^1 = r$, $x^2 = \theta$. The metric and its inverse are
$$g_{ij}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&(x^1)^2\end{bmatrix},\qquad g^{ij}=\begin{bmatrix
}1&0\\0&\dfrac{1}{(x^1)^2}\end{bmatrix}.$$

The only nonzero partial derivative of the metric is
$$\partial_1 g_{22}=2x^1,\qquad \partial_2 g_{22}=0,\quad \partial_k g_{11}=0,\q
uad \partial_k g_{12}=0.$$

---

### First kind: $\Gamma_{ij,k}=\tfrac12\bigl(\partial_i g_{jk}+\partial_j g_{ik}
-\partial_k g_{ij}\bigr)$

Evaluating all independent components (using symmetry $\Gamma_{ij,k}=\Gamma_{ji,
k}$):

- $\Gamma_{11,1}=\tfrac12(\partial_1 g_{11}+\partial_1 g_{11}-\partial_1 g_{11})
=0$
- $\Gamma_{11,2}=\tfrac12(\partial_1 g_{12}+\partial_1 g_{12}-\partial_2 g_{11})
=0$
- $\Gamma_{12,1}=\tfrac12(\partial_1 g_{21}+\partial_2 g_{11}-\partial_1 g_{12})
=0$
- $\Gamma_{12,2}=\tfrac12(\partial_1 g_{22}+\partial_2 g_{12}-\partial_2 g_{12})
=\tfrac12(2x^1)=x^1$
- $\Gamma_{22,1}=\tfrac12(\partial_2 g_{21}+\partial_2 g_{21}-\partial_1 g_{22})
=-\tfrac12(2x^1)=-x^1$
- $\Gamma_{22,2}=\tfrac12(\partial_2 g_{22}+\partial_2 g_{22}-\partial_2 g_{22})
=0$

**Nonzero first-kind symbols:**
$$\boxed{\Gamma_{12,2}=\Gamma_{21,2}=x^1,\qquad \Gamma_{22,1}=-x^1.}$$

---

### Second kind: $\Gamma^k_{ij}=g^{k\ell}\,\Gamma_{ij,\ell}$

Since $g^{k\ell}$ is diagonal, $\Gamma^k_{ij}=g^{kk}\,\Gamma_{ij,k}$ (no sum on
$k$):

- $\Gamma^1_{22}=g^{11}\,\Gamma_{22,1}=1\cdot(-x^1)=-x^1$
- $\Gamma^2_{12}=g^{22}\,\Gamma_{12,2}=\dfrac{1}{(x^1)^2}\cdot x^1=\dfrac{1}{x^1
}$
- $\Gamma^2_{21}=g^{22}\,\Gamma_{21,2}=\dfrac{1}{x^1}$ (by symmetry $\Gamma_{ij,
k}=\Gamma_{ji,k}$)
- All remaining components vanish (they contract a zero first-kind symbol).

**Nonzero second-kind symbols:**
$$\boxed{\Gamma^1_{22}=-x^1,\qquad \Gamma^2_{12}=\Gamma^2_{21}=\frac{1}{x^1}.}$$

---

### Verification (geodesic consistency)

For polar coordinates the geodesic equations $\ddot x^k+\Gamma^k_{ij}\dot x^i\do
t x^j=0$ read
$$\ddot r - r\,\dot\theta^{\,2}=0,\qquad \ddot\theta+\frac{2}{r}\dot r\,\dot\the
ta=0,$$
which are exactly the standard polar-coordinate geodesic (straight-line) equatio
ns, confirming the computed symbols.
