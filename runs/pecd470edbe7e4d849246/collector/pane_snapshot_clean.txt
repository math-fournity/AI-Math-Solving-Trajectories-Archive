## Solution

We evaluate
$$I=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{\left(x^{3}+\dfrac{1}{x^{3}}\right)^{2}}.$$

**Step 1: Simplify the integrand.**

Since
$$x^{3}+\frac{1}{x^{3}}=\frac{x^{6}+1}{x^{3}},$$
we have
$$\left(x^{3}+\frac{1}{x^{3}}\right)^{2}=\frac{(x^{6}+1)^{2}}{x^{6}},$$
hence
$$\frac{1}{\left(x^{3}+\dfrac{1}{x^{3}}\right)^{2}}=\frac{x^{6}}{(x^{6}+1)^{2}}.
$$

Therefore
$$I=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{6}}{(1+x^{6})^{2}}\,dx.$$

**Step 2: Apply the beta-integral formula.**

We use the standard identity (valid for $q>0$, $0<p<qr$):
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p-1}}{(1+x^{q})^{r}}\,dx=\frac{1}{q}\,B\!\left(\frac
{p}{q},\,r-\frac{p}{q}\right)=\frac{1}{q}\,\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{p}{q}\right
)\,\Gamma\!\left(r-\frac{p}{q}\right)}{\Gamma(r)}.$$

Here $x^{6}=x^{7-1}$, so $p=7$; $q=6$; $r=2$. The convergence condition $0<p/q<r
$ becomes $0<\tfrac{7}{6}<2$, which holds. Thus
$$I=\frac{1}{6}\,\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{7}{6}\right)\,\Gamma\!\left(2-\tfrac
{7}{6}\right)}{\Gamma(2)}=\frac{1}{6}\,\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{7}{6}\right)\,
\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{5}{6}\right)}{\Gamma(2)}.$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the gamma products.**

Since $\Gamma(2)=1!=1$, we need $\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{7}{6}\right)\Gamma\!\left(
\tfrac{5}{6}\right)$.

Using the recurrence $\Gamma(z+1)=z\,\Gamma(z)$ with $z=\tfrac{1}{6}$:
$$\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{7}{6}\right)=\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{1}{6}+1\right)=\tfrac{1
}{6}\,\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{1}{6}\right).$$

By the reflection formula $\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\dfrac{\pi}{\sin(\pi z)}$ with $
z=\tfrac{1}{6}$:
$$\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{1}{6}\right)\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{5}{6}\right)=\frac{\pi}{
\sin(\pi/6)}=\frac{\pi}{1/2}=2\pi.$$

Therefore
$$\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{7}{6}\right)\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{5}{6}\right)=\frac{1}{6}
\,\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{1}{6}\right)\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{5}{6}\right)=\frac{1}{6}
\cdot 2\pi=\frac{\pi}{3}.$$

**Step 4: Conclude.**

$$I=\frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{\pi}{3}=\boxed{\dfrac{\pi}{18}}.$$

**Verification (consistency check).** Under the substitution $x=1/t$, the integr
al transforms to
$$I=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{4}}{(1+t^{6})^{2}}\,dt,$$
i.e. the same value with $p=5$, $q=6$, $r=2$, giving $\tfrac{1}{6}B\!\left(\tfra
c{5}{6},\tfrac{7}{6}\right)=\tfrac{1}{6}\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{5}{6}\right)\Gamma\
!\left(\tfrac{7}{6}\right)=\tfrac{\pi}{18}$, confirming the result.
