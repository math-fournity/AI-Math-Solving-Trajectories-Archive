## Proof

Let $X, Y \sim \chi^2(1)$. We show that $\mathbb{E}[X/Y] = \infty$ **even when $
X$ and $Y$ are independent**, so the expectation is certainly unbounded.

**Independent case.** When $X \perp Y$,
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{X}{Y}\right] = \mathbb{E}[X]\cdot\mathbb{E}\!\left[\fr
ac{1}{Y}\right] = 1 \cdot \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{1}{Y}\right] = \mathbb{E}\!\le
ft[\frac{1}{Y}\right].$$

**Density of $Y$.** For $Y \sim \chi^2(1)$, with density (for $y > 0$):
$$f_Y(y) = \frac{1}{2^{1/2}\,\Gamma(1/2)}\, y^{-1/2}\, e^{-y/2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt
{2\pi}}\, y^{-1/2}\, e^{-y/2}.$$

**Computing $\mathbb{E}[1/Y]$.**
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{1}{Y}\right] = \int_0^\infty \frac{1}{y}\, f_Y(y)\, dy
 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_0^\infty y^{-3/2}\, e^{-y/2}\, dy.$$

**Divergence near $0$.** For $0 < y \le 1$, we have $e^{-y/2} \ge e^{-1/2} > 0$,
 hence
$$\int_0^1 y^{-3/2}\, e^{-y/2}\, dy \;\ge\; e^{-1/2}\int_0^1 y^{-3/2}\, dy \;=\;
 e^{-1/2}\Big[-2\,y^{-1/2}\Big]_0^1 \;=\; +\infty.$$

Therefore $\mathbb{E}[1/Y] = \infty$, and consequently
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{X}{Y}\right] = \infty.$$

**Conclusion.** The expectation $\mathbb{E}[X/Y]$ is already infinite in the ind
ependent case, so it is unbounded regardless of any dependence structure. The an
swer is **yes**, $\mathbb{E}[X/Y]$ can be unbounded.
