## Solution

**Answer: No.** A subset of $\mathbb{R}$ with non-empty interior does **not** al
ways have a boundary of non-zero (Lebesgue) measure.

### Counterexample

Let $A = (0,1) \subset \mathbb{R}$.

**Non-empty interior.** Since $A$ is open, $\operatorname{int}(A) = A = (0,1) \n
eq \varnothing$.

**Boundary.** The boundary is
$$\partial A = \overline{A} \setminus \operatorname{int}(A) = [0,1] \setminus (0
,1) = \{0, 1\}.$$

Equivalently, $\partial A = \overline{A} \cap \overline{A^c}$, where $\overline{
A} = [0,1]$ and $\overline{A^c} = (-\infty, 0] \cup [1, +\infty)$, giving $\part
ial A = \{0,1\}$.

**Measure of the boundary.** The set $\{0,1\}$ is finite, hence has Lebesgue mea
sure
$$m(\partial A) = m(\{0,1\}) = 0.$$

### Conclusion

The set $A = (0,1)$ has non-empty interior yet its boundary has measure zero. Th
erefore it is **not true** that a subset of $\mathbb{R}$ with non-empty interior
 always has a boundary with non-zero measure. $\blacksquare$
