Define the relation on $\mathbb{R}$ by $x \sim y \iff x - y \in \mathbb{Q}$. Thi
s is an equivalence relation:

- **Reflexivity:** $x - x = 0 \in \mathbb{Q}$.
- **Symmetry:** if $x - y \in \mathbb{Q}$, then $y - x = -(x-y) \in \mathbb{Q}$.
- **Transitivity:** if $x - y \in \mathbb{Q}$ and $y - z \in \mathbb{Q}$, then $
x - z = (x-y)+(y-z) \in \mathbb{Q}$.

The equivalence class of any $x \in \mathbb{R}$ is
$$[x] = \{y \in \mathbb{R} : y - x \in \mathbb{Q}\} = x + \mathbb{Q} = \{x + q :
 q \in \mathbb{Q}\}.$$

**Each equivalence class is countably infinite.** The map $\varphi_x : \mathbb{Q
} \to [x]$ defined by $\varphi_x(q) = x + q$ is a bijection: it is injective sin
ce $x+q_1 = x+q_2 \Rightarrow q_1 = q_2$, and surjective by definition of $[x]$.
 Hence $|[x]| = |\mathbb{Q}| = \aleph_0$ for every $x$.

**There are continuum many classes** (for completeness). The set of classes $\ma
thbb{R}/{\sim}$ is in bijection with $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}$ (the quotient of th
e additive group $\mathbb{R}$ by its subgroup $\mathbb{Q}$). Since $|\mathbb{R}|
 = \aleph_0 \cdot |\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}|$ and $|\mathbb{R}| = 2^{\aleph_0} > \a
leph_0$, we get $|\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}| = 2^{\aleph_0}$. (Concretely, distinct
cosets are disjoint and each is countable, so countably many cosets would give o
nly a countable union of countable sets, contradicting $|\mathbb{R}| = 2^{\aleph
_0}$.)

---

**Conclusion.** Every equivalence class of $\sim$ has the form $x + \mathbb{Q}$
and is therefore in bijection with $\mathbb{Q}$. Thus the **only** possible card
inality of an equivalence class is

$$\boxed{\aleph_0}$$

(countably infinite). All equivalence classes share this single cardinality; the
re are $2^{\aleph_0}$ such classes.
