$$\frac{e^{ix}}{x} = \frac{\cos x}{x} + i \frac{\sin x}{x}$$

So $\text{P.V.} \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} \frac{e^{ix}}{x} dx = 0 + i\pi = i\pi$.

Evaluation of $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} \frac{e^{ix}}{x},dx$

Setup and Interpretation

The integrand $\dfrac{e^{ix}}{x}$ has a simple pole at $x=0$, so the integral is
interpreted as the Cauchy principal value:

$$I = \text{P.V.}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} \frac{e^{ix}}{x},dx =
\lim_{\substack{R\to\infty\ \varepsilon\to 0^+}}\left(\int_{-R}^{-
\varepsilon}\frac{e^{ix}}{x},dx + \int_{\varepsilon}^{R}\frac{e^{ix}}
{x},dx\right).$$

Contour Choice

Consider $f(z)=\dfrac{e^{iz}}{z}$, which is meromorphic with a single simple
pole at $z=0$ (residue $=1$).

Use the closed contour $\Gamma$ consisting of (traversed counterclockwise):

  1. The segment $[-R,-\varepsilon]$ on the real axis,
  2. The small semicircle $C_\varepsilon: z=\varepsilon e^{i\theta}$, $\theta$
  from $\pi$ to $0$ (upper half-plane, indenting above the pole),
  3. The segment $[\varepsilon, R]$ on the real axis,
  4. The large semicircle $C_R: z=Re^{i\theta}$, $\theta$ from $0$ to $\pi$
  (upper half-plane).

The pole at $z=0$ lies outside $\Gamma$ (excluded by the indentation), so by
Cauchy's theorem:

$$\oint_\Gamma f(z),dz = 0.$$

Estimating the Large Arc $C_R$

On $C_R$, $z=Re^{i\theta}$ with $\theta\in[0,\pi]$, so $\operatorname{Im} z =
R\sin\theta \ge 0$, hence

$$|e^{iz}| = e^{-\operatorname{Im}z} = e^{-R\sin\theta} \le 1.$$

By Jordan's lemma (applicable since $|f(z)|=|e^{iz}/z|\to 0$ uniformly as
$R\to\infty$ on the arc and $e^{iz}$ decays in the upper half-plane):

$$\left|\int_{C_R}\frac{e^{iz}}{z},dz\right| \le \int_0^\pi e^{-
R\sin\theta},d\theta ;\xrightarrow{R\to\infty}; 0.$$

Computing the Small Arc $C_\varepsilon$

Parametrize $z=\varepsilon e^{i\theta}$, $\theta\in[\pi,0]$, $dz=i\varepsilon
e^{i\theta},d\theta$:

$$\int_{C_\varepsilon}\frac{e^{iz}}{z},dz = \int_{\pi}^{0}\frac{e^{i\varepsilon
e^{i\theta}}}{\varepsilon e^{i\theta}}\cdot i\varepsilon e^{i\theta},d\theta =
i\int_{\pi}^{0}e^{i\varepsilon e^{i\theta}},d\theta.$$

As $\varepsilon\to 0^+$, $e^{i\varepsilon e^{i\theta}}\to 1$ uniformly, so

$$\int_{C_\varepsilon}\frac{e^{iz}}{z},dz ;\longrightarrow; i\int_{\pi}^{0}
1,d\theta = i(0-\pi) = -i\pi.$$

Assembling the Limit

Taking $R\to\infty$ and $\varepsilon\to 0^+$ in $\displaystyle\oint_\Gamma = 0$:

$$\underbrace{\text{P.V.}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{ix}}{x},dx}{I} ;+;
\underbrace{(-i\pi)}{\text{small arc}} ;+; \underbrace{0}_{\text{large arc}} ;=;
0.$$

Therefore:

$$\boxed{,I = i\pi,}.$$

Cross-Check via Real and Imaginary Parts

Writing $e^{ix}=\cos x + i\sin x$:

  • Real part: $\dfrac{\cos x}{x}$ is odd, so $\text{P.V.}\int_{-\infty}^{+
  \infty}\dfrac{\cos x}{x},dx = 0$.
  • Imaginary part: $\dfrac{\sin x}{x}$ is even, and the classical Dirichlet
  integral gives $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x},dx =
  \pi$.

Hence $I = 0 + i\cdot\pi = i\pi$, confirming the contour result.
