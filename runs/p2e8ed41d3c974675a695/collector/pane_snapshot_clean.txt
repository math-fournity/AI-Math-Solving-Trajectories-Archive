## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** This is Fejér's theorem. We prove that if $f \in L^1[-\pi,\pi]$
 (extended $2\pi$-periodically) is continuous at $x \in [-\pi,\pi]$, then $\sigm
a_n(f)(x) \to f(x)$.

---

**Setup and key properties of $F_n$.**

The convolution is
$$
\sigma_n(f)(x) = (f * F_n)(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(x-t)\,F_n(t)\,
dt,
$$
and since $\{F_n\}$ is an approximate identity, we have:

1. **Normalization:** $\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} F_n(t)\,dt =
 1$.
2. **Non-negativity:** $F_n(t) = \frac{1}{2n\pi}\frac{\sin^2(nt/2)}{\sin^2(t/2)}
 \ge 0$, so $|F_n| = F_n$ and $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int F_n \le 1$ uniformly.
3. **Concentration:** For every $\delta > 0$, $\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{
\delta \le |t| \le \pi} F_n(t)\,dt \to 0$.

Additionally, from the **explicit formula** we get a **uniform decay** property:

> **Claim.** For every $\delta \in (0,\pi)$, $\displaystyle\sup_{\delta \le |t|
\le \pi} F_n(t) \le \frac{1}{2n\pi\,\sin^2(\delta/2)} \to 0$.

*Proof of Claim.* For $\delta \le |t| \le \pi$, we have $|\sin(t/2)| \ge \sin(\d
elta/2) > 0$, so
$$
F_n(t) = \frac{1}{2n\pi}\frac{\sin^2(nt/2)}{\sin^2(t/2)} \le \frac{1}{2n\pi\,\si
n^2(\delta/2)}. \qquad\square
$$

---

**Main argument.**

Using property (1),
$$
\sigma_n(f)(x) - f(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\bigl[f(x-t) - f(x)\bigr]
\,F_n(t)\,dt.
$$

Let $\varepsilon > 0$. Since $f$ is continuous at $x$, there exists $\delta \in
(0,\pi)$ such that
$$
|t| < \delta \implies |f(x-t) - f(x)| < \varepsilon.
$$

Split the integral into the near part ($|t|<\delta$) and the far part ($|t|\ge\d
elta$):

$$
\sigma_n(f)(x) - f(x) = I_n + J_n,
$$
where
$$
I_n = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|t|<\delta}\bigl[f(x-t)-f(x)\bigr]F_n(t)\,dt, \qquad
J_n = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|t|\ge\delta}\bigl[f(x-t)-f(x)\bigr]F_n(t)\,dt.
$$

**Estimating $I_n$:** By continuity and non-negativity of $F_n$,
$$
|I_n| \le \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|t|<\delta}|f(x-t)-f(x)|\,F_n(t)\,dt \le \varepsil
on\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}F_n(t)\,dt = \varepsilon.
$$

**Estimating $J_n$:** Using $|f(x-t)-f(x)|\le|f(x-t)|+|f(x)|$ and non-negativity
 of $F_n$,
$$
|J_n| \le \underbrace{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|t|\ge\delta}|f(x-t)|\,F_n(t)\,dt}_{(A
)} + |f(x)|\cdot\underbrace{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|t|\ge\delta}F_n(t)\,dt}_{(B)}.
$$

- **Term $(B)$:** By property (3) (concentration of the approximate identity), $
(B)\to 0$. Since $f$ is continuous at $x$, $f(x)$ is finite, so $|f(x)|\cdot(B)\
to 0$.

- **Term $(A)$:** By the Claim, $\sup_{|t|\ge\delta}F_n(t)\le \frac{C_\delta}{n}
$ where $C_\delta = \frac{1}{2\pi\sin^2(\delta/2)}$. Therefore
$$
(A) \le \sup_{|t|\ge\delta}F_n(t)\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|t|\ge\delta}|f(x-t)|\
,dt \le \frac{C_\delta}{n}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(x-t)|\,dt = \fr
ac{C_\delta}{n}\|f\|_{L^1}\to 0,
$$
where we used that $f\in L^1$ and periodicity ensures $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(x-t)|
\,dt = \int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(s)|\,ds = \|f\|_{L^1}$.

Thus $|J_n|\to 0$ as $n\to\infty$.

---

**Conclusion.** For $n$ sufficiently large,
$$
|\sigma_n(f)(x) - f(x)| \le |I_n| + |J_n| \le \varepsilon + o(1).
$$
Since $\varepsilon > 0$ was arbitrary,
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sigma_n(f)(x) = f(x). \qquad\blacksquare
$$
