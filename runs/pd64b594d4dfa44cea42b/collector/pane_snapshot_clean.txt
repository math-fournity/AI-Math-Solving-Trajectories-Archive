## Solution

**Answer: No.** The conclusion does not follow in general. Below is a counterexa
mple.

### Setup

Let $C \subseteq [0,1]$ be a **fat Cantor set** (closed, nowhere dense, with Leb
esgue measure $|C| = 1/2$). Set $U = [0,1]\setminus C$; then $U$ is open and den
se in $[0,1]$, with $|U| = 1/2$.

Let $S$ have Lebesgue density $p_S(s) = \frac{1}{|U|}\mathbf{1}_U(s) = 2\cdot\ma
thbf{1}_U(s)$, so $\mathbb{P}_S$ is the normalized restriction of Lebesgue measu
re to $U$.

Let $\xi$ be independent of $S$, with $\mathbb{P}(\xi=1)=\mathbb{P}(\xi=-1)=\tfr
ac12$.

Define measurable functions $f,g:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$ by
$$f(s,x) = -\mathbf{1}_C(s)\cdot x, \qquad g(s,x)=0.$$

### Verification of the hypothesis

Since $\mathbb{P}_S(U)=1$ and $U\cap C=\varnothing$, we have $\mathbf{1}_C(S)=0$
 $\mathbb{P}$-a.s. Therefore
$$f(S(\omega),\xi(\omega)) = -\mathbf{1}_C(S(\omega))\cdot\xi(\omega) = 0 = g(S(
\omega),\xi(\omega)) \quad\text{for $\mathbb{P}$-a.e. }\omega.$$
So the hypothesis $f(S,\xi)\le g(S,\xi)$ a.s. is satisfied. ✓

### The support of $\mathbb{P}_S$

The support of $\mathbb{P}_S$ is the smallest closed set of full $\mathbb{P}_S$-
measure. Since $U$ is dense in $[0,1]$, any closed set containing $U$ must conta
in $\overline{U}=[0,1]$, and $\mathbb{P}_S([0,1])=1$. Hence
$$\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{P}_S)=[0,1].$$

### Failure of the conclusion

For any $s\in C$ (which is a subset of $\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{P}_S)=[0,1]$
), we have $\mathbf{1}_C(s)=1$, so
$$f(s,\xi)=-\xi, \qquad g(s,\xi)=0.$$
When $\xi=-1$ (which occurs with probability $1/2$), $f(s,\xi)=1>0=g(s,\xi)$. Th
us for every $s\in C$,
$$\mathbb{P}\bigl(f(s,\xi)>g(s,\xi)\bigr)=\tfrac12>0,$$
so $f(s,\xi)\le g(s,\xi)$ **fails** $\mathbb{P}$-a.s. for every $s\in C$.

Since $|C|=1/2>0$ and $C\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{P}_S)$, the set of
$s\in\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{P}_S)$ where the inequality fails has **positiv
e Lebesgue measure**. The conclusion does not hold.

### Why the gap exists

By independence, $(S,\xi)$ has law $\mathbb{P}_S\otimes\mathbb{P}_\xi$. Fubini's
 theorem gives: $f\le g$ $\mathbb{P}_S\otimes\mathbb{P}_\xi$-a.e. implies $f(s,\
cdot)\le g(s,\cdot)$ $\mathbb{P}_\xi$-a.s. for **$\mathbb{P}_S$-a.e.** $s$. Sinc
e $\mathbb{P}_S$ has density $p_S$, this means the inequality holds for Lebesgue
-a.e. $s\in\{p_S>0\}=U$.

However, $\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{P}_S)=\overline{\{p_S>0\}}$ can be strictl
y larger than $\{p_S>0\}$, and the difference $\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{P}_S)
\setminus\{p_S>0\}$ (where $p_S=0$) can have **positive Lebesgue measure** — as
demonstrated by the fat Cantor set above. On this "gap" set, $\mathbb{P}_S$ prov
ides no information, and the inequality can fail.
