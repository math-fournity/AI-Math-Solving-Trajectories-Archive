## Solution

Let $p_n$ denote the probability the bug is at vertex $A$ after $n$ moves, start
ing from $A$ at step $0$ (so $p_0 = 1$).

By symmetry, let $q_n$ denote the probability of being at $B$ (which equals the
probability of being at $C$). Since the bug is certainly at one of the three ver
tices,

$$p_n + 2q_n = 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad q_n = \frac{1 - p_n}{2}.$$

**Transition.** At each step, from any vertex the bug moves to either adjacent v
ertex with probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$. The bug can reach $A$ at step $n+1$ only
by being at $B$ or $C$ at step $n$ and moving to $A$:

$$p_{n+1} = q_n \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} + q_n \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} = q_n = \frac{1 - p_
n}{2}.$$

**Solving the recurrence.** The fixed point is $p = \frac{1-p}{2}$, giving $p^*
= \tfrac{1}{3}$ (the uniform stationary distribution on three vertices — a good
sanity check). Setting $r_n = p_n - \tfrac{1}{3}$:

$$r_{n+1} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\, r_n, \qquad r_0 = 1 - \tfrac{1}{3} = \tfrac{2}{3}.$$

Hence $r_n = \tfrac{2}{3}\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^n$, and

$$p_n = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{2}{3}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^n.$$

**Computing $p_6$:**

$$p_6 = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{2}{3}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^6 = \frac{1}{3} + \fr
ac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{64} = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{96} = \frac{32}{96} + \frac{1
}{96} = \frac{33}{96} = \frac{11}{32}.$$

**Verification via the recurrence directly:**

| $n$ | $p_n = \frac{1-p_{n-1}}{2}$ |
|---|---|
| 0 | $1$ |
| 1 | $0$ |
| 2 | $\tfrac{1}{2}$ |
| 3 | $\tfrac{1}{4}$ |
| 4 | $\tfrac{3}{8}$ |
| 5 | $\tfrac{5}{16}$ |
| 6 | $\tfrac{11}{32}$ |

The probability that the bug ends up at $A$ after its six moves is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{11}{32}}.$$
