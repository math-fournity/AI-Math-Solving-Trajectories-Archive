# Solution

**Claim:** Yes. Every simply connected open set in $\mathbb{R}^2$ is homeomorphi
c to an open ball.

**Proof.**

We use the standard convention that "simply connected" entails non-empty and pat
h-connected.

**Step 1: An open ball is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^2$.**

Let $B = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^2 : |x| < 1\}$ be the open unit ball. The map
$$f: \mathbb{R}^2 \to B, \qquad f(x) = \frac{x}{1+|x|}$$
is continuous, with continuous inverse
$$f^{-1}(y) = \frac{y}{1-|y|}, \qquad y \in B.$$
Hence $B \cong \mathbb{R}^2$. So "homeomorphic to an open ball" is the same as "
homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^2$."

**Step 2: The case $U = \mathbb{R}^2$.**

If $U = \mathbb{R}^2$, then by Step 1, $U$ is homeomorphic to an open ball.

**Step 3: The case $U \subsetneq \mathbb{R}^2$, $U \neq \emptyset$.**

Identify $\mathbb{R}^2 \cong \mathbb{C}$. Then $U$ is a non-empty, simply connec
ted, open, **proper** subset of $\mathbb{C}$. By the **Riemann Mapping Theorem**
, there exists a biholomorphic (conformal) bijection
$$\varphi: U \to D,$$
where $D = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| < 1\}$ is the open unit disk. A biholomorphi
c map is, in particular, a homeomorphism. Since $D$ is an open ball, $U$ is home
omorphic to an open ball.

**Conclusion.**

In both cases — $U = \mathbb{R}^2$ and $U$ a proper non-empty simply connected o
pen subset — $U$ is homeomorphic to an open ball. Therefore every simply connect
ed open set in $\mathbb{R}^2$ is homeomorphic to an open ball. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** This is special to dimension 2. In $\mathbb{R}^3$ (and higher) the a
nalogous statement fails: there exist simply connected open subsets of $\mathbb{
R}^3$ not homeomorphic to a ball, e.g. the Whitehead manifold. The two-dimension
al result relies crucially on complex analysis (the Riemann Mapping Theorem), wh
ich has no direct analogue in higher dimensions.
