Yes, such a translation exists. Here is the proof.

**Claim.** There exists $t\in\mathbb{R}$ (in fact a set of $t$'s of positive Leb
esgue measure) such that
$$m\bigl((A_{1}+t)\cap A_{2}\bigr)>0.$$

**Proof.** Since $A_{1},A_{2}$ are bounded and measurable with positive measure,
 their indicator functions $\mathbf{1}_{A_{1}},\mathbf{1}_{A_{2}}$ belong to $L^
{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})$, and $m(A_{i})>0$ for $i=1,2$.

Define the function $g:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)$ by
$$g(t)\;=\;m\bigl((A_{1}+t)\cap A_{2}\bigr)\;=\;\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{
2}}(x)\,\mathbf{1}_{A_{1}}(x-t)\,dx.$$
Equivalently $g=\mathbf{1}_{A_{2}}*\mathbf{1}_{-A_{1}}$, where $\mathbf{1}_{-A_{
1}}(x)=\mathbf{1}_{A_{1}}(-x)$. Being the convolution of an $L^{1}$ function wit
h an $L^{\infty}$ function (both with compact support), $g$ is well defined, non
negative, and uniformly continuous.

We compute the total integral of $g$. By Tonelli's theorem (the integrand below
is nonnegative),
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}} g(t)\,dt
=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{2}}(x)\,\mathbf{1}_{A_{1}}(x-t
)\,dx\,dt
=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{2}}(x)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{1}}
(x-t)\,dt\right)dx.
$$
For every fixed $x$, the substitution $u=x-t$ gives $\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{1}
_{A_{1}}(x-t)\,dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{1}}(u)\,du=m(A_{1})$. Hence
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}} g(t)\,dt
=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{2}}(x)\,m(A_{1})\,dx
=m(A_{1})\,m(A_{2})>0.
$$

Since $g\ge 0$ and $\int g>0$, the set $E=\{t\in\mathbb{R}:g(t)>0\}$ has positiv
e Lebesgue measure (otherwise $g=0$ a.e. and the integral would be $0$). In part
icular $E$ is nonempty, so there exists $t\in\mathbb{R}$ with $g(t)=m\bigl((A_{1
}+t)\cap A_{2}\bigr)>0$.

Moreover, because $g$ is continuous and positive at some point, $g>0$ on an open
 neighborhood of that point, so the set of admissible translations actually cont
ains a nonempty open interval (and thus has positive measure).

Taking $\tau(x)=x+t$ for any such $t$ yields the desired translation. $\blacksqu
are$
