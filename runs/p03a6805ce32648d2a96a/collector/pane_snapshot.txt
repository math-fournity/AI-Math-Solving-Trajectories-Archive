## Proof

**Yes**, one can necessarily choose one winning team from each day without repet
ition.

**Setup.** Label the teams $1, 2, \dots, 2n$ and the days $1, 2, \dots, 2n-1$. O
n day $d$, exactly $n$ teams win (one per game); let $S_d$ be the set of winners
 on day $d$, so $|S_d| = n$. We seek a *system of distinct representatives* (SDR
) for the family $\{S_1, S_2, \dots, S_{2n-1}\}$: a choice $w_d \in S_d$ with al
l $w_d$ distinct. By **Hall's Marriage Theorem**, such an SDR exists if and only
 if for every subcollection of days $D \subseteq \{1,\dots,2n-1\}$,
$$\Bigl|\bigcup_{d\in D} S_d\Bigr| \;\geq\; |D|.$$

**Verifying Hall's condition.** Fix any nonempty $D$ and let $k = |D|$. Set
$$W = \bigcup_{d\in D} S_d \quad\text{(teams that won on at least one day in }D\
text{)}, \qquad L = \{1,\dots,2n\}\setminus W.$$
A team in $L$ did **not** win on any day in $D$; since every team plays exactly
one game per day and every game has a winner, each team in $L$ **lost** on every
 day in $D$.

**Key observation.** On each day $d \in D$, every team in $L$ plays a game and l
oses, so its opponent on that day is a winner, hence belongs to $W$. Over the $k
$ days in $D$, a team in $L$ plays $k$ games against $k$ **distinct** opponents
(distinct because the tournament is round-robin—each pair meets exactly once). T
herefore each team in $L$ has at least $k$ distinct neighbors in $W$.

- **If $L \neq \emptyset$:** any single team in $L$ supplies $k$ distinct elemen
ts of $W$, so $|W| \geq k$.
- **If $L = \emptyset$:** then $W = \{1,\dots,2n\}$, so $|W| = 2n \geq 2n-1 \geq
 k$.

In both cases $|W| \geq k = |D|$, which is precisely Hall's condition.

**Conclusion.** Hall's condition holds for every subcollection $D$, so by Hall's
 Marriage Theorem the family $\{S_1, \dots, S_{2n-1}\}$ admits a system of disti
nct representatives. That is, we can select one winner $w_d$ from each day $d$ w
ith $w_1, w_2, \dots, w_{2n-1}$ all distinct. $\blacksquare$
