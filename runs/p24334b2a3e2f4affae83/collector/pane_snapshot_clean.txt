## Solution

We evaluate $\oint_C \frac{e^z-1}{\sin^3 z}\,dz$ where $C$ is the unit circle $|
z|=1$, using the residue theorem.

### Singularities inside $C$

The integrand $f(z)=\frac{e^z-1}{\sin^3 z}$ is singular where $\sin z=0$, i.e. a
t $z=n\pi$, $n\in\mathbb{Z}$. Since $\pi\approx 3.14159>1$, the only singularity
 inside $|z|<1$ is at $z=0$.

### Order of the pole at $z=0$

Near $z=0$: $\sin z\sim z$, so $\sin^3 z\sim z^3$; and $e^z-1\sim z$. Hence
$$f(z)\sim \frac{z}{z^3}=\frac{1}{z^2},$$
so $z=0$ is a **pole of order 2**.

### Laurent expansion and residue

Use the identity $\sin^3 z=\frac{3\sin z-\sin 3z}{4}$:
$$\sin^3 z = z^3-\frac{z^5}{2}+\cdots = z^3\!\left(1-\frac{z^2}{2}+\cdots\right)
.$$

Also
$$e^z-1=z+\frac{z^2}{2}+\frac{z^3}{6}+\cdots=z\!\left(1+\frac{z}{2}+\frac{z^2}{6
}+\cdots\right).$$

Therefore
$$f(z)=\frac{z\left(1+\frac{z}{2}+\frac{z^2}{6}+\cdots\right)}{z^3\left(1-\frac{
z^2}{2}+\cdots\right)}=\frac{1}{z^2}\left(1+\frac{z}{2}+\frac{z^2}{6}+\cdots\rig
ht)\!\left(1+\frac{z^2}{2}+\cdots\right).$$

The factor $\left(1-\frac{z^2}{2}+\cdots\right)^{-1}=1+\frac{z^2}{2}+\cdots$ con
tributes only even powers, so the coefficient of $z$ in the product is exactly $
\frac{1}{2}$. Thus

$$f(z)=\frac{1}{z^2}+\frac{1}{2z}+\frac{2}{3}+\cdots$$

and the residue is
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z)=\frac{1}{2}.$$

**Verification via the pole-of-order-2 formula.** With $h(z)=z^2 f(z)=\frac{z^2(
e^z-1)}{\sin^3 z}$, the residue is $h'(0)$. From the expansion above, $h(z)=1+\f
rac{z}{2}+\frac{2z^2}{3}+\cdots$, so $h'(0)=\frac{1}{2}$. ✓

### Applying the residue theorem

$$\oint_C \frac{e^z-1}{\sin^3 z}\,dz = 2\pi i\cdot \operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z)
=2\pi i\cdot\frac{1}{2}=\boxed{\pi i}.$$
