## Proof

Let $Q \subset \mathbb{P}^3$ be a nonsingular quadric over an algebraically clos
ed field $k$.

**Step 1: $Q \cong \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$.**

Pick coordinates $x_0, x_1, x_2, x_3$ on $\mathbb{P}^3$ so that $Q$ is given by
$x_0 x_3 - x_1 x_2 = 0$ (every nonsingular quadric over an algebraically closed
field is projectively equivalent to this one, since the associated quadratic for
m has full rank and splits completely). The map
$$\varphi: \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^3, \quad ([s:t],[u:v]
) \mapsto [su : sv : tu : tv]$$
has image exactly $\{x_0 x_3 - x_1 x_2 = 0\}$, and is an isomorphism onto $Q$ (i
t is bijective and birational, hence an isomorphism since the source is smooth a
nd the target is normal).

**Step 2: On a smooth variety, Weil divisors coincide with Cartier divisors.**

Since $Q$ is nonsingular, every local ring $\mathcal{O}_{Q,p}$ is a regular loca
l ring, hence a UFD. Therefore every height-one prime is principal locally, so e
very Weil divisor is Cartier. This gives a natural isomorphism
$$\mathrm{WCl}(Q) = \mathrm{Cl}(Q) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \mathrm{Pic}(Q).$$

**Step 3: $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1) \cong \mathbb{Z} \oplu
s \mathbb{Z}$.**

Let $\pi_1, \pi_2$ be the two projections. By the Künneth formula (or the standa
rd computation of line bundles on a product of projective spaces),
$$\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1) \cong \pi_1^*\mathrm{Pic}(\math
bb{P}^1) \oplus \pi_2^*\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^1) \cong \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathb
b{Z}.$$
Concretely, every line bundle is of the form $\mathcal{O}(a,b) = \pi_1^*\mathcal
{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(a) \otimes \pi_2^*\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(b)$, and $\mathc
al{O}(a,b) \cong \mathcal{O}(a',b')$ iff $a=a'$ and $b=b'$ (e.g., by restricting
 to the two ruling fibers $\{p\}\times\mathbb{P}^1$ and $\mathbb{P}^1\times\{q\}
$).

**Step 4: Geometric interpretation via the two rulings.**

Under the isomorphism $Q \cong \mathbb{P}^1\times\mathbb{P}^1$, the two classica
l rulings of the quadric correspond to the fibers of $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$:
- $F_1 = \pi_2^*([\text{point}])$: a line of the first ruling (class $(0,1)$),
- $F_2 = \pi_1^*([\text{point}])$: a line of the second ruling (class $(1,0)$).

The hyperplane section $H = Q \cap \mathbb{P}^2$ has class $(1,1)$, i.e. $H \sim
 F_1 + F_2$, which is the classical fact that a plane section of a quadric is a
conic splitting into one line from each ruling.

**Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 1–3,
$$\boxed{\mathrm{WCl}(Q) \cong \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z},}$$
freely generated by the classes of the two rulings $F_1, F_2$, with the hyperpla
ne class $H = F_1 + F_2$.
