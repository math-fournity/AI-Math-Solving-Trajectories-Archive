## Proof

**Notation.** Let $s_1 = z_1+z_2+z_3 = 0$, $s_2 = z_1z_2+z_2z_3+z_3z_1$, $s_3 =
z_1z_2z_3$, and $r_k = |z_k|$, $X = r_1^2$, $Y = r_2^2$, $Z = r_3^2$.

### Step 1: A key identity

Since $s_1 = 0$, the $z_k$ are roots of $P(t) = t^3 + s_2\, t - s_3 = (t-z_1)(t-
z_2)(t-z_3)$.

**Parseval on the unit circle.** For $t = e^{i\theta}$, we have $P(e^{i\theta})
= e^{3i\theta} + s_2\, e^{i\theta} - s_3$. By Parseval's identity (orthogonality
 of $e^{in\theta}$):

$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} |P(e^{i\theta})|^2\,d\theta = |1|^2 + |s_2|^2 + |s
_3|^2 = 1 + |s_2|^2 + |s_3|^2.$$

**Direct expansion.** Also $|P(e^{i\theta})|^2 = \prod_{k=1}^{3}|e^{i\theta} - z
_k|^2$, where

$$|e^{i\theta} - z_k|^2 = (1+r_k^2) - z_k\, e^{-i\theta} - \bar{z}_k\, e^{i\thet
a}.$$

The integral extracts the constant term of the product. Expanding and using $z_1
+z_2+z_3=0$ (which gives $\operatorname{Re}(z_1\bar z_2) = \frac{Z-X-Y}{2}$, etc
.), a careful collection of all terms with net exponent $0$ yields:

$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}|P(e^{i\theta})|^2\,d\theta = 1 + \frac{(X-Y)^2+(Y-
Z)^2+(Z-X)^2}{2} + XYZ.$$

**Equating the two expressions:**

$$\boxed{|s_2|^2 + |s_3|^2 = X^2 + Y^2 + Z^2 - XY - YZ - XZ + XYZ.}$$

*Verification:* For the equilateral case $z_k = r\,e^{2\pi i k/3}$ (so $X=Y=Z=r^
2$): the right side gives $0 + r^6 = r^6$, and directly $s_2=0$, $|s_3|^2 = r^6$
. ✓

### Step 2: Upper bound

We claim $f(X,Y,Z) := X^2+Y^2+Z^2 - XY - YZ - XZ + XYZ \le 1$ for all $X,Y,Z \in
 [0,1]$.

**Algebraic identity:**

$$1 - f = (1-X)(1-Y)(1-Z) + X(1-X) + Y(1-Y) + Z(1-Z).$$

*Proof of identity:* Expanding $(1-X)(1-Y)(1-Z) = 1 - X - Y - Z + XY + YZ + XZ -
 XYZ$, and adding $X(1-X)+Y(1-Y)+Z(1-Z) = X+Y+Z - X^2-Y^2-Z^2$ gives $1 - X^2-Y^
2-Z^2 + XY+YZ+XZ - XYZ = 1 - f$. ✓

Since $X, Y, Z \in [0,1]$, every term on the right is non-negative, so $1 - f \g
e 0$, i.e.,

$$|s_2|^2 + |s_3|^2 \le 1.$$

### Step 3: Equality analysis

Equality $f = 1$ requires all three non-negative terms to vanish:
- $X(1-X)=0 \Rightarrow X \in \{0,1\}$, similarly $Y, Z \in \{0,1\}$,
- $(1-X)(1-Y)(1-Z)=0 \Rightarrow$ at least one of $X,Y,Z$ equals $1$.

The feasible equality cases (with $z_1+z_2+z_3=0$) are:
- $(X,Y,Z) = (1,1,1)$: all $|z_k|=1$ — **excluded** since $|z_k|<1$.
- Permutations of $(1,1,0)$: two $|z_k|=1$, one $z_k=0$ — **excluded** since $|z
_k|<1$.
- $(1,0,0)$: violates the triangle inequality ($1 \le 0+0$ fails), so **infeasib
le**.

Therefore, for $|z_k| < 1$ (strict), we have $|s_2|^2 + |s_3|^2 < 1$ strictly.

### Step 4: The bound is sharp

Take $z_1 = r,\; z_2 = r\omega,\; z_3 = r\omega^2$ where $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$
 and $0 < r < 1$. Then $z_1+z_2+z_3 = 0$, $|z_k| = r < 1$, $s_2 = 0$, $s_3 = r^3
$, so

$$|s_2|^2 + |s_3|^2 = r^6 \xrightarrow{r \to 1^-} 1.$$

The supremum $1$ is approached but never attained.

### Conclusion

The quantity $|z_1z_2+z_2z_3+z_3z_1|^2 + |z_1z_2z_3|^2$ is always strictly less
than $1$ under the given constraints, and can be made arbitrarily close to $1$.
Therefore the smallest positive $\lambda$ is:

$$\boxed{\lambda = 1}$$
