# Proof that $d$ is measurable

**Claim.** The function $d:\mathcal{P}(X)\times\mathcal{P}(X)\to[0,1]$, defined
by $d(p,q)=\sup_{A\in\mathcal{B}(X)}|p(A)-q(A)|$, is Borel measurable.

---

## Step 1. The evaluation maps $p\mapsto p(A)$ are measurable for every $A\in\ma
thcal{B}(X)$.

Since $X$ is a standard Borel space, we fix a compatible Polish topology on $X$.
 The weak convergence topology on $\mathcal{P}(X)$ is generated by the maps $p\m
apsto\int f\,dp$ for $f\in C_b(X)$, and $\mathcal{P}(X)$ is itself a Polish spac
e.

For any closed set $F\subseteq X$, define $f_n(x)=\max(0,\,1-n\cdot d(x,F))\in C
_b(X,[0,1])$. Then $f_n\downarrow\mathbf{1}_F$, so by monotone convergence $p(F)
=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int f_n\,dp$. Each map $p\mapsto\int f_n\,dp$ is continuous,
hence measurable, so $p\mapsto p(F)$ is measurable for closed $F$.

The class $\mathcal{D}=\{A\in\mathcal{B}(X):p\mapsto p(A)\text{ is measurable}\}
$ is a $\sigma$-algebra: it contains $X$; it is closed under complements since $
p(A^c)=1-p(A)$; and it is closed under countable disjoint unions by dominated co
nvergence ($p(\bigcup A_n)=\lim_N\sum_{n\leq N}p(A_n)$, a limit of measurable fu
nctions). Since $\mathcal{D}$ contains all closed sets (a $\pi$-system generatin
g $\mathcal{B}(X)$), we conclude $\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{B}(X)$.

---

## Step 2. Reduction to a countable supremum.

Since $X$ is standard Borel, $\mathcal{B}(X)$ is countably generated. Let $\{G_n
\}_{n\geq 1}$ be a countable generator, and let $\mathcal{A}_0$ be the algebra g
enerated by $\{G_n\}$—i.e., the collection of all finite unions of finite inters
ections of sets from $\{G_n, G_n^c\}$. Then $\mathcal{A}_0$ is **countable** and
 $\sigma(\mathcal{A}_0)=\mathcal{B}(X)$.

**Lemma.** For any $p,q\in\mathcal{P}(X)$,
$$\sup_{A\in\mathcal{A}_0}|p(A)-q(A)|=\sup_{B\in\mathcal{B}(X)}|p(B)-q(B)|.$$

*Proof.* The inequality $\leq$ is immediate since $\mathcal{A}_0\subseteq\mathca
l{B}(X)$. For the reverse, fix $B\in\mathcal{B}(X)$ and $\varepsilon>0$. Conside
r the finite measure $\mu:=p+q$ on $\mathcal{B}(X)$. By the **approximation theo
rem** for finite measures on a countably generated $\sigma$-algebra, there exist
s $A\in\mathcal{A}_0$ with $\mu(A\triangle B)<\varepsilon$. Then
$$|p(A)-p(B)|\leq p(A\triangle B)\leq\mu(A\triangle B)<\varepsilon,\qquad |q(A)-
q(B)|\leq q(A\triangle B)\leq\mu(A\triangle B)<\varepsilon.$$
By the triangle inequality, $|p(A)-q(A)|\geq|p(B)-q(B)|-2\varepsilon$. Taking su
premum over $A\in\mathcal{A}_0$ and letting $\varepsilon\to 0$ gives $\sup_{A\in
\mathcal{A}_0}|p(A)-q(A)|\geq|p(B)-q(B)|$ for every $B$, hence the reverse inequ
ality. $\square$

*(The approximation theorem used here is standard: the class $\{B\in\sigma(\math
cal{A}_0):\forall\varepsilon>0\,\exists A\in\mathcal{A}_0,\;\mu(A\triangle B)<\v
arepsilon\}$ is a $\sigma$-algebra containing $\mathcal{A}_0$, hence equals $\si
gma(\mathcal{A}_0)=\mathcal{B}(X)$.)*

Enumerate $\mathcal{A}_0=\{A_1,A_2,A_3,\dots\}$. By the lemma:
$$d(p,q)=\sup_{n\geq 1}|p(A_n)-q(A_n)|.$$

---

## Step 3. $d$ is a countable supremum of measurable functions.

Since $\mathcal{P}(X)$ is Polish (hence separable and metrizable), the Borel $\s
igma$-algebra on $\mathcal{P}(X)\times\mathcal{P}(X)$ equals the product $\sigma
$-algebra $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{P}(X))\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{P}(X))$.

For each $n$, the map
$$(p,q)\longmapsto p(A_n)$$
is the composition of the projection $\pi_1(p,q)=p$ (measurable) with $p\mapsto
p(A_n)$ (measurable by Step 1), hence measurable. Similarly $(p,q)\mapsto q(A_n)
$ is measurable. Therefore
$$(p,q)\longmapsto |p(A_n)-q(A_n)|$$
is measurable for each $n$.

Since a countable supremum of measurable functions is measurable:
$$d(p,q)=\sup_{n\geq 1}|p(A_n)-q(A_n)|$$
is Borel measurable. $\blacksquare$
