## Solution

We need to find the smallest positive integer $n$ such that

$$S = \sum_{k=2}^{2015} (-1)^k \binom{2015}{k}(n\cdot k^{2015} - 1) \equiv 1 \pm
od{2017}.$$

### Step 1: Separate the sum

Expanding the summand:

$$S = n\underbrace{\sum_{k=2}^{2015}(-1)^k\binom{2015}{k}k^{2015}}_{A} - \underb
race{\sum_{k=2}^{2015}(-1)^k\binom{2015}{k}}_{B}.$$

### Step 2: Compute $B$

By the binomial theorem, $\sum_{k=0}^{2015}(-1)^k\binom{2015}{k} = (1-1)^{2015}
= 0$. Extracting the $k=0$ and $k=1$ terms:

$$1 - 2015 + B = 0 \implies B = 2014.$$

### Step 3: Compute $A$ via a standard identity

Recall the identity (from inclusion–exclusion / Stirling numbers):

$$\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^k\binom{m}{k}k^m = (-1)^m\, m!.$$

With $m = 2015$:

$$\sum_{k=0}^{2015}(-1)^k\binom{2015}{k}k^{2015} = (-1)^{2015}\cdot 2015! = -201
5!.$$

The $k=0$ term is $0$, and the $k=1$ term is $(-1)\binom{2015}{1}\cdot 1 = -2015
$. Therefore:

$$-2015 + A = -2015! \implies A = -2015! + 2015.$$

### Step 4: Reduce mod 2017 using Wilson's theorem

Since $2017$ is prime, Wilson's theorem gives $2016! \equiv -1 \pmod{2017}$. Sin
ce $2016! = 2016 \cdot 2015!$ and $2016 \equiv -1 \pmod{2017}$:

$$(-1)\cdot 2015! \equiv -1 \pmod{2017} \implies 2015! \equiv 1 \pmod{2017}.$$

Thus:

$$A \equiv -1 + 2015 = 2014 \equiv -3 \pmod{2017}.$$

### Step 5: Solve the congruence

We need $nA - B \equiv 1 \pmod{2017}$:

$$n(-3) - 2014 \equiv 1 \pmod{2017}$$
$$-3n \equiv 2015 \pmod{2017}$$
$$-3n \equiv -2 \pmod{2017}$$
$$3n \equiv 2 \pmod{2017}.$$

The inverse of $3$ mod $2017$: since $3 \times 672 = 2016 \equiv -1$, we get $3^
{-1} \equiv -672 \equiv 1345 \pmod{2017}$.

$$n \equiv 2 \cdot 1345 = 2690 \equiv 2690 - 2017 = 673 \pmod{2017}.$$

**Verification:** $3 \times 673 = 2019 = 2017 + 2 \equiv 2 \pmod{2017}$. ✓

### Answer

The smallest positive integer value for $n$ is $\boxed{673}$.
