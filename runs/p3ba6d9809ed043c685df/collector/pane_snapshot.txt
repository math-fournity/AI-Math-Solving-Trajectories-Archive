## Construction of a Trihedral Angle from Its Dihedral Angles

### Setup and Key Ideas

A **trihedral angle** is the figure formed by three planes through a common vert
ex $O$. It has three **dihedral angles** $A, B, C$ (between pairs of planes, mea
sured along their lines of intersection) and three **face angles** $\alpha, \bet
a, \gamma$ (between pairs of edges on each face). Intersecting the trihedral ang
le with a unit sphere centered at $O$ yields a **spherical triangle** whose *ang
les* are the dihedral angles $A, B, C$ and whose *sides* are the face angles $\a
lpha, \beta, \gamma$.

The construction rests on two ingredients:

1. **Constructing a trihedral angle from its face angles** (a known procedure).
2. **Polarity**: every trihedral angle $T$ has a *polar* trihedral angle $T^*$ w
hose face angles are $\pi - A,\; \pi - B,\; \pi - C$ and whose dihedral angles a
re $\pi - \alpha,\; \pi - \beta,\; \pi - \gamma$. Since $(T^*)^* = T$, we constr
uct $T^*$ from the supplementary angles $\pi-A, \pi-B, \pi-C$ (as face angles) a
nd then polarize to recover $T$.

### Step 1 — Construct a Trihedral Angle from Face Angles $a, b, c$

**Given** face angles $a, b, c$ satisfying the trihedral inequalities ($0 < a,b,
c < \pi$; each less than the sum of the other two; $a+b+c < 2\pi$).

**Procedure:**

1. In a plane $\Pi$, draw two rays $OA$, $OB$ with $\angle AOB = c$.
2. Compute the dihedral angle $D$ along edge $OA$ via the **spherical law of cos
ines**:
$$\cos D = \frac{\cos b - \cos a\,\cos c}{\sin a\,\sin c}.$$
3. Construct a plane $\Pi'$ through the line $OA$ making dihedral angle $D$ with
 $\Pi$.
4. In $\Pi'$, draw ray $OC$ with $\angle AOC = a$ (on the side consistent with t
he orientation).
5. The three planes $\Pi = (OAB)$, $(OBC)$, and $\Pi' = (OAC)$ form the trihedra
l angle with face angles $a, b, c$.

**Proof of correctness.** Set coordinates with $O$ at the origin, $OA$ along the
 $x$-axis, $OB$ in the $xy$-plane at angle $c$ from $OA$. Since $C$ lies in plan
e $\Pi'$ (tilted by angle $D$ from $\Pi$ about axis $OA$) at angle $a$ from $OA$
, on the unit sphere:
$$C = (\cos a,\; \sin a \cos D,\; \sin a \sin D), \qquad B = (\cos c,\; \sin c,\
; 0).$$

Then
$$\cos \angle BOC = B \cdot C = \cos c \cos a + \sin c \sin a \cos D.$$

Substituting $\cos D = \frac{\cos b - \cos a \cos c}{\sin a \sin c}$:
$$\cos \angle BOC = \cos a \cos c + \sin c \sin a \cdot \frac{\cos b - \cos a \c
os c}{\sin a \sin c} = \cos a \cos c + \cos b - \cos a \cos c = \cos b.$$

Hence $\angle BOC = b$, confirming all three face angles are $a, b, c$. $\square
$

**Note on constructibility.** Each step is a compass-and-straightedge operation:
 the trigonometric quantities $\cos a, \sin a$, etc., are realized as lengths vi
a right triangles; products and quotients are constructed via similar triangles;
 $\arccos$ is realized by constructing a right triangle with the appropriate rat
io. Thus the entire construction is compass-and-straightedge, provided the given
 angles are constructible.

### Step 2 — Polarity: From a Trihedral Angle to Its Polar

**Definition.** Given a trihedral angle $T$ with vertex $O$ and face-planes $P_1
, P_2, P_3$, its **polar** $T^*$ is the trihedral angle whose edges are the inwa
rd unit normals $n_1, n_2, n_3$ to $P_1, P_2, P_3$ (pointing into $T$), and whos
e faces are the planes through pairs $(n_2, n_3)$, $(n_1, n_3)$, $(n_1, n_2)$.

**Polarity Theorem.** The face angles of $T^*$ are $\pi - A, \pi - B, \pi - C$ (
supplementary to the dihedral angles of $T$), and the dihedral angles of $T^*$ a
re $\pi - \alpha, \pi - \beta, \pi - \gamma$ (supplementary to the face angles o
f $T$). Moreover, $(T^*)^* = T$.

*Proof.* The face angle of $T^*$ between edges $n_j$ and $n_k$ equals the angle
between the normals to $P_j$ and $P_k$, which is $\pi$ minus the dihedral angle
of $T$ along the edge $P_j \cap P_k$ (i.e., along $e_i$). Labeling so that dihed
ral angle $A$ is along $e_1 = P_2 \cap P_3$, the face angle of $T^*$ opposite $n
_1$ (between $n_2, n_3$) is $\pi - A$, and cyclically. The statement about dihed
ral angles of $T^*$ follows by applying the same argument to $(T^*)^* = T$. $\sq
uare$

**Geometric construction of the polar.** Given $T^*$ with faces $P_1^*, P_2^*, P
_3^*$ through $O$:
- For each face plane $P_i^*$, construct the line through $O$ perpendicular to $
P_i^*$ (by erecting perpendiculars to two lines in $P_i^*$ through $O$), choosin
g the inward direction.
- These three perpendicular rays are the edges of $T = (T^*)^*$.
- The planes through pairs of these edges are the faces of $T$.

### Complete Construction from Dihedral Angles $A, B, C$

**Given:** dihedral angles $A, B, C$ satisfying the existence conditions (see be
low).

1. **Form the supplementary angles** $a^* = \pi - A$, $b^* = \pi - B$, $c^* = \p
i - C$. These are the face angles of the polar trihedral angle $T^*$.

2. **Construct $T^*$ with face angles $a^*, b^*, c^*$** using Step 1:
   - (a) In a plane $\Pi$, draw rays $OA, OB$ with $\angle AOB = c^* = \pi - C$.
   - (b) Compute $D^* = \arccos\!\left(\frac{\cos b^* - \cos a^* \cos c^*}{\sin
a^*\, \sin c^*}\right)$.
   - (c) Erect plane $\Pi'$ through $OA$ at dihedral angle $D^*$ from $\Pi$.
   - (d) In $\Pi'$, draw $OC$ with $\angle AOC = a^* = \pi - A$.
   - (e) The planes $(OAB)$, $(OBC)$, $(OAC)$ form $T^*$.

3. **Polarize $T^*$ to obtain $T$:**
   - (a) For each of the three face-planes of $T^*$, erect the inward perpendicu
lar ray from $O$.
   - (b) These three rays are the edges of $T$; the planes through pairs of edge
s are the faces of $T$.

**Proof of correctness.** By construction, $T^*$ has face angles $\pi-A, \pi-B,
\pi-C$. By the Polarity Theorem, $T = (T^*)^*$ has dihedral angles $\pi - (\pi -
 A) = A$, $\pi - (\pi - B) = B$, $\pi - (\pi - C) = C$. $\square$

### Existence Conditions

The construction succeeds if and only if a spherical triangle with angles $A, B,
 C$ exists, which requires:

- $0 < A, B, C < \pi$ (each dihedral angle strictly between $0$ and $\pi$),
- $A + B + C > \pi$ (sum of dihedral angles exceeds $\pi$),
- $A + B < C + \pi$, $\;B + C < A + \pi$, $\;A + C < B + \pi$ (each angle less t
han the sum of the other two plus $\pi$).

Equivalently, the supplementary angles $\pi-A, \pi-B, \pi-C$ must satisfy the tr
ihedral face-angle inequalities: each positive, each less than the sum of the ot
her two, and their sum less than $2\pi$ (which is $A+B+C > \pi$).

### Summary

The construction proceeds in two phases: **(1)** build the *polar* trihedral ang
le $T^*$ from the supplementary face angles $\pi-A, \pi-B, \pi-C$ using the sphe
rical law of cosines to determine the necessary dihedral angle $D^*$, and **(2)*
* recover $T$ by erecting inward normals to the faces of $T^*$. The polarity the
orem guarantees that the resulting trihedral angle has exactly the prescribed di
hedral angles $A, B, C$.
