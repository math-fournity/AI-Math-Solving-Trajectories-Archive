**Answer: Yes, $S$ must be a group.**

## Proof

Let $S$ be a non-empty associative semigroup, left and right cancellative, such
that for every $a \in S$ the set $\{a^n : n \geq 1\}$ is finite.

### Step 1. Idempotents are two-sided identities.

**Claim.** If $e \in S$ is idempotent ($e^2 = e$), then $e$ is a two-sided ident
ity for all of $S$.

*Proof.* For any $x \in S$:
$$e \cdot (ex) = (ee) \cdot x = e \cdot x.$$
By **left cancellation** (canceling $e$ on the left), $ex = x$. Similarly,
$$(xe) \cdot e = x \cdot (ee) = x \cdot e,$$
and by **right cancellation** (canceling $e$ on the right), $xe = x$. Hence $e$
is a two-sided identity. $\square$

**Corollary.** There is at most one idempotent: if $e, f$ are both two-sided ide
ntities, then $e = ef = f$.

### Step 2. Every element satisfies $a = a^{c+1}$ for some $c \geq 1$.

Since $\{a^n : n \geq 1\}$ is finite, by the pigeonhole principle there exist $1
 \leq i < j$ with $a^i = a^j$. Set $c = j - i \geq 1$, so $a^i = a^{i+c}$.

If $i = 1$, we are done: $a = a^{1+c}$.

If $i \geq 2$, write $a^i = a \cdot a^{i-1}$ and $a^{i+c} = a \cdot a^{i-1+c}$.
Then
$$a \cdot a^{i-1} = a \cdot a^{i-1+c}.$$
By **left cancellation**, $a^{i-1} = a^{i-1+c}$. Repeating this reduction $i-1$
times yields
$$a = a^{1+c} = a^{c+1}. \quad \square$$

### Step 3. $a^c$ is an idempotent.

From $a = a^{c+1}$, multiplying on the left by $a^{n-1}$ gives $a^n = a^{n+c}$ f
or all $n \geq 1$. In particular, with $n = c$:
$$a^c = a^{2c} = (a^c)^2.$$
So $a^c$ is idempotent. $\square$

### Step 4. $S$ is a group.

Since $S$ is non-empty, pick any $a \in S$. By Steps 2–3, $a^c$ is an idempotent
 for some $c \geq 1$. By Step 1, $e := a^c$ is a two-sided identity for all of $
S$, and by the Corollary it is the **unique** idempotent.

Now for **any** $b \in S$, the same argument produces an idempotent $b^{c'}$, wh
ich by uniqueness equals $e$. Thus $b^{c'} = e$ for some $c' \geq 1$, and
$$b \cdot b^{c'-1} = b^{c'} = e, \qquad b^{c'-1} \cdot b = b^{c'} = e.$$
(If $c' = 1$, then $b = e$ and $b$ is its own inverse.) Hence every element has
a two-sided inverse in $S$.

Therefore $S$ is a group. $\blacksquare$
