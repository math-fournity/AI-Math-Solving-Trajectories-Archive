**Counterexample.**

Consider the two nonnegative integer matrices

$$X_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad X_2 = \begin{pmatri
x} 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Both are non-degenerate (invertible: $\det X_1 = 2$, $\det X_2 = -1$) with nonne
gative integer entries.

**Perron–Frobenius eigenvalues.** For a $2\times 2$ matrix $\begin{pmatrix} a &
b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$, the eigenvalues are $\frac{(a+d)\pm\sqrt{(a-d)^2+4bc}
}{2}$.

For $X_1$: trace $= 4$, determinant $= 2$, so eigenvalues are $\frac{4 \pm \sqrt
{16-8}}{2} = 2 \pm \sqrt{2}$. The Perron–Frobenius eigenvalue is

$$\lambda_{FP}(X_1) = 2 + \sqrt{2} \approx 3.414.$$

For $X_2$: trace $= 2$, determinant $= -1$, so eigenvalues are $\frac{2 \pm \sqr
t{4+4}}{2} = 1 \pm \sqrt{2}$. The Perron–Frobenius eigenvalue is

$$\lambda_{FP}(X_2) = 1 + \sqrt{2} \approx 2.414.$$

**The difference is an integer:**

$$\lambda_{FP}(X_1) - \lambda_{FP}(X_2) = (2+\sqrt{2}) - (1+\sqrt{2}) = 1 \in \m
athbb{Z}.$$

**Neither eigenvalue is an integer:** $2+\sqrt{2}$ and $1+\sqrt{2}$ are both irr
ational (since $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational).

**Verification of Perron–Frobenius conditions.** Both matrices are irreducible n
onnegative matrices (their associated directed graphs are strongly connected: $X
_1$ has edges $1\to 2, 2\to 1$ and self-loops; $X_2$ has edges $1\to 2, 2\to 1$)
. The Perron–Frobenius eigenvalue is the spectral radius, which is the larger ei
genvalue in each case: $|2-\sqrt{2}| < 2+\sqrt{2}$ and $|1-\sqrt{2}| < 1+\sqrt{2
}$. ✓

**General construction.** The phenomenon is systematic: for any non-square posit
ive integer $d$ and any nonnegative integers $a_1 \neq a_2$, the matrices $\begi
n{pmatrix} a_i & d \\ 1 & a_i \end{pmatrix}$ have Perron–Frobenius eigenvalues $
a_i + \sqrt{d}$, whose difference $a_1 - a_2$ is an integer, while neither eigen
value is an integer.
