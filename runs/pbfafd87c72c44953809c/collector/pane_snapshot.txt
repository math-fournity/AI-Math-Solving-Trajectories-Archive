## Solution

Consider the driftless control-affine system on an open set $U \subseteq \mathbb
{R}^n$:

$$\dot{x} = \sum_{i=1}^m h_i(x)\,u_i, \qquad x \in U,\; u \in \mathbb{R}^m,$$

with the $h_i$ smooth vector fields on $U$. The associated distribution is

$$\Delta(x) = \operatorname{span}\{h_1(x),\ldots,h_m(x)\}.$$

We assume throughout that $\Delta$ has **constant rank** $m$ on $U$ (in particul
ar the $h_i$ are pointwise linearly independent).

---

### 1. Involutivity (the algebraic condition)

**Definition.** $\Delta$ is *involutive* if for every pair of vector fields $\ta
u_1, \tau_2$ taking values in $\Delta$ (i.e. $\tau_1(x), \tau_2(x) \in \Delta(x)
$ for all $x$), their Lie bracket also lies in $\Delta$:

$$[\tau_1, \tau_2](x) \in \Delta(x) \quad \forall x \in U.$$

Because $\Delta$ is spanned by $\{h_1,\ldots,h_m\}$, it suffices to check bracke
ts of the generators:

$$[h_i, h_j](x) \in \Delta(x) \quad \forall\, i,j \in \{1,\ldots,m\},\; \forall
x \in U.$$

Equivalently, there exist smooth functions $c_{ij}^{\,k}(x)$ such that

$$[h_i, h_j](x) = \sum_{k=1}^m c_{ij}^{\,k}(x)\, h_k(x).$$

This is a purely algebraic/computational test: form the $m(m-1)/2$ brackets $[h_
i,h_j]$ and verify each is a linear combination (with smooth coefficients) of $h
_1,\ldots,h_m$.

---

### 2. Complete integrability (the geometric condition)

**Definition.** $\Delta$ is *completely integrable* on $U$ if through every poin
t $x_0 \in U$ there passes an $(n-m)$-dimensional *integral manifold* $M$ of $\D
elta$, i.e. a submanifold $M$ with $x_0 \in M$, $\dim M = n - m$, and

$$T_x M = \Delta(x) \quad \forall x \in M.$$

Equivalently (and this is the form most useful for control), there exist $n - m$
 smooth functions $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{n-m}: U \to \mathbb{R}$ with linear
ly independent differentials $d\lambda_1,\ldots,d\lambda_{n-m}$ such that

$$d\lambda_k(x)\, h_i(x) = 0 \quad \forall\, k=1,\ldots,n-m,\; i=1,\ldots,m,\; x
 \in U.$$

The level sets $\{x : \lambda_1(x)=a_1,\ldots,\lambda_{n-m}(x)=a_{n-m}\}$ are th
en the integral manifolds, and the $\lambda_k$ are **first integrals** (conserve
d quantities) of the system: along any trajectory $\dot x = \sum h_i u_i$,

$$\frac{d}{dt}\lambda_k(x(t)) = d\lambda_k\,\dot x = \sum_i u_i\, d\lambda_k\, h
_i = 0.$$

So complete integrability means the system has $n-m$ independent conserved quant
ities, and the motion is confined to their level sets.

---

### 3. The Frobenius theorem (the equivalence)

> **Frobenius Theorem.** Let $\Delta$ be a smooth distribution of *constant rank
* $m$ on an open set $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$. Then the following are equivale
nt:
>
> 1. $\Delta$ is **involutive** on $U$.
> 2. $\Delta$ is **completely integrable** on $U$.

**Sketch of proof of the nontrivial direction (involutive $\Rightarrow$ integrab
le).** Fix $x_0 \in U$. Since $\operatorname{rank}\Delta = m$, after a smooth co
ordinate change we may assume $h_1,\ldots,h_m$ have the form $h_i = \partial/\pa
rtial x_i + \sum_{\alpha>m} \tilde h_i^\alpha(x)\,\partial/\partial x_\alpha$ ne
ar $x_0$ (the first $m$ components form an invertible block). Involutivity $[h_i
,h_j]\in\Delta$ forces the compatibility conditions

$$\frac{\partial \tilde h_i^\alpha}{\partial x_j} - \frac{\partial \tilde h_j^\a
lpha}{\partial x_i} + \sum_\beta\!\left(\tilde h_j^\beta \frac{\partial \tilde h
_i^\alpha}{\partial x_\beta} - \tilde h_i^\beta \frac{\partial \tilde h_j^\alpha
}{\partial x_\beta}\right) = 0,$$

which are exactly the integrability conditions for the Pfaffian system $dx_\alph
a - \sum_i \tilde h_i^\alpha\,dx_i = 0$, $\alpha>m$. By the classical Frobenius/
Pfaff theorem, there exist functions $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{n-m}$ with $d\la
mbda_k \perp \Delta$, giving the integral manifolds $\{\lambda = \text{const}\}$
. The converse (integrable $\Rightarrow$ involutive) follows because $d\lambda_k
[\tau_1,\tau_2] = \tau_1(d\lambda_k\tau_2) - \tau_2(d\lambda_k\tau_1) = 0$, so $
[\tau_1,\tau_2]\in (\operatorname{span}\{d\lambda_k\})^\perp = \Delta$. $\square
$

---

### 4. Conclusion: the test applied to the control system

For the driftless control-affine system $\dot{x} = \sum_{i=1}^m h_i(x)u_i$, the
system is **completely integrable** (equivalently, it admits $n-m$ independent f
irst integrals and every trajectory is confined to an $(n-m)$-dimensional leaf)
**if and only if** the distribution $\Delta = \operatorname{span}\{h_1,\ldots,h_
m\}$ satisfies the following two conditions:

1. **Constant rank:** $\operatorname{rank}\Delta(x) = m$ for all $x \in U$ (the
$h_i$ are pointwise linearly independent, so $\Delta$ is a genuine smooth distri
bution of fixed dimension).

2. **Involutivity:** for all $i,j \in \{1,\ldots,m\}$ and all $x \in U$,
$$[h_i, h_j](x) \in \Delta(x),$$
i.e. each Lie bracket $[h_i,h_j]$ is a smooth linear combination of $h_1,\ldots,
h_m$.

By the Frobenius theorem, these two conditions are **necessary and sufficient**
for complete integrability. When they hold, there exist $n-m$ smooth functions $
\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{n-m}$ with independent differentials annihilating $\De
lta$, the level sets of $(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{n-m})$ are the maximal integ
ral manifolds (leaves of the foliation), and the $\lambda_k$ are conserved along
 every controlled trajectory. When involutivity fails — i.e. some $[h_i,h_j]$ ha
s a component transverse to $\Delta$ — the system is *not* completely integrable
, and the bracket-generating (Chow) condition typically makes it instead *contro
llable* (motion in the transverse bracket direction becomes achievable by switch
ing controls), which is the complementary, "non-integrable" regime.
