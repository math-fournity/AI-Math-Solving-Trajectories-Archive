## Solution

**Claim:** For all $n \geq 1$, $\;f_n(x) = x(x+n)^{n-1}$.

**Proof by induction.**

*Base case ($n=1$):* From $f_0(x)=1$ and $f_1'(x) = 1\cdot f_0(x+1) = 1$, we get
 $f_1(x) = x + C$. The condition $f_1(0)=0$ gives $C=0$, so $f_1(x) = x = x(x+1)
^0$. ✓

*Inductive step:* Assume $f_n(x) = x(x+n)^{n-1}$. Then
$$f_n(x+1) = (x+1)(x+n+1)^{n-1}.$$

The recurrence gives $f_{n+1}'(x) = (n+1)\,f_n(x+1) = (n+1)(x+1)(x+n+1)^{n-1}$.

Integrating with substitution $t = x+n+1$ (so $x+1 = t-n$):
$$f_{n+1}(x) = (n+1)\int (t-n)\,t^{n-1}\,dt = (n+1)\!\left[\frac{t^{n+1}}{n+1} -
 t^n\right] = (x+n+1)^{n+1} - (n+1)(x+n+1)^n.$$

Factoring:
$$f_{n+1}(x) = (x+n+1)^n\bigl[(x+n+1)-(n+1)\bigr] = (x+n+1)^n \cdot x = x\,(x+n+
1)^n.$$

This is exactly the claimed form with $n$ replaced by $n+1$. Also $f_{n+1}(0) =
0\cdot(n+1)^n = 0$, satisfying the boundary condition. ✓

By induction, $f_n(x) = x(x+n)^{n-1}$ for all $n \geq 1$.

**Applying to $n=100$:**
$$f_{100}(1) = 1 \cdot (1+100)^{99} = 101^{99}.$$

Since $101$ is prime, the explicit factorization of $f_{100}(1)$ into powers of
distinct primes is:

$$\boxed{f_{100}(1) = 101^{99}}$$
