## Proof

Let $m$ denote Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}$, and let $A \subset \mathbb{R}$
be a Borel set with $m(A) > 0$. We show that such a decomposition always exists.

**Define the distribution function.** Consider
$$F(t) := m\!\big(A \cap (-\infty, t]\big), \qquad t \in \mathbb{R}.$$

**$F$ is non-decreasing.** If $s \le t$, then $A \cap (-\infty, s] \subseteq A \
cap (-\infty, t]$, so $F(s) \le F(t)$ by monotonicity of measure.

**$F$ is continuous.** For continuity from the right at $t$: as $s \downarrow t$
, the sets $A \cap (-\infty, s]$ decrease to $A \cap (-\infty, t]$, so by contin
uity of measure from above (all sets have finite measure since $m(A) < \infty$ o
n bounded intervals, and we may restrict to a bounded region), $F(s) \to F(t)$.
For continuity from the left: as $s \uparrow t$, the sets $A \cap (-\infty, s]$
increase to $A \cap (-\infty, t)$, so $F(s) \to m(A \cap (-\infty, t))$. Since
$$m\big(A \cap (-\infty, t]\big) - m\big(A \cap (-\infty, t)\big) = m\big(A \cap
 \{t\}\big) \le m(\{t\}) = 0,$$
we get $F(t-) = F(t)$. Hence $F$ is continuous everywhere.

**Limits of $F$.** Since $A \cap (-\infty, t] \nearrow A$ as $t \to +\infty$, by
 continuity of measure from below,
$$\lim_{t \to +\infty} F(t) = m(A) > 0.$$
Since $A \cap (-\infty, t] \searrow \emptyset$ as $t \to -\infty$, and these set
s have finite measure (for $t$ sufficiently negative, $m(A \cap (-\infty, t]) \l
e m((-\infty, t]) < \infty$),
$$\lim_{t \to -\infty} F(t) = 0.$$

**Apply the Intermediate Value Theorem.** Since $F$ is continuous with $\lim_{t\
to-\infty} F(t) = 0$ and $\lim_{t\to+\infty} F(t) = m(A) > 0$, there exists $t_0
 \in \mathbb{R}$ such that
$$F(t_0) = \frac{m(A)}{2}.$$

**Construct $B$ and $C$.** Set
$$B := A \cap (-\infty, t_0], \qquad C := A \cap (t_0, +\infty).$$

Both are Borel sets (intersections of Borel sets). They are disjoint, and $B \cu
p C = A$. Moreover,
$$m(B) = F(t_0) = \frac{m(A)}{2} > 0,$$
and
$$m(C) = m(A) - m(B) = m(A) - \frac{m(A)}{2} = \frac{m(A)}{2} > 0.$$

Thus $B$ and $C$ are disjoint Borel sets whose union is $A$, each with positive
Lebesgue measure. $\blacksquare$
