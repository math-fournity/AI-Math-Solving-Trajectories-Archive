## Solution

**Setup.** Place the rectangular hub with side lengths 12 km (N–S) and 9 km (E–W

2 \le r \le 3$) has center $P$. The constraints are:

1. The circle must **touch** the boundary $\partial R$ (at least one common poin
t).
2. The circle must **not overlap** the interior of $R$.

Since the drone "orbits the hub" and maintains "constant contact with the **exte
rior**," the circle lies outside the hub. For a circle of radius $r$ centered at
 an exterior point $P$ to touch $\partial R$ without entering the interior, the
closest point of $R$ to $P$ must lie on $\partial R$ at distance **exactly** $r$
:

$$\operatorname{dist}(P, R) = r, \quad P \notin \operatorname{int}(R).$$

If $\operatorname{dist}(P,R) > r$, the circle misses $R$ entirely (no contact).
If $\operatorname{dist}(P,R) < r$, the circle crosses $\partial R$ and penetrate
s the interior (overlap forbidden). Hence for each fixed $r$, the locus of valid
 centers is exactly the **offset curve** at distance $r$ from $R$.

**The region.** Taking the union over all $r \in [2, 3]$, the set of all valid c
enters is the closed annular region

$$\mathcal{A} = \{\, P \notin \operatorname{int}(R) : 2 \le \operatorname{dist}(
P, R) \le 3 \,\}.$$

This is precisely the region between the outer parallel bodies $R_3$ and $R_2$,
where $R_t = \{P : \operatorname{dist}(P, R) \le t\}$:

$$\mathcal{A} = R_3 \setminus R_2^\circ.$$

**Applying Steiner's formula.** For a convex body $K$ with area $A$ and perimete
r $L$, the area of the outer parallel body at distance $t$ is

$$\operatorname{Area}(K_t) = A + Lt + \pi t^2.$$

For our rectangle: $A = 12 \cdot 9 = 108$, $L = 2(12 + 9) = 42$.

$$\operatorname{Area}(R_3) = 108 + 42 \cdot 3 + 9\pi = 234 + 9\pi,$$
$$\operatorname{Area}(R_2) = 108 + 42 \cdot 2 + 4\pi = 192 + 4\pi.$$

Since the rectangle interior (where $\operatorname{dist}=0$) is contained in $R_
2$, subtracting removes it automatically, leaving only the exterior annulus:

$$\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{A}) = \operatorname{Area}(R_3) - \operatorname{Ar
ea}(R_2) = (234 + 9\pi) - (192 + 4\pi) = 42 + 5\pi.$$

**Direct verification (decomposition).** The annulus decomposes into:
- **4 rectangular strips** along the sides, each of width $3 - 2 = 1$ km and len

- **4 quarter-annular sectors** at the corners, each spanning radii $2$ to $3$:
total area $= 4 \cdot \tfrac{1}{4}\pi(3^2 - 2^2) = 5\pi$.

Sum: $42 + 5\pi$. ✓
