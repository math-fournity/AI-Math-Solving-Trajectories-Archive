## Solution

Let $b_1, b_2 \in \mathbb{R}$ (scalars, since they are added to the scalar quant
ities $c_i^\top z$). Assume $S \neq \emptyset$ (equivalently, $c_2 \neq 0$ or $b
_2 > 0$), otherwise the supremum is taken over the empty set.

**Claim.** The supremum is finite if and only if the augmented vectors $(c_1, b_
1)$ and $(c_2, b_2)$ are proportional, i.e. there exists $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}
$ with
$$c_1 = \lambda\, c_2 \quad\text{and}\quad b_1 = \lambda\, b_2.$$
In that case $\sup_{z \in S} \dfrac{|c_1^\top z + b_1|}{c_2^\top z + b_2} = |\la
mbda|$ (in fact a maximum). Otherwise the supremum is $+\infty$.

---

### Proof

Set $f(z) = \dfrac{|c_1^\top z + b_1|}{c_2^\top z + b_2}$ and $t(z) = c_2^\top z
 + b_2 > 0$ on $S$.

**Finite case.** Suppose $(c_1, b_1) = \lambda (c_2, b_2)$. Then for every $z \i
n S$,
$$c_1^\top z + b_1 = \lambda\,(c_2^\top z + b_2) = \lambda\, t(z),$$
so
$$f(z) = \frac{|\lambda|\, t(z)}{t(z)} = |\lambda|.$$
Thus $f$ is constant on $S$, and $\sup_{z\in S} f(z) = \max_{z\in S} f(z) = |\la
mbda|$.

**Infinite case, sub-case (i): $c_1$ is not a scalar multiple of $c_2$.**
We show $f$ is unbounded on $S$.

- If $c_2 \neq 0$: fix $t = 1$, i.e. consider the affine hyperplane $H = \{z : c
_2^\top z = 1 - b_2\} \subset S$. Since $c_1$ is not a multiple of $c_2$, the li
near functional $z \mapsto c_1^\top z$ is unbounded on $H$ (its restriction to t
he direction subspace $\ker(c_2^\top)$ is nontrivial). Hence $|c_1^\top z + b_1|
 \to \infty$ along $H$, while the denominator equals $1$. So $\sup f = +\infty$.

- If $c_2 = 0$: non-emptiness of $S$ forces $b_2 > 0$, and "$c_1$ not a multiple
 of $c_2 = 0$" forces $c_1 \neq 0$. Then $f(z) = |c_1^\top z + b_1|/b_2$, which
is unbounded since $c_1^\top z$ ranges over all of $\mathbb{R}$. So $\sup f = +\
infty$.

**Infinite case, sub-case (ii): $c_1 = \lambda c_2$ for some $\lambda$, but $b_1
 \neq \lambda b_2$.**
Here $c_2 \neq 0$ (if $c_2 = 0$ then $c_1 = 0$ and $b_1 \neq \lambda b_2 = 0$ fo
r the relevant $\lambda$ would require $b_2 = 0$, making $(c_2,b_2) = (0,0)$ and
 $S = \emptyset$, excluded). With $c_2 \neq 0$ we may let $t = c_2^\top z + b_2$
 approach $0^+$ (choose $z$ along the $c_2$-direction). Then
$$f(z) = \frac{|\lambda\, t + (b_1 - \lambda b_2)|}{t} = \left|\lambda + \frac{b
_1 - \lambda b_2}{t}\right| \xrightarrow{t \to 0^+} +\infty,$$
since $b_1 - \lambda b_2 \neq 0$. Hence $\sup f = +\infty$.

**Conclusion.** The only way to avoid $+\infty$ is to have *both* $c_1 = \lambda
 c_2$ and $b_1 = \lambda b_2$, i.e. $(c_1, b_1) = \lambda (c_2, b_2)$. Combining
 both parts:

$$\boxed{\sup_{z \in S} \frac{|c_1^\top z + b_1|}{c_2^\top z + b_2} = \begin{cas
es} |\lambda|, & \text{if } (c_1, b_1) = \lambda (c_2, b_2) \text{ for some } \l
ambda \in \mathbb{R},\\[4pt] +\infty, & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}}$$

(When finite, the value $|\lambda|$ is attained at every point of $S$, so it is
a maximum.) $\blacksquare$
