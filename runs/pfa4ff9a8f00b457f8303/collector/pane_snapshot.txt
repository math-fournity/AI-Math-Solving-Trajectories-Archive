## Key Lemma

**Claim:** P(x) and P(y) share a prime factor p if and only if p | P(x) and [p |
 (x−y) or p | (x+y+1)].

**Proof:** Note P(x) − P(y) = (x²+x+1) − (y²+y+1) = (x−y)(x+y+1). If p | P(x) an
d p | P(y), then p | (x−y)(x+y+1), so p | (x−y) or p | (x+y+1). Conversely, if p
 | P(x) and p | (x−y), then y ≡ x (mod p), so P(y) ≡ P(x) ≡ 0. If p | P(x) and p
 | (x+y+1), then y ≡ −1−x (mod p), so P(y) = y²+y+1 ≡ (1+x)² −(1+x) + 1 = x²+x+1
 = P(x) ≡ 0 (mod p). □

Setting x = a+i, y = a+j, d = j−i: the shared prime p must satisfy p | d or p |
(2(a+i)+d+1), with p | P(a+i).

**Observation:** P(n) = n(n+1)+1 is always **odd** (since n(n+1) is even), so p
= 2 never works.
