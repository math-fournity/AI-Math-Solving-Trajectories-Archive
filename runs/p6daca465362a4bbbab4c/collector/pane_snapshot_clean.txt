## Proof

**Setup.** Let $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n \in (0,\pi)$ be the interior angles of
 a convex $n$-gon, so $\sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i = (n-2)\pi$. Denote the ordered ang
les (decreasing) as $\alpha_{(1)} \ge \alpha_{(2)} \ge \cdots \ge \alpha_{(n)}$.
 We seek the set of all possible values of $\alpha_{(k)}$.

**Realizability fact.** A tuple $(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n) \in (0,\pi)^n$ with $
\sum \alpha_i = (n-2)\pi$ is realizable as the interior angles of a convex $n$-g
on. Indeed, the exterior angles $\beta_i = \pi - \alpha_i \in (0,\pi)$ satisfy $
\sum \beta_i = 2\pi$. The corresponding unit direction vectors $\mathbf{u}_1,\do
ts,\mathbf{u}_n$ trace strictly around the unit circle (since all $\beta_i>0$ an
d they sum to $2\pi$), so the origin lies in the interior of their convex hull.
Hence there exist $l_i > 0$ with $\sum l_i \mathbf{u}_i = 0$, giving a closed co
nvex polygon with the prescribed angles.

**Deriving the bounds.** Fix $x = \alpha_{(k)}$. Then:
- $\alpha_{(1)}, \dots, \alpha_{(k-1)} \in [x, \pi)$ (the $k-1$ angles above $x$
),
- $\alpha_{(k)} = x$,
- $\alpha_{(k+1)}, \dots, \alpha_{(n)} \in (0, x]$ (the $n-k$ angles below $x$).

The remaining sum to distribute among the other $n-1$ angles is $S = (n-2)\pi -
x$. The achievable range of this remaining sum is the open interval
$$\big((k-1)x,\; (k-1)\pi + (n-k)x\big),$$
since the infimum $(k-1)x + 0$ is not attained (lower angles must be $>0$) and t
he supremum $(k-1)\pi + (n-k)x$ is not attained (upper angles must be $<\pi$). B
y continuity and connectedness of the domain, every value in this open interval
is attained.

For $S = (n-2)\pi - x$ to lie in this interval, we need both:

1. **Lower bound on the sum:** $(k-1)x < (n-2)\pi - x$, giving $kx < (n-2)\pi$,
i.e., $$x < \frac{(n-2)\pi}{k}.$$

2. **Upper bound on the sum:** $(n-2)\pi - x < (k-1)\pi + (n-k)x$, giving $(n-k-
1)\pi < (n-k+1)x$, i.e., $$x > \frac{(n-k-1)\pi}{n-k+1}.$$

Combined with the constraint $x \in (0,\pi)$, we obtain:
$$\max\!\left(0,\,\frac{(n-k-1)\pi}{n-k+1}\right) < x < \min\!\left(\pi,\,\frac{
(n-2)\pi}{k}\right).$$

**Simplification into cases.** Note:
- $\frac{(n-k-1)\pi}{n-k+1} \ge 0$ iff $k \le n-1$; it equals $0$ when $k=n-1$ a
nd is negative when $k=n$.
- $\frac{(n-2)\pi}{k} \ge \pi$ iff $k \le n-2$; it is $<\pi$ when $k \ge n-1$.

So the answer splits cleanly:

- **Case $k \le n-2$:** $\displaystyle\frac{(n-k-1)\pi}{n-k+1} < x < \pi$.

- **Case $k \ge n-1$:** $\displaystyle 0 < x < \frac{(n-2)\pi}{k}$.

**The interval is non-empty.** For $k \le n-2$: $\frac{n-k-1}{n-k+1} < 1$ always
 holds. For $k \ge n-1$: $0 < \frac{(n-2)\pi}{k}$ since $n \ge 3$ and $k \ge 1$.

**Endpoints are not attained.**
- The lower bound $\frac{(n-k-1)\pi}{n-k+1}$ (when $>0$) would require the $k-1$
 upper angles to all equal $\pi$ (impossible). When the lower bound is $0$, it w
ould require $x=0$ (impossible).
- The upper bound $\pi$ (when binding) would require $x=\pi$ (impossible). The u
pper bound $\frac{(n-2)\pi}{k}$ (when binding, $<\pi$) would require the $n-k$ l
ower angles to all equal $0$ (impossible).

**Achievability of every interior value.** For any $x$ strictly inside the inter
val, $S = (n-2)\pi - x$ lies in the open interval $\big((k-1)x,\, (k-1)\pi + (n-
k)x\big)$, so we can find $k-1$ angles in $[x,\pi)$ and $n-k$ angles in $(0,x]$
summing to $S$. Together with $\alpha_{(k)}=x$, this gives a valid angle tuple i
n $(0,\pi)^n$ summing to $(n-2)\pi$, which is realizable as a convex $n$-gon by
the realizability fact above.

**Verification with examples.**
- $k=1, n=3$ (largest angle of triangle): $\left(\frac{\pi}{3}, \pi\right)$. ✓ (
Largest angle exceeds the average $\pi/3$ and is less than $\pi$.)
- $k=n, n=4$ (smallest angle of quadrilateral): $\left(0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$.
 ✓ (Smallest angle is below the average $\pi/2$.)

---

**Answer.** The set of all values that the $k$th-largest interior angle of a con
vex $n$-gon can take is the open interval

$$\boxed{\left(\max\!\left(0,\,\frac{(n-k-1)\pi}{n-k+1}\right),\;\min\!\left(\pi
,\,\frac{(n-2)\pi}{k}\right)\right)}.$$

Equivalently:
- If $k \le n-2$: $\displaystyle\left(\frac{(n-k-1)\pi}{n-k+1},\;\pi\right)$.
- If $k \ge n-1$: $\displaystyle\left(0,\;\frac{(n-2)\pi}{k}\right)$.
