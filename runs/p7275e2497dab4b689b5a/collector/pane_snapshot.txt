## Solution

**Question (restated).** Given a probability space $(X,\mu)$, $\mu(X)=1$, can we
 impose conditions on $(X,\mu)$ so that for *every* probability measure $\nu\ll\
mu$ the Radon–Nikodym derivative $f:=d\nu/d\mu$ satisfies $\|f\|_{L^\infty(\mu)}
<\infty$?

**Answer.** Yes. The necessary and sufficient condition is:

> **(C)** There exists $\delta>0$ such that every measurable $A$ with $\mu(A)>0$
 satisfies $\mu(A)\ge\delta$.

Equivalently, $\mu$ is purely atomic with only finitely many atoms. Under (C) on
e even has the uniform bound $\sup_{\nu}\|d\nu/d\mu\|_\infty\le 1/\delta$.

---

### 1. Reformulation

A probability measure $\nu\ll\mu$ corresponds bijectively to a non-negative $f\i
n L^1(\mu)$ with $\int_X f\,d\mu=1$, via $f=d\nu/d\mu$. Thus the question become
s:

> Is every non-negative $f\in L^1(\mu)$ with $\int f\,d\mu=1$ automatically in $
L^\infty(\mu)$?

This is exactly the condition $L^1(\mu)=L^\infty(\mu)$ (restricted to non-negati
ve functions, but the two formulations are equivalent by linearity).

### 2. Sufficiency of (C)

Assume (C). Let $f\ge0$ with $\int f\,d\mu=1$. For any $t>0$ set $A_t=\{f>t\}$.
By Chebyshev/Markov,
$$
\mu(A_t)\le \frac{1}{t}\int f\,d\mu=\frac{1}{t}.
$$
If $t>1/\delta$ then $\mu(A_t)<\delta$, so by (C) we must have $\mu(A_t)=0$. Hen
ce $f\le 1/\delta$ $\mu$-a.e., i.e.
$$
\|f\|_\infty\le \frac{1}{\delta}.
$$
So every RN derivative is essentially bounded, uniformly in $\nu$.

### 3. Necessity of (C)

Suppose (C) fails: there exist measurable sets $A_n$ with $0<\mu(A_n)\to 0$. Def
ine the probability measure
$$
\nu_n(B):=\frac{\mu(B\cap A_n)}{\mu(A_n)},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad f_n:=\frac{d\n
u_n}{d\mu}=\frac{\mathbf 1_{A_n}}{\mu(A_n)}.
$$
Then $\int f_n\,d\mu=1$ but
$$
\|f_n\|_\infty=\frac{1}{\mu(A_n)}\longrightarrow\infty.
$$
So without (C) there exist $\nu\ll\mu$ with $\|d\nu/d\mu\|_\infty=\infty$.

### 4. Equivalence of (C) with "purely atomic, finitely many atoms"

**(C) $\Rightarrow$ finitely many atoms.** Let $\{x_i\}$ be the atoms, $p_i=\mu(
\{x_i\})>0$. By (C), $p_i\ge\delta$, so there are at most $\lfloor 1/\delta\rflo
or$ atoms. If there were a non-atomic part, by Saks's theorem it would contain s
ets of arbitrarily small positive measure, contradicting (C). Hence $\mu$ is pur
ely atomic with finitely many atoms.

**Finitely many atoms $\Rightarrow$ (C).** Write $X=\{x_1,\dots,x_N\}\cup N$ wit
h $\mu(N)=0$ and $p_i=\mu(\{x_i\})>0$. Any $A$ with $\mu(A)>0$ contains some $x_
i$, so $\mu(A)\ge p_i\ge\min_j p_j=:\delta>0$.

### 5. Sharpness of the bound

When $X=\{x_1,\dots,x_N\}$ with masses $p_i$, the extremal $\nu=\delta_{x_{i^*}}
$ with $p_{i^*}=\min_i p_i$ gives $f=\mathbf 1_{\{x_{i^*}\}}/p_{i^*}$ and $\|f\|
_\infty=1/p_{i^*}=1/\delta$. So the uniform bound $1/\delta$ is attained and sha
rp.

**Conclusion.** Conditions *can* be imposed: the necessary and sufficient condit
ion is that $(X,\mu)$ be a finite probability space (purely atomic with finitely
 many atoms), equivalently that the measures of non-null sets be bounded below b
y some $\delta>0$. Under this condition every Radon–Nikodym derivative $d\nu/d\m
u$ is essentially bounded, with the sharp uniform bound $\|d\nu/d\mu\|_\infty\le
 1/\delta$.
