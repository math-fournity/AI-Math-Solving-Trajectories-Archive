Looking at this problem, I need to find the largest $c > 0$ such that the inequa
lity holds for all $n$ and all positive $a_j, b_j$.

**Answer:** $c = \dfrac{1}{(e-1)^2}$.

## Proof

**Notation.** Let $S_k = \sum_{j=1}^k a_j b_{k+1-j}$ (Stage Yield) and $T_k = \s
um_{j=1}^k b_j^2\, j!$ (Stage Stability). Define $A_k = \sum_{m=1}^k \frac{1}{m!
}$.

---

### Upper bound: $\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{S_k^4}{T_k^2} \leq (e-1)^2 \sum_{j=1}^n a_j
^4$

**Step 1 (Cauchy–Schwarz with factorial weights).** For each $k$, apply Cauchy–S
chwarz to $S_k = \sum_{j=1}^k a_j\, b_{k+1-j}$ with weights $w_j = \frac{1}{(k+1
-j)!}$:

$$S_k^2 = \left(\sum_{j=1}^k a_j\, b_{k+1-j}\right)^2 \leq \left(\sum_{j=1}^k \f
rac{a_j^2}{(k+1-j)!}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^k b_{k+1-j}^2\,(k+1-j)!\right).$$

Substituting $i = k+1-j$ in the second factor gives $\sum_{i=1}^k b_i^2\, i! = T
_k$. Therefore:

$$\frac{S_k^4}{T_k^2} \leq \left(\sum_{j=1}^k \frac{a_j^2}{(k+1-j)!}\right)^2.$$

**Step 2 (Cauchy–Schwarz again).** Apply Cauchy–Schwarz to the square of the sum
 with weights $\lambda_j = \frac{1}{(k+1-j)!}$:

$$\left(\sum_{j=1}^k \frac{a_j^2}{(k+1-j)!}\right)^2 \leq \left(\sum_{j=1}^k \fr
ac{a_j^4}{(k+1-j)!}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^k \frac{1}{(k+1-j)!}\right) = A_k \su
m_{j=1}^k \frac{a_j^4}{(k+1-j)!}.$$

**Step 3 (Sum over $k$ and swap order).** Summing over $k = 1, \ldots, n$:

$$\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{S_k^4}{T_k^2} \leq \sum_{k=1}^n A_k \sum_{j=1}^k \frac{a_j^
4}{(k+1-j)!} = \sum_{j=1}^n a_j^4 \underbrace{\sum_{k=j}^n \frac{A_k}{(k+1-j)!}}
_{=:\, C_j(n)}.$$

**Step 4 (Bound $C_j(n)$).** Substituting $m = k+1-j$:

$$C_j(n) = \sum_{m=1}^{n+1-j} \frac{A_{m+j-1}}{m!} = \sum_{m=1}^{n+1-j} \frac{1}
{m!}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m+j-1}\frac{1}{\ell!}.$$

Since $A_{m+j-1} = \sum_{\ell=1}^{m+j-1}\frac{1}{\ell!} \leq \sum_{\ell=1}^{\inf
ty}\frac{1}{\ell!} = e-1$ and $\sum_{m=1}^{n+1-j}\frac{1}{m!} \leq e-1$:

$$C_j(n) \leq (e-1) \cdot (e-1) = (e-1)^2.$$

Therefore:

$$\boxed{\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{S_k^4}{T_k^2} \leq (e-1)^2 \sum_{j=1}^n a_j^4},$$

which establishes the inequality with $c = \frac{1}{(e-1)^2}$.

---

### Sharpness: the constant $(e-1)^2$ cannot be improved

**Test sequence.** Set $a_j = 1$ for all $j$, and $b_j = \frac{1}{j!}$.

**Compute $T_k$:** $\displaystyle T_k = \sum_{j=1}^k \frac{1}{(j!)^2}\cdot j! =
\sum_{j=1}^k \frac{1}{j!} = A_k.$

**Compute $S_k$:** $\displaystyle S_k = \sum_{j=1}^k 1 \cdot \frac{1}{(k+1-j)!}
= \sum_{m=1}^k \frac{1}{m!} = A_k.$

**Therefore:** $\displaystyle \frac{S_k^4}{T_k^2} = \frac{A_k^4}{A_k^2} = A_k^2.
$

**The ratio becomes:**

$$\frac{\sum_{j=1}^n a_j^4}{\sum_{k=1}^n S_k^4/T_k^2} = \frac{n}{\sum_{k=1}^n A_
k^2}.$$

Since $A_k \to e - 1$ as $k \to \infty$, by the Cesàro mean theorem:

$$\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^n A_k^2 \;\longrightarrow\; (e-1)^2,$$

so

$$\frac{n}{\sum_{k=1}^n A_k^2} \;\longrightarrow\; \frac{1}{(e-1)^2}.$$

For any $c > \frac{1}{(e-1)^2}$, choosing $n$ sufficiently large makes $\frac{n}
{\sum A_k^2} < c$, violating the inequality. Hence no constant larger than $\fra
c{1}{(e-1)^2}$ is valid.

---

### Conclusion

The largest constant is:

$$c = \frac{1}{(e-1)^2}.$$
