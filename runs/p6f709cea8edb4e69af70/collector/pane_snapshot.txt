## Proof

**Yes**, such a monic polynomial $f \in A[x]$ exists.

**Setup.** Since $L/K$ is unramified of degree $n$, the ramification index is $e
(L/K)=1$ and the residue degree is $f(L/K)=n$. Let $k = A/\mathfrak{m}$ and $\el
l = B/\mathfrak{m}B$ denote the residue fields. Then $\ell/k$ is a finite separa
ble extension of degree $n$ (separability of the residue field extension is part
 of the definition of an unramified separable extension). Moreover, $B$ is a fre
e $A$-module of rank $n$: a uniformizer $\pi$ of $A$ also serves as a uniformize
r of $B$ (since $e=1$), and any $k$-basis of $\ell$ lifts to an $A$-basis of $B$
.

**Choosing a primitive element.** By the primitive element theorem, there exists
 $\bar{\alpha} \in \ell$ with $\ell = k(\bar{\alpha})$. Let $\bar{g}(x) \in k[x]
$ be the minimal polynomial of $\bar{\alpha}$ over $k$; it is monic, separable,
and $\deg \bar{g} = [\ell:k] = n$.

**Lifting via Hensel's lemma.** Choose a lift $\alpha_0 \in B$ of $\bar{\alpha}$
 (under the reduction $B \to \ell$), and lift $\bar{g}$ to a monic polynomial $g
(x) \in A[x]$ of degree $n$. We have $\bar{g}(\bar{\alpha}) = 0$ and $\bar{g}'(\
bar{\alpha}) \neq 0$ (by separability of $\bar{g}$). Hensel's lemma applies: the
re exists a unique $\alpha \in B$ such that $g(\alpha) = 0$ and $\alpha \equiv \
alpha_0 \pmod{\mathfrak{m}B}$.

**The key map.** Define the $A$-algebra homomorphism
$$\varphi: A[x]/(g) \longrightarrow B, \qquad \bar{x} \longmapsto \alpha.$$
Since $g$ is monic of degree $n$, $A[x]/(g)$ is a free $A$-module of rank $n$ wi
th basis $1, \bar{x}, \dots, \bar{x}^{n-1}$.

**Reduction modulo $\mathfrak{m}$.** Tensoring with $k$, the map $\varphi \otime
s k: k[x]/(\bar{g}) \to \ell$ sends $x \mapsto \bar{\alpha}$. Since $\bar{g}$ is
 the minimal polynomial of $\bar{\alpha}$ over $k$, this is an isomorphism:
$$k[x]/(\bar{g}) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} k(\bar{\alpha}) = \ell.$$

**Injectivity via Nakayama.** Let $I = \ker(\varphi)$. Since $\varphi \otimes k$
 is injective, we have $I \subseteq \mathfrak{m}\cdot A[x]/(g)$. The module $A[x
]/(g)$ is finite over $A$, and $\mathfrak{m}$ lies in the Jacobson radical of th
e local ring $A$. By Nakayama's lemma, $I = 0$, so $\varphi$ is injective.

**Surjectivity via Nakayama.** Let $M = \operatorname{coker}(\varphi) = B/\varph
i(A[x]/(g))$. Since $\varphi \otimes k$ is surjective, $M = \mathfrak{m}M$. As $
B$ is a finite $A$-module, so is $M$, and Nakayama's lemma gives $M = 0$. Thus $
\varphi$ is surjective.

**Conclusion.** The map $\varphi: A[x]/(g) \xrightarrow{\sim} B$ is an isomorphi
sm of $A$-algebras, and $g \in A[x]$ is monic. Setting $f = g$, we obtain
$$B \cong A[x]/(f)$$
with $f$ monic. $\blacksquare$
