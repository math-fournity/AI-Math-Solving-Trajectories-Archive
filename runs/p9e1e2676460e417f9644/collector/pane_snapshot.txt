### a) A homeomorphism with a unique invariant measure

**Construction.** Identify $S^1 = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ and define
$$\phi([x]) = [x^2], \qquad x \in [0,1].$$

**Well-definedness and continuity.** The map $x \mapsto x^2$ on $[0,1]$ is conti
nuous, strictly increasing, and maps $[0,1]$ bijectively onto $[0,1]$ (with inve
rse $x \mapsto \sqrt{x}$). Since $0^2 = 0$ and $1^2 = 1$ and $[0]=[1]$ in $\math
bb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$, this descends to a homeomorphism $\phi$ of $S^1$.

**Fixed point set.** $\phi([x])=[x]$ requires $x^2 - x \in \mathbb{Z}$. For $x \
in [0,1]$, we have $x(x-1) \in [-\tfrac{1}{4}, 0]$, so $x(x-1) \in \mathbb{Z}$ f
orces $x(x-1)=0$, i.e., $x=0$ or $x=1$. These represent the same point $p=[0]$.
Thus $p$ is the **unique** fixed point.

**Orbit behavior.** For $x \in (0,1)$:
- Forward: $\phi^n([x]) = [x^{2^n}] \to [0] = p$ since $x^{2^n} \to 0$.
- Backward: $\phi^{-n}([x]) = [x^{1/2^n}] \to [1] = [0] = p$ since $x^{1/2^n} \t
o 1$.

So every orbit converges to $p$ in both forward and backward time.

**Uniqueness of invariant measure.** Let $\mu$ be any $\phi$-invariant Borel pro
bability measure. For any continuous $f: S^1 \to \mathbb{R}$ and any $n \geq 1$:
$$\int f\, d\mu = \int f \circ \phi^n\, d\mu \quad \text{(by invariance, iterate
d } n \text{ times).}$$
Since $\phi^n([x]) \to p$ for every $[x] \in S^1$, we have $f(\phi^n([x])) \to f
(p)$ pointwise. By the Dominated Convergence Theorem ($|f \circ \phi^n| \leq \|f
\|_\infty$):
$$\int f \circ \phi^n\, d\mu \;\longrightarrow\; f(p).$$
Therefore $\int f\, d\mu = f(p)$ for all continuous $f$, which gives $\mu = \del
ta_p$. $\quad\checkmark$

### b) Two homeomorphisms with no common invariant measure

**Construction.** Let $\phi_1 = \phi$ from part (a), with unique fixed point $p
= [0]$ and the property that the only $\phi_1$-invariant probability measure is
$\delta_p$.

Let $\phi_2$ be the rotation $\phi_2([x]) = [x + \tfrac{1}{2}]$. Then $\phi_2(p)
 = [\tfrac{1}{2}] \neq [0] = p$.

**Proof that $\langle \phi_1, \phi_2 \rangle$ has no invariant probability measu
re.** Suppose for contradiction that $\mu$ is a Borel probability measure on $S^
1$ invariant under both $\phi_1$ and $\phi_2$.

- Since $\mu$ is $\phi_1$-invariant, part (a) gives $\mu = \delta_p$.
- Since $\mu$ is $\phi_2$-invariant, $(\phi_2)_*\mu = \mu$, i.e., $\delta_{\phi_
2(p)} = \delta_p$.
- This requires $\phi_2(p) = p$, but $\phi_2(p) = [\tfrac{1}{2}] \neq p$.

Contradiction. Therefore $\langle \phi_1, \phi_2 \rangle$ has no invariant proba
bility measure on $S^1$. $\quad\checkmark$

### c) The free group $F_2$ is not amenable

**Key fact.** A discrete group $G$ is **amenable** if and only if every continuo
us affine action of $G$ on a non-empty compact convex subset of a locally convex
 topological vector space has a fixed point (Day's fixed-point theorem). An equi
valent and directly useful consequence is:

> *If $G$ is amenable and acts by homeomorphisms on a compact Hausdorff space $X
$, then there exists a $G$-invariant Borel probability measure on $X$.*

This follows because the space $\mathcal{P}(X)$ of Borel probability measures on
 $X$ is compact and convex in the weak-* topology, and $G$ acts affinely on $\ma
thcal{P}(X)$ by pushforward $(g \cdot \mu = g_*\mu)$. A fixed point of this affi
ne action is precisely a $G$-invariant probability measure.

**Deduction.** Let $F_2 = \langle a, b \rangle$ be the free group on two generat
ors. By the universal property of $F_2$, the assignment
$$a \mapsto \phi_1, \qquad b \mapsto \phi_2$$
extends uniquely to a homomorphism $F_2 \to \mathrm{Homeo}(S^1)$, giving an acti
on of $F_2$ on $S^1$ by homeomorphisms.

Suppose for contradiction that $F_2$ is amenable. Then by the key fact above, th
is action on the compact space $S^1$ must admit an $F_2$-invariant Borel probabi
lity measure $\mu$. Such a measure is invariant under both $\phi_1$ and $\phi_2$
, hence invariant under $\langle \phi_1, \phi_2 \rangle$.

But part (b) proves that no such measure exists. This is a contradiction.

Therefore $F_2$ is **not amenable**. $\quad\checkmark$
