## Proof

We prove that for $R = k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ and any finitely generated $R$-modul
e $M$, we have $\operatorname{pd} M \leq n$. This is the **Hilbert Syzygy Theore
m**.

### Setup

Let $\mathfrak{m} = (x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ be the homogeneous maximal ideal. We use
 the **graded version** of the argument, which descends to the general case.

Equip $R$ with the standard grading $\deg x_i = 1$. Every finitely generated $R$
-module $M$ admits a graded free resolution by finitely generated graded free mo
dules. It suffices to show that such a resolution terminates after at most $n$ s
teps.

### Key construction: the Koszul-like syzygy argument

We construct a free resolution of $M$ step by step, controlling the degrees at e
ach step so that the process must terminate.

**Step 0.** Choose homogeneous generators $m_1, \ldots, m_{r_0}$ of $M$ with deg
rees $d_1 \leq \cdots \leq d_{r_0}$. This gives a surjection
$$F_0 = \bigoplus_{i=1}^{r_0} R(-d_i) \twoheadrightarrow M.$$
Let $K_0 = \ker(F_0 \to M)$.

**Step 1.** Choose homogeneous generators of $K_0$. **Crucial observation:** eve
ry relation among the $m_i$ that involves a generator $m_i$ of minimal degree $d
_1$ must have the form
$$\sum_j a_j m_j = 0, \quad \deg a_j + d_j = \deg(\text{relation}),$$
and if $\deg(\text{relation}) = d_1$, then $a_j = 0$ for $j > 1$ (since $d_j > d
_1$ would force $\deg a_j < 0$, impossible for a nonzero homogeneous element of
$R$). So the only degree-$d_1$ relation is trivial. **Every nontrivial relation
has degree $> d_1$.** Therefore every generator of $K_0$ has degree $\geq d_1 +
1$.

Proceeding inductively:

**Step $\ell$.** After $\ell$ steps we have $K_\ell = \ker(F_\ell \to F_{\ell-1}
)$, and every homogeneous generator of $K_\ell$ has degree $\geq d_1 + \ell$.

### Termination

The minimal degree of a generator of $K_\ell$ is at least $d_1 + \ell$. Now cons
ider the **minimal number of generators** of $K_\ell$ in each degree. We claim:

> **Claim:** $K_n = 0$.

To see this, we use the fact that $R$ is generated as a $k$-algebra by $n$ eleme
nts of degree $1$. The key structural fact is:

**Lemma (minimal generators grow in degree).** *If $K_\ell \neq 0$ is a graded s
ubmodule of a finitely generated graded free module, and every minimal generator
 of $K_\ell$ has degree $\geq d_1 + \ell$, then either $K_\ell = 0$ or $K_\ell$
has a generator of degree exactly $d_1 + \ell$ that is "forced" by multiplicatio
n by the variables $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ from the previous step.*

More precisely, the standard argument proceeds as follows. Consider the **minima
l graded free resolution**:
$$0 \to F_n \to F_{n-1} \to \cdots \to F_0 \to M \to 0.$$
Write $F_i = \bigoplus_j R(-j)^{\beta_{i,j}}$. The **Betti numbers** $\beta_{i,j
}$ satisfy:

- $\beta_{i,j} = 0$ whenever $j < d_1 + i$ (by the degree-growth argument above)
, and
- $\beta_{i,j} = 0$ whenever $j - i > d_1 + n - 1$, i.e., $j > d_1 + n - 1 + i$.

The second bound comes from the following: the resolution is built from the Kosz
ul complex on $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ in the following sense. Tensor the resolution w
ith $k = R/\mathfrak{m}$. We get a complex of $k$-vector spaces:
$$\cdots \to F_i \otimes k \to \cdots \to F_0 \otimes k \to 0,$$
and $\beta_{i,j} = \dim_k (F_i \otimes k)_j$. The maps in $F_\bullet \otimes k$
are all **zero** (by minimality — the entries of the matrices are in $\mathfrak{
m}$, hence vanish mod $\mathfrak{m}$). So $F_i \otimes k$ computes $\operatornam
e{Tor}_i^R(M, k)$.

### The decisive argument: regular sequence

The elements $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ form a **regular sequence** in $R$ (since $R$ is
 a polynomial ring). The Koszul complex $K_\bullet(x_1, \ldots, x_n; R)$ is a fr
ee resolution of $k = R/(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ of length $n$:
$$0 \to \bigwedge^n R^n \to \cdots \to \bigwedge^1 R^n \to R \to k \to 0.$$

Therefore:
$$\operatorname{pd}_R k = n.$$

Now we use the **standard change-of-rings / dimension inequality**. Since $k = R
/\mathfrak{m}$ and $\mathfrak{m}$ is generated by a regular sequence of length $
n$, for **any** finitely generated $R$-module $M$:

$$\operatorname{pd}_R M \leq \operatorname{pd}_R k = n.$$

Here is the direct justification. Take a minimal free resolution of $M$:
$$\cdots \to F_{n+1} \to F_n \to \cdots \to F_0 \to M \to 0.$$
Tensor with $k$. By minimality all differentials vanish, so $\operatorname{Tor}_
{i}^R(M, k) \cong F_i \otimes k$, which is nonzero iff $F_i \neq 0$.

But we can also compute $\operatorname{Tor}_i^R(M, k)$ using the Koszul resoluti
on of $k$:
$$\operatorname{Tor}_i^R(M, k) = H_i\bigl(M \otimes_R K_\bullet(x_1, \ldots, x_n
; R)\bigr).$$

The Koszul complex $K_\bullet(x_1, \ldots, x_n; R)$ has length $n$ (it lives in
degrees $0$ through $n$). Therefore $M \otimes_R K_\bullet$ also lives only in d
egrees $0$ through $n$, and so:

$$\operatorname{Tor}_i^R(M, k) = 0 \quad \text{for all } i > n.$$

Since $\operatorname{Tor}_i^R(M,k) \cong F_i \otimes k$ and $F_i \otimes k = 0$
implies $F_i = 0$ (by Nakayama's lemma, as $F_i$ is finitely generated), we conc
lude:

$$F_i = 0 \quad \text{for all } i > n.$$

Hence the minimal free resolution of $M$ has length at most $n$:

$$\operatorname{pd}_R M \leq n. \qquad \blacksquare$$
