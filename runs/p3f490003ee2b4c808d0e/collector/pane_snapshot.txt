The monomials in $\mathbb{C}[u,v]$ are $u^a v^b$ for $a, b \geq 0$. We can
partition them:
- $a \equiv b \pmod 2$: these are in $A$, hence in $A \cdot 1$.
- $a \not\equiv b \pmod 2$, $a \geq 1$: $u^a v^b = u^{a-1} v^b \cdot u$. Is
$u^{a-1} v^b \in A$? We need $a-1 \equiv b \pmod 2$, which is true since $a
\not\equiv b$. So yes, $u^a v^b \in A \cdot u$.
- $a \not\equiv b \pmod 2$, $a = 0$: $v^b$ with $b$ odd. $v^b = v^{b-1} \cdot
v$. Is $v^{b-1} \in A$? We need $0 \equiv b-1 \pmod 2$, i.e., $b$ is odd, which
is true. So $v^b \in A \cdot v$.

So every monomial is in $A \cdot 1 + A \cdot u + A \cdot v$, and the three sets
are disjoint (they correspond to different parity classes). So $B = A \cdot 1
\oplus A \cdot u \oplus A \cdot v$ is free of rank 3 over $A$. Hence $B$ is flat
over $A$ everywhere. Not helpful.
