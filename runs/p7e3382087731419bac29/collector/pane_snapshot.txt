# Proof: A Non-Archimedean Ordered Field Larger than ℝ

**Yes.** Such a field exists. We construct one explicitly: the field of formal L
aurent series **ℝ((t))** over ℝ, equipped with a lexicographic order that makes
the indeterminate *t* a positive infinitesimal.

---

## Construction

Let $K = \mathbb{R}((t))$ be the set of all formal Laurent series

$$f = \sum_{n=N}^{\infty} a_n\, t^n, \qquad a_n \in \mathbb{R},\quad N \in \math
bb{Z},$$

where only finitely many coefficients with $n < 0$ are nonzero (i.e., the series
 has a lowest-degree term). Addition is coefficient-wise; multiplication is the
Cauchy product, which is well-defined because the coefficient of $t^k$ in $fg$ r
eceives contributions from only finitely many pairs $(a_i, b_j)$ with $i+j=k$ an
d $i,j \geq$ their respective lower bounds. This makes $K$ a commutative field w
ith $0$ and $1$.

## Ordering

Define $f > 0$ if and only if the **leading coefficient** (the coefficient of th
e lowest-degree nonzero term) of $f$ is strictly positive. Define $f > g$ iff $f
 - g > 0$.

**Claim.** This is a total order compatible with the field structure, making $K$
 an ordered field.

*Total order.* For any nonzero $f$, the leading coefficient $a_N$ is either posi
tive or negative, so exactly one of $f > 0$, $f < 0$, $f = 0$ holds. Trichotomy
follows.

*Compatibility with addition.* If $f > 0$ and $g > 0$, write their leading terms
 as $a_N t^N$ ($a_N > 0$) and $b_M t^M$ ($b_M > 0$). If $N \neq M$, the lower-de
gree term dominates in $f+g$, and its coefficient is positive. If $N = M$, the l
eading coefficient of $f+g$ is $a_N + b_N > 0$. Either way $f + g > 0$.

*Compatibility with multiplication.* If $f > 0$ and $g > 0$ with leading coeffic
ients $a_N > 0$, $b_M > 0$, then $fg$ has leading term $a_N b_M\, t^{N+M}$ with
$a_N b_M > 0$, so $fg > 0$.

Hence $(K, +, \cdot, >)$ is an ordered field.

## K is larger than ℝ

The map $\varphi: \mathbb{R} \hookrightarrow K$ sending $r \mapsto r\, t^0$ (the
 series with the single constant term $r$) is an injective field homomorphism pr
eserving the order. Thus ℝ embeds as an ordered subfield of $K$.

Moreover, the element $t = 1 \cdot t^1 \in K$ is not in the image of $\varphi$,
so the containment is **proper**: $\mathbb{R} \subsetneq K$.

## K is non-Archimedean

The element $t$ is a **positive infinitesimal**: for every positive integer $n$,

$$0 < t < \frac{1}{n},$$

because $\frac{1}{n} - t = \frac{1}{n}\, t^0 - 1\cdot t^1$ has leading coefficie
nt $\frac{1}{n} > 0$, so $\frac{1}{n} - t > 0$, i.e., $t < \frac{1}{n}$; and $t
> 0$ by definition.

An ordered field is Archimedean if and only if it contains no positive infinites
imals (equivalently, for every $x > 0$ there exists $n \in \mathbb{N}$ with $n x
 > 1$). Since $t > 0$ yet $n \cdot t < 1$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, the field
$K$ is **non-Archimedean**.

## Conclusion

$K = \mathbb{R}((t))$ with the lexicographic order is an ordered field that (i)
properly contains ℝ and (ii) is non-Archimedean. Therefore, **yes, it is possibl
e to construct a non-Archimedean ordered field larger than ℝ.**
