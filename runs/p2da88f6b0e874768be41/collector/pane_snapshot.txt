## Solution

We solve the heat equation $\partial_t u = \Delta u$ on the rectangle $\mathbb{X
} = [0,X]\times[0,Y]$ with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions and initial
 data $u_0(x,y) = f(x,y)$.

### Step 1: Separation of variables

We look for solutions of the form $u(x,y,t) = \phi(x)\,\psi(y)\,T(t)$. Substitut
ing into $\partial_t u = \Delta u$ gives

$$\phi\,\psi\,T' = \phi''\,\psi\,T + \phi\,\psi''\,T,$$

which separates into

$$\frac{T'}{T} = \frac{\phi''}{\phi} + \frac{\psi''}{\psi}.$$

Since the left side depends only on $t$ and the right only on $(x,y)$, both side
s equal a constant $-\lambda$. Setting $\frac{\phi''}{\phi} = -\mu$ and $\frac{\
psi''}{\psi} = -\nu$ with $\mu + \nu = \lambda$, we obtain three ODEs:

$$\phi'' + \mu\,\phi = 0, \qquad \psi'' + \nu\,\psi = 0, \qquad T' + \lambda\,T
= 0.$$

### Step 2: Spatial eigenvalue problems with Dirichlet conditions

The homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions $u(0,y,t)=u(X,y,t)=0$ force $\phi(
0)=\phi(X)=0$, and $u(x,0,t)=u(x,Y,t)=0$ force $\psi(0)=\psi(Y)=0$.

For $\phi''+\mu\phi=0$ with $\phi(0)=\phi(X)=0$: nontrivial solutions exist only
 for $\mu_m = \left(\frac{m\pi}{X}\right)^2$, $m\in\mathbb{N}^+$, with eigenfunc
tions

$$\phi_m(x) = \sin\!\left(\frac{m\pi x}{X}\right).$$

For $\psi''+\nu\psi=0$ with $\psi(0)=\psi(Y)=0$: nontrivial solutions exist only
 for $\nu_n = \left(\frac{n\pi}{Y}\right)^2$, $n\in\mathbb{N}^+$, with eigenfunc
tions

$$\psi_n(y) = \sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{Y}\right).$$

### Step 3: Time equation

For each pair $(m,n)$, the eigenvalue is

$$\lambda_{m,n} = \mu_m + \nu_n = \left(\frac{m\pi}{X}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{n\
pi}{Y}\right)^2,$$

and $T' + \lambda_{m,n}\,T = 0$ gives

$$T_{m,n}(t) = e^{-\lambda_{m,n}\,t}.$$

### Step 4: General solution as a series

By superposition (and noting the family $\{\sin(m\pi x/X)\sin(n\pi y/Y)\}_{m,n\g
e 1}$ is a complete orthogonal system on $L^2(\mathbb{X})$), the solution is

$$u(x,y,t) = \sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} A_{m,n}\, e^{-\lambda_{m,n}\
,t}\,\sin\!\left(\frac{m\pi x}{X}\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{Y}\right),$$

where

$$\lambda_{m,n} = \pi^2\!\left(\frac{m^2}{X^2}+\frac{n^2}{Y^2}\right).$$

### Step 5: Determine coefficients from the initial condition

Setting $t=0$ and using $u(x,y,0)=f(x,y)$:

$$f(x,y) = \sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} A_{m,n}\,\sin\!\left(\frac{m\p
i x}{X}\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{Y}\right).$$

By orthogonality, multiplying by $\sin(m'\pi x/X)\sin(n'\pi y/Y)$ and integratin
g over $\mathbb{X}$, using

$$\int_0^X \sin\!\left(\frac{m\pi x}{X}\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{m'\pi x}{X}\righ
t)dx = \frac{X}{2}\,\delta_{m,m'}, \qquad \int_0^Y \sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{Y}\
right)\sin\!\left(\frac{n'\pi y}{Y}\right)dy = \frac{Y}{2}\,\delta_{n,n'},$$

we obtain the double sine coefficients:

$$\boxed{\,A_{m,n} = \frac{4}{XY}\int_0^X\!\int_0^Y f(x,y)\,\sin\!\left(\frac{m\
pi x}{X}\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{Y}\right)\,dy\,dx\,}.$$

### Step 6: Verification

- **PDE**: Each term $e^{-\lambda_{m,n}t}\sin(m\pi x/X)\sin(n\pi y/Y)$ satisfies
 $\partial_t = \Delta$ since $\partial_t$ produces $-\lambda_{m,n}$ and $\Delta$
 produces $-\mu_m - \nu_n = -\lambda_{m,n}$. Linearity preserves this.
- **Boundary conditions**: Each sine factor vanishes at $x=0,X$ and $y=0,Y$ resp
ectively, so every term — hence the sum — satisfies the homogeneous Dirichlet co
nditions.
- **Initial condition**: The double sine series is the Fourier expansion of $f$
on $\mathbb{X}$, so $u(x,y,0)=f(x,y)$ in $L^2$ (and pointwise under standard reg
ularity assumptions on $f$).

### Final Answer

The solution is

$$u(x,y,t) = \sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} A_{m,n}\,\exp\!\left[-\pi^2\
!\left(\frac{m^2}{X^2}+\frac{n^2}{Y^2}\right)t\right]\sin\!\left(\frac{m\pi x}{X
}\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{Y}\right),$$

with

$$A_{m,n} = \frac{4}{XY}\int_0^X\!\int_0^Y f(x,y)\,\sin\!\left(\frac{m\pi x}{X}\
right)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi y}{Y}\right)\,dy\,dx, \qquad m,n \in \mathbb{N}^+.$
$
