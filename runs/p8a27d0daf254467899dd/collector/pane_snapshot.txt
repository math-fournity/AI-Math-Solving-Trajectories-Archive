**Step 1: Classification of faithful 4-dimensional real representations of U(2).

Recall $U(2) \cong (SU(2) \times U(1))/\mathbb{Z}_2$, where $\mathbb{Z}_2 = \{(I
,1),(-I,-1)\}$. The complex irreducible representations of $U(2)$ are $W_{k,n} =
 V_k \otimes \chi_n$, where $V_k = \operatorname{Sym}^k(\mathbb{C}^2)$ (dimensio
n $k+1$, $k \geq 0$), $\chi_n = (\det)^n$ ($n \in \mathbb{Z}$), and $k \equiv n
\pmod{2}$ (so that $(-I,-1)$ acts trivially).

An embedding $\rho: U(2) \to SO(4)$ is a faithful 4-dimensional real representat
ion. We classify all such representations by analyzing the complexification $\rh
o_{\mathbb{C}}$ (dimension 4 over $\mathbb{C}$) according to real representation
 type:

- **Real type** ($\rho_{\mathbb{C}}$ irreducible, $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} = 4$): Requ
ires a self-conjugate 4-dim complex irrep $W_{3,n} \cong \overline{W}_{3,n} = W_
{3,-n}$, forcing $n=0$. But $k=3$ requires $n$ odd, contradiction. **None exist.
**

- **Complex type** ($\rho_{\mathbb{C}} = W \oplus \overline{W}$, $\dim_{\mathbb{
C}} W = 2$): The 2-dim complex irreps are $W_{1,n}$ ($n$ odd). The kernel of $W_
{1,n} \oplus W_{1,-n}$ is $\{(\lambda^{-n}I, \lambda) : \lambda^{2n}=1\}$. For $
|n|=1$, this is exactly $\mathbb{Z}_2$ (trivial in $U(2)$), giving a faithful re
presentation. For $|n|>1$, the kernel is larger. **Only $n = \pm 1$ works**, yie
lding the standard representation and its conjugate.

- **Quaternionic type** ($\rho_{\mathbb{C}} = W \oplus W$, $W \cong \overline{W}
$, $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} W = 2$): Requires $W_{1,n} \cong W_{1,-n}$, so $n=0$, but
$n$ must be odd. **None exist.**

- **Reducible**: Any decomposition into smaller pieces has a 1-dimensional summa
nd (necessarily trivial, since $U(2)$ is connected) or 2-dimensional summands (c
oming from characters $\chi_n$, whose kernels contain $SU(2)$). In all cases, th
e kernel is nontrivial. **None are faithful.**

Since the standard representation and its conjugate $\overline{A}$ are related b
y conjugation by the complex conjugation matrix $C = \operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1,
-1) \in SO(4)$, we conclude:

> **Every embedding $U(2) \hookrightarrow SO(4)$ is $SO(4)$-conjugate to the sta
ndard embedding.**

**Step 2: The space of conjugates of $U(2)$ in $SO(4)$.**

The space of subgroups of $SO(4)$ that are conjugate to the standard $U(2)$ is $
SO(4)/N_{SO(4)}(U(2))$, where $N_{SO(4)}(U(2))$ is the normalizer.

Since $U(2)$ is a maximal connected subgroup of $SO(4)$ (its Lie algebra $\mathf
rak{u}(2) = \mathfrak{su}(2) \oplus \mathfrak{u}(1)$ sits inside $\mathfrak{so}(
4) = \mathfrak{su}(2)_L \oplus \mathfrak{su}(2)_R$ as $\mathfrak{su}(2)_L \oplus
 \mathfrak{u}(1)_R$, and the only intermediate subalgebra is all of $\mathfrak{s
o}(4)$), we have $N_{SO(4)}(U(2))^\circ = U(2)$.

The quotient $N_{SO(4)}(U(2))/U(2)$ embeds into $\operatorname{Out}(U(2)) \cong
\mathbb{Z}_2$ (generated by complex conjugation $A \mapsto \overline{A}$). This
$\mathbb{Z}_2$ is realized by $C = \operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1,-1) \in SO(4)$, wh
ich conjugates $A$ to $\overline{A}$. Therefore:

$$N_{SO(4)}(U(2)) = U(2) \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_2.$$

**Step 3: Computing the parameter space.**

Using the double cover $\operatorname{Spin}(4) = SU(2)_L \times SU(2)_R \to SO(4
)$, the standard $U(2)$ lifts to $(SU(2)_L \times U(1)_R)/\mathbb{Z}_2$, so:

$$SO(4)/U(2) \cong SU(2)_R / U(1)_R \cong S^2.$$

This $S^2$ is the space of orthogonal complex structures on $\mathbb{R}^4$ compa
tible with the orientation (via $J \mapsto U(J) = \{A \in SO(4) : AJ = JA\}$).

The $\mathbb{Z}_2$ factor in the normalizer acts on $SO(4)/U(2) \cong S^2$ by se
nding a complex structure $J$ to $-J$ (since $C$ is complex conjugation, $CJC^{-
1} = -J$). This is the **antipodal map** on $S^2$.

Therefore, the space of conjugates of $U(2)$ in $SO(4)$ is:

$$SO(4)/N_{SO(4)}(U(2)) = (SO(4)/U(2))/\mathbb{Z}_2 = S^2/\mathbb{Z}_2 = \mathbb
{R}P^2.$$

**Step 4: Why the parametrization is $\mathbb{R}P^2$, not $S^2$.**

The space $S^2 = SO(4)/U(2)$ parametrizes orthogonal complex structures $J$ on $
\mathbb{R}^4$. Each $J$ determines a subgroup $U(J) \cong U(2) \subset SO(4)$. H
owever, $J$ and $-J$ yield the **same** subgroup:

$$U(-J) = \{A \in SO(4) : A(-J) = (-J)A\} = \{A : AJ = JA\} = U(J).$$

This is because the center of $U(J) \cong U(2)$ is $U(1)$, and the only complex
structures in this center are $\pm J$. Hence the map $S^2 \to \{\text{subgroups}
\}$ sending $J \mapsto U(J)$ is exactly **2-to-1**, with $J \sim -J$.

**Conclusion.**

The space of embeddings $U(2) \hookrightarrow SO(4)$ (viewed as subgroups, i.e.,
 images of embeddings) is $\mathbb{R}P^2$, **not** $S^2$. The sphere $S^2 = SO(4
)/U(2)$ that naturally arises is a double cover of the true parameter space, bec
ause the complex structures $J$ and $-J$ — which are antipodal points on $S^2$ —
 produce the same copy of $U(2)$ inside $SO(4)$.

Moreover, if one considers embeddings up to $SO(4)$-conjugacy as *homomorphisms*
 (not just images), the moduli space is even simpler: it is a **single point**,
since every faithful 4-dimensional real representation of $U(2)$ is equivalent t
o the standard one.

In neither interpretation is the parameter space $S^2$. **The answer is no: embe
ddings $U(2) \hookrightarrow SO(4)$ are not parametrized by $S^2$; they are para
metrized by $\mathbb{R}P^2$ (as subgroups) or by a point (up to conjugacy).**
