## Solution

**The answer is 12.**

### Lower Bound: 12 rows are sometimes necessary

Consider the configuration: **56 schools of 35 students** and **30 schools of 1
student**. Total: $56 \times 35 + 30 \times 1 = 1960 + 30 = 1990$. ✓

Each row holds at most $\lfloor 199/35 \rfloor = 5$ schools of size 35 (since $5
 \times 35 = 175 \leq 199$ but $6 \times 35 = 210 > 199$).

With 11 rows, we can place at most $11 \times 5 = 55$ schools of size 35, but we
 have 56. The 56th school of size 35 cannot fit in any row (every row already ha
s 5 such schools, leaving $\leq 24$ seats, and even if a row has fewer size-35 s
chools, placing 6 is impossible). Thus **11 rows do not suffice**, so at least *
*12 rows** are required.

### Upper Bound: 12 rows always suffice

We prove that **First-Fit Decreasing (FFD)** with 12 bins always packs all items
.

**Algorithm:** Sort schools in decreasing order of size. Place each school in th
e first row that has enough remaining capacity (among 12 rows of capacity 199).

**Proof by contradiction.** Suppose FFD fails, and let $s$ be the size of the fi
rst school that cannot be placed. At this moment:

1. All 12 rows have remaining capacity $< s$, i.e., load $\geq 200 - s$.
2. All already-placed schools have size $\geq s$ (decreasing order).
3. All unplaced schools (including the current one) have size $\leq s$.

From (1): total load $\geq 12(200 - s) = 2400 - 12s$.

Unplaced total $= 1990 - \text{load} \leq 1990 - (2400 - 12s) = 12s - 410$.

Since the current unplaced item has size $s$: $12s - 410 \geq s$, giving $11s \g
eq 410$, so $s \geq 38$.

Since $s \leq 39$, we have $s \in \{38, 39\}$.

**Case $s = 38$:** All placed items have size $\in \{38, 39\}$. Each row has loa
d $\geq 162$. Since $4 \times 39 = 156 < 162$, each row contains $\geq 5$ items.
 Since $6 \times 38 = 228 > 199$, each row contains $\leq 5$ items. So **each ro
w has exactly 5 items**, giving $\geq 60$ placed items, each of size $\geq 38$.
Total load $\geq 60 \times 38 = 2280 > 1990$. **Contradiction.**

**Case $s = 39$:** All placed items have size exactly 39. Each row has load $\ge
q 161$. Since $4 \times 39 = 156 < 161$, each row has $\geq 5$ items. Since $6 \
times 39 = 234 > 199$, each row has $\leq 5$ items. So **each row has exactly 5
items of size 39**, giving 60 placed items with total load $60 \times 39 = 2340
> 1990$. **Contradiction.**

Both cases are impossible, so FFD with 12 rows **never fails**. ∎

### Conclusion

- **12 rows are necessary** (exhibited configuration with 56 schools of 35 + 30
schools of 1).
- **12 rows are sufficient** (FFD always succeeds with 12 rows, proved by contra
diction).

The minimum number of rows required is $\boxed{12}$.
