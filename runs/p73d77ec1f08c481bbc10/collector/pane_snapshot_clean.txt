## Proof

**Key Characterization.** An element $\sigma \in S_n$ is a square (i.e., $\sigma
 = \tau^2$ for some $\tau \in S_n$) if and only if, for every **even** positive
integer $m$, the number of cycles of length $m$ in the disjoint cycle decomposit
ion of $\sigma$ is **even**.

**Proof of the characterization.**

*($\Rightarrow$)* Suppose $\sigma = \tau^2$. Consider a cycle of $\tau$ of lengt
h $\ell$:
- If $\ell$ is **odd**: since $\gcd(2, \ell) = 1$, squaring permutes the element
s within the cycle, producing a single cycle of length $\ell$ (odd) in $\sigma$.
- If $\ell = 2m$ is **even**: $\gcd(2, 2m) = 2$, so squaring splits this cycle i
nto **two** cycles of length $m$ in $\sigma$.

Thus, cycles of even length $m$ in $\sigma$ arise **only** from even cycles of l
ength $2m$ in $\tau$, and each such cycle of $\tau$ contributes exactly **two**
$m$-cycles. Therefore, for every even $m$, the number of $m$-cycles in $\sigma$
is even. (Cycles of odd length $m$ in $\sigma$ can come from either odd $m$-cycl
es or even $2m$-cycles of $\tau$, so their count is unrestricted.)

*($\Leftarrow$)* Suppose $\sigma$ satisfies the parity condition. We construct $
\tau$ with $\tau^2 = \sigma$:
- **Odd cycle** $(a_1\, a_2\, \ldots\, a_k)$, $k$ odd: Since 2 is invertible mod
 $k$, let $j$ satisfy $2j \equiv 1 \pmod{k}$. Define $\tau$ on $\{a_1, \ldots, a
_k\}$ as the cycle $(a_1\, a_{1+j}\, a_{1+2j}\, \ldots\, a_{1+(k-1)j})$ (indices
 mod $k$). Then $\tau^2$ sends $a_i \mapsto a_{i+2j} = a_{i+1}$, recovering the
original cycle.
- **Pair of even cycles** of the same length $m$: say $(a_1\, \ldots\, a_m)$ and
 $(b_1\, \ldots\, b_m)$. Define $\tau$ on their union as the $2m$-cycle $(a_1\,
b_1\, a_2\, b_2\, \ldots\, a_m\, b_m)$. Then $\tau^2 = (a_1\, a_2\, \ldots\, a_m
)(b_1\, b_2\, \ldots\, b_m)$.

Since the even-length cycles of $\sigma$ come in pairs (by hypothesis), this con
struction covers all cycles, yielding $\tau$ with $\tau^2 = \sigma$. $\square$

**Main Result.** The answer is **no** in general: for $n \geq 6$, not every elem
ent of $A_n$ is a square in $S_n$.

**Counterexample ($n \geq 6$).** Consider
$$\sigma = (1\;2\;3\;4)(5\;6) \in S_n$$
(with elements $7, \ldots, n$ fixed when $n > 6$).

- **$\sigma$ is even**, hence $\sigma \in A_n$: the sign is $(-1)^{4-1} \cdot (-
1)^{2-1} = (-1)(-1) = +1$.
- **$\sigma$ is not a square**: its cycle decomposition contains **one** 4-cycle
 (even length, count = 1, which is **odd**) and **one** 2-cycle (even length, co
unt = 1, which is **odd**). By the characterization above, $\sigma$ cannot be wr
itten as $\tau^2$ for any $\tau \in S_n$.

Concretely, to obtain a 4-cycle in $\tau^2$, one would need either an odd cycle
of length 4 in $\tau$ (impossible, since 4 is even) or an even cycle of length 8
 in $\tau$ (impossible, since $8 > n$ for $n \geq 6$ with only $n$ elements avai
lable—actually for $n \geq 8$ one could have an 8-cycle, but that produces *two*
 4-cycles, not one). In all cases, a single 4-cycle cannot appear in any square,
 confirming the obstruction.

**Remark (small $n$).** For completeness, every element of $A_n$ **is** a square
 for $n \leq 5$:
- $n \leq 2$: $A_n$ is trivial.
- $n = 3$: $A_3 = \{e, (1\,2\,3), (1\,3\,2)\}$, and $(1\,3\,2)^2 = (1\,2\,3)$, $
(1\,2\,3)^2 = (1\,3\,2)$.
- $n = 4$: Elements are the identity, 3-cycles (odd length, no even cycles), and
 double transpositions $(a\,b)(c\,d)$ (two 2-cycles — even count). All satisfy t
he characterization.
- $n = 5$: Elements are the identity, 3-cycles, 5-cycles, and double transpositi
ons. None contain an odd number of even-length cycles, so all are squares.

The threshold is $n = 6$, where the cycle type $(4, 2)$ first becomes available
inside $A_n$.

---

**Conclusion.** Not every element of $A_n$ is a square of an element of $S_n$. F
or all $n \geq 6$, the permutation $(1\;2\;3\;4)(5\;6) \in A_n$ provides an expl
icit counterexample: it is an even permutation, yet it cannot be expressed as $\
tau^2$ for any $\tau \in S_n$ because it contains a single 4-cycle (and a single
 2-cycle), violating the necessary condition that even-length cycles in a square
 must occur in pairs of equal length.
