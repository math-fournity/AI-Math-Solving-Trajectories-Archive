## Solution

We compute the extension degree via the tower

$$F_p(t) \;\subseteq\; F_{p^2}(t) \;\subseteq\; F_{p^2}(t)(t^{1/p}).$$

By the tower law,

$$[F_{p^2}(t)(t^{1/p}):F_p(t)] = [F_{p^2}(t):F_p(t)]\cdot [F_{p^2}(t)(t^{1/p}):F
_{p^2}(t)].$$

**First factor.** Since $t$ is transcendental over both $F_p$ and $F_{p^2}$, the
 rational function field extension satisfies

$$[F_{p^2}(t):F_p(t)] = [F_{p^2}:F_p] = 2,$$

using the given fact $[F_{p^2}:F_p]=2$. (Concretely, $\{1,t\}$-linearly, a basis
 of $F_{p^2}(t)$ over $F_p(t)$ is $\{1,\alpha\}$ where $\alpha\in F_{p^2}\setmin
us F_p$.)

**Second factor.** The element $t^{1/p}$ is a root of $X^p - t \in F_{p^2}(t)[X]
$. We claim this polynomial is irreducible over $F_{p^2}(t)$.

It suffices to show $t$ is **not** a $p$-th power in $F_{p^2}(t)$. Suppose for c
ontradiction that $t = (a/b)^p$ with $a,b\in F_{p^2}[t]$ coprime. Then $t\,b^p =
 a^p$. Comparing degrees,

$$p\cdot\deg a = 1 + p\cdot\deg b \implies p(\deg a - \deg b) = 1.$$

Since $p\ge 2$ is prime, the left side is a multiple of $p$ while the right side
 equals $1$ — a contradiction. Hence $t\notin F_{p^2}(t)^p$.

In characteristic $p$, the binomial $X^p - t$ is irreducible over a field $K$ if
f $t\notin K^p$ (it is purely inseparable: $X^p-t = (X-t^{1/p})^p$ in a splittin
g field, so the minimal polynomial of $t^{1/p}$ is $(X-t^{1/p})^m$ with $m\mid p
$; since $p$ is prime, $m\in\{1,p\}$, and $m=1$ would force $t^{1/p}\in K$, i.e.
 $t\in K^p$, which we just ruled out). Therefore the minimal polynomial of $t^{1
/p}$ over $F_{p^2}(t)$ is $X^p-t$, of degree $p$, giving

$$[F_{p^2}(t)(t^{1/p}):F_{p^2}(t)] = p.$$

**Combining.** Multiplying the two factors,

$$[F_{p^2}(t)(t^{1/p}):F_p(t)] = 2 \cdot p = 2p.$$
