## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** In fact, $S_2$ is compact regardless of any hypothesis on $S_1$
 or on the relationship between $k_1$ and $k_2$; the condition $\int_\Omega k_2(
x,x)\,d\mu(x)<\infty$ alone already forces $S_2$ to be Hilbert–Schmidt, hence co
mpact.

---

Let $i\in\{1,2\}$ and let $\{f_n^{(i)}\}_{n}$ be an orthonormal basis of $H_i$.

**Step 1 (Kernel diagonal as a sum of squares).** By the reproducing property, f
or every $x\in\Omega$,
$$
k_i(x,x) \;=\; \bigl\|k_i(\cdot,x)\bigr\|_{H_i}^{2} \;=\; \sum_{n}\bigl|f_n^{(i)
}(x)\bigr|^{2},
$$
where the series converges in $[0,\infty]$ (it is the squared norm of $k_i(\cdot
,x)=\sum_n \overline{f_n^{(i)}(x)}\,f_n^{(i)}$ in $H_i$).

**Step 2 (Hilbert–Schmidt norm of $S_i$).** Since $S_i:f\mapsto f$ is the inclus
ion,
$$
\|S_i\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}
\;=\;\sum_{n}\bigl\|S_i f_n^{(i)}\bigr\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}
\;=\;\sum_{n}\int_{\Omega}\bigl|f_n^{(i)}(x)\bigr|^{2}\,d\mu(x).
$$
Every summand is non-negative, so by Tonelli's theorem (monotone convergence) we
 may exchange the sum and the integral:
$$
\|S_i\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}
\;=\;\int_{\Omega}\sum_{n}\bigl|f_n^{(i)}(x)\bigr|^{2}\,d\mu(x)
\;=\;\int_{\Omega} k_i(x,x)\,d\mu(x)
\;<\;\infty,
$$
where the last inequality is exactly the hypothesis $\int_\Omega k_i(x,x)\,d\mu(
x)<\infty$.

**Step 3 (Conclusion).** Thus $S_i$ is a Hilbert–Schmidt operator from $H_i$ int
o $L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)$ for $i=1,2$. Every Hilbert–Schmidt operator is compact. In
 particular, **$S_2$ is compact**.

The additional hypotheses—that $k_1$ and $k_2$ represent the same element of $L^
{2}(\Omega\times\Omega,\mu\otimes\mu)$ (equivalently, the integral operators $S_
1S_1^{*}$ and $S_2S_2^{*}$ on $L^{2}(\mu)$ coincide) and that $S_1$ is compact—a
re not needed for the conclusion: the finiteness of $\int_\Omega k_2(x,x)\,d\mu(
x)$ alone already guarantees that $S_2$ is Hilbert–Schmidt, hence compact.
