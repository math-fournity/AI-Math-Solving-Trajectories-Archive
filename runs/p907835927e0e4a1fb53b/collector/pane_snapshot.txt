## Proof

Let $\mathbf{r}$ be the position vector in Euclidean space and let $q^1,\dots,q^
n$ be generalized coordinates. Define the **covariant basis vectors**

$$\mathbf{e}_i = \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial q^i},$$

and the **contravariant (reciprocal) basis** $\mathbf{e}^k$ by $\mathbf{e}^k \cd
ot \mathbf{e}_i = \delta^k_i$. The metric tensor is

$$g_{ij} = \mathbf{e}_i \cdot \mathbf{e}_j.$$

The **Christoffel symbol of the first kind** $[ij,k]$ is defined by expanding th
e derivative of a covariant basis vector in the contravariant basis:

$$\frac{\partial \mathbf{e}_i}{\partial q^j} = [ij,k]\,\mathbf{e}^k. \tag{1}$$

(The Christoffel symbol of the second kind is $\Gamma^k_{ij} = g^{kl}[ij,l]$, eq
uivalently $\partial_j \mathbf{e}_i = \Gamma^k_{ij}\mathbf{e}_k$.)

### Step 1. Differentiate the metric

Differentiating $g_{ij} = \mathbf{e}_i \cdot \mathbf{e}_j$ with respect to $q^k$
 and applying the product rule:

$$\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial q^k} = \frac{\partial \mathbf{e}_i}{\partial q
^k}\cdot \mathbf{e}_j + \mathbf{e}_i \cdot \frac{\partial \mathbf{e}_j}{\partial
 q^k}. \tag{2}$$

Substituting (1) into (2):

$$\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial q^k} = [ik,l]\,\mathbf{e}^l \cdot \mathbf{e}_j
 + \mathbf{e}_i \cdot [jk,l]\,\mathbf{e}^l = [ik,l]\,\delta^l_j + [jk,l]\,\delta
^l_i.$$

Hence

$$\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial q^k} = [ik,j] + [jk,i]. \tag{3}$$

### Step 2. Cyclically permute the indices

Applying (3) with the three cyclic permutations of $(i,j,k)$:

$$\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial q^k} = [ik,j] + [jk,i], \tag{3a}$$
$$\frac{\partial g_{ik}}{\partial q^j} = [ij,k] + [jk,i], \tag{3b}$$
$$\frac{\partial g_{jk}}{\partial q^i} = [ji,k] + [ik,j]. \tag{3c}$$

Since partial differentiation $\partial\mathbf{e}_i/\partial q^j = \partial\math
bf{e}_j/\partial q^i$ commutes (the basis comes from a position vector $\mathbf{
r}(q)$), the symbol is symmetric in its first two indices:

$$[ij,k] = [ji,k]. \tag{4}$$

Thus (3c) becomes

$$\frac{\partial g_{jk}}{\partial q^i} = [ij,k] + [ik,j]. \tag{3c'}$$

### Step 3. Combine to isolate $[ij,k]$

Form the combination $\text{(3b)} + \text{(3c')} - \text{(3a)}$:

$$\frac{\partial g_{ik}}{\partial q^j} + \frac{\partial g_{jk}}{\partial q^i} -
\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial q^k}
= \bigl([ij,k]+[jk,i]\bigr) + \bigl([ij,k]+[ik,j]\bigr) - \bigl([ik,j]+[jk,i]\bi
gr).$$

The terms $[jk,i]$ and $[ik,j]$ cancel, leaving

$$\frac{\partial g_{ik}}{\partial q^j} + \frac{\partial g_{jk}}{\partial q^i} -
\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial q^k} = 2\,[ij,k].$$

### Conclusion

Solving for $[ij,k]$:

$$\boxed{\;[ij,k] = \frac{1}{2}\!\left(\frac{\partial g_{ik}}{\partial q^j} + \f
rac{\partial g_{jk}}{\partial q^i} - \frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial q^k}\right)
.\;}$$

This verifies the desired expression. The derivation used only: (i) the definiti
on $g_{ij}=\mathbf{e}_i\cdot\mathbf{e}_j$, (ii) the defining expansion $\partial
_j\mathbf{e}_i = [ij,k]\mathbf{e}^k$, (iii) the product rule, (iv) the reciproci
ty $\mathbf{e}^l\cdot\mathbf{e}_j=\delta^l_j$, and (v) symmetry of $[ij,k]$ in $
i,j$ (which follows from equality of mixed partials of $\mathbf{r}$). $\blacksqu
