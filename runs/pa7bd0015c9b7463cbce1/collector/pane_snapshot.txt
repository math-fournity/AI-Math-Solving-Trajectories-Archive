## Proof

Let $v_n := f(x^n) + n\,g(x^n) = \inf_{x \in X}\bigl[f(x) + n\,g(x)\bigr]$ denot
e the optimal value at stage $n$.

**Step 1: Monotonicity of $(v_n)$.** Since $g \geq 0$, for every $x \in X$ and e
very $n$,
$$f(x) + (n+1)\,g(x) = f(x) + n\,g(x) + g(x) \geq f(x) + n\,g(x).$$
Taking the infimum over $x \in X$ on both sides gives $v_{n+1} \geq v_n$. Thus $
(v_n)$ is non-decreasing.

**Step 2: Boundedness.** The hypothesis $\lim_{n\to\infty} v_n < \infty$ togethe
r with monotonicity means there exists a finite constant $L$ with $v_n \leq L$ f
or all $n$.

**Step 3: Squeezing $g(x^n)$.** Since $f \geq 0$,
$$n\,g(x^n) \;\leq\; f(x^n) + n\,g(x^n) \;=\; v_n \;\leq\; L.$$
Dividing by $n > 0$:
$$0 \;\leq\; g(x^n) \;\leq\; \frac{L}{n}.$$

**Step 4: Conclusion.** As $n \to \infty$, $L/n \to 0$, so by the squeeze theore
m
$$\lim_{n \to \infty} g(x^n) = 0.$$

**Answer: Yes.** The finiteness of the limit of the optimal values forces $g(x^n
) \to 0$.
