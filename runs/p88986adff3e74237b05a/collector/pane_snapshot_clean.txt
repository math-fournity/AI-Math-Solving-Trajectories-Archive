## Proof

Let $K$ be a number field with ring of integers $\mathscr{O}_K$, and let $\mathf
rak{a}$ be a fractional ideal of $\mathscr{O}_K$. We construct an invertible she
af $\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}$ on $X = \operatorname{Spec}(\mathscr{O}_K)$ and ve
rify it is invertible (locally free of rank $1$).

---

### Step 1. $\mathfrak{a}$ is a finitely generated $\mathscr{O}_K$-module

By definition there exists $0 \neq a \in \mathscr{O}_K$ with $a\mathfrak{a} \sub
set \mathscr{O}_K$, i.e. $\mathfrak{a} \subset a^{-1}\mathscr{O}_K$. Since $\mat
hscr{O}_K$ is a Dedekind domain it is Noetherian, so $a^{-1}\mathscr{O}_K$ is a
finitely generated $\mathscr{O}_K$-module (generated by $1$ and the finitely man
y generators of the ideal $(a)^{-1}$, or more directly: $a^{-1}\mathscr{O}_K \co
ng \mathscr{O}_K$ as a module scaled by $a^{-1}$, so it is generated by $a^{-1}$
 over $\mathscr{O}_K$). Being a submodule of a finitely generated module over a
Noetherian ring, $\mathfrak{a}$ is itself finitely generated over $\mathscr{O}_K
$.

Hence the standard tilde construction applies: we define a sheaf of $\mathscr{O}
_X$-modules

$$\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}\bigl(D(f)\bigr) \;=\; \mathfrak{a}_f \;=\; \mathfrak{
a}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_K}\mathscr{O}_K\!\left[\tfrac{1}{f}\right] \qquad (f\in\m
athscr{O}_K,\; f\neq 0),$$

on the distinguished opens $D(f)$, which form a basis of the topology of $X$. Th
is extends uniquely to a sheaf on all of $X$; it is coherent because $\mathfrak{
a}$ is finitely generated.

---

### Step 2. $\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}$ is locally free of rank $1$

The ring $\mathscr{O}_K$ is a Dedekind domain, so every nonzero prime ideal $\ma
thfrak{p}$ is maximal and the local ring $\mathscr{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}$ is a dis
crete valuation ring. Localizing $\mathfrak{a}$ at $\mathfrak{p}$ gives the frac
tional ideal

$$\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{p}} \;=\; \mathfrak{a}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_K}\mathscr{
O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}} \;\subset\; K.$$

Since $\mathscr{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}$ is a DVR (hence a PID), every nonzero fract
ional ideal of $\mathscr{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}$ is principal. Therefore there exis
ts $t_{\mathfrak{p}}\in K^{\times}$ with

$$\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{p}} \;=\; t_{\mathfrak{p}}\,\mathscr{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p
}} \;\cong\; \mathscr{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}} \quad\text{as an }\mathscr{O}_{K,\math
frak{p}}\text{-module}.$$

The stalk of $\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}$ at $\mathfrak{p}$ is exactly $\mathfrak{
a}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}$, which is free of rank $1$ o
ver the local ring $\mathscr{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}$. Since this holds at every poi
nt, $\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}$ is locally free of rank $1$.

---

### Step 3. $\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}$ is invertible

A sheaf of $\mathscr{O}_X$-modules is **invertible** precisely when it is locall
y free of rank $1$. Equivalently, there exists a sheaf $\mathscr{L}^{-1}$ with $
\mathscr{L}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_X}\mathscr{L}^{-1}\cong\mathscr{O}_X$. We exhibi
t the inverse explicitly.

Define the **inverse fractional ideal**

$$\mathfrak{a}^{-1} \;=\; \{\,x\in K : x\,\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathscr{O}_K\,\}.$
$

This is again a fractional ideal of $\mathscr{O}_K$. Because $\mathscr{O}_K$ is
a Dedekind domain, every nonzero fractional ideal is invertible at the ideal lev
el:

$$\mathfrak{a}\cdot\mathfrak{a}^{-1} \;=\; \mathscr{O}_K.$$

(One way to see this: localizing at each $\mathfrak{p}$, the DVR $\mathscr{O}_{K
,\mathfrak{p}}$ makes $\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{p}}=t_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathscr{O}_{
K,\mathfrak{p}}$ with inverse $\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}=t_{\mathfrak{p}}
^{-1}\mathscr{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}$, so $(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{-1})_{\mathfr
ak{p}}=\mathscr{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}$ for all $\mathfrak{p}$; equality of ideals
is local, hence $\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{-1}=\mathscr{O}_K$.)

Passing to sheaves,

$$\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}\;\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_X}\;\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}^{-1
}} \;=\; \widetilde{\mathfrak{a}\cdot\mathfrak{a}^{-1}} \;=\; \widetilde{\mathsc
r{O}_K} \;=\; \mathscr{O}_X,$$

confirming that $\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}$ is invertible with inverse $\widetild
e{\mathfrak{a}^{-1}}$.

---

### Summary of the construction

Given a fractional ideal $\mathfrak{a}\subset K$:

1. **Sheafify.** Since $\mathfrak{a}$ is a finitely generated $\mathscr{O}_K$-mo
dule (Step 1), the tilde construction produces a coherent sheaf $\widetilde{\mat
hfrak{a}}$ on $\operatorname{Spec}(\mathscr{O}_K)$ with $\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}
}(D(f))=\mathfrak{a}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_K}\mathscr{O}_K[1/f]$.

2. **Local triviality.** Because $\mathscr{O}_K$ is a Dedekind domain, each loca
lization $\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is principal over the DVR $\mathscr{O}_{K
,\mathfrak{p}}$, so every stalk is isomorphic to $\mathscr{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}$;
 thus $\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}$ is locally free of rank $1$ (Step 2).

3. **Invertibility.** The inverse fractional ideal $\mathfrak{a}^{-1}=\{x\in K:x
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathscr{O}_K\}$ satisfies $\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{-1}=\ma
thscr{O}_K$, giving $\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}\otimes\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}^{-1}
}\cong\mathscr{O}_X$ (Step 3).

This is the desired invertible sheaf. In fact this construction sets up an equiv
alence between the group of invertible fractional ideals of $\mathscr{O}_K$ (mod
ulo principal fractional ideals) and the Picard group $\operatorname{Pic}(\opera
torname{Spec}(\mathscr{O}_K))$, which is the ideal class group $\mathrm{Cl}(K)$
of the number field.
