## Proof

We need to count the number of integers $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, 2013\}$ such that
 $k^k \equiv 1 \pmod{10}$.

The last digit of $k^k$ depends only on the last digit of $k$ (i.e., $k \bmod 10
$) and on $k$ modulo the cycle length of that digit's powers. Let $d = k \bmod 1
0$. We examine each possible $d$:

| $d$ | Cycle of $d^n \bmod 10$ | Period | Condition for $d^k \equiv 1 \pmod{10}
$ |
|-----|------------------------|--------|---------------------------------------
-|
| 0 | 0 | 1 | never (always 0) |
| 1 | 1 | 1 | always |
| 2 | 2,4,8,6 | 4 | never (no 1 in cycle) |
| 3 | 3,9,7,1 | 4 | $k \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$ |
| 4 | 4,6 | 2 | never |
| 5 | 5 | 1 | never |
| 6 | 6 | 1 | never |
| 7 | 7,9,3,1 | 4 | $k \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$ |
| 8 | 8,4,2,6 | 4 | never |
| 9 | 9,1 | 2 | $k \equiv 0 \pmod{2}$ |

So the contributing cases are:

**Case 1: $d = 1$, i.e., $k \equiv 1 \pmod{10}$.** No further condition. The val
ues are $1, 11, 21, \ldots, 2011$. The count is
$$\left\lfloor \frac{2013 - 1}{10}\right\rfloor + 1 = 201 + 1 = 202.$$

**Case 2: $d = 3$, i.e., $k \equiv 3 \pmod{10}$ and $k \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$.** But
 $k \equiv 3 \pmod{10}$ forces $k$ to be **odd**, while $k \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$ fo
rces $k$ to be **even**. Contradiction — **0 solutions**.

**Case 3: $d = 7$, i.e., $k \equiv 7 \pmod{10}$ and $k \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$.** Aga
in $k \equiv 7 \pmod{10}$ forces $k$ odd, while $k \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$ forces $k$
 even. Contradiction — **0 solutions**.

**Case 4: $d = 9$, i.e., $k \equiv 9 \pmod{10}$ and $k \equiv 0 \pmod{2}$.** Her
e $k \equiv 9 \pmod{10}$ forces $k$ odd, while $k \equiv 0 \pmod{2}$ forces $k$
even. Contradiction — **0 solutions**.

**Total:** $202 + 0 + 0 + 0 = \boxed{202}$.
