## Computation of $H_n(M-\{p\};\mathbb{Z})$

onsider the long exact sequence of the pair $(M, M-\{p\})$:

$$\cdots \to H_{n+1}(M,M-\{p\}) \to H_n(M-\{p\}) \xrightarrow{i_*} H_n(M) \xrigh
tarrow{j_*} H_n(M,M-\{p\}) \xrightarrow{\partial} H_{n-1}(M-\{p\}) \to H_{n-1}(M
) \to \cdots$$

**Step 1 — Identify the relative groups via excision.** Choose a coordinate ball
 $U\cong \mathbb{R}^n$ around $p$ with $U\subseteq M$ open. By excision of $M-\{
p\}$ from the complement of $U$,

$$H_k(M, M-\{p\}) \cong H_k(U, U-\{p\}).$$

Since $U\cong\mathbb{R}^n$ and $U-\{p\}\cong\mathbb{R}^n-\{0\}\simeq S^{n-1}$, t
he long exact sequence of the pair $(U,U-\{p\})$ together with $H_k(U)=0$ for $k
>0$ gives

$$H_k(U, U-\{p\}) \cong \widetilde{H}_{k-1}(S^{n-1}) \cong \begin{cases} \mathbb
{Z}, & k=n,\\ 0, & k\neq n.\end{cases}$$

Hence

$$H_k(M, M-\{p\};\mathbb{Z}) \cong \begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}, & k=n,\\ 0, & k\neq
n.\end{cases}$$

In particular $H_{n+1}(M,M-\{p\})=0$.

**Step 2 — Identify the absolute group.** Since $M$ is closed, oriented, and con
nected, $H_n(M;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}$, generated by the fundamental class $
[M]$.

n(M,M-\{p\})\cong H_n(U,U-\{p\})\cong\mathbb{Z}$, the map $j_*:H_n(M)\to H_n(M,M

ng\mathbb{Z}$ at every point $p$. (Equivalently: the orientation of $M$ is a con
sistent choice of local generators, and $[M]$ is the unique global class whose l
ocal restriction at each $p$ is the chosen local generator.) Therefore $j_*$ sen
ds the generator $[M]$ to a generator, so

$$j_*: H_n(M)\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} H_n(M,M-\{p\})$$

is an isomorphism.

**Step 4 — Conclude via exactness.** From the segment

$$0 = H_{n+1}(M,M-\{p\}) \to H_n(M-\{p\}) \xrightarrow{i_*} H_n(M) \xrightarrow{
j_*}_{\sim} H_n(M,M-\{p\}),$$

exactness gives $\ker j_* = \operatorname{im} i_*$, and since $j_*$ is an isomor
phism, $\ker j_*=0$. Also $i_*$ is injective (its kernel equals $\operatorname{i
m} H_{n+1}(M,M-\{p\})=0$). Thus $i_*$ is an injection with image $\ker j_*=0$, f

$$H_n(M-\{p\};\mathbb{Z}) = 0.$$

**Consistency check.** For $n\geq 2$, $M-\{p\}$ is a connected noncompact $n$-ma
nifold, and a noncompact $n$-manifold has trivial top homology — consistent with
