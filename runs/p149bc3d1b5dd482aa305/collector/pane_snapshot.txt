## Proof

We derive the leading asymptotics of the modified Bessel function $I_\nu(x)$ as
$x\to+\\infty$ (with $\nu$ fixed) via Laplace's method applied to the Schläfli i
ntegral representation.

### Step 1. Integral representation

For $x>0$ and $\nu\in\mathbb{R}$, the modified Bessel function of the first kind
 admits the Schläfli representation

$$
I_\nu(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{x\cos\theta}\cos(\nu\theta)\,d\theta\;-\;
\frac{\sin(\nu\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x\cosh t-\nu t}\,dt.
\tag{1}
$$

### Step 2. The second integral is exponentially smaller

In the second integral, $\cosh t\ge 1$ for all $t\ge 0$, with equality only at $
t=0$. Hence

$$
\left|\frac{\sin(\nu\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x\cosh t-\nu t}\,dt\right|
\le \frac{|\sin(\nu\pi)|}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x\cosh t}\,dt
\le \frac{|\sin(\nu\pi)|}{\pi}\,e^{-x}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(\cosh t-1)x}\,dt.
$$

Since $\cosh t -1 \ge t^2/2$ near $0$ and grows exponentially for large $t$, the
 last integral is $O(x^{-1/2})$, so the whole second term is $O(e^{-x}/\sqrt{x})
$. Meanwhile the first integral will turn out to be of order $e^{x}/\sqrt{x}$, w
hich is larger by a factor of $e^{2x}$. Thus the second term is **exponentially
negligible**, and

$$
I_\nu(x)\sim \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{x\cos\theta}\cos(\nu\theta)\,d\theta,
\qquad x\to+\infty.
\tag{2}
$$

### Step 3. Laplace's method on the first integral

Write

$$
J(x):=\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{x\cos\theta}\cos(\nu\theta)\,d\theta
=\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{x\,h(\theta)}\,g(\theta)\,d\theta,
$$

with $h(\theta)=\cos\theta$ and $g(\theta)=\cos(\nu\theta)$. The phase $h$ attai
ns its **unique global maximum** on $[0,\pi]$ at the endpoint $\theta=0$, where

$$
h(0)=1,\qquad h'(0)=0,\qquad h''(0)=-1<0.
$$

So the maximum is quadratic (non-degenerate) and occurs at the boundary. Laplace
's method at a non-degenerate boundary maximum gives

$$
J(x)\sim e^{x h(0)}\,g(0)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\frac{x}{2}h''(0)\theta^{2}}\,d\the
ta
=e^{x}\cdot 1\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x\theta^{2}/2}\,d\theta.
$$

(We used $g(0)=\cos 0=1$, and extended the upper limit to $\infty$ since the Gau
ssian decays on the scale $\theta\sim x^{-1/2}\to 0$.) Evaluating the Gaussian,

$$
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x\theta^{2}/2}\,d\theta
=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{x}}
=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2x}}.
$$

Therefore

$$
J(x)\sim e^{x}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2x}},\qquad x\to+\infty.
\tag{3}
$$

### Step 4. Assemble the result

Combining (1), (2), and (3),

$$
I_\nu(x)\sim \frac{1}{\pi}\,e^{x}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2x}}
=\frac{e^{x}}{\sqrt{2\pi x}},\qquad x\to+\infty.
$$

### Step 5. Verification (sanity checks)

- **Dependence on $\nu$**: At leading order the answer is independent of $\nu$,
consistent with the fact that $\nu$ enters only through the slowly-varying ampli
tude $\cos(\nu\theta)$ evaluated at the saddle $\theta=0$, where $\cos(\nu\cdot
0)=1$ for every $\nu$. The $\nu$-dependence first appears in the next-order corr
ection $\bigl(1-\frac{4\nu^{2}-1}{8x}+\cdots\bigr)$.
- **Consistency with the differential equation**: $I_\nu$ satisfies $x^{2}y''+xy
'-(x^{2}+\nu^{2})y=0$. Substituting the ansatz $y\sim C\,e^{x}x^{-1/2}$ gives, a
t leading order in $1/x$, $x^{2}(1)+x(1)-x^{2}=0$, which is satisfied; the $\nu^
{2}$ term is balanced by the next correction, confirming the leading factor $e^{
x}/\sqrt{x}$.
- **Known special case $\nu=0$**: $I_0(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi e^{x\cos\theta
}\,d\theta$ (no second term since $\sin 0=0$), and the same Laplace computation
yields $e^{x}/\sqrt{2\pi x}$, matching tabulated asymptotics.

d \nu\text{ fixed.}\,}
$$
