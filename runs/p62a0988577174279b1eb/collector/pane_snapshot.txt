The answer is **no**: the Hausdorff dimension of $\bigcap_k E_k$ need not equal
$1$; it can be strictly smaller (even $0$). Below is a counterexample that respe
cts the "residual" (comeager) interpretation.

---

## Counterexample

Let $L \subset [0,1]$ be the set of **Liouville numbers** in $[0,1]$. We use two
 classical facts about $L$:

**Fact 1.** $L$ is a dense $G_\delta$ subset of $[0,1]$, hence **comeager** (res
idual) in $[0,1]$.

*Proof sketch.* By definition,
$$L = \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} U_n, \qquad U_n := \bigcup_{q=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{p
=0}^{q}\left(\tfrac{p}{q}-\tfrac{1}{q^n},\;\tfrac{p}{q}+\tfrac{1}{q^n}\right).$$
Each $U_n$ is open, and $U_n$ is dense because it contains a neighborhood of eve
ry rational. So $L$ is a dense $G_\delta$, hence comeager. $\square$

**Fact 2.** $\dim_H L = 0$.

*Proof.* Fix any $s > 0$ and choose $n$ with $ns > 2$. For any $\delta > 0$, set
 $Q = \lceil(2/\delta)^{1/n}\rceil$. Since $L \subset U_n$, we cover $L$ by the
intervals $I_{p,q} = (p/q - 1/q^n,\; p/q + 1/q^n)$ with $q \ge Q$ (these have di
ameter $2/q^n \le \delta$). The $s$-dimensional Hausdorff content satisfies
$$\mathcal{H}^s_\delta(L) \;\le\; \sum_{q=Q}^{\infty}(q+1)\left(\frac{2}{q^n}\ri
ght)^s \;\le\; C\sum_{q=Q}^{\infty} q^{1-ns}.$$
Since $ns > 2$, the series $\sum q^{1-ns}$ converges, and the tail $\sum_{q \ge
Q} q^{1-ns} \to 0$ as $Q \to \infty$ (equivalently $\delta \to 0$). Thus $\mathc
al{H}^s(L) = 0$ for every $s > 0$, giving $\dim_H L = 0$. $\square$

### Construction of $(E_k)$

Write $L = \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} U_n$ as above, with each $U_n$ open and dense
in $[0,1]$. Define
$$E_k := \bigcap_{n=1}^{k} U_n, \qquad k = 1, 2, 3, \dots$$

We verify all required properties:

1. **$E_k \subset [0,1]$ and $E_{k+1} \subset E_k$:** Clear, since $E_{k+1} = E_
k \cap U_{k+1} \subset E_k$.

2. **Each $E_k$ is residual (comeager) in $[0,1]$:** $E_k$ is a *finite* interse
ction of open dense sets, hence open and dense in $[0,1]$, hence comeager.

3. **$\dim_H E_k = 1$ for all $k$:** Since $E_k$ is open and dense in $[0,1]$, i
t is nonempty and open, so it contains a nonempty open interval. Any nonempty op
en interval has Hausdorff dimension $1$, and $\dim_H E_k \le 1$ since $E_k \subs
et [0,1]$. Hence $\dim_H E_k = 1$.

4. **$\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty} E_k = L$, so $\dim_H\!\left(\bigcap_k E_k\right) =
0 \neq 1$:**
$$\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty} E_k = \bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigcap_{n=1}^{k} U_n = \bi
gcap_{n=1}^{\infty} U_n = L,$$
and by Fact 2, $\dim_H L = 0$.

### Conclusion

We have produced a decreasing sequence of residual (comeager) subsets $E_k \subs
et [0,1]$, each of Hausdorff dimension $1$, whose intersection is the set of Lio
uville numbers $L$ with $\dim_H L = 0$. Therefore the Hausdorff dimension of $\b
igcap_k E_k$ is **not** necessarily $1$; it can be $0$.

(Even without the comeager requirement, the simpler example $E_k = [0,\,1/k]$ —
each a closed interval of dimension $1$, decreasing, with intersection $\{0\}$ o
f dimension $0$ — already disproves the claim. The Liouville construction above
shows the failure persists even under the stronger residual/comeager constraint.
