## Proof

Recall **Steinhaus' theorem** (a standard consequence of the Lebesgue density th
eorem): if $A, B \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ are Lebesgue measurable with $\lambda(A)
> 0$ and $\lambda(B) > 0$, then the difference set
$$A - B = \{a - b : a \in A,\; b \in B\}$$
contains an open neighborhood of $0$. In particular, $A - B$ contains a nontrivi
al open interval, hence contains rational numbers.

---

Suppose, for contradiction, that $E$ contains a measurable rectangle $A_1 \times
 A_2$ with $\lambda_2(A_1 \times A_2) > 0$.

Since $\lambda_2(A_1 \times A_2) = \lambda(A_1)\,\lambda(A_2)$, we have $\lambda
(A_1) > 0$ and $\lambda(A_2) > 0$.

The containment $A_1 \times A_2 \subseteq E$ means: for every $x_1 \in A_1$ and
every $x_2 \in A_2$,
$$x_1 - x_2 \notin \mathbb{Q}.$$
Equivalently,
$$A_1 - A_2 \subseteq \mathbb{R} \setminus \mathbb{Q}.$$
That is, the difference set $A_1 - A_2$ contains **no** rational number.

But by Steinhaus' theorem, since $A_1$ and $A_2$ are measurable sets of positive
 measure, $A_1 - A_2$ contains an open interval around $0$, and every nonempty o
pen interval contains rational numbers. This contradicts $A_1 - A_2 \subseteq \m
athbb{R} \setminus \mathbb{Q}$.

Therefore no such measurable rectangle exists. $\blacksquare$
