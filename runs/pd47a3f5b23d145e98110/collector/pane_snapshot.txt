Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether two non-isomorphic represen
tations of $S_n$ can have the same restriction to $S_{n-1}$.

**Answer: Yes, such representations exist for every $n \geq 2$.**

## Proof

**Setup.** The irreducible complex representations of $S_n$ are the Specht modul
es $S^\lambda$, indexed by partitions $\lambda \vdash n$. The number of such irr
educibles is $p(n)$, the partition function. By the Young branching rule:

$$\operatorname{Res}^{S_n}_{S_{n-1}} S^\lambda \;\cong\; \bigoplus_{\mu \lessdot
 \lambda} S^\mu,$$

where $\mu \lessdot \lambda$ means $\mu$ is obtained from $\lambda$ by removing
one box from the Young diagram.

**Key counting argument.** For $n \geq 2$, we have $p(n) > p(n-1)$. This holds b
ecause the map $\mu \mapsto \mu \cup (1)$ (appending a part of size 1) injects p
artitions of $n-1$ into partitions of $n$, and the partition $(n)$ is not in the
 image (its smallest part is $n \geq 2$).

**Linear dependence of restricted characters.** The restriction map sends each o
f the $p(n)$ irreducible characters $\chi^\lambda$ of $S_n$ to a character of $S
_{n-1}$, which is a $\mathbb{Z}$-linear combination of the $p(n-1)$ irreducible
characters of $S_{n-1}$. Since $p(n) > p(n-1)$, these $p(n)$ restricted characte
rs live in a $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space of dimension $p(n-1) < p(n)$, so they are
 **linearly dependent over $\mathbb{Q}$** (and hence over $\mathbb{Z}$, since th
e coefficients are integers).

Therefore, there exist integers $c_\lambda$ (not all zero) with

$$\sum_{\lambda \vdash n} c_\lambda \, \chi^\lambda\Big|_{S_{n-1}} = 0.$$

**Both signs appear.** Not all $c_\lambda$ can be non-negative (or non-positive)
: evaluating at the identity gives $\sum c_\lambda \dim S^\lambda = 0$, and if a
ll $c_\lambda \geq 0$ with some $c_\lambda > 0$, this sum would be strictly posi
tive. So both sets

$$P = \{\lambda : c_\lambda > 0\}, \qquad N = \{\lambda : c_\lambda < 0\}$$

are nonempty and disjoint.

**Constructing $U$ and $V$.** Define:

$$U = \bigoplus_{\lambda \in P} c_\lambda \, S^\lambda, \qquad V = \bigoplus_{\l
ambda \in N} (-c_\lambda) \, S^\lambda.$$

These are genuine representations (non-negative integer multiplicities). Their r
estrictions satisfy:

$$\chi_{\operatorname{Res}(U)} - \chi_{\operatorname{Res}(V)} = \sum_{\lambda} c
_\lambda \, \chi^\lambda\Big|_{S_{n-1}} = 0,$$

so $\operatorname{Res}^{S_n}_{S_{n-1}} U \cong \operatorname{Res}^{S_n}_{S_{n-1}
} V$.

Since $P \cap N = \emptyset$ and both are nonempty, $U$ and $V$ share no irreduc
ible constituents, so $U \not\cong V$.

**Explicit example ($n = 3$).** $S_3$ has irreducibles $S^{(3)}$ (trivial), $S^{
(1,1,1)}$ (sign), and $S^{(2,1)}$ (standard, 2-dimensional). The restrictions to
 $S_2$ are:

- $\operatorname{Res} S^{(3)} = S^{(2)}$ (trivial of $S_2$),
- $\operatorname{Res} S^{(1,1,1)} = S^{(1,1)}$ (sign of $S_2$),
- $\operatorname{Res} S^{(2,1)} = S^{(2)} \oplus S^{(1,1)}$.

Setting $U = S^{(3)} \oplus S^{(1,1,1)}$ and $V = S^{(2,1)}$, we get $\operatorn
ame{Res}(U) \cong S^{(2)} \oplus S^{(1,1)} \cong \operatorname{Res}(V)$, yet $U
\not\cong V$ (different irreducible decompositions).

**Explicit example ($n = 2$).** $S_2$ has the trivial and sign representations,
both of which restrict to the unique (trivial) representation of $S_1$. So $U =
\text{trivial}$, $V = \text{sign}$ gives $\operatorname{Res}(U) \cong \operatorn
ame{Res}(V)$ but $U \not\cong V$.

**Conclusion.** For every $n \geq 2$, there exist non-isomorphic representations
 $U, V$ of $S_n$ with $\operatorname{Res}^{S_n}_{S_{n-1}} U \cong \operatorname{
Res}^{S_n}_{S_{n-1}} V$. The existence follows from the fact that $p(n) > p(n-1)
$, forcing a linear dependence among the restricted irreducible characters, whic
h yields two distinct representations with identical restrictions.
