## Solution

**Setup.** Place $F_1 = (0,0)$ and $F_2 = (1,0)$, so $F_1F_2 = 1$ lies along the
 $x$-axis. Since $F_1F_2$ is parallel to both directrices, $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$
 are horizontal lines: $\ell_1: y = c_1$ and $\ell_2: y = c_2$.

**Equations of the parabolas.** A point $(x,y)$ on $\mathcal{P}_1$ satisfies $\t
ext{dist}((x,y),F_1) = \text{dist}((x,y),\ell_1)$:
$$x^2 + y^2 = (y-c_1)^2 \implies x^2 = -2c_1 y + c_1^2.$$

Similarly for $\mathcal{P}_2$ with focus $F_2=(1,0)$ and directrix $\ell_2: y =
c_2$:
$$(x-1)^2 = -2c_2 y + c_2^2.$$

**Using the focus-on-parabola conditions.** Since $F_1=(0,0)\in\mathcal{P}_2$:
$$(0-1)^2 = c_2^2 \implies c_2 = \pm 1.$$

Since $F_2=(1,0)\in\mathcal{P}_1$:
$$(1)^2 = c_1^2 \implies c_1 = \pm 1.$$

The directrices are distinct, so $c_1 \neq c_2$, giving $(c_1,c_2) = (1,-1)$ or
$(-1,1)$.

**Case $(c_1,c_2)=(1,-1)$.** The parabolas become:
$$\mathcal{P}_1:\ x^2 = 1-2y, \qquad \mathcal{P}_2:\ (x-1)^2 = 2y+1.$$

From $\mathcal{P}_1$: $y = \frac{1-x^2}{2}$. From $\mathcal{P}_2$: $y = \frac{x^
2-2x}{2}$. Setting equal:
$$1 - x^2 = x^2 - 2x \implies 2x^2 - 2x - 1 = 0 \implies x = \frac{1\pm\sqrt{3}}
{2}.$$

For $x_A = \frac{1+\sqrt{3}}{2}$: $x_A^2 = \frac{2+\sqrt{3}}{2}$, so $y_A = \fra
c{1-\frac{2+\sqrt{3}}{2}}{2} = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$.

For $x_B = \frac{1-\sqrt{3}}{2}$: $x_B^2 = \frac{2-\sqrt{3}}{2}$, so $y_B = \fra
c{1-\frac{2-\sqrt{3}}{2}}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$.

Thus:
$$A = \left(\tfrac{1+\sqrt{3}}{2},\, -\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\right),\quad B = \left
(\tfrac{1-\sqrt{3}}{2},\, \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\right).$$

**Computing $AB^2$:**
$$\Delta x = \sqrt{3},\qquad \Delta y = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},$$
$$AB^2 = (\sqrt{3})^2 + \left(-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^2 = 3 + \frac{3}{4} = \
frac{15}{4}.$$

**Case $(c_1,c_2)=(-1,1)$.** By symmetry (reflecting across the $x$-axis), the s
ame intersection $x$-values arise and the $y$-values are negated, yielding the s
ame $AB^2 = \frac{15}{4}$.

**Verification.** Directrices $y=1$ and $y=-1$ are distinct ✓; foci $(0,0)$ and
$(1,0)$ are distinct ✓; $F_1\in\mathcal{P}_2$ and $F_2\in\mathcal{P}_1$ hold by
construction ✓; $A\neq B$ since $x_A\neq x_B$ ✓.

**Result.** $AB^2 = \frac{15}{4}$ with $\gcd(15,4)=1$, so $m=15$, $n=4$, and
$$100m + n = 1500 + 4 = \boxed{1504}.$$
