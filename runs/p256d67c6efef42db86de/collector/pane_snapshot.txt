## Solution

We compute
$$I = \oint_{|z|=3} \frac{z^{17}}{(z^2+2)^3(z^3+3)^4}\,dz.$$

**Step 1: Locate the singularities.**

- $z^2+2=0 \Rightarrow z=\pm i\sqrt{2}$, with $|z|=\sqrt{2}<3$. Both lie inside
$|z|=3$.
- $z^3+3=0 \Rightarrow z^3=-3$, so $z=-\sqrt[3]{3}\,\omega^k$ for $k=0,1,2$ (cub
e roots of unity $\omega^k$), each with $|z|=\sqrt[3]{3}<3$. All lie inside $|z|
=3$.

All five singularities are enclosed by $|z|=3$, so by the Residue Theorem
$$I = 2\pi i \sum_{\text{finite }a} \operatorname{Res}(f,a).$$

**Step 2: Use the residue at infinity.**

The sum of **all** residues of a rational function (finite poles $+$ the residue
 at $\infty$) equals $0$:
$$\sum_{\text{finite }a} \operatorname{Res}(f,a) + \operatorname{Res}(f,\infty)
= 0.$$

So we only need $\operatorname{Res}(f,\infty)$.

**Step 3: Compute $\operatorname{Res}(f,\infty)$.**

By definition, $\operatorname{Res}(f,\infty) = -\operatorname{Res}\!\bigl(f(1/w)
/w^2,\, w=0\bigr)$.

With $f(z)=z^{17}/\bigl((z^2+2)^3(z^3+3)^4\bigr)$:
$$f(1/w) = \frac{w^{-17}}{\bigl((w^{-2}+2)^3\bigr)\bigl((w^{-3}+3)^4\bigr)}.$$

Rewrite the denominator factors:
$$(w^{-2}+2)^3 = \frac{(1+2w^2)^3}{w^6}, \qquad (w^{-3}+3)^4 = \frac{(1+3w^3)^4}
{w^{12}}.$$

Hence
$$\frac{f(1/w)}{w^2} = \frac{w^{-17}}{w^2}\cdot\frac{w^6\,w^{12}}{(1+2w^2)^3(1+3
w^3)^4} = \frac{w^{-1}}{(1+2w^2)^3(1+3w^3)^4}.$$

This has a simple pole at $w=0$, and
$$\operatorname{Res}\!\left(\frac{f(1/w)}{w^2},\,0\right) = \lim_{w\to 0} w\cdot
\frac{w^{-1}}{(1+2w^2)^3(1+3w^3)^4} = \frac{1}{1\cdot 1} = 1.$$

Therefore
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,\infty) = -1.$$

**Step 4: Conclude.**

$$\sum_{\text{finite }a} \operatorname{Res}(f,a) = -\operatorname{Res}(f,\infty)
 = 1.$$

Thus
$$I = 2\pi i \cdot 1 = \boxed{2\pi i}.$$

**Sanity check.** The numerator has degree $17$ and the denominator has degree $
2\cdot3+3\cdot4=18$, so $f(z)\sim 1/z$ as $z\to\infty$. The coefficient of $z^{-
1}$ in the Laurent expansion at $\infty$ is $1$, confirming $\operatorname{Res}(
f,\infty)=-1$ and hence the sum of finite residues is $1$. The integral is $2\pi
 i$.
