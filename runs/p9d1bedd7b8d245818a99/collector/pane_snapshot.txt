## Solution

**Claim.** Let $\{A_n\}_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ be a countable family of sets, each
satisfying $|A_n| = |\mathbb{R}| = \mathfrak{c}$. Then
$$\left|\bigcup_{n \in \mathbb{N}} A_n\right| = \mathfrak{c}.$$

**Proof.**

We establish the equality by proving both inequalities.

**Lower bound.** Since $A_1 \subseteq \bigcup_n A_n$ and $|A_1| = \mathfrak{c}$,
 we immediately have
$$\left|\bigcup_{n \in \mathbb{N}} A_n\right| \geq |A_1| = \mathfrak{c}.$$

**Upper bound.** We construct an injection from $\bigcup_n A_n$ into a set of ca
rdinality $\mathfrak{c}$.

By hypothesis, for each $n \in \mathbb{N}$ there exists a bijection $f_n : A_n \
to \mathbb{R}$ (using the Axiom of Choice to select one such bijection for each
$n$ simultaneously — this uses countable choice).

Define $F : \bigcup_{n \in \mathbb{N}} A_n \to \mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{R}$ as
follows. For each $x \in \bigcup_n A_n$, let
$$n(x) = \min\{n \in \mathbb{N} : x \in A_n\},$$
and set
$$F(x) = \bigl(n(x),\; f_{n(x)}(x)\bigr).$$

**$F$ is injective.** Suppose $F(x) = F(y)$. Then $n(x) = n(y) = n$ and $f_n(x)
= f_n(y)$. Since $f_n$ is a bijection (hence injective) and both $x, y \in A_n$,
 we conclude $x = y$.

**$|\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{R}| = \mathfrak{c}$.** We show this by two inequal
ities:
- $|\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{R}| \geq |\{0\} \times \mathbb{R}| = |\mathbb{R}|
= \mathfrak{c}$.
- $|\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{R}| \leq |\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}| = \mathfra
k{c}$, since $\mathbb{N} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ gives an injection $\mathbb{N} \t
imes \mathbb{R} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}$, and $|\mathbb{R}
\times \mathbb{R}| = \mathfrak{c}$ is the classical result that $\mathbb{R}^2$ i
s in bijection with $\mathbb{R}$ (e.g., via interleaving decimal expansions, or
the Cantor–Schröder–Bernstein theorem applied to the injections $\mathbb{R} \hoo
krightarrow \mathbb{R}^2$, $x \mapsto (x,0)$, and $\mathbb{R}^2 \hookrightarrow
\mathbb{R}$, $(x,y) \mapsto $ a fixed injection constructed from a bijection $\m
athbb{R} \cong 2^{\mathbb{N}}$ and the map $2^{\mathbb{N}} \times 2^{\mathbb{N}}
 \to 2^{\mathbb{N}}$ given by interleaving bits).

Therefore $|\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{R}| = \mathfrak{c}$.

Combining the injection $F$ with $|\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{R}| = \mathfrak{c}$
, we obtain
$$\left|\bigcup_{n \in \mathbb{N}} A_n\right| \leq \mathfrak{c}.$$

**Conclusion.** From the lower and upper bounds,
$$\mathfrak{c} \leq \left|\bigcup_{n \in \mathbb{N}} A_n\right| \leq \mathfrak{c
},$$
so by the Cantor–Schröder–Bernstein theorem,
$$\left|\bigcup_{n \in \mathbb{N}} A_n\right| = \mathfrak{c} = |\mathbb{R}|. \qq
uad \blacksquare$$

**Remark.** The result relies on the Axiom of Choice (specifically, countable ch
oice) to simultaneously select a bijection $f_n : A_n \to \mathbb{R}$ for each $
n$. Without any choice, one cannot in general guarantee the existence of such a
family of bijections, and the conclusion may fail. With countable choice — the s
tandard assumption in mainstream set theory — the proof above is complete.
