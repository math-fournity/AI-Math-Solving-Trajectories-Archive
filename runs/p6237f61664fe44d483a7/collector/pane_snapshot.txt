## Analysis

**Structure of the matrix.** The matrix $A$ is the infinitesimal generator (Q-ma
trix) of a continuous-time Markov chain on states $\{1, 2, \ldots, n\}$. The giv
en conditions are:

- $A_{i,i} = -A_{i+1,i}$ for $1 \le i \le n-1$, with $A_{i+1,i} \neq 0$,
- $A_{n,n} = 0$.

For $A$ to be a valid Q-matrix, every row must sum to zero: $A_{i,i} + \sum_{j \
ne i} A_{i,j} = 0$, all off-diagonal entries must be non-negative, and diagonal
entries non-positive. The condition $A_{i,i} = -A_{i+1,i}$ with $A_{i+1,i} > 0$
forces $A_{i,i} < 0$ for $i < n$, and the row-sum condition forces $A_{i+1,i}$ t
o be the **total** rate out of state $i$. Since all off-diagonal entries are non
-negative and their sum equals $A_{i+1,i}$, the natural and consistent interpret
ation is that $A_{i+1,i}$ is the **only** non-zero off-diagonal entry in row $i$
. Thus the chain is a **pure birth process** (linear/sequential chain):

$$1 \xrightarrow{\lambda_1} 2 \xrightarrow{\lambda_2} 3 \xrightarrow{\lambda_3}
\cdots \xrightarrow{\lambda_{n-1}} n \text{ (absorbing)}$$

where $\lambda_i = A_{i+1,i} > 0$ for $i = 1, \ldots, n-1$, and $\lambda_n := 0$
.

## Forward Equations

The transition probabilities $P_{1j}(t) = (e^{At})_{1j}$ satisfy the Kolmogorov
forward equations:

$$\frac{d}{dt}P_{1j}(t) = \lambda_{j-1}\,P_{1,j-1}(t) - \lambda_j\,P_{1j}(t), \q
quad j = 1, \ldots, n,$$

with initial conditions $P_{11}(0) = 1$, $P_{1j}(0) = 0$ for $j \ge 2$, and the
convention $\lambda_0 = 0$.

**Base case ($j=1$):** $P_{11}'(t) = -\lambda_1 P_{11}(t)$, giving $P_{11}(t) =
e^{-\lambda_1 t}$.

**Inductive step:** Each equation is a first-order linear ODE of the form $y' +
\lambda_j y = \lambda_{j-1} P_{1,j-1}(t)$, solvable by the integrating factor $e
^{\lambda_j t}$. By induction on $j$, one obtains the **general closed-form solu
tion**:

$$\boxed{P_{1j}(t) = \left(\prod_{k=1}^{j-1} \lambda_k\right) \sum_{k=1}^{j} \fr
ac{e^{-\lambda_k t}}{\prod_{\substack{m=1 \\ m \ne k}}^{j} (\lambda_m - \lambda_
k)}}$$

valid when all $\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_j$ are pairwise distinct.

## Formula for $P_{1n}(t)$

Setting $j = n$ and $\lambda_n = 0$:

$$P_{1n}(t) = \left(\prod_{k=1}^{n-1} \lambda_k\right) \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{e^{-
\lambda_k t}}{\prod_{\substack{m=1 \\ m \ne k}}^{n} (\lambda_m - \lambda_k)}.$$

Separating the $k=n$ term (where $\lambda_n = 0$):

$$P_{1n}(t) = \left(\prod_{k=1}^{n-1} \lambda_k\right)\left[\frac{1}{\prod_{m=1}
^{n-1}\lambda_m} + \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \frac{e^{-\lambda_k t}}{(-\lambda_k)\prod_{\
substack{m=1\\m\ne k}}^{n-1}(\lambda_m - \lambda_k)}\right]$$

$$= 1 - \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \frac{\prod_{\substack{j=1\\j\ne k}}^{n-1}\lambda_j}{\p
rod_{\substack{m=1\\m\ne k}}^{n-1}(\lambda_m - \lambda_k)}\, e^{-\lambda_k t}.$$

**Verification of correctness:**

1. **Initial condition:** At $t=0$, $P_{1n}(0) = 1 - \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \frac{\pro
d_{j\ne k}\lambda_j}{\prod_{m\ne k}(\lambda_m-\lambda_k)}$. The sum is a standar
d partial-fraction identity equal to $1$ (it is the partial fraction decompositi
on of $\frac{\prod \lambda_j}{\prod(\lambda - \lambda_k)}$ evaluated at $\lambda
 = 0$, which equals $1$). So $P_{1n}(0) = 0$. ✓

2. **Long-time limit:** As $t \to \infty$, all exponential terms vanish, giving
$P_{1n}(t) \to 1$. This is correct since state $n$ is the unique absorbing state
. ✓

3. **Small example ($n=2$):** $P_{12}(t) = 1 - e^{-\lambda_1 t}$, which is the s
tandard CDF of an exponential random variable with rate $\lambda_1$. ✓

4. **Example ($n=3$):** $P_{13}(t) = 1 - \frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_2 - \lambda_1}
e^{-\lambda_1 t} + \frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2 - \lambda_1}e^{-\lambda_2 t}$, whi
ch is the CDF of the sum of two independent exponentials (a hypoexponential dist
ribution). ✓

## Handling Repeated Rates

If some rates $\lambda_i$ coincide, the partial-fraction formula develops remova
ble singularities. Applying L'Hôpital's rule (confluent partial fraction decompo
sition) replaces the simple-pole terms with terms of the form $t^r e^{-\lambda t
}$ (for a pole of order $r+1$). The result remains a **closed-form analytical ex
pression** involving polynomials in $t$ multiplied by exponentials. Since the pr
oblem guarantees $A_{i+1,i} \neq 0$ (all rates positive and distinct from $\lamb
da_n = 0$), the only potential degeneracy is among $\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_{
n-1}$, which is handled by this generalization.

da_k t}, \qquad \lambda_k = A_{k+1,k},$$
