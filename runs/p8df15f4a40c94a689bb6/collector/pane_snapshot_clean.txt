# The Action of $\pi_1(X,x_0)$ on $\pi_n(X,x_0)$

## Setup and Definitions

Let $X$ be a path-connected space with basepoint $x_0$. For $n \geq 1$, recall t
hat $\pi_n(X,x_0)$ is the set of homotopy classes of maps $(I^n, \partial I^n) \
to (X, x_0)$, where $I = [0,1]$ and $\partial I^n$ is the boundary of the $n$-cu
be. The group structure on $\pi_n$ for $n \geq 1$ comes from concatenation in th
e first coordinate.

A loop $\gamma : (I, \partial I) \to (X, x_0)$ represents an element $[\gamma] \
in \pi_1(X, x_0)$.

## Constructing the Action

**Definition of the action.** Given $[\gamma] \in \pi_1(X, x_0)$ and $[\alpha] \
in \pi_n(X, x_0)$, where $\alpha : (I^n, \partial I^n) \to (X, x_0)$, we define
a new map $\gamma \cdot \alpha : (I^n, \partial I^n) \to (X, x_0)$ as follows.

Think of $I^n = I \times I^{n-1}$, writing coordinates as $(t, s_2, \dots, s_n)$
. Define:

$$
(\gamma \cdot \alpha)(t, s_2, \dots, s_n) = \begin{cases} \gamma(3t) & 0 \leq t
\leq 1/3, \\ \alpha(3t - 1,\, s_2, \dots, s_n) & 1/3 \leq t \leq 2/3, \\ \gamma(
3 - 3t) & 2/3 \leq t \leq 1. \end{cases}
$$

**Intuition:** Along the first coordinate, we first traverse $\gamma$, then run
$\alpha$, then traverse $\gamma$ in reverse. Equivalently, we drag the basepoint
 of $\alpha$ around the loop $\gamma$ and back.

**Boundary check.** On $\partial I^n$ we must check the value is $x_0$. If $t \i
n \{0, 1\}$ (i.e., the first coordinate is on the boundary), then $\gamma \cdot
\alpha$ evaluates to $\gamma(0) = x_0$ or $\gamma(0) = x_0$. If any of $s_2, \do
ts, s_n$ is on the boundary of $I^{n-1}$, then $\alpha$ on that face is $x_0$ by
 hypothesis, and the middle third gives $x_0$, while the outer thirds give $\gam
ma(\cdot)$ which is independent of $s_i$ — but we need the whole thing to be $x_
0$.

The subtlety: when $s_i$ is on $\partial I^{n-1}$, the middle third gives $\alph
a(\cdot, s_2,\dots,s_n) = x_0$, but the outer thirds give $\gamma(3t)$ and $\gam
ma(3-3t)$, which are **not** constantly $x_0$. So the naive definition above doe
s **not** send $\partial I^n$ to $x_0$ in general.

**Corrected construction (the standard one).** The correct approach uses the fac
t that $\alpha$ maps the entire boundary to $x_0$, so we can "absorb" the loop t
ravel into the boundary. Here is the clean formulation:

Define $\gamma \cdot \alpha$ by replacing the basepoint $x_0$ (the constant valu
e on $\partial I^n$) with the loop $\gamma$ along a "collar" of the boundary. Co
ncretely, view $\alpha$ as a map $S^n \to X$ sending the basepoint $\infty$ (the
 collapsed boundary) to $x_0$. The loop $\gamma$ gives a path from $x_0$ to $x_0
$. We use the **change-of-basepoint isomorphism**:

For any path $\gamma$ from $x_0$ to $x_0$, there is an isomorphism $\gamma_\# :
\pi_n(X, x_0) \to \pi_n(X, x_0)$ defined as follows. Represent $[\alpha] \in \pi
_n(X, x_0)$ by a map $\alpha : (I^n, \partial I^n) \to (X, x_0)$. Since $\alpha(
\partial I^n) = x_0 = \gamma(0) = \gamma(1)$, we can define a homotopy that drag
s the value on $\partial I^n$ along $\gamma$. The resulting map $\gamma_\#(\alph
a)$ is the representative of the class $[\gamma] \cdot [\alpha]$.

**Explicit formula via the pinch/collar.** Identify $I^n$ with $I \times I^{n-1}
$. Define a continuous "radial" retraction parameter $r = r(t, s_2, \dots, s_n)
\in [0,1]$ that equals $0$ on $\partial I^n$ and $1$ at the center. Then set:

$$
(\gamma_\# \alpha)(t, s_2, \dots, s_n) = \begin{cases} \gamma(r) & \text{if } r
\leq 1 \text{ (on a collar near } \partial I^n), \\ \alpha(\text{rescaled interi
or point}) & \text{in the interior region}. \end{cases}
$$

More precisely: choose a continuous function $\rho : I^n \to I$ with $\rho^{-1}(
0) = \partial I^n$ (e.g., $\rho = \min_i \min(t_i, 1-t_i)$, scaled). Pick a thre
shold $\epsilon > 0$. On the region $\rho \leq \epsilon$ (a collar of the bounda
ry), define the map to be $\gamma(\rho/\epsilon)$ — this traces the loop $\gamma
$ as we move from the boundary inward. On the region $\rho \geq \epsilon$, resca
le $\alpha$ to fill the inner cube $[\epsilon, 1-\epsilon]^n$. The two pieces ag
ree on the interface $\rho = \epsilon$ (both give $\gamma(1) = x_0 = \alpha(\par
tial)$). On $\partial I^n$ ($\rho = 0$), the value is $\gamma(0) = x_0$. So $\ga
mma_\# \alpha : (I^n, \partial I^n) \to (X, x_0)$.

## Well-Definedness

We must show the class $[\gamma_\# \alpha] \in \pi_n(X, x_0)$ depends only on $[
\gamma]$ and $[\alpha]$.

- **Dependence only on $[\alpha]$:** If $\alpha \simeq \alpha'$ rel $\partial I^
n$ via a homotopy $H : I^n \times I \to X$ fixing the boundary at $x_0$, then ap
plying the same collar construction to $H$ (using $\gamma$ on the collar, $H$ on
 the interior, for each time slice) gives a homotopy $\gamma_\# \alpha \simeq \g
amma_\# \alpha'$ rel $\partial I^n$.

- **Dependence only on $[\gamma]$:** If $\gamma \simeq \gamma'$ rel $\partial I$
 via a homotopy $G : I \times I \to X$ with $G(0,t) = G(1,t) = x_0$, then replac
ing $\gamma$ by $G(\cdot, s)$ in the collar construction gives a homotopy $\gamm
a_\# \alpha \simeq \gamma'_\# \alpha$ rel $\partial I^n$. (On $\partial I^n$, th
e value is $G(0, s) = x_0$ for all $s$.)

Hence the map $([\gamma], [\alpha]) \mapsto [\gamma_\# \alpha]$ is well-defined.

## It Is a Group Action

We verify the two axioms.

**Identity:** The constant loop $c$ at $x_0$ gives $c_\# \alpha$. In the collar
construction, $\gamma = c$ means the collar region is constantly $x_0$, and the
interior is $\alpha$ itself (after rescaling, which is homotopic to the identity
 rescaling). So $[c_\# \alpha] = [\alpha]$, i.e., $e \cdot [\alpha] = [\alpha]$.

**Compatibility:** Let $\gamma, \delta$ be two loops. We show $(\gamma \cdot \de
lta)_\# \alpha \simeq \gamma_\#(\delta_\# \alpha)$ rel $\partial I^n$, where $\g
amma \cdot \delta$ is loop concatenation (first $\delta$, then $\gamma$ — or whi
chever convention; the point is the two constructions agree up to homotopy).

The key observation: $(\gamma \cdot \delta)_\#$ uses the concatenated loop in th
e collar. $\gamma_\#(\delta_\# \alpha)$ applies $\delta_\#$ first (creating a $\
delta$-collar), then applies $\gamma_\#$ (creating a $\gamma$-collar outside the
 $\delta$-collar). By reparametrizing the radial coordinate — merging the two ne
sted collars into one collar traversing $\delta$ then $\gamma$ — we obtain a map
 homotopic to $(\gamma \cdot \delta)_\# \alpha$. The reparametrization is a homo
topy of the radial function $\rho$, which induces a homotopy of the resulting ma
ps rel $\partial I^n$.

Thus $[\gamma] \cdot ([\delta] \cdot [\alpha]) = ([\gamma][\delta]) \cdot [\alph
a]$, and we have a **right action** of $\pi_1$ on $\pi_n$ (or a left action depe
nding on concatenation convention; the standard convention gives a right action,
 which is equivalently a left action of the opposite group — many texts simply c
all it "the action of $\pi_1$ on $\pi_n$").

## It Is an Action by Group Automorphisms

For $n \geq 2$, $\pi_n(X, x_0)$ is an abelian group. We show each $\gamma_\#$ is
 a group homomorphism, hence an automorphism (it has inverse $\bar{\gamma}_\#$,
where $\bar{\gamma}$ is the reversed loop, since $\gamma_\# \circ \bar{\gamma}_\
# = (\gamma \cdot \bar{\gamma})_\# = c_\# = \mathrm{id}$).

**Homomorphism property:** The group operation on $\pi_n$ is concatenation in, s
ay, the first coordinate. Let $\alpha, \beta : (I^n, \partial I^n) \to (X, x_0)$
, and let $\alpha + \beta$ denote concatenation in the first coordinate. We need
 $\gamma_\#(\alpha + \beta) \simeq \gamma_\#\alpha + \gamma_\#\beta$ rel $\parti
al I^n$.

The collar construction for $\alpha + \beta$ places the $\gamma$-collar around t
he boundary of the concatenated cube. The concatenation $\gamma_\#\alpha + \gamm
a_\#\beta$ places a $\gamma$-collar around each half. These differ only in how t
he collar is arranged near the "seam" where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ join (at $t = 1
/2$ in the first coordinate). Near this seam, both constructions trace $\gamma$
along the relevant boundary portions. A reparametrization homotopy (redistributi
ng the collar region) shows the two are homotopic rel $\partial I^n$.

Therefore $\gamma_\#$ is a group homomorphism for $n \geq 2$. Since $\gamma_\#$
has inverse $\bar\gamma_\#$, it is an automorphism.

For $n = 1$, $\pi_1$ acts on itself by **conjugation**: $\gamma_\#(\alpha) = \ga
mma \cdot \alpha \cdot \bar{\gamma}$, which is exactly inner automorphism. This
is the familiar action of $\pi_1$ on itself by conjugation.

## Summary

**Theorem.** *Let $X$ be path-connected with basepoint $x_0$. For each $n \geq 1
$, there is a natural action of $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ on $\pi_n(X, x_0)$:*

$$\pi_1(X, x_0) \times \pi_n(X, x_0) \to \pi_n(X, x_0), \qquad ([\gamma], [\alph
a]) \mapsto [\gamma] \cdot [\alpha] := [\gamma_\# \alpha],$$

*where $\gamma_\# \alpha$ is obtained from $\alpha$ by dragging its basepoint ar
ound the loop $\gamma$. This action is:*

1. *A well-defined group action (the change-of-basepoint action).*
2. *By automorphisms: for $n \geq 2$, each $[\gamma]$ acts as an automorphism of
 the abelian group $\pi_n(X,x_0)$; for $n = 1$, the action is by conjugation $[\
alpha] \mapsto [\gamma][\alpha][\gamma]^{-1}$.*
3. *Natural in $X$: a based map $f : (X, x_0) \to (Y, y_0)$ satisfies $f_*([\gam
ma] \cdot [\alpha]) = f_*[\gamma] \cdot f_*[\alpha]$, i.e., $f_*$ is $\pi_1$-equ
ivariant.*
4. *Inner for $n = 1$ and trivial when $X$ is simply connected (or more generall
y when $[\gamma]$ lies in the center of the action).*

*Equivalently, the action gives a homomorphism $\pi_1(X, x_0) \to \mathrm{Aut}(\
pi_n(X, x_0))$ for $n \geq 2$, and $\pi_1(X,x_0) \to \mathrm{Inn}(\pi_1(X,x_0))
\hookrightarrow \mathrm{Aut}(\pi_1(X,x_0))$ for $n = 1$. The kernel of this acti
on (for $n \geq 2$) is the subgroup of $\pi_1$ acting trivially, and the quotien
t $\pi_n / \pi_1$ (the coinvariants) measures the obstruction to $\pi_n$ being a
 direct invariant.*
