## Solution

**Example.** The Weierstrass ℘-function associated to a lattice $\Lambda = \math
bb{Z}\omega_1 + \mathbb{Z}\omega_2$ with $\omega_1/\omega_2 \notin \mathbb{R}$ (
so the two periods are not parallel) is an analytic (meromorphic) function with
two non-parallel periods.

---

### Construction

Choose two complex numbers $\omega_1, \omega_2$ with $\operatorname{Im}(\omega_1
/\omega_2) \neq 0$, so they are not real multiples of one another (hence not par
allel as vectors in $\mathbb{R}^2$). Define the lattice
$$\Lambda = \{m\omega_1 + n\omega_2 : m, n \in \mathbb{Z}\}.$$
The **Weierstrass ℘-function** is
$$\wp(z) = \frac{1}{z^2} + \sum_{\omega \in \Lambda\setminus\{0\}}\left(\frac{1}
{(z-\omega)^2} - \frac{1}{\omega^2}\right).$$

### It is analytic (meromorphic)

The series converges absolutely and uniformly on every compact subset of $\mathb
b{C}\setminus\Lambda$ (this is a standard result: the subtraction of $1/\omega^2
$ ensures convergence, since the tail behaves like $O(1/\omega^3)$ and the latti
ce sum $\sum_{\omega\neq 0}|\omega|^{-3}$ converges). Hence $\wp$ is holomorphic
 on $\mathbb{C}\setminus\Lambda$, with double poles at each lattice point. Being
 holomorphic on its domain $\mathbb{C}\setminus\Lambda$, it is an analytic funct
ion.

### It has two non-parallel periods

**Periodicity.** For any $\lambda \in \Lambda$, the translated lattice $\Lambda
+ \lambda = \Lambda$ is identical to $\Lambda$, so re-indexing the sum gives
$$\wp(z+\lambda) = \frac{1}{(z+\lambda)^2} + \sum_{\omega\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\
}}\left(\frac{1}{(z+\lambda-\omega)^2}-\frac{1}{\omega^2}\right).$$
Substituting $\omega' = \omega - \lambda$ (a bijection of $\Lambda$), and noting
 the constant term $\sum 1/\omega^2$ is invariant under re-indexing (the symmetr
ic lattice makes $\sum_{\omega\neq 0}1/\omega^2$ converge to the same value afte
r translation, using the standard symmetric-summation convention), one obtains
$$\wp(z+\lambda) = \wp(z).$$
In particular, taking $\lambda = \omega_1$ and $\lambda = \omega_2$:
$$\wp(z+\omega_1)=\wp(z), \qquad \wp(z+\omega_2)=\wp(z).$$

**The two periods are not parallel.** Since $\omega_1/\omega_2 \notin \mathbb{R}
$, the vectors $\omega_1, \omega_2$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb{R}$;
geometrically they point in different directions, so they are not parallel.

### Conclusion

The Weierstrass ℘-function $\wp(z)$ is an analytic (meromorphic) function on $\m
athbb{C}\setminus\Lambda$ possessing two periods $\omega_1, \omega_2$ that are n
ot parallel. $\blacksquare$
