## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** With the new (equivalent) inner products, $V$ remains dense in
$H$ and both spaces remain separable.

**Key observation.** Two norms on the same vector space are *equivalent* (i.e.,
there exist constants $c, C > 0$ with $c\|u\| \le \|u\|' \le C\|u\|$ for all $u$
) if and only if they induce the **same topology**. Consequently, any property t
hat depends only on the topology is preserved under passage to an equivalent nor
m. Both *density* and *separability* are such topological properties. We verify
this directly.

---

### 1. Separability is preserved

**For $H$:** Let $\|\cdot\|_{H_2} \sim \|\cdot\|_H$, so there exist $c_H, C_H >
0$ with
$$c_H\|u\|_H \le \|u\|_{H_2} \le C_H\|u\|_H, \quad \forall\, u \in H.$$
Since $(H, \|\cdot\|_H)$ is separable, there exists a countable set $\{h_n\}_{n=
1}^\infty \subset H$ dense in $H$ with respect to $\|\cdot\|_H$. We claim $\{h_n
\}$ is also dense with respect to $\|\cdot\|_{H_2}$.

Given $h \in H$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, set $\delta = \varepsilon / C_H > 0$. By
density in $\|\cdot\|_H$, there exists $h_n$ with $\|h - h_n\|_H < \delta$. Then
$$\|h - h_n\|_{H_2} \le C_H\|h - h_n\|_H < C_H \cdot \frac{\varepsilon}{C_H} = \
varepsilon.$$
Hence $\{h_n\}$ is dense in $(H, \|\cdot\|_{H_2})$, so $H$ remains separable.

**For $V$:** The identical argument, using $\|\cdot\|_{V_2} \sim \|\cdot\|_V$, s
hows that any countable $\|\cdot\|_V$-dense subset of $V$ is also $\|\cdot\|_{V_
2}$-dense. Hence $V$ remains separable.

---

### 2. Density of $V$ in $H$ is preserved

Recall: $V$ is dense in $H$ (with respect to $\|\cdot\|_H$) means $\overline{V}^
{\,\|\cdot\|_H} = H$, i.e., for every $h \in H$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, there exi
sts $v \in V$ with $\|h - v\|_H < \varepsilon$.

We show $V$ is dense in $(H, \|\cdot\|_{H_2})$. Given $h \in H$ and $\varepsilon
 > 0$, set $\delta = \varepsilon / C_H$. By the original density, there exists $
v \in V$ with $\|h - v\|_H < \delta$. Then
$$\|h - v\|_{H_2} \le C_H\|h - v\|_H < C_H \cdot \frac{\varepsilon}{C_H} = \vare
psilon.$$
Thus for every $h \in H$ and $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $v \in V$ with $\|h
- v\|_{H_2} < \varepsilon$, i.e., $\overline{V}^{\,\|\cdot\|_{H_2}} = H$.

So $V$ remains dense in $H$ under the new norm.

### 3. Summary / conceptual remark

The underlying reason is simple: **equivalent norms define the same topology**,
and both density (of a subset) and separability (existence of a countable dense
subset) are purely topological notions—they make no reference to the specific va
lues of the norm, only to which sets are open. Since the new inner products yiel
d norms equivalent to the originals, the topologies on both $V$ and $H$ are unch
anged, and therefore:

- The closure of $V$ in $H$ is the same set (namely $H$), so **$V$ is still dens
e in $H$**.
- The same countable dense subsets witness separability, so **both $V$ and $H$ r
emain separable**.

Moreover, $V \subset H$ as sets is unaffected (only the inner products change, n
ot the underlying vector spaces), and $V$ remains a Hilbert space under $\|\cdot
\|_{V_2}$ (completeness is preserved because equivalent norms have the same Cauc
hy sequences: $\|u_n - u_m\|_{V_2} \le C_V\|u_n - u_m\|_V$ and vice versa). Henc
e the entire Gelfand-triple structure $V \hookrightarrow H$ is preserved.
