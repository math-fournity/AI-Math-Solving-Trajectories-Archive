## Proof

**Setup and symmetry.** Label the columns $1,2,\dots,2018$ and rows $1,2,\dots,2
018$, with the bottom‑left square $(1,1)$ (red) and the bottom‑right square $(20
18,1)$ (blue). Since $2018$ is even, the vertical center line $x=1009.5$ lies st
rictly between columns $1009$ and $1010$. Define the reflection
$$\rho(x,y)=(2019-x,\;y).$$
This is a fixed‑point‑free involution of the board (every square has a distinct
mirror partner, since $2019-x=x$ would force $x=1009.5$, not an integer). The st
arting positions are mirrors of each other:
$$\rho(1,1)=(2018,1),\qquad \rho(2018,1)=(1,1).$$

**Move set is reflection‑invariant.** A legal move is a vector $(\Delta x,\Delta
 y)$ with $\{|\Delta x|,|\Delta y|\}=\{20,17\}$. Reflecting across the vertical
axis sends $(\Delta x,\Delta y)\mapsto (-\Delta x,\Delta y)$, which is again a l
egal move (both signs are permitted). So if a step from $A$ to $B$ is legal, the
 step from $\rho(A)$ to $\rho(B)$ is legal.

**Mirror strategy.** Sasha maintains the invariant that after each of his moves
the two knights stand at mirror positions: if red is at $R$, blue is at $\rho(R)
$. This holds initially. When Keliang moves red from $R$ to some legal square $R
'$, Sasha responds by moving blue from $\rho(R)$ to $\rho(R')$.

We verify this response is always legal:

1. **On the board:** $\rho(R')$ is on the board by symmetry.
2. **Legal knight step:** The displacement $\rho(R')-\rho(R)=-(R'-R)$ in the $x$
‑coordinate and the same $y$‑displacement, which is a legal move by reflection‑i
nvariance.
3. **Not occupied by red:** Red is now at $R'\neq \rho(R')$ (no square equals it
s own mirror, as $2018$ is even), so $\rho(R')$ is free.
4. **Not previously visited:** The set of visited squares is kept symmetric by t
he strategy (squares are always added in mirror pairs $\{S,\rho(S)\}$). Since $R
'$ was unvisited before Keliang's move, symmetry implies $\rho(R')$ was also unv
isited; otherwise $\rho(\rho(R'))=R'$ would have been visited, contradiction.

Thus whenever Keliang has a legal move, Sasha has a legal mirror reply.

**Conclusion.** The game is finite (a finite board with no repeated positions),
so some player eventually cannot move. Since Sasha can always answer Keliang's m
ove, it is Keliang who first runs out of legal moves. Therefore **Sasha, the sec
ond player (blue), wins under optimal play.**
