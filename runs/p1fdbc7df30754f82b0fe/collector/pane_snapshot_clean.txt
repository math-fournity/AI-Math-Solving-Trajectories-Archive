# Proof

**Step 1: Extract a subsequence with uniformly bounded second moments.**

Since $\liminf_{n\to\infty} E(X_n^2) < \infty$, there exist a finite constant $M
$ and a subsequence (which we relabel as $\{X_n\}$ for convenience) such that
$$E(X_n^2) \le M \quad \text{for all } n.$$

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, $E|X_n| \le \sqrt{E(X_n^2)} \le \sqrt{M}$, so
$\sup_n E|X_n| < \infty$.

**Step 2: Tightness via Chebyshev.**

For every $t > 0$ and every $n$,
$$P(|X_n| > t) \le \frac{E(X_n^2)}{t^2} \le \frac{M}{t^2}.$$
Given $\varepsilon > 0$, choose $t$ so large that $M/t^2 < \varepsilon$; then $P
(|X_n| > t) < \varepsilon$ for all $n$ simultaneously. Hence the family of distr
ibutions $\{\mathcal{L}(X_n)\}$ is **tight**.

**Step 3: Apply Prohorov's theorem.**

By Prohorov's theorem, tightness on $\mathbb{R}$ (a Polish space) implies relati
ve sequential compactness. Therefore there exists a further subsequence $\{n_k\}
$ and a random variable $X$ such that
$$X_{n_k} \Rightarrow X \quad \text{(weak convergence / convergence in distribut
ion)}.$$

**Step 4: Uniform integrability of $\{X_{n_k}\}$.**

For any $c > 0$, using Cauchy–Schwarz and Chebyshev,
$$E\bigl[|X_{n_k}|\,\mathbf{1}_{\{|X_{n_k}| > c\}}\bigr] \le \sqrt{E(X_{n_k}^2)\
cdot P(|X_{n_k}|>c)} \le \sqrt{M \cdot \frac{M}{c^2}} = \frac{M}{c}.$$
Thus
$$\sup_k E\bigl[|X_{n_k}|\,\mathbf{1}_{\{|X_{n_k}| > c\}}\bigr] \le \frac{M}{c}
\xrightarrow{c\to\infty} 0,$$
which is precisely the definition of **uniform integrability** of $\{X_{n_k}\}$.

**Step 5: Integrability of $X$ and convergence of expectations.**

By the Skorokhod representation theorem, since $X_{n_k} \Rightarrow X$, there ex
ists a common probability space carrying random variables $\tilde X_{n_k}$ and $
\tilde X$ such that
$$\tilde X_{n_k} \stackrel{d}{=} X_{n_k}, \quad \tilde X \stackrel{d}{=} X, \qua
d \tilde X_{n_k} \to \tilde X \text{ a.s.}$$

The family $\{|\tilde X_{n_k}|\}$ is uniformly integrable (same distribution as
$\{|X_{n_k}|\}$). Applying Fatou's lemma to the non-negative sequence $|\tilde X
_{n_k}|$:
$$E|\tilde X| = E\!\left[\liminf_{k\to\infty} |\tilde X_{n_k}|\right] \le \limin
f_{k\to\infty} E|\tilde X_{n_k}| \le \sup_k E|X_{n_k}| \le \sqrt{M} < \infty.$$
Hence $X$ is **integrable**.

Now, since $\tilde X_{n_k} \to \tilde X$ almost surely and $\{|\tilde X_{n_k}|\}
$ is uniformly integrable, the Vitali convergence theorem yields $L^1$-convergen
ce:
$$E|\tilde X_{n_k} - \tilde X| \to 0.$$
In particular,
$$E(\tilde X_{n_k}) \to E(\tilde X).$$
Since $\tilde X_{n_k} \stackrel{d}{=} X_{n_k}$ and $\tilde X \stackrel{d}{=} X$,
 their expectations coincide, giving
$$\lim_{k\to\infty} E(X_{n_k}) = E(X).$$

**Conclusion.** We have produced an integrable random variable $X$ and a subsequ
ence $\{n_k\}$ with $X_{n_k} \Rightarrow X$ and $\lim_{k\to\infty} E(X_{n_k}) =
E(X)$, as required. $\blacksquare$
