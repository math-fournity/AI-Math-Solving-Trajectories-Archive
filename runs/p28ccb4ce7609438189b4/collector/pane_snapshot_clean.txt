## Solution

We compute $\iint_S K\,dA$ where $S = \{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^3 : x^4+y^4+z^4=1\}
$.

**Step 1: $S$ is a smooth closed surface.**

Let $F(x,y,z)=x^4+y^4+z^4$. Then $S = F^{-1}(1)$. The gradient is
$$\nabla F = (4x^3,\, 4y^3,\, 4z^3),$$
which vanishes only at the origin. Since $F(0,0,0)=0\neq 1$, the origin is not o
n $S$, so $\nabla F \neq 0$ everywhere on $S$. By the regular value theorem, $S$
 is a smooth (codimension-1) submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^3$, i.e. a smooth closed
 surface.

**Step 2: $S$ is compact and homeomorphic to $S^2$.**

Each function $t\mapsto t^4$ is convex, so $F$ is convex. The sublevel set $\Ome
ga = \{F\le 1\}$ is a compact convex body (bounded since $|x_i|\le 1$ for points
 in $\Omega$). Its boundary $\partial\Omega = S$ is a smooth closed surface boun
ding a convex body, hence homeomorphic to the 2-sphere $S^2$. In particular $S$
is orientable with Euler characteristic
$$\chi(S) = \chi(S^2) = 2.$$

**Step 3: Apply the Gauss–Bonnet theorem.**

$S$ is a closed (compact, without boundary) orientable Riemannian 2-manifold. Th
e Gauss–Bonnet theorem gives
$$\iint_S K\,dA = 2\pi\,\chi(S).$$

Substituting $\chi(S)=2$:
$$\iint_S K\,dA = 2\pi\cdot 2 = 4\pi.$$

**Result.** The integral of the Gaussian curvature over $S$ is

$$\boxed{4\pi}.$$
