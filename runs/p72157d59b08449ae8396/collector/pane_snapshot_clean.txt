## Investigation of the Functional

$$J[y(x)] = \int_a^b (y^2 + 2xyy')\,dx, \qquad y(a)=A,\; y(b)=B$$

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains only the functional and bo
undary conditions — no solution, sketch, or answer is embedded. Proceeding.

---

### Step 1. The Euler–Lagrange equation

The integrand is $F(x,y,y') = y^2 + 2xyy'$. The Euler–Lagrange equation is

$$\frac{\partial F}{\partial y} - \frac{d}{dx}\!\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial
 y'}\right) = 0.$$

Computing the partial derivatives:

$$\frac{\partial F}{\partial y} = 2y + 2xy', \qquad \frac{\partial F}{\partial y
'} = 2xy.$$

Taking the total derivative:

$$\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial y'}\right) = \frac{d}{dx}(2xy)
= 2y + 2xy'.$$

Substituting into the Euler–Lagrange equation:

$$(2y + 2xy') - (2y + 2xy') \equiv 0.$$

The equation is **identically satisfied** for every smooth $y(x)$. The Euler–Lag
range equation imposes no restriction on the extremal.

---

### Step 2. The integrand is an exact derivative

Observe that

$$\frac{d}{dx}\!\bigl(xy^2\bigr) = y^2 + 2xyy' = F(x,y,y').$$

Hence the functional reduces to a boundary term:

$$J[y] = \int_a^b \frac{d}{dx}\!\bigl(xy^2\bigr)\,dx = \bigl[xy^2\bigr]_{x=a}^{x
=b} = b\,B^2 - a\,A^2.$$

---

### Step 3. Conclusion

The value of $J[y]$ depends **only on the boundary values** $A$ and $B$ (and on
the endpoints $a,b$), and is **independent of the path** $y(x)$ chosen to connec
t them:

$$\boxed{J[y] = bB^2 - aA^2 \quad \text{for every admissible } y.}$$

Therefore the functional is a **null Lagrangian** (path-independent functional).
 Every admissible function $y(x)$ satisfying $y(a)=A$, $y(b)=B$ yields exactly t
he same value. Consequently:

- There is **no proper (strict) extremum** — no single curve distinguishes itsel
f.
- Equivalently, **every admissible function is simultaneously both a maximum and
 a minimum**, since the first variation vanishes identically:

$$\delta J = \int_a^b \left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}\,\delta y + \frac{\par
tial F}{\partial y'}\,\delta y'\right)dx = \bigl[2xy\,\delta y\bigr]_a^b = 0,$$

because the fixed-endpoint condition forces $\delta y(a)=\delta y(b)=0$.

The functional $J[y]$ has no genuine extremum; it is constant on the entire clas
s of admissible functions, equal to $bB^2 - aA^2$.
