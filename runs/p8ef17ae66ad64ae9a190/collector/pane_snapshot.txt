**Answer: Yes, φ(e_A) = e_B.**

**Proof.**

Let p = φ(e_A). Since φ is an algebra homomorphism, it preserves multiplication.
 As e_A is the identity of A, we have e_A² = e_A, hence

p = φ(e_A) = φ(e_A · e_A) = φ(e_A)·φ(e_A) = p²,

so p is an idempotent in B.

Moreover, for every a ∈ A, since e_A · a = a,

φ(a) = φ(e_A · a) = φ(e_A)·φ(a) = p·φ(a),

and likewise, since a · e_A = a,

φ(a) = φ(a · e_A) = φ(a)·φ(e_A) = φ(a)·p.

Thus p acts as a two-sided identity on the range φ(A).

Now by hypothesis there exists x ∈ A such that φ(x) is invertible in B. Let y ∈
B be its (two-sided) inverse, so

φ(x)·y = e_B and y·φ(x) = e_B.

From the identity p·φ(x) = φ(x) (established above), multiply both sides on the
right by y:

p·φ(x)·y = φ(x)·y
p·e_B = e_B
p = e_B.

(Equivalently, from φ(x)·p = φ(x), multiply on the left by y to get e_B·p = e_B,
 giving the same conclusion.)

Therefore φ(e_A) = p = e_B. ∎
