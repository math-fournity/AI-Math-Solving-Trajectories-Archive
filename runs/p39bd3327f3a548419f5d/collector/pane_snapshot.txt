All four cones share apex $A$. A cone with apex angle $2\theta$ corresponds, on
the unit sphere centered at $A$, to a **spherical circle** of angular radius $\t

Let:

**Tangency conditions** (spherical circle tangency):

## Determining the Configuration

Place $O$ at the north pole, $P_1$ at colatitude $\alpha$, longitude $0$; $P_2$
at colatitude $\alpha$, longitude $\varphi$.

**Spherical law of cosines** for $P_1P_2$:
$$\cos(2\alpha) = \cos^2\alpha + \sin^2\alpha\cos\varphi.$$
Using $\cos 2\alpha = 2\cos^2\alpha - 1$:
$$2\cos^2\alpha - 1 - \cos^2\alpha = \sin^2\alpha\cos\varphi \implies -\sin^2\al
pha = \sin^2\alpha\cos\varphi \implies \cos\varphi = -1,$$
so $\varphi = \pi$: $P_1$ and $P_2$ are at longitudes $0$ and $\pi$.

Let $P_3$ be at colatitude $2\alpha - \beta$, longitude $\psi$. Applying the sph
erical law of cosines to $P_1P_3$ and $P_2P_3$ (both $= \alpha + \beta$):
$$\cos(\alpha+\beta) = \cos\alpha\cos(2\alpha-\beta) + \sin\alpha\sin(2\alpha-\b
eta)\cos\psi,$$
$$\cos(\alpha+\beta) = \cos\alpha\cos(2\alpha-\beta) + \sin\alpha\sin(2\alpha-\b
eta)\cos(\psi-\pi).$$

Subtracting: $2\sin\alpha\sin(2\alpha-\beta)\cos\psi = 0$. Since $\sin\alpha\neq
 0$ and $\sin(2\alpha-\beta)\neq 0$ (nondegenerate), we get $\cos\psi = 0$, i.e.
, $\psi = \pi/2$.

Substituting back:
$$\boxed{\cos(\alpha+\beta) = \cos\alpha\cos(2\alpha-\beta).}$$

## Solving the Equation

Expand both sides:
- LHS: $\cos\alpha\cos\beta - \sin\alpha\sin\beta$.
- RHS: $\cos\alpha(\cos 2\alpha\cos\beta + \sin 2\alpha\sin\beta) = \cos\alpha\c
os 2\alpha\cos\beta + 2\sin\alpha\cos^2\alpha\sin\beta$.

Rearranging:
$$\cos\alpha\cos\beta(1 - \cos 2\alpha) = \sin\alpha\sin\beta(1 + 2\cos^2\alpha)
.$$

Using $1 - \cos 2\alpha = 2\sin^2\alpha$ and $1 + 2\cos^2\alpha = 2 + \cos 2\alp
ha$:
$$2\sin^2\alpha\cos\alpha\cos\beta = \sin\alpha\sin\beta(2+\cos 2\alpha).$$

Dividing by $\sin\alpha$:
$$\sin 2\alpha\cos\beta = \sin\beta(2 + \cos 2\alpha).$$

With $\sin\beta = \tfrac{1}{4}$, $\cos\beta = \tfrac{\sqrt{15}}{4}$:
$$\sqrt{15}\sin 2\alpha = 2 + \cos 2\alpha.$$

Let $c = \cos 2\alpha$, $s = \sin 2\alpha$. Then $\sqrt{15}\,s = 2 + c$, so $15(
1-c^2) = (2+c)^2$:
$$15 - 15c^2 = 4 + 4c + c^2 \implies 16c^2 + 4c - 11 = 0.$$
$$c = \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{16 + 704}}{32} = \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{720}}{32} = \frac{-
4 \pm 12\sqrt{5}}{32} = \frac{-1 \pm 3\sqrt{5}}{8}.$$
