**Proof.**

**Step 1: Identify the target measure.**

The group $SO(3)$ acts on itself by left multiplication. A Borel measure on $SO(
3)$ invariant under this action is a left Haar measure. Since $SO(3)$ is compact
, its left Haar measure is finite and normalizes to a probability measure. By un
iqueness of Haar measure on compact groups, the normalized left Haar measure $\m
u_{\mathrm{Haar}}$ is the **unique** left-invariant Borel probability measure on
 $SO(3)$. Thus the condition "the pushforward is invariant under the action of $
SO(3)$ on itself" is equivalent to requiring the pushforward to equal $\mu_{\mat
hrm{Haar}}$.

(If the action is instead conjugation, $\mu_{\mathrm{Haar}}$ is still invariant—
Haar measure on a compact group is bi-invariant, hence conjugation-invariant—so
the same target measure works.)

**Step 2: Both spaces are standard non-atomic probability spaces.**

- $\bigl([0,1]^n, \mathcal{B}, \lambda\bigr)$ is a standard probability space: i
t is a Borel probability measure on a compact metric space. It is **non-atomic**

- $\bigl(SO(3), \mathcal{B}, \mu_{\mathrm{Haar}}\bigr)$ is a standard probabilit

nsion $3$ (hence uncountable and not discrete), so every singleton has Haar meas
ure $0$.

l Descriptive Set Theory*, Theorem 17.41, or Royden, *Real Analysis*):

> *Any two non-atomic standard probability spaces are isomorphic modulo null set

$$f_*\lambda = \varphi_*(\lambda|_A) = \mu_{\mathrm{Haar}}.$$
