## Proof

**Key identity.** For any non-negative integer $i$, the Stirling numbers of the
second kind $S(i,k)$ satisfy:
$$x^i = \sum_{k=0}^{i} S(i,k)\, k!\, \binom{x}{k}.$$
This is the standard expansion of powers in the falling factorial basis $x^{\und
erline{k}} = k!\binom{x}{k}$.

**Factorization of $A$.** Since $S(i,0) = 0$ for all $i \ge 1$, the sum effectiv
ely starts at $k=1$. For $1 \le i \le n$ and $1 \le j \le n$:
$$a_{ij} = j^i = \sum_{k=1}^{n} S(i,k)\, k!\, \binom{j}{k},$$
where $S(i,k)=0$ for $k > i$ and $\binom{j}{k}=0$ for $j < k$. This gives the ma
trix factorization:
$$A = \widetilde{S} \cdot D \cdot W,$$
where:

- $\widetilde{S} \in M_n(\mathbb{Z})$ with $\widetilde{S}_{ik} = S(i,k)$ for $1
\le i,k \le n$.
- $D = \operatorname{diag}(1!,\, 2!,\, \ldots,\, n!)$.
- $W \in M_n(\mathbb{Z})$ with $W_{kj} = \binom{j}{k}$ for $1 \le k,j \le n$.

**$\widetilde{S}$ is unimodular.** Since $S(i,k) = 0$ for $k > i$, the matrix $\
widetilde{S}$ is lower triangular. Its diagonal entries are $S(i,i) = 1$ for all
 $i$. Hence $\det(\widetilde{S}) = 1$, so $\widetilde{S} \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\math
bb{Z})$.

**$W$ is unimodular.** Since $\binom{j}{k} = 0$ for $j < k$, the matrix $W$ is u
pper triangular. Its diagonal entries are $\binom{k}{k} = 1$ for all $k$. Hence
$\det(W) = 1$, so $W \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$.

**Computing the Smith normal form.** The Smith normal form is invariant under le
ft and right multiplication by unimodular matrices (these correspond to invertib
le integer row and column operations). Therefore:
$$\operatorname{SNF}(A) = \operatorname{SNF}(\widetilde{S} \cdot D \cdot W) = \o
peratorname{SNF}(D).$$

**SNF of $D$.** The matrix $D = \operatorname{diag}(1!, 2!, \ldots, n!)$ is diag
onal, and its diagonal entries satisfy the divisibility chain:
$$1!\mid 2!\mid 3!\mid \cdots \mid n!,$$
since $k! \mid (k+1)!$ for all $k \ge 1$.

For a diagonal matrix $\operatorname{diag}(d_1, \ldots, d_n)$ with $d_1 \mid d_2
 \mid \cdots \mid d_n$, the gcd of all $k \times k$ minors is $\Delta_k = d_1 d_
2 \cdots d_k$. To see this: any $k \times k$ minor is a product $\prod_{j=1}^{k}
 d_{i_j}$ with $i_1 < \cdots < i_k$, and since $i_j \ge j$ implies $d_j \mid d_{
i_j}$, we get $d_1 \cdots d_k \mid \prod d_{i_j}$; the minor $d_1 \cdots d_k$ (f
rom the first $k$ rows/columns) achieves this bound. Thus the invariant factors
are:
$$\delta_k = \frac{\Delta_k}{\Delta_{k-1}} = d_k = k!,$$
with $\Delta_0 = 1$. Therefore:
$$\operatorname{SNF}(D) = \operatorname{diag}(1!,\, 2!,\, \ldots,\, n!).$$

**Verification for small cases.**
- $n=1$: $A=(1)$, SNF $= (1) = (1!)$. ✓
- $n=2$: $A = \begin{pmatrix}1&2\\1&4\end{pmatrix}$, $\det = 2$, $\Delta_1 = \gc
d(1,2,1,4)=1$, SNF $= \operatorname{diag}(1,2) = \operatorname{diag}(1!,2!)$. ✓
- $n=3$: $A = \begin{pmatrix}1&2&3\\1&4&9\\1&8&27\end{pmatrix}$, $\det = 12$, $\
Delta_1=1$, $\Delta_2 = \gcd(2,6,30,24,36,\ldots)=2$, SNF $= \operatorname{diag}
(1,2,6) = \operatorname{diag}(1!,2!,3!)$. ✓

The factorization can also be directly verified for $n=3$:
$$\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\1&1&0\\1&3&1\end{pmatrix}}_{\widetilde{S}} \
underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&2&0\\0&0&6\end{pmatrix}}_{D} \underbrace{\beg
in{pmatrix}1&2&3\\0&1&3\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}}_{W} = \begin{pmatrix}1&2&3\\1&4&9\\
1&8&27\end{pmatrix} = A. \quad\checkmark$$

**Conclusion.** The Smith normal form of $A = (j^i)_{1 \le i,j \le n}$ is:

$$\boxed{\operatorname{SNF}(A) = \operatorname{diag}(1!,\; 2!,\; 3!,\; \ldots,\;
 n!)}.$$
