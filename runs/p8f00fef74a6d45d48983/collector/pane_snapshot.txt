Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $V^* \cong W^*$ implies $V
\cong W$ for infinite-dimensional vector spaces over a field $k$, where $V^*$ de
notes the algebraic dual.

**Answer: No.** The implication fails in the infinite-dimensional case. Below is
 a ZFC counterexample.

---

## Key Tool: The Erdős–Kaplansky Theorem

**Theorem (Erdős–Kaplansky, 1955).** *Let $D$ be a division ring and $\kappa$ an
 infinite cardinal. Then*
$$\dim_D(D^\kappa) = |D|^\kappa.$$

*The upper bound is trivial ($\dim \leq |D^\kappa| = |D|^\kappa$). The lower bou
nd is the substantive part: one constructs $|D|^\kappa$ linearly independent ele
ments in $D^\kappa$ using an almost-disjoint family of size $2^\kappa$ in $[\kap
pa]^\kappa$ and a transfinite recursion.*

**Corollary.** *If $V$ is an infinite-dimensional vector space over $k$ with $\d
im V = \kappa$, then $V^* \cong k^\kappa$ and*
$$\dim V^* = |k|^\kappa.$$

*Proof of corollary.* Choose a basis $B$ of $V$ with $|B| = \kappa$. A linear fu
nctional $f \in V^*$ is uniquely determined by its values on $B$, and every func
tion $B \to k$ extends uniquely to a linear functional. So $V^* \cong k^B \cong
k^\kappa$ as $k$-vector spaces. By Erdős–Kaplansky, $\dim V^* = |k|^\kappa$. $\s
quare$

---

## The Counterexample

Let $k$ be a field of cardinality $|k| = 2^{\aleph_1}$. Such a field exists: for
 instance, the field of rational functions $\mathbb{Q}(x_i : i \in I)$ with $|I|
 = 2^{\aleph_1}$ has cardinality $2^{\aleph_1}$ (the set of rational functions i
s built from finite subsets of $I$, giving $\sum_{n < \omega} |I|^n = |I| = 2^{\
aleph_1}$).

Define:
- $V$: a vector space over $k$ with $\dim V = \aleph_0$ (countably infinite dime
nsion).
- $W$: a vector space over $k$ with $\dim W = \aleph_1$ (uncountably infinite di
mension).

Both $V$ and $W$ are infinite-dimensional. Now compute the dimensions of their d
uals.

**Dimension of $V^*$:**
$$\dim V^* = |k|^{\aleph_0} = (2^{\aleph_1})^{\aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_1 \cdot \ale
ph_0} = 2^{\aleph_1},$$
since $\aleph_1 \cdot \aleph_0 = \aleph_1$ (product of infinite cardinals equals
 the maximum).

**Dimension of $W^*$:**
$$\dim W^* = |k|^{\aleph_1} = (2^{\aleph_1})^{\aleph_1} = 2^{\aleph_1 \cdot \ale
ph_1} = 2^{\aleph_1},$$
since $\aleph_1 \cdot \aleph_1 = \aleph_1$.

**Conclusion:**
$$\dim V^* = \dim W^* = 2^{\aleph_1}.$$

Two vector spaces over the same field are isomorphic if and only if they have th
e same dimension. Therefore:
$$V^* \cong W^*.$$

However, $\dim V = \aleph_0 \neq \aleph_1 = \dim W$, so:
$$V \not\cong W.$$

---

## Why This Works

The mechanism is that **cardinal exponentiation is not injective**. The map $\ka
ppa \mapsto |k|^\kappa$ can take the same value at two distinct infinite cardina
ls. Here, the base $|k| = 2^{\aleph_1}$ is large enough that both $\aleph_0$ and
 $\aleph_1$ are "absorbed":
$$(2^{\aleph_1})^{\aleph_0} = (2^{\aleph_1})^{\aleph_1} = 2^{\aleph_1}.$$

The exponent $\aleph_0$ or $\aleph_1$ is dominated by $\aleph_1$ in the base, so
 the result collapses to $2^{\aleph_1}$ in both cases. This collapse is what all
ows two non-isomorphic spaces to have isomorphic duals.

**Remark.** For small fields the situation is more subtle. Over a finite field (
or $\mathbb{Q}$), $\dim V^* = 2^{\dim V}$ for infinite $V$, and whether $2^{\ale
ph_0} = 2^{\aleph_1}$ is *independent of ZFC* (consistent by Easton's theorem).
So for such fields, the implication "$V^* \cong W^* \Rightarrow V \cong W$" is i
ndependent of ZFC. The counterexample above works unconditionally in ZFC by choo
sing a sufficiently large base field.
