## Proof

**No.** The conclusion $x \leq y$ does **not** follow from $\|x\| \leq \|y\|$ fo
r positive elements in a unital $C^*$-algebra. We provide an explicit counterexa
mple.

**Counterexample.** Consider the unital $C^*$-algebra $A = M_2(\mathbb{C})$, wit
h the operator norm and the usual positive cone (positive semidefinite matrices)
.

Let
$$x = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad y = \begin{pmatrix} 0
 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Both $x$ and $y$ are diagonal matrices with non-negative entries, hence both are
 positive: $x \geq 0$ and $y \geq 0$.

The spectrum of $x$ is $\{2, 0\}$, so $\|x\| = 2$. The spectrum of $y$ is $\{2,
0\}$, so $\|y\| = 2$. Thus
$$\|x\| = 2 \leq 2 = \|y\|,$$
and the hypothesis $\|x\| \leq \|y\|$ is satisfied.

Now consider the difference:
$$x - y = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

The eigenvalues of $x - y$ are $2$ and $-2$. Since $x - y$ has a negative eigenv
alue, it is **not** positive semidefinite. Therefore $x - y \not\geq 0$, i.e.,
$$x \not\leq y.$$

**Conclusion.** The norm of a positive element in a $C^*$-algebra equals its spe
ctral radius (its largest spectral value), which captures only the "size" of the
 element, not its position in the positive cone. The order relation $x \leq y$ r
equires $y - x \geq 0$, a much stronger condition that is not controlled by the
comparison of norms. The counterexample above—two positive matrices of equal nor
m that are supported on orthogonal subspaces—shows the implication fails.
