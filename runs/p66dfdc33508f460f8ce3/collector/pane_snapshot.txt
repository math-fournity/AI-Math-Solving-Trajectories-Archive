## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** The quantity $\frac{\log(\|A^{-1}\|)}{\log(\|A\|)}$ is bounded
by $n-1$, and this bound is tight.

---

**Setup.** Let $\|{\cdot}\|$ denote the operator (spectral) norm. For $A \in SL(
n,\mathbb{R})$ with $A \neq$ orthogonal (so $\|A\| > 1$), let $\sigma_1 \ge \sig
ma_2 \ge \cdots \ge \sigma_n > 0$ be the singular values. Since $\det A = 1$, we
 have $\prod_{i=1}^n \sigma_i = 1$.

The spectral norm gives $\|A\| = \sigma_1$ and $\|A^{-1}\| = 1/\sigma_n$ (the si
ngular values of $A^{-1}$ are $1/\sigma_n \ge \cdots \ge 1/\sigma_1$). Thus:

$$\frac{\log(\|A^{-1}\|)}{\log(\|A\|)} = \frac{-\log \sigma_n}{\log \sigma_1}.$$

Set $a_i = \log \sigma_i$, so $a_1 \ge a_2 \ge \cdots \ge a_n$ and $\sum_{i=1}^n
 a_i = 0$, with $a_1 > 0$ (since $\|A\| > 1$) and $a_n < 0$.

**Upper bound.** Since $a_i \le a_1$ for all $i$:

$$-a_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} a_i \le (n-1)\,a_1.$$

Therefore:

$$\frac{-a_n}{a_1} \le n-1.$$

**Tightness.** The bound $n-1$ is achieved. For any $\lambda > 1$, consider the
diagonal matrix:

$$A_\lambda = \operatorname{diag}(\underbrace{\lambda, \ldots, \lambda}_{n-1},\;
 \lambda^{-(n-1)}).$$

Then $\det A_\lambda = \lambda^{n-1} \cdot \lambda^{-(n-1)} = 1$, so $A_\lambda
\in SL(n,\mathbb{R})$. The singular values are $\sigma_1 = \cdots = \sigma_{n-1}
 = \lambda$ and $\sigma_n = \lambda^{-(n-1)}$, giving:

$$\frac{\log(\|A_\lambda^{-1}\|)}{\log(\|A_\lambda\|)} = \frac{(n-1)\log \lambda
}{\log \lambda} = n-1.$$

**Conclusion.** For all $A \in SL(n,\mathbb{R})$ with $\|A\| > 1$:

$$\frac{\log(\|A^{-1}\|)}{\log(\|A\|)} \le n-1,$$

and equality is attained by the family $A_\lambda$ above. Hence the quantity is
bounded by a constant depending only on $n$, namely $n-1$. $\blacksquare$
