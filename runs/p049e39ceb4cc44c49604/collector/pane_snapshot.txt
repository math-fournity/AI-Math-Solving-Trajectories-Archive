Looking at this problem, I need to find all positive integers $k$ where the two
parabolas intersect in four distinct concyclic points with circle radius $\leq 2
1$.

**Setup.** Let $u = x$, $v = y - 20$, and $K = k + 20$. The parabolas become:
- $v = u^2 - K$ (so $u^2 = v + K$)
- $u = 2v^2 - k$

**The intersection quartic.** Substituting $u = 2v^2 - k$ into $u^2 = v + K$:
$$(2v^2 - k)^2 = v + k + 20$$
$$f(v) = (2v^2 - k)^2 - (v + K) = 4v^4 - 4kv^2 - v + k^2 - k - 20 = 0$$

Four distinct real roots of this quartic give four distinct intersection points
(each root $v_i$ yields a unique point $(u_i, v_i) = (2v_i^2 - k,\, v_i)$).

**Concyclicity is automatic.** A general circle $u^2 + v^2 + Du + Ev + F = 0$ at
 each intersection point becomes:
$$(v_i + K) + v_i^2 + D(2v_i^2 - k) + Ev_i + F = (1+2D)v_i^2 + (1+E)v_i + (K - D
k + F) = 0$$
This is a quadratic in $v_i$ that vanishes at all four distinct roots $v_i$. A n
onzero degree-$\leq 2$ polynomial has at most 2 roots, so it must be identically
 zero:
$$D = -\tfrac{1}{2},\quad E = -1,\quad F = -\tfrac{3k}{2} - 20$$

So the four points **always** lie on a circle with center $\left(\frac{1}{4}, \f
rac{1}{2}\right)$ and:
$$r^2 = \frac{D^2 + E^2}{4} - F = \frac{5}{16} + \frac{3k}{2} + 20 = \frac{3k}{2
} + \frac{325}{16}$$

**Radius condition.** We need $r^2 \leq 441$:
$$\frac{3k}{2} + \frac{325}{16} \leq 441 \implies k \leq \frac{6731}{24} = 280.4
58\ldots$$
So $k \leq 280$ (for positive integers). At $k = 280$: $r^2 = 440.3125$, $r \app
rox 20.98 \leq 21$ ✓. At $k = 281$: $r^2 = 441.8125$, $r \approx 21.02 > 21$ ✗.

**Four-distinct-roots condition.** Write $f(v) = g(v) - h(v)$ where $g(v) = (2v^
2 - k)^2$ (a W-shaped curve) and $h(v) = v + K$ (a line of slope 1). The W-curve
 has minima $g(\pm\sqrt{k/2}) = 0$ and local maximum $g(0) = k^2$.

For four crossings, the line must pass **below** the central bump and **above**
both minima:
- **Above minima:** $h(\pm\sqrt{k/2}) = K \mp \sqrt{k/2} > 0$ for all $k \geq 1$
 ✓
- **Below central bump:** $h(0) = k + 20 < k^2 = g(0)$, i.e., $k^2 - k - 20 > 0$
, i.e., $(k-5)(k+4) > 0$, giving $k > 5$.

On each monotonic segment of $g$, the sign change of $g - h$ guarantees exactly
one transversal crossing (and since the quartic has at most 4 roots, each segmen
t contributes exactly one). So for all $k \geq 6$: four distinct roots ✓.

**Boundary case $k = 5$:** $f(v) = (2v^2 - 5)^2 - (v + 25) = 4v^4 - 20v^2 - v =
v(4v^3 - 20v - 1)$. The root $v = 0$ is simple ($f'(0) = -1 \neq 0$). The cubic
$4v^3 - 20v - 1 = 0$ has discriminant $500 - 27/16 > 0$, giving three distinct r
eal roots, none equal to 0. So $k = 5$ yields **four distinct roots** ✓, with $r
^2 = 445/16 \approx 27.8$, $r \approx 5.27 \leq 21$ ✓.

**For $k \leq 4$:** $k + 20 > k^2$, so the line lies above the central bump — on
ly 2 intersections. Not in $S$ ✗.

**Conclusion.** $S = \{5, 6, 7, \ldots, 280\}$.

- Greatest element: $280$
- Sum: $5 + 280 = \boxed{285}$
