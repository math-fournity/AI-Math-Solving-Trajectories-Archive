## Proof

We show that such a constant $c$ exists; in fact $c = \tfrac{1}{4}$ works.

**Step 1: Relate $\binom{2n}{n+10}$ to the central binomial coefficient $\binom{
2n}{n}$.**

For $n \ge 10$,
$$
\frac{\binom{2n}{n+10}}{\binom{2n}{n}}
= \frac{n!\,n!}{(n+10)!\,(n-10)!}
= \prod_{k=1}^{10}\frac{n-k+1}{n+k}.
$$

Each factor satisfies $\frac{n-k+1}{n+k}\to 1$ as $n\to\infty$, so the finite pr
oduct
$$
R(n):=\prod_{k=1}^{10}\frac{n-k+1}{n+k}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}1.
$$
In particular there exists $N_1$ such that for all $n\ge N_1$, $R(n)\ge \tfrac12
$.

**Step 2: Lower bound for the central binomial coefficient.**

By Stirling's approximation,
$$
\binom{2n}{n} \sim \frac{4^n}{\sqrt{\pi n}},
\qquad\text{i.e.}\quad
\binom{2n}{n} = \frac{4^n}{\sqrt{\pi n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{8n}+O(n^{-2})\right).
$$
Hence $\binom{2n}{n}\Big/\frac{4^n}{\sqrt{\pi n}}\to 1$, so there exists $N_2$ s
uch that for all $n\ge N_2$,
$$
\binom{2n}{n}\ge \frac12\cdot\frac{4^n}{\sqrt{\pi n}}.
$$

**Step 3: Combine.**

For $n\ge \max(N_1,N_2,10)$,
$$
\binom{2n}{n+10}
= R(n)\cdot\binom{2n}{n}
\ge \frac12\cdot\frac12\cdot\frac{4^n}{\sqrt{\pi n}}
= \frac14\cdot\frac{2^{2n}}{\sqrt{\pi n}}.
$$

Thus the inequality holds for all sufficiently large $n$ with $c=\tfrac14$ (inde
ed any $c<1$ works by the same argument, since both ratios tend to $1$).
