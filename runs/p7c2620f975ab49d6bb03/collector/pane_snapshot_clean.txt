Definitions

A hyperkähler structure on a Riemannian manifold $(M, g)$ consists of three
endomorphism fields $I, J, K \in \Gamma(\mathrm{End}(TM))$ satisfying the
quaternionic relations $$ I^2 = J^2 = K^2 = IJK = -\mathrm{id}_{TM}, $$ such
that $g$ is Hermitian (Kähler) with respect to each of $I$, $J$, $K$, and each
of $I$, $J$, $K$ is parallel with respect to the Levi-Civita connection of $g$.

Equivalently, the holonomy group of $g$ is contained in $\mathrm{Sp}(n)$, which
is the subgroup of $\mathrm{SO}(4n)$ preserving a triple of quaternionic complex
structures on $\mathbb{R}^{4n}$.

Key Lemma: The Real Dimension of a Hyperkähler Manifold Is Divisible by 4

Proof of lemma. Let $(M^{2m}, g, I, J, K)$ be a hyperkähler manifold. At any
point $p \in M$, the tangent space $T_p M$ is a real vector space of dimension
$2m$ equipped with linear maps $I_p, J_p, K_p$ satisfying $$ I_p^2 = J_p^2 =
K_p^2 = I_p J_p K_p = -\mathrm{id}. $$ These relations are precisely the
defining relations of the quaternion algebra $\mathbb{H}$. Concretely, setting $
\mathbf{1} = \mathrm{id}$, $\mathbf{i} = I_p$, $\mathbf{j} = J_p$, $\mathbf{k} =
K_p$, the map $$ a + b,\mathbf{i} + c,\mathbf{j} + d,\mathbf{k} ;\longmapsto; a,
\mathrm{id} + b,I_p + c,J_p + d,K_p $$ defines a representation of $\mathbb{H}$
on $T_p M$, i.e., $T_p M$ is a left $\mathbb{H}$-module.

Since $\mathbb{H}$ is a division algebra of real dimension $4$, every (non-
trivial, finite-dimensional) left $\mathbb{H}$-module is isomorphic to $
\mathbb{H}^n$ for some integer $n \geq 1$, and therefore has real dimension
$4n$. Hence $$ \dim_{\mathbb{R}} T_p M = 4n \quad \text{for some } n \in
\mathbb{Z}{\geq 1}. $$ Since this holds at every point, $\dim{\mathbb{R}} M =
4n$ with $n \geq 1$, i.e., $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} M \in {4, 8, 12, \dots}$.

(Equivalently, from the holonomy characterization: $\mathrm{Sp}(n) \subset
\mathrm{SO}(4n)$ acts faithfully on $\mathbb{R}^{4n}$, and there is no embedding
$\mathrm{Sp}(n) \hookrightarrow \mathrm{SO}(2)$ for any $n$, since $\mathrm{SO}
(2) \cong U(1)$ is abelian while $\mathrm{Sp}(n)$ is non-abelian for $n \geq 1$,
and $\mathrm{Sp}(0)$ is trivial acting on $\mathbb{R}^0$.) $\square$

Application to Dimension Two

A manifold of two real dimensions has $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} M = 2$. By the lemma,
any hyperkähler manifold must have real dimension $4n$ for some integer $n \geq
1$, so $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} M \in {4, 8, 12, \dots}$.

Since $$ 2 \notin {4n : n \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}} = {4, 8, 12, \dots}, $$ a 2-
real-dimensional manifold cannot admit a hyperkähler structure.

Additional Perspective (Complex Structures)

One can also see the obstruction directly: on a 2-real-dimensional (oriented)
manifold, the space of orthogonal complex structures at a point consists of
exactly two elements ${I, -I}$ (a rotation by $\pm\pi/2$ in $T_pM \cong
\mathbb{R}^2$). A hyperkähler structure requires three complex structures $I, J,
K$ with $I \neq \pm J$ (since $IJ = K$ and $K \neq \pm I$ generically), which is
impossible when only two complex structures exist. The quaternionic relations
force the complex structures to be distinct and non-antipodal, requiring at
least 4 real dimensions.

Conclusion

No, a hyperkähler structure cannot exist on a manifold of two real dimensions.
The quaternionic structure underlying any hyperkähler manifold forces the real
dimension to be a positive multiple of $4$, and $2$ is not a multiple of $4$.
