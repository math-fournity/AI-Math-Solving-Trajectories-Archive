## Solution

**Step 1: Reduce to unit intervals.**

Write $x = k + t$ with $t \in [0,1]$. Since $|\sin(\pi(k+t))| = \sin(\pi t)$ for
 $t \in [0,1]$,

$$\int_0^n |\sin(\pi x)|^x\,dx = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} I_k, \qquad I_k := \int_0^1 (\
sin \pi t)^{k+t}\,dt.$$

**Step 2: Asymptotics of $I_k$.**

Set $u = t - \tfrac12$. Near $u=0$, $\sin(\pi t)=\cos(\pi u)=1-\frac{\pi^2 u^2}{
2}+O(u^4)$, so

$$(k+t)\ln\sin(\pi t) = \bigl(k+\tfrac12+u\bigr)\!\left(-\tfrac{\pi^2 u^2}{2}+O(
u^4)\right) = -\tfrac{(k+1/2)\pi^2 u^2}{2} + O(ku^4)+O(u^3).$$

The Gaussian width is $\sigma \sim 1/\sqrt{k}$, so in the effective region $|u|\
lesssim 1/\sqrt{k}$ the correction terms are $O(k^{-1/2})$. Therefore

$$I_k \sim \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-(k+1/2)\pi^2 u^2/2}\,du = \sqrt{\frac{2}{
\pi(k+1/2)}} \sim \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi k}} \qquad (k\to\infty).$$

A rigorous justification: split $[0,1]$ into $|u|\le k^{-2/5}$ and the complemen
t. On the central part, Taylor expansion with uniform error bounds gives the Gau
ssian integral $\sim\sqrt{2/(\pi k)}$. On the complement, $\sin(\pi t)\le e^{-c
k^{1/5}}$ for some $c>0$, contributing $o(k^{-N})$ for all $N$. Hence $I_k \sim
\sqrt{2/(\pi k)}$.

**Step 3: Growth of the partial sums.**

Let $S_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} I_k$. Since $I_k \sim c\,k^{-1/2}$ with $c=\sqrt{2/\
pi}$ and $\sum_{k=1}^{n-1} k^{-1/2}\sim 2\sqrt{n}$,

$$S_n \sim 2c\sqrt{n} = 2\sqrt{\frac{2n}{\pi}}.$$

(Formally: $I_k/k^{-1/2}\to c>0$, so by Stolz–Cesàro / limit comparison of parti
al sums, $S_n/\sqrt{n}\to 2c$.)

**Step 4: Apply the limit comparison test.**

$$a_n = \frac{S_n}{n^p} \sim 2\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\; n^{1/2-p}.$$

Since $a_n\big/\,n^{1/2-p}\to 2\sqrt{2/\pi}>0$, the series $\sum a_n$ converges
if and only if $\sum n^{1/2-p}$ converges, i.e., if and only if

$$\frac12 - p < -1 \iff p > \frac32.$$

- **$p>3/2$:** $a_n\sim C\,n^{1/2-p}$ with exponent $< -1$; converges.
- **$p=3/2$:** $a_n\sim C/n$ with $C=2\sqrt{2/\pi}>0$; diverges (harmonic).
- **$p<3/2$:** exponent $1/2-p\ge -1$; diverges.

**Conclusion.** The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$ converges if and
 only if $\,p>\dfrac32$.
