## Solution

Let $a$ denote the edge length of the regular tetrahedron $ABCD$.

### Step 1: Identify the isometry $\mathcal{Z}$

The map $A \to B \to C \to D \to A$ is the 4-cycle $(ABCD)$. The rotational symm
etry group of the tetrahedron is $A_4$, which contains no 4-cycles (its elements
 have orders 1, 2, 3). Therefore $\mathcal{Z}$ is an **orientation-reversing** i
sometry — a rotoreflection.

### Step 2: Coordinate setup

Place the tetrahedron with centroid at the origin:
$$A=(1,1,1),\; B=(1,-1,-1),\; C=(-1,1,-1),\; D=(-1,-1,1).$$
Edge length $a_0 = 2\sqrt{2}$, and $A+B+C+D=0$, so $\mathcal{Z}$ is linear: $\ma
thcal{Z}(\mathbf{x})=M\mathbf{x}$.

Solving $MA=B,\; MB=C,\; MC=D$ (with $D=-A-B-C$ guaranteeing $MD=A$):
$$M=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\0&-1&0\\-1&0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \mathcal{Z}(x,y,z)=
(z,-y,-x).$$
(Check: $\det M = -1$, confirming orientation reversal. Eigenvalues $-1, \pm i$:
 a $90°$ rotation about the $y$-axis composed with reflection in the $xz$-plane.
)

### Step 3: Parametrize face $ABC$

A point $X$ on face $ABC$ has barycentric coordinates $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ wi
th $\alpha+\beta+\gamma=1$, $\alpha,\beta,\gamma\geq 0$:
$$X = \alpha A + \beta B + \gamma C = (\alpha+\beta-\gamma,\;\alpha-\beta+\gamma
,\;\alpha-\beta-\gamma).$$

Then:
$$\mathcal{Z}(X) = (\alpha-\beta-\gamma,\;-\alpha+\beta-\gamma,\;-\alpha-\beta+\
gamma),$$
$$X - \mathcal{Z}(X) = \bigl(2\beta,\;2(\alpha-\beta+\gamma),\;2(\alpha-\gamma)\
bigr).$$

### Step 4: Compute $|X-\mathcal{Z}(X)|^2$

$$|X-\mathcal{Z}(X)|^2 = 4\beta^2 + 4(\alpha-\beta+\gamma)^2 + 4(\alpha-\gamma)^
2.$$

Set $u = \alpha - \gamma$. Since $\alpha+\beta+\gamma=1$, we get $\alpha-\beta+\
gamma = 1-2\beta$, so:
$$|X-\mathcal{Z}(X)|^2 = 4\bigl[\beta^2 + (1-2\beta)^2 + u^2\bigr] = 4\bigl[5\be
ta^2 - 4\beta + 1 + u^2\bigr].$$

Completing the square: $5\beta^2 - 4\beta + 1 = 5\!\left(\beta - \tfrac{2}{5}\ri
ght)^2 + \tfrac{1}{5}$, giving:
$$|X-\mathcal{Z}(X)|^2 = 4\!\left[u^2 + 5\!\left(\beta-\tfrac{2}{5}\right)^2 + \
tfrac{1}{5}\right].$$

### Step 5: The locus is an ellipse

The condition $|X-\mathcal{Z}(X)|=t$ becomes (for edge length $a_0=2\sqrt{2}$):
$$u^2 + 5\!\left(\beta - \tfrac{2}{5}\right)^2 = \frac{5t^2-4}{20}.$$

For general edge length $a$, scaling by $a/a_0$ gives:
$$u^2 + 5\!\left(\beta-\tfrac{2}{5}\right)^2 = \frac{10t^2 - a^2}{5a^2}.\tag{$\s
tar$}$$

**Geometry on the face.** Set 2D coordinates with the midpoint of $AC$ at the or
igin, $AC$ along the $x$-axis, $B$ on the positive $y$-axis at height $h = a\sqr
t{3}/2$. Then $\beta = \frac{2y}{a\sqrt{3}}$ and $u = -\frac{2x}{a}$. Substituti
ng into $(\star)$:
$$4x^2 + \frac{20}{3}\!\left(y - \frac{a\sqrt{3}}{5}\right)^2 = \frac{10t^2-a^2}
{5}.\tag{$\star\star$}$$

This is an **ellipse** centered at the point $P = \bigl(0,\,\frac{a\sqrt{3}}{5}\
bigr)$, which has barycentric coordinates $\left(\frac{3}{10},\,\frac{2}{5},\,\f
rac{3}{10}\right)$ — the point on the median from $B$ to the midpoint of $AC$, a
t $\frac{2}{5}$ of the way from $AC$ toward $B$.

The semi-axes are:
- **Parallel to $AC$ (major axis):** $\rho = \sqrt{\dfrac{10t^2-a^2}{20}}$,
- **Along the median from $B$ (minor axis):** $\rho\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{5}} = \sqrt{\
dfrac{3(10t^2-a^2)}{100}}$.

> **The set $\mathcal{M}$ is the intersection of face $ABC$ with this ellipse.**

### Step 6: Nonemptiness conditions

**Lower bound.** The right-hand side of $(\star)$ must be $\geq 0$:
$$10t^2 \geq a^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t \geq \frac{a}{\sqrt{10}}.$$

At $t = a/\sqrt{10}$, the ellipse degenerates to the single point $P$, which lie
s in the **interior** of face $ABC$ (since $0 < \frac{2}{5} < 1$ and $|\alpha-\g
amma|=0 < 1-\beta$). So $\mathcal{M}\neq\emptyset$.

*Verification of the minimum:* At $P$, $X = \frac{3}{10}A+\frac{2}{5}B+\frac{3}{
10}C = (\frac{2}{5},\frac{1}{5},-\frac{2}{5})$, $\mathcal{Z}(X)=(-\frac{2}{5},-\
frac{1}{5},-\frac{2}{5})$, so $|X-\mathcal{Z}(X)|^2 = \frac{16+4}{25}=\frac{4}{5
}$, giving $|X-\mathcal{Z}(X)|=\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}=\frac{a}{\sqrt{10}}$. ✓

**Upper bound.** The maximum of $|X-\mathcal{Z}(X)|$ on the face: maximize $5\be
ta^2-4\beta+1+u^2$ subject to $0\leq\beta\leq 1$, $u^2\leq(1-\beta)^2$. For fixe
d $\beta$, take $u^2=(1-\beta)^2$, giving $6\beta^2-6\beta+2$, maximized at $\be
ta=0$ or $\beta=1$ with value $2$. Thus $|X-\mathcal{Z}(X)|^2 \leq 4\cdot 2 = 8
= a^2$ (for $a=2\sqrt{2}$), i.e., $|X-\mathcal{Z}(X)|\leq a$.

Equality holds exactly at the three vertices: $|\mathcal{Z}(A)-A|=|B-A|=a$, etc.
 ✓

**Intermediate values.** The function $X\mapsto|X-\mathcal{Z}(X)|$ is continuous
 on the compact, connected face $ABC$, with minimum $a/\sqrt{10}$ (at the interi
or point $P$) and maximum $a$ (at the vertices). By the Intermediate Value Theor
em, every value in $\left[\frac{a}{\sqrt{10}},\,a\right]$ is attained, and no va
lue outside this interval is.

### Conclusion

The set $\mathcal{M}$ consists of all points $X$ on face $ABC$ lying on the elli
pse centered at the point $P$ with barycentric coordinates $\left(\frac{3}{10},\
frac{2}{5},\frac{3}{10}\right)$ (on the median from $B$, at $\frac{2}{5}$ from e
dge $AC$), with major axis parallel to $AC$ of semi-axis $\sqrt{\frac{10t^2-a^2}
{20}}$ and minor axis along the median of semi-axis $\sqrt{\frac{3(10t^2-a^2)}{1
00}}$, where $a$ is the edge length of the tetrahedron. Explicitly, $\mathcal{M}
$ is the intersection of this ellipse with the triangular face $ABC$.

The set $\mathcal{M}$ is **nonempty if and only if**
$$\boxed{\dfrac{a}{\sqrt{10}} \;\leq\; t \;\leq\; a.}$$

At $t = a/\sqrt{10}$, $\mathcal{M}$ is the single point $P$; at $t=a$, $\mathcal
{M}$ is the three vertices $\{A,B,C\}$; for intermediate values, $\mathcal{M}$ i
s (a portion of) the ellipse within the face.
