## Analysis

**Question:** In Geometric Algebra, does $A\tilde A = 0$ necessarily imply $\til
de A A = 0$?

**Short answer:** **No, not in general.** The implication holds in Euclidean GA
$\mathrm{Cl}(n,0)$ (trivially, because $A\tilde A=0$ forces $A=0$), but **fails
in spacetime algebra** (indefinite signature). We give an explicit counterexampl
e in STA.

---

## 1. Reversion does not relate the two products

Recall reversion is an involution ($\tilde{\tilde A}=A$), linear, fixes scalars
and vectors, and satisfies $(XY)^\sim=\tilde Y\tilde X$. Hence

$$\widetilde{A\tilde A}=\tilde{\tilde A}\,\tilde A=A\tilde A,\qquad \widetilde{\
tilde A A}=\tilde A\,\tilde{\tilde A}=\tilde A A,$$

so **both** $A\tilde A$ and $\tilde A A$ are *individually* reversion-symmetric.
 Reversion does **not** send one to the other, so it gives no implication betwee
n them. The two products are genuinely different multivectors in general.

---

## 2. Euclidean case: the implication holds (trivially)

Let $\mathrm{Cl}(n,0)$ with $e_i^2=+1$. Write $A=\sum_{r=0}^{n}A_r$ by grade. Si
nce $\langle A_r\tilde A_s\rangle_0=0$ for $r\neq s$ (different grades produce n
o scalar part),

$$\langle A\tilde A\rangle_0=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\langle A_r\tilde A_r\rangle_0.$$

For a grade-$r$ part, $\tilde A_r=(-1)^{r(r-1)/2}A_r$ and $\langle A_r\tilde A_r
\rangle_0=\|A_r\|^2\ge 0$ (the squared norm, positive definite in Euclidean sign
ature). Therefore

$$\langle A\tilde A\rangle_0=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\|A_r\|^2=\|A\|^2\ge 0,$$

with equality **iff** every $A_r=0$, i.e. $A=0$.

Consequently, in Euclidean GA,

$$A\tilde A=0\;\Longrightarrow\;\langle A\tilde A\rangle_0=0\;\Longrightarrow\;A
=0\;\Longrightarrow\;\tilde A A=0.$$

The implication holds, but only vacuously: the hypothesis already forces $A=0$.

---

## 3. Spacetime algebra: the implication FAILS — explicit counterexample

Work in STA $\mathrm{Cl}(1,3)$ with $\gamma_0^2=+1$ and $\gamma_i^2=-1$ for $i=1
,2,3$. Consider the subalgebra generated by $\gamma_0,\gamma_1$ (signature $(1,1
)$, indefinite). Set

$$\boxed{\,A=1+\gamma_0+\gamma_1+\gamma_0\gamma_1\,}.$$

Reversion fixes $1,\gamma_0,\gamma_1$ and sends $\gamma_0\gamma_1\mapsto\gamma_1
\gamma_0=-\gamma_0\gamma_1$, so

$$\tilde A=1+\gamma_0+\gamma_1-\gamma_0\gamma_1.$$

Let $u=1+\gamma_0+\gamma_1$ and $P=\gamma_0\gamma_1$, so $A=u+P$ and $\tilde A=u
-P$. Using $\gamma_0^2=1$, $\gamma_1^2=-1$, $\gamma_0\gamma_1=-\gamma_1\gamma_0$
:

$$u^2=1+\gamma_0^2+\gamma_1^2+2\gamma_0+2\gamma_1=1+2\gamma_0+2\gamma_1,$$
$$P^2=\gamma_0\gamma_1\gamma_0\gamma_1=-\gamma_0^2\gamma_1^2=-(1)(-1)=1,$$
$$uP=(1+\gamma_0+\gamma_1)\gamma_0\gamma_1=\gamma_0\gamma_1+\gamma_1+\gamma_0,$$
$$Pu=\gamma_0\gamma_1(1+\gamma_0+\gamma_1)=\gamma_0\gamma_1-\gamma_1-\gamma_0.$$

Now compute the two products:

$$A\tilde A=(u+P)(u-P)=u^2-uP+Pu-P^2$$
$$=(1+2\gamma_0+2\gamma_1)-(\gamma_0\gamma_1+\gamma_1+\gamma_0)+(\gamma_0\gamma_
1-\gamma_1-\gamma_0)-1=\mathbf{0}.$$

$$\tilde A A=(u-P)(u+P)=u^2+uP-Pu-P^2$$
$$=(1+2\gamma_0+2\gamma_1)+(\gamma_0\gamma_1+\gamma_1+\gamma_0)-(\gamma_0\gamma_
1-\gamma_1-\gamma_0)-1=4(\gamma_0+\gamma_1)\neq\mathbf{0}.$$

Thus

$$A\tilde A=0\quad\text{but}\quad \tilde A A=4(\gamma_0+\gamma_1)\neq 0.$$

This is a concrete, nonzero multivector in spacetime algebra for which $A\tilde
A=0$ yet $\tilde A A\neq 0$.

---

## 4. Why the difference

In Euclidean GA, $\langle A\tilde A\rangle_0=\|A\|^2$ is a **positive-definite**
 scalar, so $A\tilde A=0$ collapses to $A=0$ and the implication is vacuous.

In spacetime algebra the metric is **indefinite**, so $\langle A\tilde A\rangle_
0$ is no longer positive-definite: there exist nonzero "null-like" multivectors
with $A\tilde A=0$. For such $A$, the reversion-symmetric products $A\tilde A$ a
nd $\tilde A A$ need not coincide (reversion fixes each of them separately but d
oes not exchange them), and indeed the counterexample above has $A\tilde A=0$ wh
ile $\tilde A A$ is a nonzero vector.

---

## Conclusion

The implication $A\tilde A=0\Rightarrow\tilde A A=0$ does **not** hold in genera
l.

- **Euclidean GA $\mathrm{Cl}(n,0)$:** holds, but only vacuously — $A\tilde A=0$
 forces $A=0$ because $\langle A\tilde A\rangle_0=\|A\|^2>0$ for $A\neq 0$.
- **Spacetime algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(1,3)$:** **fails**. The multivector $A=1+\gam
ma_0+\gamma_1+\gamma_0\gamma_1$ satisfies $A\tilde A=0$ while $\tilde A A=4(\gam
ma_0+\gamma_1)\neq 0$.

The root cause is that reversion makes each of $A\tilde A$ and $\tilde A A$ indi
vidually symmetric without relating them to each other; only the positive-defini
teness of the Euclidean norm saves the implication there, and that positive-defi
niteness is lost in indefinite signature.
