## Answer

**No**, there is not necessarily such a projector. We give an explicit counterex
ample.

### Key reduction

Let $p$ be a rank‑1 projector. Write $p = v\alpha$, where $\operatorname{im} p =
 \mathbb{C}v$ (with $pv=v$) and $\alpha\in V^{*}$ with $\ker\alpha = \ker p$ and
 $\alpha(v)=1$. Then for any endomorphism $T$,
$$
pTp(x)=p\bigl(T(\alpha(x)v)\bigr)=\alpha(x)\,\alpha(Tv)\,v=\alpha(Tv)\cdot p(x),
$$
so
$$
pTp = \alpha(Tv)\cdot p = \operatorname{tr}(pT)\cdot p.
$$
Hence
$$
pTp = 0 \iff \operatorname{tr}(pT)=0.
$$
The question is therefore equivalent to: *if $\operatorname{tr}(pT)=0$ for every
 $p\in\mathscr S$ and $\sum_{p\in\mathscr S}pV=V$, must $\operatorname{tr}T=0$?*
 We show the answer is **no** by counterexample.

### Counterexample

Take $V=\mathbb C^2$ with basis $e_1,e_2$. Let $\mathscr S=\{p_1,p_2\}$ where

- $p_1$ is the projection onto $\mathbb C e_1$ along $\mathbb C e_2$:
  $$
  p_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
  $$
- $p_2$ is the projection onto $\mathbb C(e_1+e_2)$ along $\mathbb C e_1$:
  $$
  p_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
  $$

Both are rank‑1 projectors ($p_i^2=p_i$, $\operatorname{rank}p_i=1$). Their imag
es are $\mathbb C e_1$ and $\mathbb C(e_1+e_2)$, which are linearly independent,
 so
$$
p_1V+p_2V=\mathbb C e_1+\mathbb C(e_1+e_2)=\mathbb C^2=V.
$$

Now set
$$
T=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \operatorname{tr}T = -1\neq 0.
$$

Check $p_1Tp_1$:
$$
Tp_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}=\
begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
p_1Tp_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
=0.
$$

Check $p_2Tp_2$:
$$
Tp_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}=\
begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
p_2Tp_2=p_2\cdot 0=0.
$$

(Consistent with the reduction: $\operatorname{tr}(p_1T)=T_{11}=0$ and $\operato
rname{tr}(p_2T)=\alpha_2(Tv_2)=T_{21}+T_{22}=1+(-1)=0$.)

### Conclusion

We have produced a finite‑dimensional $\mathbb C$‑vector space $V$, a collection
 $\mathscr S$ of rank‑1 projectors with $\sum_{p\in\mathscr S}pV=V$, and an endo
morphism $T$ with $\operatorname{tr}T\neq 0$, yet $pTp=0$ for **every** $p\in\ma
thscr S$. Therefore such a projector does **not** necessarily exist.
