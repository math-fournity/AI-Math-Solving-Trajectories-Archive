## Proof

Let $S = \omega_1$, the smallest uncountable ordinal. We seek the cardinality of
$$\mathcal{F} = \{f:\omega_1\to\mathbb{R} : \operatorname{supp}(f)=\{\alpha<\ome
ga_1:f(\alpha)\neq 0\}\text{ is countable}\}.$$

**Step 1: Use CH to identify the continuum with $\aleph_1$.**

By the Continuum Hypothesis, $|\mathbb{R}|=2^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_1$. Hence $|\math
bb{R}|=|\omega_1|=\aleph_1$.

**Step 2: Count the countable subsets of $\omega_1$.**

Let $[\omega_1]^{\le\aleph_0}$ denote the collection of countable subsets of $\o
mega_1$.

- *Upper bound.* Every countable subset is the range of a function $\omega\to\om
ega_1$, so
$$\left|[\omega_1]^{\le\aleph_0}\right|\le |\omega_1|^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_1^{\alep
h_0}.$$
Under CH, $\aleph_1=2^{\aleph_0}$, so
$$\aleph_1^{\aleph_0}=(2^{\aleph_0})^{\aleph_0}=2^{\aleph_0\cdot\aleph_0}=2^{\al
eph_0}=\aleph_1.$$

- *Lower bound.* Every countable ordinal $\alpha<\omega_1$ is itself a countable
 subset of $\omega_1$, and there are $\aleph_1$ many such ordinals. Thus $\left|
[\omega_1]^{\le\aleph_0}\right|\ge\aleph_1$.

Therefore $\left|[\omega_1]^{\le\aleph_0}\right|=\aleph_1$.

**Step 3: Upper bound for $|\mathcal{F}|$.**

Each $f\in\mathcal{F}$ is determined by its (countable) support $A\subseteq\omeg
a_1$ and the nonzero values $f|_A:A\to\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}$. Hence
$$|\mathcal{F}|\le \sum_{A\in[\omega_1]^{\le\aleph_0}}|\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}|
^{|A|}.$$
For each countable $A$, $|\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}|^{|A|}\le\aleph_1^{\aleph_0}=
\aleph_1$ (by Step 2's computation). Since there are $\aleph_1$ many such $A$,
$$|\mathcal{F}|\le \aleph_1\cdot\aleph_1=\aleph_1.$$

**Step 4: Lower bound for $|\mathcal{F}|$.**

Consider functions supported on a single point: for each $\alpha<\omega_1$ and e
ach nonzero real $r\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}$, define $f_{\alpha,r}(\beta)=r$
if $\beta=\alpha$, and $0$ otherwise. These are all distinct elements of $\mathc
al{F}$. The number of such pairs is
$$|\omega_1|\cdot|\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}|=\aleph_1\cdot\aleph_1=\aleph_1.$$
Thus $|\mathcal{F}|\ge\aleph_1$.

**Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 3 and 4,
$$|\mathcal{F}|=\aleph_1.$$

Under CH, $\aleph_1=2^{\aleph_0}=|\mathbb{R}|$, so equivalently the cardinality
equals the cardinality of the continuum.
