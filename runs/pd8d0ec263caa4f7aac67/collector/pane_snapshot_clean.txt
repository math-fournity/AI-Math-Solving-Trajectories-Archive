## Solution

We evaluate the integral using the **Residue Theorem**. The contour $C$ is $|z|=
3$, which encloses all three singularities of the integrand at $z=0$, $z=1$, and
 $z=2$.

Let $g(z) = 1 + z + z^2$ and
$$f(z) = g(z)\left(e^{1/z} + e^{1/(z-1)} + e^{1/(z-2)}\right).$$

Each exponential term $e^{1/(z-a)}$ has an essential singularity at $z=a$ and is
 analytic elsewhere. Thus the residue at each singularity $z=a$ receives contrib
utions **only** from the term $g(z)\,e^{1/(z-a)}$, since the other two exponenti
al terms are analytic at $z=a$.

### Residue at $z = 0$

Using $e^{1/z} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{z^{-n}}{n!}$, the coefficient of $z^{
-1}$ in $g(z)\,e^{1/z}$ is:

| Term of $g$ | Power condition | $n$ | Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1 \cdot z^{-n}/n!$ | $-n=-1$ | $1$ | $1/1! = 1$ |
| $z \cdot z^{-n}/n!$ | $1-n=-1$ | $2$ | $1/2! = 1/2$ |
| $z^2 \cdot z^{-n}/n!$ | $2-n=-1$ | $3$ | $1/3! = 1/6$ |

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f = 1 + \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{5}{3}.$$

### Residue at $z = 1$

Set $w = z-1$, so $z = 1+w$ and $g(1+w) = 1+(1+w)+(1+w)^2 = 3 + 3w + w^2$. The c
oefficient of $w^{-1}$ in $(3+3w+w^2)\,e^{1/w}$ is:

| Term | $n$ | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| $3 \cdot w^{-n}/n!$ | $1$ | $3/1! = 3$ |
| $3w \cdot w^{-n}/n!$ | $2$ | $3/2! = 3/2$ |
| $w^2 \cdot w^{-n}/n!$ | $3$ | $1/3! = 1/6$ |

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=1} f = 3 + \frac{3}{2} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{14}{3}.$$

### Residue at $z = 2$

Set $w = z-2$, so $z = 2+w$ and $g(2+w) = 1+(2+w)+(2+w)^2 = 7 + 5w + w^2$. The c
oefficient of $w^{-1}$ in $(7+5w+w^2)\,e^{1/w}$ is:

| Term | $n$ | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| $7 \cdot w^{-n}/n!$ | $1$ | $7/1! = 7$ |
| $5w \cdot w^{-n}/n!$ | $2$ | $5/2! = 5/2$ |
| $w^2 \cdot w^{-n}/n!$ | $3$ | $1/3! = 1/6$ |

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=2} f = 7 + \frac{5}{2} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{29}{3}.$$

### Summing the residues

$$\sum \operatorname{Res} = \frac{5}{3} + \frac{14}{3} + \frac{29}{3} = \frac{48
}{3} = 16.$$

### Final result

By the Residue Theorem (the contour is traversed counterclockwise, enclosing all
 three singularities):

$$\int_{C} (1 + z + z^2)\left(e^{1/z}+e^{1/(z-1)}+e^{1/(z-2)}\right) dz = 2\pi i
 \cdot 16 = \boxed{32\pi i}.$$
