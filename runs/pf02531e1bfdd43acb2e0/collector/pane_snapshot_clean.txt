## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $f_{a_n}\to f_a$ uniformly on $B$.

Fix $\varepsilon>0$. We must find $N$ such that for all $n\ge N$ and all $b\in B
$,
$$d_C\big(f(a_n,b),\,f(a,b)\big)<\varepsilon.$$

**Step 1: A uniform neighborhood of $a$.** For each $b\in B$, continuity of $f$
at the point $(a,b)\in A\times B$ gives open neighborhoods $U_b\subseteq A$ of $
a$ and $V_b\subseteq B$ of $b$ such that
$$a'\in U_b,\; b'\in V_b \implies d_C\big(f(a',b'),\,f(a,b)\big)<\tfrac{\varepsi
lon}{2}.$$

The family $\{V_b\}_{b\in B}$ is an open cover of $B$. Since $B$ is compact, the
re is a finite subcover $\{V_{b_1},\dots,V_{b_k}\}$. Set
$$U:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{k} U_{b_i}\subseteq A,$$
an open neighborhood of $a$.

**Step 2: Uniform control on $\{a\}\times B$ and $U\times B$.** Take any $a'\in
U$ and any $b\in B$. Choose $i$ with $b\in V_{b_i}$. Since $a'\in U\subseteq U_{
b_i}$ and $b\in V_{b_i}$,
$$d_C\big(f(a',b),\,f(a,b_i)\big)<\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}.$$
Also $a\in U\subseteq U_{b_i}$ and $b\in V_{b_i}$, so
$$d_C\big(f(a,b),\,f(a,b_i)\big)<\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}.$$
By the triangle inequality in $C$,
$$d_C\big(f(a',b),\,f(a,b)\big)<\varepsilon \qquad\text{for all } a'\in U,\; b\i
n B. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Step 3: Use $a_n\to a$.** Since $U$ is an open neighborhood of $a$ and $a_n\to
 a$ in $A$, there exists $N$ with $a_n\in U$ for all $n\ge N$. Applying $(\ast)$
 with $a'=a_n$,
$$d_C\big(f(a_n,b),\,f(a,b)\big)<\varepsilon \qquad\text{for all } n\ge N,\; b\i
n B.$$

This is precisely the definition of $f_{a_n}\to f_a$ uniformly on $B$.

**Key ingredients:** continuity of $f$ on the product (giving pointwise tubes),
compactness of $B$ (reducing infinitely many tubes to finitely many via a finite
 subcover), and the triangle inequality in the metric target $C$ (combining the
two halves). The argument is the standard "tube lemma" style proof; the metric s
tructure of $C$ is used only to obtain the $\varepsilon/2$–$\varepsilon/2$ split
, and the conclusion in fact holds more generally for any Hausdorff target with
a uniform structure, but the metric hypothesis as stated is sufficient.
