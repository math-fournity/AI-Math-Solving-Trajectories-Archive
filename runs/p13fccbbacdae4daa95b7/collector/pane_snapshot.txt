## Solution

**Interpretation.** In the context of a representation decomposed into a direct
sum of distinct irreducibles, $V_\lambda$ denotes the isotypic component of high
est weight $\lambda$—that is, the direct sum of all irreducible summands whose h
ighest weight is $\lambda$. Since the irreducibles in the decomposition are *dis
tinct*, $V_\lambda$ is either a single irreducible summand (if one with highest
weight $\lambda$ is present) or $\{0\}$.

**Claim:** The statement is **false**. A weight vector of dominant integral weig
ht $\lambda$ need not lie in the isotypic component $V_\lambda$.

**Counterexample.**

Let $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ with standard Cartan $\mathfrak
{h} = \mathbb{C}h$, and let $L(n)$ denote the $(n+1)$-dimensional irreducible re
presentation of highest weight $n$ (a non-negative integer). The weights of $L(n
)$ are $n, n-2, \ldots, -n$.

Set
$$V = L(0) \oplus L(2).$$
This is a direct sum of two distinct finite-dimensional irreducible representati
ons (countably many, trivially).

- $L(0)$ is 1-dimensional (the trivial representation), with sole weight $0$.
- $L(2)$ is 3-dimensional, with weights $2, 0, -2$.

Now $\lambda = 0$ is a dominant integral weight (it lies in the dominant chamber
 and the weight lattice). The isotypic component $V_0$ is $L(0)$, since $L(0)$ i
s the unique summand with highest weight $0$.

However, $L(2)$ also has a weight space of weight $0$ (its middle weight space,
which is 1-dimensional). Let $v \in L(2)$ be a nonzero vector in this weight-$0$
 space. Then:

1. **$v$ is a weight vector** of $V$: indeed $h \cdot v = 0 \cdot v = 0$ for the
 Cartan element $h$.
2. **The weight $\lambda = 0$ is dominant integral.**
3. **$v \notin V_0 = L(0)$**, since $v$ lies in the summand $L(2)$, and the dire
ct sum decomposition means $L(0) \cap L(2) = \{0\}$.

Thus $v$ is a weight vector of dominant integral weight $\lambda = 0$, but $v \n
otin V_\lambda$.

**Why the statement fails in general.** A dominant integral weight $\lambda$ can
 appear as a *non-highest* weight in an irreducible representation $L(\mu)$ with
 $\mu \neq \lambda$ (and $\mu > \lambda$ in the partial order, or simply $\mu \n
eq \lambda$ with $\lambda$ in the weight set of $L(\mu)$). Any weight vector in
such a component has weight $\lambda$ but belongs to the isotypic component $V_\
mu$, not $V_\lambda$. The condition that $\lambda$ is dominant integral only gua
rantees that the irreducible $L(\lambda)$ *exists*; it does not prevent $\lambda
$ from occurring as a weight inside other irreducibles $L(\mu)$.

**Remark.** If instead $V_\lambda$ were interpreted as the *weight space* $\{v \
in V : h \cdot v = \lambda(h)\,v \;\forall h \in \mathfrak{h}\}$, the statement
would be trivially true by definition. The non-trivial and intended reading—give
n the emphasis on the decomposition into distinct irreducibles—is the isotypic-c
omponent reading, under which the statement is false as demonstrated.
