**No. A right circular cylinder in ℝ³ has trivial holonomy.**

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $S$ be the right circular cylinder of radius $R>0$:
$$S = \{(R\cos\theta,\, R\sin\theta,\, z) : \theta \in \mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
,\; z \in \mathbb{R}\} \subset \mathbb{R}^3,$$
equipped with the Riemannian metric $g$ induced from the Euclidean metric of $\m
athbb{R}^3$. Holonomy is that of the Levi-Civita connection $\nabla$ of $(S,g)$.

**Step 1 — The cylinder is globally flat.** Parametrize $S$ by $(\theta, z)$. Th
e induced metric is
$$g = R^2\,d\theta^2 + dz^2.$$
Setting $u = R\theta$, this becomes $g = du^2 + dz^2$, the standard flat Euclide
an metric. Hence the Gaussian curvature vanishes identically: $K \equiv 0$.

**Step 2 — The cylinder is a Euclidean quotient.** The map
$$\pi: \mathbb{R}^2_{(u,z)} \to S, \qquad \pi(u,z) = (R\cos(u/R),\, R\sin(u/R),\
, z)$$
is a Riemannian covering map, where $\mathbb{R}^2$ carries the metric $du^2+dz^2
$. The group of deck transformations is $\Gamma = \langle (u,z) \mapsto (u + 2\p
i R,\, z)\rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}$, acting freely, properly discontinuously, and
 **by isometries that are pure translations**. Thus $S \cong \mathbb{R}^2 / \Gam
ma$ as a flat Riemannian manifold.

**Step 3 — Parallel transport on the universal cover is trivial.** On $(\mathbb{
R}^2, du^2+dz^2)$ the Levi-Civita connection is the standard flat one. In the gl
obal coordinate frame $\{\partial_u, \partial_z\}$, parallel transport along any
 path sends a vector $(a,b)$ at the start to the vector $(a,b)$ at the end — the
 components are constant. Since $\nabla$ on $S$ is the quotient of $\nabla$ on $
\mathbb{R}^2$ via $\pi$, parallel transport on $S$ is the projection of parallel
 transport on $\mathbb{R}^2$.

**Step 4 — Holonomy of every loop is the identity.** Let $\gamma$ be any smooth
loop in $S$ based at $p$, and let $v \in T_p S$. Lift $\gamma$ to a path $\widet
ilde{\gamma}$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ starting at a lift $\widetilde{p}$. The endpoint
 is $\widetilde{p} + (2\pi R\, n,\, 0)$ for some integer $n$ (the winding number
 of $\gamma$ around the cylinder). By Step 3, parallel transport of $v$ along $\
widetilde{\gamma}$ yields the **same** vector $v$ at $\widetilde{p} + (2\pi R\,
n,\, 0)$. The deck transformation identifying this endpoint with $\widetilde{p}$
 is the translation $(u,z)\mapsto(u+2\pi R\, n, z)$, which fixes every vector (t
ranslations act trivially on tangent vectors). Projecting back to $S$, the vecto
r returns to $v$ itself.

Therefore the parallel-transport map $P_\gamma: T_p S \to T_p S$ is the identity
 for **every** loop $\gamma$, contractible or not.

**Step 5 — The holonomy group is trivial.** The holonomy group at $p$ is
$$\mathrm{Hol}_p(S) = \{P_\gamma : \gamma \text{ a loop based at } p\} = \{\math
rm{id}_{T_p S}\}.$$
This is the trivial group, so the cylinder has **no non-trivial holonomy**.

**Remark on why the non-trivial topology does not help.** The obstruction to tri
vial holonomy on a flat surface comes from the **cone angle** (curvature concent
rated at singular points) or from deck transformations that **rotate** tangent v
ectors. A flat cone of angle $\alpha \neq 2\pi$ has holonomy $\mathbb{Z}/(\text{
order})$ around its apex; a flat Möbius band (quotient by a glide reflection) ac
quires a $\mathbb{Z}/2$ holonomy because the deck transformation flips the tange
nt space. The cylinder's deck group consists only of **translations**, which act
 trivially on tangent vectors, so the fundamental group contributes nothing to h
olonomy. Combined with $K \equiv 0$ (no local contribution), both sources of hol
onomy vanish.

### Conclusion

A right circular cylinder in $\mathbb{R}^3$ **cannot** have non-trivial holonomy
: it is a flat ($K\equiv 0$) quotient of the Euclidean plane by a group of trans
lations, and translations preserve tangent vectors, so parallel transport around
 every loop — including the non-contractible loop winding around the cylinder —
is the identity.
