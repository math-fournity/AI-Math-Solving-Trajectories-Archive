## Quadratic Convergence of Newton's Method

**Setup.** Let $f \in C^2$ in a neighborhood of a simple root $\alpha$ (so $f(\a
lpha)=0$ and $f'(\alpha)\neq 0$). The Newton iteration is
$$
x^{(k+1)} = x^{(k)} - \frac{f(x^{(k)})}{f'(x^{(k)})}.
$$
Define the error $\delta x^{(k)} = x^{(k)} - \alpha$, so that $x^{(k)} = \alpha
+ \delta x^{(k)}$.

---

### Step 1. Taylor expand $f$ and $f'$ about the root $\alpha$

Since $f(\alpha)=0$, expanding to second order with a cubic remainder:
$$
f(x^{(k)}) = f(\alpha+\delta x^{(k)}) = f'(\alpha)\,\delta x^{(k)} + \tfrac{1}{2
}f''(\alpha)\,(\delta x^{(k)})^2 + O\!\big((\delta x^{(k)})^3\big). \tag{1}
$$

Expanding the derivative to first order with a quadratic remainder:
$$
f'(x^{(k)}) = f'(\alpha+\delta x^{(k)}) = f'(\alpha) + f''(\alpha)\,\delta x^{(k
)} + O\!\big((\delta x^{(k)})^2\big). \tag{2}
$$

---

### Step 2. Form the error recurrence

From the iteration,
$$
\delta x^{(k+1)} = x^{(k+1)}-\alpha = \delta x^{(k)} - \frac{f(x^{(k)})}{f'(x^{(
k)})} = \frac{\delta x^{(k)}\,f'(x^{(k)}) - f(x^{(k)})}{f'(x^{(k)})}. \tag{3}
$$

**Numerator.** Using (1) and (2):
$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta x^{(k)}\,f'(x^{(k)}) - f(x^{(k)})
&= \delta x^{(k)}\Big[f'(\alpha) + f''(\alpha)\,\delta x^{(k)} + O\!\big((\delta
 x^{(k)})^2\big)\Big] \\
&\quad - \Big[f'(\alpha)\,\delta x^{(k)} + \tfrac{1}{2}f''(\alpha)\,(\delta x^{(
k)})^2 + O\!\big((\delta x^{(k)})^3\big)\Big] \\[2pt]
&= \cancel{f'(\alpha)\,\delta x^{(k)}} + f''(\alpha)\,(\delta x^{(k)})^2 + O\!\b
ig((\delta x^{(k)})^3\big) \\
&\quad - \cancel{f'(\alpha)\,\delta x^{(k)}} - \tfrac{1}{2}f''(\alpha)\,(\delta
x^{(k)})^2 - O\!\big((\delta x^{(k)})^3\big) \\[2pt]
&= \tfrac{1}{2}f''(\alpha)\,(\delta x^{(k)})^2 + O\!\big((\delta x^{(k)})^3\big)
. \tag{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Denominator.** From (2), factoring out $f'(\alpha)\neq 0$:
$$
f'(x^{(k)}) = f'(\alpha)\Big[1 + \frac{f''(\alpha)}{f'(\alpha)}\,\delta x^{(k)}
+ O\!\big((\delta x^{(k)})^2\big)\Big]. \tag{5}
$$

---

### Step 3. Combine — the quadratic error law

Substituting (4) and (5) into (3):
$$
\delta x^{(k+1)} = \frac{\tfrac{1}{2}f''(\alpha)\,(\delta x^{(k)})^2 + O\!\big((
\delta x^{(k)})^3\big)}{f'(\alpha)\Big[1 + O\!\big(\delta x^{(k)}\big)\Big]}.
$$

For $\delta x^{(k)}\to 0$ the bracket $[1+O(\delta x^{(k)})]^{-1} = 1 + O(\delta
 x^{(k)})$, so
$$
\boxed{\;\delta x^{(k+1)} = \frac{f''(\alpha)}{2\,f'(\alpha)}\,(\delta x^{(k)})^
2 + O\!\big((\delta x^{(k)})^3\big).\;} \tag{6}
$$

---

### Step 4. Consequences — quadratic convergence

Define the **asymptotic error constant**
$$
C \;=\; \frac{f''(\alpha)}{2\,f'(\alpha)}.
$$

**(a) Quadratic order.** Dividing (6) by $(\delta x^{(k)})^2$ and taking $k\to\i
nfty$ (so $\delta x^{(k)}\to 0$ and the cubic remainder vanishes):
$$
\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\big|\delta x^{(k+1)}\big|}{\big|\delta x^{(k)}\big|^2} =
 |C| = \left|\frac{f''(\alpha)}{2\,f'(\alpha)}\right| < \infty. \tag{7}
$$
This is precisely the definition of **convergence of order $p=2$** with finite a
symptotic error constant $|C|$.

**(b) Local convergence condition.** From (6), for $\delta x^{(k)}$ small enough
 the cubic term is negligible, giving $|\delta x^{(k+1)}| \approx |C|\,|\delta x
^{(k)}|^2$. The iteration contracts whenever
$$
|C|\,|\delta x^{(k)}| < 1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad |\delta x^{(k)}| < \frac
{1}{|C|} = \frac{2|f'(\alpha)|}{|f''(\alpha)|}.
$$
Hence there exists a neighborhood $U$ of $\alpha$ such that any $x^{(0)}\in U$ p
roduces a sequence with $|\delta x^{(k)}|\to 0$, and once $|\delta x^{(k)}|$ is
small the number of correct digits **doubles** at each step (since $\log_{10}|\d
elta x^{(k+1)}| \approx 2\log_{10}|\delta x^{(k)}| + \log_{10}|C|$).

**(c) Role of the hypotheses.**
- $f'(\alpha)\neq 0$ (simple root) keeps the denominator $f'(\alpha)$ bounded aw
ay from zero; without it the denominator in (5) would vanish and the expansion (
6) would fail — this is why multiple roots give only *linear* convergence.
- $f\in C^2$ supplies the $f''(\alpha)$ term that produces the $(\delta x^{(k)})
^2$ leading behavior; with only $C^1$ regularity one cannot conclude quadratic o
rder.

---

### Summary

Starting from the Taylor expansion of $f$ about the simple root $\alpha$, the Ne
wton error $\delta x^{(k)}=x^{(k)}-\alpha$ satisfies
$$
\delta x^{(k+1)} = \frac{f''(\alpha)}{2f'(\alpha)}\,(\delta x^{(k)})^2 + O\!\big
((\delta x^{(k)})^3\big),
$$
so
$$
\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{|\delta x^{(k+1)}|}{|\delta x^{(k)}|^2} = \left|\frac{f''
(\alpha)}{2f'(\alpha)}\right|,
$$
which establishes **quadratic convergence** of Newton's method near a simple roo
t, with asymptotic error constant $C = f''(\alpha)/(2f'(\alpha))$. $\blacksquare
$
