## Proof

**Identifying the function.** The problem defines $I(x) = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{\
infty} e^{x\cos\theta}\,d\theta$. The integral $\int_0^{\infty} e^{x\cos\theta}\
,d\theta$ does not converge (the integrand oscillates without decay for $x \neq
0$). The standard integral representation of the **modified Bessel function of t
he first kind of order zero** is:

$$I_0(x) = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi} e^{x\cos\theta}\,d\theta,$$

so the upper limit $\infty$ is a typographical error for $\pi$, and $I(x) = I_0(
x)$. We use the equivalent, well-known **series representation**:

$$I_0(z) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(z/2)^{2k}}{(k!)^2}.$$

Setting $z = 2x$:

$$I_0(2x) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^{2k}}{(k!)^2}.$$

**Setting up the integral.** We wish to compute

$$\mathcal{I} = \int_0^{\infty} x\, I_0(2x)\, e^{-x^2}\, dx = \int_0^{\infty} x\
, e^{-x^2} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^{2k}}{(k!)^2}\, dx.$$

**Interchanging sum and integral.** Every term in the series is non-negative on
$[0,\infty)$, and $x\,e^{-x^2} \geq 0$. By **Tonelli's theorem** (monotone conve
rgence for non-negative functions), we may interchange the sum and integral:

$$\mathcal{I} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(k!)^2} \int_0^{\infty} x^{2k+1}\,
e^{-x^2}\, dx.$$

**Evaluating the inner integral.** Substitute $u = x^2$, so $du = 2x\,dx$ and $x
^{2k} = u^k$:

$$\int_0^{\infty} x^{2k+1}\, e^{-x^2}\, dx = \int_0^{\infty} x^{2k} \cdot x\, e^
{-x^2}\, dx = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\infty} u^k\, e^{-u}\, du = \frac{1}{2}\,\Gamma
(k+1) = \frac{k!}{2}.$$

**Summing the series.** Substituting back:

$$\mathcal{I} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(k!)^2} \cdot \frac{k!}{2} = \frac{
1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k!} = \frac{e}{2}.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\int_0^{\infty} x\, I(2x)\, e^{-x^2}\, dx = \frac{e}{2}.}$$
