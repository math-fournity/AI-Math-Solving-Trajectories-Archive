## Solution

**Problem.** Find the derivative of $u=\dfrac{1}{r^{2}}$, where $r^{2}=x^{2}+y^{
2}+z^{2}$, in the direction of its fastest growth.

---

### Step 1. Compute the gradient

Since $r^{2}=x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}$, we have $u=\dfrac{1}{x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}}=\dfrac{
1}{r^{2}}$.

The partial derivatives are:

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}=\frac{-2x}{(x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2})^{2}}=\frac{-2x}{r
^{4}},$$

and by symmetry,

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}=\frac{-2y}{r^{4}},\qquad \frac{\partial u}{\part
ial z}=\frac{-2z}{r^{4}}.$$

Hence the gradient is

$$\nabla u=\left(\frac{-2x}{r^{4}},\,\frac{-2y}{r^{4}},\,\frac{-2z}{r^{4}}\right
)=-\frac{2}{r^{4}}\,(x,\,y,\,z).$$

### Step 2. Direction of fastest growth

The direction of fastest growth of a scalar field is the direction of its gradie
nt. The unit vector in this direction is

$$\mathbf{e}=\frac{\nabla u}{\lVert\nabla u\rVert}.$$

The derivative in the direction of fastest growth equals the magnitude of the gr
adient:

$$\left(\frac{du}{ds}\right)_{\max}=\lVert\nabla u\rVert.$$

### Step 3. Compute the magnitude

$$\lVert\nabla u\rVert=\frac{2}{r^{4}}\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}}=\frac{2}{r^{4}}\c
dot r=\frac{2}{r^{3}}.$$

### Step 4. The direction itself

The unit direction of fastest growth is

$$\mathbf{e}=\frac{\nabla u}{\lVert\nabla u\rVert}=\frac{-\frac{2}{r^{4}}(x,y,z)
}{\frac{2}{r^{3}}}=-\frac{(x,y,z)}{r}=-\frac{\mathbf{r}}{r},$$

i.e., toward the origin (the radial inward direction). This is consistent with t
he fact that $u=1/r^{2}$ increases as $r$ decreases.

### Result

The derivative of $u=\dfrac{1}{r^{2}}$ in the direction of its fastest growth is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{2}{r^{3}}}$$

taken in the direction $-\dfrac{\mathbf{r}}{r}$ (toward the origin).
