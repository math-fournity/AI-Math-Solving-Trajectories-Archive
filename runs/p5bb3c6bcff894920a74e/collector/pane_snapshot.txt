## Proof

**Answer leak check:** The problem statement contains only the problem; no solut
ion, answer, or hint is leaked. Proceeding.

---

**Notation.** Let $k>1$ be a fixed integer. The recursion is $a_{n+1}=a_n+a_n^{-
1/k}$ (we read the index condition as $n\ge 0$, which does not affect the limit)
. Set
$$
b_n:=a_n^{k+1},\qquad c_n:=n^k .
$$
We prove
$$
\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_n^{k+1}}{n^k}=\left(\frac{k+1}{k}\r
ight)^k }.
$$

---

### Step 1. $a_n\to\infty$.

The sequence is increasing ($a_{n+1}>a_n$). If it were bounded, it would converg
e to some $L>0$, but then $a_{n+1}-a_n=a_n^{-1/k}\to L^{-1/k}>0$, contradicting
convergence. Hence $a_n\to\infty$.

---

### Step 2. A key auxiliary limit: $\displaystyle\frac{a_n^{(k+1)/k}}{n}\to\frac
{k+1}{k}$.

Define $d_n:=a_n^{(k+1)/k}$ and $g(x)=x^{(k+1)/k}$. With $h_n:=a_n^{-1/k}$,
$$
d_{n+1}-d_n=g(a_n+h_n)-g(a_n).
$$
Since $g\in C^2(0,\infty)$, Taylor's theorem gives, for some $\xi_n\in(a_n,a_n+h
_n)$,
$$
d_{n+1}-d_n=g'(a_n)\,h_n+\tfrac12 g''(\xi_n)\,h_n^2 .
$$
Now
$$
g'(x)=\frac{k+1}{k}\,x^{1/k},\qquad g''(x)=\frac{k+1}{k^2}\,x^{(1-k)/k}.
$$
Because $k>1$, the exponent $(1-k)/k$ is negative, so $g''$ is positive and *dec
reasing*. Thus $\xi_n>a_n$ implies $g''(\xi_n)\le g''(a_n)$, and
$$
g'(a_n)h_n=\frac{k+1}{k}\,a_n^{1/k}\cdot a_n^{-1/k}=\frac{k+1}{k},
$$
$$
0\le \tfrac12 g''(\xi_n)h_n^2\le \tfrac12 g''(a_n)\,a_n^{-2/k}
=\frac{k+1}{2k^2}\,a_n^{(1-k)/k-2/k}
=\frac{k+1}{2k^2}\,a_n^{-(k+1)/k}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0 .
$$
Therefore
$$
d_{n+1}-d_n=\frac{k+1}{k}+o(1).
$$
Applying the Stolz–Cesàro theorem ($\infty/\infty$ form) with denominator $n$ (w
hose successive differences are $1$):
$$
\frac{d_n}{n}=\frac{a_n^{(k+1)/k}}{n}\longrightarrow \frac{k+1}{k}. \tag{1}
$$

---

### Step 3. Asymptotics of $b_{n+1}-b_n$.

Since $k+1$ is a positive integer, the binomial expansion is exact:
$$
b_{n+1}-b_n=(a_n+h_n)^{k+1}-a_n^{k+1}
=\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}\binom{k+1}{j}a_n^{k+1-j}\,h_n^{\,j}
=\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}\binom{k+1}{j}a_n^{\,(k+1)(k-j)/k}.
$$
The $j=1$ term is
$$
(k+1)\,a_n^{(k+1)(k-1)/k}=(k+1)\,a_n^{(k^2-1)/k}.
$$
For $j\ge 2$ the exponent $(k+1)(k-j)/k$ is strictly smaller than $(k^2-1)/k=(k+
1)(k-1)/k$ (the gap for $j=2$ is $(k+1)/k>0$, and larger for bigger $j$). As $a_
n\to\infty$, every $j\ge 2$ term is $o\!\left(a_n^{(k^2-1)/k}\right)$. Hence
$$
b_{n+1}-b_n=(k+1)\,a_n^{(k^2-1)/k}\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr). \tag{2}
$$

---

### Step 4. Apply Stolz–Cesàro to $b_n/c_n=b_n/n^k$.

We have $b_n=a_n^{k+1}\to\infty$ and $c_n=n^k\to\infty$ strictly increasing. Com
pute the denominator differences:
$$
c_{n+1}-c_n=(n+1)^k-n^k=k\,n^{k-1}+O(n^{k-2})=k\,n^{k-1}\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr). \tag
{3}
$$
Using (2) and (3),
$$
\frac{b_{n+1}-b_n}{c_{n+1}-c_n}
=\frac{(k+1)\,a_n^{(k^2-1)/k}\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)}{k\,n^{k-1}\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)}.
$$
Now observe $(k^2-1)/k=(k-1)(k+1)/k$, so
$$
a_n^{(k^2-1)/k}=\left(a_n^{(k+1)/k}\right)^{k-1}.
$$
By (1),
$$
\frac{a_n^{(k^2-1)/k}}{n^{k-1}}
=\left(\frac{a_n^{(k+1)/k}}{n}\right)^{k-1}
\longrightarrow\left(\frac{k+1}{k}\right)^{k-1}. \tag{4}
$$
Therefore
$$
\frac{b_{n+1}-b_n}{c_{n+1}-c_n}
\longrightarrow\frac{k+1}{k}\cdot\left(\frac{k+1}{k}\right)^{k-1}
=\left(\frac{k+1}{k}\right)^k .
$$
By the Stolz–Cesàro theorem ($\infty/\infty$ form),
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_n^{k+1}}{n^k}
=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{b_n}{c_n}
=\left(\frac{k+1}{k}\right)^k .
$$

---

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_n^{\,k+1}}{n^k}=\left(\frac{k+1}{k}
\right)^k}
$$
