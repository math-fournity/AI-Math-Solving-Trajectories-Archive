**Answer: No.** $L^p$ convergence of $\sup_{t \in [0,T]} |x_t^\epsilon|^p$ to ze
ro does **not** imply $\limsup_{\epsilon \to 0} \sup_{t \in [0,T]} |x_t^\epsilon
|^p = 0$ almost surely.

## Proof by Counterexample

**Construction.** Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ be a probability space supporti
ng a sequence of independent events $(A_n)_{n \geq 1}$ with $P(A_n) = \frac{1}{n
}$. Set $\epsilon_n = \frac{1}{n}$.

Define the family of stochastic processes $x_t^\epsilon$ (taking values in $\mat
hbb{R}$, which is a special case of vector-valued) as follows:

- For $\epsilon = \frac{1}{n}$: set $x_t^{1/n}(\omega) = \mathbf{1}_{A_n}(\omega
)$ for all $t \in [0,T]$.
- For $\epsilon \neq \frac{1}{n}$ (any $n$): set $x_t^\epsilon(\omega) = 0$ for
all $t \in [0,T]$.

Each process $x^\epsilon$ is constant in $t$, hence continuous in $t$. Thus $(x_
t^\epsilon)$ is a family of continuous (vector-valued) stochastic processes.

**Step 1: The $L^p$ condition holds.** For any $p > 0$:

$$\sup_{t \in [0,T]} |x_t^{1/n}|^p = |\mathbf{1}_{A_n}|^p = \mathbf{1}_{A_n},$$

so $E\!\left[\sup_{t \in [0,T]} |x_t^{1/n}|^p\right] = P(A_n) = \frac{1}{n} \to
0$.

For $\epsilon \neq \frac{1}{n}$, $E\!\left[\sup_{t \in [0,T]} |x_t^\epsilon|^p\r
ight] = 0$.

Given any $\delta > 0$, for all $\epsilon < \delta$: either $\epsilon = \frac{1}
{n}$ with $n > \frac{1}{\delta}$ (giving $E = \frac{1}{n} < \delta$), or $\epsil
on \neq \frac{1}{n}$ (giving $E = 0$). In either case $E\!\left[\sup_{t} |x_t^\e
psilon|^p\right] < \delta$. Therefore:

$$E\!\left[\sup_{t \in [0,T]} |x_t^\epsilon|^p\right] \to 0 \quad \text{as } \ep
silon \to 0.$$

**Step 2: The $\limsup$ condition fails almost surely.** Since the events $(A_n)
$ are independent and $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P(A_n) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}
{n} = \infty$, the **second Borel–Cantelli lemma** gives:

$$P(A_n \text{ infinitely often}) = 1.$$

That is, almost surely, infinitely many $A_n$ occur. Consequently:

$$\limsup_{n \to \infty} \mathbf{1}_{A_n} = 1 \quad \text{a.s.}$$

Now, since $\limsup_{\epsilon \to 0}$ is monotone along any subsequence $\epsilo
n_n \to 0$:

$$\limsup_{\epsilon \to 0}\, \sup_{t \in [0,T]} |x_t^\epsilon|^p \;\geq\; \limsu
p_{n \to \infty}\, \sup_{t \in [0,T]} |x_t^{1/n}|^p \;=\; \limsup_{n \to \infty}
 \mathbf{1}_{A_n} \;=\; 1 \quad \text{a.s.}$$

**Conclusion.** We have constructed a family of continuous vector-valued stochas
tic processes satisfying $E\!\left[\sup_{t \in [0,T]} |x_t^\epsilon|^p\right] \t
o 0$ as $\epsilon \to 0$, yet $\limsup_{\epsilon \to 0} \sup_{t \in [0,T]} |x_t^
\epsilon|^p = 1$ almost surely (not $0$).

The implication is **false**. $L^p$ convergence of the supremum to zero guarante
es convergence in probability (by Markov's inequality), but it does **not** guar
antee almost sure convergence. The gap between $L^p$ convergence and almost sure
 convergence is fundamental and cannot be bridged without additional assumptions
 (such as summability of the $L^p$ norms along a subsequence, which would enable
 a Borel–Cantelli argument in the *opposite* direction).
