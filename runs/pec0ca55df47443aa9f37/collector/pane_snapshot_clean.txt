**Claim:** $\bigcap_{i \in \mathbb{N}} \mathfrak{p}_i = \{0\}$.

**Proof.**

Let $\mathcal{O}$ be the ring of integers of a number field $K$. Then $\mathcal{
O}$ is a Dedekind domain: every nonzero proper ideal factors uniquely into a fin
ite product of nonzero prime ideals.

**Case 1: One of the $\mathfrak{p}_i$ is the zero ideal.**
Since $\{0\}$ is contained in every ideal, if $\mathfrak{p}_j = \{0\}$ for some
$j$, then $\bigcap_i \mathfrak{p}_i \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_j = \{0\}$, so $\bigc
ap_i \mathfrak{p}_i = \{0\}$.

**Case 2: All $\mathfrak{p}_i$ are nonzero prime ideals.**
In a Dedekind domain, every nonzero prime ideal is maximal. We show the intersec
tion is $\{0\}$.

Suppose for contradiction that there exists a nonzero element $x \in \bigcap_i \
mathfrak{p}_i$. Since $x \neq 0$ and $\mathcal{O}$ is a Dedekind domain, the pri
ncipal ideal $(x)$ admits a unique factorization into nonzero prime ideals:
$$(x) = \mathfrak{q}_1^{e_1} \mathfrak{q}_2^{e_2} \cdots \mathfrak{q}_r^{e_r}$$
for some nonzero prime ideals $\mathfrak{q}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{q}_r$ (pairwise
distinct) and positive integers $e_1, \dots, e_r$. This is a **finite** set $\{\
mathfrak{q}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{q}_r\}$.

Now, $x \in \mathfrak{p}_i$ means $(x) \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_i$. In a Dedekind
domain, for ideals $I, J$ we have $I \subseteq J$ if and only if $J \mid I$ (i.e
., $I = J \cdot K$ for some ideal $K$). Applying this with $I = (x)$ and $J = \m
athfrak{p}_i$:
$$(x) \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_i \iff \mathfrak{p}_i \mid (x).$$
By unique factorization, $\mathfrak{p}_i \mid (x)$ holds if and only if $\mathfr
ak{p}_i \in \{\mathfrak{q}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{q}_r\}$.

Since $x \in \mathfrak{p}_i$ for **every** $i \in \mathbb{N}$, every $\mathfrak{
p}_i$ must belong to the finite set $\{\mathfrak{q}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{q}_r\}$.
 But the $\mathfrak{p}_i$ are pairwise distinct and indexed by $\mathbb{N}$, so
there are infinitely many of them. An infinite set of pairwise distinct prime id
eals cannot all lie in a finite set. **Contradiction.**

Therefore no nonzero $x$ lies in $\bigcap_i \mathfrak{p}_i$, and we conclude:
$$\bigcap_{i \in \mathbb{N}} \mathfrak{p}_i = \{0\}. \qquad \blacksquare$$
