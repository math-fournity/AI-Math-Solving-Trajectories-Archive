## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $f_n \to f$ in $L^1(X)$ if and only if $\int_X f_n\,d\mu \to \in
t_X f\,d\mu$.

---

### (⇒) Forward direction

This direction is immediate and requires none of the hypotheses beyond $f_n, f \
in L^1$. If $f_n \to f$ in $L^1$, then

$$\left|\int_X f_n\,d\mu - \int_X f\,d\mu\right| \leq \int_X |f_n - f|\,d\mu \to
 0.$$

---

### (⇐) Reverse direction (Scheffé's lemma)

Assume $\int_X f_n\,d\mu \to \int_X f\,d\mu$. We prove $\|f_n - f\|_1 \to 0$.

**Step 1: $f \geq 0$ a.e.** Since $f_n \geq 0$ a.e. and $f_n \to f$ pointwise a.
e., the pointwise a.e. limit of non-negative functions is non-negative, so $f \g
eq 0$ a.e. In particular $\int_X f\,d\mu = \|f\|_1 < \infty$ (as $f \in L^1$).

**Step 2: A useful identity.** Define the positive and negative parts
$$g_n^+ := \max(f_n - f,\, 0), \qquad g_n^- := \max(f - f_n,\, 0),$$
so that $f_n - f = g_n^+ - g_n^-$ and $|f_n - f| = g_n^+ + g_n^-$. Then
$$g_n^+ = (f_n - f) + g_n^-,$$
and integrating (all terms are non-negative and finite since $f_n, f \in L^1$ an
d $f_n, f \geq 0$ a.e.):
$$\int g_n^+\,d\mu = \int(f_n - f)\,d\mu + \int g_n^-\,d\mu = \left(\int f_n\,d\
mu - \int f\,d\mu\right) + \int g_n^-\,d\mu.$$
Therefore
$$\int |f_n - f|\,d\mu = \int g_n^+\,d\mu + \int g_n^-\,d\mu = \left(\int f_n\,d
\mu - \int f\,d\mu\right) + 2\int g_n^-\,d\mu. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Step 3: $\int g_n^-\,d\mu \to 0$ by dominated convergence.** We check the hypo
theses of DCT for $\{g_n^-\}$:

- *Pointwise a.e. convergence to 0:* Since $f_n \to f$ a.e., we have $f - f_n \t
o 0$ a.e., hence $g_n^- = \max(f - f_n, 0) \to 0$ a.e.
- *Dominating function:* Since $f_n \geq 0$ a.e., we have $f - f_n \leq f$ a.e.,
 and so
$$0 \leq g_n^- = \max(f - f_n, 0) \leq \max(f, 0) = f \quad \text{a.e.}$$
(using $f \geq 0$ a.e.). Since $f \in L^1$, the constant dominator $f$ is integr
able.

By the Dominated Convergence Theorem, $\int g_n^-\,d\mu \to 0$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.** By assumption $\int f_n\,d\mu - \int f\,d\mu \to 0$, and
 by Step 3, $\int g_n^-\,d\mu \to 0$. Applying these to ($\ast$):
$$\int |f_n - f|\,d\mu = \underbrace{\left(\int f_n\,d\mu - \int f\,d\mu\right)}
_{\to\, 0} + 2\underbrace{\int g_n^-\,d\mu}_{\to\, 0} \longrightarrow 0.$$

Hence $f_n \to f$ in $L^1(X)$. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark on the hypotheses.** The $L^1$-boundedness of $\{f_n\}$ is in fact redu
ndant: it follows from $\int f_n\,d\mu \to \int f\,d\mu < \infty$ together with
$f_n \geq 0$ (which gives $\|f_n\|_1 = \int f_n\,d\mu$). The essential ingredien
ts for the reverse direction are: (i) $f_n \geq 0$ a.e., (ii) $f_n \to f$ a.e. (
so $f \geq 0$ a.e.), (iii) $f \in L^1$ (providing the DCT dominator), and (iv) c
onvergence of the integrals.
