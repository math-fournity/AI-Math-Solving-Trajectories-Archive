# The J-Homomorphism

## Definition

The **J-homomorphism** is a natural homomorphism

$$J \colon \pi_k\bigl(O(n)\bigr) \longrightarrow \pi_{n+k}\bigl(S^n\bigr)$$

constructed by exploiting the action of the orthogonal group $O(n)$ on $\mathbb{
R}^n$ together with the one-point compactification $\mathbb{R}^n \cong S^n \setm
inus\{\infty\}$. In the stable limit it becomes a map

$$J \colon \pi_k(O) \longrightarrow \pi_k^{S}$$

from the homotopy groups of the infinite orthogonal group $O = \operatorname{col
im}_n O(n)$ to the stable homotopy groups of spheres $\pi_k^{S} = \operatorname{
colim}_n \pi_{n+k}(S^n)$.

---

## Construction

### Step 1 — One-point compactification

Compactify $\mathbb{R}^n$ to the sphere $S^n = \mathbb{R}^n \cup \{\infty\}$, wi
th $\infty$ serving as the basepoint. Every linear map $A \colon \mathbb{R}^n \t
o \mathbb{R}^n$ that is **proper** (preimage of compact is compact) extends uniq
uely to a based continuous map $\widehat{A} \colon S^n \to S^n$ by setting $\wid
ehat{A}(\infty) = \infty$.

### Step 2 — From $O(n)$ to the based mapping space

Every orthogonal transformation $A \in O(n)$ is a linear isometry, hence a homeo
morphism of $\mathbb{R}^n$ onto itself, hence proper. Therefore $A$ extends to a
 based homeomorphism $\widehat{A} \in \operatorname{Homeo}_*(S^n)$. The assignme
nt

$$\Phi \colon O(n) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Map}_*(S^n, S^n), \qquad A \lon
gmapsto \widehat{A}$$

is continuous: it is induced by the continuous action of $O(n)$ on $\mathbb{R}^n
$ followed by the (continuous) one-point compactification functor. Since $\opera
torname{Map}_*(S^n, S^n) \cong \Omega^n S^n$ (the $n$-fold loop space), we obtai
n a continuous map

$$\Phi \colon O(n) \longrightarrow \Omega^n S^n.$$

### Step 3 — Induced map on homotopy groups

Applying $\pi_k$ and using the adjunction isomorphism $\pi_k(\Omega^n S^n) \cong
 \pi_{n+k}(S^n)$, we define

$$\boxed{J \colon \pi_k\bigl(O(n)\bigr) \xrightarrow{\;\pi_k(\Phi)\;} \pi_k(\Ome
ga^n S^n) \cong \pi_{n+k}(S^n).}$$

Concretely, an element $\alpha \in \pi_k(O(n))$ is represented by a map $f \colo
n S^k \to O(n)$. For each $x \in S^k$, $f(x)$ is an orthogonal transformation of
 $\mathbb{R}^n$, hence a based map $S^n \to S^n$. Assembling these gives the **a
djoint** map

$$\widetilde{f} \colon S^k \wedge S^n \longrightarrow S^n,$$

i.e. a map $S^{k+n} \to S^n$, which represents $J(\alpha) \in \pi_{n+k}(S^n)$. E
quivalently, $J(\alpha)$ is the class of the composite

$$S^{k+n} \cong S^k \wedge S^n \xrightarrow{\;f \wedge \mathrm{id}\;} O(n) \wedg
e S^n \xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{eval}\;} S^n,$$

where $\mathrm{eval}(A, v) = \widehat{A}(v)$ is the evaluation of the compactifi
ed action.

### Step 4 — Stabilization

The inclusions $O(n) \hookrightarrow O(n+1)$ (adding a $1$ in the lower-right co
rner) and the suspension isomorphisms $\pi_{n+k}(S^n) \to \pi_{n+k+1}(S^{n+1})$
are compatible with $J$, so passing to the colimit over $n$ yields the **stable
J-homomorphism**

$$J \colon \pi_k(O) \longrightarrow \pi_k^{S}, \qquad O = \operatorname{colim}_n
 O(n).$$

By Bott periodicity $\pi_k(O)$ is $4$-periodic (with values $\mathbb{Z}/2, \math
bb{Z}/2, 0, \mathbb{Z}$ for $k \equiv 1,2,3,0 \pmod 4$), so $J$ is nontrivial on
ly in degrees $k \equiv 0, 1 \pmod 4$ (mod $8$ in the full $KO$-theoretic pictur
e).

## Key properties (Adams's theorem)

The **image of $J$** was determined by J. F. Adams (1963):

- For $k = 4m - 1$ (i.e. $k \equiv 3 \pmod 4$), the source is $\pi_{4m-1}(SO) \c
ong \mathbb{Z}$, and

$$\operatorname{im}(J) \subseteq \pi_{4m-1}^{S}$$

is a **cyclic group of order equal to the denominator of** $\dfrac{B_{2m}}{4m}$,
 where $B_{2m}$ is the $2m$-th Bernoulli number. (Equivalently, the order is the
 greatest common divisor of the numbers $2, 3, \ldots, 2m$ suitably adjusted — i
t equals $c_m = \text{denominator}(B_{2m}/(4m))$.)

- For $k \equiv 0 \pmod 4$ with $k > 0$, the source is $\pi_k(SO) \cong \mathbb{
Z}$ but $J$ maps onto a $\mathbb{Z}/2$ summand (the "Kervaire invariant" part in
 some descriptions), or more precisely the image is $\mathbb{Z}/2$ detected by t
he $e$-invariant / $KO$-theoretic $e$-invariant.

- For $k \equiv 1, 2 \pmod 8$, the source is $\mathbb{Z}/2$ and $J$ is injective
 onto a $\mathbb{Z}/2$ in $\pi_k^S$.

- For $k \equiv 3, 5, 6, 7 \pmod 8$ (with $k \not\equiv 3 \pmod 4$), the source
is $0$ and $J$ is trivial.

Adams proved that $J$ is **injective** on the $2$-primary torsion and that the $
c$-invariant (related to the Adams $e$-invariant and the $\psi^k$ operations in
$K$-theory) detects the full image. The connection to $K$-theory is that $J$ fac
tors through the $J$-group $J(X) = \widetilde{KO}(X) / \operatorname{im}(\psi^k
- 1)$, and for $X = S^{k+1}$ this recovers the above.

## Summary

The J-homomorphism is the map on homotopy groups induced by the continuous map $
\Phi \colon O(n) \to \Omega^n S^n$ obtained by sending each orthogonal transform
ation of $\mathbb{R}^n$ to the based self-map of $S^n$ given by one-point compac
tification. Explicitly, an element $[f] \in \pi_k(O(n))$ is sent to the class of
 $S^k \wedge S^n \xrightarrow{f \wedge \mathrm{id}} O(n) \wedge S^n \xrightarrow
{\mathrm{eval}} S^n$ in $\pi_{n+k}(S^n)$. Stabilizing in $n$ gives $J \colon \pi
_k(O) \to \pi_k^S$, whose image Adams computed in terms of Bernoulli numbers, es
tablishing it as a fundamental and computable piece of the stable homotopy group
s of spheres.
