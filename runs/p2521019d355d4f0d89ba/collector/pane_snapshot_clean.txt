**Answer: Yes.**

Let $A = \{y \in Y : |\pi^{-1}(y)| = 1\}$. By hypothesis $\nu(A) = 1$. We will s
how that $A$ is Borel and that the natural inverse $g:A\to X$, $g(y)=\pi^{-1}(y)
$, is Borel measurable; extending $g$ by a constant off $A$ gives the desired se
ction.

---

**Step 1. $A$ is a $G_\delta$ (hence Borel) subset of $Y$.**

The multifunction $F(y)=\pi^{-1}(y)$ has closed graph
$$\Gamma = \{(y,x)\in Y\times X : \pi(x)=y\},$$
which is closed in $Y\times X$ (it is the graph of the continuous map $\pi$, wit
h coordinates swapped). Since $X$ is compact, $F$ has compact values and is uppe
r hemicontinuous: for every open $U\subset X$, the set $\{y : F(y)\subset U\}$ i
s open in $Y$.

For $\varepsilon>0$,
$$\{y : \operatorname{diam} F(y) < \varepsilon\} = \bigcup_{z\in X}\{y : F(y)\su
bset B(z,\varepsilon/2)\}$$
is open (using a countable dense subset of $X$ to make the union countable, sinc
e $X$ is compact metric). Therefore
$$A = \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\{y : \operatorname{diam} F(y) < 1/n\}$$
is a $G_\delta$ set, hence Borel.

---

**Step 2. The inverse $g:A\to X$ is Borel measurable.**

It suffices to show $g^{-1}(F)$ is Borel for every closed $F\subset X$ (closed s
ets generate the Borel $\sigma$-algebra of $X$). For $y\in A$, the fiber $\pi^{-
1}(y)=\{g(y)\}$ is a singleton, so
$$g(y)\in F \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \pi^{-1}(y)\subset F \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
 \pi^{-1}(y)\cap (X\setminus F)=\varnothing \;\Longleftrightarrow\; y\notin \pi(
X\setminus F).$$
Hence
$$g^{-1}(F) = A \cap \bigl(Y\setminus \pi(X\setminus F)\bigr).$$

Now $X\setminus F$ is open in the compact metric space $X$, hence $\sigma$-compa
ct: $X\setminus F = \bigcup_n K_n$ with each $K_n$ compact. Thus $\pi(X\setminus
 F)=\bigcup_n \pi(K_n)$ is an $F_\sigma$ set (each $\pi(K_n)$ is compact, hence
closed in $Y$). Consequently $Y\setminus\pi(X\setminus F)$ is $G_\delta$, hence
Borel, and so $g^{-1}(F)$ is Borel. Therefore $g$ is Borel measurable.

---

**Step 3. Measurable extension.**

Pick any $x_0\in X$ (if $Y=\varnothing$ the statement is vacuous; otherwise $X\n
eq\varnothing$). Define
$$s(y) = \begin{cases} g(y), & y\in A,\\ x_0, & y\in Y\setminus A. \end{cases}$$
For any closed $F\subset X$,
$$s^{-1}(F) = \bigl(A\cap g^{-1}(F)\bigr)\;\cup\;\bigl((Y\setminus A)\cap C_F\bi
gr),\qquad C_F=\begin{cases}Y,&x_0\in F\\\varnothing,&x_0\notin F.\end{cases}$$
Both pieces are Borel, so $s$ is Borel measurable. Finally, for every $y\in A$,
$$(\pi\circ s)(y)=\pi(g(y))=y,$$
and $\nu(A)=1$, so $\pi\circ s = \mathrm{id}_Y$ holds $\nu$-almost everywhere.

---
