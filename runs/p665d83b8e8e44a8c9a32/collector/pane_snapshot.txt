**Claim.** The solutions are exactly $f(x) = Ce^{x}$ for constants $C \ge 0$.

---

### Verification that $f(x)=Ce^{x}$, $C\ge 0$, works

$f' = Ce^{x}$, $f'' = Ce^{x}$, so $f' = f = f''$, hence $f' \ge \max\{f,f''\}$ h
olds with equality. Also $f = Ce^{x}\ge 0$. ∎

---

### Necessity

Assume $f:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)$ is twice differentiable with $f'(x)\ge \max\{
f(x),f''(x)\})$. Define
$$g(x) = e^{-x}f(x), \qquad u(x) = g'(x) = e^{-x}(f'(x)-f(x)).$$

**Step 1. $g\ge 0$ and $g'\ge 0$.** Since $f\ge 0$, clearly $g\ge 0$. Since $f'\
ge f$, we have $g' = e^{-x}(f'-f)\ge 0$, so $g$ is non‑decreasing.

**Step 2. A differential inequality for $u=g'$.** The condition $f'\ge f''$ give
s
$$f''-f' \le 0.$$
Compute
$$u'(x) = \frac{d}{dx}\bigl[e^{-x}(f'-f)\bigr] = e^{-x}(f''-f') - e^{-x}(f'-f) =
 e^{-x}(f''-2f'+f).$$
That is not directly useful; instead work with $h(x):=e^{-x}f'(x)$. Then
$$h'(x)=e^{-x}(f''-f')\le 0,$$
so $h$ is non‑increasing. Observe
$$h(x)=e^{-x}f'(x)=e^{-x}(f'-f)+e^{-x}f = g'(x)+g(x)=u(x)+g(x).$$
Hence $h=u+g$ is non‑increasing, i.e.
$$(u+g)' = u'+u \le 0. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Step 3. $u\equiv 0$.** From $(\ast)$, $(e^{x}u(x))' = e^{x}(u'+u)\le 0$, so $e
^{x}u(x)$ is non‑increasing. Thus for any $x\le x_{0}$,
$$e^{x}u(x)\ge e^{x_{0}}u(x_{0})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u(x)\ge u(x_{0})\,e^{x
_{0}-x}.$$

Suppose for contradiction that $u(x_{0})>0$ for some $x_{0}$. Then for $x<x_{0}$
,
$$g(x_{0})-g(x)=\int_{x}^{x_{0}} u(t)\,dt \;\ge\; u(x_{0})\int_{x}^{x_{0}} e^{x_
{0}-t}\,dt \;=\; u(x_{0})\bigl(e^{x_{0}-x}-1\bigr)\xrightarrow[x\to-\infty]{}+\i
nfty.$$
Therefore $g(x)\to -\infty$ as $x\to-\infty$, contradicting $g\ge 0$.

Hence $u(x_{0})=0$ for every $x_{0}$; i.e. $g'\equiv 0$.

**Step 4. Conclusion.** $g$ is constant, say $g\equiv C$. Since $g\ge 0$, $C\ge
0$. Then
$$f(x)=e^{x}g(x)=Ce^{x},\qquad C\ge 0.$$

Combined with the verification above, the solutions are precisely $\boxed{f(x)=C
e^{x},\ C\ge 0}$.
