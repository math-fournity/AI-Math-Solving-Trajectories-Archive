## Proof

The ellipse is $\frac{x^2}{4} + y^2 = 1$, with semi-major axis $a=2$ and semi-mi
nor axis $b=1$.

**Setting up coordinates.** An altitude of an equilateral triangle passes throug
h a vertex and is perpendicular to the opposite side. Since one altitude lies al
ong the $y$-axis, the vertex it passes through lies on the $y$-axis, and the opp
osite side is horizontal (perpendicular to the $y$-axis).

The ellipse meets the $y$-axis at $(0,1)$ and $(0,-1)$. The fixed vertex is $A=(
0,1)$, so the altitude through $A$ is the $y$-axis, and the opposite side $BC$ i
s horizontal. By symmetry about the $y$-axis, the other two vertices are $B=(x_0
, k)$ and $C=(-x_0, k)$ for some $k$.

**Using the ellipse constraint.** Since $B$ lies on the ellipse:
$$x_0^2 + 4k^2 = 4 \implies x_0^2 = 4(1-k^2) = 4(1-k)(1+k).$$

**Using the equilateral condition.** The side lengths are:
- $BC = 2x_0$
- $AB = \sqrt{x_0^2 + (1-k)^2}$

Setting $BC = AB$ and squaring:
$$4x_0^2 = x_0^2 + (1-k)^2 \implies 3x_0^2 = (1-k)^2.$$

Substituting $x_0^2 = 4(1-k)(1+k)$:
$$12(1-k)(1+k) = (1-k)^2.$$

Since $k=1$ would give $x_0=0$ (degenerate), we divide by $(1-k)$:
$$12(1+k) = 1-k \implies 12 + 12k = 1 - k \implies 13k = -11 \implies k = -\frac
{11}{13}.$$

**Computing the side length.**
$$x_0^2 = 4\left(1 - \frac{121}{169}\right) = 4\cdot\frac{48}{169} = \frac{192}{
169}.$$

The side length is $2x_0$, so:
$$\text{side length}^2 = 4x_0^2 = \frac{768}{169}.$$

Thus the side length is $\sqrt{\frac{768}{169}}$.

**Checking relative primality.** $768 = 2^8 \cdot 3$ and $169 = 13^2$, so $\gcd(
768, 169) = 1$. Hence $m = 768$, $n = 169$.

**Verification of the altitude condition.** The altitude from $A=(0,1)$ to the h
orizontal side $BC$ at $y = -11/13$ is the vertical line $x=0$, which is exactly
 the $y$-axis. ✓

$$m + n = 768 + 169 = \boxed{937}.$$
