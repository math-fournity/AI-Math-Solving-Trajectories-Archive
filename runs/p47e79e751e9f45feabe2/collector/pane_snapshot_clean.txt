## Proof

**Yes**, such a Hausdorff space $X$ and group $G$ exist.

### Construction

Let $X = \mathbb{R}$ equipped with the standard (usual) topology, which is Hausd
orff ($T_2$).

Let $G = (\mathbb{Q}, +)$, the additive group of rational numbers, act on $\math
bb{R}$ by translation:
$$q \cdot x = x + q, \qquad q \in \mathbb{Q},\; x \in \mathbb{R}.$$

**This is a valid group action:**
- **Identity:** $0 \cdot x = x + 0 = x$.
- **Compatibility:** $(q_1 + q_2)\cdot x = x + q_1 + q_2 = q_2 \cdot (q_1 \cdot
x)$.

Each $q \in \mathbb{Q}$ acts as the homeomorphism $x \mapsto x+q$ (continuous wi
th continuous inverse $x \mapsto x - q$), so this is an action by homeomorphisms
.

### The quotient $X/G = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}$ is not $T_2$

Let $\pi: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}$ be the quotient map. We show som
ething stronger: $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}$ is not even $T_1$.

Recall that a space is $T_1$ if and only if every singleton is closed. Consider
the point $[0] = \pi(0) \in \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}$, whose orbit is
$$\pi^{-1}(\{[0]\}) = 0 + \mathbb{Q} = \mathbb{Q} \subset \mathbb{R}.$$

By the definition of the quotient topology, $\{[0]\}$ is closed in $\mathbb{R}/\
mathbb{Q}$ if and only if $\pi^{-1}(\{[0]\}) = \mathbb{Q}$ is closed in $\mathbb
{R}$.

**But $\mathbb{Q}$ is not closed in $\mathbb{R}$:**
- $\mathbb{Q}$ is **dense** in $\mathbb{R}$: every nonempty open interval $(a,b)
$ contains a rational number.
- $\mathbb{Q} \neq \mathbb{R}$: irrational numbers (e.g., $\sqrt{2}$) exist.

A subset that is dense and not equal to the whole space cannot be closed (since
the only closed dense subset of a space is the space itself). Therefore $\mathbb
{Q}$ is not closed in $\mathbb{R}$.

Consequently, $\{[0]\}$ is not closed in $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}$, so $\mathbb{R}
/\mathbb{Q}$ is **not $T_1$**.

Since every $T_2$ space is $T_1$, the quotient $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}$ is **not
$T_2$**.

### Conclusion

We have exhibited a Hausdorff space $X = \mathbb{R}$ and a group $G = \mathbb{Q}
$ acting on $X$ by translations such that the quotient space $X/G = \mathbb{R}/\
mathbb{Q}$ is not $T_2$ (indeed, not even $T_1$). $\blacksquare$
