## Proof

We evaluate $I = \oint_{|z|=1} \frac{\tan z}{z^2}\,dz$, where $C$ is the unit ci
rcle traversed counterclockwise.

**Step 1: Identify the singularities of $f(z) = \frac{\tan z}{z^2} = \frac{\sin
z}{z^2 \cos z}$ inside $|z|=1$.**

The singularities occur where:
- $z^2 = 0$, i.e., $z = 0$;
- $\cos z = 0$, i.e., $z = \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$ for $k \in \mathbb{Z}$.

The zeros of $\cos z$ nearest the origin are $z = \pm\frac{\pi}{2} \approx \pm 1
.5708$. Since $\frac{\pi}{2} > 1$, **no zero of $\cos z$ lies inside the unit ci
rcle**. Thus the only singularity of $f$ inside $|z|=1$ is at $z = 0$.

**Step 2: Determine the nature of the singularity at $z=0$ and compute the resid
ue.**

Recall the Taylor expansion $\tan z = z + \frac{z^3}{3} + \frac{2z^5}{15} + \cdo
ts$ for $|z| < \frac{\pi}{2}$. Therefore

$$
\frac{\tan z}{z^2} = \frac{1}{z^2}\left(z + \frac{z^3}{3} + \frac{2z^5}{15} + \c
dots\right) = \frac{1}{z} + \frac{z}{3} + \frac{2z^3}{15} + \cdots
$$

This Laurent expansion shows that $z = 0$ is a **simple pole** with residue equa
l to the coefficient of $1/z$, namely

$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} \frac{\tan z}{z^2} = 1.
$$

**Step 3: Apply the Residue Theorem.**

Since $z=0$ is the only singularity enclosed by $C$, and $C$ is positively orien
ted,

$$
I = \oint_{|z|=1} \frac{\tan z}{z^2}\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}_{z=0}
 \frac{\tan z}{z^2} = 2\pi i \cdot 1 = 2\pi i.
$$
