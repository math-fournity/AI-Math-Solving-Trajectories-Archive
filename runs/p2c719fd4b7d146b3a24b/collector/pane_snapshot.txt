## Note on the Problem Statement

The set $\mathcal{M}$ is referenced but **not defined** in the problem statement
. I will solve the problem under the natural and standard interpretation that $\
mathcal{M}$ is a class of (measurable / $L^2$ / continuous) functions on $[-1,1]
$ that contains $f(x) = x^2$ — this covers the usual settings in which such a pr
oblem is posed. If $\mathcal{M}$ carries an additional constraint excluding $x^2
$, the answer would change accordingly.

## Solution

**Step 1: Simplify the integrand.**

Expand the expression inside the integral:

$$
(f(x) - x^2)^2 - x^4 = f(x)^2 - 2x^2 f(x) + x^4 - x^4 = f(x)^2 - 2x^2 f(x).
$$

**Step 2: Complete the square.**

Rewrite the integrand by completing the square in $f(x)$:

$$
f(x)^2 - 2x^2 f(x) = (f(x) - x^2)^2 - x^4.
$$

(This is just the original form, confirming consistency.) Therefore

$$
\phi(f) = \int_{-1}^{1} (f(x) - x^2)^2\, dx - \int_{-1}^{1} x^4\, dx.
$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the constant term.**

$$
\int_{-1}^{1} x^4\, dx = 2\int_{0}^{1} x^4\, dx = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{5} = \frac{2}
{5}.
$$

Hence

$$
\phi(f) = \int_{-1}^{1} (f(x) - x^2)^2\, dx - \frac{2}{5}.
$$

**Step 4: Bound the functional from below.**

Since $(f(x) - x^2)^2 \geq 0$ for every $x$, the integral is non-negative:

$$
\int_{-1}^{1} (f(x) - x^2)^2\, dx \geq 0.
$$

Therefore

$$
\phi(f) \geq -\frac{2}{5}
$$

for every $f \in \mathcal{M}$.

**Step 5: Show the bound is attained (hence it is the infimum, indeed the minimu
m).**

Take $f_0(x) = x^2$. Then $(f_0(x) - x^2)^2 = 0$ for all $x$, so

$$
\phi(f_0) = 0 - \frac{2}{5} = -\frac{2}{5}.
$$

Provided $f_0 = x^2 \in \mathcal{M}$ (which holds for all standard function clas
ses: measurable functions, $L^p([-1,1])$ for any $p \geq 1$, $C([-1,1])$, polyno
mials, etc.), the lower bound is achieved.

**Conclusion.**

$$
\boxed{\inf_{f \in \mathcal{M}} \phi(f) = -\dfrac{2}{5}},
$$

attained at $f(x) = x^2$.
