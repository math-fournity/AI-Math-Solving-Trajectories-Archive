## Proof

Let $C$ be a nonsingular plane curve of degree $d$, so its genus is $g = \frac{(
d-1)(d-2)}{2}$. Let $D = (2g+2)\,q$. We seek $f \in L(D)$ (rational functions wi
th no poles except possibly at $q$, of order $\leq 2g+2$) satisfying:
- $\operatorname{ord}_q(f) = -(2g+2)$ (exact pole order $2g+2$ at $q$, i.e. $f \
in L(D)\setminus L(D-q)$),
- $f(p_1) = a_1$ and $f(p_2) = a_2$.

**Dimensions via Riemann–Roch.** Since $\deg D = 2g+2 > 2g-2 = \deg K_C$, the di
visor $D$ is non-special, so
$$\ell(D) = \deg D + 1 - g = (2g+2)+1-g = g+3.$$
Likewise $\deg(D-q) = 2g+1 > 2g-2$, so $\ell(D-q) = (2g+1)+1-g = g+2$.

The functions with **exact** pole order $2g+2$ at $q$ form the Zariski-open set
$$V := L(D)\setminus L(D-q), \qquad \dim L(D) = g+3,\ \dim L(D-q)=g+2.$$

**Evaluation map.** Consider $\operatorname{ev}: L(D) \to \mathbb{C}^2$, $f\maps
to (f(p_1),f(p_2))$ (well-defined since $p_i\neq q$, so every $f\in L(D)$ is reg
ular at $p_i$). Its kernel is $L(D-p_1-p_2)$. Now $\deg(D-p_1-p_2)=2g \geq 2g-2$
, so this is non-special and
$$\ell(D-p_1-p_2) = 2g+1-g = g+1.$$
Hence $\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{ev})$ has dimension $(g+3)-(g+1)=2$; i.e.
 **$\operatorname{ev}: L(D)\to\mathbb{C}^2$ is surjective** for every $g\geq 0$
(for $g=0$: $\deg(D-p_1-p_2)=0$, $\ell=1=g+1$, still giving image dimension $2$)
.

**The fiber is not trapped in $L(D-q)$.** Fix $(a_1,a_2)\in\mathbb{C}^2$ and pic
k $f_0\in L(D)$ with $\operatorname{ev}(f_0)=(a_1,a_2)$. The full fiber is the a
ffine subspace
$$\operatorname{ev}^{-1}(a_1,a_2) = f_0 + L(D-p_1-p_2), \qquad \dim = g+1.$$
We claim this fiber is **not** contained in $L(D-q)$. Indeed,
$$L(D-p_1-p_2)\cap L(D-q) = L\bigl(\min(D-p_1-p_2,\;D-q)\bigr) = L(D-q-p_1-p_2),
$$
and $\deg(D-q-p_1-p_2)=2g-1\geq 2g-2$ (for $g\geq 1$; for $g=0$, $\deg=-1$), giv
ing
$$\ell(D-q-p_1-p_2) = \begin{cases} g & g\geq 1,\\ 0 & g=0, \end{cases} \quad =
g \text{ in all cases.}$$
Since $\ell(D-p_1-p_2)=g+1 > g = \ell(D-q-p_1-p_2)$, the inclusion $L(D-p_1-p_2)
\subseteq L(D-q)$ is **strictly false**: there exist elements of $L(D-p_1-p_2)$
lying outside $L(D-q)$. Consequently, no translate $f_0 + L(D-p_1-p_2)$ can be c
ontained in the linear subspace $L(D-q)$.

**Conclusion.** For every $(a_1,a_2)$, the fiber $f_0+L(D-p_1-p_2)$ contains an
element $f\notin L(D-q)$, i.e. an $f\in L(D)$ with $\operatorname{ord}_q(f)=-(2g
+2)$ and $f(p_1)=a_1$, $f(p_2)=a_2$.

Therefore, **for every choice of complex numbers $a_1$ and $a_2$, such a rationa
l function $f$ exists.** The key points are: (i) $\ell(D)=g+3\geq 3$ gives enoug
h dimensions, (ii) Riemann–Roch shows the evaluation map $L(D)\to\mathbb{C}^2$ i
s surjective, and (iii) the strict inclusion $\ell(D-p_1-p_2)>\ell(D-q-p_1-p_2)$
 guarantees the fiber always escapes the lower-pole-order subspace $L(D-q)$, so
the exact pole order $2g+2$ can always be achieved.
