## Proof

**Answer: No.**

**Counterexample.** Consider three vectors in $\mathbb{C}^2$:
$$u = (1,\, 1), \quad v = (1,\, 1), \quad w = (1,\, 1).$$

Each pair of vectors is nonzero at both positions 1 and 2, so every pair has exa
ctly **2 overlaps**, satisfying the "at least two overlaps" condition.

**Allowed operation.** We may change any nonzero entry to a complex number of ma
gnitude 1. Since every entry already has magnitude 1, any allowed operation mere
ly replaces an entry with $e^{i\theta}$ for some phase $\theta$. After any seque
nce of such operations, the vectors take the form
$$u = (e^{ia},\, e^{ib}), \quad v = (e^{ic},\, e^{id}), \quad w = (e^{ie},\, e^{
if})$$
for real numbers $a, b, c, d, e, f$.

**Orthogonality requirements.** For pairwise orthogonality we need all three inn
er products to vanish:

1. $\langle u, v\rangle = e^{i(a-c)} + e^{i(b-d)} = 0$
2. $\langle u, w\rangle = e^{i(a-e)} + e^{i(b-f)} = 0$
3. $\langle v, w\rangle = e^{i(c-e)} + e^{i(d-f)} = 0$

**Deriving a contradiction.** Each condition states that two unit-modulus comple
x numbers sum to zero, so they must be exact negatives of each other. This yield
s the phase relations (all modulo $2\pi$):

- From (1): $a - c \equiv b - d + \pi$, i.e., $(a-b) - (c-d) \equiv \pi$.
- From (2): $a - e \equiv b - f + \pi$, i.e., $(a-b) - (e-f) \equiv \pi$.
- From (3): $c - e \equiv d - f + \pi$, i.e., $(c-d) - (e-f) \equiv \pi$.

Subtracting the second relation from the first:
$$(c - d) \equiv (e - f) \pmod{2\pi}.$$

But the third relation requires:
$$(c - d) - (e - f) \equiv \pi \pmod{2\pi}.$$

Substituting $(c-d) \equiv (e-f)$ gives $0 \equiv \pi \pmod{2\pi}$, which is a *
*contradiction**.

**Conclusion.** No assignment of unit-magnitude complex values to the nonzero en
tries can make all three vectors pairwise orthogonal. This counterexample—three
identical vectors $(1,1)$ in $\mathbb{C}^2$, with each pair sharing exactly 2 ov
erlaps—shows that it is **not always possible** to transform such a tuple $S$ in
to an orthogonal set under the stated operation.
