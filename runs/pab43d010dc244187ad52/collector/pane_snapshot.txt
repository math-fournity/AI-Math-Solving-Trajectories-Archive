Looking at this problem, I need to count the ways to assign each of three distin
guishable grandsons a denomination (50, 20, or 10 yuan) and a positive integer c
ount, such that the total is 400 yuan and at least one grandson's count equals t
he product of the other two's counts.

## Setup

Let the grandsons be A, B, C with denominations $d_A, d_B, d_C \in \{50, 20, 10\
}$ and counts $a, b, c \geq 1$, satisfying:
$$d_A \cdot a + d_B \cdot b + d_C \cdot c = 400$$

The condition is: $a = bc$ **or** $b = ac$ **or** $c = ab$.

I use inclusion-exclusion: $|A \cup B \cup C| = 3|A| - 3|A \cap B| + |A \cap B \
cap C|$, where $A$ is the event $a = bc$, etc.

## Computing $|A|$: the case $a = bc$

Substituting: $d_A \cdot bc + d_B \cdot b + d_C \cdot c = 400$, i.e., $b = \frac
{400 - d_C \cdot c}{d_A \cdot c + d_B}$, requiring $b$ to be a positive integer.

I enumerate all 27 denomination triples $(d_A, d_B, d_C)$, grouped by $d_A$:

**$d_A = 50$** (5 solutions): $(50,50,50){:}(b,c)=(2,2)$; $(50,50,20){:}(1,5)$;
$(50,20,50){:}(5,1)$; $(50,20,20){:}(3,2),(2,3)$.

**$d_A = 20$** (13 solutions): $(20,50,50){:}(5,1),(1,5)$; $(20,50,20){:}(4,2),(
2,5)$; $(20,50,10){:}(2,6)$; $(20,20,50){:}(5,2),(2,4)$; $(20,20,20){:}(2,6),(6,
2)$; $(20,10,50){:}(6,2)$; $(20,10,10){:}(13,1),(4,4),(1,13)$.

**$d_A = 10$** (18 solutions): $(10,50,20){:}(3,5)$; $(10,50,10){:}(4,4),(2,10)$
; $(10,20,50){:}(5,3)$; $(10,20,20){:}(9,2),(2,9)$; $(10,20,10){:}(13,1),(6,4),(
5,5),(2,12),(1,19)$; $(10,10,50){:}(10,2),(4,4)$; $(10,10,20){:}(19,1),(12,2),(5
,5),(4,6),(1,13)$.

**Total $|A| = 5 + 13 + 18 = 36$.**

By symmetry (permuting grandsons), $|A| = |B| = |C| = 36$.

## Computing $|A \cap B|$: both $a = bc$ and $b = ac$

From $a = bc$ and $b = ac$: substituting gives $b = bc^2$, so $c = 1$ and $a = b
$. The equation becomes $a(d_A + d_B) + d_C = 400$.

Enumerating all ordered pairs $(d_A, d_B)$ and $d_C$:

| $(d_A, d_B)$ | $d_C$ | $a$ | Valid? |
|---|---|---|---|
| $(50, 20)$ | $50$ | $350/70 = 5$ | ✓ |
| $(20, 50)$ | $50$ | $350/70 = 5$ | ✓ |
| $(20, 10)$ | $10$ | $390/30 = 13$ | ✓ |
| $(10, 20)$ | $10$ | $390/30 = 13$ | ✓ |
| $(10, 10)$ | $20$ | $380/20 = 19$ | ✓ |

**$|A \cap B| = 5$.** By symmetry, $|A \cap C| = |B \cap C| = 5$.

## Computing $|A \cap B \cap C|$

All three conditions force $a = b = c = 1$, giving $d_A + d_B + d_C = 400$, impo
ssible since the maximum is $150$. So **$|A \cap B \cap C| = 0$**.

## Final Count

$$|A \cup B \cup C| = 3 \times 36 - 3 \times 5 + 0 = 108 - 15 = \boxed{93}$$

## Verification of representative solutions

- $(d_A, d_B, d_C) = (50, 50, 50)$, $(a, b, c) = (4, 2, 2)$: $200 + 100 + 100 =
400$ ✓, $4 = 2 \times 2$ ✓
- $(d_A, d_B, d_C) = (10, 20, 10)$, $(a, b, c) = (25, 5, 5)$: $250 + 100 + 50 =
400$ ✓, $25 = 5 \times 5$ ✓
- Overlap case $(d_A, d_B, d_C) = (50, 20, 50)$, $(a, b, c) = (5, 5, 1)$: $250 +
 100 + 50 = 400$ ✓, $5 = 5 \times 1$ and $5 = 5 \times 1$ ✓ (both A and B satisf
y)

The phrase "其中一人的张数恰好等于另外两人张数之积" (one of them has count exact
ly equal to the product of the other two) is an existential condition — at least
 one grandson satisfies it — so inclusion-exclusion over the union is the correc
t approach.

**The answer is 93.**
