# Solution

## Setup and Key Facts

Let $\lambda = 1 - \zeta_5$ in the cyclotomic integer ring $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_5]$
, where $[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5):\mathbb{Q}] = \phi(5) = 4$.

**Fact 1 (Total ramification of 5).** The rational prime $5$ is totally ramified
 in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_5]$:
$$(5) = (\lambda)^4, \quad \text{i.e., } v_\lambda(5) = 4.$$
This follows from the standard cyclotomic theory: $N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)/\mathb
b{Q}}(1-\zeta_5) = \Phi_5(1) = 5$, so $(\lambda)$ is the unique prime ideal abov
e $5$, with ramification index $e = 4$.

**Fact 2 ($\lambda$-adic valuation of rational integers).** For any $m \in \math
bb{Z}$, write $m = 5^a \cdot k$ with $\gcd(k,5)=1$. Since $(\lambda)$ is the **o
nly** prime above $5$, the ideal $(k)$ is coprime to $(\lambda)$, so $v_\lambda(
k) = 0$. Therefore:
$$v_\lambda(m) = a \cdot v_\lambda(5) = 4a = 4\,v_5(m).$$

## Derivation

The congruence $n \equiv 1 \pmod{\lambda^5}$ means $\lambda^5 \mid (n-1)$ in $\m
athbb{Z}[\zeta_5]$, i.e.,
$$v_\lambda(n-1) \geq 5.$$

For $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, we have $n - 1 \in \mathbb{Z}$, so by Fact 2:
$$v_\lambda(n-1) = 4\,v_5(n-1).$$

The condition $v_\lambda(n-1) \geq 5$ becomes:
$$4\,v_5(n-1) \geq 5 \iff v_5(n-1) \geq \tfrac{5}{4} \iff v_5(n-1) \geq 2,$$
where the last step uses that $v_5(n-1)$ is a non-negative integer. The conditio
n $v_5(n-1) \geq 2$ is equivalent to:
$$25 \mid (n-1).$$

## Verification via the Minimal Polynomial

To confirm $v_\lambda(5)=4$ concretely, substitute $\zeta_5 = 1-\lambda$ into $\
Phi_5(\zeta_5)=0$:
$$(1-\lambda)^4+(1-\lambda)^3+(1-\lambda)^2+(1-\lambda)+1=0,$$
which expands to:
$$\lambda^4 - 5\lambda^3 + 10\lambda^2 - 10\lambda + 5 = 0.$$
Rearranging: $5 = \lambda(10 - 10\lambda + 5\lambda^2 - \lambda^3)$. Setting $\a
lpha = 10-10\lambda+5\lambda^2-\lambda^3$, we get $N(\alpha) = N(5)/N(\lambda) =
 5^4/5 = 5^3 = 125$. Since $N(\alpha)=5^3$, the element $\alpha$ has $v_\lambda(
\alpha)=3$, so $5 = \lambda \cdot \lambda^3 \cdot (\text{unit}) = \lambda^4 \cdo
t (\text{unit})$, confirming $v_\lambda(5)=4$ and $(\lambda^4)=(5)$.

**Concrete checks:**
- $n=26$: $n-1=25=5^2$, $v_\lambda=8\geq 5$ ✓, and $26\equiv 1\pmod{25}$ ✓
- $n=6$: $n-1=5$, $v_\lambda=4<5$ ✗, and $6\not\equiv 1\pmod{25}$ ✓

## Conclusion

For $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, the congruence $n \equiv 1 \pmod{\lambda^5}$ in $\mathbb
{Z}[\zeta_5]$ simplifies to the integer congruence:

$$\boxed{n \equiv 1 \pmod{25}}.$$

The key mechanism is that $5$ is totally ramified as $(5)=(\lambda)^4$, so the $
\lambda$-adic valuation of any rational integer is always a multiple of $4$. The
 threshold $v_\lambda \geq 5$ is thus met precisely at the next multiple of $4$
that is $\geq 5$, namely $8 = 4 \times 2$, corresponding to $v_5 \geq 2$, i.e.,
divisibility by $25$.
