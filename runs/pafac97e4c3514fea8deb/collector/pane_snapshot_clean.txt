## Proof

**Claim.** If $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is a bounded open set with $C^1$ bou
ndary and $u \in W^{1,p}(\Omega)$ with $p > n$, then $u$ has a continuous repres
entative on $\overline{\Omega}$. In particular, $u$ is continuous in $\Omega$.

We prove this in three steps: (1) interior Hölder continuity via the Morrey ineq
uality, (2) extension to a neighborhood of the boundary using the $C^1$ boundary
 hypothesis, and (3) conclusion.

---

### Step 1. Interior estimate (Morrey's inequality on balls)

Let $B_R(x_0) \subset \Omega$ be a ball of radius $R$ centered at $x_0$. We clai
m the key inequality

$$\frac{1}{|B_R|}\int_{B_R(x_0)} |u(x) - u_{x_0,R}|\,dx \;\le\; C\, R\!\left(\fr
ac{1}{|B_R|}\int_{B_R(x_0)} |\nabla u|^p\,dx\right)^{1/p}, \tag{1}$$

where $u_{x_0,R} = \frac{1}{|B_R|}\int_{B_R(x_0)} u\,dx$ is the average and $C =
 C(n,p)$.

*Proof of (1).* By density, it suffices to prove it for $u \in C^1(B_R(x_0))$; t
he general case follows by approximation. For $x, y \in B_R(x_0)$, write $y = x
+ t\,\omega$ with $t = |x-y|$, $\omega = (y-x)/|y-x|$. Then

$$u(y) - u(x) = \int_0^{|x-y|} \nabla u(x + s\omega)\cdot \omega\, ds.$$

Integrate over all pairs $(x,y) \in B_R \times B_R$. A standard computation (int
egrating in polar-type coordinates and using Hölder's inequality) gives

$$\int_{B_R}\int_{B_R} |u(x)-u(y)|\,dx\,dy \;\le\; C\, R^{n+1}\!\left(\int_{B_R}
 |\nabla u|^p\,dx\right)^{1/p} R^{n(1-1/p)}.$$

Dividing by $|B_R|^2 \sim R^{2n}$ and using that $\int\int |u(x)-u(y)| = 2|B_R|\
int|u - u_{x_0,R}|$ yields (1). $\square$

---

### Step 2. Hölder continuity in the interior

From (1) and the Markov/Chebyshev-type argument, one derives the **Morrey inequa
lity**: for a.e. $x, y \in B_R(x_0)$,

$$|u(x) - u(y)| \;\le\; C\,|x-y|^{1-n/p}\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^p(B_R(x_0))}\,|B_R|^{1
/p - 1}.$$

More precisely, the standard form is: for a.e. $x \in B_R(x_0)$,

$$|u(x) - u_{x_0,R}| \;\le\; C\, R^{1-n/p}\!\left(\int_{B_R(x_0)}|\nabla u|^p\ri
ght)^{1/p}. \tag{2}$$

This is obtained from (1) by a **Lebesgue-point / Calderón–Zygmund-type argument
**: the set of points where $|u(x) - u_{x_0,R}|$ exceeds $k$ times the right-han
d side has measure $\le |B_R|/k^{p/(p-1)}$; iterating this over a sequence of sh
rinking balls $B_{R/2^j}(x_0)$ and summing a geometric series yields (2) at Lebe
sgue points.

Set $\alpha = 1 - n/p \in (0,1)$ (since $p > n$). Applying (2) at a Lebesgue poi
nt $x$ with $B_R(x_0) = B_{2|x-y|}(x) \ni y$ (and symmetrically), and using that
 $\Omega$ is bounded so $\|\nabla u\|_{L^p(\Omega)} < \infty$, we get

$$|u(x) - u(y)| \;\le\; C\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^p(\Omega)}\,|x-y|^{1-n/p} \;=\; C\,\|
\nabla u\|_{L^p(\Omega)}\,|x-y|^{\alpha} \tag{3}$$

for a.e. $x, y \in \Omega$ with $|x-y|$ small, where $C = C(n,p,\Omega)$.

Inequality (3) shows that $u$ (on the set where it is finite) is **Hölder contin
uous of exponent $\alpha = 1 - n/p$** in the interior. Redefining $u$ on the mea
sure-zero set of non-Lebesgue points by the limit of averages (which exists and
is consistent by (3)), we obtain a representative — still denoted $u$ — that is
Hölder continuous, hence continuous, on every compact $K \subset \Omega$.

---

### Step 3. Behavior near the $C^1$ boundary

It remains to show continuity up to (and hence at) the boundary. Since $\partial
\Omega$ is $C^1$, there is a finite cover of $\partial\Omega$ by coordinate patc
hes and $C^1$ diffeomorphisms that **flatten the boundary**: in a neighborhood $
U$ of each boundary point, there exists a $C^1$ diffeomorphism $\Phi: U \to B_1(
0)$ mapping $U \cap \Omega$ to $B_1^+(0) = B_1(0)\cap\{y_n > 0\}$ and $U\cap\par
tial\Omega$ to $\{y_n = 0\}$.

Because $\Phi$ is $C^1$ with $C^1$ inverse, the composition $v = u \circ \Phi^{-
1}$ belongs to $W^{1,p}(B_1^+(0))$ (Sobolev spaces are preserved under $C^1$ dif
feomorphisms, with equivalent norms). Extend $v$ to all of $B_1(0)$ by **even re
flection** across $\{y_n = 0\}$:

$$\tilde v(y', y_n) = \begin{cases} v(y', y_n), & y_n \ge 0,\\ v(y', -y_n), & y_
n < 0. \end{cases}$$

Since $v \in W^{1,p}(B_1^+)$, the even reflection satisfies $\tilde v \in W^{1,p
}(B_1(0))$ (the reflection preserves the weak derivative across the flat boundar
y $\{y_n=0\}$; this is the standard extension for flat boundaries). Now $p > n$,
 so by the interior result of Step 2 applied to $\tilde v$ on $B_1(0)$, the func
tion $\tilde v$ has a Hölder-continuous representative on $B_1(0)$. Restricting
back, $v = \tilde v|_{B_1^+}$ is Hölder continuous on $\overline{B_1^+}$, and pu
lling back by $\Phi^{-1}$ (a $C^1$ diffeomorphism, which preserves Hölder contin
uity), $u$ is Hölder continuous on $\overline{U \cap \Omega}$.

Covering $\overline{\Omega}$ by finitely many such patches (interior balls + bou
ndary patches), and using that the Hölder estimates are consistent on overlaps (
both sides equal the $L^p$-representative a.e., hence everywhere after redefinit
ion), we conclude:

$$u \in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}), \qquad \alpha = 1 - \frac{n}{p} \in (0,
1),$$

with the estimate

$$\|u\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})} \;\le\; C\,\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}, \q
quad C = C(n,p,\Omega).$$

---

### Conclusion

The function $u \in W^{1,p}(\Omega)$ with $p > n$ admits a representative that i
s Hölder continuous (with exponent $1 - n/p$) on $\overline{\Omega}$, hence in p
articular **continuous on $\Omega$**. The hypotheses used are precisely: $p > n$
 (for the Morrey embedding), boundedness of $\Omega$ (for the uniform norm bound
), and $C^1$ regularity of $\partial\Omega$ (for the local flattening and even-r
eflection extension near the boundary).

**Therefore, $u$ is continuous in $\Omega$.** $\blacksquare$
