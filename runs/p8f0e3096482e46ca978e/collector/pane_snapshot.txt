## Solution

We evaluate $\int_{\gamma} \frac{dz}{z\sin z}$ where $\gamma(t)=e^{it}$ is the u
nit circle traversed once counterclockwise.

**Singularities inside $|z|=1$.** The integrand $f(z)=\frac{1}{z\sin z}$ is sing
ular where $z\sin z=0$, i.e. at $z=0$ and at $z=n\pi$ ($n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\
{0\}$). Since $\pi>1$, the only singularity with $|z|<1$ is $z=0$.

**Nature of the singularity at $z=0$.** Expanding $\sin z = z - \frac{z^{3}}{6}+
\frac{z^{5}}{120}-\cdots$, we get
$$z\sin z = z^{2}\!\left(1-\frac{z^{2}}{6}+\frac{z^{4}}{120}-\cdots\right),$$
so $z\sin z$ has a zero of order $2$ at the origin. Hence $f$ has a **pole of or
der $2$** at $z=0$.

**Residue computation.** For a pole of order $2$,
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,0)=\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{d}{dz}\!\left[z^{2}f(z)\right]=\lim
_{z\to 0}\frac{d}{dz}\!\left[\frac{z}{\sin z}\right].$$

Differentiating,
$$\frac{d}{dz}\frac{z}{\sin z}=\frac{\sin z - z\cos z}{\sin^{2}z}.$$

Using the expansions $\sin z = z-\frac{z^{3}}{6}+O(z^{5})$ and $z\cos z = z-\fra
c{z^{3}}{2}+O(z^{5})$:
$$\sin z - z\cos z = \left(z-\frac{z^{3}}{6}\right)-\left(z-\frac{z^{3}}{2}\righ
t)+O(z^{5})=\frac{z^{3}}{3}+O(z^{5}),$$
$$\sin^{2}z = z^{2}+O(z^{4}).$$

Therefore
$$\frac{\sin z - z\cos z}{\sin^{2}z}=\frac{z^{3}/3+O(z^{5})}{z^{2}+O(z^{4})}=\fr
ac{z}{3}+O(z^{3})\xrightarrow{\,z\to 0\,}0.$$

Equivalently, the Laurent series is
$$\frac{1}{z\sin z}=\frac{1}{z^{2}}+\frac{1}{6}+\frac{7z^{2}}{360}+\cdots,$$
confirming the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ (the residue) is $0$.

**Applying the residue theorem.** Since $z=0$ is the only singularity enclosed b
y $\gamma$,
$$\int_{\gamma}\frac{dz}{z\sin z}=2\pi i\cdot\operatorname{Res}(f,0)=2\pi i\cdot
 0=0.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}\frac{dz}{z\sin z}=0}$$
