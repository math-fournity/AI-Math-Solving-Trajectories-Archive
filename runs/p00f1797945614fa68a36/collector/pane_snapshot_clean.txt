## Theorem

**Let $(X_n)_{n\geq 1}$ be a sequence of lattice-valued random variables and $Y$
 a lattice-valued random variable, all supported on a common lattice $L = \{a +
kh : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ (for some $a \in \mathbb{R}$, $h > 0$). Then $X_n$ admi
ts a local limit law converging to $Y$, i.e.,**

$$\sup_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} \bigl|P(X_n = a+kh) - P(Y = a+kh)\bigr| \to 0,$$

**if and only if $X_n \Rightarrow Y$ in distribution.**

**The essential condition is *lattice compatibility*: $X_n$ and $Y$ must be supp
orted on a common lattice. This is what upgrades convergence in distribution (a
"global" limit law) to convergence of probability mass functions (a "local" limi
t law).**

## Proof

### ($\Rightarrow$) Local convergence implies distributional convergence.

Assume $\sup_k |P(X_n = a+kh) - P(Y = a+kh)| \to 0$. Since both $X_n$ and $Y$ ar
e supported on $L$, for any $x \in \mathbb{R}$:

$$\bigl|F_{X_n}(x) - F_Y(x)\bigr| = \left|\sum_{\substack{k \in \mathbb{Z} \\ a+
kh \leq x}} \bigl[P(X_n = a+kh) - P(Y = a+kh)\bigr]\right| \leq \sum_{k} \bigl|P
(X_n = a+kh) - P(Y = a+kh)\bigr|.$$

By **Scheffé's lemma**: pointwise convergence $p_n(k) \to p(k)$ with $\sum_k p_n
(k) = \sum_k p(k) = 1$ implies $\sum_k |p_n(k) - p(k)| \to 0$. Hence $|F_{X_n}(x
) - F_Y(x)| \to 0$ for all $x$, giving $X_n \Rightarrow Y$. $\checkmark$

### ($\Leftarrow$) Distributional convergence + lattice compatibility implies lo
cal convergence.

Assume $X_n \Rightarrow Y$ and both are supported on $L = \{a + kh\}$.

**Key observation.** For any $k_0 \in \mathbb{Z}$ and any $\epsilon \in (0, h)$:
- The points $a + k_0 h + \epsilon$ and $a + k_0 h - \epsilon$ are **not** in $L
$, so $Y$ has no atom there. Thus they are **continuity points** of $F_Y$.
- Since $X_n$ is supported on $L$ and $\epsilon < h$, the interval $(a + k_0 h -
 \epsilon,\; a + k_0 h + \epsilon)$ contains exactly **one** lattice point of $L
$, namely $a + k_0 h$. Therefore:

$$P(X_n = a + k_0 h) = F_{X_n}(a + k_0 h + \epsilon) - F_{X_n}(a + k_0 h - \epsi
lon).$$

By convergence in distribution at the continuity points $a + k_0 h \pm \epsilon$
:

$$F_{X_n}(a + k_0 h + \epsilon) \to F_Y(a + k_0 h + \epsilon), \qquad F_{X_n}(a
+ k_0 h - \epsilon) \to F_Y(a + k_0 h - \epsilon).$$

Subtracting:

$$P(X_n = a + k_0 h) \to F_Y(a + k_0 h + \epsilon) - F_Y(a + k_0 h - \epsilon) =
 P(Y = a + k_0 h),$$

where the last equality holds because $Y$ is supported on $L$ and the interval $
(a+k_0 h - \epsilon, a+k_0 h+\epsilon)$ contains only the lattice point $a + k_0
 h$.

This establishes **pointwise convergence** of PMFs: $P(X_n = a+kh) \to P(Y = a+k
h)$ for every $k \in \mathbb{Z}$.

Applying **Scheffé's lemma** once more (pointwise convergence + equal total mass
 1):

$$\sum_{k} \bigl|P(X_n = a+kh) - P(Y = a+kh)\bigr| \to 0 \implies \sup_k \bigl|P
(X_n = a+kh) - P(Y = a+kh)\bigr| \to 0. \quad \checkmark$$

## Necessity of Lattice Compatibility

The lattice condition is **essential**: without it, distributional convergence d
oes **not** imply local convergence.

**Counterexample.** Let $Y \equiv 0$ (point mass at $0$) and $X_n \equiv 1/n$. T
hen $X_n \Rightarrow Y$ in distribution, since $P(X_n \leq x) \to \mathbf{1}_{[0
,\infty)}(x) = F_Y(x)$ at all $x \neq 0$. But $P(X_n = 0) = 0$ for all $n$, whil
e $P(Y = 0) = 1$. The local limit law **fails** because $X_n$ is not on the same
 lattice as $Y$ — the mass of $X_n$ "slides" along a shifting lattice and never
lands on the atom of $Y$.

## Equivalent Characterization via Characteristic Functions

The local limit law is equivalent to **$L^1$-convergence of characteristic funct
ions on the fundamental domain**:

$$\int_{-\pi/h}^{\pi/h} \bigl|\phi_{X_n}(t) - \phi_Y(t)\bigr|\, dt \to 0.$$

This follows from the **Fourier inversion formula on the lattice**: for any latt
ice-valued $X$ on $L = \{a+kh\}$,

$$P(X = a+kh) = \frac{h}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi/h}^{\pi/h} \phi_X(t)\, e^{-it(a+kh)}\,
dt,$$

so

$$\sup_k \bigl|P(X_n = a+kh) - P(Y = a+kh)\bigr| \leq \frac{h}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi/h
}^{\pi/h} \bigl|\phi_{X_n}(t) - \phi_Y(t)\bigr|\, dt.$$

Conversely, by Parseval's identity on the lattice:

$$\sum_k \bigl|P(X_n = a+kh) - P(Y = a+kh)\bigr|^2 = \frac{h}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi/h}
^{\pi/h} \bigl|\phi_{X_n}(t) - \phi_Y(t)\bigr|^2\, dt,$$

so $L^2$-convergence of characteristic functions is equivalent to $\ell^2$-conve
rgence of PMFs.

The lattice compatibility condition itself can be read off from the characterist
ic function: $X$ is supported on a lattice with span $h$ if and only if $|\phi_X
(2\pi/h)| = 1$ (i.e., $e^{i(2\pi/h)X}$ is a.s. constant). The **maximal span** i
s the largest such $h$, and the common-lattice condition requires that the maxim
al spans of $X_n$ and $Y$ be **compatible** (the span of $Y$'s lattice divides t
he span of $X_n$'s lattice, so that $L_Y \subseteq L_{X_n}$).

## Summary

**The condition for a sequence of discrete random variables $X_n$ to admit a loc
al limit law converging to a discrete random variable $Y$ is *lattice compatibil
ity*: $X_n$ and $Y$ must be supported on a common lattice $L = \{a + kh : k \in
\mathbb{Z}\}$ for all sufficiently large $n$.**

Under this condition:
- Convergence in distribution $X_n \Rightarrow Y$ is **equivalent** to local con
vergence $\sup_k |P(X_n = a+kh) - P(Y = a+kh)| \to 0$.
- The proof uses the fact that on a common lattice, the CDF at continuity points
 (non-lattice points) determines the point probabilities, since each lattice poi
nt is isolated in an interval of length $< h$.
- Equivalently, the local limit law holds if and only if the characteristic func
tions converge in $L^1([-
                         pi/h, \pi/h])$.

Without lattice compatibility, distributional convergence is strictly weaker tha
n local convergence — mass can "escape" to off-lattice points or shift between i
ncompatible lattices, breaking pointwise convergence of probability mass functio
ns.
