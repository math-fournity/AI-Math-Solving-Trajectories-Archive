## Theorem (Wolpert's Lemma)

Let $f: X \to Y$ be a $K$-quasiconformal homeomorphism between hyperbolic Rieman
n surfaces, and let $\gamma$ be a closed geodesic on $X$ of length $\ell_X(\gamm
a)$. Let $\gamma'$ be the unique closed geodesic on $Y$ freely homotopic to $f(\
gamma)$. Then:

$$\ell_Y(\gamma') \le K \cdot \ell_X(\gamma).$$

By applying the same argument to $f^{-1}$ (which is also $K$-quasiconformal), we
 obtain the two-sided bound:

$$\frac{1}{K}\,\ell_X(\gamma) \;\le\; \ell_Y(\gamma') \;\le\; K\,\ell_X(\gamma).
$$

---

## Proof

**Step 1: Lift to universal covers.**

Let $\mathbb{D}$ denote the unit disk equipped with the Poincaré hyperbolic metr
ic, and let $\pi_X: \mathbb{D} \to X$, $\pi_Y: \mathbb{D} \to Y$ be the universa
l covering maps. The deck transformation groups are Fuchsian groups $\Gamma_X, \
Gamma_Y \subset \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ acting isometrically on $\mathbb{D}$
.

Since $f: X \to Y$ is a $K$-quasiconformal homeomorphism, it lifts to a $K$-quas
iconformal homeomorphism $F: \mathbb{D} \to \mathbb{D}$ that conjugates the deck
 groups: there exists a group isomorphism $\varphi: \Gamma_X \to \Gamma_Y$ such
that

$$F \circ A = \varphi(A) \circ F \quad \text{for all } A \in \Gamma_X. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2: Identify the geodesic with a hyperbolic isometry.**

The closed geodesic $\gamma$ on $X$ corresponds to a conjugacy class of a hyperb
olic element $A \in \Gamma_X$. The length $\ell_X(\gamma)$ equals the **translat
ion length** of $A$:

$$\ell_X(\gamma) = \tau(A) := \inf_{z \in \mathbb{D}} d(z, Az),$$

where $d$ is the hyperbolic distance on $\mathbb{D}$. This infimum is attained a
long the axis of $A$ (the geodesic in $\mathbb{D}$ fixed by $A$), and equals $2\
,\mathrm{arcosh}\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}(A)\bigr)$.

The image $f(\gamma)$ is a closed curve on $Y$, freely homotopic to the unique c
losed geodesic $\gamma'$ on $Y$. This geodesic $\gamma'$ corresponds to the conj
ugacy class of $\varphi(A) \in \Gamma_Y$, and

$$\ell_Y(\gamma') = \tau(\varphi(A)) = \inf_{z \in \mathbb{D}} d(z, \varphi(A)\,
z).$$

**Step 3: Apply the Ahlfors–Schwarz lemma (quasiconformal Schwarz–Pick lemma).**

Since $F: \mathbb{D} \to \mathbb{D}$ is a $K$-quasiconformal homeomorphism of th
e disk onto itself, the **Ahlfors–Schwarz lemma** states that $F$ is $K$-Lipschi
tz with respect to the hyperbolic metric:

$$d(F(z), F(w)) \le K \cdot d(z, w) \quad \text{for all } z, w \in \mathbb{D}. \
tag{2}$$

*Justification:* The Ahlfors–Schwarz lemma asserts that a $K$-quasiconformal map
 from a Riemann surface carrying a conformal metric of curvature $\le -1$ into a
nother such surface pulls back the target metric by at most $K$ times the source
 metric. Both $\mathbb{D}$ (domain) and $\mathbb{D}$ (target) carry the Poincaré
 metric with curvature $-1$, so the pullback satisfies $F^*(ds^2_{\mathrm{hyp}})
 \le K\, ds^2_{\mathrm{hyp}}$, which integrates to the distance bound (2).

**Step 4: Bound the translation length.**

Using the conjugation relation (1), we substitute $z = F(w)$ in the definition o
f $\tau(\varphi(A))$:

$$\tau(\varphi(A)) = \inf_{z \in \mathbb{D}} d\bigl(z,\; \varphi(A)\,z\bigr) = \
inf_{w \in \mathbb{D}} d\bigl(F(w),\; \varphi(A)\,F(w)\bigr).$$

By (1), $\varphi(A)\,F(w) = F(Aw)$, so:

$$\tau(\varphi(A)) = \inf_{w \in \mathbb{D}} d\bigl(F(w),\; F(Aw)\bigr).$$

Applying the Lipschitz bound (2):

$$\tau(\varphi(A)) \le \inf_{w \in \mathbb{D}} K \cdot d(w, Aw) = K \cdot \inf_{
w \in \mathbb{D}} d(w, Aw) = K \cdot \tau(A).$$

Therefore:

$$\ell_Y(\gamma') = \tau(\varphi(A)) \le K \cdot \tau(A) = K \cdot \ell_X(\gamma
). \tag{3}$$

**Step 5: The lower bound (by symmetry).**

The inverse $f^{-1}: Y \to X$ is also $K$-quasiconformal (the dilatation of the
inverse of a $K$-quasiconformal map equals $K$). Applying inequality (3) to $f^{
-1}$ and the geodesic $\gamma'$ on $Y$:

$$\ell_X(\gamma) \le K \cdot \ell_Y(\gamma'),$$

which gives:

$$\ell_Y(\gamma') \ge \frac{1}{K}\,\ell_X(\gamma). \tag{4}$$

**Conclusion.** Combining (3) and (4):

$$\boxed{\frac{1}{K}\,\ell_X(\gamma) \;\le\; \ell_Y(\gamma') \;\le\; K\,\ell_X(\
gamma),}$$

where $\gamma'$ is the unique closed geodesic on $Y$ freely homotopic to $f(\gam
ma)$. The upper bound $\ell_Y(\gamma') \le K\,\ell_X(\gamma)$ is **Wolpert's Lem
ma**. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** This result is sharp: equality in the upper bound is achieved when $
f$ is a Teichmüller map whose associated quadratic differential has a cylinder c
orresponding to $\gamma$, and the horizontal stretch factor is exactly $K$. The
lemma is fundamental in Teichmüller theory, as it controls how geodesic lengths
vary under quasiconformal deformation, providing Lipschitz continuity of length
functions on Teichmüller space with respect to the Teichmüller metric.
