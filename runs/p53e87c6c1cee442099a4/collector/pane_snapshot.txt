## Problem

Find the area of the part of the surface $x^2+y^2=2x$ that is delimited by (lies
 inside) the cone $z^2=x^2+y^2$.

## Setup

Rewrite the surface:
$$x^2+y^2=2x \;\Longleftrightarrow\; (x-1)^2+y^2=1.$$
This is a **right circular cylinder** of radius $1$ with axis the line $(x,y)=(1
,0)$, parallel to the $z$-axis.

The cone $z^2=x^2+y^2$ bounds the region $|z|\le\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$.

## Reducing the condition onto the cylinder

On the cylinder we have $x^2+y^2=2x$, so the cone condition becomes
$$z^2 \le x^2+y^2 = 2x, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad |z|\le\sqrt{2x}.$$
Since $(x-1)^2+y^2=1$ forces $x\in[0,2]$, the quantity $2x\ge0$ and the bound is
 well-defined.

## Parametrization

Use cylindrical coordinates around the cylinder's axis:
$$x=1+\cos\theta,\quad y=\sin\theta,\quad z=z,\qquad \theta\in[0,2\pi].$$
Then
$$2x = 2(1+\cos\theta)=4\cos^2\!\tfrac{\theta}{2},$$
so the height bound is
$$|z|\le 2\left|\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}\right|.$$

The surface element: $r_\theta=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta,0)$, $r_z=(0,0,1)$, so
$$r_\theta\times r_z=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta,0),\qquad \|r_\theta\times r_z\|=1.$
$
Hence $dS = d\theta\,dz$.

## Computing the area

$$A=\int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_{-2|\cos(\theta/2)|}^{\,2|\cos(\theta/2)|} dz\,d\theta
   =\int_0^{2\pi}4\left|\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}\right|d\theta.$$

Substitute $u=\theta/2$, $d\theta=2\,du$, $u:0\to\pi$:
$$A = 4\cdot 2\int_0^{\pi}|\cos u|\,du = 8\cdot 2 = 16,$$
using $\int_0^{\pi}|\cos u|\,du = 2$.

## Result

$$\boxed{A = 16}$$
