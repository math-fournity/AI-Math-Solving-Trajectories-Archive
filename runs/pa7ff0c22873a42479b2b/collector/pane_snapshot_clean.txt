## Proof

**Claim.** The maximum number of algebraically independent homogeneous polynomia
ls of degree $d \geq 2$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ is exactly $n$. In partic
ular, it cannot be as large as $n^2$ or $n^3$.

We work with algebraic independence over $\mathbb{Z}$, which is equivalent to al
gebraic independence over $\mathbb{Q}$ (a polynomial relation with integer coeff
icients exists iff one with rational coefficients exists, by clearing denominato
rs). Hence we may reason over the field $\mathbb{Q}$.

### Upper bound: at most $n$

Recall that a set $S = \{f_1,\dots,f_k\} \subset \mathbb{Q}(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ is *
algebraically independent over $\mathbb{Q}$* if there is no nonzero polynomial $
P \in \mathbb{Q}[T_1,\dots,T_k]$ with $P(f_1,\dots,f_k) = 0$.

The field $\mathbb{Q}(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ has transcendence degree $n$ over $\mathbb
{Q}$, with $\{x_1,\dots,x_n\}$ a transcendence basis. A standard theorem of fiel
d theory states:

> *Any algebraically independent subset of a field extension of transcendence de
gree $n$ has cardinality at most $n$.*

Since every homogeneous polynomial of degree $d$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$
is in particular an element of $\mathbb{Q}(x_1,\dots,x_n)$, any collection of al
gebraically independent such polynomials has size $\leq n$.

This immediately rules out $n^2$ and $n^3$ for $n \geq 2$ (and for $n=1$ trivial
ly $n^2 = n^3 = 1 = n$).

### Lower bound: $n$ is achievable

Consider the $n$ homogeneous polynomials of degree $d$:
$$f_i = x_i^d, \qquad i = 1, \dots, n.$$

We show they are algebraically independent over $\mathbb{Q}$. Suppose $P(T_1,\do
ts,T_n) \in \mathbb{Q}[T_1,\dots,T_n]$ is nonzero and $P(x_1^d,\dots,x_n^d) = 0$
. Write
$$P = \sum_{\alpha} c_\alpha\, T_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots T_n^{\alpha_n}, \qquad c_\al
pha \in \mathbb{Q}, \text{ almost all } 0.$$
Substituting gives
$$0 = P(x_1^d,\dots,x_n^d) = \sum_{\alpha} c_\alpha\, x_1^{d\alpha_1}\cdots x_n^
{d\alpha_n}.$$

Because $d \geq 1$, the map $\alpha \mapsto (d\alpha_1,\dots,d\alpha_n)$ is inje
ctive on $\mathbb{N}^n$. Hence the monomials $x_1^{d\alpha_1}\cdots x_n^{d\alpha
_n}$ are *distinct* for distinct multi-indices $\alpha$. The monomials $\{x_1^{m
_1}\cdots x_n^{m_n} : m_i \geq 0\}$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$ (
they form a basis of $\mathbb{Q}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ as a $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space)
. Therefore every coefficient $c_\alpha = 0$, contradicting $P \neq 0$.

Thus $\{x_1^d,\dots,x_n^d\}$ is algebraically independent, giving $n$ algebraica
lly independent homogeneous polynomials of degree $d$.

### Conclusion

The maximum number of algebraically independent homogeneous polynomials of degre
e $d \geq 2$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ is **exactly $n$**. It is limited to
 $n$ by the transcendence degree of $\mathbb{Q}(x_1,\dots,x_n)/\mathbb{Q}$, and
this bound is attained by $\{x_1^d,\dots,x_n^d\}$. The number cannot be as large
 as $n^2$ or $n^3$ (for $n \geq 2$).
