## Proof

Let $E(n)$ denote the expected total number of measurements recorded when the ex
periment starts with $n$ active bonds, **including** the initial measurement of
$n$ itself and the final measurement of $1$.

**Base case.** When $n=1$, the experiment terminates immediately after recording
 the single measurement of $1$:
$$E(1) = 1.$$

**Recurrence.** For $n > 1$, one measurement of $n$ is recorded, then a divisor
$d \mid n$ is chosen uniformly at random (from all $d(n)$ positive divisors of $
n$), and the process continues from $d$. Hence
$$E(n) = 1 + \frac{1}{d(n)}\sum_{d \mid n} E(d).$$

The term $E(n)$ appears on the right (since $n \mid n$); isolating it:
$$E(n)\Bigl(d(n)-1\Bigr) = d(n) + \sum_{\substack{d \mid n \\ d \neq n}} E(d).$$

We now compute the values needed, working from small $n$ upward.

**$n=2$.** Divisors $\{1,2\}$, $d(2)=2$:
$$E(2) = 1 + \tfrac{1}{2}(E(1)+E(2)) \;\Longrightarrow\; \tfrac{1}{2}E(2) = 1 +
\tfrac{1}{2} \;\Longrightarrow\; E(2) = 3.$$

**$n=3$.** Divisors $\{1,3\}$, $d(3)=2$:
$$E(3) = 1 + \tfrac{1}{2}(E(1)+E(3)) \;\Longrightarrow\; E(3) = 3.$$

**$n=4$.** Divisors $\{1,2,4\}$, $d(4)=3$:
$$E(4) = 1 + \tfrac{1}{3}(E(1)+E(2)+E(4)) = 1 + \tfrac{1}{3}(1+3+E(4)).$$
$$2E(4) = 3 + 4 = 7 \;\Longrightarrow\; E(4) = \tfrac{7}{2}.$$

**$n=6$.** Divisors $\{1,2,3,6\}$, $d(6)=4$:
$$E(6) = 1 + \tfrac{1}{4}(E(1)+E(2)+E(3)+E(6)) = 1 + \tfrac{1}{4}(1+3+3+E(6)) =
1 + \tfrac{1}{4}(7+E(6)).$$
$$3E(6) = 4 + 7 = 11 \;\Longrightarrow\; E(6) = \tfrac{11}{3}.$$

**$n=12$.** Divisors $\{1,2,3,4,6,12\}$, $d(12)=6$:
$$E(12) = 1 + \tfrac{1}{6}\bigl(E(1)+E(2)+E(3)+E(4)+E(6)+E(12)\bigr).$$

Sum of the known terms:
$$E(1)+E(2)+E(3)+E(4)+E(6) = 1 + 3 + 3 + \tfrac{7}{2} + \tfrac{11}{3} = 7 + \tfr
ac{7}{2} + \tfrac{11}{3} = \tfrac{42+21+22}{6} = \tfrac{85}{6}.$$

Therefore
$$E(12) = 1 + \tfrac{1}{6}\Bigl(\tfrac{85}{6} + E(12)\Bigr) = 1 + \tfrac{85}{36}
 + \tfrac{1}{6}E(12).$$

Isolating $E(12)$:
$$\tfrac{5}{6}E(12) = 1 + \tfrac{85}{36} = \tfrac{36+85}{36} = \tfrac{121}{36},$
$
$$E(12) = \tfrac{121}{36}\cdot\tfrac{6}{5} = \tfrac{121}{30}.$$

**Conclusion.** The expected total number of measurements recorded in a single c
omplete experiment (including the initial measurement of $12$ and the final meas
urement of $1$) is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{121}{30}}.$$
