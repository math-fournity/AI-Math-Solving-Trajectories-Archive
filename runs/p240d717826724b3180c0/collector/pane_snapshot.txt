## Problem

If $H$ is a Lie subgroup of $G$, is $T_e H$ a subspace of $T_e G$?

## Proof

**Yes.** $T_e H$ is a Lie subalgebra of $T_e G$ (hence in particular a linear su
bspace).

### Setup

Let $G$ be a Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g} = T_e G$. Let $H \subseteq
 G$ be a Lie subgroup, i.e., $H$ is a subgroup of $G$ that is simultaneously an
immersed (or embedded) submanifold of $G$, and the group operations on $H$ are s
mooth. Let $\mathfrak{h} = T_e H$.

### Step 1: The inclusion is smooth

Since $H$ is a Lie subgroup of $G$, the inclusion map $\iota: H \hookrightarrow
G$ is a smooth injective immersion (this is the definition of a Lie subgroup, in
 the immersed-submanifold sense of Chevalley). Its differential at the identity
is a linear map

$$d\iota_e : T_e H \longrightarrow T_e G.$$

Because $\iota$ is an immersion, $d\iota_e$ is **injective**. We may therefore i
dentify $T_e H$ with its image $d\iota_e(T_e H) \subseteq T_e G$, and this image
 is a linear subspace of $T_e G$ (being the image of a linear map).

### Step 2: Closure under the Lie bracket

It remains to show that this subspace is closed under the Lie bracket of $\mathf
rak{g}$, so that it is a Lie subalgebra (not merely a vector subspace).

Let $X, Y \in \mathfrak{h} = T_e H$. Extend them to left-invariant vector fields
 $\widetilde{X}, \widetilde{Y}$ on $G$ (so $\widetilde{X}_e = X$, $\widetilde{Y}
_e = Y$). Similarly, since $H$ is itself a Lie group, extend $X, Y$ to left-inva
riant vector fields $\widetilde{X}^H, \widetilde{Y}^H$ on $H$.

**Claim:** $\iota_* \widetilde{X}^H = \widetilde{X}$, where we view both as vect
or fields along $H$ in $G$.

*Proof of claim.* For $h \in H$, let $L_h^G, L_h^H$ denote left-translation on $
G$ and $H$ respectively. Since $\iota$ is a group homomorphism, $\iota \circ L_h
^H = L_h^G \circ \iota$. Differentiating at $e$:

$$d\iota_h \circ d(L_h^H)_e = d(L_h^G)_{\iota(e)} \circ d\iota_e.$$

That is, $d\iota_h(\widetilde{X}^H_h) = \widetilde{X}_{\iota(h)}$, proving the c
laim. $\square$

Now the Lie bracket is natural with respect to smooth maps that are group homomo
rphisms (equivalently, the bracket is preserved by the differential of a Lie gro
up homomorphism). Concretely:

$$d\iota_e\big([\widetilde{X}^H, \widetilde{Y}^H]_e\big) = [\widetilde{X}, \wide
tilde{Y}]_{\iota(e)}.$$

The left-hand side lies in $d\iota_e(\mathfrak{h}) \subseteq \mathfrak{g}$, and
$[\widetilde{X}, \widetilde{Y}]_e = [X, Y]_{\mathfrak{g}}$ is the Lie bracket of
 $X, Y$ computed in $\mathfrak{g}$. Since $[\widetilde{X}^H, \widetilde{Y}^H]_e
= [X, Y]_{\mathfrak{h}}$ is the bracket computed in $\mathfrak{h}$, we obtain

$$[X, Y]_{\mathfrak{g}} = d\iota_e\big([X, Y]_{\mathfrak{h}}\big) \in d\iota_e(\
mathfrak{h}).$$

Therefore $d\iota_e(\mathfrak{h})$ is closed under the bracket of $\mathfrak{g}$
.

### Conclusion

Identifying $\mathfrak{h} = T_e H$ with $d\iota_e(\mathfrak{h}) \subseteq T_e G$
:

1. $T_e H$ is a **linear subspace** of $T_e G$ (injectivity of $d\iota_e$), and
2. it is closed under the Lie bracket, hence a **Lie subalgebra** of $\mathfrak{
g} = T_e G$.

In particular, the answer to the question as stated is **yes**: $T_e H$ is a sub
space of $T_e G$. (More precisely, it is a Lie subalgebra, which is the stronger
 and standard statement — this is the differential of the Lie subgroup–Lie subal
gebra correspondence.)
