Let $N_n$ denote the number of cards purchased to collect all $n$ types. Decompo
se

$$N_n = T_1 + T_2 + \cdots + T_n,$$

where $T_k$ is the number of additional cards needed to obtain the $k$-th *disti
nct* card after $k-1$ distinct cards have already been collected. Conditional on
 the past, each new card has probability $p_k = \frac{n-k+1}{n}$ of being a new
type, so the $T_k$ are independent geometric random variables with

$$\mathbb{E}[T_k] = \frac{1}{p_k} = \frac{n}{n-k+1}, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(T
_k) = \frac{1-p_k}{p_k^2} = \frac{n(k-1)}{(n-k+1)^2}.$$

**Step 1: Expectation.**

$$\mathbb{E}[N_n] = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{n}{n-k+1} = n\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j}
= n\,H_n,$$

where $H_n$ is the $n$-th harmonic number. Since $H_n = \ln n + \gamma + O(1/n)$
,

$$\frac{\mathbb{E}[N_n]}{n\ln n} = \frac{H_n}{\ln n} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 1.
 \tag{1}$$

**Step 2: Variance.** By independence of the $T_k$,

$$\operatorname{Var}(N_n) = \sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{n(k-1)}{(n-k+1)^2}.$$

Substituting $j = n-k+1$ (so $k-1 = n-j$),

$$\operatorname{Var}(N_n) = \sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{n(n-j)}{j^2} = n^2\sum_{j=1}^{n}
\frac{1}{j^2} - n\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j}.$$

The first sum converges: $\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j^2} \le \frac{\pi^2}{6}$, and
the second is $H_n = O(\ln n)$. Hence

$$\operatorname{Var}(N_n) = O(n^2). \tag{2}$$

**Step 3: Chebyshev.** For any $\varepsilon > 0$,

$$\mathbb{P}\!\left(\left|\frac{N_n}{n\ln n} - 1\right| \ge \varepsilon\right) =
 \mathbb{P}\!\left(|N_n - \mathbb{E}[N_n]| \ge \left|\varepsilon\, n\ln n - (\ma
thbb{E}[N_n]-n\ln n)\right|\right).$$

Since $\mathbb{E}[N_n] - n\ln n = n(H_n - \ln n) = O(n)$, for all large $n$ we h
ave $|\mathbb{E}[N_n]-n\ln n| \le \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,n\ln n$, so the event a
bove is contained in

$$\left\{|N_n - \mathbb{E}[N_n]| \ge \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,n\ln n\right\}.$$

Applying Chebyshev and using (2),

$$\mathbb{P}\!\left(\left|\frac{N_n}{n\ln n} - 1\right| \ge \varepsilon\right) \
le \frac{\operatorname{Var}(N_n)}{(\varepsilon/2)^2\, n^2(\ln n)^2} = \frac{O(n^
2)}{n^2(\ln n)^2} = O\!\left(\frac{1}{(\ln n)^2}\right) \xrightarrow{n\to\infty}
 0.$$

Since $\varepsilon > 0$ was arbitrary,

$$\frac{N_n}{n\ln n} \xrightarrow{p} 1. \qquad\blacksquare$$
