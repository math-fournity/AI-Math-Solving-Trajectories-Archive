## Answer: **No**

**Claim.** Pointwise convergence of a sequence of measurable functions does not
always imply convergence in measure for some subsequence. The implication fails
on measure spaces of infinite measure.

### Proof

We exhibit a counterexample on an infinite measure space.

**The space.** Let $(X, \mathcal{M}, \mu) = (\mathbb{R}, \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
, m)$ where $m$ is Lebesgue measure. Note $m(\mathbb{R}) = \infty$.

**The sequence.** Define $f_n : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ by
$$f_n = \mathbf{1}_{[n,\, n+1]}.$$

**Pointwise convergence to 0.** For any fixed $x \in \mathbb{R}$, once $n > x$ w
e have $x \notin [n, n+1]$, so $f_n(x) = 0$ for all sufficiently large $n$. Henc
e
$$f_n(x) \to 0 \quad \text{for every } x \in \mathbb{R}.$$

**No subsequence converges in measure to 0.** Let $(f_{n_k})_{k \geq 1}$ be any
subsequence. For convergence in measure to $0$ we would need, for every $\vareps
ilon > 0$,
$$m\bigl(\{|f_{n_k} - 0| \geq \varepsilon\}\bigr) \xrightarrow{k \to \infty} 0.$
$
Take $\varepsilon = \tfrac{1}{2}$. Since $f_{n_k}$ takes only the values $0$ and
 $1$,
$$\{|f_{n_k}| \geq \tfrac{1}{2}\} = [n_k,\, n_k+1],$$
so
$$m\bigl(\{|f_{n_k}| \geq \tfrac{1}{2}\}\bigr) = m([n_k, n_k+1]) = 1 \quad \text
{for every } k.$$
This does **not** tend to $0$. Therefore no subsequence of $(f_n)$ converges to
$0$ in measure.

**Conclusion.** We have a sequence $(f_n)$ converging pointwise to $0$ on $(\mat
hbb{R}, \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}), m)$, yet no subsequence converges in measure. S
ince the question asks whether the implication holds *regardless of the measure
of the space* (i.e., for every measure space, including infinite ones), this sin
gle counterexample on an infinite measure space shows the answer is **no**.

(Remark: on a *finite* measure space, pointwise convergence does imply convergen
ce in measure — hence the whole sequence, and a fortiori every subsequence, conv
erges in measure. The obstruction is precisely the infinitude of the measure, as
 the counterexample exploits the mass "escaping to infinity" without decreasing.
)
