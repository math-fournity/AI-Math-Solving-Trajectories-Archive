**Proof.**

For any integer $n$, let $q = \lfloor n/k \rfloor$ and $r = n - kq$. Then $r \in
 \{0, 1, \dots, k-1\}$ (the non-negative remainder under Euclidean division by $
k$) and $q = (n - r)/k$.

We seek polynomials $P_0(n), \dots, P_{k-1}(n) \in \mathbb{Q}[n]$ such that for
every integer $n$:

$$q^k = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} P_j(n)\, q^j. \tag{$*$}$$

**Reduction to polynomial identities.** Since $q = (n-r)/k$ where $r$ is the uni
que element of $\{0,\dots,k-1\}$ with $n \equiv r \pmod{k}$, equation $(*)$ is e
quivalent to: for each $r \in \{0, 1, \dots, k-1\}$,

$$\left(\frac{n-r}{k}\right)^k = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} P_j(n)\left(\frac{n-r}{k}\righ
t)^j \tag{$**_r$}$$

holds for all integers $n \equiv r \pmod{k}$. Since the set $\{n \in \mathbb{Z}
: n \equiv r \pmod{k}\}$ is infinite, $(**_r)$ holding for all such $n$ is equiv
alent to $(**_r)$ being a **polynomial identity** in $n$.

**Construction via Lagrange interpolation.** Treat $n$ as a symbolic variable an
d define the $k$ affine-linear functions

$$t_r = \frac{n - r}{k}, \qquad r = 0, 1, \dots, k-1.$$

These are $k$ distinct values (consecutive ones differ by $1/k$). Consider the m
onomial $f(t) = t^k$ and the $k$ data points $(t_0, t_0^k), (t_1, t_1^k), \dots,
 (t_{k-1}, t_{k-1}^k)$.

By Lagrange interpolation, there is a **unique** polynomial $L(t)$ of degree at
most $k-1$ in $t$ satisfying $L(t_r) = t_r^k$ for all $r = 0, \dots, k-1$:

$$L(t) = \sum_{r=0}^{k-1} t_r^k \prod_{\substack{s=0 \\ s \neq r}}^{k-1} \frac{t
 - t_s}{t_r - t_s}. \tag{1}$$

**The coefficients of $L(t)$ (as a polynomial in $t$) are polynomials in $n$.**
We verify this by examining each factor:

- **Denominators are constants.** For $r \neq s$:
$$t_r - t_s = \frac{n-r}{k} - \frac{n-s}{k} = \frac{s - r}{k},$$
which is a **nonzero constant** independent of $n$. Hence the denominator $D_r =
 \prod_{s \neq r}(s-r)/k$ is a nonzero constant.

- **Numerators are polynomial in $t$ and $n$.** Each factor
$$t - t_s = t - \frac{n-s}{k} = \frac{kt - n + s}{k}$$
is linear in both $t$ and $n$ with rational coefficients. Therefore each Lagrang
e basis polynomial
$$\ell_r(t) = \prod_{\substack{s=0\\s\neq r}}^{k-1} \frac{t - t_s}{t_r - t_s}$$
is a polynomial in $t$ and $n$ with rational coefficients, of degree exactly $k-
1$ in $t$.

- **Weights are polynomial in $n$.** The weight $t_r^k = \left(\frac{n-r}{k}\rig
ht)^k = \frac{(n-r)^k}{k^k}$ is a polynomial in $n$ of degree $k$ with rational
coefficients.

Combining these, $L(t) = \sum_{r=0}^{k-1} t_r^k \, \ell_r(t)$ is a polynomial in
 $t$ and $n$ with rational coefficients, of degree at most $k-1$ in $t$. We may
therefore write

$$L(t) = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} P_j(n)\, t^j,$$

where each $P_j(n) \in \mathbb{Q}[n]$ is the coefficient of $t^j$ in $L(t)$, hen
ce a polynomial in $n$ with rational coefficients. These are our desired polynom
ials.

**Verification.** Let $n$ be any integer. Set $r = n - k\lfloor n/k \rfloor \in
\{0, \dots, k-1\}$ and $q = \lfloor n/k \rfloor = \frac{n-r}{k} = t_r$. By the d

$$L(t_r) = t_r^k = q^k.$$

On the other hand, expanding $L(t_r)$ using the coefficient representation:

$$L(t_r) = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} P_j(n)\, t_r^j = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} P_j(n)\, q^j.$$

$$q^k = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} P_j(n)\, q^j = P_0(n) + P_1(n)\left\lfloor \frac{n}{k}\
