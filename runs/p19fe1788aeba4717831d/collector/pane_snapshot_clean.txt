## Proof

**Claim:** No such graph exists. Every graph $G = (\omega, E)$ in which every ve
rtex has infinite degree admits a perfect matching.

**Proof.** We construct a perfect matching by a greedy algorithm.

Since the vertex set is $\omega = \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$, we process vertices in th
eir natural order. We build a matching $M$ inductively. Let $M_0 = \emptyset$. F
or each $n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$:

- **If $n$ is already matched in $M_n$**, do nothing: set $M_{n+1} = M_n$.
- **If $n$ is unmatched in $M_n$**, select a partner as follows and add the edge
 to the matching.

**Key observation — a partner always exists.** At step $n$, the matching $M_n$ c
ontains at most $n$ edges (at most one is added per step), so at most $2n$ verti
ces are matched. Since vertex $n$ has **infinite** degree, it has infinitely man
y neighbors. At most $2n$ of these neighbors are already matched (a finite numbe
r). Therefore infinitely many neighbors of $n$ remain unmatched. Let $u_n$ be th
e **least** unmatched neighbor of $n$ (well-defined since the unmatched neighbor
s form a non-empty subset of $\omega$). Set $M_{n+1} = M_n \cup \{\{n, u_n\}\}$.

This defines a sequence of matchings $M_0 \subseteq M_1 \subseteq M_2 \subseteq
\cdots$. Let
$$M = \bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty} M_{n+1}.$$

**$M$ is a matching (pairwise disjoint edges).** Each edge $\{n, u_n\}$ is added
 at a step where both $n$ and $u_n$ are unmatched. After the edge is added, both
 endpoints become matched, and all subsequent steps only pair *unmatched* vertic
es. Hence no vertex ever appears in two edges of $M$.

**$M$ covers every vertex.** Consider any vertex $n \in \omega$. At step $n$, ei
ther:
- $n$ is already matched in $M_n$ — meaning some earlier step $k < n$ selected $
n$ as the partner $u_k$. Then $n \in M$.
- $n$ is unmatched in $M_n$ — then the algorithm adds edge $\{n, u_n\}$, so $n \
in M$.

In both cases, $n$ is covered by $M$. Since $n$ was arbitrary, $M$ covers all of
 $\omega$.

**Conclusion.** $M$ is a set of pairwise disjoint edges covering every vertex of
 $\omega$ — a perfect matching. The construction works for *any* graph on $\omeg
a$ where every vertex has infinite degree, using only that at each finite stage
only finitely many vertices are consumed while every vertex offers infinitely ma
ny partners.

Therefore, **there is no** infinite graph $G = (\omega, E)$ such that every vert
ex has infinite degree yet $G$ has no perfect matching. $\blacksquare$
