## Proof

Let the cube have interior edge length $a = 2\sqrt{2}$. Place it as $[0,a]^3$.

**Step 1 — The length‑4 segment must be a face diagonal.**
Every boundary segment of the cross‑section lies on a face of the cube, so its l
ength is at most a face diagonal, $a\sqrt{2} = 2\sqrt{2}\cdot\sqrt{2} = 4$. Henc
e a segment of length exactly $4$ is a *full* face diagonal. By cube symmetry, t
ake it to be the diagonal from $P_1=(0,0,0)$ to $P_2=(a,a,0)$ on the face $z=0$.

**Step 2 — Parameterize the plane.**
A plane through this diagonal has equation $y = x + kz$ for some real $k$ (norma
l $(1,-1,k)$ up to scaling). The case $k=0$ is the face itself (no cross‑section
), so $k\neq 0$; by reflection symmetry we may take $k>0$.

Tracing the cross‑section polygon:

- On $z=0$: the diagonal $P_1P_2$, length $4$.
- From $P_2=(a,a,0)$ the plane exits along face $y=a$: $x=a-kz$.
- From $P_1=(0,0,0)$ the plane exits along face $x=0$: $y=kz$.

For $0<k\le 1$ the polygon is a quadrilateral with vertices
$$P_1=(0,0,0),\quad P_2=(a,a,0),\quad P_3=(a(1-k),\,a,\,a),\quad P_4=(0,\,ka,\,a
),$$
and edge lengths
$$|P_1P_2|=4,\quad |P_2P_3|=|P_4P_1|=a\sqrt{k^2+1},\quad |P_3P_4|=a\sqrt{2}\,(1-
k).$$

For $k\ge 1$ the polygon degenerates to a triangle with edges $4,\;a\sqrt{1+1/k^
2},\;a\sqrt{1+1/k^2}$; setting the repeated edge equal to $2$ gives $1+1/k^2 = 1
/2$, impossible. So the solution lies in the quadrilateral regime.

**Step 3 — Determine $k$ from the length‑2 segment.**
Setting $a\sqrt{k^2+1}=2$ gives $k^2=-\tfrac12$, impossible. Setting
$$a\sqrt{2}\,(1-k)=2 \;\Longrightarrow\; 2\sqrt{2}\cdot\sqrt{2}\,(1-k)=2 \;\Long
rightarrow\; 4(1-k)=2 \;\Longrightarrow\; k=\tfrac12.$$
With $k=\tfrac12$ (and $0<k\le1$ ✓), the edges are $4,\;\sqrt{10},\;2,\;\sqrt{10
}$, confirming two distinct boundary segments of lengths $4$ and $2$. The reflec
ted case $k=-\tfrac12$ gives the same polygon by symmetry, so the configuration
is unique.

**Step 4 — Compute the area.**
With $k=\tfrac12$:
$$P_1=(0,0,0),\;P_2=(a,a,0),\;P_3=\bigl(\tfrac a2,a,a\bigr),\;P_4=\bigl(0,\tfrac
 a2,a\bigr).$$
The plane is $2x-2y+z=0$ with unit normal $\hat n=(2,-2,1)/3$. Orthographically
projecting onto the $xz$‑plane (direction $\hat j=(0,1,0)$, $|\hat n\cdot\hat j|
=2/3$) gives the trapezoid
$$(0,0),\;(a,0),\;\bigl(\tfrac a2,a\bigr),\;(0,a),$$
with projected area $\tfrac12(a+\tfrac a2)\,a=\tfrac{3a^2}{4}$.

Therefore
$$\text{Area}=\frac{\text{Area}_{xz}}{|\hat n\cdot\hat j|}=\frac{3a^2/4}{2/3}=\f
rac{9a^2}{8}.$$
Since $a^2=(2\sqrt2)^2=8$,
$$\text{Area}=\frac{9\cdot 8}{8}=9.$$

**Verification via cross products:** $\sum \vec P_i\times\vec P_{i+1}=\bigl(\tfr
ac{3a^2}{2},-\tfrac{3a^2}{2},\tfrac{3a^2}{4}\bigr)$, with magnitude $\tfrac{9a^2
}{4}$, giving area $\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac{9a^2}{4}=\tfrac{9a^2}{8}=9$. ✓

The total surface area (one side) of the cooling baffle is $\boxed{9}$ square me
ters.
