# Proof

Let $R := S(I - \lambda S)^{-1}$. By hypothesis, $R$ is a bounded inverse of $T
- \lambda I$, so:

$$(T - \lambda I)R = I_H, \qquad R(T - \lambda I) = I_{\mathscr{D}(T)}.$$

In particular $R$ is bijective (bounded, with bounded inverse $T - \lambda I$).

**Step 1: Algebraic identities.**

From $(T-\lambda I)R = I$:
$$TR = I + \lambda R. \tag{1}$$
From $R(T-\lambda I) = I$:
$$RT = I + \lambda R. \tag{2}$$

Since $(I-\lambda S)^{-1}$ is a bounded function of $S$ (resolvent), it commutes
 with $S$, giving $R = S(I-\lambda S)^{-1} = (I-\lambda S)^{-1}S$. Hence:
$$R(I-\lambda S) = S \implies R - \lambda RS = S \implies R = (I+\lambda R)S, \t
ag{3}$$
$$(I-\lambda S)R = S \implies R - \lambda SR = S \implies R = S(I+\lambda R). \t
ag{4}$$

**Step 2: $I + \lambda R$ is bijective.**

$R$ is bijective. From (3), $R = (I+\lambda R)S$ is bijective, so $(I+\lambda R)
$ is surjective and $S$ is injective. From (4), $R = S(I+\lambda R)$ is bijectiv
e, so $S$ is surjective and $(I+\lambda R)$ is injective. Therefore $I + \lambda
 R$ is both injective and surjective on $H$.

Since $I + \lambda R$ is bounded and bijective on the Hilbert space $H$, the Ope
n Mapping Theorem gives that $(I+\lambda R)^{-1}$ is bounded.

**Step 3: $S$ is bounded.**

From (3): $S = (I+\lambda R)^{-1}R$, a product of bounded operators, hence bound
ed.

**Step 4: $S = T^{-1}$.**

From (1): $T = (I+\lambda R)R^{-1}$, so $T$ is bijective from $\mathscr{D}(T)$ o
nto $H$ with
$$T^{-1} = \big((I+\lambda R)R^{-1}\big)^{-1} = R\,(I+\lambda R)^{-1}.$$
Since $R$ commutes with $I+\lambda R$ (the latter being a function of $R$), we h
ave $R\,(I+\lambda R)^{-1} = (I+\lambda R)^{-1}R = S$.

Therefore $S = T^{-1}$, and $S$ is bounded.

**Conclusion.** Yes, $S$ is necessarily a bounded inverse for $T$.
