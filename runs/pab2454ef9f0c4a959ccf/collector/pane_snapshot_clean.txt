## Proof

**Claim:** The statement is **true**: $\int_{\mathbb{R}^3} f(x)\,dx = 0$.

**Setup.** Let $I := \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} f(x)\,dx$, which is finite since $f \in
 L^1(\mathbb{R}^3)$.

**Step 1: The inner integral is constant in $y$.**

For each fixed $y \in \mathbb{R}^3$, the map $x \mapsto f(x+y)$ is measurable, a
nd by translation-invariance of the Lebesgue measure,

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}^3} |f(x+y)|\,dx = \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} |f(x)|\,dx < \infty,$$

so $x \mapsto f(x+y) \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^3)$. The substitution $u = x+y$ (a trans
late, which preserves Lebesgue measure) gives

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}^3} f(x+y)\,dx = \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} f(u)\,du = I, \qquad \tex
t{for every } y \in \mathbb{R}^3. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2: The double integral is well-defined (Fubini applicable).**

For any bounded measurable set $B \subset \mathbb{R}^3$,

$$\int_B\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^3} |f(x+y)|\,dx\,dy = \int_B \|f\|_{L^1}\,dy = \|f\|_
{L^1}\cdot m(B) < \infty,$$

since $B$ is bounded (hence $m(B)<\infty$) and $f\in L^1$. By Fubini–Tonelli, th
e iterated integral equals the double integral and may be evaluated in either or
der.

**Step 3: Use the hypothesis.**

The hypothesis states that for every bounded set $B$,

$$0 = \int_B\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^3} f(x+y)\,dx\,dy = \int_B I\,dy = I\cdot m(B), \
tag{2}$$

where we used (1) to pull the constant $I$ out of the $y$-integral.

**Step 4: Conclude $I=0$.**

Choose $B = \overline{B(0,1)}$, the closed unit ball in $\mathbb{R}^3$, which is
 bounded with $m(B) = \tfrac{4\pi}{3} > 0$. Equation (2) gives

$$I \cdot \tfrac{4\pi}{3} = 0 \implies I = 0.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{\int_{\mathbb{R}^3} f(x)\,dx = 0.} \qquad \blacksquare$$
