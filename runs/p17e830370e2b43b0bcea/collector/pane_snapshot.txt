Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest triangle containing three e
xternal squares built on an equilateral triangle of side 2.

## Setup

Place the equilateral triangle with B=(0,0), C=(2,0), A=(1,√3). The three extern
al squares yield 6 outer vertices:

- **Square ABDE** (external to AB): D=(-√3, 1), E=(1-√3, √3+1)
- **Square BCFG** (external to BC): F=(2,-2), G=(0,-2)
- **Square CAHI** (external to CA): H=(1+√3, √3+1), I=(2+√3, 1)

The convex hull of all 9 points (A,B,C,D,E,F,G,H,I) is the hexagon **D-E-H-I-F-G
** (the triangle vertices A,B,C lie inside). The problem reduces to finding the
minimum-area triangle enclosing this hexagon.

## Symmetry Argument

The entire configuration has **3-fold rotational symmetry** about the centroid O
=(1, √3/3). The minimum-area enclosing triangle of a convex polygon is unique, s
o it must inherit this 3-fold symmetry — hence it is **equilateral** with center
 O.

By the flush theorem, each side of the minimum enclosing triangle contains an ed
ge of the hexagon. The hexagon's 6 edges lie in 3 directions (parallel to AB, BC
, CA — each direction appearing twice), so the equilateral triangle's sides must
 be parallel to these directions. This gives exactly **two orientations**.

## Orientation 1 (pointing up, same as ABC)

Each side is flush with a **far edge** of a square:
- Base (∥ BC): through FG, y = -2
- Left side (∥ AB): through DE, y = √3·x + 4
- Right side (∥ CA): through HI, y = -√3·x + 2√3 + 4

Vertices: (1, √3+4), (-2√3, -2), (2+2√3, -2).

Base = 2+4√3, Height = √3+6. Area = ½(2+4√3)(√3+6) = **12 + 13√3 ≈ 34.5**

## Orientation 2 (pointing down, opposite to ABC)

Each side is flush with one far edge (EH) and two side edges (IF, GD):
- Top (∥ BC): through EH, y = √3+1
- Lower-right (∥ AB): through IF, y = √3·x − 2√3 − 2
- Lower-left (∥ CA): through GD, y = −√3·x − 2

**Vertices:**
- Apex (bottom): intersection of lower sides → (1, −√3−2)
- Top-left: y=√3+1 with y=−√3·x−2 → x = −1−√3 → (−1−√3, √3+1)
- Top-right: y=√3+1 with y=√3·x−2√3−2 → x = 3+√3 → (3+√3, √3+1)

**Verification that all hexagon vertices are enclosed:** The top side y=√3+1 con
tains E and H (topmost points). The lower-left side contains D and G; all other
vertices lie to its right (interior). The lower-right side contains I and F; all
 other vertices lie to its left (interior). ✓

**Area computation:**
- Top side length: (3+√3) − (−1−√3) = 4+2√3
- Height: (√3+1) − (−√3−2) = 2√3+3

$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}(4+2\sqrt{3})(3+2\sqrt{3}) = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(12 + 8\
sqrt{3} + 6\sqrt{3} + 12\bigr) = \frac{1}{2}(24 + 14\sqrt{3}) = 12 + 7\sqrt{3}$$

## Conclusion

Comparing both orientations:
- Upward: 12 + 13√3 ≈ 34.5
- Downward: 12 + 7√3 ≈ 24.1

The downward-pointing equilateral triangle is smaller. By the uniqueness and sym
metry argument, this is the global minimum.

The area of the smallest triangle containing the three squares is:

$$\boxed{12 + 7\sqrt{3}}$$
