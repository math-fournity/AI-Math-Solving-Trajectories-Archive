# Proof: The Standard Representation of $S_N$ is Irreducible

**Setup.** Let $V = \mathbb{C}^N$ with basis $e_1, \ldots, e_N$, and let $S_N$ a
ct on $V$ by permuting coordinates: $\sigma \cdot e_i = e_{\sigma(i)}$. This is
the natural $N$-dimensional representation.

The 1-dimensional subspace $T = \operatorname{span}(e_1 + e_2 + \cdots + e_N)$ i
s invariant (it carries the trivial representation). The $(N-1)$-dimensional com
plement is:
$$U = \left\{v = (a_1, \ldots, a_N) \in \mathbb{C}^N : \sum_{i=1}^N a_i = 0\righ
t\}.$$

Since $T$ is invariant and $V = T \oplus U$ (as $S_N$-modules, because $U$ is th
e kernel of the $S_N$-equivariant map $\varphi(v) = \sum a_i$), $U$ is also an $
S_N$-invariant subspace. This is the **standard representation** of $S_N$.

**Claim.** $U$ is irreducible for all $N \geq 2$. (For $N = 1$, $U = \{0\}$ is t
rivially irreducible by convention.)

**Proof.** Suppose $W \subseteq U$ is a nonzero $S_N$-invariant subspace. We sho
w $W = U$.

Take any nonzero $w = (a_1, \ldots, a_N) \in W$. Since $w \in U$, we have $\sum
a_i = 0$. Since $w \neq 0$, not all $a_i$ are zero, and since they sum to zero,
they cannot all be equal. Hence there exist indices $i, j$ with $a_i \neq a_j$.

Consider the transposition $\tau = (i\; j) \in S_N$. Since $W$ is $S_N$-invarian
t, $\tau \cdot w \in W$, so:
$$\tau \cdot w - w \in W.$$

Computing this difference: $\tau$ swaps coordinates $i$ and $j$ and fixes all ot
hers, so
$$(\tau \cdot w - w)_i = a_j - a_i, \quad (\tau \cdot w - w)_j = a_i - a_j, \qua
d (\tau \cdot w - w)_k = 0 \text{ for } k \neq i,j.$$

Thus $\tau \cdot w - w = (a_j - a_i)(e_i - e_j)$. Since $a_i \neq a_j$, the scal
ar $a_j - a_i \neq 0$, so:
$$e_i - e_j \in W.$$

Now we use the full symmetry of $S_N$. For **any** pair $(k, l)$ with $k \neq l$
, there exists a permutation $\pi \in S_N$ with $\pi(i) = k$ and $\pi(j) = l$ (t
his is possible for $N \geq 2$). By $S_N$-invariance:
$$\pi \cdot (e_i - e_j) = e_{\pi(i)} - e_{\pi(j)} = e_k - e_l \in W.$$

Therefore $e_k - e_l \in W$ for **all** pairs $k \neq l$.

In particular, $W$ contains the vectors:
$$e_1 - e_2,\; e_1 - e_3,\; \ldots,\; e_1 - e_N.$$

These $N - 1$ vectors are linearly independent (each introduces a new basis vect
or $e_2, e_3, \ldots, e_N$ that cannot be canceled by the others) and they all l
ie in $U$. Since $\dim U = N - 1$, they form a **basis** of $U$.

Hence $W$ contains a basis of $U$, so $W = U$.

Since the only nonzero invariant subspace of $U$ is $U$ itself, $U$ is irreducib
le. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** This representation corresponds to the partition $(N-1, 1)$ in the r
epresentation theory of $S_N$, and is one of the fundamental irreducible represe
ntations from which all others are built via Young symmetrizers. The key insight
 of the proof is that the transpositions generate enough "difference vectors" $e
_i - e_j$ to span the entire complement.
