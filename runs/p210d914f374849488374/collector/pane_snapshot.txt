# Proof

**Claim:** Yes. Szemerédi's theorem implies that a set of integers with positive
 natural density contains an arithmetic progression of every finite length.

---

## Definitions

Let $A \subseteq \mathbb{N}$. Define:

- **Upper (asymptotic) density:** $\displaystyle \bar{d}(A) = \limsup_{N \to \in
fty} \frac{|A \cap \{1, 2, \dots, N\}|}{N}.$

- **Natural (asymptotic) density:** $\displaystyle d(A) = \lim_{N \to \infty} \f
rac{|A \cap \{1, 2, \dots, N\}|}{N},$ provided this limit exists.

## Szemerédi's Theorem (density form)

*If $A \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ satisfies $\bar{d}(A) > 0$, then $A$ contains arith
metic progressions of every finite length $k \geq 1$.*

Equivalently (finite form): for every integer $k \geq 1$ and every $\delta > 0$,
 there exists $N_0(k, \delta)$ such that every subset of $\{1, \dots, N\}$ with
at least $\delta N$ elements contains a $k$-term arithmetic progression, for all
 $N \geq N_0(k,\delta)$.

## Main Argument

Suppose $A \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ has positive natural density, i.e., the limit

$$d(A) = \lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{|A \cap \{1, \dots, N\}|}{N} = d$$

exists and $d > 0$.

**Key observation:** Whenever a limit exists, the limsup equals the limit. There
fore:

$$\bar{d}(A) = \limsup_{N \to \infty} \frac{|A \cap \{1, \dots, N\}|}{N} = \lim_
{N \to \infty} \frac{|A \cap \{1, \dots, N\}|}{N} = d > 0.$$

So $A$ has positive upper density.

By Szemerédi's theorem (density form), since $\bar{d}(A) > 0$, the set $A$ conta
ins arithmetic progressions of every finite length $k$.

## Explicit verification via the finite form (optional reinforcement)

For any fixed $k$, set $\delta = d/2 > 0$. Since $d(A) = d$, for all sufficientl
y large $N$ (say $N \geq N_1$),

$$\frac{|A \cap \{1, \dots, N\}|}{N} \geq d/2 = \delta.$$

Choose $N \geq \max(N_1, N_0(k, \delta))$. Then $A \cap \{1, \dots, N\}$ has at
least $\delta N$ elements, so by the finite form of Szemerédi's theorem it conta
ins a $k$-term arithmetic progression. Since $k$ was arbitrary, $A$ contains APs
 of every finite length.

## Conclusion

Positive natural density is a **stronger** condition than positive upper density
 (the former requires the limit to exist and be positive; the latter only requir
es the limsup to be positive). Since Szemerédi's theorem applies under the weake
r hypothesis of positive upper density, it applies *a fortiori* to any set with
positive natural density. Therefore:

**Yes, Szemerédi's theorem implies that a set of integers with positive natural
density contains an arithmetic progression of every finite length.** $\blacksqua
