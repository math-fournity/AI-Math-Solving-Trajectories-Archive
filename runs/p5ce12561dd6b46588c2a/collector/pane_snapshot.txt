Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest positive integer $s$ such t
hat operating on cells of a $5\times 5$ board (each operation adds 1 to a chosen
 cell and its side-neighbors) can produce all cells equal to $s$.

## Setup

Let $x_{i,j}$ be the number of times we operate on cell $(i,j)$. The value at ce
ll $(i,j)$ is:
$$v_{i,j} = x_{i,j} + \sum_{\text{neighbors}} x = s$$

This is the system $M\mathbf{x} = s\cdot\mathbf{1}$, where $M = I + A$ ($A$ = ad
jacency matrix of the $5\times 5$ grid).

## Symmetric Solution

By the dihedral symmetry of the grid and target, I classify cells into 6 orbits
with variables $a,b,c,d,e,f$:

| Orbit | Representative | Count |
|-------|---------------|-------|
| Corner | $(1,1)$ | 4 |
| Edge-adj-corner | $(1,2)$ | 8 |
| Edge-middle | $(1,3)$ | 4 |
| Inner-adj-corner | $(2,2)$ | 4 |
| Inner-edge-middle | $(2,3)$ | 4 |
| Center | $(3,3)$ | 1 |

Writing equations for each orbit representative yields the system:
$$a+2b=s,\quad b+a+c+d=s,\quad c+2b+e=s,\quad d+2b+2e=s,\quad e+c+2d+f=s,\quad f
+4e=s$$

Solving: $b=\frac{7e}{3}$, so $e=3t$, giving $s=22t$, $a=8t$, $b=7t$, $c=5t$, $d
=2t$, $e=3t$, $f=10t$.

## Null Space of $M$

The eigenvalues of $A$ for $P_5\times P_5$ are $2\cos\frac{i\pi}{6}+2\cos\frac{j
\pi}{6}$, $i,j=1,\dots,5$, giving values $\sqrt3,1,0,-1,-\sqrt3$. Eigenvalue $-1
$ occurs at $(i,j)=(3,4)$ and $(4,3)$, so $M=I+A$ has eigenvalue $0$: **$M$ is s
ingular** with 2-dimensional null space.

The null space is spanned by integer vectors $\mathbf{n}_1 = \mathbf{u}_3\otimes
\mathbf{w}$ and $\mathbf{n}_2 = \mathbf{w}\otimes\mathbf{u}_3$, where $\mathbf{u
}_3=(1,0,-1,0,1)$ and $\mathbf{w}=(1,-1,0,1,-1)$.

## General Solution and Integrality Constraint

The general solution is $\mathbf{x} = \frac{s}{22}\mathbf{x}_{\text{sym}} + \alp
ha\,\mathbf{n}_1 + \beta\,\mathbf{n}_2$.

**Key observation:** At cells $(2,2),(2,4),(4,2),(4,4)$ and $(3,3)$, both $\math
bf{n}_1$ and $\mathbf{n}_2$ are **zero** (since $\mathbf{u}_3(2)=\mathbf{u}_3(4)
=0$ and $\mathbf{w}(3)=0$). At these cells:
$$x = \frac{s}{22}\cdot 2 = \frac{s}{11} \quad\text{and}\quad x = \frac{s}{22}\c
dot 10 = \frac{5s}{11}$$

For $x$ to be a non-negative integer, we need $\frac{s}{11}\in\mathbb{Z}$, i.e.,
 **$11\mid s$**.

## Achieving $s=11$

Set $s=11$ ($m=1$), $\alpha=\beta=\frac{1}{2}$ (so $p=q=1$, both odd, satisfying
 parity conditions). Then:
$$x(i,j) = \frac{\text{sym}(i,j) + n_1(i,j) + n_2(i,j)}{2}$$

The resulting board is:
$$\begin{pmatrix} 5&3&2&4&4\\3&1&2&1&3\\2&2&5&1&3\\4&1&1&1&4\\4&3&3&4&3 \end{pma
trix}$$

**Verification** (checking that each cell plus its neighbors sums to 11):
- $(1,1)$: $5+3+3=11$ ✓
- $(1,2)$: $3+5+2+1=11$ ✓
- $(2,2)$: $1+3+3+2+2=11$ ✓
- $(3,3)$: $5+2+2+1+1=11$ ✓
- $(5,5)$: $3+4+4=11$ ✓
- $(4,4)$: $1+1+1+4+4=11$ ✓

All 25 cells verify (each cell's value plus its side-neighbors' values equals 11
).

## Conclusion

Since $11\mid s$ is necessary and $s=11$ is achievable, the smallest such $s$ is
 $\boxed{11}$.
