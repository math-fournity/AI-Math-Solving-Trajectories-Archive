Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the derivative functor $d:
Rep(G) \to Rep(\mathfrak{g})$ being bijective on objects (for simply connected $
G$) forces it to be bijective on morphisms.

**Answer: Yes.** Here is the proof.

---

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $G$ be a simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g
}$. The derivative functor $d: Rep(G) \to Rep(\mathfrak{g})$ sends a $G$-represe
ntation $(\rho, V)$ to the $\mathfrak{g}$-representation $(d\rho, V)$, and sends
 a $G$-equivariant map $f$ to the same linear map $f$ (now viewed as a $\mathfra
k{g}$-equivariant map). We are given that $d$ is bijective on objects and must s
how it is bijective on morphisms (fully faithful).

**Key observation: simply connected $\Rightarrow$ connected.** A simply connecte
d topological space is path-connected, hence connected. So $G$ is connected.

**Step 1: Reduction to an invariance question.**

Let $V, W$ be $G$-representations with representation maps $\rho_V: G \to GL(V)$
 and $\rho_W: G \to GL(W)$. The space $\text{Hom}(V, W)$ carries a $G$-action:
$$(g \cdot f)(v) = \rho_W(g)\, f\!\left(\rho_V(g^{-1})\, v\right), \quad g \in G
,\; f \in \text{Hom}(V,W).$$

A linear map $f: V \to W$ is:
- **$G$-equivariant** $\iff$ $g \cdot f = f$ for all $g \in G$ $\iff$ $f \in \te
xt{Hom}(V,W)^G$ (the $G$-invariants).
- **$\mathfrak{g}$-equivariant** $\iff$ $f(d\rho_V(X)\, v) = d\rho_W(X)\, f(v)$
for all $X \in \mathfrak{g}$ $\iff$ $f \in \text{Hom}(V,W)^{\mathfrak{g}}$ (the
$\mathfrak{g}$-invariants).

The derivative of the $G$-action on $\text{Hom}(V,W)$ is exactly the $\mathfrak{
g}$-action:
$$(X \cdot f)(v) = d\rho_W(X)\, f(v) - f\!\left(d\rho_V(X)\, v\right).$$

So the morphism question reduces to:

> **Claim:** For any $G$-representation $U$, the $\mathfrak{g}$-invariants equal
 the $G$-invariants: $U^{\mathfrak{g}} = U^G$.

**Step 2: Proof of the Claim (using connectedness of $G$).**

The inclusion $U^G \subseteq U^{\mathfrak{g}}$ is immediate: if $g \cdot u = u$
for all $g$, differentiating at the identity gives $X \cdot u = 0$ for all $X \i
n \mathfrak{g}$.

For the reverse inclusion, let $u \in U^{\mathfrak{g}}$, so $X \cdot u = 0$ for
all $X \in \mathfrak{g}$. Fix $X \in \mathfrak{g}$ and consider the smooth curve
 $\gamma(t) = \exp(tX) \cdot u$ in $U$. Its derivative is:
$$\gamma'(t) = \left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0} \exp((t+s)X) \cdot u = \exp(tX) \
cdot (X \cdot u) = \exp(tX) \cdot 0 = 0.$$

So $\gamma(t)$ is constant, giving $\exp(X) \cdot u = u$ for all $X \in \mathfra
k{g}$.

Since $G$ is **connected**, it is generated (as a group) by the image of the exp
onential map: every $g \in G$ can be written as $g = \exp(X_1) \cdots \exp(X_k)$
 for some $X_1, \dots, X_k \in \mathfrak{g}$. (This is because the subgroup gene
rated by $\exp(\mathfrak{g})$ is both open and closed in $G$, hence equals $G$ b
y connectedness.) Therefore:
$$g \cdot u = \exp(X_1) \cdots \exp(X_k) \cdot u = u.$$

So $u \in U^G$, proving $U^{\mathfrak{g}} \subseteq U^G$, and hence $U^{\mathfra
k{g}} = U^G$. $\square$

**Step 3: $d$ is fully faithful.**

Applying the Claim to $U = \text{Hom}(V, W)$:
$$\text{Hom}_{Rep(G)}(V, W) = \text{Hom}(V,W)^G = \text{Hom}(V,W)^{\mathfrak{g}}
 = \text{Hom}_{Rep(\mathfrak{g})}(dV, dW).$$

The functor $d$ acts as the identity on these linear maps (a $G$-equivariant map
 is sent to itself, now viewed as $\mathfrak{g}$-equivariant). The equality abov
e shows:
- **Faithful (injective on morphisms):** If $d(f) = d(f')$, then $f = f'$ as lin
ear maps, so $f = f'$.
- **Full (surjective on morphisms):** Every $\mathfrak{g}$-equivariant map $dV \
to dW$ is a $\mathfrak{g}$-invariant in $\text{Hom}(V,W)$, hence a $G$-invariant
, hence $G$-equivariant — so it lies in the image of $d$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Since $d$ is bijective on objects (given) and fully faithful (proved), it is an
**isomorphism of categories** $Rep(G) \cong Rep(\mathfrak{g})$.

**Remark on the hypotheses.** The bijectivity on objects uses simply connectedne
ss (every $\mathfrak{g}$-representation integrates uniquely to a $G$-representat
ion). However, the bijectivity on morphisms only requires $G$ to be **connected*
* — the argument never uses the full strength of simply connectedness. The simpl
y connected hypothesis is needed for the object-level bijection, and the morphis
m-level bijection follows from connectedness alone.
