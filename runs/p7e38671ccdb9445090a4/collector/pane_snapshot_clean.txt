## Solution

We evaluate
$$I = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \frac{\sin^{4}(x)}{x^{2}}\,dx.$$

**Step 1. Trigonometric reduction.** Using $\sin^{2}x=\frac{1-\cos 2x}{2}$ and $
\cos^{2}2x=\frac{1+\cos 4x}{2}$,

$$\sin^{4}x=\frac{(1-\cos 2x)^{2}}{4}=\frac{1-2\cos 2x+\cos^{2}2x}{4}=\frac{3-4\
cos 2x+\cos 4x}{8}.$$

**Step 2. Rewrite in terms of $1-\cos(ax)$.** Observe
$$3-4\cos 2x+\cos 4x = 4(1-\cos 2x)-(1-\cos 4x),$$
since $4-4\cos 2x-1+\cos 4x = 3-4\cos 2x+\cos 4x$. Hence
$$\frac{\sin^{4}x}{x^{2}}=\frac{4(1-\cos 2x)-(1-\cos 4x)}{8\,x^{2}}.$$

This decomposition is legitimate because each term $\frac{1-\cos(ax)}{x^{2}}$ is
 bounded near $x=0$ (behaving like $\frac{a^{2}}{2}$) and decays as $O(x^{-2})$
at infinity, so both pieces are integrable; equivalently the original integrand
$\sim x^{2}$ near $0$ and $\sim x^{-2}$ at infinity.

**Step 3. Use the standard integral.** For $a>0$,
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos(ax)}{x^{2}}\,dx = \frac{\pi a}{2},$$
obtained by differentiating in $a$: $\frac{d}{da}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos a
x}{x^{2}}dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin ax}{x}dx=\frac{\pi}{2}$, with value $0$
at $a=0$. The integrand is even, so
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1-\cos(ax)}{x^{2}}\,dx = \pi a, \qquad a>0.$$

**Step 4. Combine.**
$$I=\frac{1}{8}\left[4\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1-\cos 2x}{x^{2}}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R
}}\frac{1-\cos 4x}{x^{2}}dx\right]=\frac{1}{8}\bigl[4\cdot(2\pi)-4\pi\bigr]=\fra
c{1}{8}(8\pi-4\pi)=\frac{4\pi}{8}.$$

Therefore
$$\boxed{\int_{\mathbb{R}} \frac{\sin^{4}(x)}{x^{2}}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{2}}.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
