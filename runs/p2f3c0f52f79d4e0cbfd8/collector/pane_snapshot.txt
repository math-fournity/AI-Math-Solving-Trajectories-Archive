$$
\mathrm{Hom}(A,A) = \{\mathrm{id}_A,\; h\}, \qquad
\mathrm{Hom}(B,B) = \{\mathrm{id}_B,\; k\}, \qquad
\mathrm{Hom}(A,B) = \{f\}, \qquad
\mathrm{Hom}(B,A) = \{g\},
$$

where $h$ and $k$ are distinct from the respective identities. Composition is de
fined by the relations

$$
g \circ f = h, \qquad f \circ g = k, \qquad h \circ h = h, \qquad k \circ k = k,
$$

together with

$$
f \circ h = f, \quad h \circ g = g, \quad k \circ f = f, \quad g \circ k = g,
$$

and the usual identity laws. (These auxiliary relations are in fact forced: e.g.
 $f \circ h = f \circ (g \circ f) = (f \circ g) \circ f = k \circ f$, and $h \ci
rc g = (g \circ f) \circ g = g \circ (f \circ g) = g \circ k$.)

**Well-definedness.** The relations $f \circ g \circ f = f$ and $g \circ f \circ
 g = g$ (equivalent to the four auxiliary relations above) ensure that composing
 any morphism with $h$ on the appropriate side or with $k$ on the appropriate si

ity directly: the only nontrivial triples to check are

$$
(f \circ g)\circ f = f \circ (g \circ f) \;(= f), \qquad (g \circ f)\circ g = g
\circ (f \circ g)\;(= g),
$$

and the idempotent triples $(h\circ h)\circ h = h\circ(h\circ h)\;(=h)$, $(k\cir
c k)\circ k = k\circ(k\circ k)\;(=k)$, together with triples obtained by inserti

Hom}(B,A) = \{g\}$, so there is a unique morphism $A \to B$ and a unique morphis

$$
g \circ f = h \neq \mathrm{id}_A \qquad\text{and}\qquad f \circ g = k \neq \math
