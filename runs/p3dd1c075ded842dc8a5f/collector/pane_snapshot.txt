Looking at this problem, I need to find the number of ways to write 1000 as a su
m of consecutive (positive) integers, where a "sum" requires at least two terms.

## Proof

**Setup.** Suppose 1000 is written as the sum of $k$ consecutive positive intege
rs starting at $a \geq 1$, with $k \geq 2$:

$$1000 = a + (a+1) + (a+2) + \cdots + (a+k-1) = ka + \frac{k(k-1)}{2}.$$

Multiplying both sides by 2:

$$2000 = k(2a + k - 1).$$

**Key observation.** The two factors $k$ and $2a+k-1$ have **different parity**:
 their difference is $(2a+k-1) - k = 2a - 1$, which is odd, so one factor is odd
 and the other is even.

Since $2000 = 2^4 \times 5^3$, the odd factor among $\{k,\; 2a+k-1\}$ must divid
e the odd part $5^3 = 125$, hence is an **odd divisor of 1000**.

**Bijection.** I claim there is a bijection between:
- representations of 1000 as a sum of $k \geq 1$ consecutive positive integers,
and
- odd divisors of 1000.

*From representation to odd divisor:* Given a representation, $2000 = k(2a+k-1)$
 with $k$ and $2a+k-1$ of different parity. The odd one among them divides 125,
hence is an odd divisor of 1000.

*From odd divisor to representation:* Given an odd divisor $d$ of 1000, set the
two factors of 2000 to be $d$ (odd) and $2000/d$ (even). Assign the **smaller**
factor to $k$ and the **larger** to $2a+k-1$:
- If $d < 2000/d$: set $k = d$, $2a+k-1 = 2000/d$, giving $a = \frac{2000/d - d
+ 1}{2}$.
- If $d > 2000/d$: set $k = 2000/d$, $2a+k-1 = d$, giving $a = \frac{d - 2000/d
+ 1}{2}$.

Since $d \neq 2000/d$ (as $d$ is odd and $2000/d$ is even, they can't be equal),
 the smaller factor is strictly less than the larger, so $a = \frac{\text{larger
} - \text{smaller} + 1}{2} \geq \frac{2}{2} = 1$. Thus $a \geq 1$ always holds.
✓

*Injectivity:* Different odd divisors yield different values of $k$ (the smaller
 factor), so the representations are distinct. (Two odd divisors $d_1 \neq d_2$
can't produce the same $k$: if both are smaller than their partners, $k = d_1 \n
eq d_2$; if both are larger, $k = 2000/d_1 \neq 2000/d_2$; if one is smaller and
 one larger, the resulting $k$ values have different parities — odd vs. even — s
o they differ.)

**Counting.** The odd divisors of $1000 = 2^3 \times 5^3$ are exactly the diviso
rs of $5^3 = 125$:

$$1,\; 5,\; 25,\; 125 \quad \text{(4 odd divisors)}.$$

This gives **4 representations** (including the trivial $k=1$ case where $1000 =
 1000$). Since a "sum of consecutive integers" requires at least two terms, we e
xclude $k=1$, leaving:

$$4 - 1 = \boxed{3}.$$

**Explicit verification of the three representations:**

| Odd divisor $d$ | $k$ | $a$ | Representation | Check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $d=5$ | $k=5$ | $a=198$ | $198+199+200+201+202$ | $5 \times 200 = 1000$ ✓ |
| $d=25$ | $k=25$ | $a=28$ | $28+29+\cdots+52$ | $25 \times 40 = 1000$ ✓ |
| $d=125$ | $k=16$ | $a=55$ | $55+56+\cdots+70$ | $16 \times 62.5 = 1000$ ✓ |

(The odd divisor $d=1$ gives $k=1$, the trivial single-term sum $1000$, which we
 exclude.)
