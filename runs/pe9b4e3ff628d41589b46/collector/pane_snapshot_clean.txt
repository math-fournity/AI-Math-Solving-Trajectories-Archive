Yes, $F_X^{-1}(U)$ is a Borel set. In fact it is open.

## Proof

**Step 1: $X$ is bounded.**

The operator $X$ is stated to be a self-adjoint linear operator on $H$, and $F_X
$ is defined on **all** of $H$ by $F_X(a)=\langle a,Xa\rangle$. Hence $X$ is def
ined on the entire space $H$ and is symmetric: for all $a,b\in H$,
$$\langle a,Xb\rangle=\langle Xa,b\rangle,$$
since $X=X^{*}$. By the **Hellinger–Toeplitz theorem**, a symmetric (self-adjoin
t) operator defined on the whole Hilbert space $H$ is necessarily bounded. Thus
there exists $M\ge 0$ with $\|Xa\|\le M\|a\|$ for all $a\in H$.

**Step 2: $F_X$ is continuous.**

For $a,b\in H$, using symmetry of $X$ and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,
$$
\begin{aligned}
|F_X(a)-F_X(b)|
&=|\langle a,Xa\rangle-\langle b,Xb\rangle| \\
&=|\langle a,Xa\rangle-\langle a,Xb\rangle+\langle a,Xb\rangle-\langle b,Xb\rang
le| \\
&\le |\langle a,X(a-b)\rangle|+|\langle a-b,Xb\rangle| \\
&\le \|a\|\,\|X\|\,\|a-b\|+\|a-b\|\,\|X\|\,\|b\| \\
&=\|X\|\,\|a-b\|\bigl(\|a\|+\|b\|\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
Fix $b$; as $a\to b$ we have $\|a\|\to\|b\|$ (bounded near $b$), so $|F_X(a)-F_X
(b)|\to 0$. Hence $F_X$ is continuous at every $b\in H$.

**Step 3: Preimage of an open set under a continuous map is open.**

Since $F_X:H\to\mathbb{R}$ is continuous and $U\subseteq\mathbb{R}$ is open, the
 preimage
$$F_X^{-1}(U)=\{a\in H:F_X(a)\in U\}$$
is open in the norm topology of $H$.

**Step 4: Open sets are Borel.**

Every open set in the norm topology belongs to the Borel $\sigma$-algebra of tha
t topology. Therefore $F_X^{-1}(U)$ is a Borel set.
