## Proof

**Setup.** The component is released from height $h = 500\text{ m}$ with initial
 horizontal velocity $v = 100\text{ km/h}$, while the artificial gravity is $g =
 10\text{ m/s}^2$. There is no air resistance, so horizontal and vertical motion
s are independent.

**Convert units.**
$$v = 100\text{ km/h} = \frac{100 \times 1000}{3600}\text{ m/s} = \frac{1000}{36
}\text{ m/s} = \frac{250}{9}\text{ m/s}.$$

**Vertical motion (determines time of flight).** The component is released with
zero initial vertical velocity. Under constant acceleration $g$:
$$h = \tfrac{1}{2} g t^2.$$
Substituting:
$$500 = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 10 \cdot t^2 = 5t^2,$$
$$t^2 = 100, \qquad t = 10\text{ s}.$$

**Horizontal motion (determines the release distance).** Since there is no horiz
ontal acceleration, the horizontal distance traveled during the fall is:
$$d = v \cdot t = \frac{250}{9} \cdot 10 = \frac{2500}{9}\text{ m}.$$

**Numerical value.**
$$d = \frac{2500}{9} \approx 277.78\text{ m}.$$

**Conclusion.** The robot must release the component at a horizontal distance of
 $\boxed{\dfrac{2500}{9}\text{ m} \approx 277.78\text{ m}}$ from the collection
bin.

### PROOF COMPLETE
