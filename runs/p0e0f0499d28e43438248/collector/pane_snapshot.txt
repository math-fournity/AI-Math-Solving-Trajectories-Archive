## Solution

**Setup.** Place the origin at the common intersection point, with the horizonta
l plane as the $xy$-plane. The three cylinder axes are undirected lines through
the origin at angles $0$, $\pi/3$, $2\pi/3$. Each cylinder has radius $1$.

A cylinder whose axis lies along direction $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta,0)$ with radi
us $1$ is the set of points $(x,y,z)$ satisfying
$$z^2 + (x\sin\theta - y\cos\theta)^2 \le 1,$$
since $x\sin\theta - y\cos\theta$ is the signed in-plane distance from $(x,y)$ t
o the axis line, and $z$ is the out-of-plane distance.

Define the three signed in-plane distances:
$$u_1 = y, \qquad u_2 = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}x - \tfrac12 y, \qquad u_3 = \tfrac{\sq
rt3}{2}x + \tfrac12 y.$$
Note that $u_3 - u_2 = y = u_1$, so $u_3 = u_1 + u_2$. The three cylinder constr
aints become
$$z^2 + u_i^2 \le 1, \quad i=1,2,3.$$

**Change of variables.** Use $(u_1, u_2)$ as coordinates (with $u_3 = u_1+u_2$).
 Since $x = \frac{u_1 + 2u_2}{\sqrt3}$ and $y = u_1$, the Jacobian is
$$\left|\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u_1,u_2)}\right| = \left|\det\begin{pmatri
x}1/\sqrt3 & 2/\sqrt3 \\ 1 & 0\end{pmatrix}\right| = \frac{2}{\sqrt3}.$$

The intersection requires $|u_i|\le 1$ for all $i$, defining the domain
$$D = \{(u_1,u_2): |u_1|\le1,\; |u_2|\le1,\; |u_1+u_2|\le1\}$$
(a hexagon with vertices $(1,0),(1,-1),(0,-1),(-1,0),(-1,1),(0,1)$, area $= 3$).

For each $(u_1,u_2)\in D$, the $z$-range is $|z|\le m$ where $m = \min_i\sqrt{1-
u_i^2}$, giving a $z$-extent of $2m$. Thus
$$V = \frac{2}{\sqrt3}\iint_D 2m\;du_1\,du_2 = \frac{4}{\sqrt3}\iint_D \min\!\Bi
g(\sqrt{1-u_1^2},\,\sqrt{1-u_2^2},\,\sqrt{1-(u_1+u_2)^2}\Big)\,du_1\,du_2.$$

**Exploiting symmetry.** The $120°$ rotation in the $xy$-plane cyclically permut
es the three axes, hence cyclically permutes $(u_1,u_2,u_3)$. In $(u_1,u_2)$ coo
rdinates this is the linear map $(u_1,u_2)\mapsto(u_1+u_2,\,u_1)$ with $|\det|=1
$, which preserves $D$ and the integrand. Therefore the three sub-regions of $D$
 where each $|u_i|$ is the largest contribute equally:
$$V = \frac{4}{\sqrt3}\cdot 3 \iint_{R} \sqrt{1-u_1^2}\;du_1\,du_2,$$
where $R = \{(u_1,u_2)\in D : |u_1|\ge|u_2|,\; |u_1|\ge|u_1+u_2|\}$.

**Evaluating the integral over $R$.** Split by the sign of $u_1$.

*Case $u_1 \ge 0$:* $|u_1|=u_1$. The conditions $u_1\ge|u_2|$ and $u_1\ge|u_1+u_
2|$ give $-u_1\le u_2\le 0$ and $0\le u_1\le 1$:
$$I_+ = \int_0^1\int_{-u_1}^{0}\sqrt{1-u_1^2}\;du_2\,du_1 = \int_0^1 u_1\sqrt{1-
u_1^2}\;du_1 = \frac{1}{3}.$$

*Case $u_1 \le 0$:* $|u_1|=-u_1$. The conditions give $0\le u_2\le -u_1$ and $-1
\le u_1\le 0$:
$$I_- = \int_{-1}^{0}\int_{0}^{-u_1}\sqrt{1-u_1^2}\;du_2\,du_1 = \int_{-1}^{0}(-
u_1)\sqrt{1-u_1^2}\;du_1 = \frac{1}{3}.$$

(The last equality follows by the substitution $t=-u_1$, reducing to the same in
tegral as $I_+$. This equality also reflects the $180°$ rotational symmetry $(u_
1,u_2)\mapsto(-u_1,-u_2)$.)

**Combining.**
$$\iint_R \sqrt{1-u_1^2}\;du_1\,du_2 = I_+ + I_- = \frac{2}{3},$$
$$V = \frac{4}{\sqrt3}\cdot 3 \cdot \frac{2}{3} = \frac{8}{\sqrt3} = \frac{8\sqr
t3}{3}.$$

**The volume of the intersection of the three cylinders is**

$$\boxed{\dfrac{8\sqrt{3}}{3}}.$$
