## Proof

We evaluate
$$I = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \sin\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}\,\sin x}{3}\right)\cos\!\left(\
frac{\cos x}{3}\right)dx.$$

**Step 1: Product-to-sum.** Set $a = \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{3}$, $b = \tfrac{1}{3}$. U
sing $\sin A\cos B = \tfrac{1}{2}[\sin(A+B)+\sin(A-B)]$:

$$I = \frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big[\sin\!\big(a\sin x + b\cos x\big)+\sin\!\bi
g(a\sin x - b\cos x\big)\Big]\,dx.$$

**Step 2: Combine into a single sinusoid.** Both expressions are of the form $R\
sin(x\pm\phi)$ with

$$R = \sqrt{a^2+b^2} = \sqrt{\tfrac{2}{9}+\tfrac{1}{9}} = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}} =
\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}},$$

and some phase $\phi$ (with $\cos\phi = a/R$, $\sin\phi = b/R$). Specifically:

$$a\sin x + b\cos x = R\sin(x+\phi),\qquad a\sin x - b\cos x = R\sin(x-\phi).$$

**Step 3: Exploit periodicity.** Since $\sin(R\sin(\cdot))$ is $2\pi$-periodic,
shifting the integration variable by a constant phase does not change the integr
al over a full period:

$$\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin\!\big(R\sin(x+\phi)\big)\,dx = \int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin(R\sin u
)\,du,$$

and likewise for $x-\phi$. Hence

$$I = \frac{1}{2}\cdot 2\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin(R\sin u)\,du = \int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin(R
\sin u)\,du,\qquad R=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}.$$

**Step 4: Apply the Jacobi–Anger expansion (Bessel function representation).** T
he expansion

$$e^{iR\sin u} = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} J_n(R)\,e^{inu}$$

gives, by taking the imaginary part,

$$\sin(R\sin u) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} J_n(R)\,\sin(nu).$$

Integrating term-by-term over $[0,2\pi]$:

$$\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin(R\sin u)\,du = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}J_n(R)\underbrace{
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin(nu)\,du}_{=\,0\ \text{for all }n\in\mathbb{Z}} = 0.$$

(Every term vanishes: for $n=0$, $\sin(0\cdot u)=0$; for $n\neq 0$, the integral
 of $\sin(nu)$ over a full period is zero.)

**Symmetry verification.** This result is confirmed by a direct symmetry argumen
t: under $u\mapsto u+\pi$, $\sin u\mapsto -\sin u$, so $\sin(R\sin(u+\pi)) = -\s
in(R\sin u)$. Splitting $[0,2\pi]=[0,\pi]\cup[\pi,2\pi]$ and substituting $v=u-\
pi$ in the second piece shows the two halves cancel, yielding $0$.

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{I = \int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}\sin x}{3}\right)\cos\!\le
ft(\frac{\cos x}{3}\right)dx = 0.}$$

The Bessel-function content is that $\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos(z\sin u)\,du = 2\pi J_0
(z)$ while $\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin(z\sin u)\,du = 0$ (the latter following from the
 Jacobi–Anger expansion, since only the $J_0$ term survives integration and it c
ontributes to the cosine, not the sine, part).
