# Spectrum of the Backward Shift on $\ell_2$

**Setup.** Let $T: \ell_2 \to \ell_2$ be defined by $Tx = (x_2, x_3, \ldots)$ fo
r $x = (x_1, x_2, \ldots) \in \ell_2$. This is the *backward* (left) unilateral
shift.

---

## Step 1. $T$ is bounded with $\|T\| = 1$

For $x \in \ell_2$,
$$\|Tx\|^2 = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} |x_{n+1}|^2 = \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} |x_n|^2 \le \
sum_{n=1}^{\infty} |x_n|^2 = \|x\|^2,$$
so $\|T\| \le 1$. Equality holds (take $x = e_2 = (0,1,0,\ldots)$, then $\|Tx\|
= \|e_1\| = 1 = \|x\|$). Hence $\|T\| = 1$.

Since the spectrum of a bounded operator satisfies $\sigma(T) \subseteq \{\lambd
a : |\lambda| \le \|T\|\}$, we get
$$\sigma(T) \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{D}} := \{\lambda \in \mathbb{C} : |\lamb
da| \le 1\}.$$

---

## Step 2. The open unit disk $\mathbb{D}$ lies in the point spectrum $\sigma_p(
T)$

Let $|\lambda| < 1$. Define $x^{(\lambda)} = (1, \lambda, \lambda^2, \lambda^3,
\ldots)$, i.e. $x_n = \lambda^{n-1}$. Then
$$\|x^{(\lambda)}\|^2 = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} |\lambda|^{2n} = \frac{1}{1 - |\lamb
da|^2} < \infty,$$
so $x^{(\lambda)} \in \ell_2$ and $x^{(\lambda)} \ne 0$. Moreover
$$Tx^{(\lambda)} = (\lambda, \lambda^2, \lambda^3, \ldots) = \lambda(1, \lambda,
 \lambda^2, \ldots) = \lambda\, x^{(\lambda)}.$$
Thus every $\lambda$ with $|\lambda| < 1$ is an eigenvalue of $T$. Therefore
$$\mathbb{D} \subseteq \sigma_p(T) \subseteq \sigma(T).$$

---

## Step 3. $\sigma(T)$ is closed, hence $\overline{\mathbb{D}} \subseteq \sigma(
T)$

The spectrum of any bounded operator on a Banach space is a non-empty **closed**
 subset of $\mathbb{C}$. Since $\mathbb{D} \subseteq \sigma(T)$ and $\sigma(T)$
is closed,
$$\overline{\mathbb{D}} = \overline{\mathbb{D}} \subseteq \sigma(T).$$

---

## Step 4. Conclusion

Combining the two inclusions:
$$\overline{\mathbb{D}} \subseteq \sigma(T) \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{D}},$$
we obtain
$$\boxed{\sigma(T) = \{\lambda \in \mathbb{C} : |\lambda| \le 1\}.}$$

---

**Remark on the boundary.** For $|\lambda| = 1$, the candidate eigenvector $(1,
\lambda, \lambda^2, \ldots)$ is *not* in $\ell_2$ (its norm diverges), so bounda
ry points are not eigenvalues. They belong to the *continuous* spectrum of $T$:
for $|\lambda| = 1$, $T - \lambda I$ is injective but not surjective (its range
is not closed / is a proper dense subspace), confirming they lie in $\sigma(T) \
setminus \sigma_p(T)$.
