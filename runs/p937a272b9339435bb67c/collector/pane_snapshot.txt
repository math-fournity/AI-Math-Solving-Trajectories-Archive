# Proof

**Claim:** The smallest value of $p \geq 1$ for which $\sum_{n=1}^\infty |b_n|^p
$ converges (for every bounded linear functional $\Phi$ on $\ell^2$) is $p = 2$.

---

## Step 1: $(b_n) \in \ell^2$

By the Riesz representation theorem for $\ell^2$, every bounded linear functiona
l $\Phi$ on $\ell^2$ has the form
$$\Phi(x) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n\, x_n$$
for a unique $a = (a_n) \in \ell^2$, with $\|\Phi\| = \|a\|_2$.

Since $e_n$ has $n$-th coordinate $1$ and all others $0$:
$$b_n = \Phi(e_n) = a_n.$$
Therefore $(b_n) = (a_n) \in \ell^2$, i.e.,
$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty |b_n|^2 < \infty.$$

---

## Step 2: $p = 2$ always works

This is immediate from Step 1: $\sum |b_n|^2 < \infty$ for every bounded linear
functional $\Phi$.

---

## Step 3: $p > 2$ also works

Since $(b_n) \in \ell^2$, we have $b_n \to 0$, so there exists $N$ with $|b_n| \
leq 1$ for all $n \geq N$. For $p > 2$ and $n \geq N$:
$$|b_n|^p = |b_n|^2 \cdot |b_n|^{p-2} \leq |b_n|^2.$$
Hence $\sum |b_n|^p \leq \sum_{n<N}|b_n|^p + \sum_{n\geq N}|b_n|^2 < \infty$.

---

## Step 4: $p < 2$ fails — there exists $\Phi$ with $\sum |b_n|^p = \infty$

We construct a counterexample. Define for $n \geq 2$:
$$b_n = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\,\log n}, \qquad b_1 = 0.$$

**Check $(b_n) \in \ell^2$:** We have $b_n^2 = \frac{1}{n(\log n)^2}$. By the in
tegral test,
$$\int_2^\infty \frac{dx}{x(\log x)^2} = \left[-\frac{1}{\log x}\right]_2^\infty
 = \frac{1}{\log 2} < \infty,$$
so $\sum_{n=2}^\infty b_n^2 < \infty$. Thus $(b_n) \in \ell^2$, and by Riesz, th
ere exists a bounded linear functional $\Phi$ on $\ell^2$ with $\Phi(e_n) = b_n$
.

**Check $\sum |b_n|^p = \infty$ for $p < 2$:** We have
$$|b_n|^p = \frac{1}{n^{p/2}(\log n)^p}.$$
Since $p < 2$, we have $\alpha := p/2 < 1$. The series $\sum \frac{1}{n^\alpha (
\log n)^p}$ diverges because $\alpha < 1$ (the power $n^{-\alpha}$ with $\alpha
< 1$ decays too slowly; the logarithmic factor cannot compensate — by comparison
/limit comparison with $\sum 1/n^\alpha$, since $\frac{n^{-\alpha}(\log n)^{-p}}
{n^{-\alpha}} = (\log n)^{-p} \to 0$ is insufficient, or more precisely by the i
ntegral test: $\int \frac{dx}{x^\alpha (\log x)^p}$ diverges for $\alpha < 1$).

Therefore $\sum_{n=1}^\infty |b_n|^p = \infty$ for this particular bounded linea
r functional $\Phi$.

---

## Conclusion

- For $p < 2$: there exists a bounded linear functional $\Phi$ on $\ell^2$ such
that $\sum |b_n|^p$ diverges. ✗
- For $p = 2$: $\sum |b_n|^p$ converges for every bounded linear functional $\Ph
i$. ✓
- For $p > 2$: also converges (but $p=2$ is smaller).

The smallest value is $\boxed{p = 2}$.
