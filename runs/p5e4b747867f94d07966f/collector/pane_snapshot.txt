## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. For $K$ algebraically closed and $M \subset K[X_1,\dots,X_n]$ an
y maximal ideal, there is a $K$-algebra isomorphism $K[X_1,\dots,X_n]/M \cong K$
 fixing every element of $K$.

---

**Step 1. The residue field is a finitely generated $K$-algebra that is a field.
**

Since $M$ is maximal, $L := K[X_1,\dots,X_n]/M$ is a field. It is generated as a
 $K$-algebra by the images $\bar X_1,\dots,\bar X_n$ of the variables, so $L = K
[\bar X_1,\dots,\bar X_n]$ is a finitely generated $K$-algebra. The quotient map
 $\pi: K[X_1,\dots,X_n] \to L$ restricts to the identity on $K$ (constants are n
ot in $M$ since $M \cap K = \{0\}$, as $M \neq K[X_1,\dots,X_n]$), so $L$ is a f
ield extension of $K$ and the natural embedding $K \hookrightarrow L$ fixes $K$.

**Step 2. Zariski's Lemma: a field that is a finitely generated $K$-algebra is a
n algebraic extension of $K$.**

This is Zariski's Lemma. Briefly: if $L = K[y_1,\dots,y_n]$ is a field, we induc
t on $n$. If $n=0$, $L=K$. Otherwise, if some $y_i$ is transcendental over $K$,
then $K[y_i] \cong K[t]$ and $L$ is a finite-dimensional (hence integral) algebr
a over the PID $K[y_i]$; since $L$ is a field, $K[y_i]$ must also be a field, co
ntradicting $K[t]$ not being a field. Hence every $y_i$ is algebraic over $K$, s
o $L/K$ is algebraic.

**Step 3. Since $K$ is algebraically closed, the only algebraic field extension
of $K$ is $K$ itself.**

By Step 2, $L/K$ is algebraic. Since $K$ is algebraically closed, it admits no p
roper algebraic extension: every element of $L$ satisfies a polynomial over $K$,
 which splits completely over $K$, so every element of $L$ already lies in $K$.
Thus $L = K$.

**Step 4. The isomorphism fixes $K$.**

The composition
$$K \xhookrightarrow{\iota} K[X_1,\dots,X_n] \xrightarrow{\pi} L = K[X_1,\dots,X
_n]/M \xrightarrow{\,\cong\,} K$$
is the identity on $K$, because $\pi$ fixes constants (Step 1) and the identific
ation $L \cong K$ from Step 3 sends each element of $L$ — which is algebraic ove
r $K$ and hence equal to an element of $K$ — to that same element. Therefore the
 isomorphism $K[X_1,\dots,X_n]/M \xrightarrow{\sim} K$ fixes every element of $K
$.

---

**Conclusion.** For every maximal ideal $M$ of $K[X_1,\dots,X_n]$ with $K$ algeb
raically closed, the quotient $K[X_1,\dots,X_n]/M$ is $K$-algebra isomorphic to
$K$ via an isomorphism fixing $K$ pointwise. This is precisely the weak Hilbert
Nullstellensatz (together with Zariski's Lemma).
