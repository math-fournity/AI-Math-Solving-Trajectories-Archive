## Solution

**Setup.** Let $\alpha = \sqrt[5]{3}$ and let $\zeta$ be a primitive 5th root of
 unity. The roots of $x^5 - 3$ are $\alpha, \zeta\alpha, \zeta^2\alpha, \zeta^3\
alpha, \zeta^4\alpha$.

**The splitting field over $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$.** Since $\zeta \in \mathbb{Q}(\z
eta)$ already, adjoining a single root $\alpha$ to $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$ produces
all five roots (each $\zeta^k\alpha \in \mathbb{Q}(\zeta)(\alpha)$). Hence

$$K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta)(\alpha) = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta, \alpha).$$

**Irreducibility of $x^5 - 3$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$.** The polynomial $x^5 -
3$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ by Eisenstein's criterion at $p = 3$. We hav
e $[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta):\mathbb{Q}] = \varphi(5) = 4$ and $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\ma
thbb{Q}] = 5$. The degree $:\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)]$ divides 5 (since $\alpha$ sati
sfies $x^5 - 3$), so it is either 1 or 5. If it were 1, then $\alpha \in \mathbb
{Q}(\zeta)$, which would give $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$ a
nd hence $5 \mid 4$, a contradiction. Therefore $x^5 - 3$ is irreducible over $\
mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$ and

$$ : \mathbb{Q}(\zeta)] = 5.$$

**The Galois group.** Since $K$ is the splitting field of the separable polynomi
al $x^5 - 3$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$ (we are in characteristic 0), the extensio
n $K/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$ is Galois. Thus

$$|\text{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta))| = :\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)] = 5.$$

Since 5 is prime, the Galois group is cyclic of order 5:

$$\text{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}.$$

A generator $\sigma$ is the automorphism determined by $\sigma(\alpha) = \zeta\a
lpha$ and $\sigma|_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)} = \text{id}$. One checks: $\sigma^k(\alph
a) = \zeta^k \alpha$ for $k = 0,1,2,3,4$, and $\sigma^5(\alpha) = \zeta^5 \alpha
 = \alpha$, so $\sigma$ has order 5. The elements are:

$$\text{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)) = \{e, \sigma, \sigma^2, \sigma^3, \sigma^4\}.
$$

**Subgroups and the Galois correspondence.** Since $|\text{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}(\ze
ta))| = 5$ is prime, the only subgroups are:

| Subgroup | Order | Description |
|---|---|---|
| $\text{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)) = \langle \sigma \rangle$ | 5 | The full grou
p |
| $\{e\}$ | 1 | The trivial subgroup |

By the Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory, subgroups correspond to intermediat
e fields $E$ with $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta) \subseteq E \subseteq K$, with the correspo
ndence **order-reversed** (larger subgroups correspond to smaller fields):

| Subgroup $H$ | $|H|$ | Fixed field $K^H$ | $^H : \mathbb{Q}(\zeta)]$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\{e\}$ | 1 | $K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta, \alpha)$ | 5 |
| $\langle \sigma \rangle$ | 5 | $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$ | 1 |

**The lattice (order-reversed picture).**

```
        Fields                          Subgroups

  K = Q(ζ, α)                      {e}          (order 1)
      |                              |
      |  [degree 5]                  |  [index 5]
      |                              |
  Q(ζ)                        ⟨σ⟩ = Gal(K/Q(ζ))  (order 5)
```

Since 5 is prime, there are **no proper nontrivial subgroups** and consequently
**no intermediate fields** strictly between $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$ and $K$. The ext
ension $K/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$ has prime degree, so it is a minimal (and maximal)
extension.

**Summary of orders.** The Galois group $\text{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta))$ has or
der 5. Its two subgroups have orders 5 (the full group, corresponding to the bas
e field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$) and 1 (the trivial group, corresponding to the spli
tting field $K$). There are no other subgroups, and no intermediate fields exist
 between $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$ and $K$.
