Looking at this problem, I need to find the ratio of the radii of two Dandelin s
pheres inscribed in a cone, tangent to the cone and to a cutting plane at the fo
ci of the resulting ellipse.

## Setting Up the Geometry

**The triangle ABC.** We have AB = 3, AC = 5, BC = 4. Since 3² + 4² = 5², triang
le ABC is a right triangle with the right angle at B. So AB ⊥ BC.

**Cone structure.** A is the vertex of the cone. B and C lie on the cone surface
, so AB and AC are generators of the cone. The plane π passes through B and C, c
utting the cone in ellipse E with major axis BC. Since BC = 4, the semi-major ax
is is a = 2.

**Angle between generators.** By the law of cosines:
$$\cos(\angle BAC) = \frac{AB^2 + AC^2 - BC^2}{2 \cdot AB \cdot AC} = \frac{9 +
25 - 16}{30} = \frac{3}{5}$$

## Dandelin Sphere Properties

Let the cone have half-angle α (angle between axis and generator). The two Dande
lin spheres are tangent to the cone along circles at heights h₁ and h₂ (measured
 along the axis from A), with h₁ < h₂.

**Key theorem:** For any point P on the ellipse at distance r_P from A along its
 generator:
- Sphere 1 (between A and π): tangent circle at height h₁, so **PF₁ = r_P − h₁/c
os α**
- Sphere 2 (beyond π): tangent circle at height h₂, so **PF₂ = h₂/cos α − r_P**
- Sum: PF₁ + PF₂ = (h₂ − h₁)/cos α = 2a = 4 ✓

## Locating the Foci

Place B at position 0 and C at position 4 on the major axis. The center is at po
sition 2; foci are at 2−c and 2+c.

**Using point B (r_B = 3):** BF₁ = 3 − h₁/cos α, BF₂ = h₂/cos α − 3
**Using point C (r_C = 5):** CF₁ = 5 − h₁/cos α, CF₂ = h₂/cos α − 5

The difference CF₁ − BF₁ = 2. Since F₁ is the focus from the sphere on the A-sid
e (closer to B), F₁ is at position 2−c, giving CF₁ − BF₁ = 2c = 2, so **c = 1**.

Therefore:
- BF₁ = 2 − c = **1**, BF₂ = 2 + c = **3**
- CF₁ = 2 + c = **3**, CF₂ = 2 − c = **1**

Verification: BF₁ + BF₂ = 4 ✓, CF₁ + CF₂ = 4 ✓.

## Computing the Heights

From BF₁ = 3 − h₁/cos α = 1: **h₁/cos α = 2**, so h₁ = 2 cos α.

From BF₂ = h₂/cos α − 3 = 3: **h₂/cos α = 6**, so h₂ = 6 cos α.

## Computing the Radii

A sphere centered on the cone axis at height h, tangent internally to the cone,
has radius:
$$r = h \sin\alpha$$

Therefore:
$$r_1 = h_1 \sin\alpha = 2\cos\alpha\sin\alpha = \sin 2\alpha$$
$$r_2 = h_2 \sin\alpha = 6\cos\alpha\sin\alpha = 3\sin 2\alpha$$

## The Ratio

$$\frac{r_1}{r_2} = \frac{h_1 \sin\alpha}{h_2 \sin\alpha} = \frac{h_1}{h_2} = \f
rac{2\cos\alpha}{6\cos\alpha} = \frac{1}{3}$$

The ratio is independent of the cone's half-angle α, confirming the result is fu
lly determined by the given distances.

**The ratio of the radius of the smaller space station to the radius of the larg
er space station is $\boxed{\dfrac{1}{3}}$.**
