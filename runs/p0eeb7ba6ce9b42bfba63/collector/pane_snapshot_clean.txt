## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. The isomorphism $\varphi_\gamma : \pi_1(X, x_0) \to \pi_1(X, y_0
)$ induced by a path $\gamma$ from $x_0$ to $y_0$ preserves conjugacy classes. M
oreover, the induced bijection on conjugacy classes is *independent* of the choi
ce of path $\gamma$.

---

### Setup

Let $\gamma : [0,1] \to X$ be a path with $\gamma(0) = x_0$ and $\gamma(1) = y_0
$. Let $\bar{\gamma}$ denote the reverse path. The induced isomorphism is

$$
\varphi_\gamma : \pi_1(X, x_0) \longrightarrow \pi_1(X, y_0), \qquad \varphi_\ga
mma([f]) = [\bar{\gamma} \cdot f \cdot \gamma],
$$

where $\cdot$ denotes path concatenation. This is a group isomorphism with inver
se $\varphi_{\bar\gamma}$.

### Step 1: Any group isomorphism preserves conjugacy classes

Let $\varphi: G \to H$ be any group isomorphism. If $g_1, g_2 \in G$ are conjuga
te, say $g_2 = h^{-1} g_1 h$ for some $h \in G$, then

$$
\varphi(g_2) = \varphi(h^{-1} g_1 h) = \varphi(h)^{-1}\, \varphi(g_1)\, \varphi(
h),
$$

so $\varphi(g_1)$ and $\varphi(g_2)$ are conjugate in $H$. Applying the same arg
ument to $\varphi^{-1}$ gives the converse. Thus $\varphi$ induces a bijection b
etween conjugacy classes of $G$ and conjugacy classes of $H$.

Since $\varphi_\gamma$ is a group isomorphism, it immediately follows that **$\v
arphi_\gamma$ preserves conjugacy classes**.

### Step 2: The induced bijection on conjugacy classes is independent of the pat
h

This is the deeper point that makes the statement meaningful (otherwise Step 1 a
lone would be a triviality true of any isomorphism between any two groups).

Let $\gamma_1, \gamma_2$ be two paths from $x_0$ to $y_0$. Set $\alpha = \gamma_
2 \cdot \bar{\gamma}_1$, a loop based at $x_0$, so $[\alpha] \in \pi_1(X, x_0)$.
 A direct computation shows:

$$
\varphi_{\gamma_2}([f]) = [\bar{\gamma}_2 \cdot f \cdot \gamma_2] = [\bar{\gamma
}_1 \cdot \bar{\alpha} \cdot f \cdot \alpha \cdot \gamma_1] = [\bar{\gamma}_1 \c
dot (\bar{\alpha}\cdot f \cdot \alpha) \cdot \gamma_1] = \varphi_{\gamma_1}\!\bi
g([\alpha]^{-1}\,[f]\,[\alpha]\big).
$$

Therefore

$$
\varphi_{\gamma_2} = \varphi_{\gamma_1} \circ \, c_{[\alpha]},
$$

where $c_{[\alpha]} : \pi_1(X,x_0) \to \pi_1(X,x_0)$ is the inner automorphism $
c_{[\alpha]}([f]) = [\alpha]^{-1}[f][\alpha]$.

Inner automorphisms preserve conjugacy classes: $[f]$ and $c_{[\alpha]}([f])$ ar
e conjugate (via $[\alpha]$) by definition. Since both $\varphi_{\gamma_1}$ (a g
roup isomorphism, Step 1) and $c_{[\alpha]}$ (an inner automorphism) preserve co
njugacy classes, their composition $\varphi_{\gamma_2}$ induces the **same** bij
ection on conjugacy classes as $\varphi_{\gamma_1}$.

### Conclusion

The isomorphism $\varphi_\gamma$ induced by any path $\gamma$ from $x_0$ to $y_0
$ preserves conjugacy classes (Step 1). Furthermore, the resulting bijection bet
ween the set of conjugacy classes of $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ and the set of conjugacy cl
asses of $\pi_1(X, y_0)$ is **canonical**—it does not depend on the choice of pa
th $\gamma$ (Step 2), because changing the path only composes with an inner auto
morphism, which acts trivially on conjugacy classes.

Hence conjugacy classes of the fundamental group are an invariant of the connect
ed space $X$ itself, independent of basepoint, and the path-induced isomorphism
preserves them. $\blacksquare$
