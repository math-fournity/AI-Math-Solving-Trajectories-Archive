Looking at this problem, I need to find $S_a + S_b + S_c - S$ for three circles
centered at the midpoints of an equilateral triangle's sides, each with diameter
 equal to the side length.

## Setup

Place the equilateral triangle with side 1:
- $B = (0,0)$, $C = (1,0)$, $A = (1/2, \sqrt{3}/2)$

The three circles (radius $r = 1/2$, area $\pi/4$ each):
- $O_a$: center $M_a = (1/2, 0)$ (midpoint of $BC$)
- $O_b$: center $M_b = (3/4, \sqrt{3}/4)$ (midpoint of $CA$)
- $O_c$: center $M_c = (1/4, \sqrt{3}/4)$ (midpoint of $AB$)

These are the three circles having the sides of the triangle as diameters.

## Key Symmetry

By the equilateral symmetry, all pairwise intersections are equal: let $p = |O_i
 \cap O_j|$ for any pair, and $t = S = |O_a \cap O_b \cap O_c|$.

The exclusive regions are:
$$S_a = S_b = S_c = \frac{\pi}{4} - 2p + t$$

Therefore:
$$S_a + S_b + S_c - S = 3\!\left(\frac{\pi}{4} - 2p + t\right) - t = \frac{3\pi}
{4} - 6p + 2t$$

## Computing the Pairwise Intersection $p$

The distance between any two centers (e.g., $M_a$ and $M_b$) is:
$$d = \sqrt{(1/4)^2 + (\sqrt{3}/4)^2} = \sqrt{1/16 + 3/16} = \frac{1}{2}$$

Two equal circles of radius $r=1/2$ with center distance $d=1/2$:
$$p = 2r^2 \cos^{-1}\!\!\left(\frac{d}{2r}\right) - \frac{d}{2}\sqrt{4r^2 - d^2}
 = 2\cdot\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{\pi}{3} - \frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \f
rac{\pi}{6} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{8}$$

## Computing the Triple Intersection $t = S$

**Finding pairwise intersection points.** The radical axis of $O_a$ and $O_b$ is
 $x + \sqrt{3}\,y = 1$. Substituting into $O_a$ yields intersection points $C =
(1,0)$ and $(1/4, \sqrt{3}/4) = M_c$.

By symmetry, each pair of circles intersects at one vertex of $\triangle ABC$ an
d at the midpoint of the opposite side (the third circle's center):
- $O_a \cap O_b$: $C$ and $M_c$
- $O_a \cap O_c$: $B$ and $M_b$
- $O_b \cap O_c$: $A$ and $M_a$

**Identifying the triple overlap.** The vertices $A, B, C$ lie outside the third
 circle (e.g., $C$ is at distance $\sqrt{3}/2 > 1/2$ from $M_c$). The midpoints
$M_a, M_b, M_c$ each lie on two circles and inside the third (being the third ci
rcle's center). So the triple overlap is the curvilinear triangle with vertices
$M_a, M_b, M_c$.

**This is a Reuleaux triangle.** The centers $M_a, M_b, M_c$ form an equilateral
 triangle of side $1/2$. Each boundary arc is centered at one vertex with radius
 $1/2$, passing through the other two vertices (e.g., the arc from $M_b$ to $M_c
$ lies on $O_a$, centered at $M_a$). The minor arcs (which bulge outward from th
e medial triangle) stay inside all three disks — verified by checking that the a
rc midpoint $(1/2, 1/2)$ lies within $O_b$ and $O_c$.

The area of a Reuleaux triangle of width $w$ is $\frac{1}{2}(\pi - \sqrt{3})\,w^
2$. With $w = 1/2$:
$$t = S = \frac{(\pi - \sqrt{3})}{8}$$

## Final Computation

$$S_a + S_b + S_c - S = \frac{3\pi}{4} - 6\!\left(\frac{\pi}{6} - \frac{\sqrt{3}
}{8}\right) + 2\cdot\frac{\pi - \sqrt{3}}{8}$$

$$= \frac{3\pi}{4} - \pi + \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4} + \frac{\pi - \sqrt{3}}{4}$$

$$= \frac{3\pi}{4} - \pi + \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}
{4}$$

$$= \left(\frac{3\pi + \pi - 4\pi}{4}\right) + \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{4}$$

$$= 0 + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$$

$$\boxed{S_a + S_b + S_c - S = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}$$
