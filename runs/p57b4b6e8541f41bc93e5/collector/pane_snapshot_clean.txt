## Proof

**Step 1 (Growth bound on $f$).** For any $w\in\mathbb{C}$, choose $z\in\mathbb{
C}$ with $z^{2}=w$. Then $|z|=|w|^{1/2}$, and the hypothesis gives
$$
|f(w)|=|f(z^{2})|\le e^{|z|}=e^{\sqrt{|w|}},\qquad \forall\,w\in\mathbb{C}. \tag
{1}
$$
In particular $f$ is an entire function of order $\rho\le \tfrac12$.

**Step 2 (Remove the zero at the origin).** Since $0\in\mathbb{Z}$, we have $f(0
)=0$. Assuming $f\not\equiv 0$, write
$$
f(w)=w^{m}\,\varphi(w),\qquad m\ge 1,
$$
with $\varphi$ entire and $\varphi(0)\ne 0$. For $|w|\ge 1$, (1) yields
$$
|\varphi(w)|=\frac{|f(w)|}{|w|^{m}}\le e^{\sqrt{|w|}},
$$
and on the compact set $|w|\le 1$ the continuous function $|\varphi|$ is bounded
, so there is a constant $C$ with
$$
|\varphi(w)|\le C\,e^{\sqrt{|w|}},\qquad \forall\,w\in\mathbb{C}. \tag{2}
$$
Because $f(k)=0$ for every $k\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $k^{m}\ne 0$ for $k\ne 0$, the f
unction $\varphi$ vanishes at every nonzero integer:
$$
\varphi(k)=0,\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}. \tag{3}
$$

**Step 3 (Jensen's formula).** Apply Jensen's formula to $\varphi$ on the circle
 $|w|=r$ (with $r$ chosen so that no nonzero integer lies on the circle):
$$
\log|\varphi(0)|+\sum_{|a|<r}\log\frac{r}{|a|}
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|\varphi(re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta, \tag{4}
$$
where the sum ranges over the zeros $a$ of $\varphi$ (with multiplicity) inside
$|w|<r$.

By (2) the right–hand side of (4) satisfies
$$
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|\varphi(re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta
\le \sqrt{r}+\log C. \tag{5}
$$

**Step 4 (Lower bound from the integer zeros).** Set $r=n+\tfrac12$ with $n\in\m
athbb{Z}_{>0}$. Then every nonzero integer with $|k|\le n$ lies strictly inside
$|w|<r$, so by (3) the sum in (4) contains at least the terms $k=\pm1,\dots,\pm
n$:
$$
\sum_{|a|<r}\log\frac{r}{|a|}
\ge 2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log\frac{r}{k}
=2\Bigl(n\log r-\log(n!)\Bigr). \tag{6}
$$
Using Stirling's estimate $\log(n!)\le n\log n-n+\tfrac12\log n+D$ (for a univer
sal constant $D$), and $r=n+\tfrac12$ so that $\log(r/n)=\log(1+\tfrac{1}{2n})\g
e 0$, we obtain
$$
n\log r-\log(n!)\ge n\log r-n\log n+n-\tfrac12\log n-D
= n\log\!\Bigl(1+\tfrac{1}{2n}\Bigr)+n-\tfrac12\log n-D
\ge n-\tfrac12\log n-D. \tag{7}
$$
Combining (4), (5), (6), (7):
$$
\log|\varphi(0)|+2n-\log n-2D\;\le\;\sqrt{n+\tfrac12}+\log C. \tag{8}
$$

**Step 5 (Contradiction).** The left–hand side of (8) grows like $2n$ while the
right–hand side grows like $\sqrt{n}$. Letting $n\to\infty$ gives a contradictio
n.

Therefore the assumption $f\not\equiv 0$ is impossible. Hence
$$
\boxed{\,f(z)\equiv 0\,}.
$$
