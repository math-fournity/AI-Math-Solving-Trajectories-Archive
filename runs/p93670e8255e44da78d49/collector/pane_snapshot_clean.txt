# Finite Subgroups of $SO(3)$

**Theorem.** Every finite subgroup of $SO(3)$ is isomorphic to exactly one of th
e following:

1. **Cyclic** $C_n$ of order $n$ ($n \geq 1$);
2. **Dihedral** $D_n$ of order $2n$ ($n \geq 2$);
3. **Tetrahedral group** $\cong A_4$ of order $12$;
4. **Octahedral group** $\cong S_4$ of order $24$;
5. **Icosahedral group** $\cong A_5$ of order $60$.

Each is realized, and no other finite subgroup occurs.

---

## Proof

### 1. Setup and the counting identity

Let $G \leq SO(3)$ be finite, $|G| = N$. $G$ acts on the unit sphere $S^2$. Ever
y non-identity element of $SO(3)$ is a rotation about some axis, hence fixes **e
xactly two** points of $S^2$ (the two poles).

Let
$$P = \{p \in S^2 : \text{some } g \in G\setminus\{e\} \text{ fixes } p\}.$$
$G$ acts on $P$. For $p \in P$, the stabilizer $G_p \leq SO(2)$ (rotations about
 the axis through $p$) is a finite subgroup of $SO(2) \cong S^1$, hence **cyclic
**; write $n_p = |G_p| \geq 2$.

Count pairs $(g, p)$ with $g \neq e$, $g \cdot p = p$:
- Fixing $g \neq e$: exactly $2$ points $\Rightarrow$ total $= 2(N-1)$.
- Fixing $p \in P$: exactly $n_p - 1$ non-identity elements $\Rightarrow$ total
$= \sum_{p \in P}(n_p - 1)$.

Decompose $P$ into $G$-orbits $O_1, \dots, O_k$ with stabilizer orders $n_1, \do
ts, n_k$ (constant on each orbit, $|O_i| = N/n_i$):
$$2(N-1) = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \frac{N}{n_i}(n_i - 1) = N \sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(1 - \fr
ac{1}{n_i}\right).$$
Dividing by $N$:
$$\boxed{\;\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(1 - \frac{1}{n_i}\right) = 2 - \frac{2}{N}\;}\qqu
ad (\star)$$
with each $n_i \geq 2$.

### 2. Bounding the number of orbits

Each term $1 - 1/n_i \geq 1/2$, and the right side is $< 2$. Hence $k \leq 3$.

- If $G = \{e\}$: $P = \emptyset$, $k = 0$, $N = 1$ — this is $C_1$.
- Henceforth $N \geq 2$, so $P \neq \emptyset$ and $k \geq 1$. In fact $k \geq 2
$: a non-identity rotation has two antipodal fixed points, giving at least two o
rbits (or one orbit of size $\geq 2$); a direct check shows $k = 1$ forces $|O_1
| = N/n_1$ with $1 - 1/n_1 = 2 - 2/N$, i.e. $1/n_1 = 2/N - 1 < 0$ for $N \geq 2$
, impossible. So $k \in \{2, 3\}$.

### 3. Case $k = 2$: cyclic groups

$(\star)$ gives $(1 - 1/n_1) + (1 - 1/n_2) = 2 - 2/N$, i.e.
$$\frac{1}{n_1} + \frac{1}{n_2} = \frac{2}{N}.$$
Since $n_i \leq N$, we have $1/n_1 + 1/n_2 \geq 2/N$, with equality iff $n_1 = n
_2 = N$. Thus $n_1 = n_2 = N$, both orbits have size $1$: there is a single axis
 with two fixed points, and every element fixes that axis. So $G$ embeds in $SO(
2)$ and is cyclic of order $N$.

**Realization:** rotation by $2\pi/n$ about the $z$-axis generates $C_n \leq SO(
3)$.

### 4. Case $k = 3$: the three exceptional families

Order so $n_1 \leq n_2 \leq n_3$. From $(\star)$:
$$\frac{1}{n_1} + \frac{1}{n_2} + \frac{1}{n_3} = 1 + \frac{2}{N} > 1. \qquad (\
star\star)$$

**$n_1 = 2$.** If $n_1 \geq 3$ then the left side $\leq 1$, contradicting $(\sta
r\star)$. So $n_1 = 2$.

**Subcase 4a: $n_2 = 2$ (dihedral).** Then $1/2 + 1/2 + 1/n_3 = 1 + 2/N$, so $N
= 2n_3$. Signature $(2, 2, n_3)$, $n_3 \geq 2$.

**Subcase 4b: $n_2 = 3$.** Then $1/2 + 1/3 + 1/n_3 = 1 + 2/N$, i.e. $1/n_3 = 1/6
 + 2/N$. Since $n_3 \geq 3$:
- $n_3 = 3$: $1/3 = 1/6 + 2/N \Rightarrow N = 12$. Signature $(2,3,3)$ — **tetra
hedral**.
- $n_3 = 4$: $1/4 = 1/6 + 2/N \Rightarrow N = 24$. Signature $(2,3,4)$ — **octah
edral**.
- $n_3 = 5$: $1/5 = 1/6 + 2/N \Rightarrow N = 60$. Signature $(2,3,5)$ — **icosa
hedral**.
- $n_3 \geq 6$: $1/n_3 \leq 1/6 \Rightarrow 2/N \leq 0$, impossible.

**Subcase 4c: $n_2 \geq 4$.** Then $1/2 + 1/4 + 1/4 = 1$, contradicting $(\star\
star)$ (strictly $>1$). So no further cases.

This exhausts all possibilities. The five signatures are:

| signature $(n_1,n_2,n_3)$ | $|G|$ | type |
|---|---|---|
| $(2,2,n)$, $n\geq 2$ | $2n$ | dihedral $D_n$ |
| $(2,3,3)$ | $12$ | tetrahedral $\cong A_4$ |
| $(2,3,4)$ | $24$ | octahedral $\cong S_4$ |
| $(2,3,5)$ | $60$ | icosahedral $\cong A_5$ |

### 5. Realization of each signature

**Dihedral $D_n$ (signature $(2,2,n)$, order $2n$).** Place a regular $n$-gon in
 the $xy$-plane centered at the origin. Its rotation symmetry group in $SO(3)$ c
onsists of $n$ rotations about the $z$-axis (forming $C_n$) and $n$ half-turns a
bout axes in the $xy$-plane through the center (through vertices / midpoints of
edges). This is $D_n$ of order $2n$. The three orbit types on $S^2$: the two pol
es (stabilizer $C_n$, order $n$), and the $n$ points over vertices $\cup$ $n$ po
ints over edge-midpoints projected to $S^2$ — two orbits each of size $n$ with s
tabilizer of order $2$. Signature $(2,2,n)$. ✓

**Tetrahedral (signature $(2,3,3)$, order $12$).** The rotation symmetry group o
f a regular tetrahedron inscribed in $S^2$: $8$ rotations of $120°$/$240°$ about
 $4$ vertex-to-opposite-face axes (stabilizer order $3$, two orbits of $4$ point
s each), and $3$ half-turns about axes through midpoints of opposite edges (stab
ilizer order $2$, one orbit of $6$ points). Total $|G| = 12$. The group is $A_4$
 (even permutations of the $4$ vertices). Signature $(2,3,3)$. ✓

**Octahedral (signature $(2,3,4)$, order $24$).** The rotation symmetry group of
 the cube (equivalently the regular octahedron): $6$ quarter/half/three-quarter
turns about $3$ face-center axes (stabilizer order $4$, one orbit of $6$ face-ce
nters), $8$ rotations about $4$ vertex-to-opposite-vertex axes (stabilizer order
 $3$, one orbit of $8$ vertices), and $6$ half-turns about $6$ edge-midpoint axe
s (stabilizer order $2$, one orbit of $12$ edge-midpoints). Total $|G| = 24$. Th
e group is $S_4$ (permutations of the $4$ long diagonals of the cube). Signature
 $(2,3,4)$. ✓

**Icosahedral (signature $(2,3,5)$, order $60$).** The rotation symmetry group o
f the regular icosahedron (equivalently dodecahedron): half-turns about $15$ axe
s through opposite edge-midpoints (stabilizer order $2$, orbit of $30$), rotatio
ns about $10$ axes through opposite vertices (stabilizer order $3$, orbit of $20

 $5$, orbit of $12$). Total $|G| = 60$. The group is $A_5$ (even permutations of
 $5$ inscribed tetrahedra / of $5$ cubes inscribed in the dodecahedron). Signatu
re $(2,3,5)$. ✓

### 6. Uniqueness of type

The counting argument fixes, for each finite $G \leq SO(3)$, the multiset $\{n_1
, \dots, n_k\}$ and $|G|$. The five signatures above are mutually distinct, so a
 finite subgroup falls into exactly one family. Within each family the order det
ermines the group:

- $C_n$ is the unique cyclic group of order $n$;
- $D_n$ is the unique dihedral group of order $2n$;
- orders $12, 24, 60$ with the corresponding signatures are realized by $A_4, S_
4, A_5$ respectively, and these are the unique groups of those orders admitting
the prescribed orbit-stabilizer structure (equivalently, the rotation groups of
the tetrahedron, cube/octahedron, and icosahedron/dodecahedron are the unique tr
ansitive rotation groups with those signatures — any group with the same signatu
re is conjugate in $SO(3)$ to the corresponding polyhedral rotation group, since
 the orbit points form the vertex set of the unique regular polyhedron of that t
ype up to scale).

Conjugacy (hence isomorphism) follows because the orbit of the highest-stabilize
r points determines, up to rotation of the sphere, the regular polyhedron: e.g.
the $12$ points with stabilizer of order $5$ are the vertices of a regular icosa
hedron; the $6$ points with stabilizer of order $4$ are the face-centers of a cu
be; the $4$ points with stabilizer of order $3$ in the $(2,3,3)$ case are the ve
rtices of a regular tetrahedron. The group is then the full rotation symmetry gr
oup of that polyhedron, unique up to conjugacy.

### 7. Conclusion

Combining the counting classification (which forces one of the five signatures,
including the $k=2$ cyclic case and the trivial group $C_1$) with the explicit r
ealizations and the uniqueness argument, the finite subgroups of $SO(3)$ are, up
 to isomorphism, precisely:

$$\boxed{C_n\ (n\geq 1),\quad D_n\ (n\geq 2),\quad A_4,\quad S_4,\quad A_5.}$$
