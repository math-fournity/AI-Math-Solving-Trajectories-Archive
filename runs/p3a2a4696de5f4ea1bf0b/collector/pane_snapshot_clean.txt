## Solution

We evaluate
$$I=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\arctan x}{x}\right)^{3}dx.$$

**Substitution.** Let $x=\tan\theta$, so $\arctan x=\theta$, $dx=\sec^{2}\theta\
,d\theta$, and as $x:0\to\infty$ we have $\theta:0\to\pi/2$. Then
$$
I=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\theta^{3}\,\frac{\sec^{2}\theta}{\tan^{3}\theta}\,d\theta
 =\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\theta^{3}\,\frac{\cos\theta}{\sin^{3}\theta}\,d\theta.
$$

**First integration by parts.** Take $u=\theta^{3}$ and $dv=\dfrac{\cos\theta}{\
sin^{3}\theta}\,d\theta$, so $v=-\dfrac{1}{2\sin^{2}\theta}$. Since $\theta^{3}/
\sin^{2}\theta\to 0$ as $\theta\to 0$ and $\sin(\pi/2)=1$,
$$
I=\left[-\frac{\theta^{3}}{2\sin^{2}\theta}\right]_{0}^{\pi/2}+\frac{3}{2}\int_{
0}^{\pi/2}\frac{\theta^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta}\,d\theta
=-\frac{\pi^{3}}{16}+\frac{3}{2}J,
$$
where $J=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{\theta^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta}\,d\theta
$.

**Second integration by parts.** For $J$, take $u=\theta^{2}$, $dv=\csc^{2}\thet
a\,d\theta$, so $v=-\cot\theta$. The boundary term vanishes because $\cot(\pi/2)
=0$ and $\theta^{2}\cot\theta\sim\theta\to 0$:
$$
J=\Big[-\theta^{2}\cot\theta\Big]_{0}^{\pi/2}+2\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\theta\cot\theta\
,d\theta=2K,
$$
where $K=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\theta\cot\theta\,d\theta$.

**Third integration by parts.** For $K$, take $u=\theta$, $dv=\cot\theta\,d\thet
a$, so $v=\ln\sin\theta$. The boundary term again vanishes ($\ln\sin(\pi/2)=0$ a
nd $\theta\ln\sin\theta\to 0$):
$$
K=\Big[\theta\ln\sin\theta\Big]_{0}^{\pi/2}-\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\ln\sin\theta\,d\the
ta.
$$

**The classical log-sine integral.** Using the well-known identity (obtained, e.
g., from $\sin\theta=2\sin(\theta/2)\cos(\theta/2)$ and a symmetry argument, or
from the Fourier series $-\ln\sin\theta=\ln 2+\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\cos 2n\theta}{n
}$):
$$
\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\ln\sin\theta\,d\theta=-\frac{\pi}{2}\ln 2.
$$
Hence
$$
K=0-\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}\ln 2\right)=\frac{\pi}{2}\ln 2,
\qquad
J=2K=\pi\ln 2.
$$

**Assembling.**
$$
I=-\frac{\pi^{3}}{16}+\frac{3}{2}\,J=-\frac{\pi^{3}}{16}+\frac{3}{2}\,\pi\ln 2.
$$

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\arctan x}{x}\right)^{3}dx=\fra
c{3\pi}{2}\ln 2-\frac{\pi^{3}}{16}.}
$$

A quick sanity check: $\frac{3\pi}{2}\ln 2\approx 3.266$ and $\frac{\pi^{3}}{16}
\approx 1.938$, giving $I\approx 1.328>0$, consistent with a positive integrand.
