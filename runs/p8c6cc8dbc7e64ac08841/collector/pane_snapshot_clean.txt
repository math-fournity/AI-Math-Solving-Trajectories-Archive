## Counterexample Construction

Let $a = 1$. Set $z_n = 1 - \frac{1}{n^2}$ for $n \geq 2$. Then $z_n \in D(0,1)$
, $z_n \to 1$, $|1|=1$, and the Blaschke condition $\sum_{n=2}^\infty (1-|z_n|)
= \sum_{n=2}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} < \infty$ is satisfied.

**Step 1: The Blaschke product.** Define
$$B(z) = \prod_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{z_n - z}{1 - z_n z}.$$
Since $z_n$ are real and positive, $\bar{z}_n = z_n$. The Blaschke condition ens
ures $B$ converges uniformly on compact subsets of $D(0,1)$, giving a bounded ho
lomorphic function with $|B(z)| \le 1$, zeros exactly at $\{z_n\}$, and $B \not\
equiv 0$.

**Key property:** $B$ extends continuously to $\overline{D(0,1)} \setminus \{1\}
$. At any boundary point $\zeta = e^{i\theta_0} \neq 1$, only finitely many $z_n
$ lie nearby (since $z_n \to 1$ is the sole boundary limit point), so the produc
t converges uniformly in a neighborhood of $\zeta$. On $|z|=1$, each factor sati
sfies $\left|\frac{z_n - z}{1 - z_n z}\right| = 1$, and the argument series conv
erges since each term is $O(1/n^2)$.

However, $B$ does **not** extend continuously to $z=1$: along the real axis $B(z
_n)=0 \to 0$, but $|B(e^{i\theta})|=1$ for $\theta \neq 0$.

**Step 2: The flat (envelope) function.** Define
$$\varphi(z) = \exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{(1-z)^{1/2}}\right),$$
using the principal branch of the square root. This is valid because for $z \in
D(0,1)$, we have $\operatorname{Re}(1-z) = 1 - \operatorname{Re}(z) > 0$, so $1-
z$ lies in the open right half-plane, where the principal branch is holomorphic.

- $\varphi$ is holomorphic on $D(0,1)$ and never vanishes (exponential).
- $\varphi$ is continuous on $\overline{D(0,1)}$ with $\varphi(1)=0$.

**Proof that $\varphi(z) \to 0$ as $z \to 1$ within $\overline{D(0,1)}$:** Let $
w = 1-z$. For $z \in \overline{D(0,1)}$, $\operatorname{Re}(w) \ge 0$ and $w \ne
q 0$ when $z \neq 1$. Write $w = re^{i\psi}$ with $|\psi| \le \pi/2$. Then
$$\operatorname{Re}\!\left(w^{-1/2}\right) = r^{-1/2}\cos(\psi/2).$$
Since $|\psi| \le \pi/2$, we have $|\psi/2| \le \pi/4$, so $\cos(\psi/2) \ge \co
s(\pi/4) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} > 0$. Therefore
$$|\varphi(z)| = e^{-\operatorname{Re}(w^{-1/2})} \le e^{-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\, r
^{-1/2}} \to 0 \quad \text{as } r \to 0.$$
This holds for **all** approaches to $z=1$ within $\overline{D(0,1)}$, including
 tangential approaches along the unit circle. $\checkmark$

The exponent $\beta = 1/2 \in (0,1)$ is crucial: for $\beta \ge 1$, tangential a
pproaches along the circle (where $\psi \to \pm\pi/2$) would give $\cos(\beta\ps
i) \le 0$, failing to force $\varphi \to 0$.

**Step 3: The counterexample.** Set
$$f(z) = \varphi(z) \cdot B(z).$$

**Verification of all hypotheses:**

1. **$f$ is holomorphic on $D(0,1)$:** Product of holomorphic functions. $\check
mark$

2. **$f$ is continuous on $\overline{D(0,1)}$:**
   - On $\overline{D(0,1)} \setminus \{1\}$: both $\varphi$ and $B$ are continuo
us. $\checkmark$
   - At $z = 1$: $|f(z)| = |\varphi(z)| \cdot |B(z)| \le |\varphi(z)| \cdot 1 \t
o 0 = f(1)$. $\checkmark$

3. **$f(z_n) = 0$ for all $n$:** $f(z_n) = \varphi(z_n) \cdot B(z_n) = \varphi(z
_n) \cdot 0 = 0$. $\checkmark$

4. **$z_n \to a$ with $|a|=1$:** $z_n \to 1$, $|1| = 1$. $\checkmark$

5. **$f \not\equiv 0$:** $\varphi$ is never zero on $D(0,1)$ (it's an exponentia
l), and $B \not\equiv 0$ (it's a nontrivial Blaschke product with $|B| \to 1$ a.
e. on $\partial D$). Therefore $f \not\equiv 0$. $\checkmark$

## Conclusion

The function $f(z) = \exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-z}}\right) \cdot \prod_{n=2}^
{\infty} \frac{(1-1/n^2) - z}{1 - (1-1/n^2)z}$ is holomorphic on $D(0,1)$, conti
nuous on $\overline{D(0,1)}$, vanishes on the sequence $z_n = 1-1/n^2 \to 1 \in
\partial D(0,1)$, yet is not identically zero.

The key mechanism: the flat function $\varphi$ vanishes at $z=1$ rapidly enough
to "absorb" the discontinuity of the Blaschke product $B$ at the accumulation po
int, while $B$ provides the interior zeros. The identity theorem does not apply
because the accumulation point is on the boundary, not in the interior.

**The proposition is FALSE.**
