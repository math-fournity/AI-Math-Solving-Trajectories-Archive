## Fundamental Solution of the Heat Operator via Fourier Transform

**Problem.** Find a fundamental solution $E(x,t)$ for the operator $L = -\Delta_
n + D_t$, i.e. a distribution satisfying
$$
LE = (-\Delta_n + D_t)\,E = \delta(x)\,\delta(t), \qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^n,\; t\i
n\mathbb{R}.
$$

Here $\Delta_n = \sum_{j=1}^n D_j^2$ with $D_j = \partial/\partial x_j$, and $D_
t = \partial/\partial t$.

---

### Step 1. Fourier transform in the spatial variables

Define the Fourier transform in $x$ and its inverse by
$$
\widehat{f}(\xi,t) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} f(x,t)\,e^{-i\,x\cdot\xi}\,dx,
\qquad
f(x,t) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \widehat{f}(\xi,t)\,e^{i\,x\cdot\
xi}\,d\xi.
$$
Under this convention $D_j \mapsto i\xi_j$, hence $\Delta_n = \sum D_j^2 \mapsto
 -|\xi|^2$, so
$$
-\Delta_n \;\mapsto\; |\xi|^2.
$$
Transforming the equation $(-\Delta_n + D_t)E = \delta(x)\delta(t)$ in $x$, and
using $\widehat{\delta}(\xi)=1$, gives an **ordinary differential equation in $t
$** parameterized by $\xi$:
$$
\bigl(|\xi|^2 + \partial_t\bigr)\,\widehat{E}(\xi,t) = \delta(t). \tag{1}
$$

---

### Step 2. Solve the ODE (1) — the causal fundamental solution

For $t\neq 0$ the equation is homogeneous: $\partial_t \widehat{E} = -|\xi|^2\,\
widehat{E}$, with general solution
$$
\widehat{E}(\xi,t) = C(\xi)\,e^{-|\xi|^2 t}.
$$
We seek the **causal** (retarded) fundamental solution, supported in $t\ge 0$, s
o we set
$$
\widehat{E}(\xi,t) = e^{-|\xi|^2 t}\,H(t), \tag{2}
$$
where $H$ is the Heaviside function. **Verification.** Using $\partial_t H = \de
lta$ and $e^{-|\xi|^2\cdot 0}=1$,
$$
\partial_t\!\left(e^{-|\xi|^2 t}H(t)\right) = -|\xi|^2 e^{-|\xi|^2 t}H(t) + e^{-
|\xi|^2 t}\delta(t) = -|\xi|^2\,\widehat{E} + \delta(t).
$$
Therefore $(|\xi|^2 + \partial_t)\widehat{E} = \delta(t)$, confirming (2) solves
 (1). $\checkmark$

---

### Step 3. Invert the Fourier transform

From (2) and the inversion formula,
$$
E(x,t) = \frac{H(t)}{(2\pi)^n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \exp\!\Bigl(i\,x\cdot\xi - |\x
i|^2 t\Bigr)\,d\xi. \tag{3}
$$
For $t>0$ the integral is a Gaussian. Completing the square componentwise,
$$
-t\,\xi_j^2 + i\,x_j\,\xi_j = -t\!\left(\xi_j - \frac{i\,x_j}{2t}\right)^{\!2} -
 \frac{x_j^2}{4t},
$$
so, summing over $j$,
$$
i\,x\cdot\xi - |\xi|^2 t = -t\sum_{j=1}^n\!\left(\xi_j - \frac{i\,x_j}{2t}\right
)^{\!2} - \frac{|x|^2}{4t}.
$$
The shifted Gaussian integrates to the same value as the centered one:
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}} e^{-t(\xi_j - i x_j/(2t))^2}\,d\xi_j = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{t}},
$$
hence, by Fubini,
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} e^{i\,x\cdot\xi - |\xi|^2 t}\,d\xi = \left(\frac{\pi}{t}\rig
ht)^{\!n/2} e^{-|x|^2/(4t)}, \qquad t>0.
$$
Substituting into (3):
$$
E(x,t) = H(t)\,\frac{1}{(2\pi)^n}\left(\frac{\pi}{t}\right)^{\!n/2} e^{-|x|^2/(4
t)} = H(t)\,\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{n/2}}\,e^{-|x|^2/(4t)}.
$$

---

### Step 4. Verification that $E$ is a fundamental solution

For $t>0$, $E$ is smooth and
$$
(\partial_t - \Delta_n)E = 0
$$
by direct differentiation (it is the classical heat kernel). The entire contribu
tion to $LE$ is therefore concentrated at $t=0$. Integrating $LE$ against a test
 function $\varphi(x,t)$ over a small slab $|t|<\varepsilon$ and using that $E(\
cdot,t)\to\delta(x)$ as $t\downarrow 0$ (since $\int E\,dx=1$ and $E$ is an appr
oximate identity):
$$
\langle LE,\varphi\rangle = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\!\Bigl[E(x,t)\varphi(x,t)\Bigr]_
{t=0^-}^{t=0^+} dx = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\varphi(x,0)\,dx = \langle\delta(x)\delt
a(t),\varphi\rangle.
$$
Thus $(-\Delta_n + D_t)E = \delta(x)\delta(t)$ in the sense of distributions. $\
checkmark$

---

### Result

The **fundamental solution** (heat kernel) of $-\Delta_n + D_t$ is
$$
\boxed{\,E(x,t) \;=\; \frac{H(t)}{(4\pi t)^{n/2}}\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{|x|^2}{4t}
\right)\,}
$$
where $H(t)$ is the Heaviside step function (so $E$ is supported in the half-spa
ce $t\ge 0$). It satisfies
$$
(-\Delta_n + D_t)\,E(x,t) = \delta(x)\,\delta(t).
$$
