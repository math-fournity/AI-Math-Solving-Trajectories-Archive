## Solution

**Goal.** Find the radius of convergence of $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n^{n!}}{(
n^n)!}\,x^n$ using the root test and the hint $3^n\,n!>n^n$.

---

### Step 1 — Upper bound the numerator via the hint

Since $n^n < 3^n\,n!$ and $(n-1)!>0$, raise both sides to the power $(n-1)!$:

$$n^{n!}=n^{\,n\cdot(n-1)!}=(n^n)^{(n-1)!}<\bigl(3^n\,n!\bigr)^{(n-1)!}=3^{\,n!}
\,(n!)^{(n-1)!}.$$

### Step 2 — Lower bound the denominator

For $n\ge 2$ we have $n!\le n^n$, so we may split the factorial:

$$(n^n)!=\prod_{k=1}^{n^n}k\;\ge\;\prod_{k=n!+1}^{n^n}k\;\ge\;(n!)^{\,n^n-n!},$$

since the second product has $n^n-n!$ factors, each $\ge n!+1>n!$.

### Step 3 — Combine

$$a_n=\frac{n^{n!}}{(n^n)!}<\frac{3^{n!}\,(n!)^{(n-1)!}}{(n!)^{n^n-n!}}=3^{n!}\,
(n!)^{(n-1)!+n!-n^n}.$$

Using $(n-1)!+n!=(n+1)(n-1)!$:

$$a_n<3^{n!}\,(n!)^{(n+1)(n-1)!-n^n}.$$

### Step 4 — Show $a_n^{1/n}\to 0$

Take logarithms of the $n$-th root:

$$\ln\!\bigl(a_n^{1/n}\bigr)<(n-1)!\ln 3+\frac{(n+1)(n-1)!-n^n}{n}\,\ln(n!).$$

**Claim.** $\dfrac{(n+1)(n-1)!}{n^n}=\dfrac{(n+1)!}{n^{n+1}}\to 0$.

*Proof of claim.* Write $\dfrac{(n+1)!}{n^{n+1}}=\prod_{k=1}^{n+1}\frac{k}{n}$.
For $k\le n/2$ we have $k/n\le 1/2$; there are $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ such factors
, so

$$\frac{(n+1)!}{n^{n+1}}\le\Bigl(\frac12\Bigr)^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\cdot 2\le 2^
{2-n/2}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.\qquad\checkmark$$

Hence for all sufficiently large $n$, say $n\ge N$, we have $(n+1)(n-1)!\le n^n/
2$, giving

$$\ln\!\bigl(a_n^{1/n}\bigr)<(n-1)!\ln 3-\frac{n^{n-1}}{2}\,\ln(n!).$$

Use the elementary bound $\ln(n!)\ge \tfrac{n}{2}\ln\tfrac{n}{2}$ (valid for $n\
ge 4$, since at least half the factors in $n!$ are $\ge n/2$):

$$\ln\!\bigl(a_n^{1/n}\bigr)<(n-1)!\ln 3-\frac{n^n}{4}\ln\frac{n}{2}.$$

Now compare the two terms. Since

$$\frac{n^n}{(n-1)!}=n\cdot\frac{n^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}=n\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{n}{k}\
ge n\cdot n\cdot\frac{n}{2}=\frac{n^3}{2}\quad(n\ge 2),$$

we get

$$\frac{n^n\ln(n/2)}{4\,(n-1)!\ln 3}\ge\frac{n^3\ln(n/2)}{8\ln 3}\xrightarrow[n\
to\infty]{}\infty.$$

Therefore the negative term dominates and

$$\ln\!\bigl(a_n^{1/n}\bigr)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}-\infty,\qquad\text{so}\qq
uad a_n^{1/n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.$$

### Conclusion

By the Cauchy–Hadamard theorem,

$$R=\frac{1}{\limsup_{n\to\infty}a_n^{1/n}}=\frac{1}{0}=+\infty.$$

The power series converges for **every** $x\in\mathbb{R}$ (indeed for every $x\i
n\mathbb{C}$).

$$\boxed{R=\infty}$$
