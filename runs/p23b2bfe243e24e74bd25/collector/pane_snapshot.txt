## Proof

**Claim.** A compact Hausdorff space $X$ in which every point has an open neighb
orhood embeddable in $\mathbb{R}^k$ can be homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^k$ **only
** in the trivial case $k=0$ (where both spaces are a single point). For every $
k \geq 1$, no such homeomorphism can exist.

---

### Key fact: compactness is a topological invariant

Compactness is preserved under homeomorphism. If $X$ is compact and $h : X \to \
mathbb{R}^k$ is a homeomorphism, then $\mathbb{R}^k = h(X)$ must also be compact
.

### When is $\mathbb{R}^k$ compact?

- **$k \geq 1$:** $\mathbb{R}^k$ is **not compact**. For instance, the open cove
r $\{B(0,n)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ (open balls of radius $n$) has no finite subcover.
 Equivalently, $\mathbb{R}^k$ is not bounded, so by Heine–Borel it is not compac
t.
- **$k = 0$:** $\mathbb{R}^0 = \{0\}$ is a single point, which is compact.

### Case $k \geq 1$: impossible

Suppose $X$ is a compact Hausdorff space (with the stated local-embeddability pr
operty, which is irrelevant here) and $h : X \to \mathbb{R}^k$ is a homeomorphis
m. Then $\mathbb{R}^k = h(X)$ is the continuous image of a compact space, hence
compact. But $\mathbb{R}^k$ is not compact for $k \geq 1$. **Contradiction.** Th
erefore no such homeomorphism exists.

### Case $k = 0$: possible, and only trivially

$\mathbb{R}^0 = \{\ast\}$ is a single point. Take $X = \{\ast\}$. Then:
- $X$ is compact (finite) and Hausdorff (trivially).
- The unique point $\ast$ has open neighborhood $X = \{\ast\}$ itself, which emb
eds in $\mathbb{R}^0 = \{\ast\}$ via the identity.
- $X \cong \mathbb{R}^0$.

Conversely, any $X$ homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^0$ is a single point, so this is
 the only example.

---

### Conclusion

A compact Hausdorff space with the stated local property **cannot** be homeomorp
hic to $\mathbb{R}^k$ for any $k \geq 1$, because compactness — a topological in
variant — is violated: $\mathbb{R}^k$ is non-compact for $k \geq 1$. The only va
lue of $k$ for which it is possible is $k = 0$, yielding the trivial one-point s
pace.
