## Proof

Let $S_n = \xi_1 + \cdots + \xi_n$ with $S_0 = 0$, and $X_t = \sup\{n : S_n \le
t\}$ the renewal counting process associated with the i.i.d. inter-arrival times
 $\xi_i \sim \mathrm{Exp}(\lambda)$.

**Step 1. Strong Law for the arrival times.**

Since $\{\xi_i\}$ are i.i.d. with $\mathbb{E}[\xi_i] = 1/\lambda < \infty$, the
Strong Law of Large Numbers gives

$$\frac{S_n}{n} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \mathbb{E}[\xi_1] = \frac{1}{\lambda}
\quad \text{as } n \to \infty.$$

**Step 2. Relating $X_t$ and $S_{X_t}$.**

By definition of $X_t$, we have the fundamental sandwiching inequality

$$S_{X_t} \le t < S_{X_t + 1}.$$

Dividing through by $X_t$ (for $X_t \ge 1$, which holds for all large $t$ a.s. s
ince $X_t \to \infty$):

$$\frac{S_{X_t}}{X_t} \le \frac{t}{X_t} < \frac{S_{X_t+1}}{X_t}.$$

**Step 3. $X_t \to \infty$ almost surely.**

Since $S_n/n \to 1/\lambda > 0$, for any $M$ we have $S_n \to \infty$, hence for
 every $t$ there exists $n$ with $S_n \le t$ only up to some finite index; conve
rsely $S_n \to \infty$ ensures $X_t \to \infty$ as $t \to \infty$. More precisel
y, $X_t \ge n$ iff $S_n \le t$, and since $S_n \sim \Gamma(n,\lambda)$ with $S_n
/n \to 1/\lambda$, for every fixed $n$, eventually $t \ge S_n$, so $X_t \to \inf
ty$ a.s.

**Step 4. Squeeze.**

Since $X_t \to \infty$ a.s., and $X_t + 1 \to \infty$ a.s., we apply the SLLN al
ong the random indices $X_t$ and $X_t+1$:

$$\frac{S_{X_t}}{X_t} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \frac{1}{\lambda}, \qquad \frac{
S_{X_t+1}}{X_t} = \frac{S_{X_t+1}}{X_t+1}\cdot\frac{X_t+1}{X_t} \xrightarrow{\te
xt{a.s.}} \frac{1}{\lambda}\cdot 1 = \frac{1}{\lambda}.$$

(The convergence $\frac{S_{X_t}}{X_t}\to 1/\lambda$ along the random subsequence
 $X_t$ follows because $X_t$ is a stopping-time index that tends to infinity, an
d the SLLN convergence $S_n/n \to 1/\lambda$ holds for *every* sequence of indic
es tending to infinity, random or deterministic, on the probability-one set wher
e the SLLN limit holds.)

By the squeeze theorem applied to

$$\frac{S_{X_t}}{X_t} \le \frac{t}{X_t} < \frac{S_{X_t+1}}{X_t},$$

we obtain

$$\frac{t}{X_t} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \frac{1}{\lambda}.$$

**Step 5. Invert.**

Since $t/X_t \to 1/\lambda > 0$ a.s., we may invert:

$$\frac{X_t}{t} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \lambda.$$

**Conclusion.** The almost sure limit is

$$\boxed{\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{X_t}{t} = \lambda \quad \text{a.s.}}$$

This is consistent with the interpretation that $X_t$ is a Poisson process with
rate $\lambda$ (the renewal process with exponential inter-arrival times of mean
 $1/\lambda$), whose long-run rate equals the reciprocal of the mean inter-arriv
al time.
