## Solution

We compute the Lebesgue integral $\int_{[0,1]} f\,d\mu$ where $f(x) = 9^n$ for i
rrational $x$ and $f(x)=0$ for rational $x$, with $n$ the number of zeros immedi
ately following the decimal point of $x$.

**Step 1: Reduce to the irrational part.** Since $\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]$ is counta
ble, it has Lebesgue measure $0$. Hence the rationals contribute nothing to the
integral, and
$$\int_{[0,1]} f\,d\mu = \int_{[0,1]} 9^{n(x)}\,d\mu(x).$$

**Step 2: Decompose $[0,1]$ by the value of $n$.** For $k = 0, 1, 2, \dots$, let
$$A_k = \{x \in [0,1] : n(x) = k\}.$$
These are the numbers whose decimal expansion begins with exactly $k$ zeros afte
r the decimal point, followed by a nonzero digit. Up to a countable set of ratio
nals (numbers with two decimal representations, e.g. $0.1 = 0.0\overline{9}$),
$$A_k \approx \big(10^{-(k+1)},\; 10^{-k}\big),$$
which has Lebesgue measure
$$\mu(A_k) = 10^{-k} - 10^{-(k+1)} = 9\cdot 10^{-(k+1)}.$$
The remaining case $n = \infty$ corresponds only to $x = 0$ (the expansion $0.00
0\dots$), which is rational and has measure $0$.

**Step 3: Compute the integral.** Since $f \geq 0$ is measurable and the $A_k$ a
re disjoint (up to null sets),
$$\int_{[0,1]} f\,d\mu = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} 9^{k}\cdot \mu(A_k) = \sum_{k=0}^{\
infty} 9^{k}\cdot 9\cdot 10^{-(k+1)} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{9}{10}\rig
ht)^{k+1}.$$

This is a geometric series with ratio $r = 9/10 < 1$:
$$\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{9}{10}\right)^{k+1} = \frac{9/10}{1 - 9/10} = \
frac{9/10}{1/10} = 9.$$

**Conclusion.** The Lebesgue integral of $f$ over $[0,1]$ is

$$\boxed{9}.$$
