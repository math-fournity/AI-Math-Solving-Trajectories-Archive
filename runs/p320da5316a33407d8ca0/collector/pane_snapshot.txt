## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. For any $f,g\in L^2_{\mathbb P}(\Omega)$ with $\|f\|=\|g\|$, the
re exists a unitary operator $U$ on $L^2_{\mathbb P}(\Omega)$ with $Uf=g$ $\math
bb P$-a.e.

**Proof.** We use only the Hilbert-space structure of $H:=L^2_{\mathbb P}(\Omega
)$, which is separable by hypothesis. The argument is the standard "equal-norm v
ectors are unitarily equivalent" construction.

**Case 1: $\|f\|=\|g\|=0$.** Then $f=g=0$ a.e. Any unitary operator works; in pa
rticular the identity $U=I$ satisfies $Uf=0=g$ a.e. So assume from now on that $
r:=\|f\|=\|g\|>0$.

**Case 2: $r>0$.** Set $e_f:=f/r$ and $e_g:=g/r$, so $\|e_f\|=\|e_g\|=1$.

Since $H$ is separable, every orthonormal set extends to a countable orthonormal
 basis (Gram–Schmidt on a countable dense subset, applied after the prescribed u
nit vector). Concretely:

- Extend $\{e_f\}$ to a countable orthonormal basis $\mathcal B_f=\{e_f\}\cup\{u
_n\}_{n\ge 1}$ of $H$.
- Extend $\{e_g\}$ to a countable orthonormal basis $\mathcal B_g=\{e_g\}\cup\{v
_n\}_{n\ge 1}$ of $H$.

(If $\dim H=1$, the index sets $\{u_n\},\{v_n\}$ are empty and the construction
below reduces to the obvious one-dimensional map.)

Define a linear operator $U:H\to H$ by specifying it on the basis $\mathcal B_f$
:
$$
U e_f := e_g,\qquad U u_n := v_n\quad(n\ge 1),
$$
and extending linearly and continuously. Explicitly, for $x=\alpha e_f+\sum_{n\g
e1}\alpha_n u_n$ (with $\sum|\alpha_n|^2<\infty$),
$$
Ux := \alpha\, e_g + \sum_{n\ge1}\alpha_n\, v_n.
$$

**$U$ is well defined and isometric.** Since $\mathcal B_f$ is an orthonormal ba
sis, Parseval gives $\|x\|^2=|\alpha|^2+\sum_{n\ge1}|\alpha_n|^2$. Because $\mat
hcal B_g$ is orthonormal,
$$
\|Ux\|^2 = |\alpha|^2+\sum_{n\ge1}|\alpha_n|^2 = \|x\|^2.
$$
Thus $U$ preserves norms, hence is an isometry. As $U$ maps an orthonormal basis
 onto an orthonormal basis, its range is all of $H$, so $U$ is a surjective isom
etry, i.e. a unitary operator.

**$U$ sends $f$ to $g$ a.e.** We have $f=r\,e_f$, so
$$
Uf = U(r\,e_f)=r\,Ue_f = r\,e_g = r\cdot\frac{g}{r}=g.
$$
Equality in $L^2$ is equality $\mathbb P$-a.e., so $Uf=g$ $\mathbb P$-a.e.

**Remark on the role of separability.** Separability guarantees that the bases o
btained by extending $\{e_f\}$ and $\{e_g\}$ are countable and can be put in bij
ection, which makes the "map the $n$-th basis vector to the $n$-th basis vector"
 definition above legitimate without any choice-theoretic subtlety. (The same co
nclusion in fact holds for non-separable Hilbert spaces too, via a transfinite e
xtension of the same argument, but separability is the hypothesis given and is s
ufficient.)

Therefore, for every pair $f,g\in L^2_{\mathbb P}(\Omega)$ with $\|f\|=\|g\|$, t
here exists a unitary $U$ on $L^2_{\mathbb P}(\Omega)$ such that $Uf=g$ almost e
verywhere with respect to $\mathbb P$. $\blacksquare$
