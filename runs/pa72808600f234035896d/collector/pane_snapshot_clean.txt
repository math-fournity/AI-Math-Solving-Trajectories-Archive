# Proof

**Claim:** Yes. Every elliptic curve over $\mathbb{C}$ is a Kähler manifold (as
a compact, connected, complex manifold).

**Setup.** An elliptic curve over $\mathbb{C}$ is a smooth projective curve of g
enus 1 with a distinguished base point. As a complex manifold it is a compact Ri
emann surface of genus 1, analytically isomorphic to a quotient $E = \mathbb{C}/
\Lambda$ where $\Lambda \subset \mathbb{C}$ is a rank-2 lattice. It is compact,
connected, and of complex dimension 1 (real dimension 2).

We give three independent arguments; any one suffices.

---

**Argument 1 (Dimension counting — the cleanest).** A Kähler manifold is a compl
ex manifold equipped with a Hermitian metric $h$ whose associated $(1,1)$-form $
\omega$ is closed: $d\omega = 0$.

Every complex manifold admits a Hermitian metric (construct one via a partition
of unity subordinate to coordinate charts, patching the local flat metrics $h =
\sum dz_i \otimes d\bar z_i$). So $E$ carries a Hermitian metric with associated
 real $(1,1)$-form $\omega$.

Now $\omega$ is a 2-form, so $d\omega$ is a 3-form. But $E$ has **real dimension
 2**, and on a 2-dimensional real manifold every 3-form vanishes identically ($\
bigwedge^3 T^*E = 0$). Hence $d\omega = 0$ **automatically**, with no further co
ndition.

Therefore *every* Hermitian metric on a Riemann surface is Kähler. Since $E$ adm
its a Hermitian metric, $E$ is Kähler. ∎

---

**Argument 2 (Explicit flat Kähler metric via the uniformization).** Write $E =
\mathbb{C}/\Lambda$. The Euclidean metric $g = dx^2 + dy^2$ on $\mathbb{C}$ is i
nvariant under translations, hence under the action of $\Lambda$. It therefore d
escends to a well-defined Riemannian metric $\bar g$ on the quotient $E$. With r
espect to the complex coordinate $z = x+iy$, the associated $(1,1)$-form is
$$\omega = \frac{i}{2}\,dz \wedge d\bar z = dx \wedge dy,$$
which is the standard area form. Since $dx\wedge dy$ is constant in the flat coo
rdinates, $d\omega = 0$. Thus $\bar g$ is a flat Kähler metric on $E$. ∎

---

**Argument 3 (Projective embedding / Fubini–Study).** Every elliptic curve over
$\mathbb{C}$ is projective: it embeds into $\mathbb{P}^2$ via the Weierstrass eq
uation $Y^2 Z = X^3 + aXZ^2 + bZ^3$ (with non-vanishing discriminant), or more g
enerally into $\mathbb{P}^{n}$ via a sufficiently ample line bundle. The Fubini–
Study form $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$ on $\mathbb{P}^n$ is Kähler, and the restricti
on of a Kähler form to a complex submanifold is again Kähler (closedness and the
 $(1,1)$-type are both preserved under pullback). Hence $E$, as a smooth complex
 submanifold of $\mathbb{P}^n$, inherits a Kähler metric. ∎

---

**Conclusion.** All three arguments confirm the same fact. The first is the most
 general: *every* compact Riemann surface — hence every elliptic curve — is Kähl
er, because in complex dimension 1 the closedness condition $d\omega=0$ is autom
atic for any Hermitian metric. The second exhibits an explicit flat Kähler metri
c. The third uses projectivity. The answer to the question is **yes**.
