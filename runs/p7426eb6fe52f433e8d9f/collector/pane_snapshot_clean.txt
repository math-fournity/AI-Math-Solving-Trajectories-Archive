## Solution

**Setup.** The five bays form a ring with tonnages (in clockwise order): $x_1=15
, x_2=5, x_3=20, x_4=40, x_5=20$. The adjacency rule means the set of emptied ba
ys always forms a connected path in the ring: each new pick extends the path at
one of its two ends. Alpha picks first (turn 1), then Beta (turn 2), Alpha (turn
 3), Beta (turn 4), Alpha (turn 5). So **Alpha gets 3 bays, Beta gets 2 bays**.

**Reduction to a two-arm game.** Once Alpha chooses a starting bay $v$, the rema
ining four bays split into two "arms" — the clockwise sequence and the countercl
ockwise sequence from $v$. Each subsequent turn, the player pops the front eleme
nt of either arm. The four extension turns go: Beta, Alpha, Beta, Alpha. Alpha's
 payoff = (value of $v$) + (turn-2 pick) + (turn-4 pick); Beta minimizes this.

**Solving by minimax for each start.** I denote the clockwise arm as $A$ and cou
nterclockwise arm as $B$.

- **Start at $x_1=15$:** $A=[5,20,40,20]$, $B=[20,40,20,5]$. Beta's best respons
e (taking $A[0]=5$) holds Alpha to extensions $=40$, total $=15+40=\mathbf{55}$.
- **Start at $x_2=5$:** $A=[20,40,20,15]$, $B=[15,20,40,20]$. Beta's best (takin
g $B[0]=15$) holds Alpha to extensions $=40$, total $=5+40=\mathbf{45}$.
- **Start at $x_3=20$:** $A=[40,20,15,5]$, $B=[5,15,20,40]$. Beta's best (taking
 $A[0]=40$, the big prize) holds Alpha to extensions $=25$, total $=20+25=\mathb
f{45}$.
- **Start at $x_5=20$:** $A=[15,5,20,40]$, $B=[40,20,5,15]$. Beta's best (taking
 $B[0]=40$) holds Alpha to extensions $=25$, total $=20+25=\mathbf{45}$.
- **Start at $x_4=40$:** $A=[20,15,5,20]$, $B=[20,5,15,20]$. Both arms begin wit
h $20$, and the arms are reverses of each other, so both of Beta's opening moves
 are equivalent.

**Detailed tree for start at $x_4$ (the optimum).** With $A=[20,15,5,20]$, $B=[2
0,5,15,20]$:

*Turn 1 (Beta)* takes $A[0]=20$ (vertex $x_5$). Remaining: $A=[15,5,20]$, $B=[20
,5,15,20]$.

*Turn 2 (Alpha).* Options:
- Take $B[0]=20$ (vertex $x_3$). Remaining $A=[15,5,20]$, $B=[5,15,20]$.
  - *Turn 3 (Beta):* take $A[0]=15$ → *Turn 4 (Alpha):* $\max(5,5)=5$. Alpha ext
ensions $=20+5=25$.
  - *Turn 3 (Beta):* take $B[0]=5$ → *Turn 4 (Alpha):* $\max(15,15)=15$. Alpha e
xtensions $=20+15=35$.
  - Beta chooses the minimum: $25$.
- Take $A[0]=15$ (vertex $x_1$). Remaining $A=[5,20]$, $B=[20,5,15,20]$.
  - *Turn 3 (Beta):* take $A[0]=5$ → *Turn 4:* $\max(20,20)=20$. Extensions $=15
+20=35$.
  - *Turn 3 (Beta):* take $B[0]=20$ → *Turn 4:* $\max(5,5)=5$. Extensions $=15+5
=20$.
  - Beta chooses the minimum: $20$.

Alpha maximizes: $\max(25,20)=25$. So Alpha's extensions $=25$, total $=40+25=\m
athbf{65}$.

**Why starting at $x_4$ dominates.** Starting at $x_4=40$ (the largest bay) is o
ptimal because Alpha immediately secures the 40-ton prize as the first pick, and
 both neighbors ($x_3=20$, $x_5=20$) are equal, so Beta cannot use the opening m
ove to deny Alpha a 20-ton bay. The subsequent play forces Alpha to extensions o
f $25$ (one $20$-ton bay plus a $5$-ton bay), yielding $40+20+5=65$. Starting el
sewhere lets Beta seize the $40$-ton bay on turn 1, capping Alpha far lower.

**Verification of the equilibrium line (start at $x_4$):** One optimal play is:
Alpha takes $x_4=40$; Beta takes $x_5=20$; Alpha takes $x_3=20$; Beta takes $x_1
=15$; Alpha takes $x_2=5$. Alpha's total $=40+20+5=65$, Beta's $=20+15=35$, sum
$=100$. ✓ Beta cannot do better (both opening moves are symmetric and lead to $6
5$), and Alpha cannot do better at any other start.

**Answer.** The total weight of minerals Alpha receives is $\boxed{65}$ tons.
