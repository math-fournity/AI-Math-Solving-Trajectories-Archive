## Solution

$$\sin z = z\,g(z), \qquad \ln|\sin z| = \ln|z| + \ln|g(z)|.$$

The zeros of $g$ are $z = \pm\pi, \pm 2\pi, \pm 3\pi, \ldots$ (all zeros of $\si
n$ except the origin). Note that $z = \pm\pi$ lie **on** the circle $|z|=\pi$, w
hile **no** zeros of $g$ lie strictly inside $|z|<\pi$.

**Applying the mean-value property for $R<\pi$.** For any $R$ with $0<R<\pi$, th
e function $g$ has no zeros in the closed disk $|z|\le R$, so $\ln|g(z)|$ is har
monic there. By the mean-value property of harmonic functions:
$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\ln|g(Re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta = \ln|g(0)| = \ln 1 =
 0. \tag{1}$$

**Passing to the limit $R\to\pi^-$.** The only obstruction to taking the limit i
s the logarithmic singularity of $\ln|g|$ at $z=\pm\pi$ (on the boundary $|z|=\p
i$). Near $z=\pi$:
$$g(z)=\frac{\sin z}{z}\approx\frac{-\cos\pi\,(z-\pi)}{\pi}=\frac{z-\pi}{\pi}, \
qquad \ln|g(z)|\approx\ln|z-\pi|-\ln\pi.$$

On $|z|=R$ near $\theta=0$: $z=Re^{i\theta}\approx R+iR\theta$, so $|z-\pi|\appr
ox\sqrt{(R-\pi)^2+\pi^2\theta^2}$. Setting $a=|R-\pi|\to 0$:
$$\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\tfrac12\ln\!\bigl(a^2+\pi^2\theta^2\bigr)\,d\theta \;\
longrightarrow\; \int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\ln(\pi|\theta|)\,d\theta = 2\bigl(\delt
a\ln\delta-\delta+\delta\ln\pi\bigr)<\infty,$$
where the convergence follows from dominated convergence (the integrands are bou
nded above by the integrable function $\ln(\pi|\theta|)+C$ and converge pointwis
e for $\theta\neq 0$). The same analysis holds near $z=-\pi$ (i.e., $\theta=\pi$
). Away from these two points, $\ln|g(Re^{i\theta})|$ converges uniformly. There
fore:
$$\lim_{R\to\pi^-}\int_0^{2\pi}\ln|g(Re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi}\ln|
g(\pi e^{i\theta})|\,d\theta. \tag{2}$$

Combining (1) and (2):
$$\int_0^{2\pi}\ln|g(\pi e^{i\theta})|\,d\theta = 0. \tag{3}$$

**Final computation.** Using $\sin z = z\,g(z)$ with $z=\pi e^{i\theta}$:
$$\int_0^{2\pi}\ln|\sin(\pi e^{i\theta})|\,d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi}\ln|\pi e^{i\t
heta}|\,d\theta + \int_0^{2\pi}\ln|g(\pi e^{i\theta})|\,d\theta.$$

The first integral: $\ln|\pi e^{i\theta}|=\ln\pi$ is constant, so
$$\int_0^{2\pi}\ln\pi\,d\theta = 2\pi\ln\pi.$$

By (3) the second integral is $0$. Therefore:

$$\boxed{\int_0^{2\pi}\ln|\sin(\pi e^{i\theta})|\,d\theta = 2\pi\ln\pi.}$$
