## Solution

**Claim:** The composite numbers $n$ satisfying the property are $n = 21$ and $n
 = 25$.

### Bounding $n$

Let $p$ be the smallest prime factor of $n$. Since $n$ is composite, $p$ is a pr
oper divisor with $1 < p < n$, so by hypothesis $p \geq n - 20$. Also $p \leq \s
qrt{n}$ (since $n = p \cdot m$ with $m \geq p$). Therefore:

$$n - 20 \leq p \leq \sqrt{n} \implies n - \sqrt{n} \leq 20.$$

Setting $t = \sqrt{n}$, we get $t^2 - t - 20 \leq 0$, so $t \leq \frac{1+\sqrt{8
1}}{2} = 5$, giving $n \leq 25$.

### Bounding via the largest proper divisor

The largest proper divisor of $n$ is $n/p$. It must satisfy $n/p \leq n - 12$, i
.e., $\frac{n(p-1)}{p} \geq 12$, and $n/p \geq n - 20$, i.e., $\frac{n(p-1)}{p}
\leq 20$. Combining:

$$\frac{12p}{p-1} \leq n \leq \frac{20p}{p-1}.$$

### Case analysis by smallest prime factor $p$

**$p = 2$:** $24 \leq n \leq 40$ and $n \leq 25$, so $n = 24$. Divisors $> 1$: $
\{2,3,4,6,8,12\}$. Required range: $[4,12]$. But $2, 3 \notin [4,12]$. **Fail.**

**$p = 3$:** $18 \leq n \leq 30$ and $n \leq 25$, $n$ odd, divisible by $3$, sma
llest prime factor $3$. Candidates: $n = 21$. Divisors $> 1$: $\{3, 7\}$. Requir
ed range: $[1, 9]$. Both $3, 7 \in [1,9]$. **✓ $n = 21$ works.**

**$p = 5$:** $15 \leq n \leq 25$, smallest prime factor $5$. Candidates: $n = 25
$ (since $15 = 3\cdot 5$ has $p=3$, $20$ is even). Divisors $> 1$: $\{5\}$. Requ
ired range: $[5, 13]$. $5 \in [5,13]$. **✓ $n = 25$ works.**

**$p \geq 7$:** Then $n \geq p^2 \geq 49 > 25$, contradicting $n \leq 25$. No so
lutions.

### Verification of all composite $n \leq 25$

The remaining composites $\leq 25$ all fail: for $n \leq 20$, the range $[n-20,
n-12]$ has upper bound $\leq 8$, but every such composite has a proper divisor $
> 8$ or (for small $n$) the range is entirely negative. Specifically, $n = 22$ h
as divisor $11 > 10 = n-12$; $n = 24$ has divisors $2, 3 < 4 = n-20$. All others
 fail similarly.

### Conclusion

The composite numbers satisfying the property are $\boxed{n \in \{21, 25\}}$.
