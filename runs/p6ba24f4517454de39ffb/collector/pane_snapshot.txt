## Proof

**Setup.** Define
$$\Lambda(n) = \log\!\left(\frac{n!}{n^n}\right) = \log(n!) - n\log n.$$

**Step 1. Verify the difference equation.**

$$\Lambda(n)-\Lambda(n-1) = \bigl[\log n! - n\log n\bigr]-\bigl[\log(n-1)!-(n-1)
\log(n-1)\bigr]$$
$$= \log n - n\log n + (n-1)\log(n-1).$$

Write $(n-1)\log(n-1)=(n-1)\bigl[\log n+\log(1-\tfrac1n)\bigr]=(n-1)\log n+(n-1)
\log(1-\tfrac1n)$. Then
$$\Lambda(n)-\Lambda(n-1)=\log n-n\log n+(n-1)\log n+(n-1)\log(1-\tfrac1n)=(n-1)
\log(1-\tfrac1n).$$

Using $\log(1-\tfrac1n)=-\tfrac1n-\tfrac1{2n^2}-\tfrac1{3n^3}-\cdots$,
$$(n-1)\log(1-\tfrac1n)=(n-1)\!\left(-\tfrac1n-\tfrac1{2n^2}-\cdots\right)=-1+\t
frac1n-\tfrac1{2n}+\tfrac1{2n^2}-\tfrac1{3n^2}+\cdots$$
$$=-1+\frac1{2n}+\frac1{6n^2}+O\!\left(\frac1{n^3}\right).$$

This confirms the given equation. (In fact the exact series is $-1+\sum_{j\ge1}\
frac{1}{j(j+1)n^j}$, giving coefficients $\tfrac12,\tfrac16,\tfrac1{12},\tfrac1{
20},\dots$.)

**Step 2. Determine the asymptotic form of $\Lambda(n)$ by coefficient matching.

$$\Lambda(n)-\Lambda(n-1)=-1+\tfrac12\log\!\tfrac{n}{n-1}+a_1\!\left(\tfrac1n-\t

$$\log\tfrac{n}{n-1}=-\log(1-\tfrac1n)=\tfrac1n+\tfrac1{2n^2}+\tfrac1{3n^3}+\cdo

**Step 3. Determine the constant $C$ via Wallis's product.**

Wallis's formula states
$$\frac{\pi}{2}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2^{4n}(n!)^4}{((2n)!)^2\,(2n+1)}.$$

Substituting $n!\sim e^C\sqrt n\,(n/e)^n$ (and $(2n)!\sim e^C\sqrt{2n}\,(2n/e)^{
2n}$):
$$\frac{2^{4n}(n!)^4}{((2n)!)^2}\sim\frac{2^{4n}\,e^{4C}\,n^2\,(n/e)^{4n}}{e^{2C

{4n}\,n^{4n}\,e^{-4n}}=\frac{e^{2C}\,n}{2}.$$

Multiplying by $\tfrac1{2n+1}$ and taking the limit:
$$\frac{\pi}{2}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{e^{2C}\,n}{2(2n+1)}=\frac{e^{2C}}{4}\;\Lo
ngrightarrow\;e^{2C}=2\pi\;\Longrightarrow\;e^C=\sqrt{2\pi}.$$

**Step 4. Assemble the asymptotic expansion of $n!$.**
