## Solution

**Setup.** Let District A have $a$ teams and District B have $b = a+8$ teams, so
 $n = a+b = 2a+8$ teams total. The total points in the tournament equal $\binom{
n}{2}$.

**Decomposing by match type.** Points come from three sources:
- **A vs A:** $\binom{a}{2}$ matches, all points stay in A.
- **B vs B:** $\binom{b}{2}$ matches, all points stay in B.
- **A vs B:** $ab$ matches; let $x$ = points A earns, $y$ = points B earns, with
 $x+y = ab$.

Then $S_A = \binom{a}{2} + x$ and $S_B = \binom{b}{2} + y$. The condition $S_B -
 S_A = 4$ gives:

$$\binom{b}{2} - \binom{a}{2} + y - x = 4.$$

With $b = a+8$:
$$\binom{b}{2} - \binom{a}{2} = \frac{(a+8)(a+7)-a(a-1)}{2} = 8a+28.$$

So $y - x = 4 - (8a+28) = -8a-24$, i.e., $x - y = 8a+24$. Combined with $x+y = a
(a+8) = a^2+8a$:

$$x = \frac{a^2+16a+24}{2}, \qquad y = \frac{a^2-24}{2}.$$

**Integrality and non-negativity.** For $x,y$ to be non-negative integers: $a$ m
ust be even (so $a^2+16a+24$ is even), and $y \geq 0$ requires $a^2 \geq 24$, i.
e., $a \geq 5$. With $a$ even: **$a \geq 6$**.

**Lower bound on the A-team maximum.** The total A score is:
$$S_A = \binom{a}{2} + x = \frac{2a^2+15a+24}{2}.$$

The highest-scoring A team has score $\geq \lceil S_A/a \rceil$. Computing $S_A/
a = a + 7.5 + 12/a$ for even $a \geq 6$:

| $a$ | $S_A/a$ | $\lceil S_A/a \rceil$ |
|-----|---------|----------------------|
| 6   | 15.5    | **16** |
| 8   | 17.0    | 17 |
| 10  | 18.7    | 19 |
| 12  | 20.5    | 21 |

The lower bound is minimized at $a=6$, giving **16**.

**Construction achieving 16 with $a=6$, $b=14$, $n=20$.** Here $x = 78$, $y = 6$
, $S_A = 93$, $S_B = 97$ (verified: $97-93=4$, $93+97=190=\binom{20}{2}$).

- **A-internal tournament (transitive):** scores $p = (5,4,3,2,1,0)$, sum $= 15
= \binom{6}{2}$. ✓
- **Cross scores for A:** set $q = (11,12,13,14,14,14)$, sum $= 78 = x$. ✓
- **A-team total scores:** $s_i = p_i + q_i = (16,16,16,16,15,14)$, sum $= 93$,
**maximum $= 16$**. ✓

**Verifying the cross tournament exists.** We need a $6\times 14$ bipartite 0-1
matrix (1 = A beats B) with row sums $q = (11,12,13,14,14,14)$ and column sums $
c_j = 6 - r_j$ where $\sum r_j = y = 6$.

Rows 4,5,6 have sum 14 (all 1s), contributing 3 to every column. The remaining $
3\times 14$ submatrix (rows 1,2,3) needs row sums $(11,12,13)$ and column sums $
c'_j = c_j - 3 \geq 0$ summing to $36$. Equivalently, we place 6 zeros in this $
3\times 14$ grid (row 1: 3 zeros, row 2: 2 zeros, row 3: 1 zero), giving column
zero-counts $z_j$ with $\sum z_j = 6$, and $r_j = z_j$. For example, place the z
eros in columns 1–3 (row 3: col 1; row 2: cols 1,2; row 1: cols 1,2,3), yielding
 $r = (3,2,1,0,\ldots,0)$ with $\sum r_j = 6$. ✓ This 0-1 matrix exists, so the
bipartite tournament is realizable.

- **B-internal tournament:** 14 teams, $\binom{14}{2}=91$ matches; a transitive
tournament gives scores $(13,12,\ldots,0)$ summing to $91$. B's total: $91 + 6 =
 97 = S_B$. ✓ (No constraint on individual B scores beyond the aggregate.)

All three match sets are independent and valid, so the full round-robin tourname
nt exists with the highest A-team score equal to **16**.

**Conclusion.** Since $a=6$ yields the lowest lower bound (16) among all feasibl
e values, and this bound is achieved by an explicit construction, the minimum po
ssible score of the highest-scoring District A team is:

$$\boxed{16}$$
