# Derivation of the Gauss–Bonnet Theorem

**Theorem.** Let $S \subset \mathbb{E}^3$ be a smooth compact oriented surface,
possibly with boundary $\partial S$ (a union of smooth closed curves). Then
$$\int_S K\,dA + \int_{\partial S} k_g\,ds = 2\pi\,\chi(S).$$

The proof proceeds in three stages: (A) the **local** Gauss–Bonnet formula via m
oving frames; (B) **globalization** by a geodesic triangulation; (C) a **combina
torial** reduction to the Euler characteristic.

---

## A. Local Gauss–Bonnet

Let $R\subset S$ be a simply connected region whose boundary $\partial R$ is pie
cewise smooth, positively oriented, with corners $p_1,\dots,p_n$ and interior an
gles $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n$.

**Moving frame.** Since $R$ is simply connected, choose a smooth oriented orthon
ormal frame $\{e_1,e_2\}$ on a neighborhood of $R$, with dual coframe $\{\omega_
1,\omega_2\}$ and connection form $\omega_{12}$ defined by $\nabla_X e_1=\omega_
{12}(X)e_2$, $\nabla_X e_2=-\omega_{12}(X)e_1$. The Cartan structure equations g
ive
$$d\omega_{12} = -K\,\omega_1\wedge\omega_2 = -K\,dA. \tag{1}$$

**Geodesic curvature of the boundary.** Parametrize each smooth arc of $\partial
 R$ by arc length $s$ with unit tangent $T$. Let $\varphi$ be the angle from $e_
1$ to $T$, so $T=\cos\varphi\,e_1+\sin\varphi\,e_2$. A direct computation of $\n
abla_T T$ gives
$$k_g = \frac{d\varphi}{ds} + \omega_{12}(T),\qquad\text{i.e.}\quad k_g\,ds = d\
varphi + \omega_{12}\big|_{\partial R}. \tag{2}$$

**Integrate and apply Stokes.** Using (2) and then (1) together with Stokes' the
orem ($R$ is simply connected, so $\omega_{12}$ is a globally defined $1$-form o
n $R$):
$$\int_{\partial R} k_g\,ds = \oint_{\partial R} d\varphi + \oint_{\partial R}\o
mega_{12} = \oint_{\partial R}d\varphi + \int_R d\omega_{12} = \oint_{\partial R
}d\varphi - \int_R K\,dA.$$

Hence
$$\int_R K\,dA + \int_{\partial R} k_g\,ds = \oint_{\partial R} d\varphi. \tag{3
}$$

**The tangent rotation.** The right-hand side of (3) is the total smooth rotatio
n of $T$ relative to the frame. As one traverses $\partial R$ once, the unit tan
gent $T$ traces a closed curve on the unit circle. By the **Hopf Umlaufsatz**, t
he total signed rotation of $T$ around a simple closed positively oriented curve
 is $2\pi$. This total rotation is the sum of the smooth part $\oint d\varphi$ a
nd the jumps at the corners. At a corner with interior angle $\alpha_i$ the tang
ent turns by the exterior angle $\delta_i = \pi - \alpha_i$. Therefore
$$\oint_{\partial R} d\varphi + \sum_{i=1}^n (\pi-\alpha_i) = 2\pi.$$

Substituting into (3) yields the **local Gauss–Bonnet formula**:
$$\boxed{\;\int_R K\,dA + \int_{\partial R} k_g\,ds + \sum_{i=1}^n(\pi-\alpha_i)
 = 2\pi.\;} \tag{LGB}$$

**Geodesic triangle.** If $T$ is a geodesic triangle (all three edges geodesic,
so $k_g\equiv 0$ on $\partial T$) with angles $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$, then (LGB)
gives the classical **angle-excess formula**
$$\int_T K\,dA = \alpha+\beta+\gamma-\pi. \tag{4}$$

## B. Globalization via a geodesic triangulation

**Existence.** Any smooth compact surface with boundary admits a triangulation w
hose *interior* edges are geodesic segments and whose *boundary* edges are sub-a
rcs of $\partial S$: start from any sufficiently fine triangulation with every t
riangle inside a geodesically convex normal neighborhood, then replace each inte
rior edge by the unique minimizing geodesic in that neighborhood.

Let the triangulation have
- $V$ vertices, of which $V_{\mathrm{int}}$ are interior and $V_{\mathrm{bd}}$ a
re on $\partial S$;
- $E$ edges, of which $E_{\mathrm{int}}$ are interior (geodesic) and $E_{\mathrm
{bd}}$ lie on $\partial S$;
- $F$ triangular faces.

**Apply (LGB) face by face.** For an interior triangle, every edge is geodesic s
o only the area term and the three corner angles contribute, giving (4). For a b
oundary triangle, exactly one edge lies on $\partial S$ and carries geodesic cur
vature $k_g$ (the value of $k_g$ of $\partial S$ along that arc); the other two
edges are geodesic. Summing (LGB) over all $F$ faces:

- the area integrals add up to $\int_S K\,dA$;
- the boundary-edge $k_g$ integrals add up to $\int_{\partial S} k_g\,ds$ (the b
oundary edges partition $\partial S$);
- the corner-angle terms add up to the sum of **all** angles at **all** vertices
.

Thus
$$\int_S K\,dA + \int_{\partial S} k_g\,ds = \Bigl(\sum_{\text{all vertices}}\te
xt{angles at that vertex}\Bigr) - \pi F. \tag{5}$$

**Sum of angles at a vertex.**
- At an **interior** vertex, the incident triangles tile a full disk, so their a
ngles sum to $2\pi$. Total over interior vertices: $2\pi V_{\mathrm{int}}$.
- At a **boundary** vertex, the incident triangles tile a half-disk. Since $\par
tial S$ is smooth (no corner of $S$), the interior angle of $S$ at that vertex i
s $\pi$, so the angles sum to $\pi$. Total over boundary vertices: $\pi V_{\math
rm{bd}}$.

Substituting into (5):
$$\int_S K\,dA + \int_{\partial S} k_g\,ds = 2\pi V_{\mathrm{int}} + \pi V_{\mat
hrm{bd}} - \pi F. \tag{6}$$

## C. Combinatorial reduction to $\chi(S)$

We claim
$$2\pi V_{\mathrm{int}} + \pi V_{\mathrm{bd}} - \pi F = 2\pi(V-E+F) = 2\pi\,\chi
(S). \tag{7}$$

It suffices to show $2E = V_{\mathrm{bd}} + 3F$, since then
$$2\pi(V-E+F) = 2\pi(V_{\mathrm{int}}+V_{\mathrm{bd}}) - 2\pi E + 2\pi F = 2\pi
V_{\mathrm{int}} + 2\pi V_{\mathrm{bd}} - \pi(V_{\mathrm{bd}}+3F) + 2\pi F = 2\p
i V_{\mathrm{int}} + \pi V_{\mathrm{bd}} - \pi F.$$

**Proof of $2E = V_{\mathrm{bd}} + 3F$.** Count edge–face incidences: each of th
e $F$ triangles has $3$ edges, so $3F$ counts every interior edge twice and ever
y boundary edge once:
$$3F = 2E_{\mathrm{int}} + E_{\mathrm{bd}}. \tag{8}$$
Since $E = E_{\mathrm{int}} + E_{\mathrm{bd}}$, equation (8) gives $3F = 2E - E_
{\mathrm{bd}}$, i.e. $2E = 3F + E_{\mathrm{bd}}$. Finally, the boundary edges fo
rm a triangulation of $\partial S$, which is a disjoint union of circles; each c
ircle is a cycle, so the number of boundary edges equals the number of boundary
vertices:
$$E_{\mathrm{bd}} = V_{\mathrm{bd}}.$$
Therefore $2E = 3F + V_{\mathrm{bd}}$, as required. ∎ (of the combinatorial iden
tity)

Combining (6) and (7):
$$\boxed{\;\int_S K\,dA + \int_{\partial S} k_g\,ds = 2\pi\,\chi(S).\;}$$

## Summary of the argument

1. **Local GB** (moving frames + Stokes + Hopf Umlaufsatz): for a simply connect
ed patch with corners, $\int_R K\,dA + \int_{\partial R}k_g\,ds + \sum(\pi-\alph
a_i)=2\pi$.
2. **Triangulate** $S$ with geodesic interior edges and boundary edges along $\p
artial S$; sum (LGB) over all faces. Interior $k_g$ terms vanish; boundary $k_g$
 terms reassemble into $\int_{\partial S}k_g\,ds$; angle sums give $2\pi V_{\mat
hrm{int}}+\pi V_{\mathrm{bd}}$.
3. **Combinatorics**: the incidence count $2E=V_{\mathrm{bd}}+3F$ (using $E_{\ma
thrm{bd}}=V_{\mathrm{bd}}$, since $\partial S$ is a union of cycles) converts $2
\pi V_{\mathrm{int}}+\pi V_{\mathrm{bd}}-\pi F$ into $2\pi(V-E+F)=2\pi\chi(S)$.

The result is intrinsic (depends only on the induced metric, not on the embeddin
g in $\mathbb{E}^3$) and holds for any smooth compact surface with boundary, wit
h $\chi(S)$ its Euler characteristic.
