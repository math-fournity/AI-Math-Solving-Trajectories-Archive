## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, there can be two distinct irreducible polynomials of the same de
gree $d$ (with $d \mid n$) in the factorization of $x^{p^n} - x$ over $\mathbb{F
}_p$.

**Key fact (classical).** The polynomial $x^{p^n} - x$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ is th
e product of all monic irreducible polynomials over $\mathbb{F}_p$ whose degree
divides $n$:
$$x^{p^n} - x = \prod_{\substack{f \text{ monic irreducible over } \mathbb{F}_p
\\ \deg f \mid n}} f(x).$$
This follows because $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ is the splitting field of $x^{p^n}-x$, i
ts roots are exactly the elements of $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$, and the minimal polynom
ial over $\mathbb{F}_p$ of any $\alpha \in \mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ has degree $d$ wher
e $d \mid n$ (since $\mathbb{F}_p(\alpha) \subseteq \mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ is a subfi
eld, forcing $\deg(\alpha) \mid n$). Conversely, every monic irreducible of degr
ee $d \mid n$ splits in $\mathbb{F}_{p^d} \subseteq \mathbb{F}_{p^n}$, so its ro
ots lie in $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ and it divides $x^{p^n}-x$.

**Exhibiting two distinct irreducibles of the same degree.** Take $d = 1$, which
 divides every $n \geq 1$. The monic irreducible polynomials of degree $1$ over
$\mathbb{F}_p$ are exactly
$$\{x - a : a \in \mathbb{F}_p\},$$
and there are $p$ of them. Since $p$ is prime, $p \geq 2$, so in particular the
two polynomials
$$x \quad \text{and} \quad x - 1$$
are **distinct** monic irreducible polynomials of degree $1$.

Both divide $x^{p^n} - x$: indeed $0$ and $1$ are elements of $\mathbb{F}_p \sub
seteq \mathbb{F}_{p^n}$, hence both are roots of $x^{p^n} - x$ (every element of
 $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ satisfies $\alpha^{p^n} = \alpha$). Equivalently, $x \mid x^
{p^n}-x$ and $(x-1) \mid x^{p^n}-x$.

**Conclusion.** For $d = 1$ (a divisor of $n$), the factorization of $x^{p^n} -
x$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ contains at least two distinct irreducible polynomials of
 degree $1$, namely $x$ and $x-1$. Therefore the answer is **yes**.
