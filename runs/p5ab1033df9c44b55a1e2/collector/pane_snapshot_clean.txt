## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, it is possible for a proper field extension $L/K$ to satisfy $L
\cong K$ as fields.

**Construction.** Let $\mathbb{Q}$ be the rationals and let $\{t_0, t_1, t_2, \d
ots\}$ be countably many algebraically independent indeterminates. Set

$$K = \mathbb{Q}(t_1, t_2, t_3, \dots), \qquad L = \mathbb{Q}(t_0, t_1, t_2, t_3
, \dots).$$

**$L/K$ is a proper extension.** Clearly $K \subseteq L$ since $\{t_1, t_2, \dot
s\} \subseteq \{t_0, t_1, t_2, \dots\}$. The extension is *proper*: the element
$t_0 \in L$ does not belong to $K$, because $t_0$ is algebraically independent f
rom $\{t_1, t_2, \dots\}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, hence $t_0 \notin \mathbb{Q}(t_1, t
_2, \dots) = K$. Thus $K \subsetneq L$.

**$L \cong K$ as fields.** Define a map on the generating indeterminates by shif
ting indices:

$$\varphi(t_i) = t_{i-1}, \qquad i = 1, 2, 3, \dots$$

This assignment extends uniquely to a $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra isomorphism of polyno
mial rings

$$\varphi: \mathbb{Q}[t_1, t_2, t_3, \dots] \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; \mathbb{Q
}[t_0, t_1, t_2, \dots],$$

since it merely relabels the algebraically independent generators (sending the s
et $\{t_1, t_2, \dots\}$ bijectively onto $\{t_0, t_1, t_2, \dots\}$). Passing t
o fraction fields, $\varphi$ extends to a field isomorphism

$$\varphi: K = \mathbb{Q}(t_1, t_2, \dots) \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; \mathbb{Q}
(t_0, t_1, t_2, \dots) = L.$$

**Surjectivity:** every generator $t_j$ of $L$ ($j \geq 0$) lies in the image: $
t_0 = \varphi(t_1)$, and $t_j = \varphi(t_{j+1})$ for $j \geq 1$. Hence $\varphi
(K) = L$.

**Injectivity:** $\varphi$ is a nonzero field homomorphism, hence injective.

Therefore $\varphi: K \xrightarrow{\sim} L$ is a field isomorphism, while $K \su
bsetneq L$. $\square$

**Remark.** The key mechanism is that $K$ has "infinitely many degrees of freedo
m" (countably many independent transcendentals), so absorbing one more transcend
ental ($t_0$) does not change the isomorphism type—exactly as an infinite set ca
n be in bijection with a proper subset. Finitely generated fields cannot exhibit
 this phenomenon: if $K$ is finitely generated over its prime field and $L/K$ is
 proper, then $L$ has strictly larger transcendence degree or strictly larger al
gebraic part, forcing $L \not\cong K$.
