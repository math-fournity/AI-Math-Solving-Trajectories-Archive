## Proof

**Theorem.** Every geodesically convex subset $C$ of a product of two trees $T_1
 \times T_2$ equipped with the $\ell_1$-metric is a median space. That is, for e
very $A, B, C \in C$, there exists $D \in C$ lying on geodesics between each pai
r.

The proof proceeds in two steps: (1) the ambient space $T_1 \times T_2$ is a med
ian space, and (2) geodesically convex subsets of any median space are median su
bspaces.

### Step 1: $T_1 \times T_2$ with the $\ell_1$-metric is a median space.

**Trees are median spaces.** A tree is a uniquely geodesic metric space. For any
 three points $a, b, c$ in a tree, the three geodesic segments $[a,b]$, $[b,c]$,
 $[a,c]$ pairwise intersect, and their triple intersection consists of a single
point $m$, the *median*. This point $m$ lies on the (unique) geodesic between ea
ch pair.

**Products of median spaces are median spaces (coordinate-wise median).** Let $(
X_1, d_1)$ and $(X_2, d_2)$ be median spaces with medians $\mu_1, \mu_2$. Equip
$X_1 \times X_2$ with $d = d_1 + d_2$ ($\ell_1$-metric). Define:
$$\mu\big((a_1,a_2),\,(b_1,b_2),\,(c_1,c_2)\big) = \big(\mu_1(a_1,b_1,c_1),\;\mu
_2(a_2,b_2,c_2)\big).$$

We verify this is a median. Set $m_i = \mu_i(a_i, b_i, c_i)$ and $M = (m_1, m_2)
$. Since $m_1$ lies on a geodesic from $a_1$ to $b_1$ in $X_1$:
$$d_1(a_1, m_1) + d_1(m_1, b_1) = d_1(a_1, b_1).$$
Since $m_2$ lies on a geodesic from $a_2$ to $b_2$ in $X_2$:
$$d_2(a_2, m_2) + d_2(m_2, b_2) = d_2(a_2, b_2).$$
Adding:
$$d(A, M) + d(M, B) = d(A, B),$$
so $M$ lies on a geodesic from $A$ to $B$ in $X_1 \times X_2$. By the same argum
ent with the other pairs, $M$ lies on geodesics from $B$ to $C$ and from $A$ to
$C$. Hence $T_1 \times T_2$ with the $\ell_1$-metric is a median space. $\checkm
ark$

### Step 2: Geodesically convex subsets of a median space are median subspaces.

Let $X$ be a median space with median operation $\mu$, and let $C \subseteq X$ b
e **geodesically convex**: for every $x, y \in C$, *every* geodesic from $x$ to
$y$ in $X$ is contained in $C$.

**Claim.** For any $A, B, C \in C$, the median $M = \mu(A, B, C)$ belongs to $C$
 and lies on geodesics (within $C$) between each pair.

**Proof of claim.** By the median property of $X$, the point $M$ lies on *some*
geodesic $\gamma_{AB}$ from $A$ to $B$ in $X$. Since $A, B \in C$ and $C$ is geo
desically convex, *every* geodesic from $A$ to $B$ lies in $C$; in particular $\
gamma_{AB} \subseteq C$, so $M \in C$.

Now $M$ also lies on some geodesic $\gamma_{BC}$ from $B$ to $C$. Since $B, C \i
n C$ and $C$ is geodesically convex, $\gamma_{BC} \subseteq C$, so the portion o
f $\gamma_{BC}$ from $B$ to $M$ (and from $M$ to $C$) lies in $C$. Likewise, $M$
 lies on some geodesic $\gamma_{AC}$ from $A$ to $C$, and $\gamma_{AC} \subseteq
 C$.

Therefore $M \in C$ lies on geodesics contained in $C$ connecting $A$ to $B$, $B
$ to $C$, and $A$ to $C$. Taking $D = M$, the median property holds in $C$. $\ch
eckmark$

### Conclusion

Combining Steps 1 and 2: $T_1 \times T_2$ with the $\ell_1$-metric is a median s
pace, and every geodesically convex subset of a median space is a median subspac

etric is a median space**: for every three points $A, B, C$ in the subset, the a
mbient median $D = \mu(A,B,C)$ lies in the subset (by convexity) and lies on geo
desics between each pair (by the median property), with those geodesics containe

The answer is **yes**. $\blacksquare$
