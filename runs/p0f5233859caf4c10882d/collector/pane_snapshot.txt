### Evaluation

We evaluate
$$I=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x^2}{1+x^6}\,dx.$$

**Substitution.** Set $t = x^3$. Then $dt = 3x^2\,dx$, so $x^2\,dx = \tfrac{1}{3
}\,dt$. As $x$ runs from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$, the strictly increasing map $t=
x^3$ also runs from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$. Moreover,
$$1+x^6 = 1+(x^3)^2 = 1+t^2.$$
Hence
$$I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1+t^2}\cdot\frac{dt}{3} = \frac{1}{3}\int_
{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{1+t^2}.$$

The remaining integral is elementary:
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{1+t^2} = \arctan(t)\Big|_{-\infty}^{\infty} =
 \frac{\pi}{2}-\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=\pi.$$

Therefore
$$\boxed{I = \frac{\pi}{3}}.$$

### Verification (residue calculus)

The integrand $f(z)=\dfrac{z^2}{1+z^6}$ has simple poles where $z^6=-1=e^{i\pi}$
, i.e. at $z_k=e^{i(\pi+2k\pi)/6}$, $k=0,\dots,5$. Those in the upper half-plane
 are
$$z_0=e^{i\pi/6},\quad z_1=e^{i\pi/2}=i,\quad z_2=e^{i5\pi/6}.$$
At a simple pole $z_k$, the residue is
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,z_k)=\frac{z_k^2}{6z_k^5}=\frac{1}{6z_k^3}.$$
Computing $z_k^3$:
$$z_0^3=e^{i\pi/2}=i,\quad z_1^3=e^{i3\pi/2}=-i,\quad z_2^3=e^{i5\pi/2}=i.$$
Thus $\frac{1}{z_k^3}=\frac{1}{i},\,\frac{1}{-i},\,\frac{1}{i}=-i,\,i,\,-i$, who
se sum is $-i$. The total residue in the upper half-plane is $\frac{1}{6}(-i)=-\
frac{i}{6}$. By the residue theorem,
$$I = 2\pi i\left(-\frac{i}{6}\right)=\frac{2\pi}{6}=\frac{\pi}{3},$$
confirming the result.
