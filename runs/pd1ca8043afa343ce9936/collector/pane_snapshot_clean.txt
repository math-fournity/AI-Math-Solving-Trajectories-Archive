## Fundamental Group of $\mathbb{P}^n(\mathbb{C})$ for $n \geq 1$

**Claim.** For every $n \geq 1$, the complex projective space $\mathbb{P}^n(\mat
hbb{C})$ is simply connected:
$$\pi_1\!\bigl(\mathbb{P}^n(\mathbb{C})\bigr) \;=\; 0.$$

---

### Proof

We use the **Hopf fibration**
$$S^1 \;\longrightarrow\; S^{2n+1} \;\xrightarrow{\;p\;}\; \mathbb{P}^n(\mathbb{
C}),$$
where $S^{2n+1}\subset \mathbb{C}^{n+1}$ is the unit sphere and $p$ sends a unit
 vector $z=(z_0,\dots,z_n)$ to its complex line $[z_0:\cdots:z_n]$. The fiber ov
er a line is the set of unit vectors in that line, which is a copy of $S^1$ (mul
tiplication by unit complex numbers). This is a locally trivial fiber bundle, he
nce a Serre (indeed Hurewicz) fibration.

Applying the **long exact sequence of homotopy groups** to this fibration yields
$$\cdots \to \pi_1(S^1) \to \pi_1(S^{2n+1}) \to \pi_1\!\bigl(\mathbb{P}^n(\mathb
b{C})\bigr) \to \pi_0(S^1) \to \pi_0(S^{2n+1}) \to \cdots$$

Now we read off the relevant terms.

- **The total space $S^{2n+1}$ is simply connected.** For $n\geq 1$ we have $2n+
1\geq 3$, and every sphere $S^m$ with $m\geq 2$ is simply connected: $\pi_1(S^{2
n+1})=0$.

- **The base and total space are path connected**, and the fiber $S^1$ is path c
onnected, so $\pi_0(S^1)=0$ and $\pi_0(S^{2n+1})=0$.

Substituting, the exact sequence around $\pi_1(\mathbb{P}^n(\mathbb{C}))$ become
s
$$\pi_1(S^1) \;\longrightarrow\; 0 \;\longrightarrow\; \pi_1\!\bigl(\mathbb{P}^n
(\mathbb{C})\bigr) \;\longrightarrow\; 0.$$

By exactness at $\pi_1(\mathbb{P}^n(\mathbb{C}))$: the map $0\to\pi_1(\mathbb{P}
^n(\mathbb{C}))$ has image $0$, and the kernel of the next map $\pi_1(\mathbb{P}
^n(\mathbb{C}))\to 0$ is all of $\pi_1(\mathbb{P}^n(\mathbb{C}))$. Exactness say
s
$$\operatorname{im}\bigl(0\to\pi_1(\mathbb{P}^n(\mathbb{C}))\bigr)=\ker\bigl(\pi
_1(\mathbb{P}^n(\mathbb{C}))\to 0\bigr),$$
i.e.
$$0 \;=\; \pi_1\!\bigl(\mathbb{P}^n(\mathbb{C})\bigr).$$

Therefore $\pi_1(\mathbb{P}^n(\mathbb{C}))=0$ for all $n\geq 1$.

**Sanity check for $n=1$.** Here $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})\cong S^2$ (the Rieman
n sphere), and indeed $\pi_1(S^2)=0$, consistent with the result. The fibration
used is the classical Hopf map $S^1\to S^3\to S^2$.

---

**Conclusion.** For every $n\geq 1$,
$$\boxed{\pi_1\!\bigl(\mathbb{P}^n(\mathbb{C})\bigr)\;=\;0,}$$
i.e. complex projective space is simply connected.
