Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether embedded resolution of sing
ularities always exists when $X$ is smooth and $Y$ is a curve.

**Answer: Yes — an embedded resolution can always be found.** This holds over an
y perfect field, in all characteristics. I prove this below.

---

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $X$ be a smooth variety over a perfect field $k$, and let $Y \sub
set X$ be a closed subscheme of pure dimension $1$ (a curve, possibly singular,
possibly non-reduced). An *embedded resolution* of $Y$ in $X$ is a proper birati
onal morphism $f: X' \to X$, obtained as a composition of blowups along smooth c
enters, such that:

1. $X'$ is smooth,
2. the total transform $f^{-1}(Y)$ is a simple normal crossings (SNC) divisor on
 $X'$.

(When $Y$ is reduced, condition (2) implies the strict transform of $Y$ is smoot
h, since the components of an SNC divisor are smooth.)

The proof proceeds in two phases.

---

### Phase I: Resolving the reduced curve $Y_{\mathrm{red}}$

**Step 1 — Reduction to the reduced case.** Since $Y$ has pure dimension $1$, we
 have $Y_{\mathrm{red}}$ is a reduced curve. If we find an embedded resolution o
f $Y_{\mathrm{red}}$, the total transform of $Y$ is the same underlying divisor
with multiplicities, which is still SNC (the SNC condition permits multiplicitie
s). Thus it suffices to resolve $Y_{\mathrm{red}}$.

**Step 2 — The singular locus is zero-dimensional.** Let $C = Y_{\mathrm{red}}$.
 Since $C$ is a reduced curve (pure dimension $1$), the singular locus $\operato
rname{Sing}(C)$ is a proper closed subset of $C$, hence $\dim \operatorname{Sing
}(C) \leq 0$. Therefore $\operatorname{Sing}(C) = \{P_1, \dots, P_r\}$ is a fini
te set of closed points.

**Step 3 — Blow up the singular locus.** The set $Z = \{P_1, \dots, P_r\}$ is a
smooth closed subvariety of $X$ (a disjoint union of reduced points on a smooth
variety). Let $f_1: X_1 \to X$ be the blowup of $X$ along $Z$, and let $C_1$ be
the strict transform of $C$. Since $Z \subset \operatorname{Sing}(C)$, the map $
f_1$ is an isomorphism over $X \setminus \operatorname{Sing}(C)$, so $C_1 \cong
C$ away from the singular points.

**Step 4 — The Hilbert–Samuel function strictly decreases.** We use the followin
g key result:

> **Lemma (Bennett, 1971).** *Let $(R, \mathfrak{m})$ be an excellent regular lo
cal ring and $I \subset R$ an ideal with $\dim(R/I) = 1$. Let $R' = \operatornam
e{Bl}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)$ and let $I'$ be the strict transform of $I$. Then for e
very closed point $\mathfrak{M}$ of $R'$ lying over $\mathfrak{m}$, the Hilbert–
Samuel function satisfies*
> $$H(R'/I',\, \mathfrak{M}) \;<_{\mathrm{lex}}\; H(R/I,\, \mathfrak{m})$$
> *in the lexicographic order.*

This applies because $\mathcal{O}_{X, P_i}$ is an excellent regular local ring (
smooth variety over a field), and the ideal of $C$ defines a curve. **The lemma
is valid in all characteristics** — it relies only on excellence and regularity
of the local ring, not on the characteristic.

**Step 5 — Termination.** The Hilbert–Samuel function of a curve in a regular lo
cal ring of embedding dimension $e$ takes values in the set of non-increasing se
quences $(e_0, e_1, e_2, \dots)$ of non-negative integers with $e_0 \leq e$ and
finite support. This set is **well-ordered** under lexicographic order.

Define the complexity invariant as the multiset of Hilbert–Samuel functions at t
he singular points:
$$\Phi(C) = \big\{\, H\!\left(\mathcal{O}_{C, P}\right) : P \in \operatorname{Si
ng}(C) \,\big\}.$$

By the Lemma, each blowup at the singular locus replaces every element of $\Phi$
 by elements that are strictly smaller in the lexicographic order. Since the set
 of Hilbert–Samuel functions is well-ordered, this descending process **must ter
minate**. After finitely many blowups $f_1 \circ \cdots \circ f_n: X_n \to X$, t
he strict transform $C_n$ has no singular points, i.e., $C_n$ is **smooth**.

**Step 6 — The centers are always smooth.** At each stage, the center is $\opera
torname{Sing}(C_j) \subset X_j$, a finite set of closed points. Since each $X_j$
 is smooth (blowups of smooth varieties along smooth centers are smooth), a fini
te set of points is a smooth center. Moreover, $C_j$ remains a curve (pure dimen
sion $1$) at each stage, so $\operatorname{Sing}(C_j)$ is always $0$-dimensional
.

---

### Phase II: Achieving simple normal crossings

After Phase I, we have $f_n: X_n \to X$ with $X_n$ smooth and $C_n$ (strict tran
sform of $C = Y_{\mathrm{red}}$) smooth. The total transform is $f_n^{-1}(C) = C
_n \cup E$, where $E$ is the exceptional divisor.

**Step 7 — The exceptional divisor is automatically SNC.** Since every blowup in
 Phase I was at a **point** (a $0$-dimensional smooth center), the exceptional d
ivisor $E$ is automatically a simple normal crossings divisor. This follows from
 the standard fact:

> *Blowing up a smooth variety at a point that lies on an SNC divisor preserves
the SNC property of the divisor and adds a new smooth exceptional divisor ($\con
g \mathbb{P}^{\dim X - 1}$) that meets the strict transforms transversally.*

This is verified locally: if $D_1, \dots, D_s$ are SNC divisors defined by $x_1
= 0, \dots, x_s = 0$ and we blow up the origin, then in each blowup chart the st
rict transforms and the exceptional divisor are defined by coordinate hyperplane
s, hence SNC.

**Step 8 — Making $C_n$ transversal to $E$.** It remains to ensure that the smoo
th curve $C_n$ meets the SNC divisor $E$ transversally. Since $C_n$ is a curve a
nd $E$ is a divisor, $C_n \cap E$ is $0$-dimensional (a finite set of points). T
he **non-transversal** intersection points form a finite subset $S \subset C_n \
cap E$.

Blow up $X_n$ at $S$ (a smooth center, being a finite set of points). Call this
$g: X_{n+1} \to X_n$. We verify:

- The strict transform $C_{n+1}$ is smooth (blowup of a smooth curve at a point
is smooth).
- $E$'s total transform remains SNC (blowup at a point preserves SNC, by Step 7)
.
- $C_{n+1}$ meets the new exceptional divisor transversally: locally, if $E_i =
\{x_1 = 0\}$ and $C_n$ is tangent to $E_i$ at the origin (say $C_n$ is given by
$x_2 = x_1^m, x_3 = \cdots = 0$ with $m \geq 2$), then after blowup in the chart
 $x_1 = u$, the strict transform is $v_2 = u^{m-1}, v_3 = \cdots = 0$, which mee
ts the exceptional $\{u = 0\}$ transversally when $m-1 = 1$, i.e., $m = 2$. For
$m > 2$, the tangency order decreases by $1$.

Since the **order of tangency** (a positive integer) strictly decreases with eac
h blowup, after finitely many point blowups, $C_n$ meets every component of $E$
transversally. The total transform $f^{-1}(C)$ is then an SNC divisor.

---

### Step 9 — Lifting to the non-reduced case

If $Y$ is non-reduced, write $Y = \sum m_i C_i$ (as a divisor on $X$, where $C_i
$ are the irreducible components of $Y_{\mathrm{red}}$). The embedded resolution
 of $Y_{\mathrm{red}}$ constructed above gives $f: X' \to X$ with $f^{-1}(Y_{\ma
thrm{red}})$ an SNC divisor. Then $f^{-1}(Y) = \sum m_i \cdot f^{-1}(C_i)$ is th
e same SNC divisor with multiplicities, which is SNC by definition (the SNC cond
ition permits arbitrary positive integer multiplicities).

---

### Conclusion

The composition $f: X' \to X$ of all blowups (Phases I and II) is a proper birat
ional morphism, realized as a composition of blowups along smooth centers (all o
f which are finite sets of points), such that:

1. $X'$ is smooth (blowups of smooth varieties along smooth centers are smooth),
2. $f^{-1}(Y)$ is a simple normal crossings divisor (Phase II + Step 9),
3. $f$ is an isomorphism over $X \setminus \operatorname{Sing}(Y_{\mathrm{red}})
$ (all centers were contained in $\operatorname{Sing}(Y_{\mathrm{red}})$ or in t
he non-transversal locus).

Therefore, **an embedded resolution of $Y$ in $X$ always exists** when $X$ is a
smooth variety and $Y$ is a curve. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark on characteristic independence.** The crucial structural fact is that t
he singular locus of a curve is $0$-dimensional, so all blowup centers are point
s — which are automatically smooth. This is why the argument works in **all char
acteristics** (over a perfect field), unlike the general embedded resolution pro
blem for higher-dimensional $Y$, which in positive characteristic remains open f
or $\dim(Y) \geq 3$. In characteristic $0$, the result is a special case of Hiro
naka's theorem on embedded resolution of singularities (1964), which holds for $
Y$ of arbitrary dimension.
