Looking at this problem, I need to find the infimum of the Dirichlet energy over
 smooth functions on the unit disk with $u(0)=0$ and $u=1$ on the boundary.

**Answer: The infimum is $0$.**

## Proof

**Upper bound: constructing functions with arbitrarily small energy.**

For $0 < \varepsilon < 1$, define a radial function:
$$u_\varepsilon(r) = \begin{cases} 0 & r \leq \varepsilon, \\ 1 - \dfrac{\ln r}{
\ln \varepsilon} & \varepsilon \leq r \leq 1. \end{cases}$$

**Boundary conditions:** $u_\varepsilon(0) = 0$ ✓. At $r=1$: $u_\varepsilon(1) =
 1 - \frac{0}{\ln\varepsilon} = 1$ ✓. At $r=\varepsilon$: $u_\varepsilon(\vareps
ilon) = 1 - \frac{\ln\varepsilon}{\ln\varepsilon} = 0$, so the function is conti
nuous.

**Energy computation:** For $\varepsilon \leq r \leq 1$, $u_\varepsilon'(r) = -\
frac{1}{r\ln\varepsilon}$, so:
$$E(u_\varepsilon) = 2\pi \int_\varepsilon^1 \frac{1}{r^2(\ln\varepsilon)^2}\, r
\, dr = \frac{2\pi}{(\ln\varepsilon)^2}\int_\varepsilon^1 \frac{dr}{r} = \frac{2
\pi}{(\ln\varepsilon)^2}\bigl(-\ln\varepsilon\bigr) = \frac{2\pi}{|\ln\varepsilo
n|}.$$

As $\varepsilon \to 0^+$, $|\ln\varepsilon| \to \infty$, so $E(u_\varepsilon) \t
o 0$.

**Smoothing to $C^\infty$:** The function $u_\varepsilon$ has a kink at $r = \va
repsilon$ (derivative jumps from $0$ to $-\frac{1}{\varepsilon\ln\varepsilon}$).
 We smooth the transition over the interval $[\varepsilon/2, \varepsilon]$ using
 a standard smooth cutoff. Since $u_\varepsilon \equiv 0$ near the origin (for $
r \leq \varepsilon/2$ after smoothing), the smoothed function $\tilde{u}_\vareps
ilon$ is $C^\infty$ on the entire closed disk (it is constant near the origin, h
ence smooth there as a function of $(x,y)$, and $\ln r$ is smooth for $r > 0$).

The energy contributed by the smoothing region $[\varepsilon/2, \varepsilon]$ is
:
$$2\pi\int_{\varepsilon/2}^{\varepsilon} (\tilde{u}_\varepsilon')^2\, r\, dr \le
q 2\pi \cdot \varepsilon \cdot \int_{\varepsilon/2}^{\varepsilon} (\tilde{u}_\va
repsilon')^2\, dr.$$

The derivative in the transition region is bounded by $O\!\left(\frac{1}{\vareps
ilon\,|\ln\varepsilon|}\right)$, so this contribution is $O\!\left(\frac{1}{\var
epsilon\,(\ln\varepsilon)^2}\right) \cdot \varepsilon = O\!\left(\frac{1}{(\ln\v
arepsilon)^2}\right)$, which is negligible compared to $\frac{2\pi}{|\ln\varepsi
lon|}$.

Thus $E(\tilde{u}_\varepsilon) \to 0$ as $\varepsilon \to 0^+$, and each $\tilde
{u}_\varepsilon$ is $C^\infty$ with $\tilde{u}_\varepsilon(0)=0$ and $\tilde{u}_
\varepsilon = 1$ on $\{x^2+y^2=1\}$.

**Lower bound:** The energy is a sum of squares, so $E(u) \geq 0$ for all admiss
ible $u$.

**The infimum is not attained:** If $E(u) = 0$, then $\nabla u = 0$ a.e., so $u$
 is constant a.e. By continuity, $u$ is constant everywhere. But $u(0) = 0$ and
$u = 1$ on the boundary is a contradiction. Hence no admissible function achieve
s energy $0$.

**Conclusion:** Since the energy is always $\geq 0$ and can be made arbitrarily
small, the infimum is:

$$\inf \iint_{x^2+y^2\leq 1}\left(\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right)^2 +
 \left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right)^2\right)dx\,dy = \boxed{0}.$$
