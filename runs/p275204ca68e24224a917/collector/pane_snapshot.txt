## Proof

**Setup and notation.** Let $M$ denote the common 3-manifold. Let $S^3_1, S^3_2,
 \dots, S^3_6$ be the six 3-spheres. The space $X$ is constructed as follows:

- Each $S^3_i$ touches $M$ at a single point $t_i \in M \cap S^3_i$ (the natural
 meaning of "touches").
- Six line segments (arcs) $e_1, \dots, e_6$ connect the spheres in a cyclic ord
er: $e_i$ connects a point $b_i \in S^3_i$ to a point $a_{i+1} \in S^3_{i+1}$ (i
ndices mod 6, so $e_6$ connects $S^3_6$ to $S^3_1$).

**Key facts.**
- Each $S^3_i$ is simply connected: $\pi_1(S^3_i) = 1$.
- Each line segment $e_i \cong [0,1]$ is contractible: $\pi_1(e_i) = 1$.
- Each touching point $t_i$ is a point: $\pi_1(\{t_i\}) = 1$.
- $M$ is a 3-manifold with fundamental group $\pi_1(M)$.

**Graph-of-spaces decomposition.** The space $X$ decomposes as a graph of spaces
:

- **Vertices (7):** $M, S^3_1, \dots, S^3_6$, with vertex groups $G_M = \pi_1(M)
$ and $G_{S^3_i} = 1$.
- **Edges (12):** Six "touching" edges connecting each $S^3_i$ to $M$ (edge spac
e = a point, simply connected), and six "line segment" edges connecting $S^3_i$
to $S^3_{i+1}$ (edge space = an arc, simply connected).

All edge spaces are simply connected, so all edge groups are trivial.

**Applying the Seifert–van Kampen theorem for graphs of spaces.** When a graph o
f spaces has all edge groups trivial, the fundamental group of the total space i
s:

$$\pi_1(X) \cong \left(\ast_{v \in V} G_v\right) \ast F_r$$

where $|V| = 7$ is the number of vertices, $|E| = 12$ is the number of edges, an
d $r = |E| - |V| + 1 = 12 - 7 + 1 = 6$ is the rank of the free group (the number
 of independent cycles in the underlying graph).

**Verification of $r = 6$ via the underlying graph.** The underlying graph has 7
 vertices and 12 edges. A connected graph with $V$ vertices and $E$ edges has $E
 - V + 1$ independent cycles. Here: $12 - 7 + 1 = 6$. The six independent cycles
 correspond to the six line segments: each line segment $e_i$ creates a new loop
 (travel from $M$ to $S^3_i$ through the touching point, along $e_i$ to $S^3_{i+
1}$, and back to $M$ through its touching point). The cycle of all six segments
together is the product of these six generators, not an additional independent r
elation—since the spheres are simply connected, no 2-dimensional relations are i
ntroduced.

**Computing the free product.** Substituting the vertex groups:

$$\pi_1(X) \cong \pi_1(M) * \underbrace{1 * \cdots * 1}_{6} * F_6 = \pi_1(M) * F
_6.$$

**Conclusion.** The fundamental group of the space is:

$$\boxed{\pi_1(X) \cong \pi_1(M) * F_6}$$

where $M$ is the common 3-manifold and $F_6$ is the free group on six generators
 (one generator for each line segment in the cycle).

**Special case.** If the common 3-manifold $M$ is simply connected (e.g., $M = S
^3$, $M = B^3$, or $M = \mathbb{R}^3$), then $\pi_1(M) = 1$ and the fundamental
group reduces to $F_6$, the free group of rank 6.
