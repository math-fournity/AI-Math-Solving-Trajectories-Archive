## Solution

We solve the Dirichlet problem for Laplace's equation in the unit ball with axis
ymmetric boundary data $u|_{r=1} = 3\cos^2\vartheta$.

### Step 1: Expand the boundary data in Legendre polynomials

Recall the Legendre polynomials:
$$P_0(\cos\vartheta) = 1, \qquad P_2(\cos\vartheta) = \tfrac{1}{2}(3\cos^2\varth
eta - 1).$$

Solving for $\cos^2\vartheta$:
$$\cos^2\vartheta = \frac{2P_2(\cos\vartheta) + 1}{3}.$$

Therefore:
$$3\cos^2\vartheta = 2P_2(\cos\vartheta) + P_0(\cos\vartheta).$$

### Step 2: General axisymmetric harmonic expansion

Since the boundary data is independent of the azimuthal angle $\varphi$, the sol
ution is axisymmetric. The general harmonic function in the ball that is regular
 at the origin and axisymmetric has the expansion:
$$u(r,\vartheta) = \sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty} A_\ell\, r^\ell\, P_\ell(\cos\vartheta
),$$
where each term $r^\ell P_\ell(\cos\vartheta)$ is harmonic (it satisfies $\Delta
 u = 0$ in $r<1$), being a solid spherical harmonic of degree $\ell$.

### Step 3: Match boundary conditions

Imposing $u(1,\vartheta) = 3\cos^2\vartheta$:
$$\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty} A_\ell\, P_\ell(\cos\vartheta) = P_0(\cos\vartheta) + 2
\,P_2(\cos\vartheta).$$

By the linear independence (orthogonality) of the Legendre polynomials:
$$A_0 = 1, \qquad A_2 = 2, \qquad A_\ell = 0 \text{ for all other } \ell.$$

### Step 4: Write the solution

$$u(r,\vartheta) = 1 + 2\,r^2\,P_2(\cos\vartheta) = 1 + r^2(3\cos^2\vartheta - 1
).$$

### Verification

**Boundary condition:** At $r=1$:
$$u(1,\vartheta) = 1 + (3\cos^2\vartheta - 1) = 3\cos^2\vartheta. \quad\checkmar
k$$

**Harmonicity:** Each term is a solid spherical harmonic:
- The constant $1 = r^0 P_0$ is harmonic.
- $r^2 P_2(\cos\vartheta)$ is a solid spherical harmonic of degree $2$, hence $\
Delta(r^2 P_2) = 0$.

Explicitly, in spherical coordinates for an axisymmetric function:
$$\Delta u = \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left(r^2 \frac{\partial
u}{\partial r}\right) + \frac{1}{r^2\sin\vartheta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varth
eta}\!\left(\sin\vartheta\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial\vartheta}\right).$$

For $u = 1 + r^2(3\cos^2\vartheta - 1)$:
- Radial part: $\frac{\partial u}{\partial r} = 2r(3\cos^2\vartheta-1)$, so $\fr
ac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}(r^2 \cdot 2r(3\cos^2\vartheta-1)) = \frac{
1}{r^2}\cdot 8r^3(3\cos^2\vartheta-1)/... $

Let me compute carefully:
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^2 \cdot 2r(3\cos^2\vartheta-1)\right) = \fr
ac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(2r^3(3\cos^2\vartheta-1)\right) = 6r^2(3\cos^2\var
theta-1),$$
$$\frac{1}{r^2}\cdot 6r^2(3\cos^2\vartheta-1) = 6(3\cos^2\vartheta-1).$$

Angular part: with $\mu = \cos\vartheta$, $3\cos^2\vartheta - 1 = 3\mu^2 - 1 = 2
P_2(\mu)$. The angular Laplacian gives:
$$\frac{1}{r^2}\cdot(-\ell(\ell+1))\cdot r^\ell P_\ell = \frac{-2\cdot 3}{r^2}\c
dot r^2 P_2 = -6(3\cos^2\vartheta-1).$$

Sum: $6(3\cos^2\vartheta-1) - 6(3\cos^2\vartheta-1) = 0$. $\quad\checkmark$

**Regularity:** $u$ is smooth at the origin (no negative powers of $r$). $\quad\
checkmark$

### Final Answer

$$\boxed{u(r,\vartheta) = 1 + r^2\!\left(3\cos^2\vartheta - 1\right)}$$
