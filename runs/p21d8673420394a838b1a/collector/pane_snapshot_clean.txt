## Solution

**Setup.** The distribution $P$ places mass only at $k=0$ and $k=n$:
$$P(0)=p,\quad P(n)=1-p,\quad P(k)=0\text{ for }k\neq 0,n.$$
The binomial distribution is $Q(k)=\binom{n}{k}q^{k}(1-q)^{n-k}$, giving $Q(0)=(
1-q)^n$ and $Q(n)=q^n$.

**Reducing the KL divergence.** Using the convention $0\log 0 = 0$, only the two
 nonzero terms of $P$ contribute:
$$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|Q)=p\log\frac{p}{(1-q)^n}+(1-p)\log\frac{1-p}{q^n}.$$

Expanding,
$$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|Q)=\underbrace{p\log p+(1-p)\log(1-p)}_{\text{constant in }
q}\;-\;n\,p\log(1-q)\;-\;n(1-p)\log q.$$

So minimizing over $q\in(0,1)$ is equivalent to minimizing
$$f(q)=-n\,p\log(1-q)-n(1-p)\log q.$$

**First-order condition.** Differentiating,
$$f'(q)=\frac{np}{1-q}-\frac{n(1-p)}{q}.$$
Setting $f'(q)=0$:
$$\frac{p}{1-q}=\frac{1-p}{q}\;\Longrightarrow\;pq=(1-p)(1-q)\;\Longrightarrow\;
pq=1-q-p+pq\;\Longrightarrow\;q=1-p.$$

**Second-order check.**
$$f''(q)=\frac{np}{(1-q)^2}+\frac{n(1-p)}{q^2}>0,$$
so the critical point is a strict global minimum on $(0,1)$.

**Interpretation (sanity check).** At $q=1-p$: the mean of $Q$ is $nq=n(1-p)$, w
hich equals the mean of $P$, namely $0\cdot p+n(1-p)=n(1-p)$. This is the expect
ed moment-matching property of the information projection onto an exponential fa
mily.

**Answer.** The parameter minimizing $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|Q)$ is
$$\boxed{q=1-p}.$$
