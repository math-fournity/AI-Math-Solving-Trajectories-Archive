## Proof

**Claim.** For every positive integer \(N\), there exist infinitely many dissoci
ated sets of size \(N\) consisting only of even positive integers.

(Recall: a set is *dissociated* if all its subset sums are pairwise distinct, i.
e. no two distinct subsets have the same sum.)

---

### Step 1. A base dissociated set of even integers.

Consider

\[
S_0 \;=\; \{2,\, 4,\, 8,\, \dots,\, 2^{N}\} \;=\; \{2^{1},\, 2^{2},\, \dots,\, 2
^{N}\}.
\]

Every element is a positive even integer, and \(|S_0|=N\).

We show \(S_0\) is dissociated. Any subset sum has the form

\[
\sum_{i\in I} 2^{i}, \qquad I\subseteq\{1,2,\dots,N\}.
\]

By the **uniqueness of binary representation**, two distinct subsets \(I\neq J\)
 yield distinct sums \(\sum_{i\in I}2^{i}\neq\sum_{j\in J}2^{j}\). Hence all \(2
^{N}\) subset sums of \(S_0\) are pairwise distinct, so \(S_0\) is dissociated.

---

### Step 2. Scaling preserves dissociation.

For any positive integer \(m\), define

\[
S_m \;=\; m\cdot S_0 \;=\; \{2m,\; 4m,\; 8m,\; \dots,\; 2^{N}m\}.
\]

**Each \(S_m\) is dissociated.** Suppose two subsets \(A,B\subseteq S_m\) have t
he same sum. Writing \(A=m\cdot A'\) and \(B=m\cdot B'\) for subsets \(A',B'\sub
seteq S_0\), we get

\[
m\sum_{a'\in A'}a' \;=\; m\sum_{b'\in B'}b' \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \sum_{a'\in
A'}a'=\sum_{b'\in B'}b',
\]

which forces \(A'=B'\) (since \(S_0\) is dissociated), hence \(A=B\). So \(S_m\)
 is dissociated.

**Each \(S_m\) consists of even positive integers.** Every element is \(2^{i}\cd
ot m\) with \(i\ge 1\), so each is divisible by 2 and positive.

---

### Step 3. The sets are pairwise distinct and infinitely many.

For distinct positive integers \(m\neq m'\), the smallest elements differ:

\[
\min S_m = 2m \;\neq\; 2m' = \min S_{m'},
\]

so \(S_m\neq S_{m'}\). Since there are infinitely many choices of \(m\in\mathbb{
Z}_{>0}\), we obtain **infinitely many** distinct dissociated sets of size \(N\)
, each consisting solely of even positive integers.

---

### Conclusion

For every positive integer \(N\), the family \(\{S_m : m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\}\) p
rovides infinitely many dissociated sets of size \(N\) of even positive integers
. The answer is **yes**.
