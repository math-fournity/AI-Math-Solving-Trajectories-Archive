**Key observation.** Define $g(t) = e^t + t$. Since $g'(t) = e^t + 1 > 0$, the m
ap $g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ is strictly increasing and onto (with $\lim_{t\to
-\infty}g(t)=-\infty$, $\lim_{t\to+\infty}g(t)=+\infty$). Hence $g$ has a contin
uous, strictly increasing inverse $h = g^{-1}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$, characte
rized by
$$e^{h(y)} + h(y) = y, \qquad \forall\, y\in\mathbb{R}.$$

**Reformulating the constraint.** The condition $e^{f(x)} + f(x) \geq x+1$ is ex
actly $g(f(x)) \geq x+1$. Since $g$ is strictly increasing, this is equivalent t
o
$$f(x) \geq h(x+1), \qquad \forall\, x\in\mathbb{R}.$$

**Pointwise minimizer.** The function $\varphi(x) := h(x+1)$ is continuous (comp
osition of continuous functions), and it satisfies $g(\varphi(x)) = x+1$, i.e. $
e^{\varphi(x)}+\varphi(x) = x+1 \geq x+1$. So $\varphi\in\mathcal{F}$, and $I(\v
arphi) = \int_0^e \varphi(x)\,dx$.

For any $f\in\mathcal{F}$, we have $f(x)\geq \varphi(x)$ pointwise on $[0,e]$, h
ence
$$I(f) = \int_0^e f(x)\,dx \geq \int_0^e \varphi(x)\,dx = I(\varphi).$$
Therefore $\min_{f\in\mathcal{F}} I(f) = I(\varphi)$, **provided** the infimum i
s attained — and it is, by $\varphi$ itself. It remains to compute $I(\varphi)$.

**Computing the integral.** Substitute $y = x+1$:
$$I(\varphi) = \int_0^e h(x+1)\,dx = \int_1^{e+1} h(y)\,dy.$$

Now substitute $y = g(u) = e^u + u$, so $dy = (e^u+1)\,du$ and $h(y) = u$. The e
ndpoints:
- $y=1 \iff e^u+u = 1 \iff u = 0$ (since $g(0)=1$),
- $y=e+1 \iff e^u+u = e+1 \iff u = 1$ (since $g(1)=e+1$).

Thus
$$I(\varphi) = \int_0^1 u\,(e^u + 1)\,du = \int_0^1 u\,e^u\,du + \int_0^1 u\,du.
$$

For the first term, integration by parts:
$$\int_0^1 u\,e^u\,du = \Big[u\,e^u\Big]_0^1 - \int_0^1 e^u\,du = e - (e-1) = 1.
$$

For the second:
$$\int_0^1 u\,du = \frac{1}{2}.$$

Hence
$$I(\varphi) = 1 + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2}.$$

**Conclusion.** The minimum is attained by the continuous function $f(x) = h(x+1
)$, where $h = (e^t+t)^{-1}$, and

$$\boxed{\min_{f\in\mathcal{F}} I(f) = \dfrac{3}{2}}.$$
