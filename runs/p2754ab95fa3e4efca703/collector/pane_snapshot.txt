**Solution**

Place the semicircle with diameter $AB$. All of $A, B, C, D$ lie on the semicirc
le, so $ABCD$ is a cyclic quadrilateral, and $\angle ACB = \angle ADB = 90^\circ
$ (angles in a semicircle).

**Key observation.** Since $D, C, E$ are collinear with $C$ between $D$ and $E$,
 the ray $\overrightarrow{DE}$ passes through $C$, hence
$$\angle BDE = \angle BDC.$$

**Using $BD = BE$.** Triangle $BDE$ is isosceles with $BD = BE$, so
$$\angle BDE = \angle BED = \angle BDC. \tag{∗}$$
Let $\gamma = \angle BDC$.

**Inscribed angle theorem.** Both $\angle BDC$ and $\angle BAC$ subtend chord $B
C$, and $A$ and $D$ lie on the same arc (the line $E$–$C$–$D$ enters the semicir
cle near $B$ and exits near $A$, so $D$ is on the $A$-side of chord $BC$). There
fore
$$\angle BAC = \angle BDC = \gamma.$$

**Using $\angle DAC = 27^\circ$.** We get
$$\angle DAB = \angle DAC + \angle CAB = 27^\circ + \gamma.$$

In right triangle $ABD$ ($\angle ADB = 90^\circ$):
$$\angle ABD = 90^\circ - (27^\circ + \gamma) = 63^\circ - \gamma.$$

**The angle at $B$ in $\triangle BDE$.** Since $E$ lies on the extension of $AB$
 beyond $B$, the ray $\overrightarrow{BE}$ is opposite to $\overrightarrow{BA}$,
 so
$$\angle DBE = 180^\circ - \angle ABD = 180^\circ - (63^\circ - \gamma) = 117^\c
irc + \gamma.$$

But from the isosceles triangle $BDE$,
$$\angle DBE = 180^\circ - 2\gamma.$$

Equating:
$$180^\circ - 2\gamma = 117^\circ + \gamma \implies 3\gamma = 63^\circ \implies
\gamma = 21^\circ.$$

**Finding $\alpha = \angle ACD$.** In right triangle $ABC$ ($\angle ACB = 90^\ci
rc$):
$$\angle ABC = 90^\circ - \angle BAC = 90^\circ - 21^\circ = 69^\circ.$$

Since $ABCD$ is cyclic, opposite angles sum to $180^\circ$:
$$\angle ADC = 180^\circ - \angle ABC = 180^\circ - 69^\circ = 111^\circ.$$

In triangle $ACD$:
$$\alpha = \angle ACD = 180^\circ - \angle CAD - \angle ADC = 180^\circ - 27^\ci
rc - 111^\circ = 42^\circ.$$

**Verification (coordinate check).** With $A=(-1,0)$, $B=(1,0)$ on the unit semi
circle: $\angle BAC = 21^\circ$ gives $C=(\cos 42^\circ, \sin 42^\circ)$, and $\
angle DAB = 48^\circ$ gives $D=(\cos 96^\circ, \sin 96^\circ)$. The line $CD$ me
ets the $x$-axis at $E \approx (2.486, 0)$, giving $BE \approx 1.486$ and $BD =
\sqrt{(\cos 96^\circ -1)^2 + \sin^2 96^\circ} \approx 1.486$. ✓

$$\boxed{\alpha = 42^\circ}$$
