## Proof

**Step 1: Identify the cylinder with $\mathbb{C}^*$.**

Consider the holomorphic map
$$\varphi: \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}^*, \qquad \varphi(z) = e^{2\pi i z}.$$
This map is surjective, and $\varphi(z) = \varphi(w)$ if and only if $e^{2\pi i(
z-w)} = 1$, i.e. $z - w \in \mathbb{Z}$. Since the equivalence relation on $\mat
hbb{C}$ is exactly $z \sim z+1$ (generating $z \sim z + n$ for all $n \in \mathb
b{Z}$), the map $\varphi$ descends to a well-defined bijection
$$\bar\varphi: X = \mathbb{C}/{\sim} \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; \mathbb{C}^* = \
mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}.$$
Both $\varphi$ and its local inverse are continuous (indeed holomorphic), so $\b
ar\varphi$ is a homeomorphism. Thus
$$X \cong \mathbb{C}^*.$$

**Step 2: Identify $X^*$ with the twice-punctured plane.**

Removing a point from $X$ corresponds, under the homeomorphism $\bar\varphi$, to
 removing a point $p \in \mathbb{C}^*$ from $\mathbb{C}^*$. Hence
$$X^* \cong \mathbb{C}^* \setminus \{p\} = \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0, p\},$$
the complex plane with two points removed.

**Step 3: Compute $\pi_1(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,p\})$.**

We show that $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,p\}$ deformation retracts onto a wedge of t

Choose $\varepsilon > 0$ small enough that the closed disks $\overline{D(0,\vare
psilon)}$ and $\overline{D(p,\varepsilon)}$ are disjoint. Let $a$ be a simple ar

on $\partial D(p,\varepsilon)$. Define the subspace
$$\Gamma = \partial D(0,\varepsilon) \;\cup\; \partial D(p,\varepsilon) \;\cup\;
 \text{(the connecting segment)},$$
which is a figure-eight (a wedge $S^1 \vee S^1$) after contracting the connectin

There is a strong deformation retraction of $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,p\}$ onto $\
Gamma$: every point outside the two punctured disks is pushed radially outward t

Therefore
$$\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,p\} \;\simeq\; S^1 \vee S^1.$$

**Step 4: Apply the Seifert–van Kampen theorem.**

The wedge $S^1 \vee S^1$ is covered by two open sets $U_1, U_2$, each a slightly

$$\pi_1(S^1 \vee S^1) \cong \pi_1(S^1) * \pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z

the free group on two generators. The two generators are represented by loops go
