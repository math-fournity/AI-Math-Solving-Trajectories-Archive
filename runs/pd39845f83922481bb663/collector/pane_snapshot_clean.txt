Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every maximal ideal $M$ of
$\mathbb{Z}[x]$ must intersect $\mathbb{Z}$ nontrivially.

**Answer: Yes.** Every maximal ideal $M$ of $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ satisfies $M \cap \m
athbb{Z} \neq (0)$.

## Proof

Suppose for contradiction that $M$ is a maximal ideal of $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ with $M
 \cap \mathbb{Z} = (0)$.

**Step 1: Localization to $\mathbb{Q}[x]$.** Let $S = \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}
$. Since $M \cap S = \varnothing$ and $M$ is prime (being maximal), the localiza
tion $S^{-1}M$ is a well-defined ideal of $S^{-1}\mathbb{Z}[x] = \mathbb{Q}[x]$.
 Since $M$ is maximal in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$, it is maximal among prime ideals disjo
int from $S$, so $S^{-1}M$ is maximal in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$. As $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ is
a PID, $S^{-1}M = (f)$ for some monic irreducible $f \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ of degre
e $n$.

**Step 2: Embedding into a number field.** Set $K = \mathbb{Q}[x]/(f)$, a degree
-$n$ extension of $\mathbb{Q}$. Since $M$ is prime and disjoint from $S$, we hav
e $M = S^{-1}M \cap \mathbb{Z}[x]$, so the natural map
$$\mathbb{Z}[x]/M \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Q}[x]/S^{-1}M = K$$
is injective.

**Step 3: The quotient is a field containing $\mathbb{Q}$.** Since $M$ is maxima
l, $F := \mathbb{Z}[x]/M$ is a field. Since $M \cap \mathbb{Z} = (0)$, the map $
\mathbb{Z} \to F$ is injective; as $F$ is a field, every nonzero integer is inve
rtible, so $\mathbb{Q} \subseteq F$.

**Step 4: $F = K$.** Let $\bar{\alpha}$ be the image of $x$ in $F$ (equivalently
, in $K$). The field $F$ is generated as a ring by $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\bar{\alpha
}$; since $\mathbb{Q} \subseteq F$, it is generated as a $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra by
 $\bar{\alpha}$ alone. As $\bar{\alpha}$ is a root of the irreducible $f$, we ge
t $\mathbb{Q}[\bar{\alpha}] = \mathbb{Q}(\bar{\alpha}) = K$. Hence $F = K$.

But $F$ is also the image of $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ in $K$ under $x \mapsto \bar{\alpha
}$, namely $\mathbb{Z}[\bar{\alpha}]$. So $\mathbb{Z}[\bar{\alpha}] = K$.

**Step 5: $\mathbb{Z}[\bar{\alpha}] \neq K$ (contradiction).** Write $f(x) = x^n
 + a_{n-1}x^{n-1} + \cdots + a_0$ with $a_i \in \mathbb{Q}$. Let $d > 0$ be a co
mmon denominator of the $a_i$, so that $\beta := d\bar{\alpha}$ is integral over
 $\mathbb{Z}$. Then
$$\mathbb{Z}[\bar{\alpha}] = \mathbb{Z}[\beta/d] = \mathbb{Z}[\beta][1/d].$$
Since $\beta$ is integral over $\mathbb{Z}$ of degree $n$, the ring $\mathbb{Z}[
\beta]$ is a free $\mathbb{Z}$-module with basis $\{1, \beta, \ldots, \beta^{n-1
}\}$.

Choose a prime $q \nmid d$. If $\mathbb{Z}[\bar{\alpha}] = K$, then $\tfrac{1}{q
} \in \mathbb{Z}[\beta][1/d]$, so $\tfrac{1}{q} = \tfrac{b}{d^k}$ for some $b \i
n \mathbb{Z}[\beta]$, $k \geq 0$. Writing $b = c_0 + c_1\beta + \cdots + c_{n-1}
\beta^{n-1}$ with $c_i \in \mathbb{Z}$:
$$d^k = q\,b = q\,c_0 + q\,c_1\beta + \cdots + q\,c_{n-1}\beta^{n-1}.$$
By $\mathbb{Q}$-linear independence of $\{1, \beta, \ldots, \beta^{n-1}\}$, we g
et $c_i = 0$ for $i \geq 1$ and $d^k = q\,c_0$. This forces $q \mid d^k$, hence
$q \mid d$—contradicting the choice of $q$.

Therefore $\mathbb{Z}[\bar{\alpha}] \neq K$, contradicting $F = K$.

**Conclusion.** The assumption $M \cap \mathbb{Z} = (0)$ is impossible. Since $M
 \cap \mathbb{Z}$ is a nonzero prime ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$, we conclude $M \cap
\mathbb{Z} = (p)$ for some prime $p$. $\blacksquare$
