**Problem.** $n$ players, round-robin, no ties. "$A$ not inferior to $B$" means
$A$'s defeated opponents are **not all** defeated by $B$. Find all $n\ge 3$ for
which some tournament outcome makes every player not inferior to every other.

---

### 1. Reformulation

For each player $A$ let $D(A)=\{$opponents defeated by $A\}$. The condition "$A$
 is not inferior to $B$" is $D(A)\not\subseteq D(B)$.

**Key observation.** If $A$ beats $B$, then $B\in D(A)$ but $B\notin D(B)$ (no o
ne plays themselves), so $D(A)\not\subseteq D(B)$ automatically. Hence the only
nontrivial constraints come from pairs where $A$ **loses** to $B$:

> For every directed edge $B\to A$ (i.e. $B$ beats $A$), we need $D(A)\not\subse
teq D(B)$, i.e. there exists $x\in D(A)$ with $x\notin D(B)$.

Since $x\in D(A)$ means $A\to x$, and $x\notin D(B)$ means $B$ did not beat $x$,
 i.e. $x\to B$ (as $x\ne B$: $x=B$ would give $A\to B$, contradicting $B\to A$).
 So:

$$\text{Every player not inferior to every other}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\t
extbf{every edge of the tournament lies in a directed 3-cycle.}$$

---

### 2. $n=4$ is impossible

In such a tournament no vertex may have out-degree $0$ (it beats nobody, so edge
s $X\to v$ cannot lie in a 3-cycle) or out-degree $3$ (edges $v\to X$ cannot lie
 in a 3-cycle since nobody beats $v$). With $n=4$, out-degrees lie in $\{1,2\}$
and sum to $\binom{4}{2}=6$, so the degree multiset is $\{1,1,2,2\}$.

Let $v$ have out-degree $1$, beating only $w$, and losing to $a,b$. For edge $a\
to v$ to lie in a 3-cycle we need $x$ with $v\to x$ and $x\to a$; since $v$ beat
s only $w$, we need $w\to a$. Similarly edge $b\to v$ forces $w\to b$. Thus $w$
beats both $a,b$ (and loses to $v$), so $\deg^+(w)=2$.

Now between $a,b$ one beats the other, say $a\to b$. For edge $a\to b$ to lie in
 a 3-cycle we need $x$ with $b\to x$ and $x\to a$. But $b$'s out-neighbors: $b\t
o v$ (given), $b$ loses to $w$ ($w\to b$) and to $a$ ($a\to b$), so $b$ beats on
ly $v$, i.e. $\deg^+(b)=1$, forcing $x=v$. Yet $v\to a$ is false ($a\to v$). Con
tradiction. The case $b\to a$ is symmetric. So **no tournament on $4$ vertices w
orks**.

---

### 3. $n=3$ works

The directed 3-cycle $1\to 2\to 3\to 1$: every edge is the 3-cycle itself.

---

### 4. Odd $n\ge 5$ works

Use the **regular cyclic tournament**: vertices $0,1,\dots,n-1$, and $i\to j$ if
f $(j-i)\bmod n\in\{1,2,\dots,\tfrac{n-1}{2}\}$.

Take any edge $i\to j$; let $d=(j-i)\bmod n\in\{1,\dots,\tfrac{n-1}{2}\}$. Set $
x=j+\tfrac{n-1}{2}\pmod n$. Then
$$(x-j)\bmod n=\tfrac{n-1}{2}\in\{1,\dots,\tfrac{n-1}{2}\},\quad\text{so }j\to x
,$$
$$(i-x)\bmod n=\tfrac{n+1}{2}-d\in\{1,\dots,\tfrac{n-1}{2}\},\quad\text{so }x\to
 i.$$
Thus $i\to j\to x\to i$ is a directed 3-cycle. Every edge lies in a 3-cycle.

---

### 5. Even $n\ge 6$ works

Write $n=2k+2$ with $k\ge 2$. Start with the regular cyclic tournament $T$ on th
e $n-1=2k+1$ vertices $\{0,1,\dots,2k\}$ (odd case, already good). Add a new ver
tex $v$. Let

$$S=\{\text{even vertices }0,2,4,\dots,2k\},\qquad L=\{\text{odd vertices }1,3,\
dots,2k-1\}.$$

Set $v\to s$ for $s\in S$ and $\ell\to v$ for $\ell\in L$. The 3-cycles entirely
 inside $T$ are unaffected, so we only check edges incident to $v$.

- **Edge $v\to s$ ($s\in S$):** need $x\in L$ with $s\to x$ in $T$. For $s=2j$ (
$0\le j\le k$), vertex $2j$ beats $2j+1$ (distance $1\le k$), and $2j+1\in L$ (f
or $j\le k-1$). For $j=k$, $s=2k$ beats $0,1,\dots,k-1$ (wrapping), and $1\in L$
 (since $k\ge2$). So every $s\in S$ beats some vertex of $L$. ✓
- **Edge $\ell\to v$ ($\ell\in L$):** need $x\in S$ with $x\to \ell$ in $T$. For
 $\ell=2j+1$ ($0\le j\le k-1$), vertex $2j$ beats $2j+1$ (distance $1$), and $2j
\in S$. ✓

So every edge incident to $v$ lies in a 3-cycle, and all old edges already did.
Hence the tournament on $n$ vertices has every edge in a 3-cycle.

---

### 6. Conclusion

The constructions cover $n=3$, all odd $n\ge 5$, and all even $n\ge 6$; $n=4$ is
 impossible. Therefore the possible values of $n$ are exactly

$$\boxed{\,n\ge 3,\ n\ne 4\,}.$$
