Looking at this problem, I need to determine all $a \in \mathbb{R}$ such that $f

## Setup and Reduction

The domain is $D = \{(x,y,z) : 1 < x^2 + y^2 + z < 2\}$. For each $(x,y) \in \ma
thbb{R}^2$, $z$ ranges over the interval $(1 - x^2 - y^2,\; 2 - x^2 - y^2)$, whi
ch has length 1 and is always non-empty. Thus $(x,y)$ ranges over all of $\mathb
b{R}^2$.

Since $\arctan(|x|+|y|) \geq 0$, $e^z > 0$, and $n^4 + 4x^2 + y^2 > 0$, the func
tion $f_n$ is non-negative (for $|x|+|y|>0$), so $L^1$-membership is equivalent
to finiteness of the integral.

**Integrating over $z$:**
$$\int_{1-x^2-y^2}^{2-x^2-y^2} e^z\, dz = e^{2-x^2-y^2} - e^{1-x^2-y^2} = (e-1)\
,e^{1-x^2-y^2}.$$

So the integral becomes:
$$I_n = (e-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^2} \frac{\left(\arctan(|x|+|y|)\right)^a \, e^{1-x
^2-y^2}}{n^4 + 4x^2 + y^2}\, dx\, dy.$$

## Analysis of Convergence

**At infinity:** As $r = \sqrt{x^2+y^2} \to \infty$, the factor $e^{1-x^2-y^2} =
 e^{1-r^2}$ decays Gaussian (super-exponentially), while $\arctan(|x|+|y|)^a \to
 (\pi/2)^a$ (bounded) and $n^4+4x^2+y^2 \geq n^4 > 0$. So the integrand is domin
ated by $C\, e^{-r^2}$, which is integrable over $\mathbb{R}^2$ for **all** $a \
in \mathbb{R}$. No issue at infinity.

**Near the origin $(x,y) \to (0,0)$:** Here:
- $\arctan(|x|+|y|) \sim |x|+|y| \sim r$ (since $\arctan t \sim t$ as $t \to 0$)
,
- $e^{1-x^2-y^2} \to e$ (bounded above and below by positive constants),
- $n^4 + 4x^2 + y^2 \to n^4$ (bounded above and below by positive constants, sin
ce $n \geq 1$).

So near the origin, the integrand is comparable to:
$$\frac{r^a \cdot e}{n^4} \sim C\, r^a.$$

In polar coordinates ($dx\,dy = r\,dr\,d\theta$), the integral near the origin b
ehaves as:
$$\int_0^{\varepsilon} r^a \cdot r\, dr = \int_0^{\varepsilon} r^{a+1}\, dr.$$

This converges if and only if $a + 1 > -1$, i.e., **$a > -2$**.

## Rigorous Verification

**Case $a > -2$:** Choose $\varepsilon > 0$ small. On $B(0,\varepsilon)$, the in
tegrand is $\leq C_1\, r^a$ with $a > -2$, which is integrable in 2D. On $\mathb
b{R}^2 \setminus B(0,\varepsilon)$, $\arctan(|x|+|y|) \geq \arctan(\varepsilon)
> 0$ so $\arctan^a$ is bounded (for any $a$), and $e^{1-r^2}/(n^4+4x^2+y^2) \leq
 e \cdot e^{-r^2}/n^4$ is integrable. Thus $I_n < \infty$ for all $n \geq 1$.

**Case $a \leq -2$:** On a small ball $B(0,\varepsilon)$, the integrand is $\geq
 C_2\, r^a$ (with $C_2 > 0$, using the lower bounds $e^{1-r^2} \geq e^{1-\vareps
ilon^2}$ and $n^4+4x^2+y^2 \leq n^4 + 4\varepsilon^2$). Then:
$$I_n \geq C_2 \int_0^{\varepsilon} r^{a+1}\, dr = \infty \quad \text{when } a \
leq -2.$$

This holds for **every** $n \geq 1$ since the denominator is bounded above near
the origin. So $f_n \notin L^1(D)$ for all $n$.

## Conclusion

The function $f_n \in L^1(D)$ for all $n \geq 1$ if and only if $a > -2$.

$$\boxed{a \in (-2, +\infty)}$$
