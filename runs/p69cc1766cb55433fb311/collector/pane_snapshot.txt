**Answer: Yes, P must be the zero polynomial.**

## Proof

Let $P \in \mathbb{R}[X,Y,Z]$ vanish on $\mathcal{S}$, where $\mathcal{S}$ has c
overing radius $1$ (every point of $\mathbb{R}^3$ is within distance $1$ of $\ma
thcal{S}$). Since $\mathcal{S}\subseteq Z(P)$, the algebraic set $Z(P)$ also has
 covering radius $\le 1$:

$$\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^3,\quad \operatorname{dist}(x,Z(P))\le 1. \tag{$\star$}
$$

We show $P\equiv 0$.

**Case $d=0$.** Then $P$ is constant. Since $\mathcal{S}$ is nonempty (it covers
 $\mathbb{R}^3$), $P$ vanishing on $\mathcal{S}$ forces $P\equiv 0$.

**Case $d\ge 1$.** Suppose for contradiction $P\neq 0$, and let $d=\deg P\ge 1$.
 Let $P_d$ be the top-degree homogeneous part; $P_d$ is a nonzero homogeneous po
lynomial of degree $d$, so there exists a unit vector $v\in\mathbb{R}^3$ with $P
_d(v)\neq 0$.

Restrict $P$ to the line $t\mapsto tv$. Along this line,
$$P(tv)=P_d(v)\,t^{d}+O(t^{d-1})\quad\text{as }|t|\to\infty,$$
so there exist $T_0,c_0>0$ with
$$|P(tv)|\ge c_0\,|t|^{d}\qquad\text{for }|t|\ge T_0. \tag{1}$$

By $(\star)$, for every $t\in\mathbb{R}$ there is a point $z_t\in Z(P)$ with $|t
v-z_t|\le 1$. Since $P(z_t)=0$, the mean-value theorem for the scalar function $
P$ gives a point $\xi_t$ on the segment $[tv,z_t]$ such that
$$P(tv)-P(z_t)=\nabla P(\xi_t)\cdot(tv-z_t).$$
Hence, using $|tv-z_t|\le 1$ and $|\xi_t|\le |tv|+1=|t|+1$,
$$|P(tv)|\le |\nabla P(\xi_t)|\cdot|tv-z_t|\le |\nabla P(\xi_t)|. \tag{2}$$

The gradient $\nabla P$ is a polynomial of degree $d-1$, so its growth is at mos
t $O(|\xi_t|^{d-1})$. Concretely, there is a constant $C>0$ with
$$|\nabla P(\xi_t)|\le C\,(|t|+1)^{d-1}\le C'\,|t|^{d-1}\qquad\text{for }|t|\ge
1 \tag{3}$$
(for a suitable constant $C'$).

Combining (1), (2), (3), for all $|t|\ge \max(T_0,1)$:
$$c_0\,|t|^{d}\le C'\,|t|^{d-1}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad |t|\le \frac{C'}{c_0}.$
$

This is a contradiction once $|t|>\max\!\big(T_0,1,\,C'/c_0\big)$. Therefore the
 assumption $P\neq 0$ is false.

Hence $P\equiv 0$. $\blacksquare$

### PROOF COMPLETE
