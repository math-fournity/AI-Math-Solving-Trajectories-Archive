## Proof

**Step 1: Determine P(0).**

Set \(x = y = z = 0\). The constraint \(xy + yz + zx = 0\) is satisfied. The equ
ation becomes
\[
3P(0) = 18P(0),
\]
so \(15P(0) = 0\), hence
\[
P(0) = 0.
\]

**Step 2: Derive a functional identity from a special substitution.**

Set \(x = 0,\; y = 0,\; z = t\) for arbitrary real \(t\). The constraint \(xy +
yz + zx = 0\) is satisfied. Then:
- \((x-y)^2 = 0\), \((y-z)^2 = t^2\), \((z-x)^2 = t^2\);
- \(x + y + z = t\), so \(\left(\frac{(x+y+z)^2}{3}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{t^2}{
3}\right)^2 = \frac{t^4}{9}\).

The equation gives
\[
P(0) + 2P(t^2) = 18P\!\left(\frac{t^4}{9}\right).
\]
Using \(P(0) = 0\):
\[
P(t^2) = 9P\!\left(\frac{t^4}{9}\right) \quad \text{for all } t \in \mathbb{R}.
\tag{1}
\]

**Step 3: Apply a degree argument.**

Let \(P\) have degree \(d\) with leading coefficient \(a_d \neq 0\) (assuming \(
P \not\equiv 0\)). As a polynomial identity in \(t\):

- The left-hand side \(P(t^2)\) has degree \(2d\), with leading term \(a_d\, t^{
2d}\).
- The right-hand side \(9P(t^4/9)\) has degree \(4d\), with leading term
\[
9 \cdot a_d \cdot \left(\frac{t^4}{9}\right)^d = \frac{a_d}{9^{d-1}}\, t^{4d}.
\]

For (1) to hold as a polynomial identity, the degrees must match:
\[
2d = 4d \implies d = 0.
\]

So \(P\) must be constant. Combined with \(P(0) = 0\), we get \(P \equiv 0\).

**Step 4: Verification.**

\(P(x) \equiv 0\) trivially satisfies the original equation, since both sides eq
ual \(0\) for all \(x, y, z\).

**Conclusion.**

The only polynomial satisfying the given condition is
\[
\boxed{P(x) = 0.}
\]
