# Proof

## Interpreting the Question

The question asks whether consecutive blocks of digits extracted from the decima
l expansion of an algebraic irrational number form a "sequence of random numbers
." The natural mathematical formalization of "random digits" is **normality**: a
 real number $x$ is *normal in base $b$* if every block of $k$ consecutive digit
s (in base $b$) appears in the expansion of $x$ with asymptotic frequency $b^{-k
}$, for every $k \geq 1$. A number is *absolutely normal* if it is normal in eve
ry integer base $b \geq 2$.

If a number is normal in base 10, then consecutive blocks of length $k$ are equi
distributed over $\{0,1,\dots,9\}^k$, which is the precise sense in which they b
ehave like "random numbers."

## What Is Known

**Theorem (Borel, 1909).** Almost every real number (with respect to Lebesgue me
asure) is absolutely normal.

This means the set of non-normal numbers has Lebesgue measure zero. However, "al
most every" is an existence/measure statement — it does not identify any *specif
ic* number as normal.

**Key open problem.** For every specific algebraic irrational number $\alpha$ (s
uch as $\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}, \sqrt[3]{2}$, etc.), it is **unknown** whether $\alp
ha$ is normal in base 10, or in any other base.

This is one of the most famous open problems in number theory. Émile Borel himse
lf conjectured that every algebraic irrational is absolutely normal, but no proo
f exists.

## What Is Proven

1. **Rational numbers are not normal** (their expansions are eventually periodic
, so they cannot be normal).

2. **There exist transcendental numbers that are not normal.** For example, Liou
ville's constant $L = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 10^{-n!} = 0.110001000000000000000001\
dots$ is transcendental but clearly not normal (its digits are almost all zeros)
.

3. **There exist explicitly constructed absolutely normal numbers** (e.g., by Si
erpiński, Lebesgue, and later Schmidt), but these are *artificially constructed*
 — they are not known to be algebraic.

4. **Bailey–Crandall (2001)** showed that certain explicit constants (involving
series like $\sum 1/b^n \cdot \text{poly}(n)$) are normal *if* a certain logarit
hmic irrationality condition holds, but this condition itself is unproven for al
gebraic irrationals.

5. For algebraic irrationals, we know the digit expansion is **non-periodic** (s
ince the number is irrational), and we know it is **not eventually periodic**, b
ut non-periodicity is far weaker than normality. A non-periodic sequence can be
highly non-random (e.g., the Champernowne-like constructions, or sequences with
long runs of a single digit).

## Why the Statement Cannot Be Affirmed

The statement "by choosing consecutive blocks of digits from the decimal represe
ntation of an algebraic irrational number, we form a sequence of random numbers"
 asserts that **every** algebraic irrational has a normal (or "random") decimal
expansion. This is:

- **Not proven.** No algebraic irrational has been proven normal in any base. Th
e conjecture that all algebraic irrationals are absolutely normal remains open s
ince Borel (1909).

- **Not known to be false either.** No algebraic irrational has been proven *non
-normal*. So we cannot exhibit a counterexample among algebraic numbers.

- **Not a consequence of any known theorem.** The algebraicity of $\alpha$ gives
 us Diophantine approximation properties (Roth's theorem, etc.), but none of the
se imply normality. Normality is a much finer property about digit statistics, a
nd current tools in transcendental number theory and Diophantine approximation d
o not reach it.

## Conclusion

The statement is **not established as true**. It is a famous open conjecture (Bo
rel's conjecture) that every algebraic irrational is absolutely normal. We can n
either prove it nor refute it with current mathematics. Therefore, the answer to
 "Is it true that..." is:

**No — it is not known to be true. It remains an open problem whether the decima
l digits of any given algebraic irrational (such as $\sqrt{2}$) form a normal, i
.e., "random," sequence. The conjecture that all algebraic irrationals are absol
utely normal has stood unproven since Borel posed it in 1909.**
