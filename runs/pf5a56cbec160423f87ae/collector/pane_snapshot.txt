## Proof

We claim that **no polynomial** $P(x)$ satisfies $P(n)=n!$ for every natural num
ber $n$.

Suppose, for contradiction, that such a polynomial $P(x)$ exists. Let $d\geq 0$
be its degree and $a\neq 0$ its leading coefficient, so

$$P(x)=a\,x^{d}+a_{d-1}x^{d-1}+\cdots+a_{1}x+a_{0}.$$

Dividing by $x^{d}$ and letting $x\to\infty$,

$$\frac{P(x)}{x^{d}}=a+\frac{a_{d-1}}{x}+\cdots+\frac{a_{0}}{x^{d}}\;\longrighta
rrow\; a\qquad(x\to\infty). \tag{1}$$

On the other hand, the hypothesis $P(n)=n!$ gives

$$\frac{P(n)}{n^{d}}=\frac{n!}{n^{d}}\qquad\text{for every natural }n. \tag{2}$$

We now show $\dfrac{n!}{n^{d}}\to\infty$. For $n\geq d+1$,

$$\frac{n!}{n^{d}}=\frac{n!}{n\cdot n\cdots n}=\frac{n!}{n^{d}}.$$

A clean way: for $n\geq 2d$, split the factors of $n!$ into the last $d$ factors
 and the rest:

$$n!=\Bigl(1\cdot 2\cdots (n-d)\Bigr)\cdot\Bigl((n-d+1)(n-d+2)\cdots n\Bigr).$$

Each of the last $d$ factors is at least $n-d+1\geq n/2$ (using $n\geq 2d$), so

$$n!\geq (n-d)!\cdot\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{d}\geq 1\cdot\left(\frac{n}{2}\rig
ht)^{d}=\frac{n^{d}}{2^{d}}.$$

Hence

$$\frac{n!}{n^{d}}\geq \frac{1}{2^{d}}\quad\text{for }n\geq 2d.$$

This lower bound alone is not enough; we need divergence. Sharper: for $n\geq 2d
$, the last $d$ factors are each $\geq n/2$, but the *remaining* $(n-d)$ factors
 include many large ones. In fact for $n\geq 2d$,

$$n!\geq \left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{d}\cdot\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{n-2d}\cdot\b
igl(\text{positive product}\bigr)\;\geq\;\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{n-d},$$

since at least $n-d$ of the factors $1,2,\dots,n$ are $\geq n/2$ (namely those $
\geq \lceil n/2\rceil$, and there are $\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1\geq n-d$ of them whe
n $n\geq 2d$). Therefore

$$\frac{n!}{n^{d}}\geq \frac{(n/2)^{\,n-d}}{n^{d}}=\frac{n^{\,n-2d}}{2^{\,n-d}}\
;\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\;\infty,$$

because $n^{\,n-2d}$ grows super-exponentially while $2^{\,n-d}$ grows only expo
nentially. Consequently

$$\frac{n!}{n^{d}}\to\infty. \tag{3}$$

Combining (1), (2), and (3):

$$a=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{P(n)}{n^{d}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n!}{n^{d}}=\infty
,$$

which is impossible, since $a$ is a finite real number (the leading coefficient
of a polynomial). This is a contradiction.

The contradiction shows that our assumption was false. Hence **there is no polyn
omial $P(x)$ with $P(n)=n!$ for every natural number $n$**.

---

**Conclusion.** The set of polynomials $P(x)$ satisfying $P(n)=n!$ for every nat
ural number $n$ is **empty**.
