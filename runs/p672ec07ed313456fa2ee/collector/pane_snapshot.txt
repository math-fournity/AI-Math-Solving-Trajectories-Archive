Yes, the statement is true. Here is the justification.

**Claim.** If $B$ is Hurwitz (all eigenvalues have strictly negative real part)
and $A$ is any real $n\times n$ matrix, then there exists $\phi>0$ such that $A+
\theta B$ is Hurwitz for all $\theta\ge \phi$.

**Proof.**

Write
$$A+\theta B \;=\; \theta\!\left(B+\tfrac{1}{\theta}A\right).$$
The eigenvalues of $A+\theta B$ are exactly $\theta$ times the eigenvalues of $B
+\tfrac{1}{\theta}A$. Hence it suffices to show that, for all sufficiently large
 $\theta$, every eigenvalue of $B+\tfrac{1}{\theta}A$ has strictly negative real
 part.

Since $B$ is Hurwitz, its $n$ eigenvalues $\mu_1,\dots,\mu_n$ (counted with alge
braic multiplicity) all satisfy
$$\operatorname{Re}(\mu_i)<0,\qquad i=1,\dots,n.$$
Set
$$\delta \;:=\; -\max_{1\le i\le n}\operatorname{Re}(\mu_i) \;>\; 0,$$
so that $\operatorname{Re}(\mu_i)\le -\delta$ for every $i$.

Eigenvalues depend continuously on the matrix entries: the spectrum, viewed as a
n unordered multiset, is a continuous function of the matrix in the Hausdorff/ma
tching sense. Concretely, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $\eta>0$ such t
hat whenever $\|E\|<\eta$, the eigenvalues of $B+E$ can be paired with those of
$B$ so that each pair is within distance $\varepsilon$.

Choose $\varepsilon = \delta/2 > 0$, and let $\eta>0$ be the corresponding thres
hold. Pick $\phi>0$ large enough that $\|\tfrac{1}{\theta}A\|<\eta$ for all $\th
eta\ge\phi$ (possible since $\|\tfrac{1}{\theta}A\|=\tfrac{1}{\theta}\|A\|\to 0$
).

Then for every $\theta\ge\phi$, the eigenvalues $\nu_1(\theta),\dots,\nu_n(\thet
a)$ of $B+\tfrac{1}{\theta}A$ can be paired with $\mu_1,\dots,\mu_n$ so that $|\
nu_i(\theta)-\mu_i|<\varepsilon=\delta/2$. In particular,
$$\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\nu_i(\theta)\bigr) \;\le\; \operatorname{Re}(\mu_i)+\t
frac{\delta}{2} \;\le\; -\delta+\tfrac{\delta}{2} \;=\; -\tfrac{\delta}{2} \;<\;
 0$$
for every $i$.

Therefore every eigenvalue of $B+\tfrac{1}{\theta}A$ has real part $\le -\delta/
2<0$, and multiplying by $\theta>0$ preserves the sign of the real part. Thus ev
ery eigenvalue of $A+\theta B$ has strictly negative real part for all $\theta\g
e\phi$; i.e., $A+\theta B$ is Hurwitz.

**Remark on the role of "$A$ is non-Hurwitz".** The hypothesis that $A$ is non-H
urwitz is irrelevant to the conclusion: the argument above works for *any* real
$A$, Hurwitz or not. The point of the problem is simply that the Hurwitz propert
y of $B$ is an *open* condition stable under small perturbations, and $A/\theta\
to 0$, so for large $\theta$ the perturbation $A/\theta$ of $B$ is small enough
to remain inside the Hurwitz open set. The non-Hurwitz assumption on $A$ only ru
les out the trivial case where $A$ itself is already Hurwitz (in which case any
$\phi>0$ would do, including $\phi=0$); it does not obstruct the result.
