Looking at this problem, I need to find all positive integers $n$ such that ever
y $k$ with $1 \le k < n$ and $\gcd(k,n)=1$ is a prime power (including $1 = p^0$
).

## Key Characterization

A number is **not** a prime power (and not 1) iff it has at least 2 distinct pri
me factors. So the condition fails iff there exists $k$ with $1 < k < n$, $\gcd(
k,n)=1$, and $k$ has ≥ 2 distinct prime factors.

If $P$ = set of primes dividing $n$, and $Q$ = primes not in $P$, then a violati
ng $k$ must have all its prime factors in $Q$. The **smallest** such $k$ is $q_1
 \cdot q_2$, where $q_1 < q_2$ are the two smallest primes in $Q$.

**The condition is equivalent to:** $q_1 \cdot q_2 \ge n$.

- If $q_1 q_2 < n$: then $k = q_1 q_2$ is a counterexample (coprime to $n$, not
a prime power). ✗
- If $q_1 q_2 \ge n$: no violating $k$ exists. ✓
