Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $\sum_{k=1}^{l} h_k(x) f_k(
x) > 0$ whenever each $f_k(x) > 0$ for all $x \neq 0$, given the setup with the
partition of unity $h_k$ and the condition $\sum h_k g_k = 0$.

**Answer: Yes, it is true.**

**Proof:**

We are given:
- $h_k(x) > 0$ for all $x$ and all $k \in \{1, \ldots, l\}$,
- $\sum_{k=1}^{l} h_k(x) = 1$,
- $h_k$ are continuous,
- $\sum_{k=1}^{l} h_k(x)\, g_k(x) = 0$ for all $x \neq 0$, with $g_k$ polynomial
s.

We must show: if $f_k(x) > 0$ for all $x \neq 0$ and all $k$, then $\sum_{k=1}^{
l} h_k(x)\, f_k(x) > 0$ for all $x \neq 0$.

Fix any $x \neq 0$. By hypothesis, $h_k(x) > 0$ and $f_k(x) > 0$ for every $k$.
Since the product of two positive real numbers is positive, each term satisfies:
$$h_k(x)\, f_k(x) > 0 \quad \text{for each } k = 1, \ldots, l.$$

The sum of finitely many strictly positive real numbers is strictly positive, so
:
$$\sum_{k=1}^{l} h_k(x)\, f_k(x) > 0.$$

Since $x \neq 0$ was arbitrary, this holds for all $x \neq 0$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the role of the $g_k$ condition.** The constraint $\sum h_k g_k = 0$
 (with $h_k > 0$, $\sum h_k = 1$) tells us that for each $x \neq 0$, the value $
0$ lies in the strict convex hull of $\{g_1(x), \ldots, g_l(x)\}$, meaning the $
g_k(x)$ cannot all share the same sign. However, this condition plays **no role*
* in the conclusion about $f_k$. The positivity of $\sum h_k f_k$ follows entire
ly from the positivity of the weights $h_k$ and the positivity of the functions
$f_k$ — the weighted sum of positive quantities (with positive weights) is posit
ive, regardless of any additional algebraic constraints imposed by the $g_k$.
