## Proof

**Definitions.** A *value group* is an ordered abelian group $\Gamma$. A subgrou

 \in \Delta$ and all $\delta \in \Gamma$ with $0 \leq \delta \leq \gamma$, we ha
ve $\delta \in \Delta$. The *rank* of $\Gamma$ is the length of the chain of its
 isolated subgroups: if
$$\{0\} = \Gamma_0 \subsetneq \Gamma_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq \Gamma_n = \
Gamma,$$
then the rank is $n$ (the number of strict inclusions).

**Step 1: The isolated subgroups form a totally ordered chain under inclusion.**

Let $\Delta_1, \Delta_2$ be two isolated subgroups of $\Gamma$. We show $\Delta_
1 \subseteq \Delta_2$ or $\Delta_2 \subseteq \Delta_1$.

Suppose $\Delta_1 \not\subseteq \Delta_2$. Pick $\alpha \in \Delta_1 \setminus \
Delta_2$ with $\alpha > 0$ (replacing $\alpha$ by $-\alpha$ if necessary). We cl
aim $\Delta_2 \subseteq \Delta_1$.

For any $\beta \in \Delta_2$ with $\beta > 0$: if $\beta \geq \alpha$, then $0 <
 \alpha \leq \beta$ with $\beta \in \Delta_2$ implies $\alpha \in \Delta_2$ (con
vexity of $\Delta_2$), contradicting $\alpha \notin \Delta_2$. Hence $\beta < \a
lpha$. Then $0 < \beta < \alpha$ with $\alpha \in \Delta_1$ implies $\beta \in \
Delta_1$ (convexity of $\Delta_1$). For $\beta < 0$, we have $-\beta > 0$ and $-
\beta \in \Delta_2$, so $-\beta \in \Delta_1$, hence $\beta \in \Delta_1$. For $
\beta = 0$, trivially $\beta \in \Delta_1$.

Thus $\Delta_2 \subseteq \Delta_1$. $\square$

**Step 2: Count the isolated subgroups.**

By definition of rank $n$, the complete chain of isolated subgroups is:
$$\{0\} = \Gamma_0 \subsetneq \Gamma_1 \subsetneq \Gamma_2 \subsetneq \cdots \su
bsetneq \Gamma_n = \Gamma.$$

This chain contains exactly $n + 1$ subgroups: $\Gamma_0, \Gamma_1, \ldots, \Gam
ma_n$. By Step 1, every isolated subgroup must appear in this chain (since any t

**Conclusion.** The number of isolated subgroups of a value group with rank $n$

$$\boxed{n+1}$$
