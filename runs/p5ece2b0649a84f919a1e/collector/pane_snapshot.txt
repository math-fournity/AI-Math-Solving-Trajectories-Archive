Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether any integer polynomial $p(x
)$ can make $p(g(x))$ irreducible for every integer polynomial $g(x)$.

**Answer: No, such a polynomial does not exist.**

## Proof

We show that for every $p(x) \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$, there exists a non-constant $g(
x) \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ such that $p(g(x))$ is reducible in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$. We co
nsider all cases.

**Case 1: $\deg p = 0$.** Then $p$ is constant, so $p(g(x))$ is constant for any
 $g$—not irreducible.

**Case 2: $p$ is reducible over $\mathbb{Q}$.** Write $p(x) = f(x)\,h(x)$ with $
\deg f, \deg h \geq 1$. For any non-constant $g \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$, we get $p(g(
x)) = f(g(x))\,h(g(x))$ with $\deg f(g) = \deg f \cdot \deg g \geq 1$ and $\deg
h(g) = \deg h \cdot \deg g \geq 1$. So $p(g(x))$ is reducible.

**Case 3: $p(x) = ax + b$ with $a \neq 0$ (irreducible, degree 1).** Set $g(x) =
 ax^2 + (b+1)x \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$, which has degree 2. Then:
$$p(g(x)) = a\bigl(ax^2 + (b+1)x\bigr) + b = a^2 x^2 + a(b+1)x + b = (ax+1)(ax+b
).$$
Both factors have degree 1 (since $a \neq 0$), so $p(g(x))$ is reducible in $\ma
thbb{Q}[x]$.

**Case 4: $p$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ with $\deg p = d \geq 2$.** Let $
\alpha$ be a root of $p$ and set $K = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$. Define:
$$g(x) = x + x^2\, p(x) \in \mathbb{Z}[x].$$
Since $\deg p = d \geq 2$, we have $\deg g = d + 2 \geq 4$, so $g$ is non-consta
nt. Evaluating at $\alpha$:
$$g(\alpha) = \alpha + \alpha^2\, p(\alpha) = \alpha + 0 = \alpha.$$
Therefore $x - \alpha$ divides $g(x) - \alpha$ in $K[x]$. Since $\deg(g(x) - \al
pha) = d + 2 \geq 4 > 1$, the quotient has degree $\geq 3 \geq 1$, so $g(x) - \a
lpha$ is **reducible** over $K$.

**Applying Capelli's Theorem:** For $f, g \in F[x]$ with $\deg f, \deg g \geq 1$
, the composition $f(g(x))$ is irreducible over $F$ if and only if $f$ is irredu
cible over $F$ **and** $g(x) - \beta$ is irreducible over $F(\beta)$ for every r
oot $\beta$ of $f$.

Applying this with $f = p$, $g = g$, $F = \mathbb{Q}$: since $g(x) - \alpha$ is
reducible over $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ for the root $\alpha$ of $p$, the compositio
n $p(g(x))$ is **reducible** over $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Conclusion.** In every case—constant $p$, reducible $p$, linear irreducible $p
$, and irreducible $p$ of degree $\geq 2$—we have produced a non-constant $g \in
 \mathbb{Z}[x]$ making $p(g(x))$ reducible in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$. Therefore, no pol
ynomial $p(x) \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ satisfies the condition that $p(g(x))$ is irred
ucible for all $g(x) \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$. $\blacksquare$
