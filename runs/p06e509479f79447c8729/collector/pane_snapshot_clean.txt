## Solution

**Abscissa of convergence: $\sigma_c = 3$.**

### Setup

Write the series as a Dirichlet series in $n = a^2+b^2$:
$$L(f,s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{c_n}{n^s}, \qquad c_n = \sum_{\substack{(a,
b)\in\mathbb{Z}^2\\ a^2+b^2=n}} (a^4+b^4).$$

Let $S(N) = \sum_{n \le N} c_n = \sum_{a^2+b^2 \le N}(a^4+b^4)$ be the partial s
ums. The abscissa of convergence $\sigma_c$ is determined by the growth rate of
$S(N)$: if $S(N) \asymp N^\alpha$, then $\sigma_c = \alpha$.

### Growth estimate: $S(N) \asymp N^3$

**Upper bound.** Since $a^4+b^4 \le (a^2+b^2)^2 = n^2$ for each lattice point wi
th $a^2+b^2=n$,
$$S(N) \le \sum_{a^2+b^2\le N} (a^2+b^2)^2 \le N^2 \cdot \#\{(a,b): a^2+b^2\le N
\} \le N^2 \cdot \pi N = \pi N^3.$$

**Lower bound.** For lattice points with $a^2+b^2 = n$, we have $a^4+b^4 = (a^2+
b^2)^2 - 2a^2b^2 \ge \frac{1}{2}(a^2+b^2)^2 = \frac{n^2}{2}$ (using $2a^2b^2 \le
 (a^2+b^2)^2$). Restricting to the sector $0 \le b \le a$ with $a^2+b^2 \le N$ a
nd $a \ge \sqrt{N}/2$ (a positive-measure region containing $\asymp N$ lattice p
oints), each contributes $\ge \frac{1}{2}(a^2+b^2)^2 \ge \frac{1}{2}\cdot \frac{
N}{4} = \frac{N}{8}$... more cleanly: use the continuous comparison. The functio
n $g(x,y)=x^4+y^4$ is nonnegative and positive a.e. on the disk $B_{\sqrt{N}}$,
with
$$\int_{B_{\sqrt{N}}}(x^4+y^4)\,dA = \frac{3\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{(\sqrt{N})^6}{6} =
 \frac{\pi}{4}N^3.$$
Since $g$ is a polynomial (hence Riemann integrable with lattice sums asymptotic
 to the integral), $S(N) \sim \frac{\pi}{4}N^3$, giving $S(N) \asymp N^3$.

### Determining $\sigma_c$

By Abel summation, for real $s$:
$$\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{c_n}{n^s} = \frac{S(N)}{N^s} + s\int_1^{N}\frac{S(t)}{t^{s
+1}}\,dt.$$

Since $S(t)\asymp t^3$:

- **Convergence for $s > 3$:** The integral $\int_1^\infty \frac{S(t)}{t^{s+1}}\
,dt \asymp \int_1^\infty t^{2-s}\,dt$ converges when $2-s < -1$, i.e. $s > 3$. T
he boundary term $\frac{S(N)}{N^s}\asymp N^{3-s}\to 0$.

- **Divergence for $s = 3$:** $\sum_{n\le N}\frac{c_n}{n^3} = \frac{S(N)}{N^3} +
 3\int_1^N\frac{S(t)}{t^4}\,dt \asymp C + 3C'\int_1^N\frac{dt}{t} \to \infty.$

- **Divergence for $s < 3$:** The terms $c_n/n^s$ do not even tend to zero in ag
gregate (the partial sums grow like $N^{3-s}\to\infty$).

By analytic continuation theory for Dirichlet series, convergence in the half-pl
ane $\Re(s)>3$ and divergence on $\Re(s)<3$ (the coefficients $c_n\ge 0$ make th
e real axis the critical line) gives:

$$\boxed{\sigma_c = 3.}$$
