## Proof

Let \(F(t)=t^{5}-2013\). The object's trajectory is

\[
y=\Bigl(\bigl(\bigl((x^{5}-2013)^{5}-2013\bigr)^{5}-2013\bigr)^{5}\Bigr)=F^{4}(x
),
\]

where \(F^{4}\) denotes the 4‑fold iterate of \(F\) (the three "−2013" subtracti
ons give \(F^{3}\) applied to \(x^{5}\), and the outermost "\(^5\)" with no subt
raction makes the fourth iterate land as \(F^{4}(x)\); equivalently, setting \(b
_{0}=x,\ b_{k+1}=b_{k}^{5}-2013\), we have \(y=b_{4}\)).

The radar beam is the line \(y=x+2013\). Detection occurs exactly when a point o
f the trajectory lies on the beam, i.e.

\[
F^{4}(x)=x+2013 \iff F^{4}(x)-2013=x \iff F^{5}(x)=x.
\]

(Indeed \(F^{5}(x)=F(F^{4}(x))=F^{4}(x)^{5}-2013=(x+2013)-2013=x\).)

So we must count the real solutions of

\[
F^{5}(x)=x. \tag{1}
\]

---

**Key reduction.** The map \(F(t)=t^{5}-2013\) is *strictly increasing* on \(\ma
thbb{R}\), since \(t\mapsto t^{5}\) is strictly increasing.

> **Lemma.** If \(F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\) is strictly increasing, then every
 periodic point is a fixed point: \(F^{n}(x)=x \Rightarrow F(x)=x\).

*Proof of lemma.* Suppose \(F(x)>x\). Strict increase gives \(F^{2}(x)>F(x)\), a
nd inductively \(F^{n}(x)>F^{n-1}(x)>\cdots>F(x)>x\), contradicting \(F^{n}(x)=x
\). The case \(F(x)<x\) is symmetric. Hence \(F(x)=x\). ∎

Applying the lemma with \(n=5\), every solution of (1) satisfies

\[
F(x)=x \iff x^{5}-2013=x \iff x^{5}-x-2013=0. \tag{2}
\]

Conversely every solution of (2) clearly satisfies (1). So the number of detecti
ons equals the number of real roots of \(p(x)=x^{5}-x-2013\).

---

**Counting real roots of \(p(x)=x^{5}-x-2013\).**

\[
p'(x)=5x^{4}-1,\qquad p'(x)=0 \iff x=\pm c,\quad c=5^{-1/4}\approx 0.6687.
\]

Thus \(p\) is increasing on \((-\infty,-c)\), decreasing on \((-c,c)\), increasi
ng on \((c,\infty)\); \(-c\) is a local maximum and \(c\) a local minimum. Their
 values are

\[
p(-c)=(-c)^{5}-(-c)-2013=-\tfrac{c}{5}+c-2013=\tfrac{4c}{5}-2013<0,
\]
\[
p(c)=c^{5}-c-2013=\tfrac{c}{5}-c-2013=-\tfrac{4c}{5}-2013<0,
\]

since \(\tfrac{4c}{5}\approx 0.535\ll 2013\). Both extrema are negative. Moreove
r \(p(x)\to-\infty\) as \(x\to-\infty\) and \(p(x)\to+\infty\) as \(x\to+\infty\
).

Hence \(p\) is negative on \((-\infty,c]\) (its maximum there is \(<0\)), and th
en increases strictly on \([c,\infty)\) from a negative value to \(+\infty\), cr
ossing the axis **exactly once**. Therefore \(p(x)=0\) has exactly **one** real
root.

---

**Conclusion.** There is exactly one real value of \(x\) at which the trajectory
 meets the beam \(y=x+2013\), hence exactly one unit square of the coordinate gr
id in which the radar detects the object.
