## Answer

**Yes**, such a finite-index subgroup $H$ exists.

### Proof

Since $K \subseteq X$ is $G$-invariant and finite, every $g \in G$ permutes the
elements of $K$. This yields a group homomorphism (the permutation representatio
n of $G$ on $K$):

$$\rho : G \longrightarrow \mathrm{Sym}(K), \qquad \rho(g)(y) = \varphi(g, y) \;
\text{ for } y \in K.$$

That $\rho$ is a homomorphism follows from the action axiom: $\rho(g_1 g_2)(y) =
 \varphi(g_1 g_2, y) = \varphi(g_1, \varphi(g_2, y)) = \rho(g_1)(\rho(g_2)(y))$.

Since $K$ is finite, $\mathrm{Sym}(K)$ is a finite group (of order $|K|!$). Ther
efore the kernel

$$H := \ker(\rho) = \{g \in G : \varphi(g, y) = y \text{ for all } y \in K\}$$

is a normal subgroup of $G$ with finite index, because $G/H \cong \rho(G) \leq \
mathrm{Sym}(K)$ is finite.

By definition of $H$, every $h \in H$ fixes every point of $K$. Since $x \in K$,
 we have in particular

$$\varphi(h, x) = x \quad \text{for all } h \in H.$$

Thus $x$ is a fixed point of the induced action $\varphi: H \times X \to X$, and
 $H$ has finite index in $G$. $\blacksquare$
