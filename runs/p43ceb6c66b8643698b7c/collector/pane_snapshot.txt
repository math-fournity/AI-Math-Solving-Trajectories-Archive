Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there exists a non-zero, po
sitive, norm-decreasing linear operator on $L^1(\mu)$ (with $\Omega$ unbounded)
that is not bounded below by any $\alpha \in (0,1]$.

**Answer: Yes, such an operator exists.**

**Proof.**

Take $\Omega = \mathbb{R}^n$ equipped with Lebesgue measure $\mu$ (the same cons
truction works for any unbounded $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ with Lebesgue m
easure, provided $\Omega$ has positive measure arbitrarily far from the origin).

Define the **multiplication operator**:
$$Pf(x) = e^{-\|x\|}\, f(x), \qquad f \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^n).$$

We verify all required properties.

**1. $P$ is linear and non-zero.** Linearity is immediate from the definition. $
P$ is non-zero because, for instance, $P(\mathbf{1}_{[0,1]^n})(x) = e^{-\|x\|}\m
athbf{1}_{[0,1]^n}(x) \neq 0$ in $L^1$.

**2. $P$ preserves positivity.** If $f \geq 0$ a.e., then $e^{-\|x\|} f(x) \geq
0$ a.e., so $Pf \geq 0$.

**3. $P$ is norm-decreasing.** Since $0 < e^{-\|x\|} \leq 1$ for all $x$:
$$\|Pf\|_1 = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} e^{-\|x\|}\,|f(x)|\,dx \leq \int_{\mathbb{R}^n}
 |f(x)|\,dx = \|f\|_1.$$

**4. $P$ is not bounded below by any $\alpha \in (0,1]$.** We must show that for
 every $\alpha > 0$, there exists $f \in L^1$ with $\|Pf\|_1 < \alpha\|f\|_1$.

For each integer $n \geq 2$, let $f_n = \mathbf{1}_{B(ne_1,\, 1)}$, the indicato
r of the closed unit ball centered at $ne_1$ (where $e_1 = (1,0,\dots,0)$). Then
:
$$\|f_n\|_1 = \operatorname{vol}\big(B(ne_1,1)\big) = V_n,$$
where $V_n$ is the volume of the unit ball in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

For any $x \in B(ne_1, 1)$, the triangle inequality gives:
$$\|x\| \geq \|ne_1\| - \|x - ne_1\| \geq n - 1.$$

Therefore:
$$\|Pf_n\|_1 = \int_{B(ne_1,1)} e^{-\|x\|}\,dx \leq e^{-(n-1)} \cdot V_n = e^{-(
n-1)}\,\|f_n\|_1.$$

Since $e^{-(n-1)} \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$, for any given $\alpha \in (0,1]$ we
can choose $n$ large enough that $e^{-(n-1)} < \alpha$. Then:
$$\|Pf_n\|_1 \leq e^{-(n-1)}\|f_n\|_1 < \alpha\,\|f_n\|_1.$$

This shows that no constant $\alpha \in (0,1]$ can serve as a lower bound $\alph
a\|f\|_1 \leq \|Pf\|_1$ for all $f \in L^1$.

**Key insight.** The unboundedness of $\Omega$ is essential: it allows the multi
plier $e^{-\|x\|}$ to decay to $0$, which means we can always find $L^1$ functio
ns supported in regions where the multiplier is arbitrarily small, defeating any
 uniform lower bound. On a *bounded* domain, a strictly positive multiplier woul
d be bounded away from $0$, yielding a lower bound. $\blacksquare$
