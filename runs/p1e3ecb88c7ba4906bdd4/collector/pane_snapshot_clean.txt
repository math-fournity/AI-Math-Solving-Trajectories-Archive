Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there exists a group with t
wo non-commuting infinite-order elements that are commensurable (i.e., sharing a
 common power: $a^m = b^n$ for some positive integers $m, n$).

**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

Consider the group
$$G = \langle a, b \mid a^2 = b^3 \rangle.$$

This is the group of the $(2,3)$-torus knot (the trefoil). I verify the three re
quired properties.

### 1. $a$ and $b$ are commensurable

By the defining relation, $a^2 = b^3$, so $a$ and $b$ share a common power. ✓

### 2. $a$ and $b$ have infinite order

The group $G$ is a **free product with amalgamation**:
$$G = \langle a \rangle \;*_{\langle a^2 \rangle \,=\, \langle b^3 \rangle}\; \l
angle b \rangle,$$
where $\langle a \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and $\langle b \rangle \cong \mathbb{
Z}$, and the amalgamated subgroup is $\langle a^2 \rangle = \langle b^3 \rangle
\cong \mathbb{Z}$, embedded into each factor via $a^2 \mapsto a^2$ and $b^3 \map
sto b^3$ respectively.

By the **Normal Form Theorem for free products with amalgamation** (see Serre, *
Trees*, or Magnus–Karrass–Solitar, *Combinatorial Group Theory*), the natural ma
ps from each factor into the amalgamated product are **embeddings**. Therefore $
\langle a \rangle$ and $\langle b \rangle$ both embed into $G$, so $a$ and $b$ e
ach have infinite order. ✓

### 3. $a$ and $b$ do not commute

There is a surjective homomorphism
$$\varphi: G \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$
$
defined by $a \mapsto \bar{a}$ (generator of $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$) and $b \m
apsto \bar{b}$ (generator of $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$). This is well-defined bec
ause the relation $a^2 = b^3$ maps to $\bar{a}^2 = 1 = \bar{b}^3$, which holds i
n the target. It is surjective since $\bar{a}$ and $\bar{b}$ generate the free p
roduct.

The group $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ is the **modular gro
up** $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})$, which is well-known to be **non-abelian** (in
deed, any free product of non-trivial groups is non-abelian).

Now suppose for contradiction that $a$ and $b$ commute in $G$. Then $G$ would be
 abelian, and every quotient of $G$ would also be abelian. But $\mathbb{Z}/2\mat
hbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ is a non-abelian quotient of $G$—contradiction.
 Therefore $a$ and $b$ do **not** commute. ✓

### Conclusion

The group $G = \langle a, b \mid a^2 = b^3 \rangle$ contains elements $a$ and $b
$ that:
- both have infinite order (by the embedding theorem for free products with amal
gamation),
- are commensurable ($a^2 = b^3$),
- do not commute (since $G$ surjects onto the non-abelian modular group $\mathbb
{Z}/2 * \mathbb{Z}/3$).

Therefore, **yes**, such a group exists. $\blacksquare$
