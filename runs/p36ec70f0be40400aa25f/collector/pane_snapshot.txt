## Solution

### Step 1: Traveling-wave reduction

Let $\xi = x - ct$ and set $\phi(x,t) = U(\xi)$. Then

$$
\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial t} = -c\,U'(\xi),\qquad
\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x} = U'(\xi),\qquad
\frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial x^2} = U''(\xi).
$$

Substituting into Burgers' equation $\phi_t + \phi\,\phi_x = c\,\phi_{xx}$:

$$
-c\,U' + U\,U' = c\,U''.
$$

Rearranging gives the **ordinary differential equation for the permanent (travel
ing) wave**:

$$
\boxed{\,c\,U'' + c\,U' - U\,U' = 0\,}
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
c\,U'' = (U-c)\,U'.
$$

### Step 2: First-order system and equilibrium points

Introduce $V = U'$. The ODE becomes the planar autonomous system

$$
\boxed{\;
\begin{aligned}
U' &= V,\\[2pt]
V' &= \dfrac{1}{c}\,V\,(U - c).
\end{aligned}
\;}
$$

**Equilibrium points** satisfy $V = 0$ and $\tfrac{1}{c}V(U-c)=0$. Both conditio
ns reduce to $V = 0$, with $U$ arbitrary. Hence the equilibria are **not isolate
d**: they form the **entire $U$-axis**,

$$
\boxed{\;\{(U_e, 0) : U_e \in \mathbb{R}\}\;}
$$

i.e. a continuous line of fixed points along $V = 0$. Each constant state $U(\xi
) \equiv U_e$ is a trivial traveling wave.

### Step 3: Phase paths

For $V \neq 0$ we eliminate the parameter $\xi$:

$$
\frac{dV}{dU} = \frac{V'}{U'} = \frac{\tfrac{1}{c}V(U-c)}{V} = \frac{U - c}{c}.
$$

Integrating,

$$
V = \frac{1}{2c}(U - c)^2 + A,\qquad A \in \mathbb{R}.
$$

Thus the **phase paths are a one-parameter family of parabolas**

$$
\boxed{\;V = \dfrac{1}{2c}(U-c)^2 + A\;}
$$

with vertices on the vertical line $U = c$, all having the same shape (curvature
 $1/(2c)$) and differing only by a vertical shift $A$.

### Step 4: Interpretation of the phase diagram

The phase portrait is organized by how each parabola meets the line of equilibri
a $V = 0$:

| Regime | Condition on $A$ | Geometry | Dynamical meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| $A < 0$ | $-\frac{(U-c)^2}{2c}\big|_{\max} > 0$ | Parabola crosses $V=0$ at tw
o points $U = c \pm \sqrt{-2cA}$ | **Heteroclinic orbit**: a trajectory leaves o
ne equilibrium $(U_1,0)$ and approaches another $(U_2,0)$, with $U_1 < c < U_2$.
 This is a **shock / transition wave** connecting two constant states. |
| $A = 0$ | vertex touches $V=0$ | Parabola tangent to $V=0$ at $(c,0)$ | **Homo
clinic-type / saddle-node** orbit: the wave approaches the single state $U = c$
asymptotically (a critical, monotone kink). |
| $A > 0$ | $V > 0$ everywhere | Parabola lies entirely above $V=0$ | No equilib
rium is reached; $U$ grows without bound — **no bounded permanent wave** exists.
 |

Because $V = U'$, the sign of $V$ determines whether $U$ is increasing or decrea
sing with $\xi$:

- On the upper branch ($V>0$), $U$ increases with $\xi = x - ct$: the profile is
 a **monotone rising** transition from $U_1$ to $U_2$ (a *rarefaction-type* shoc
k).
- On the lower branch ($V<0$, the part of an $A<0$ parabola below the $U$-axis),
 $U$ decreases: a **monotone falling** transition (a *compressive* shock).

For the physically relevant case $A < 0$ with $c > 0$, the heteroclinic orbit gi
ves the classical **Taylor shock profile** of Burgers' equation: a smooth, monot
one transition layer of thickness $\sim c/\Delta U$ connecting the two uniform s
tates $U_1$ and $U_2$, traveling at speed $c$. The diffusion term $c\,U_{xx}$ ba
lances the nonlinear steepening $\phi\,\phi_x$, producing a stable permanent wav
e.

The line of equilibria reflects the fact that Burgers' equation admits **any** c
onstant as a rest state; the parabolic phase paths show that the only non-trivia
l bounded permanent waves are the heteroclinic connections (shocks) joining two
such constant states across the critical value $U = c$.
