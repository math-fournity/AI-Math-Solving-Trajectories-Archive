## Proof

Let $S = k[T_0, \ldots, T_n]$ and let $S_+ = (T_0, \ldots, T_n)$ be the irreleva
nt ideal. For a homogeneous ideal $I \subseteq S$, write
$$PV(I) = \{[a_0 : \cdots : a_n] \in \mathbb{P}^n_k : f(a_0, \ldots, a_n) = 0 \t
ext{ for every homogeneous } f \in I\}$$
for its projective vanishing set, and for a subset $V \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n_k$
write
$$I(V) = \{f \in S \text{ homogeneous} : f(P) = 0 \text{ for all } P \in V\}$$
for its homogeneous vanishing ideal. A homogeneous ideal $I$ is **saturated** if
 it is a fixed point of the closure operation $I \mapsto I(PV(I))$, i.e., $I = I
(PV(I))$; equivalently (by the projective Nullstellensatz, Step 3 below) $I$ is
both radical and saturated with respect to $S_+$ in the classical sense $I = I :
 S_+^\infty$.

We prove that
$$\Phi : \{I \subsetneq S : I \text{ saturated homogeneous}\} \longrightarrow \{
\text{projective algebraic subvarieties of } \mathbb{P}^n_k\}, \qquad \Phi(I) =
PV(I)$$
is a bijection, with inverse $\Psi(V) = I(V)$.

### Step 1 — A Galois connection

The maps $\Phi(I) = PV(I)$ and $\Psi(V) = I(V)$ are order-reversing:
- $I \subseteq J \implies PV(J) \subseteq PV(I)$,
- $V \subseteq W \implies I(W) \subseteq I(V)$,

and they satisfy the Galois connection property: for any homogeneous ideal $I$ a
nd any subset $V$,
$$I \subseteq I(V) \iff V \subseteq PV(I),$$
since both sides assert that every homogeneous element of $I$ vanishes at every
point of $V$.

Any Galois connection yields, for free:
- $I \subseteq \Psi(\Phi(I)) = I(PV(I))$ for all $I$,
- $V \subseteq \Phi(\Psi(V)) = PV(I(V))$ for all $V$,
- $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ are **mutual inverses** when restricted to the fixed points
(closed elements) on each side.

So it remains only to identify the fixed points.

### Step 2 — Fixed points on the variety side are exactly the algebraic subvarie
ties

A subset $V \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n_k$ is a projective algebraic subvariety iff $
V = PV(I)$ for some homogeneous ideal $I$, iff $V = PV(I(V)) = \Phi(\Psi(V))$, i
ff $V$ is a fixed point of $\Phi \circ \Psi$. This is the definition.

### Step 3 — Fixed points on the ideal side are exactly the saturated homogeneou
s ideals

We use the **projective Nullstellensatz**. For a homogeneous ideal $I$ with $PV(
I) \neq \emptyset$,
$$I(PV(I)) = \sqrt{I}^{\,\mathrm{sat}},$$
where $\sqrt{I}$ is the homogeneous radical and $(-)^{\mathrm{sat}} = (-):S_+^\i
nfty$ is saturation by the irrelevant ideal. (This is the projective form of the
 Nullstellensatz: on the affine cone one has $I(V_{\mathrm{aff}}(I)) = \sqrt{I}$
, and passing to the projective setting requires saturating by $S_+$, since two
homogeneous ideals define the same projective variety iff they have the same sat
uration.) Hence
$$I = I(PV(I)) \iff I = \sqrt{I}^{\,\mathrm{sat}} \iff I \text{ is radical and }
 S_+\text{-saturated}.$$
So the fixed points are precisely the saturated homogeneous ideals (in the combi
ned radical-and-$S_+$-saturated sense stated above).

**The empty-set case.** By the projective Nullstellensatz, $PV(I) = \emptyset \i
ff S_+ \subseteq \sqrt{I}$. Suppose $I$ is saturated and $I \neq S$ (the unit id
eal, which we exclude by convention as non-geometric). Then $\sqrt{I} \neq S$, s
o $\sqrt{I} = S_+$. We claim $I = S_+$: since $S_+ \subseteq \sqrt{I}$, some pow
er $S_+^N \subseteq I$; now for any $f \in S_+$, we have $f \cdot S_+^{N-1} \sub
seteq S_+^N \subseteq I$, so $f \in I : S_+^{N-1} = I$ by $S_+$-saturation. Thus
 $S_+ \subseteq I$, and $I \subseteq S_+$ (since $I \subseteq \sqrt{I} = S_+$) g
ives $I = S_+$. Therefore $S_+$ is the **unique** saturated ideal (other than $S
$) mapping to $\emptyset$, and the correspondence $S_+ \leftrightarrow \emptyset
$ is well-defined and single-valued.

### Step 4 — The bijection

Restricting the Galois connection to its fixed points gives a bijection
$$\{I \subsetneq S : I \text{ saturated homogeneous}\} \;\xrightarrow{\;\Phi\;}\
; \{\text{projective algebraic subvarieties of } \mathbb{P}^n_k\},$$
with inverse $\Psi(V) = I(V)$.

- **Injectivity.** If $PV(I) = PV(J)$ for saturated $I, J$, then
$$I = I(PV(I)) = I(PV(J)) = J,$$
using that $I, J$ are fixed points.

- **Surjectivity.** Let $V$ be a projective algebraic subvariety, so $V = PV(J)$
 for some homogeneous $J$. Then $I(V) = I(PV(J))$ is saturated: indeed $PV(I(V))
 = PV(I(PV(J))) = PV(J) = V$ (since $V$ is a fixed point on the variety side, St
ep 2), and therefore $I(PV(I(V))) = I(V)$, making $I(V)$ a fixed point on the id
eal side. Moreover $\Phi(I(V)) = PV(I(V)) = V$, so $V$ is in the image of $\Phi$
.

Both directions are established, so $\Phi$ is a bijection with inverse $\Psi$. $
\blacksquare$

**Remark on the role of saturation.** Saturation by $S_+$ is what distinguishes
the projective correspondence from the affine one. In affine space, the Nullstel
lensatz gives a bijection between radical ideals and affine varieties. In projec
tive space, different homogeneous ideals (e.g., $I$ and $I^{\mathrm{sat}}$) can
define the same projective variety because the irrelevant ideal $S_+$ vanishes a
t no point of $\mathbb{P}^n$. Saturation quotients out exactly this ambiguity: $
PV(I) = PV(I^{\mathrm{sat}})$, and $I^{\mathrm{sat}}$ is the unique saturated id
eal representing the equivalence class. Combining saturation with the radical co
ndition (via the projective Nullstellensatz) yields exactly the fixed points of
the Galois connection, giving the bijection.
