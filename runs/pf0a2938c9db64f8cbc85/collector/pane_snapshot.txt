## Proof

The answer is **yes**: for any non-empty embedded submanifold $M$ of $\mathbb{R}
^n$, there exists a non-empty open set $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ on which $d
(x,M)$ is smooth.

We handle two cases.

### Case 1: $\dim M = n$

Then $M$ is an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$. For every $x \in M$, we have $d(x,
 M) = 0$. The function $d(\cdot, M)|_M \equiv 0$ is smooth, so $\Omega = M$ suff
ices.

### Case 2: $\dim M = k < n$

Pick any point $p \in M$.

**Step 1 (Local closedness).** Since $M$ is an *embedded* submanifold, it is loc
ally closed: there exists $R > 0$ such that $M \cap B(p, R)$ is a closed submani
fold of the open ball $B(p, R)$.

*Justification:* In a coordinate chart around $p$, there is a diffeomorphism $\p
hi: V \to \phi(V) \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ with $\phi(M \cap V) = (\mathbb{R}^k \ti
mes \{0\}) \cap \phi(V)$. The latter is closed in $\phi(V)$, so $M \cap V$ is cl
osed in $V$. Shrink $V$ to a ball $B(p, R)$.

**Step 2 (Tubular neighborhood).** The set $S := M \cap B(p, R)$ is a closed emb
edded submanifold of $B(p, R) \cong \mathbb{R}^n$. By the **tubular neighborhood
 theorem**, there exists an open neighborhood $U_0 \subset B(p, R)$ of $S$ and a
 smooth map $\pi: U_0 \to S$ (the nearest-point projection) such that:
- $\pi$ is a smooth retraction ($\pi|_S = \mathrm{id}$),
- every $x \in U_0$ has $\pi(x)$ as its **unique** nearest point in $S$,
- $d(x, S) = \|x - \pi(x)\|$ for all $x \in U_0$.

Shrink $U_0$ to a smaller open neighborhood $U \subset U_0$ with $U \subset B(p,
 R/2)$ and $\|x - \pi(x)\| < R/4$ for all $x \in U$.

**Step 3 (Local = global distance).** *Claim:* For all $x \in U$, $d(x, M) = d(x
, S) = \|x - \pi(x)\|$.

*Proof of claim:* Take $x \in U \subset B(p, R/2)$. Any point $y \in M \setminus
 B(p, R)$ satisfies
$$\|x - y\| \geq \|y - p\| - \|x - p\| > R - R/2 = R/2.$$
But $\|x - \pi(x)\| < R/4 < R/2$, so no point of $M \setminus B(p,R)$ is closer
to $x$ than $\pi(x)$. Hence the infimum defining $d(x, M)$ is achieved inside $B
(p, R)$, giving $d(x, M) = d(x, S)$. $\square$

**Step 4 (Smoothness on $\Omega = U \setminus M$).** Since $S = M \cap B(p,R)$ i
s closed in $B(p,R)$ and $U \subset B(p,R)$, the set $M \cap U = S \cap U$ is cl
osed in $U$. Therefore $\Omega := U \setminus M$ is **open**.

Since $\dim M = k < n$, the submanifold $M$ has empty interior in $\mathbb{R}^n$
, so $\Omega = U \setminus M$ is **non-empty** (indeed, open and dense in $U$).

For $x \in \Omega$, we have $x \notin M$ but $\pi(x) \in M$, so $x - \pi(x) \neq
 0$. The function
$$x \longmapsto \|x - \pi(x)\|$$
is a composition of:
- $x \mapsto (x, \pi(x))$ — smooth (since $\pi$ is smooth),
- $(x, y) \mapsto x - y$ — smooth (linear),
- $v \mapsto \|v\|$ — smooth on $\mathbb{R}^n \setminus \{0\}$.

Since $x - \pi(x) \neq 0$ throughout $\Omega$, the composition is smooth. By Ste
p 3, $d(x, M) = \|x - \pi(x)\|$ on $\Omega$, so **$d(x, M)$ is smooth on $\Omega
$**.

### Conclusion

In both cases, we have produced a non-empty open set $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^
n$ on which $d(x, M)$ is smooth. The key ingredients are:
- **Local closedness** of embedded submanifolds, which lets us find a ball $B(p,
R)$ where $M$ is a closed submanifold.
- The **tubular neighborhood theorem**, which provides a smooth nearest-point pr
ojection $\pi$ on a neighborhood $U$ of $M \cap B(p,R)$.
- A **distance comparison** showing that for $x$ near $M \cap B(p,R)$, the globa
l distance $d(x, M)$ equals the local distance $d(x, M \cap B(p,R))$, because po
ints of $M$ outside $B(p,R)$ are too far away to matter.

$\blacksquare$
