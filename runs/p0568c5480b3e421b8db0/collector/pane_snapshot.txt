## Proof

We seek positive integers $x, y, z$ with $x > 2$, $y > 1$ satisfying
$$z^2 - x^y = 1.$$

### Case 1: $y$ is even

Write $y = 2m$ with $m \geq 1$. Then $z^2 - (x^m)^2 = 1$, which factors as
$$(z - x^m)(z + x^m) = 1.$$
Since $x \geq 3$ and $m \geq 1$, we have $x^m \geq 3$, and $z^2 = x^{2m} + 1 > x
^{2m}$ implies $z > x^m$. Thus $z - x^m \geq 1$ and $z + x^m \geq 2$, so their p
roduct is at least $2$, contradicting the product equaling $1$. **No solutions e
xist with $y$ even.**

### Case 2: $y$ is odd

Since $y > 1$ and $y$ is odd, we have $y \geq 3$. We verify all four parameters
exceed $1$:
- $z \geq 2$: Since $x \geq 3$, $y \geq 3$ gives $x^y \geq 27$, so $z^2 \geq 28$
, hence $z \geq 6$.
- $x \geq 3 > 1$.
- The exponent $2 > 1$.
- The exponent $y \geq 3 > 1$.

**Applying Mihailescu's Theorem** (Catalan's Conjecture, proved 2002): The only
solution to $a^m - b^n = 1$ in positive integers with $a, b, m, n > 1$ is
$$3^2 - 2^3 = 1, \quad \text{i.e., } (a, m, b, n) = (3, 2, 2, 3).$$

Setting $(a, m, b, n) = (z, 2, x, y)$, the unique solution is $z = 3$, $x = 2$,
$y = 3$. But this requires $x = 2$, which **contradicts** the condition $x > 2$.
 **No solutions exist with $y$ odd.**

### Conclusion

Combining both cases, **there are no positive integers $x, y, z$ with $x > 2$ an
d $y > 1$ satisfying $x^y + 1 = z^2$.**

The constraint set is empty: the unique solution to $z^2 - x^y = 1$ with all bas
es and exponents exceeding $1$ is $(z, x, y) = (3, 2, 3)$, which is excluded by
the requirement $x > 2$. Therefore, $p - q$ has no minimum value, as no admissib
le triple $(x, y, z)$ exists.
