**Answer: Yes, $x = a^*a$ for some $a \in A$.**

**Proof.**

Since $x = b^*b$ for some $b \in B$, the element $x$ is positive in $B$, so $x \
in B_+$. Since $x \in A = pBp$, we also have $px = xp = x$.

Because $x$ is positive in $B$, it admits a unique positive square root $x^{1/2}
 \in B$ (self-adjoint, with $(x^{1/2})^2 = x$). We claim that in fact $x^{1/2} \
in A$, which immediately yields the desired factorization $x = (x^{1/2})^*x^{1/2
} = x^{1/2}x^{1/2}$ with $a := x^{1/2} \in A$.

It remains to prove $x^{1/2} \in pBp$.

Since $x = pxp$, we have $(1-p)x(1-p) = 0$. Using $x = (x^{1/2})^2$ with $x^{1/2
}$ self-adjoint:

$$(1-p)x(1-p) = (1-p)x^{1/2}x^{1/2}(1-p) = \bigl(x^{1/2}(1-p)\bigr)^*\bigl(x^{1/
2}(1-p)\bigr) = 0.$$

A $C^*$-algebra element $y$ with $y^*y = 0$ satisfies $\|y\|^2 = \|y^*y\| = 0$,
hence $y = 0$. Applying this to $y = x^{1/2}(1-p)$:

$$x^{1/2}(1-p) = 0, \quad\text{i.e.,}\quad x^{1/2} = x^{1/2}p.$$

Taking adjoints (using $x^{1/2} = (x^{1/2})^*$):

$$x^{1/2} = \bigl(x^{1/2}p\bigr)^* = p\,x^{1/2}.$$

Combining both identities:

$$x^{1/2} = x^{1/2}p = p\,x^{1/2}p \in pBp = A.$$

Therefore $x^{1/2} \in A$, and setting $a = x^{1/2} \in A$ gives

$$a^*a = (x^{1/2})^*x^{1/2} = x^{1/2}x^{1/2} = x.$$

**Remark.** This shows that the positive cone of the corner $A = pBp$ satisfies
$A_+ = A \cap B_+$: an element of $A$ that is positive in $B$ is automatically p
ositive in $A$, with the positive square root computed in $B$ already lying in $
A$. $\blacksquare$
