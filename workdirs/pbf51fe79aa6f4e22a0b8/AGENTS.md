# Problem

In a remote territory, a high-tech surveillance grid is modeled on a coordinate plane. The entire grid is governed by a circular boundary $\Gamma$ that passes through both Alpha and Bravo, acting as the circumcircle of the square.

A mobile reconnaissance unit, Charlie ($C$), moves continuously along the circular boundary $\Gamma$. Whenever Charlie is at a position other than Alpha or Bravo, it establishes a triangular communication zone $ABC$. Within this zone, a specialized security hub, India ($I$), is established at the center of the zone’s incircle $\omega$. To synchronize data, a calibration point $C_1$ is marked at the exact spot where the incircle $\omega$ touches the direct transmission line $\overline{AB}$. In the specific instances where Charlie’s path brings it directly to the coordinates of Alpha or Bravo, the signal node Delta is simply defined to be at Charlie’s current location ($D=C$).

As Charlie completes a full circuit around the boundary $\Gamma$, the signal node Delta traces a continuous closed path $\mathfrak{C}$ in the plane. This path encloses a specific surveillance region with an area $\mathcal{A}$.

Compute $\lfloor 10^4 \mathcal{A} \rfloor$.
