## Proof

Let $U = U(n) = \{A \in M_n(\mathbb{C}) : A^*A = I\}$, viewed as a subspace of $
\mathbb{C}^{n^2} \cong \mathbb{R}^{2n^2}$.

**Compactness.** Consider the continuous map $\Phi: M_n(\mathbb{C}) \to M_n(\mat
hbb{C})$ defined by $\Phi(A) = A^*A$. Then
$$U = \Phi^{-1}(I),$$
so $U$ is closed in $M_n(\mathbb{C}) \cong \mathbb{C}^{n^2}$ (as the preimage of
 the singleton $\{I\}$, which is closed).

To see $U$ is bounded: if $A \in U$, then $A^*A = I$. Let $a_1, \dots, a_n$ deno
te the columns of $A$. The condition $A^*A = I$ says these columns are orthonorm
al, so in particular $\|a_j\|^2 = 1$ for each $j$. Hence every entry $|a_{ij}| \
le \|a_j\| = 1$. Therefore $U$ is contained in the polydisc $\{|a_{ij}| \le 1\}$
, which is bounded in $\mathbb{C}^{n^2} \cong \mathbb{R}^{2n^2}$.

Being closed and bounded in $\mathbb{R}^{2n^2}$, $U$ is compact by the Heine–Bor
el theorem.

**Connectedness.** We show $U$ is path-connected, hence connected. Let $A \in U$
. By the spectral theorem for normal matrices, $A$ is unitarily diagonalizable:
there exists $V \in U$ and $\theta_1, \dots, \theta_n \in \mathbb{R}$ such that
$$A = V \operatorname{diag}(e^{i\theta_1}, \dots, e^{i\theta_n}) V^*.$$
Define, for $t \in [0,1]$,
$$\gamma(t) = V \operatorname{diag}(e^{it\theta_1}, \dots, e^{it\theta_n}) V^*.$
$
Each $\gamma(t)$ is unitary (it is a product of unitary matrices, or equivalentl
y $\gamma(t)^*\gamma(t) = V\operatorname{diag}(e^{-it\theta_k})V^* V\operatornam
e{diag}(e^{it\theta_k})V^* = I$). Moreover $\gamma(0) = V I V^* = I$ and $\gamma
(1) = A$. The map $t \mapsto \gamma(t)$ is continuous. Thus $\gamma$ is a path i
n $U$ from $I$ to $A$.

Since every $A \in U$ is path-connected to $I$, any two elements of $U$ are path
-connected (via $I$). Hence $U$ is path-connected, and therefore connected.

**Conclusion.** $U(n)$ is both compact and connected, which is option **(c)**.
