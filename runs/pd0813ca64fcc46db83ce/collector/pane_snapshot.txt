**Problem.** Solve
$$u_{tt} + b^2\,\Delta_2\,\Delta_2\, u = 0,\qquad (x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^2,\; t>0,$$
$$u\big|_{t=0}=\Phi(x,y),\qquad u_t\big|_{t=0}=\Psi(x,y),$$
where $\Delta_2 = \partial_{xx}+\partial_{yy}$ is the two-dimensional Laplacian,
 so $\Delta_2\Delta_2 = \Delta_2^2$ is the biharmonic operator. We assume $b>0$
(the equation depends on $b^2$; for $b<0$ replace $b$ by $|b|$).

---

## Step 1. Fourier transform in the spatial variables

Set
$$\hat u(\xi,\eta,t)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^2} u(x,y,t)\,e^{-i(\xi x+\eta y)}\,dx\,dy
,\qquad \hat\Phi=\hat\Phi(\xi,\eta),\quad \hat\Psi=\hat\Psi(\xi,\eta).$$
Under the Fourier transform, $\Delta_2 \mapsto -(\xi^2+\eta^2)$, hence $\Delta_2
^2 \mapsto (\xi^2+\eta^2)^2$. Denoting $|\zeta|^2=\xi^2+\eta^2$, the PDE becomes
 the **family of ODEs** (parametrized by $(\xi,\eta)$):
$$\hat u_{tt}+b^2\,|\zeta|^4\,\hat u = 0,\qquad \hat u(\xi,\eta,0)=\hat\Phi,\qua
d \hat u_t(\xi,\eta,0)=\hat\Psi.$$

## Step 2. Solve the ODE

This is a harmonic oscillator with frequency $\omega = b\,|\zeta|^2 = b(\xi^2+\e
ta^2)$. The solution is
$$\hat u(\xi,\eta,t)=\hat\Phi(\xi,\eta)\cos\!\big(b(\xi^2+\eta^2)\,t\big)+\frac{
\hat\Psi(\xi,\eta)}{b(\xi^2+\eta^2)}\sin\!\big(b(\xi^2+\eta^2)\,t\big). \tag{2}$
$

## Step 3. Invert the Fourier transform

Applying the inverse Fourier transform to (2) gives the solution in **Fourier-in
tegral form**:

$$\boxed{\;u(x,y,t)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^2}\left[\hat\Phi(\xi,\et
a)\cos\!\big(b(\xi^2+\eta^2)t\big)+\frac{\hat\Psi(\xi,\eta)}{b(\xi^2+\eta^2)}\si
n\!\big(b(\xi^2+\eta^2)t\big)\right]e^{i(\xi x+\eta y)}\,d\xi\,d\eta.\;} \tag{3}
$$

Equivalently, using the convolution theorem $u = \Phi * K_1 + \Psi * K_2$ with t
he **fundamental kernels**

$$K_1(r,t)=\frac{1}{4\pi b t}\sin\!\frac{r^2}{4bt},\qquad K_2(r,t)=\frac{1}{4\pi
 b}\left[\frac{\pi}{2}-\operatorname{Si}\!\left(\frac{r^2}{4bt}\right)\right],\q
quad r^2=x^2+y^2, \tag{4}$$

where $\operatorname{Si}(z)=\int_0^z \frac{\sin v}{v}\,dv$, the solution reads

$$\boxed{\;u(x,y,t)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^2}\Phi(\xi,\eta)\,K_1\!\big(\sqrt{(x{-}\xi
)^2{+}(y{-}\eta)^2},\,t\big)\,d\xi\,d\eta+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^2}\Psi(\xi,\eta)\,K_
2\!\big(\sqrt{(x{-}\xi)^2{+}(y{-}\eta)^2},\,t\big)\,d\xi\,d\eta.\;} \tag{5}$$

**Derivation of the kernels.** Using the Fresnel integral $\int_{-\infty}^{\inft
y}e^{ia\xi^2+i\xi x}d\xi=\sqrt{\pi/(|a|)}\,e^{i\pi\operatorname{sgn}(a)/4}\,e^{-
ix^2/(4a)}$ with $a=bt>0$:
$$\mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\big[\cos(b|\zeta|^2 t)\big]=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}\operatorname
{Re}\!\left[\frac{\pi}{bt}\,i\,e^{-ir^2/(4bt)}\right]=\frac{1}{4\pi bt}\sin\frac
{r^2}{4bt}=K_1.$$
For $K_2$, use $\frac{\sin(b|\zeta|^2 t)}{b|\zeta|^2}=\int_0^t\cos(b|\zeta|^2 s)
\,ds$, so
$$K_2(r,t)=\int_0^t K_1(r,s)\,ds=\frac{1}{4\pi b}\int_0^t\frac{1}{s}\sin\frac{r^
2}{4bs}\,ds.$$
Substituting $v=r^2/(4bs)$ yields $\int_{r^2/(4bt)}^{\infty}\frac{\sin v}{v}\,dv
=\frac{\pi}{2}-\operatorname{Si}\!\big(\frac{r^2}{4bt}\big)$, giving (4).

## Step 4. Verification

**PDE.** From (2): $\hat u_{tt}=-b^2|\zeta|^4\hat u$, i.e. $\hat u_{tt}+b^2(\xi^
2+\eta^2)^2\hat u=0$. Inverting gives $u_{tt}+b^2\Delta_2^2 u=0$. ✓

**Initial displacement.** At $t=0$: $\cos 0=1$, $\sin 0=0$, so $\hat u(\xi,\eta,
0)=\hat\Phi$, hence $u(x,y,0)=\Phi(x,y)$. ✓

**Initial velocity.** $\hat u_t=-\hat\Phi\,\omega\sin(\omega t)+\hat\Psi\cos(\om
ega t)$. At $t=0$: $\hat u_t=\hat\Psi$, hence $u_t(x,y,0)=\Psi(x,y)$. ✓

## Step 5. Uniqueness (energy method)

For sufficiently smooth solutions decaying at infinity, define the energy
$$E(t)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^2}\Big(u_t^2+b^2\,|\Delta_2 u|^2\Big)\,dx\,dy.$$
Differentiating and integrating by parts twice on the biharmonic term:
$$E'(t)=2\iint\big(u_t u_{tt}+b^2\,\Delta_2 u\,\Delta_2 u_t\big)\,dx\,dy=2\iint
u_t\big(u_{tt}+b^2\Delta_2^2 u\big)\,dx\,dy=0,$$
since the boundary terms vanish (decay at infinity) and the integrand is $u_t\cd
ot 0$ by the PDE. Thus $E(t)\equiv E(0)$. For zero initial data ($\Phi=\Psi=0$),
 $E(0)=0$, so $u_t\equiv 0$ and $\Delta_2 u\equiv 0$; with decay conditions this
 forces $u\equiv 0$. **Uniqueness** follows.
