## Solution

**Geometry.** The conical rod has its base (cross-sectional area $S_0$) at the f
ixed end $x=0$ and tapers to a point (vertex) at $x=l$ where the force is applie
d. The cross-sectional area at position $x$ is:

$$S(x) = S_0\left(1 - \frac{x}{l}\right)^2 = \frac{S_0}{l^2}(l-x)^2$$

**Equation of motion.** For longitudinal vibrations of a rod with variable cross
-section:

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\!\left[E\,S(x)\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\righ
t] = \rho\,S(x)\,\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2}$$

For steady-state response to harmonic forcing, set $u(x,t) = u(x)\sin\omega t$:

$$\frac{d}{dx}\!\left[E\,S(x)\,\frac{du}{dx}\right] + \rho\,\omega^2\,S(x)\,u =
0$$

**Substitution.** Let $y = l - x$ (so the vertex is at $y=0$). Then $S = S_0\,y^
2/l^2$ and $d/dx = -d/dy$. The equation becomes:

$$\frac{d}{dy}\!\left[y^2\frac{du}{dy}\right] + k^2\,y^2\,u = 0, \qquad k = \ome
ga\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{E}} = \frac{\omega}{c}$$

Expanding: $u'' + \frac{2}{y}u' + k^2 u = 0$, which is the **spherical Bessel eq
uation of order 0**. The general solution is:

$$u(y) = \frac{1}{y}\big[A\sin(ky) + B\cos(ky)\big]$$

or equivalently, in terms of $x$:

$$u(x) = \frac{1}{l-x}\big[A\sin(k(l-x)) + B\cos(k(l-x))\big]$$

**Boundary conditions.**

1. **Fixed end** $x=0$: $u(0)=0$.

$$\frac{1}{l}\big[A\sin(kl)+B\cos(kl)\big]=0 \implies A\sin(kl)+B\cos(kl)=0 \qua
d (\star)$$

2. **Forced end** $x=l$ (vertex): The longitudinal force equals $F_0\sin\omega t
$, so:

$$\lim_{x\to l} E\,S(x)\,u'(x) = F_0$$

Computing $u'(x) = -du/dy$ and evaluating the limit as $y\to 0$:

$$E\,S(x)\,u'(x) = -\frac{E\,S_0}{l^2}\left[ky\big(A\cos ky - B\sin ky\big) - \b
ig(A\sin ky + B\cos ky\big)\right]$$

As $y\to 0$: the bracket $\to -B$, so:

$$\lim_{x\to l} E\,S\,u' = \frac{E\,S_0\,B}{l^2} = F_0 \implies B = \frac{F_0\,l
^2}{E\,S_0} \quad (\star\star)$$

**Determining the constants.** From $(\star)$: $A = -B\cos(kl)/\sin(kl)$. Substi
tuting into $u(x)$:

$$u(x) = \frac{B}{l-x}\left[\cos(k(l-x)) - \frac{\cos(kl)}{\sin(kl)}\sin(k(l-x))
\right]$$

$$= \frac{B}{(l-x)\,\sin(kl)}\big[\sin(kl)\cos(k(l-x))-\cos(kl)\sin(k(l-x))\big]
$$

Using $\sin\alpha\cos\beta - \cos\alpha\sin\beta = \sin(\alpha-\beta)$ with $\al
pha=kl$, $\beta=k(l-x)$:

$$= \frac{B\,\sin(kx)}{(l-x)\,\sin(kl)}$$

Substituting $B = F_0 l^2/(E S_0)$:

$$\boxed{u(x,t) = \frac{F_0\,l^2}{E\,S_0}\cdot\frac{\sin(kx)}{(l-x)\,\sin(kl)}\c
dot\sin(\omega t), \qquad k=\frac{\omega}{c},\quad c=\sqrt{\frac{E}{\rho}}}$$

**Verification.**

- **At $x=0$:** $u(0) = \frac{F_0 l^2}{E S_0}\cdot\frac{0}{l\cdot\sin(kl)} = 0$
✓ (fixed end satisfied)
- **At $x\to l$:** $E S\, u' \to E S_0 B/l^2 = F_0$ ✓ (force condition satisfied
)
- **Resonance:** occurs when $\sin(kl)=0$, i.e., $kl = n\pi$ ($n=1,2,3,\ldots$),
 giving eigenfrequencies $\omega_n = n\pi c/l$. These coincide with the natural
frequencies of the conical rod (fixed base, free point tip), determined by the b
oundedness condition at the vertex ($B=0$ for free vibrations) combined with $u(
0)=0$.

**Physical interpretation.** The displacement amplitude grows toward the vertex
($x\to l$) as $1/(l-x)$, diverging at the tip—consistent with the force being ap
plied at a point of vanishing cross-section. The mode shape $\sin(kx)/(l-x)$ red
uces to $\sin(kx)$ modulated by the geometric factor $1/(l-x)$, reflecting the c
one's tapering geometry.
