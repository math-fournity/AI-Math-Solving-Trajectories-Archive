Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a.e. pointwise convergence
of Fourier partial sums of an $L^1$ function necessarily converges back to the f
unction itself.

**Answer: Yes.** Here is the proof.

---

## Proof

Let $f \in L^1([0,1])$ with Fourier coefficients $\{a_n\}$, and suppose $S_n(x)
= \sum_{k=-n}^{n} a_k e^{ikx}$ converges pointwise a.e. to some function $g(x)$.
 We show $g = f$ a.e.

**Step 1: Fejér means converge to $f$ a.e.**

Define the Cesàro (Fejér) means:
$$\sigma_N(x) = \frac{1}{N+1}\sum_{n=0}^{N} S_n(x).$$

By the **Lebesgue–Fejér theorem**, for any $f \in L^1$, the Fejér means $\sigma_
N(x) \to f(x)$ at every Lebesgue point of $f$. Since almost every point of $[0,1
]$ is a Lebesgue point of an $L^1$ function, we have:
$$\sigma_N(x) \to f(x) \quad \text{a.e.} \tag{1}$$

**Step 2: Cesàro means preserve ordinary limits.**

This is the classical numerical Cesàro theorem: if a sequence of real/complex nu
mbers $s_n \to L$, then the arithmetic means $\frac{1}{N+1}\sum_{n=0}^{N} s_n \t
o L$.

*Proof of this fact:* Given $\varepsilon > 0$, choose $N_0$ so that $|s_n - L| <
 \varepsilon/2$ for $n \geq N_0$. Then for $N > N_0$:
$$\left|\frac{1}{N+1}\sum_{n=0}^{N} s_n - L\right| \leq \frac{1}{N+1}\sum_{n=0}^
{N_0-1}|s_n - L| + \frac{1}{N+1}\sum_{n=N_0}^{N}|s_n - L|.$$
The first term $\to 0$ as $N \to \infty$ (fixed finite sum over a vanishing frac
tion), and the second term $< \varepsilon/2$. So for $N$ large enough the whole
expression is $< \varepsilon$. $\square$

**Step 3: Combining the two.**

By hypothesis, there is a set $E \subset [0,1]$ with $m(E^c) = 0$ such that $S_n
(x) \to g(x)$ for all $x \in E$.

By (1), there is a set $F \subset [0,1]$ with $m(F^c) = 0$ such that $\sigma_N(x
) \to f(x)$ for all $x \in F$.

For any $x \in E \cap F$:
- Since $S_n(x) \to g(x)$, Step 2 gives $\sigma_N(x) \to g(x)$.
- Since $x \in F$, we have $\sigma_N(x) \to f(x)$.

By uniqueness of limits, $g(x) = f(x)$.

Since $m((E \cap F)^c) \leq m(E^c) + m(F^c) = 0$, the set $E \cap F$ has full me
asure. Therefore:
$$g(x) = f(x) \quad \text{a.e. on } [0,1].$$

**Conclusion:** The partial sums $S_n(x)$, if they converge pointwise a.e., nece
ssarily converge to $f(x)$ a.e. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** The key insight is that the Fejér means $\sigma_N$ are *always* well
-behaved (converging to $f$ a.e. for any $L^1$ function, by the Lebesgue–Fejér t
heorem), and they are the Cesàro averages of the partial sums $S_n$. Since Cesàr
o summability preserves ordinary convergence, any a.e. limit of $S_n$ must agree
 with the a.e. limit of $\sigma_N$, which is $f$.
