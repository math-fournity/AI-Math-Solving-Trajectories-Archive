## Proof

**Notation and setup.** Let $\mathbb{N}^{<\infty} = \bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty} \math
bb{N}^n$ be the set of all finite sequences of natural numbers, with $\mathbb{N}
^0 = \{\emptyset\}$ (the empty sequence). The order $s \leq t$ means $s$ is an i
nitial segment (prefix) of $t$. A subset $C \subseteq \mathbb{N}^{<\infty}$ is *
downward-closed* if $t \in C$ and $s \leq t$ implies $s \in C$.

**Claim.** The number of downward-closed subsets of $\mathbb{N}^{<\infty}$ is $2
^{\aleph_0}$.

---

### Step 1: $\mathbb{N}^{<\infty}$ is countably infinite.

Each $\mathbb{N}^n$ (for fixed $n$) is countable: $\mathbb{N}^0$ is a singleton,
 and $\mathbb{N}^n$ for $n \geq 1$ is a finite product of countable sets, hence
countable. Then
$$\mathbb{N}^{<\infty} = \bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty} \mathbb{N}^n$$
is a countable union of countable sets, hence countable. It is infinite since $\
{(n) : n \in \mathbb{N}\} \subseteq \mathbb{N}^{<\infty}$. Therefore $|\mathbb{N
}^{<\infty}| = \aleph_0$.

---

### Step 2: Upper bound — at most $2^{\aleph_0}$ downward-closed subsets.

Every downward-closed subset is, in particular, a subset of $\mathbb{N}^{<\infty
}$. Since $|\mathbb{N}^{<\infty}| = \aleph_0$, the total number of subsets is $|
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N}^{<\infty})| = 2^{\aleph_0}$. The collection of downward-c
losed subsets is a subcollection of $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N}^{<\infty})$, so ther
e are at most $2^{\aleph_0}$ of them.

---

### Step 3: Lower bound — at least $2^{\aleph_0}$ downward-closed subsets.

For each subset $A \subseteq \mathbb{N}$, define
$$C_A \;=\; \{\emptyset\} \;\cup\; \{(n) : n \in A\}.$$

**$C_A$ is downward-closed.** The elements of $C_A$ are $\emptyset$ and sequence
s of length 1 of the form $(n)$ with $n \in A$. The only proper initial segment
of a length-1 sequence $(n)$ is $\emptyset$, which is in $C_A$. The empty sequen
ce has no proper initial segments. So the downward-closure condition is satisfie
d vacuously/trivially.

**The map $A \mapsto C_A$ is injective.** If $A \neq A'$, say $n \in A \setminus
 A'$, then $(n) \in C_A \setminus C_{A'}$, so $C_A \neq C_{A'}$.

Since there are $|\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})| = 2^{\aleph_0}$ subsets $A \subseteq
\mathbb{N}$, this construction yields $2^{\aleph_0}$ distinct downward-closed su
bsets.

---

### Step 4: Conclusion.

From Step 2, the number of downward-closed subsets is $\leq 2^{\aleph_0}$. From
Step 3, it is $\geq 2^{\aleph_0}$. By the Cantor–Schröder–Bernstein theorem (or
simply sandwiching), the number of downward-closed subsets of $\mathbb{N}^{<\inf
ty}$ is exactly

$$\boxed{2^{\aleph_0}}.$$
