We verify that $\Sigma_{i \in I} \mathscr{C}_{i}$ satisfies the axioms of a cate
gory.

**Objects and morphisms.** The objects are pairs $(C, i)$ with $i \in I$ and $C
\in \operatorname{Ob}(\mathscr{C}_{i})$. For two objects $(C, i)$ and $(D, j)$,
the hom-set is defined as
$$\operatorname{Hom}\bigl((C,i),(D,j)\bigr) = \begin{cases} \{(f, i) : f \in \op
eratorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}_{i}}(C, D)\} & \text{if } i = j, \\ \emptyset & \te
xt{if } i \neq j. \end{cases}$$
This is a well-defined set in either case (a copy of a hom-set of $\mathscr{C}_{
i}$, or the empty set), so the morphism data is well-defined.

**Identities.** For each object $(C, i)$, the prescribed identity is $(1_{C}, i)
$. Since $1_{C} \in \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}_{i}}(C, C)$, the pair $(1_{C
}, i)$ lies in $\operatorname{Hom}\bigl((C,i),(C,i)\bigr)$, which is nonempty pr
ecisely when the two indices agree (here both are $i$). So the identity morphism
 exists for every object.

**Composition is well-defined.** Consider morphisms
$$(f, i): (C, i) \to (D, i), \qquad (g, i): (D, i) \to (E, i).$$
For the composition to be defined, the target of the first must equal the source
 of the second. The target of $(f, i)$ is $(D, i)$ and the source of $(g, i)$ is
 $(D, i)$, so they match. The composition is defined to be $(g \circ f, i)$, whe
re $g \circ f$ is the composition in $\mathscr{C}_{i}$. Since $g \circ f \in \op
eratorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}_{i}}(C, E)$, the pair $(g \circ f, i)$ is a valid m
orphism from $(C, i)$ to $(E, i)$. Thus composition of composable pairs always y
ields a morphism of the correct type.

Moreover, composition is only ever invoked when $i = j$ (otherwise one of the tw
o hom-sets is empty and no composable pair exists), so there is no ambiguity in
the index carried by the result: it is the common index $i$.

**Identity axiom.** Let $(f, i): (C, i) \to (D, i)$ be a morphism, so $f: C \to
D$ in $\mathscr{C}_{i}$. Then
$$(1_{D}, i) \circ (f, i) = (1_{D} \circ f,\, i) = (f, i),$$
$$(f, i) \circ (1_{C}, i) = (f \circ 1_{C},\, i) = (f, i),$$
where the equalities $1_{D} \circ f = f$ and $f \circ 1_{C} = f$ hold by the ide
ntity axiom of $\mathscr{C}_{i}$. Hence the identity morphisms act as units.

**Associativity.** Consider three composable morphisms
$$(f, i): (C, i) \to (D, i), \quad (g, i): (D, i) \to (E, i), \quad (h, i): (E,
i) \to (F, i),$$
where $f, g, h$ are morphisms in $\mathscr{C}_{i}$. Computing both bracketings:
$$\bigl((h, i) \circ (g, i)\bigr) \circ (f, i) = (h \circ g,\, i) \circ (f, i) =
 \bigl((h \circ g) \circ f,\, i\bigr),$$
$$(h, i) \circ \bigl((g, i) \circ (f, i)\bigr) = (h, i) \circ (g \circ f,\, i) =
 \bigl(h \circ (g \circ f),\, i\bigr).$$
By associativity of composition in $\mathscr{C}_{i}$, we have $(h \circ g) \circ
 f = h \circ (g \circ f)$, so the two results are equal. Hence composition in th
e sum category is associative.

**Conclusion.** All four category axioms are satisfied: well-defined hom-sets, e
xistence of identity morphisms, well-defined composition, and associativity. The
refore $\Sigma_{i \in I} \mathscr{C}_{i}$ is a category. $\blacksquare$
