## Solution

The statement is **false**. I will construct a counterexample.

### Setup

Let $K = [0,1]^{\omega_1}$, the product of $\omega_1$ copies of $[0,1]$ with the
 product topology, and fix a point $p \in K$. Define $X = K \setminus \{p\}$.

### Step 1: $K$ is compact Hausdorff and separable

$K$ is compact Hausdorff by Tychonoff's theorem ($[0,1]$ is compact Hausdorff).

$K$ is **separable** by the **Hewitt–Marczewski–Pondiczery theorem**: a product
of at most $\mathfrak{c} = 2^{\aleph_0}$ separable spaces is separable. Since $\
omega_1 \leq \mathfrak{c}$ (as $\mathfrak{c} > \aleph_0$ by Cantor's theorem, an
d $\omega_1 = \aleph_1$ is the least uncountable cardinal) and $[0,1]$ is separa
ble, $K$ is separable.

### Step 2: No point of $K$ is a $G_\delta$

Suppose for contradiction that $\{p\} = \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} O_n$ with each $O
_n$ open in $K$. For each $n$, choose a basic open neighborhood $B_n = \prod_{\a
lpha \in \omega_1} U^{(n)}_\alpha$ of $p$ with $B_n \subseteq O_n$, where $U^{(n
)}_\alpha = [0,1]$ for all but finitely many $\alpha$. Let $S_n = \{\alpha : U^{
(n)}_\alpha \neq [0,1]\}$ (finite) and $S = \bigcup_n S_n$ (countable). Since $\
omega_1$ is uncountable, pick $\alpha_0 \in \omega_1 \setminus S$.

Define $q \in K$ by $q_\alpha = p_\alpha$ for $\alpha \neq \alpha_0$ and $q_{\al
pha_0} \neq p_{\alpha_0}$. Then $q \neq p$, but $q \in B_n$ for every $n$ (coord
inate $\alpha_0 \notin S_n$ is unrestricted, and $q$ agrees with $p$ on all rest
ricted coordinates). Hence $q \in \bigcap_n B_n \subseteq \bigcap_n O_n = \{p\}$
, a contradiction. So **no point of $K$ is $G_\delta$**.

### Step 3: $X = K \setminus \{p\}$ is locally compact, separable, Hausdorff

- **Locally compact Hausdorff**: $X$ is an open subspace of the compact Hausdorf
f space $K$, and open subspaces of compact Hausdorff spaces are locally compact
Hausdorff.
- **Separable**: If $D$ is countable dense in $K$, then $D \cap X$ is countable
dense in $X$: any nonempty open $V \subseteq X$ is also open in $K$ (since $X$ i
s open in $K$), so $V \cap D \neq \emptyset$, and $V \cap D \subseteq V \cap (D
\cap X)$.

### Step 4: $X$ is not $\sigma$-compact

We use the standard equivalence: for a compact Hausdorff space $K$ and $p \in K$
, the space $K \setminus \{p\}$ is $\sigma$-compact **if and only if** $\{p\}$ i
s a $G_\delta$ in $K$.

*Proof of equivalence:*
- $(\Leftarrow)$: If $\{p\} = \bigcap_n U_n$ with $U_n$ open, then $K \setminus
\{p\} = \bigcup_n (K \setminus U_n)$, and each $K \setminus U_n$ is closed in $K
$, hence compact.
- $(\Rightarrow)$: If $K \setminus \{p\} = \bigcup_n C_n$ with $C_n$ compact, th
en each $C_n$ is closed in $K$ (Hausdorff), so $U_n = K \setminus C_n$ is open a
nd $\{p\} = \bigcap_n U_n$.

By Step 2, $\{p\}$ is not $G_\delta$ in $K$, so $X = K \setminus \{p\}$ is **not
 $\sigma$-compact**.

### Conclusion

$X = [0,1]^{\omega_1} \setminus \{p\}$ is a locally compact, separable, Hausdorf
f space that is not $\sigma$-compact. The statement is **false**.
