## Proof

Let $X(t)$ be an Itô process with dynamics
$$dX(t) = \mu(t)\,dt + \sigma(t)\,dW(t),$$
and let $p(t)$ be a deterministic (non-stochastic), continuously differentiable
function of time.

We wish to determine whether
$$d\bigl(X(t)\,p(t)\bigr) = p(t)\,dX(t) + X(t)\,p'(t)\,dt.$$

**Applying Itô's lemma.** Define $f(t,x) = x\,p(t)$. The partial derivatives are
$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} = x\,p'(t), \qquad \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}
 = p(t), \qquad \frac{\partial^{2} f}{\partial x^{2}} = 0.$$

Itô's lemma gives
$$df = \frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\,dt + \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\,dX + \
frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2} f}{\partial x^{2}}\,(dX)^{2}.$$

Substituting,
$$d\bigl(X(t)\,p(t)\bigr) = X(t)\,p'(t)\,dt + p(t)\,dX(t) + \frac{1}{2}\cdot 0 \
cdot (dX)^{2}.$$

The Itô correction term $\tfrac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2} f}{\partial x^{2}}(dX)^{
2}$ vanishes precisely because $\frac{\partial^{2} f}{\partial x^{2}} = 0$.

Therefore,
$$d\bigl(X(t)\,p(t)\bigr) = p(t)\,dX(t) + X(t)\,p'(t)\,dt.$$

**Justification.** The ordinary (classical) chain rule holds here because the pr
oduct $X(t)\,p(t)$ is **linear** in the stochastic variable $X(t)$. The Itô corr
ection term — which is the term that distinguishes Itô calculus from ordinary ca
lculus and which arises from the nonzero quadratic variation of $X(t)$ — is prop
ortional to the second derivative of the integrand with respect to the stochasti
c argument. Since $f(t,x) = x\,p(t)$ is linear in $x$, this second derivative is
 identically zero, and the correction disappears.

Equivalently, expanding directly:
$$d\bigl(X(t)\,p(t)\bigr) = p(t)\,dX(t) + X(t)\,dp(t) + \underbrace{dX(t)\,dp(t)
}_{=0},$$
where the cross term $dX(t)\,dp(t) = dX(t)\cdot p'(t)\,dt = 0$ because $dX(t)\,d
t = \sigma(t)\,dW(t)\,dt = 0$ (the mixed $dW\,dt$ term vanishes) and $\mu(t)\,dt
\cdot dt = 0$. Since $dp(t) = p'(t)\,dt$ (ordinary calculus, as $p$ is determini
stic), we recover the same result.

**Conclusion.** Yes, the ordinary chain rule holds for the product of an Itô pro
cess $X(t)$ and a non-stochastic function $p(t)$:
$$\boxed{\,d\bigl(X(t)\,p(t)\bigr) = p(t)\,dX(t) + X(t)\,p'(t)\,dt\,}.$$
The Itô correction term vanishes because the product is linear in the stochastic
 variable, making the second partial derivative with respect to $x$ equal to zer
o.
