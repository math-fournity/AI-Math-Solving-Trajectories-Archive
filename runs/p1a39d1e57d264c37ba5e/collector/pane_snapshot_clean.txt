Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether an automorphism $f$ of the
octonions with the given block-diagonal $SO(7)$ form can always be expressed in
a Cayley basis generated by a Cayley triple.

## Proof

**Answer: Yes, such a Cayley triple always exists.**

### Setup and Key Group-Theoretic Facts

The automorphism group of the octonions is the exceptional Lie group $G_2$, whic
h is a compact, connected Lie group of dimension 14 and rank 2, embedded in $SO(
7)$ via its action on $\operatorname{Im} \mathbb{O}$.

Since $G_2$ is compact and connected, **every element $f \in G_2$ is conjugate t
o an element of the maximal torus $T \subset G_2$**: there exist $g \in G_2$ and
 $t \in T$ such that $f = g \, t \, g^{-1}$.

### The Maximal Torus in a Cayley Basis

Fix a Cayley triple $(i_0, j_0, l_0)$ generating the Cayley basis:
$$\{e_1^0, e_2^0, e_3^0, e_4^0, e_5^0, e_6^0, e_7^0\} = \{i_0,\; j_0,\; k_0=i_0j
_0,\; l_0,\; i_0l_0,\; j_0l_0,\; k_0l_0\}.$$

Using the Cayley–Dickson construction $\mathbb{O} = \mathbb{H} \oplus \mathbb{H}
l_0$, I verify that the 2-parameter family of maps $t_{\alpha,\beta}$ defined by
:
- Rotation of the $(e_2^0, e_3^0)$-plane by angle $\alpha$,
- Rotation of the $(e_4^0, e_5^0)$-plane by angle $\beta$,
- Rotation of the $(e_6^0, e_7^0)$-plane by angle $\alpha + \beta$,
- Fixing $e_1^0 = i_0$,

**preserves the octonion multiplication and hence is an automorphism.** The crit
ical check is the cross-block products. For instance:

$$f(e_2) \times f(e_4) = (\cos\alpha\, e_2 - \sin\alpha\, e_3) \times (\cos\beta
\, e_4 - \sin\beta\, e_5)$$
$$= \cos(\alpha+\beta)\, e_6 - \sin(\alpha+\beta)\, e_7 = f(e_6),$$

using $e_2 \times e_4 = e_6$, $e_2 \times e_5 = e_7$, $e_3 \times e_4 = e_7$, $e
_3 \times e_5 = -e_6$. Similarly:

$$f(e_2) \times f(e_5) = \sin(\alpha+\beta)\, e_6 + \cos(\alpha+\beta)\, e_7 = f
(e_7),$$

$$f(e_3) \times f(e_4) = \sin(\alpha+\beta)\, e_6 + \cos(\alpha+\beta)\, e_7 = f
(e_7),$$

$$f(e_3) \times f(e_5) = -\cos(\alpha+\beta)\, e_6 + \sin(\alpha+\beta)\, e_7 =
f(-e_6).$$

All within-block products (e.g., $e_1 \times e_2 = e_3$) and products involving
$e_1$ (e.g., $e_1 \times e_6 = -e_7$, $e_1 \times e_7 = e_6$) are also verified
to be preserved. Since $t_{\alpha,\beta}$ preserves all basis products and is or
thogonal, it is an automorphism.

This 2-parameter family $\{t_{\alpha,\beta}\}$ forms a **2-dimensional torus**,
which is the maximal torus of $G_2$ (since $\operatorname{rank}(G_2) = 2$). In t
he problem's notation (with sign convention $\phi_1 = -\alpha$, $\phi_2 = -\beta
$), the constraint is:

$$\boxed{\phi_3 = \phi_1 + \phi_2 \pmod{2\pi}}.$$

This constraint is **automatically satisfied** for any $f \in G_2$, since $f$ is
 an automorphism and the above computations show the automorphism condition forc
es it.

### Conjugation Preserves the Cayley Basis Structure

Given $f \in G_2$, write $f = g \, t_{\alpha,\beta} \, g^{-1}$ with $g \in G_2$.
 Define:
$$(i, j, l) := (g(i_0),\; g(j_0),\; g(l_0)).$$

Since $g$ is an automorphism:
- $i, j, l$ are unit imaginary octonions (norm preserved, $\mathbb{R}$ fixed).
- $l \perp i, j, ij$ (orthogonality preserved; $g(ij_0) = g(i_0)g(j_0) = ij$).

So $(i, j, l)$ is a **Cayley triple**. The conjugated basis is:
$$\{g(e_1^0), \ldots, g(e_7^0)\} = \{i,\; j,\; ij,\; l,\; il,\; jl,\; (ij)l\},$$

which is exactly the Cayley basis generated by $(i, j, l)$. In this basis, $f =
g \, t_{\alpha,\beta} \, g^{-1}$ acts as $t_{\alpha,\beta}$ does in the original
 basis, i.e., with the block-diagonal form:
$$f(e_1) = e_1, \quad f|_{(e_2,e_3)} = R_{\phi_1}, \quad f|_{(e_4,e_5)} = R_{\ph
i_2}, \quad f|_{(e_6,e_7)} = R_{\phi_3},$$
with $\phi_3 = \phi_1 + \phi_2$.

### Conclusion

Every automorphism $f \in G_2$ is conjugate to a maximal torus element, the maxi
mal torus has exactly the required block-diagonal form in a Cayley basis, and co
njugation by an automorphism maps a Cayley basis to another Cayley basis. Theref
ore:

**Yes, there exists a Cayley triple $(i, j, l)$ in $\operatorname{Im} \mathbb{O}
$ such that $e_1 = i$, $e_2 = j$, $e_3 = ij$, $e_4 = l$, $e_5 = il$, $e_6 = jl$,
 $e_7 = (ij)l$.**
