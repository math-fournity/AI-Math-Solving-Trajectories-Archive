## Proof

Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots$ be i.i.d. random variables with mean $\mu$, variance $\si
gma^2$, and moment generating function $M_X(t) = \mathbb{E}[e^{tX}]$ existing in
 a neighborhood of $0$. Define the standardized variables
$$Y_i = \frac{X_i - \mu}{\sigma}, \qquad i = 1, 2, \ldots$$
so that $\mathbb{E}[Y_i] = 0$, $\operatorname{Var}(Y_i) = 1$, and
$$S_n^* = \frac{S_n - n\mu}{\sqrt{n}\,\sigma} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^n Y
_i.$$

---

### Step 1. Derive the mgf of $S_n^*$

The mgf of $Y$ is
$$M_Y(t) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{t(X-\mu)/\sigma}\right] = e^{-t\mu/\sigma}\, M_X
\!\left(\frac{t}{\sigma}\right).$$

By independence of the $Y_i$,
$$M_{S_n^*}(t) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\exp\!\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^n Y_
i\right)\right] = \prod_{i=1}^n M_Y\!\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right) = \left[M_Y
\!\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]^n.$$

Equivalently, in terms of $M_X$:
$$\boxed{M_{S_n^*}(t) = \left[\exp\!\left(-\frac{t\mu}{\sqrt{n}\,\sigma}\right)
M_X\!\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}\,\sigma}\right)\right]^n.}$$

---

### Step 2. Set up the limit

We wish to show
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} M_{S_n^*}(t) = e^{t^2/2}.$$

Taking logarithms,
$$\log M_{S_n^*}(t) = n \log M_Y\!\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right).$$

As $n \to \infty$, the argument $t/\sqrt{n} \to 0$, so $M_Y(t/\sqrt{n}) \to M_Y(
0) = 1$, hence $\log M_Y(t/\sqrt{n}) \to 0$. The product is therefore of the ind
eterminate form $\infty \cdot 0$.

Substitute $u = 1/\sqrt{n}$ (so $n = 1/u^2$, and $u \to 0^+$ as $n \to \infty$):
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} n \log M_Y\!\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right) = \lim_{u \to 0^
+} \frac{\log M_Y(tu)}{u^2}.$$

Since $M_Y(0)=1$, both numerator and denominator tend to $0$: this is a $\tfrac{
0}{0}$ form, so L'Hôpital's rule applies.

---

### Step 3. First application of L'Hôpital's rule

Differentiate numerator and denominator with respect to $u$:

- Numerator: $\displaystyle \frac{d}{du}\log M_Y(tu) = \frac{t\, M_Y'(tu)}{M_Y(t
u)}.$
- Denominator: $\displaystyle \frac{d}{du} u^2 = 2u.$

As $u \to 0^+$:
$$\frac{t\, M_Y'(tu)}{M_Y(tu)} \to \frac{t\, M_Y'(0)}{M_Y(0)} = \frac{t \cdot \m
athbb{E}[Y]}{1} = 0, \qquad 2u \to 0.$$

The limit is still $\tfrac{0}{0}$, so we apply L'Hôpital's rule a second time.

---

### Step 4. Second application of L'Hôpital's rule

Differentiate again:

- Numerator: $\displaystyle \frac{d}{du}\!\left[\frac{t\, M_Y'(tu)}{M_Y(tu)}\rig
ht] = t \cdot \frac{t\, M_Y''(tu)\, M_Y(tu) - t\, [M_Y'(tu)]^2}{[M_Y(tu)]^2} = \
frac{t^2\bigl[M_Y''(tu)\, M_Y(tu) - [M_Y'(tu)]^2\bigr]}{[M_Y(tu)]^2}.$
- Denominator: $\displaystyle \frac{d}{du} 2u = 2.$

As $u \to 0^+$, using $M_Y(0)=1$, $M_Y'(0) = \mathbb{E}[Y] = 0$, $M_Y''(0) = \ma
thbb{E}[Y^2] = 1$:
$$\frac{t^2\bigl[M_Y''(0)\, M_Y(0) - [M_Y'(0)]^2\bigr]}{[M_Y(0)]^2} = \frac{t^2\
,(1 \cdot 1 - 0^2)}{1^2} = t^2.$$

Therefore
$$\lim_{u \to 0^+} \frac{\log M_Y(tu)}{u^2} = \frac{t^2}{2}.$$

---

### Step 5. Conclude

We have shown
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} \log M_{S_n^*}(t) = \frac{t^2}{2},$$
and by continuity of the exponential,
$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} M_{S_n^*}(t) = e^{t^2/2}.}$$

The function $e^{t^2/2}$ is precisely the mgf of a standard normal random variab
le $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$. Hence the mgf of the standardized sum $S_n^*$ conv
erges to that of a standard normal variable as $n \to \infty$, which is the mome
nt-generating-function form of the Central Limit Theorem.

**Remark on the hypothesis $\mu \neq 0$.** The derivation above depends only on
the moments of the centered variable $Y = (X-\mu)/\sigma$, namely $\mathbb{E}[Y]
=0$ and $\mathbb{E}[Y^2]=1$, and on the existence of $M_X$ near $0$. These hold
for every $\mu$ (including $\mu = 0$); the condition $\mu \neq 0$ is not actuall
y required for the limit to hold. It is likely imposed only to make the centerin
g step $S_n - n\mu$ non-trivial, but the proof is identical in the $\mu = 0$ cas
e.
