## Proof

**Setup and approximations.** By the Prime Number Theorem, the density of primes
 near a large integer $n$ is approximately $1/\ln n$. An $x$-digit number $n$ sa
tisfies $10^{x-1} \le n < 10^x$, so $\ln n$ ranges over $[(x-1)\ln 10,\; x\ln 10
)$. The two most natural approximations for the probability that a random $x$-di
git number is prime are obtained by evaluating the prime density $1/\ln n$ at th
e two endpoints of this interval:

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{\ln(10^x)} = \frac{1}{x\ln 10}, \qquad g(x) = \frac{1}{\ln(10^
{x-1})} = \frac{1}{(x-1)\ln 10}.$$

Both are legitimate approximations to the same target quantity (the prime probab
ility for $x$-digit numbers), differing only in which boundary of the interval i
s used.

**Computing $h(x)$.** For $x \ge 2$:

$$h(x) = f(x) - g(x) = \frac{1}{x\ln 10} - \frac{1}{(x-1)\ln 10} = \frac{1}{\ln
10}\left(\frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{x-1}\right) = \frac{1}{\ln 10}\cdot\frac{(x-1)-x
}{x(x-1)} = \frac{-1}{x(x-1)\ln 10}.$$

Hence

$$|h(x)| = \frac{1}{x(x-1)\ln 10}.$$

**Existence of $N$.** For $x \ge 2$, the function $x(x-1)$ is positive and stric
tly increasing, and $x(x-1) \to \infty$ as $x \to \infty$. Therefore

$$|h(x)| = \frac{1}{x(x-1)\ln 10} \longrightarrow 0 \quad\text{as } x\to\infty.$
$

Since $|h(x)| \to 0$, for any $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $N$ such that $|h(x
)| < \varepsilon$ for all $x \ge N$. Taking $\varepsilon = 10^{-10}$, we need

$$\frac{1}{x(x-1)\ln 10} < 10^{-10} \iff x(x-1) > \frac{10^{10}}{\ln 10}.$$

Since $\ln 10 \approx 2.302585$, we have $10^{10}/\ln 10 \approx 4.343 \times 10
^9$. Solving $x^2 - x - 4.343\times 10^9 > 0$:

$$x > \frac{1 + \sqrt{1 + 4\cdot 4.343\times 10^9}}{2} \approx \frac{1 + 131\,80
7}{2} \approx 65\,904.$$

Thus $N = 65\,905$ satisfies the requirement: for every $x \ge N$,

$$x(x-1) \ge N(N-1) > \frac{10^{10}}{\ln 10}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad |h(x)| = \fr
ac{1}{x(x-1)\ln 10} < 10^{-10}.$$

**Conclusion.** Yes, such a value of $N$ exists. The difference $h(x) = f(x) - g
(x)$ decays as $O(1/x^2)$, so it eventually falls below any positive threshold.
Concretely, $N = 65\,905$ suffices.
