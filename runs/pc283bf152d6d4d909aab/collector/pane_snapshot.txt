## Proof

**Setup and reduction.** Since $u(0)=0$ is given, $0\in\Omega$. The graph $X=\{(
x,u(x)):x\in\Omega\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ contains the origin $(0,0)$. The in
tersection is

$$X\cap B_r(0)=\{(x,u(x)) : x\in\Omega,\; |x|^2+u(x)^2<r^2\}.$$

The map $\Phi:\Omega\to X$, $\Phi(x)=(x,u(x))$, is a $C^2$-diffeomorphism onto $
X$ (its inverse is the projection $(x,t)\mapsto x$). Hence $X\cap B_r(0)$ is con
nected if and only if the sublevel set

$$S_r:=\{x\in\Omega : f(x)<r^2\},\qquad f(x):=|x|^2+u(x)^2,$$

is connected. So it suffices to show $S_r$ is connected for all sufficiently sma
ll $r>0$.

**$0$ is a non-degenerate local minimum of $f$.** Since $f(x)\ge |x|^2\ge 0$ and
 $f(0)=0$, the origin is a strict global minimum restricted to any neighborhood.
 Compute the gradient:

$$\nabla f(x)=2x+2\,u(x)\,\nabla u(x),\qquad \nabla f(0)=2\cdot 0+2\,\underbrace
{u(0)}_{=0}\,\nabla u(0)=0.$$

So $0$ is a critical point. The Hessian is

$$D^2f(x)=2I+2\,\nabla u(x)\,\nabla u(x)^\top+2\,u(x)\,D^2u(x),$$

and evaluating at $0$ (using $u(0)=0$):

$$D^2f(0)=2I+2\,\nabla u(0)\,\nabla u(0)^\top = 2\bigl(I+aa^\top\bigr),\qquad a:
=\nabla u(0).$$

The matrix $I+aa^\top$ is positive definite: for any $v\neq 0$,

$$v^\top(I+aa^\top)v=|v|^2+(a\cdot v)^2>0.$$

Hence $D^2f(0)$ is positive definite, and $0$ is a **non-degenerate** local mini
mum of $f$.

**Applying the Morse lemma.** Since $f\in C^2$, $0$ is a non-degenerate critical
 point with Morse index $0$ (all eigenvalues of $D^2f(0)$ are positive), the Mor
se lemma furnishes an open neighborhood $U\subset\Omega$ of $0$ and a $C^1$-diff
eomorphism $\varphi:U\to V\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ (with $V$ open, $0\in V$) such th
at

$$f\bigl(\varphi^{-1}(y)\bigr)=y_1^2+\cdots+y_n^2=|y|^2,\qquad y\in V.$$

Consequently, for $r>0$ small enough that the Euclidean ball $\{y:|y|<r\}\Subset
 V$,

$$S_r\cap U=\{x\in U:f(x)<r^2\}=\varphi^{-1}\bigl(\{y\in V:|y|^2<r^2\}\bigr)=\va
rphi^{-1}\bigl(B_r^{\,n}(0)\bigr),$$

which is the preimage of an $n$-dimensional open ball under a diffeomorphism, he
nce **connected** (indeed diffeomorphic to a ball).

It remains to check that $S_r\setminus U$ contributes nothing for small $r$. Sin
ce $f\ge |x|^2$ and $f(0)=0$ with $0$ a strict local minimum, there is $\delta>0
$ with $f(x)>0$ for $0<|x|<\delta$. On the compact annulus $\{x:\delta/2\le |x|\
le \delta\}\subset\Omega$ (shrinking $\delta$ so this lies in $\Omega$), the con
tinuous function $f$ attains a positive minimum $m>0$. Choose $r<\sqrt{m}$; then
 $S_r\subseteq\{x:|x|<\delta/2\}\subset U$, so $S_r=S_r\cap U$, which is connect
ed.

**Conclusion.** For all sufficiently small $r>0$, the sublevel set $S_r$ is conn
ected, and therefore $X\cap B_r(0)=\Phi(S_r)$ is connected. $\blacksquare$
