## Proof

Let $p = xy$. Since $x$ and $y$ appear in the expressions $\frac{1}{x}$ and $\fr
ac{1}{y}$, both $x$ and $y$ are nonzero, hence $p = xy \neq 0$.

**From the first equation** $x + \frac{1}{y} = A$, multiply both sides by $y$:

$$xy + 1 = Ay \quad \Longrightarrow \quad p + 1 = Ay. \tag{1}$$

**From the second equation** $\frac{1}{x} + y = B$, multiply both sides by $x$:

$$1 + xy = Bx \quad \Longrightarrow \quad p + 1 = Bx. \tag{2}$$

From (1) and (2) we can express $x$ and $y$ in terms of $p$:

$$x = \frac{p+1}{B}, \qquad y = \frac{p+1}{A}.$$

Now substitute into $p = xy$:

$$p = \frac{(p+1)^2}{AB}.$$

Multiplying both sides by $AB$:

$$p \cdot AB = (p+1)^2 = p^2 + 2p + 1.$$

Rearranging:

$$p^2 + (2 - AB)\,p + 1 = 0.$$

Since $p \neq 0$, divide through by $p$:

$$p + (2 - AB) + \frac{1}{p} = 0,$$

$$p + \frac{1}{p} = AB - 2.$$

Recalling $p = xy$ and $A = \sqrt{M_1}$, $B = \lceil M_{11} \rceil$:

$$xy + \frac{1}{xy} = AB - 2 = \sqrt{M_1}\;\lceil M_{11}\rceil - 2.$$
