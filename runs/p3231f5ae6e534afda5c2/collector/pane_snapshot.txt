## Solution

We compute $I = \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{x}{x^{4}+1}\,dx$ using a **sector contou
r** of angle $\pi/2$, exploiting the rotational symmetry of $z^{4}$.

### Setup

Let $f(z) = \dfrac{z}{z^{4}+1}$. The poles satisfy $z^{4}=-1$, i.e. $z_{k}=e^{i(
2k+1)\pi/4}$ for $k=0,1,2,3$:
$$z_{0}=e^{i\pi/4},\quad z_{1}=e^{i3\pi/4},\quad z_{2}=e^{i5\pi/4},\quad z_{3}=e
^{i7\pi/4}.$$

**Contour $C_R$**: the boundary of the sector $\{z: 0\le \arg z \le \pi/2,\; |z|
\le R\}$, traversed counterclockwise:
1. $\Gamma_1$: real axis from $0$ to $R$,
2. $\Gamma_2$: circular arc $z=Re^{i\theta}$, $0\le\theta\le\pi/2$,
3. $\Gamma_3$: imaginary axis from $iR$ back to $0$.

### Poles inside the sector

Only poles with $0 < \arg z < \pi/2$ lie inside. Among the four roots, only
$$z_{0}=e^{i\pi/4}\quad(\arg=\pi/4)$$
falls in this range. ($z_{1}$ has argument $3\pi/4>\pi/2$; the others are in the
 lower half-plane.)

### Residue at $z_{0}=e^{i\pi/4}$

Since $z^{4}+1$ has simple zeros,
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,z_{0})=\frac{z}{(z^{4}+1)'}\bigg|_{z=z_{0}}=\frac{z_{0}}{
4z_{0}^{3}}=\frac{1}{4z_{0}^{2}}=\frac{1}{4e^{i\pi/2}}=\frac{1}{4i}=-\frac{i}{4}
.$$

### The arc vanishes

On $\Gamma_2$, $|z|=R$ and $|z^{4}+1|\ge R^{4}-1$, so
$$\left|\int_{\Gamma_2}f(z)\,dz\right|\le \frac{R}{R^{4}-1}\cdot\frac{\pi R}{2}\
sim\frac{\pi}{2R^{2}}\xrightarrow[R\to\infty]{}0.$$

### Parametrize the two radial sides

- **$\Gamma_1$**: $z=x$, $x:0\to R$:
$$\int_{\Gamma_1}f(z)\,dz=\int_{0}^{R}\frac{x}{x^{4}+1}\,dx.$$

- **$\Gamma_3$**: $z=it$, $t:R\to 0$, $dz=i\,dt$, and $(it)^{4}=t^{4}$:
$$\int_{\Gamma_3}f(z)\,dz=\int_{R}^{0}\frac{it}{t^{4}+1}\,i\,dt=\int_{R}^{0}\fra
c{-t}{t^{4}+1}\,dt=\int_{0}^{R}\frac{t}{t^{4}+1}\,dt.$$

### Apply the residue theorem

$$\oint_{C_R}f(z)\,dz=2\pi i\,\operatorname{Res}(f,z_{0})=2\pi i\cdot\left(-\fra
c{i}{4}\right)=\frac{\pi}{2}.$$

Taking $R\to\infty$ and using the vanishing of the arc:
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x}{x^{4}+1}\,dx+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t}{t^{4}+1}\,dt=
\frac{\pi}{2}.$$

Both integrals are identical, so
$$2I=\frac{\pi}{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad I=\frac{\pi}{4}.$$

### Verification (real-variable check)

With $u=x^{2}$, $du=2x\,dx$:
$$I=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{du}{u^{2}+1}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}
=\frac{\pi}{4}.\quad\checkmark$$

### Result

$$\boxed{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x}{x^{4}+1}\,dx=\frac{\pi}{4}}$$
