## Solution

**Step 1: Conformal map to the upper half-plane.**

Use the Cayley transform
$$w = \phi(z) = i\,\frac{1-z}{1+z},$$
which maps the unit disc $\{|z|<1\}$ conformally onto the upper half-plane $\{\o
peratorname{Im} w > 0\}$, with the unit circle mapping to the real axis.

**Step 2: Determine where the boundary values land.**

For $z = e^{i\theta}$ on the unit circle:
$$1 - e^{i\theta} = -2i\,e^{i\theta/2}\sin(\theta/2), \qquad 1 + e^{i\theta} = 2
\,e^{i\theta/2}\cos(\theta/2),$$
so
$$w = i\cdot\frac{-2i\sin(\theta/2)}{2\cos(\theta/2)} = \tan(\theta/2).$$

- For $0 < \theta < \pi$: $\theta/2 \in (0,\pi/2)$, so $\tan(\theta/2) > 0$; the
 upper semicircle maps to $(0,+\infty)$.
- For $\pi < \theta < 2\pi$: $\theta/2 \in (\pi/2,\pi)$, so $\tan(\theta/2) < 0$
; the lower semicircle maps to $(-\infty, 0)$.

Thus the boundary problem transforms to: find a harmonic function $\tilde U(w)$
on $\{\operatorname{Im} w > 0\}$ with
$$\tilde U(w) = \begin{cases} +1, & w \in (0,+\infty), \\ -1, & w \in (-\infty,
0). \end{cases}$$

**Step 3: Solve on the upper half-plane.**

The function $\operatorname{Arg}(w)$ (principal argument, taking values in $(0,\
pi)$ on the upper half-plane) is harmonic there, with boundary values $0$ on $(0
,+\infty)$ and $\pi$ on $(-\infty, 0)$. Therefore
$$\tilde U(w) = 1 - \frac{2}{\pi}\operatorname{Arg}(w)$$
satisfies $\tilde U = 1$ on $(0,+\infty)$ and $\tilde U = -1$ on $(-\infty,0)$.

Since $\operatorname{Arg}(w) = \operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Log} w)$, we may
write
$$\tilde U(w) = 1 - \frac{2}{\pi}\operatorname{Im}\!\bigl(\operatorname{Log} w\b
igr) = \operatorname{Re}\!\left[1 + \frac{2i}{\pi}\operatorname{Log} w\right].$$

**Step 4: Compose back to the disc.**

Setting $w = i(1-z)/(1+z)$:
$$\boxed{U(z) = 1 - \frac{2}{\pi}\,\operatorname{Im}\!\left[\operatorname{Log}\!
\left(i\,\frac{1-z}{1+z}\right)\right] = \operatorname{Re}\!\left[1 + \frac{2i}{
\pi}\operatorname{Log}\!\left(i\,\frac{1-z}{1+z}\right)\right].}$$

**Determination of the multi-valued function.** The logarithm $\operatorname{Log
}$ is taken as the **principal branch** with argument $\operatorname{Arg} \in (0
,\pi)$ on the upper half-plane (branch cut along $(-\infty, 0]$). This is well-d
efined because $i(1-z)/(1+z)$ lies in the open upper half-plane for every $z \in
 \{|z|<1\}$, so the argument never reaches the branch cut.

**Verification.**

- *Harmonicity:* $\operatorname{Log} w$ is holomorphic on the upper half-plane (
principal branch), so $\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Log} w)$ is harmonic ther
e. The Cayley map $w = i(1-z)/(1+z)$ is holomorphic and maps the disc to the upp
er half-plane, so the composition $U(z)$ is harmonic on $\{|z|<1\}$.

- *Boundary values:* As $r \to 1^-$ with $0 < \theta < \pi$, $w \to \tan(\theta/
2) > 0$, so $\operatorname{Arg}(w) \to 0$ and $U \to 1$. As $r \to 1^-$ with $\p
i < \theta < 2\pi$, $w \to \tan(\theta/2) < 0$, so $\operatorname{Arg}(w) \to \p
i$ and $U \to 1 - 2 = -1$. Both match the prescribed boundary data. $\blacksquar
e$
