## Solution

We determine the spectrum (Gelfand space, i.e. the space of nonzero multiplicati
ve linear functionals / characters with the weak-* topology) of the C\*-algebra
$A = C(K) \oplus C(K')$.

### Setup and notation

Let $A = C(K) \oplus C(K')$ with coordinatewise operations and sup-norm $\|(f,g)
\| = \max(\|f\|_\infty, \|g\|_\infty)$. Denote the unit by $\mathbf{1} = (1, 1)$
. Introduce the two central projections
$$p = (1, 0), \qquad q = (0, 1),$$
so that $p + q = \mathbf{1}$, $pq = 0$, and $p, q$ are self-adjoint idempotents.
 The ideals $A_p := pA = C(K) \oplus \{0\}$ and $A_q := qA = \{0\} \oplus C(K')$
 are closed \*-ideals with $A = A_p \oplus A_q$.

### Step 1. Characters split along the central projections

Let $\omega : A \to \mathbb{C}$ be a character (a nonzero, unital, multiplicativ
e linear functional). Since $p^2 = p$, the value $\omega(p) \in \mathbb{C}$ sati
sfies $\omega(p)^2 = \omega(p)$, so $\omega(p) \in \{0, 1\}$. Likewise $\omega(q
) \in \{0,1\}$. From $p + q = \mathbf{1}$ and unitality $\omega(\mathbf{1}) = 1$
,
$$\omega(p) + \omega(q) = 1.$$
Hence exactly one of $\omega(p), \omega(q)$ equals $1$.

**Case $\omega(p) = 1$, $\omega(q) = 0$.** For any $(f, g) \in A$,
$$\omega(f, g) = \omega\bigl(p\cdot(f,g)\bigr) = \omega(f, 0) = \omega\bigl((f,0
)\bigr).$$
The map $\varphi : C(K) \to \mathbb{C}$ defined by $\varphi(f) := \omega(f, 0)$
is a character of $C(K)$: it is linear, unital ($\varphi(1) = \omega(1,0) = \ome
ga(p) = 1$), and multiplicative ($\varphi(ff') = \omega(ff', 0) = \omega((f,0)(f
',0)) = \omega(f,0)\,\omega(f',0) = \varphi(f)\varphi(f')$). So $\omega = \varph
i \circ \pi_1$ where $\pi_1 : A \to C(K)$ is the first-coordinate projection.

**Case $\omega(p) = 0$, $\omega(q) = 1$.** Symmetrically $\omega = \psi \circ \p
i_2$ for a character $\psi$ of $C(K')$.

Conversely, every character $\varphi$ of $C(K)$ yields a character $\omega_\varp
hi(f,g) = \varphi(f)$ of $A$, and every character $\psi$ of $C(K')$ yields $\ome
ga_\psi(f,g) = \psi(g)$. These are distinct (they take different values on $p$).
 Therefore, as a set,
$$\sigma(A) \;\cong\; \sigma\bigl(C(K)\bigr) \;\sqcup\; \sigma\bigl(C(K')\bigr).
$$

### Step 2. Identify each piece with the underlying space

By the classical Gelfand–Kolmogorov theorem, for a compact Hausdorff space $L$ t
he evaluation map
$$\eta_L : L \longrightarrow \sigma(C(L)), \qquad x \mapsto \mathrm{ev}_x, \quad
 \mathrm{ev}_x(f) = f(x)$$
is a homeomorphism. (Injectivity follows from Urysohn separating distinct points
; surjectivity from the fact that the kernel of a character is a maximal ideal,
equivalently $\{f : f(x_0) = 0\}$ for a unique $x_0 \in L$; continuity of $\eta_
L$ and of its inverse both follow from the weak-* topology being the topology of
 pointwise convergence on $C(L)$.)

Combining with Step 1, as a set
$$\sigma(A) \;\cong\; K \;\sqcup\; K'.$$

### Step 3. The topology is the disjoint-union (coproduct) topology

Equip $\sigma(A)$ with the weak-* topology. We show the two pieces
$$\Sigma_K := \{\omega \in \sigma(A) : \omega(p) = 1\}, \qquad \Sigma_{K'} := \{
\omega \in \sigma(A) : \omega(p) = 0\}$$
are **clopen** and each carries its natural topology.

The function $\widehat{p} : \sigma(A) \to \mathbb{C}$, $\widehat{p}(\omega) = \o
mega(p)$, is continuous by definition of the weak-* topology. Since $\widehat{p}
$ takes only the values $0$ and $1$,
$$\Sigma_K = \widehat{p}^{-1}(\{1\}), \qquad \Sigma_{K'} = \widehat{p}^{-1}(\{0\
})$$
are both closed (preimages of closed singletons) and complementary, hence both c
lopen.

Now restrict to $\Sigma_K \cong \sigma(C(K)) \cong K$. A net $\omega_\alpha \in
\Sigma_K$ converges to $\omega \in \Sigma_K$ in the weak-* topology of $\sigma(A
)$ iff $\omega_\alpha(f,g) \to \omega(f,g)$ for all $(f,g) \in A$ iff $\varphi_\
alpha(f) \to \varphi(f)$ for all $f \in C(K)$ (taking $g = 0$), which is precise
ly weak-* convergence in $\sigma(C(K))$. So the subspace topology on $\Sigma_K$
agrees with the Gelfand topology of $\sigma(C(K))$, which under $\eta_K$ is the
topology of $K$. The same holds for $\Sigma_{K'} \cong K'$.

Because the two pieces are clopen and exhaust the space, a set $U \subseteq \sig
ma(A)$ is open iff $U \cap \Sigma_K$ is open in $\Sigma_K$ and $U \cap \Sigma_{K
'}$ is open in $\Sigma_{K'}$. This is exactly the **topological disjoint union**
 (coproduct) topology.

### Conclusion

The spectrum of $C(K) \oplus C(K')$ is the topological disjoint union of $K$ and
 $K'$:
$$\boxed{\;\sigma\bigl(C(K) \oplus C(K')\bigr) \;\cong\; K \;\sqcup\; K'\;}$$
where $K \sqcup K'$ denotes the disjoint union (coproduct) of the two compact Ha
usdorff spaces, equipped with the topology in which $K$ and $K'$ are clopen summ
ands each retaining its original topology. Equivalently, it is the Stone space o
f the Boolean algebra of clopen subsets of $K$ and $K'$ combined, and the Gelfan
d transform identifies $C(K) \oplus C(K')$ with $C(K \sqcup K')$, consistent wit
h the general fact that $C(X \sqcup Y) \cong C(X) \oplus C(Y)$ for compact Hausd
orff $X, Y$.
