## Proof

**Key observation:** Since $\gcd(y, y+1) = 1$, if $x \mid y(y+1)$ then $x = ab$
with $a \mid y$, $b \mid (y+1)$, $\gcd(a,b)=1$. The condition "$x \nmid y$ and $
x \nmid (y+1)$" forces $a > 1$ and $b > 1$, so $x$ must have a nontrivial coprim
e factorization — in particular, **$x$ cannot be a prime power** (a prime power
$p^k$ dividing $y(y+1)$ forces $p^k \mid y$ or $p^k \mid (y+1)$). The same appli
es to $x+1$.

**Finding the smallest $x$:** We need $x$ and $x+1$ both composite and neither a
 prime power. Checking small values:

| $x$ | $x$ | $x+1$ | Valid? |
|-----|-----|-------|--------|
| 8 | $2^3$ (pp) | $3^2$ (pp) | No |

| 12 | 12 | 13 (prime) | No |
| 13 | prime | 14 | No |
| **14** | $2\cdot7$ | $3\cdot5$ | **Yes** |

So $x = 14$ is the smallest candidate, with $x(x+1) = 210$.

**Finding the smallest $y$ for $x=14$:** We need $210 \mid y(y+1)$ with $14 \nmi
d y$, $14 \nmid (y+1)$, $15 \nmid y$, $15 \nmid (y+1)$.

- **$14 = 2\cdot 7$:** Since $2 \mid y(y+1)$ always, we need $7 \mid y$ or $7 \m
id (y+1)$. To avoid $14 \mid y$ and $14 \mid (y+1)$, the factors 2 and 7 must sp
lit: either ($7 \mid y$, $y$ odd) or ($7 \mid (y+1)$, $y$ even).

- **$15 = 3\cdot 5$:** Since $3 \mid y(y+1)$ always, we need $5 \mid y$ or $5 \m
id (y+1)$. To avoid $15 \mid y$ and $15 \mid (y+1)$, the factors 3 and 5 must sp
lit: either ($3 \mid y$, $5 \mid (y+1)$) or ($5 \mid y$, $3 \mid (y+1)$).

**Best combination:** Take $7 \mid (y+1)$, $y$ even; $5 \mid y$, $3 \mid (y+1)$.
 This gives:
$$y \equiv 6 \pmod{7},\quad y \equiv 0 \pmod{5},\quad y \equiv 2 \pmod{3},\quad
y \text{ even}.$$
From $y \equiv 0 \pmod{5}$ and $y \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$: $y \equiv 5 \pmod{15}$. Wr

**Verification:** $y = 20$: $y(y+1) = 420 = 2 \cdot 210$, so $210 \mid 420$. ✓

**No smaller $x^2+y^2$ exists:** Since $x(x+1) \mid y(y+1)$ with $y \geq 1$, we
have $y(y+1) \geq x(x+1)$, hence $y \geq x$. For $x = 14$, $y \geq 14$, and chec

violates the condition; $y = 15,\ldots,19$ all fail divisibility by 210. So $y =
 20$ is minimal for $x=14$.

For $x \geq 15$: values $x = 15, 16, 17, 18, 19$ are all eliminated (one of $x,
x+1$ is prime or a prime power). For $x \geq 20$, $x^2 + y^2 \geq 20^2 + 20^2 =

$$x^2 + y^2 = 14^2 + 20^2 = 196 + 400 = \boxed{596}.$$
