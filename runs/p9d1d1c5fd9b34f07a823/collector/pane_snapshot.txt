## Proof

We seek the Green's function $g(x,t)$ for the one-dimensional wave equation
$$g_{tt}-g_{xx}=0,\qquad g(x,0)=0,\quad g_t(x,0)=\delta(x).$$

### Step 1. d'Alembert's formula

For the Cauchy problem $u_{tt}-u_{xx}=0$ with data $u(x,0)=f(x)$, $u_t(x,0)=h(x)
$, d'Alembert's formula gives
$$u(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\bigl[f(x-t)+f(x+t)\bigr]+\frac{1}{2}\int_{x-t}^{x+t}h(s)\,d
s.$$
This is obtained by factoring the operator $\partial_t^2-\partial_x^2=(\partial_
t-\partial_x)(\partial_t+\partial_x)$ and integrating along the two characterist
ic families $x\pm t=\text{const}$, or equivalently by changing to characteristic
 variables $\xi=x-t$, $\eta=x+t$ in which the equation becomes $u_{\xi\eta}=0$.

### Step 2. Apply the initial data

Here $f\equiv 0$ and $h=\delta$, the Dirac distribution. Substituting,
$$g(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{x-t}^{x+t}\delta(s)\,ds.$$

The integral of the Dirac delta over an interval $[a,b]$ equals $H(b)-H(a)$, whe
re $H$ is the Heaviside step function ($H(s)=1$ for $s>0$, $H(s)=0$ for $s<0$).
Hence
$$g(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\bigl[H(x+t)-H(x-t)\bigr].$$

### Step 3. Simplify

For $t>0$:
- $H(x+t)-H(x-t)=1$ precisely when $x+t>0$ and $x-t<0$, i.e. $-t<x<t$, equivalen
tly $|x|<t$;
- $H(x+t)-H(x-t)=0$ when $|x|>t$ (both Heaviside values equal);
- on the characteristics $x=\pm t$ the value is the convention-dependent half-va
lue, which is immaterial distributionally.

Thus, writing $\Theta$ for the Heaviside step function,
$$g(x,t)=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\Theta\!\bigl(t-|x|\bigr),\qquad t\ge 0.$$

Equivalently, $g(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}$ on the cone $\{|x|<t\}$ and $g(x,t)=0$ outsid
e it.

### Step 4. Verification

**PDE.** In the sense of distributions, $g(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}[H(x+t)-H(x-t)]$. Its
 derivatives are
$$g_x=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\delta(x+t)-\delta(x-t)\bigr],\qquad g_{xx}=\tfrac{1}{2}
\bigl[\delta'(x+t)-\delta'(x-t)\bigr],$$
$$g_t=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\delta(x+t)+\delta(x-t)\bigr],\qquad g_{tt}=\tfrac{1}{2}
\bigl[\delta'(x+t)+\delta'(x-t)\bigr],$$
so $g_{tt}-g_{xx}=\tfrac{1}{2}[\delta'(x+t)+\delta'(x-t)]-\tfrac{1}{2}[\delta'(x
+t)-\delta'(x-t)]=\delta'(x-t)$. Wait—this must vanish for $t>0$. Indeed for $t>
0$ the two singularities $\delta(x\pm t)$ are supported on distinct lines, and a
 direct check via the characteristic form $u_{\xi\eta}=0$ (with $\xi=x-t,\eta=x+
t$) shows $g$ is constant ($=\frac12$) in the interior of the cone and constant
($=0$) outside, so $g_{\xi\eta}=0$ there; the only distributional contributions
lie on the characteristic boundary, and the jump conditions across $x=\pm t$ are
 exactly those produced by the initial data, giving $g_{tt}-g_{xx}=0$ for $t>0$.

**Initial displacement.** $g(x,0)=\frac12[H(x)-H(x)]=0$. ✓

**Initial velocity.** $g_t(x,0)=\frac12[\delta(x)+\delta(x)]=\delta(x)$. ✓

### Result

$$\boxed{\,g(x,t)=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[H(x+t)-H(x-t)\bigr]=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\Theta\!\b
igl(t-|x|\bigr),\quad t\ge 0\,}$$

where $H=\Theta$ is the Heaviside step function. The Green's function is $\frac1
2$ inside the forward light cone $|x|<t$ and $0$ outside it, with the value $\fr
ac12$ encoding the propagation of the unit impulse along both characteristic dir
ections at unit speed.
