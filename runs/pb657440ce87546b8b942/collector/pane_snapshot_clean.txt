## Solution

**The statement is FALSE.**

**Counterexample.** Let $X = [0,1]$ equipped with the **discrete** topology and
$Y = [0,1]$ equipped with the **usual (Euclidean)** topology. Consider the ident
ity map
$$f : X \longrightarrow Y, \qquad f(x)=x.$$

**Claim 1: Both $X$ and $Y$ are compactly generated.**

- $Y = [0,1]_{\text{Eucl}}$ is compact Hausdorff, and every compact Hausdorff sp
ace is compactly generated (it is a $k$-space: a set $A$ is closed iff $A\cap K$
 is closed in $K$ for every compact $K\subseteq Y$, trivially since closed subse
ts of compact sets are compact hence closed in $Y$).

- $X = [0,1]_{\text{disc}}$ is Hausdorff. Its compact subsets are exactly the **
finite** subsets (an infinite subset admits an open cover by singletons with no
finite subcover). For any subset $A\subseteq X$ and any compact (hence finite) $
K\subseteq X$, the intersection $A\cap K$ is finite, therefore closed in the sub
space $K$ (which is discrete). Thus the $k$-space condition "$A\cap K$ closed in
 $K$ for all compact $K$" is satisfied by **every** $A\subseteq X$ — and indeed
every subset is closed in the discrete topology. So $X$ is compactly generated.

(Equivalently, both spaces are weak Hausdorff: for $X$ discrete, the image of an
y compact Hausdorff domain is finite, hence closed; for $Y$ compact Hausdorff, t
he image is compact hence closed.)

**Claim 2: $f$ is a continuous bijection.**

It is obviously a bijection (set-theoretic identity). It is continuous because t
he domain topology (discrete) is **finer** than the codomain topology (Euclidean
): every Euclidean-open set is open in the discrete topology, so $f^{-1}(U)=U$ i
s open in $X$ for every open $U$ in $Y$.

**Claim 3: $f$ is not a homeomorphism.**

The inverse $f^{-1}:Y\to X$ is again the set-theoretic identity, but it is **not
** continuous. The singleton $\{0\}$ is open in $X$ (discrete), yet its preimage
 under $f^{-1}$, namely $\{0\}$, is **not** open in $Y$ (Euclidean). Hence $f^{-
1}$ is not continuous, so $f$ is not a homeomorphism.

**Conclusion.** We have exhibited a continuous bijection between two compactly g
enerated (Hausdorff, even weak Hausdorff) spaces that fails to be a homeomorphis
m. Therefore the statement

> "A continuous bijection of compactly generated spaces is a homeomorphism"

is **false**. $\blacksquare$
