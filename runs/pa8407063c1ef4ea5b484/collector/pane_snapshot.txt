## Proof

**Claim:** $S^2 \setminus \{p, q\}$ is homotopy equivalent to $S^1$.

**Step 1: Reduce to the plane minus a point.**

Let $p, q \in S^2$ be distinct. Apply stereographic projection from $p$: there i
s a homeomorphism
$$\sigma_p : S^2 \setminus \{p\} \xrightarrow{\;\cong\;} \mathbb{R}^2.$$
Under this homeomorphism, the point $q \in S^2 \setminus \{p\}$ maps to some poi
nt $q' = \sigma_p(q) \in \mathbb{R}^2$. Therefore
$$S^2 \setminus \{p, q\} \;\cong\; \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{q'\}.$$

**Step 2: Reduce to the punctured plane at the origin.**

Translation by $-q'$ is a homeomorphism $\mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ sending
$q'$ to $0$, so
$$\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{q'\} \;\cong\; \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}.$$

**Step 3: Exhibit a deformation retraction onto $S^1$.**

Define $H : (\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}) \times [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}^2 \setmin
us \{0\}$ by
$$H(x, t) = (1-t)\,x + t\,\frac{x}{\|x\|}.$$
This is well-defined and continuous: for $x \neq 0$, the segment $(1-t)x + t\fra
c{x}{\|x\|}$ is a convex combination of two nonzero vectors lying on the same ra
y through the origin, hence is itself nonzero for all $t \in [0,1]$. We verify:
- $H(x, 0) = x$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$,
- $H(x, 1) = x/\|x\| \in S^1$ for all $x$,
- $H(y, t) = y$ for all $y \in S^1$ and all $t$ (since $\|y\| = 1$).

Thus $H$ is a strong deformation retraction of $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$ on
to $S^1$.

**Step 4: Conclude.**

Composing the homeomorphisms from Steps 1 and 2 with the deformation retraction
from Step 3, we obtain a homotopy equivalence
$$S^2 \setminus \{p, q\} \;\simeq\; S^1.$$

Since $S^1$ is not contractible (e.g., $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z} \neq 0$), th
e space $S^2 \setminus \{p, q\}$ has the homotopy type of a circle. $\blacksquar
e$
