# Classification of Nilpotent Lie Groups: Maximum Dimension

## Answer

The maximum dimension for which nilpotent Lie groups have been classified is **7
**.

## Proof / Reasoning

**Step 1: Reduction to Lie algebras.**

For nilpotent Lie groups, the exponential map $\exp: \mathfrak{g} \to G$ is a di
ffeomorphism (for the simply connected case). Two simply connected nilpotent Lie
 groups are isomorphic if and only if their Lie algebras are isomorphic. Moreove
r, every nilpotent Lie algebra over $\mathbb{R}$ (or $\mathbb{C}$) is the Lie al
gebra of a unique (up to isomorphism) simply connected nilpotent Lie group. Ther
efore, the classification of nilpotent Lie groups is equivalent to the classific
ation of nilpotent Lie algebras.

**Step 2: Low dimensions — finite classification.**

Nilpotent Lie algebras over a field of characteristic zero have been completely
classified up to and including **dimension 7**:

- **Dimensions 1–5**: Trivial / small finite lists. (Dimension 1: abelian; dimen
sion 2: abelian; dimension 3: abelian and the Heisenberg algebra; etc.)
- **Dimension 6**: Finitely many isomorphism classes. Classified by various auth
ors (e.g., Morozov, Mubarakzyanov).
- **Dimension 7**: Finitely many isomorphism classes. Over $\mathbb{C}$, the cla
ssification of indecomposable nilpotent Lie algebras was completed (e.g., Gong's
 classification gives a finite list). Over $\mathbb{R}$, the classification is a
lso complete, with a finite number of real forms.

In all of these dimensions, the number of isomorphism classes is **finite**, and
 explicit lists with structure constants are available in the literature.

**Step 3: Dimension 8 and beyond — the classification problem is wild.**

Starting at **dimension 8**, the classification of nilpotent Lie algebras become
s a **wild** problem in the precise sense of representation theory. Specifically
:

- There exist **continuously parameterized families** of pairwise non-isomorphic
 nilpotent Lie algebras in dimension 8. That is, the isomorphism classes cannot
be indexed by a discrete set; they depend on continuous (real or complex) parame
ters.
- The classification problem in dimension $\geq 8$ contains (as a subproblem) th
e problem of classifying pairs of commuting matrices up to simultaneous conjugat
ion — a problem known to be **wild** (in the sense of Drozd's theorem), meaning
no reasonable discrete classification can exist.

Because of this wildness, there is **no complete classification** of nilpotent L
ie algebras (and hence nilpotent Lie groups) in dimension 8 or higher. Partial r
esults and classifications of subclasses exist, but a full classification is gen
erally considered impossible / not achievable in the traditional sense.

**Conclusion.**

The classification of nilpotent Lie groups (equivalently, nilpotent Lie algebras
) is complete and finite up to and including **dimension 7**. At dimension 8, th
e problem becomes wild with continuous parameter families, precluding a complete
 classification. Therefore, the maximum dimension for which nilpotent Lie groups
 have been classified is:

$$\boxed{7}$$
