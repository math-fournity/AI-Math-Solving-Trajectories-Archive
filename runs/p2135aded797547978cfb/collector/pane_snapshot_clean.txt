## Derivation of the Riemann Curvature Tensor from the Commutator of Covariant D
erivatives

### Setup

Let $M$ be a smooth manifold with an affine connection $\nabla$ with Christoffel
 symbols $\Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}$ (symmetric in the lower indices for a torsion-
free connection: $\Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}=\Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu\mu}$). The Riemann c
urvature tensor is *defined* by measuring the failure of covariant derivatives t
o commute when acting on a vector field $V^\rho$:

$$[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu] V^\rho \;=\; \nabla_\mu \nabla_\nu V^\rho - \nabla_\n
u \nabla_\mu V^\rho \;=:\; R^\rho_{\;\;\sigma\mu\nu}\, V^\sigma.$$

We now expand both terms explicitly and isolate $R^\rho_{\;\;\sigma\mu\nu}$.

---

### Step 1 — First covariant derivative

For a contravariant vector field,
$$\nabla_\nu V^\rho = \partial_\nu V^\rho + \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda} V^\lambda.
\tag{1}$$

The object $T^\rho_{\;\;\nu} := \nabla_\nu V^\rho$ is a $(1,1)$-tensor, so its c
ovariant derivative must account for **both** the upper index $\rho$ and the low
er index $\nu$:

$$\nabla_\mu\!\left(\nabla_\nu V^\rho\right) = \partial_\mu\!\left(\nabla_\nu V^
\rho\right) + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\alpha}\!\left(\nabla_\nu V^\alpha\right) - \Gamma
^\alpha_{\mu\nu}\!\left(\nabla_\alpha V^\rho\right). \tag{2}$$

The minus sign in the last term is the standard rule for a lower (covariant) ind
ex.

---

### Step 2 — Expand $\nabla_\mu \nabla_\nu V^\rho$

Substituting (1) into (2):

$$\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu V^\rho = \partial_\mu\!\left(\partial_\nu V^\rho + \Gamma
^\rho_{\nu\lambda}V^\lambda\right) + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\alpha}\!\left(\partial_\nu
 V^\alpha + \Gamma^\alpha_{\nu\lambda}V^\lambda\right) - \Gamma^\alpha_{\mu\nu}\
!\left(\partial_\alpha V^\rho + \Gamma^\rho_{\alpha\lambda}V^\lambda\right).$$

Expanding term by term:

$$\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu V^\rho = \partial_\mu\partial_\nu V^\rho + (\partial_\mu\
Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda})V^\lambda + \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\,\partial_\mu V^\la
mbda + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\alpha}\,\partial_\nu V^\alpha + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\alpha}\
Gamma^\alpha_{\nu\lambda}V^\lambda - \Gamma^\alpha_{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\alpha V^\
rho - \Gamma^\alpha_{\mu\nu}\Gamma^\rho_{\alpha\lambda}V^\lambda. \tag{3}$$

---

### Step 3 — Expand $\nabla_\nu \nabla_\mu V^\rho$ (swap $\mu \leftrightarrow \n
u$)

By the same computation with $\mu$ and $\nu$ interchanged:

$$\nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu V^\rho = \partial_\nu\partial_\mu V^\rho + (\partial_\nu\
Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda})V^\lambda + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\,\partial_\nu V^\la
mbda + \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\alpha}\,\partial_\mu V^\alpha + \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\alpha}\
Gamma^\alpha_{\mu\lambda}V^\lambda - \Gamma^\alpha_{\nu\mu}\,\partial_\alpha V^\
rho - \Gamma^\alpha_{\nu\mu}\Gamma^\rho_{\alpha\lambda}V^\lambda. \tag{4}$$

---

### Step 4 — Form the commutator and cancel

Subtracting (4) from (3), $[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]V^\rho = (3) - (4)$, and group
ing like terms:

| Term in (3) | Term in (4) | Result |
|---|---|---|
| $\partial_\mu\partial_\nu V^\rho$ | $\partial_\nu\partial_\mu V^\rho$ | **canc
els** (partials commute) |
| $\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\partial_\mu V^\lambda$ | $\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\alpha}\pa
rtial_\mu V^\alpha$ | **cancels** (rename $\lambda\leftrightarrow\alpha$) |
| $\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\alpha}\partial_\nu V^\alpha$ | $\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\par
tial_\nu V^\lambda$ | **cancels** (rename) |
| $-\Gamma^\alpha_{\mu\nu}\partial_\alpha V^\rho$ | $+\Gamma^\alpha_{\nu\mu}\par
tial_\alpha V^\rho$ | **cancels** ($\Gamma^\alpha_{\mu\nu}=\Gamma^\alpha_{\nu\mu
}$, torsion-free) |
| $-\Gamma^\alpha_{\mu\nu}\Gamma^\rho_{\alpha\lambda}V^\lambda$ | $+\Gamma^\alph
a_{\nu\mu}\Gamma^\rho_{\alpha\lambda}V^\lambda$ | **cancels** (same symmetry) |
| $(\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda})V^\lambda$ | $-(\partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho
_{\mu\lambda})V^\lambda$ | **survives** |
| $\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\alpha}\Gamma^\alpha_{\nu\lambda}V^\lambda$ | $-\Gamma^\rho_{
\nu\alpha}\Gamma^\alpha_{\mu\lambda}V^\lambda$ | **survives** |

Everything proportional to $\partial V$ (the "derivative-of-$V$" terms) cancels
— this is essential, since the curvature must be a tensor, i.e., linear in $V$ w
ith **no** derivatives of $V$ remaining. Only terms proportional to $V^\lambda$
survive.

---

### Step 5 — Read off the Riemann tensor

What remains is

$$[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu] V^\rho = \Big(\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda} -
\partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\alpha}\Gamma^\alpha_{\nu
\lambda} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\alpha}\Gamma^\alpha_{\mu\lambda}\Big) V^\lambda.$$

Comparing with the defining relation $[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]V^\rho = R^\rho_{\;
\;\sigma\mu\nu}\,V^\sigma$ (renaming the dummy $\lambda\to\sigma$), we identify:

$$\boxed{\,R^\rho_{\;\;\sigma\mu\nu} \;=\; \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} -
 \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\alpha}\Gamma^\alpha_{\nu
\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\alpha}\Gamma^\alpha_{\mu\sigma}\,}$$

---

### Consistency checks

1. **Antisymmetry in the last two indices:** Swapping $\mu\leftrightarrow\nu$ se
nds every term to its negative, so $R^\rho_{\;\;\sigma\mu\nu} = -R^\rho_{\;\;\si
gma\nu\mu}$. ✓ This matches the geometric meaning: reversing the order of the tw
o directions around an infinitesimal loop reverses the sign of the holonomy.

2. **Tensor character:** Although $\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\nu}$ is **not** a tensor, th
e combination $\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma}-\partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\
sigma}+\Gamma\Gamma-\Gamma\Gamma$ transforms as a $(1,3)$-tensor. The non-tensor
ial pieces of $\partial\Gamma$ are exactly cancelled by the $\Gamma\Gamma$ terms
 — this is precisely why the commutator construction is the cleanest way to *def
ine* curvature: the commutator of two covariant derivatives is manifestly a tens
or operator, so its coefficient must be a tensor.

3. **Flat-space limit:** If $\Gamma\equiv 0$ (e.g., Cartesian coordinates on $\m
athbb{R}^n$), then $R^\rho_{\;\;\sigma\mu\nu}=0$ and covariant derivatives reduc
e to partials, which commute. ✓

4. **For a covariant vector** $W_\rho$, the analogous computation gives $[\nabla
_\mu,\nabla_\nu]W_\rho = -R^\sigma_{\;\;\rho\mu\nu}W_\sigma$ (opposite sign), co
nfirming the index placement and the standard sign convention.

---

### Conclusion

Starting from the geometric definition of curvature as the obstruction to commut
ativity of covariant derivatives, expanding $\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu V^\rho$ carefu
lly (treating $\nabla_\nu V^\rho$ as a $(1,1)$-tensor so that the lower index $\
nu$ contributes a $-\Gamma$ term), and subtracting the $\mu\leftrightarrow\nu$-s
wapped expression, all derivative-of-$V$ terms cancel by torsion-freeness and in
dex relabeling, leaving a purely algebraic coefficient of $V^\sigma$. That coeff
icient is the Riemann curvature tensor:

$$R^\rho_{\;\;\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu
\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\alpha}\Gamma^\alpha_{\nu\sigma} - \Ga
mma^\rho_{\nu\alpha}\Gamma^\alpha_{\mu\sigma}.$$
