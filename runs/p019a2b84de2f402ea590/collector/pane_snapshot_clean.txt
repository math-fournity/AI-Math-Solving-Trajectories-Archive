## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. Given a Voronoi diagram of $n$ points (constructed in $\mathcal{
O}(n)$), the closest pair of points can be found in $\mathcal{O}(n)$ additional
time.

The argument rests on two facts: (1) the closest pair is always an edge of the D
elaunay triangulation, and (2) the Delaunay triangulation is the planar dual of
the Voronoi diagram and has only $\mathcal{O}(n)$ edges, extractable in $\mathca
l{O}(n)$ time.

### Step 1: The closest pair is a Delaunay edge

Let $p, q$ be the closest pair among the point set $S$, with $|pq| = d$. Conside
r the closed disk $D$ whose diameter is the segment $pq$ (center at the midpoint
 $m$ of $pq$, radius $d/2$).

**$D$ contains no other point of $S$.** Suppose for contradiction that some poin
t $r \in S \setminus \{p, q\}$ lies inside $D$. Then $|rm| < d/2$. By the triang
le inequality:

$$|rp| \leq |rm| + |mp| < \frac{d}{2} + \frac{d}{2} = d.$$

So $|rp| < d = |pq|$, contradicting the assumption that $p, q$ is the closest pa
ir. (The same bound holds for $|rq|$.) Hence $D$ is an empty circle passing thro
ugh $p$ and $q$.

**Delaunay criterion:** Two points are connected by a Delaunay edge if and only
if there exists a closed disk having those two points on its boundary and contai
ning no other point of $S$ in its interior. The disk $D$ satisfies exactly this
condition: $p$ and $q$ lie on its boundary, and no other point lies inside. Ther
efore $\{p, q\}$ is an edge of the Delaunay triangulation $\mathrm{Del}(S)$.

### Step 2: Extract the Delaunay triangulation from the Voronoi diagram in $\mat
hcal{O}(n)$

The Delaunay triangulation is the straight-line planar dual of the Voronoi diagr
am: for every Voronoi edge (a segment or ray separating two Voronoi cells), draw
 a Delaunay edge connecting the two sites whose cells share that Voronoi edge. A
 Voronoi diagram of $n$ sites in the plane has $\mathcal{O}(n)$ vertices, $\math
cal{O}(n)$ edges, and $\mathcal{O}(n)$ cells (by Euler's formula for planar grap
hs: $V - E + F = 2$ with $F = n$, giving $E = \mathcal{O}(n)$). Therefore the du
al has $\mathcal{O}(n)$ edges, and traversing the Voronoi diagram's edge list on

### Step 3: Scan Delaunay edges to find the closest pair in $\mathcal{O}(n)$

The Delaunay triangulation has $\mathcal{O}(n)$ edges. By Step 1, the closest pa
ir is among these edges. We iterate over all Delaunay edges, compute the Euclide
an distance for each (constant time per edge), and keep track of the minimum. Th
is takes $\mathcal{O}(n)$ time.
