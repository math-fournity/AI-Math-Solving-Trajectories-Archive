## Proof

Define the Dirichlet eta function
$$\eta(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n^s},$$
so that the series in the problem is $-\eta(s)$.

**Step 1: Convergence for $s > 0$.** For real $s > 0$, the sequence $a_n = n^{-s
}$ is positive, decreasing, and tends to $0$. By the Leibniz (alternating series
) test, $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^{n-1}/n^s$ converges. Hence the series definin
g $\eta(s)$ is valid for all $s > 0$ (real), and in particular for $s \to 0^+$.

**Step 2: Analytic continuation and the relation to $\zeta(s)$.** The eta functi
on is related to the Riemann zeta function by
$$\eta(s) = (1 - 2^{1-s})\,\zeta(s).$$
This identity holds first for $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 1$ (where both series conv
erge absolutely), and then extends by analytic continuation, since $\eta(s)$ is
entire (the factor $(1-2^{1-s})$ cancels the simple pole of $\zeta$ at $s=1$) an
d $\zeta(s)$ is meromorphic on $\mathbb{C}$.

**Step 3: Evaluation at $s = 0$.** The function $\eta(s)$ is entire, hence conti
nuous at $s = 0$. Therefore
$$\lim_{s \to 0^+} \eta(s) = \eta(0) = (1 - 2^{1-0})\,\zeta(0) = (1 - 2)\cdot\ze
ta(0) = -\zeta(0).$$
The well-known value $\zeta(0) = -\tfrac{1}{2}$ (obtained, e.g., from the analyt
ic continuation $\zeta(s) = 2^s \pi^{s-1}\sin\!\bigl(\tfrac{\pi s}{2}\bigr)\,\Ga
mma(1-s)\,\zeta(1-s)$ evaluated at $s=0$, using $\zeta(1-s)\to -1/2$ via the Lau
rent expansion near the pole at $s=1$) gives
$$\eta(0) = -\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right) = \tfrac{1}{2}.$$

**Step 4: Conclusion.** Since the original series equals $-\eta(s)$,
$$\lim_{s \to 0^+} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n^s} = -\lim_{s \to 0^+} \e
ta(s) = -\eta(0) = -\frac{1}{2}.$$
