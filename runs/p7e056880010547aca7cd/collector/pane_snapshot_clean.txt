## Proof

Let $f(x)$ count the number of prime powers strictly less than $x$, where a prim
e power is a number of the form $p^k$ with $p$ prime and $k \geq 1$.

**Decomposition by exponent.** Every prime power $p^k < x$ satisfies $p < x^{1/k
}$. Grouping prime powers by their exponent $k$, we get

$$f(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor \log_2 x \rfloor} \pi\!\left(x^{1/k}\right),$$

where $\pi(y)$ denotes the number of primes $\leq y$. (The sum stops at $\lfloor
 \log_2 x \rfloor$ since for $k > \log_2 x$ we have $2^k > x$, so $x^{1/k} < 2$
and $\pi(x^{1/k}) = 0$.)

**Bounding each term.** By the Prime Number Theorem, $\pi(y) \sim y/\ln y$, so i
n particular $\pi(y) \leq C\, y/\ln y$ for all $y \geq 2$ and some absolute cons
tant $C$. Hence

$$\frac{f(x)}{x} = \sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor \log_2 x \rfloor} \frac{\pi(x^{1/k})}{x}
\leq \frac{\pi(x)}{x} + \sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor \log_2 x \rfloor} \frac{C\, x^{1/k}}
{x \ln(x^{1/k})}.$$

The first term satisfies $\pi(x)/x \sim 1/\ln x \to 0$ by the PNT.

For the remaining terms ($k \geq 2$), each summand is

$$\frac{C\, x^{1/k}}{x \ln(x^{1/k})} = \frac{C\, k}{x^{1-1/k}\,\ln x}.$$

For $k=2$ this is $O(1/(\sqrt{x}\ln x))$, and for larger $k$ it is even smaller
(since $x^{1-1/k}$ grows and $k/\ln x \to 0$). The number of terms is $O(\ln x)$
, so

$$\sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor \log_2 x \rfloor} \frac{C\, k}{x^{1-1/k}\,\ln x} \leq \fra
c{C\,(\log_2 x)^2}{\sqrt{x}\,\ln x} \to 0.$$

**Conclusion.** Both the dominant term $\pi(x)/x$ and the remainder tend to $0$,
 so

$$\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{f(x)}{x} = 0.$$

The intuition is clean: prime powers are far sparser than integers. Even just th
e primes have density $0$ (by the PNT, $\pi(x)/x \to 0$), and adding the higher
powers $p^2, p^3, \dots$ contributes only $O(\sqrt{x}/\ln x)$ additional element
s, which is negligible compared to $x$.
