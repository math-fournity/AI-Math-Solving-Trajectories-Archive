## Proof

We determine the limiting distribution of $Y_n = X_n/n$ where $X_n \sim \text{Po
isson}(np_n)$ and $p_n \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$.

**Step 1: Compute the mean and variance of $Y_n$.**

Since $X_n \sim \text{Poisson}(np_n)$, we have
$$E[X_n] = np_n, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(X_n) = np_n.$$

Therefore, for $Y_n = X_n/n$:
$$E[Y_n] = \frac{E[X_n]}{n} = p_n, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(Y_n) = \frac{\opera
torname{Var}(X_n)}{n^2} = \frac{p_n}{n}.$$

**Step 2: Show $Y_n \to 0$ in probability.**

Fix $\varepsilon > 0$. Since $p_n \to 0$, for all sufficiently large $n$ we have
 $p_n < \varepsilon/2$. For such $n$, the event $\{|Y_n| > \varepsilon\}$ implie
s $\{|Y_n - p_n| > \varepsilon/2\}$ (because if $|Y_n| > \varepsilon$ and $|p_n|
 < \varepsilon/2$, then $|Y_n - p_n| \geq |Y_n| - |p_n| > \varepsilon - \varepsi
lon/2 = \varepsilon/2$). Hence
$$P(|Y_n| > \varepsilon) \leq P(|Y_n - p_n| > \varepsilon/2).$$

Applying Chebyshev's inequality:
$$P(|Y_n - p_n| > \varepsilon/2) \leq \frac{\operatorname{Var}(Y_n)}{(\varepsilo
n/2)^2} = \frac{p_n/n}{\varepsilon^2/4} = \frac{4\,p_n}{n\,\varepsilon^2}.$$

Since $p_n \to 0$ and $n \to \infty$, we have $p_n/n \to 0$, so
$$\frac{4\,p_n}{n\,\varepsilon^2} \to 0.$$

Thus $P(|Y_n| > \varepsilon) \to 0$ for every $\varepsilon > 0$, which means $Y_
n \xrightarrow{P} 0$.

**Step 3: Conclude convergence in distribution.**

Convergence in probability implies convergence in distribution. Therefore $Y_n$
converges in distribution to the degenerate random variable $Y \equiv 0$, i.e.,
the point mass $\delta_0$ at the origin.

**Conclusion.** The limiting distribution of $Y_n = X_n/n$ is the **degenerate d
istribution concentrated at $0$**:
$$Y_n \xrightarrow{d} \delta_0.$$
