**Setup.** Define $f(x) = \sin\!\bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{3}\,x\bigr)$, so that $I_n = \
int_0^3 f^{(n)}(x)\,dx$, where $f^{(n)}$ denotes the $n$-fold composition.

**Step 1: Fixed points.** Solve $x = \sin\!\bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{3}x\bigr)$.

- $x = 0$: $\sin 0 = 0$. ✓
- $x = \tfrac{1}{2}$: $\sin\!\bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{6}\bigr) = \tfrac{1}{2}$. ✓

These are the only fixed points in $[0,3]$ (the function $g(x) = x - \sin(\tfrac
{\pi}{3}x)$ has $g'(x) = 1 - \tfrac{\pi}{3}\cos(\tfrac{\pi}{3}x)$, which changes
 sign at most twice, giving at most two additional zeros beyond the analysis; di
rect check confirms exactly $\{0, \tfrac{1}{2}\}$ in $[0,1]$, and for $x > 1$, $
\sin(\tfrac{\pi}{3}x) \le 1 < x$).

**Step 2: Stability.** $f'(x) = \tfrac{\pi}{3}\cos\!\bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{3}x\bigr)$
.

- At $x=0$: $f'(0) = \tfrac{\pi}{3} \approx 1.047 > 1$ — **repelling**.
- At $x=\tfrac{1}{2}$: $f'\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr) = \tfrac{\pi}{3}\cos\!\bigl
(\tfrac{\pi}{6}\bigr) = \tfrac{\pi\sqrt{3}}{6} \approx 0.907 < 1$ — **attracting
**.

**Step 3: Range contraction.** For $x \in [0,3]$, $\tfrac{\pi}{3}x \in [0,\pi]$,
 so $f(x) \in [0,1]$. For $x \in [0,1]$, $\tfrac{\pi}{3}x \in [0,\tfrac{\pi}{3}]
$, so $f(x) \in [0, \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}] \subset [0, \tfrac{3}{2}]$. On $[0, \tf
rac{3}{2}]$, $f'(x) > 0$, so **$f$ is strictly increasing** on the invariant int
erval $[0, \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}]$ that contains all iterates from the second onwa
rd.

**Step 4: No higher-period orbits.** A strictly increasing continuous map on an
interval has **no periodic orbits of period $\ge 2$**: if $f(x) > x$ then $f^{(2
)}(x) > f(x) > x$ (contradiction with $f^{(2)}(x)=x$), and similarly for $f(x) <
 x$. Hence every orbit converges to a fixed point.

**Step 5: Basin of attraction.** The only preimages of $0$ in $[0,3]$ are $x = 0
$ and $x = 3$ (since $\sin(\tfrac{\pi}{3}x) = 0 \iff \tfrac{\pi}{3}x \in \{0, \p
i\} \iff x \in \{0, 3\}$). Since $0$ is repelling, the only points whose iterate
s converge to $0$ are $\{0, 3\}$ and their (measure-zero) preimage tree — but in
 fact the only preimages of $0$ in $[0,3]$ are $\{0,3\}$, and preimages of $3$ d
on't exist in $[0,3]$ (since $f(x) \le 1 < 3$). Therefore:

$$f^{(n)}(x) \to \tfrac{1}{2} \quad \text{for all } x \in (0, 3), \qquad f^{(n)}
(0) = 0,\quad f^{(n)}(3) = 0.$$

**Step 6: Dominated Convergence.** For all $n \ge 1$ and $x \in [0,3]$, $|f^{(n)
}(x)| \le 1$, and the constant $1$ is integrable on $[0,3]$. By the Dominated Co
nvergence Theorem:

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} I_n = \int_0^3 \lim_{n\to\infty} f^{(n)}(x)\,dx = \int_0^3
 \tfrac{1}{2}\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 3 = \tfrac{3}{2},$$

since the points $\{0, 3\}$ where the limit differs from $\tfrac{1}{2}$ form a s
et of measure zero.
