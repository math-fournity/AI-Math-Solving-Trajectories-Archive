# Proof: The Assouad Dimension of the Complete Unit Ball in $\mathbb{R}^D$ is $D
$

## Setup and Definitions

Let $B = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^D : \|x\| \leq 1\}$ be the closed (complete) unit ba
ll in $\mathbb{R}^D$.

Recall the **Assouad dimension** $\dim_A(F)$ of a metric space $F$ is the infimu
m of all $s \geq 0$ such that there exists $C \geq 1$ with: for every $x \in F$,
 every $0 < r < R$ with $R \leq \operatorname{diam}(F)$, the ball $B(x,R) \cap F
$ can be covered by at most $C(R/r)^s$ balls of radius $r$ (intersected with $F$
).

We prove $\dim_A(B) = D$.

## Part 1: Upper Bound $\dim_A(B) \leq D$

The closed unit ball $B \subset \mathbb{R}^D$ inherits the Euclidean metric. It
is a standard fact that $\mathbb{R}^D$ has Assouad dimension $D$, and more gener
ally any subset of $\mathbb{R}^D$ has Assouad dimension at most $D$.

**Detailed argument:** For any $x \in B$, $0 < r < R \leq 2$ (since $\operatorna
me{diam}(B) = 2$), the set $B(x,R) \cap B$ is a bounded subset of $\mathbb{R}^D$
 contained in a Euclidean ball of radius $R$. A Euclidean ball of radius $R$ in
$\mathbb{R}^D$ can be covered by at most
$$N \leq \left(\frac{2R}{r} + 1\right)^D \leq \left(\frac{3R}{r}\right)^D = 3^D
\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^D$$
balls of radius $r$ (by a standard volume/gridding argument: place a grid of spa
cing $r/\sqrt{D}$, each grid cell has diameter $r$, and the number of cells inte
rsecting a ball of radius $R$ is at most $(2R\sqrt{D}/r + 1)^D \leq C_D (R/r)^D$
).

Setting $C = 3^D$ (a constant depending only on $D$), we have a uniform covering
 bound $C(R/r)^D$ for all $x, r, R$. This shows $\dim_A(B) \leq D$.

## Part 2: Lower Bound $\dim_A(B) \geq D$

We show that no exponent $s < D$ can work. Consider the center $x = 0 \in B$. Ta
ke any $0 < r < R \leq 1$. The set $B(0, R) \cap B$ contains the full Euclidean
ball $\overline{B}(0, R) = \{y : \|y\| \leq R\}$ (since $R \leq 1$ means this ba
ll is entirely within $B$).

**Volume argument:** Suppose $\overline{B}(0, R)$ is covered by $N$ balls of rad
ius $r$ (intersected with $B$, but each is contained in a Euclidean ball of radi
us $r$). By volume comparison in $\mathbb{R}^D$:
$$\operatorname{vol}\big(\overline{B}(0,R)\big) \leq N \cdot \operatorname{vol}\
big(\overline{B}(0,r)\big)$$
$$\omega_D R^D \leq N \cdot \omega_D r^D$$
where $\omega_D$ is the volume of the unit ball in $\mathbb{R}^D$. Therefore:
$$N \geq \left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^D.$$

Now suppose $\dim_A(B) = s$ for some $s < D$. Then there exists $C$ such that fo
r all $0 < r < R \leq 2$, the covering number is at most $C(R/r)^s$. But we have
 just shown:
$$\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^D \leq N \leq C\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^s.$$

This gives $(R/r)^{D-s} \leq C$ for **all** $0 < r < R \leq 1$. Taking $r \to 0$
 with $R$ fixed (e.g., $R = 1$, $r = 1/n \to 0$), the left side $(R/r)^{D-s} \to
 \infty$ since $D - s > 0$. This contradicts the boundedness by $C$.

Therefore no $s < D$ is valid, and $\dim_A(B) \geq D$.

## Conclusion

Combining both parts:
$$D \leq \dim_A(B) \leq D,$$
so $\dim_A(B) = D$.

**Remark.** The key structural facts used: (1) $B$ contains full Euclidean balls
 of positive radius around interior points, enabling the volume lower bound; (2)
 $B$ is a subset of $\mathbb{R}^D$, inheriting the covering upper bound. The arg
ument applies to any subset of $\mathbb{R}^D$ with nonempty interior, confirming
 that the Assouad dimension equals the ambient dimension $D$.

The Assouad dimension of the complete unit ball in $\mathbb{R}^D$ is $\boxed{D}$
.
