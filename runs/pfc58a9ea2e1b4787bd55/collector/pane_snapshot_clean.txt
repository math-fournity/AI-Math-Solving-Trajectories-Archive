## Proof

**Interpretation of the hypothesis.** The hypothesis states that there exists a
fixed $\varepsilon > 0$ such that for every choice of orthonormal basis $\{e_i\}
$ of $H_1$ and $\{f_j\}$ of $H_2$, and for every pair of indices $i, j$,
$$|\langle f_j, T e_i \rangle| \leq (1-\varepsilon)\,\|e_i\|\,\|f_j\|.$$
Since $\{e_i\}$ and $\{f_j\}$ are orthonormal, $\|e_i\| = \|f_j\| = 1$, so this
simplifies to
$$|\langle f_j, T e_i\rangle| \leq 1 - \varepsilon \quad \text{for all orthonorm
al bases and all } i,j. \tag{$\star$}$$

**Key observation: every unit vector extends to an orthonormal basis.** Let $v \
in H_1$ and $w \in H_2$ be arbitrary unit vectors. By the Gram–Schmidt process,
$v$ can be chosen as the first element of some orthonormal basis $\{e_i\}$ of $H
_1$ (i.e., $e_1 = v$), and $w$ can be chosen as the first element of some orthon
ormal basis $\{f_j\}$ of $H_2$ (i.e., $f_1 = w$).

Applying $(\star)$ with $i = 1, j = 1$ to these particular bases yields
$$|\langle w, T v\rangle| \leq 1 - \varepsilon.$$

Since $v$ and $w$ were arbitrary unit vectors, we conclude
$$|\langle w, T v\rangle| \leq 1 - \varepsilon \quad \text{for all } v \in H_1,\
; w \in H_2 \text{ with } \|v\| = \|w\| = 1. \tag{$\star\star$}$$

**Computing the operator norm.** Recall the variational characterization of the
operator norm:
$$\|T\| = \sup_{\|v\|=1} \|Tv\| = \sup_{\|v\|=1} \sup_{\|w\|=1} |\langle w, Tv\r
angle| = \sup_{\substack{\|v\|=1 \\ \|w\|=1}} |\langle w, Tv\rangle|.$$
The second equality uses the fact that for any vector $u \in H_2$, $\|u\| = \sup
_{\|w\|=1} |\langle w, u\rangle|$, with the supremum achieved at $w = u/\|u\|$ (
when $u \neq 0$).

**Conclusion.** By $(\star\star)$, every value $|\langle w, Tv\rangle|$ with $\|
v\| = \|w\| = 1$ is bounded above by $1 - \varepsilon$. Therefore
$$\|T\| = \sup_{\substack{\|v\|=1 \\ \|w\|=1}} |\langle w, Tv\rangle| \leq 1 - \
varepsilon < 1.$$

In particular, $\|T\| \leq 1$, so **$T$ is a contraction**. $\blacksquare$
