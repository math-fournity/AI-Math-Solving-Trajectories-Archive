## Proof of the Friedlander–Granville Theorem

**Theorem.** The Elliott–Halberstam bound fails at θ = 1. That is, the following
 statement is **false**:

$$\forall\, A > 0,\; \exists\, C > 0,\; \forall\, x > 2 : \quad \sum_{q=1}^{x} E
(x;q) \le \frac{Cx}{(\log x)^A}.$$

Equivalently, we prove: there exists $A > 0$ such that for every $C > 0$ and eve
ry $X$, there exists $x > X$ with
$$\sum_{q=1}^{x} E(x;q) > \frac{Cx}{(\log x)^A}.$$

We take $A = 2$.

---

### Step 1: Lower bound on $E(x; q)$ for large prime $q \in (x/2,\, x]$.

Fix a prime $q$ with $x/2 < q \le x$. Then $\varphi(q) = q - 1 > x/2 - 1$, so

$$\frac{\pi(x)}{\varphi(q)} = \frac{\pi(x)}{q-1} \le \frac{\pi(x)}{x/2 - 1} \sim
 \frac{2}{\log x} \xrightarrow{x\to\infty} 0$$

by the Prime Number Theorem ($\pi(x) \sim x/\log x$). In particular, for $x$ suf
ficiently large, $\pi(x)/\varphi(q) < 1/2$.

By Bertrand's postulate (or the PNT), for $x$ large enough there exists a prime
$p$ with $x/2 < p \le x$ and $p \ne q$ (indeed the PNT gives $\sim x/(2\log x)$
primes in this interval). Since $p$ and $q$ are distinct primes, $\gcd(p, q) = 1
$, so $p$ lies in a coprime residue class $a \pmod{q}$ with $a \in (\mathbb{Z}/q
\mathbb{Z})^\times$.

For this residue class $a$:

$$\pi(x;\, q,\, a) \ge 1 \qquad \text{(the prime } p \le x \text{ satisfies } p
\equiv a \pmod{q}\text{)}.$$

Therefore the error for this class satisfies

$$\left|\pi(x;\,q,\,a) - \frac{\pi(x)}{\varphi(q)}\right| \ge 1 - \frac{\pi(x)}{
\varphi(q)} \ge 1 - \frac{2}{\log x} \ge \frac{1}{2}$$

for all $x$ sufficiently large. Since $E(x;q)$ is the **supremum** over all copr
ime residue classes:

$$\boxed{E(x;\, q) \ge \frac{1}{2}} \qquad \text{for every prime } q \in (x/2,\,
 x], \quad x \text{ large enough.}$$

---

### Step 2: Summing over primes $q \in (x/2, x]$.

The number of primes in $(x/2, x]$ is $\pi(x) - \pi(x/2)$. By the PNT,

$$\pi(x) - \pi\!\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) \sim \frac{x}{\log x} - \frac{x/2}{\log
(x/2)} \sim \frac{x}{2\log x}.$$

Hence there exists a constant $c_0 > 0$ such that for all $x$ sufficiently large
,

$$\pi(x) - \pi(x/2) \ge \frac{c_0\, x}{\log x}.$$

Since the sum $\sum_{q=1}^{x} E(x;q)$ includes all primes $q \in (x/2, x]$, and
each contributes at least $1/2$:

$$\sum_{q=1}^{x} E(x;q) \;\ge\; \sum_{\substack{q \text{ prime} \\ x/2 < q \le x
}} E(x;\,q) \;\ge\; \frac{1}{2}\bigl(\pi(x) - \pi(x/2)\bigr) \;\ge\; \frac{c_0}{
2} \cdot \frac{x}{\log x}.$$

Setting $c = c_0/2 > 0$:

$$\boxed{\sum_{q=1}^{x} E(x;q) \;\ge\; \frac{c\, x}{\log x}} \qquad \text{for al
l sufficiently large } x.$$

---

### Step 3: Comparison with the EH bound at $A = 2$.

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists $C > 0$ such that for all $x > 2$:

$$\sum_{q=1}^{x} E(x;q) \le \frac{Cx}{(\log x)^2}.$$

Combining with the lower bound from Step 2, for all $x$ sufficiently large:

$$\frac{c\, x}{\log x} \le \frac{Cx}{(\log x)^2} \implies c \log x \le C \implie
s x \le e^{C/c}.$$

This is a contradiction for $x > \max\!\bigl(e^{C/c},\, X_0\bigr)$ where $X_0$ i
s the threshold from Step 2.

Therefore, **for $A = 2$, no constant $C > 0$ works**: for every $C > 0$ there e
xist arbitrarily large $x$ violating the bound.

---

### Step 4: Conclusion.

We have shown that there exists $A > 0$ (namely $A = 2$) such that for every $C
> 0$, the inequality

$$\sum_{q=1}^{x} E(x;q) \le \frac{Cx}{(\log x)^A}$$

fails for arbitrarily large $x$. This is precisely the negation of

$$\forall\, A > 0,\; \exists\, C > 0,\; \forall\, x > 2:\; \sum_{q=1}^{x} E(x;q)
 \le \frac{Cx}{(\log x)^A},$$

which completes the proof. $\blacksquare$
