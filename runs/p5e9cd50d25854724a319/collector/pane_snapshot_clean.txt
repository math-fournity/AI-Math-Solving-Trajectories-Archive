## Proof

The answer is **No**. I provide an explicit counterexample with $n=2$.

Let
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,i \\[4pt] \frac{1}{2}
-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,i & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Then $A = X + iY$ with
$$X = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -\frac{1}{2} \\[4pt] \frac{1}{2} & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \q
quad Y = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \\[4pt] -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & 0
\end{pmatrix}.$$

### All square submatrices of $A$ satisfy $|\det(S)|\le 1$

**1×1 submatrices** (the entries of $A$):
- $|a_{11}|=|a_{22}|=1$,
- $|a_{12}|=\sqrt{\tfrac14+\tfrac34}=1$,
- $|a_{21}|=\sqrt{\tfrac14+\tfrac34}=1$.

**2×2 submatrix** (the whole matrix):
$$\det(A)=1\cdot 1-\Bigl(-\tfrac12+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i\Bigr)\Bigl(\tfrac12-\tfrac
{\sqrt3}{2}i\Bigr).$$

The product is
$$\Bigl(-\tfrac12+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i\Bigr)\Bigl(\tfrac12-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i\Bigr
)=-\tfrac14+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{4}i+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{4}i+\tfrac34=\tfrac12+\tfrac{\sqrt
3}{2}i.$$

Hence
$$\det(A)=1-\tfrac12-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i=\tfrac12-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i,\qquad |\det
(A)|=\sqrt{\tfrac14+\tfrac34}=1.$$

### Yet $|\det(X)|>1$

$$\det(X)=1\cdot 1-\Bigl(-\tfrac12\Bigr)\Bigl(\tfrac12\Bigr)=1+\tfrac14=\frac54.
$$

Thus $|\det(X)|=\frac54>1$, while every square submatrix $S$ of $A$ satisfies $|
\det(S)|\le 1$.

### Conclusion

The implication is **false**: the hypothesis that all square submatrices of $A$
have determinant modulus $\le 1$ does not guarantee $|\det(X)|\le 1$.
