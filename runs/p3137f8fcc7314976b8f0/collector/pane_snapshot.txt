## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. The ring $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}] = \{a + b\sqrt{2} : a, b \in \mat
hbb{Z}\}$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}$, so every real number can be approximated arb
itrarily closely by elements of $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$.

**Key fact.** Every additive subgroup $G \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ is either:
- dense in $\mathbb{R}$, or
- cyclic, i.e. $G = c\mathbb{Z}$ for some $c \geq 0$.

*Proof of the key fact.* If $G = \{0\}$, take $c = 0$. Otherwise let $\alpha = \
inf(G \cap \mathbb{R}_{>0}) \geq 0$.

- If $\alpha > 0$: pick $g \in G$ with $g > 0$. By the division algorithm on $g/
\alpha$, for any $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $g' \in G$ with $0 < g' < \alpha
 + \varepsilon$; taking $\varepsilon \to 0$ shows $\alpha \in G$ (using closure
and a standard argument), and then $G = \alpha\mathbb{Z}$.
- If $\alpha = 0$: for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $g \in G$ with $0 <
g < \varepsilon$. Then the multiples $\{ng : n \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ hit every inter
val of length $g$, hence every interval of length $\varepsilon$. Since $\varepsi
lon$ is arbitrary, $G$ is dense. $\square$

**Applying the key fact to $G = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$.** This is an additive sub
group of $\mathbb{R}$ (closed under addition and negation, contains $0$). By the
 key fact it is either dense or cyclic.

Suppose for contradiction that $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}] = c\mathbb{Z}$ for some $c
\geq 0$. Since $1 \in \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$ and $\sqrt{2} \in \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2
}]$, there exist integers $n, m$ with $1 = cn$ and $\sqrt{2} = cm$. As $1 \neq 0
$ we have $n \neq 0$, so $c = 1/n$ and thus $\sqrt{2} = m/n \in \mathbb{Q}$.

But $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational (the classical argument: if $\sqrt{2} = p/q$ in low
est terms, then $p^2 = 2q^2$ makes $p$ even, say $p = 2k$, giving $2k^2 = q^2$ m
aking $q$ even, contradicting lowest terms). This is a contradiction.

Therefore $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$ is **not** cyclic, so by the key fact it is **d
ense** in $\mathbb{R}$.

**Conclusion.** Density means: for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$ and every $\varepsil
on > 0$, there exists $a + b\sqrt{2} \in \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$ with $|x - (a + b
\sqrt{2})| < \varepsilon$. Hence elements of $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$ approximate
any real number arbitrarily closely. $\blacksquare$
