# Equation of Motion in a Central Gradient Field and Planar Motion from Angular
Momentum Conservation

## Setup

Consider a mass point of mass $m$ with position vector $x \in \mathbb{R}^3$, mov
ing in a central force field derived from a potential $U(|x|)$ that depends only
 on the radial distance $r = |x|$. The force is

$$F(x) = -\nabla U(|x|) = -U'(r)\,\frac{x}{r},$$

which is directed along the position vector (purely radial).

---

## 1. Equation of Motion

Newton's second law gives

$$m\,\ddot{x} = -\nabla U(|x|).$$

**Computing the gradient explicitly.** With $r = |x| = \sqrt{x_1^2 + x_2^2 + x_3
^2}$, the chain rule yields

$$\frac{\partial r}{\partial x_i} = \frac{x_i}{r}, \qquad \text{i.e., } \nabla r
 = \frac{x}{r}.$$

Therefore

$$\nabla U(r) = U'(r)\,\nabla r = U'(r)\,\frac{x}{r}.$$

Substituting back, the equation of motion is

$$\boxed{m\,\ddot{x} = -\,U'(r)\,\frac{x}{r}.}$$

This is a **central force equation**: the acceleration is always parallel (or an
tiparallel) to the position vector $x$.

---

## 2. Conservation of Angular Momentum

Define the angular momentum

$$L = x \times (m\,\dot{x}) = m\,(x \times \dot{x}).$$

Differentiate with respect to time:

$$\dot{L} = m\,(\dot{x} \times \dot{x}) + m\,(x \times \ddot{x}).$$

The first term vanishes since $\dot{x} \times \dot{x} = 0$. For the second term,
 substitute the equation of motion:

$$m\,(x \times \ddot{x}) = x \times \left(-U'(r)\,\frac{x}{r}\right) = -\frac{U'
(r)}{r}\,(x \times x) = 0,$$

since the cross product of any vector with itself is zero. Hence

$$\boxed{\dot{L} = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad L = \text{const}.}$$

The angular momentum is conserved. This is the direct consequence of the force b
eing **central** (always parallel to $x$), so the torque $\tau = x \times F$ van
ishes identically.

---

## 3. Planar Motion

We now show that conservation of $L$ forces the motion onto a plane.

**Case 1: $L \neq 0$.** Since $L$ is a constant (nonzero) vector, it fixes a def
inite direction in space. At every instant,

$$L = m\,(x \times \dot{x}) \quad \Longrightarrow \quad L \cdot x = m\,(x \times
 \dot{x})\cdot x = 0,$$

because the scalar triple product $(x \times \dot{x})\cdot x = 0$ (it contains t
he repeated vector $x$). Therefore

$$L \cdot x(t) = 0 \quad \text{for all } t.$$

This means the position vector $x(t)$ is always **orthogonal to the fixed vector
 $L$**, i.e., $x(t)$ always lies in the plane through the origin with normal vec
tor $L$:

$$\Pi = \{\, x \in \mathbb{R}^3 : L \cdot x = 0 \,\}.$$

Since $x(0) \in \Pi$ and $\dot{x}(0) \in \Pi$ (both are perpendicular to $L$, as
 $L = m\,x(0)\times\dot{x}(0)$ is perpendicular to both), and the equation of mo
tion $m\ddot{x} = -U'(r)\,x/r$ shows that $\ddot{x}$ is parallel to $x \in \Pi$,
 all higher derivatives remain in $\Pi$ as well. By uniqueness of solutions to O
DEs, the entire trajectory stays in $\Pi$.

**Case 2: $L = 0$.** Then $x \times \dot{x} = 0$ for all $t$, meaning the veloci
ty is always parallel to the position vector. The motion is along a fixed line t
hrough the origin (rectilinear motion), which is a degenerate plane. So the moti
on is still planar.

---

## 4. Reduction to Polar Coordinates (Equation of Motion in the Plane)

Since the motion is confined to the plane $\Pi$, introduce polar coordinates $(r
, \varphi)$ in that plane. Writing

$$x = r\,e_r, \qquad \dot{x} = \dot{r}\,e_r + r\dot{\varphi}\,e_\varphi,$$

the angular momentum magnitude is

$$|L| = m\,r^2\dot{\varphi} = \ell = \text{const} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \d
ot{\varphi} = \frac{\ell}{m\,r^2}.$$

The radial equation of motion becomes

$$m(\ddot{r} - r\dot{\varphi}^2) = -U'(r),$$

or, substituting $\dot{\varphi} = \ell/(mr^2)$,

$$\boxed{m\,\ddot{r} = \frac{\ell^2}{m\,r^3} - U'(r).}$$

This is the **radial equation** (the equation of motion reduced to one dimension
 by exploiting angular momentum conservation), with the effective potential

$$U_{\text{eff}}(r) = U(r) + \frac{\ell^2}{2m\,r^2},$$

so that $m\ddot{r} = -U_{\text{eff}}'(r)$.

---

## Summary

1. **Equation of motion:** $m\,\ddot{x} = -U'(r)\,\dfrac{x}{r}$, derived by comp
uting $\nabla U(|x|)$ via the chain rule.

2. **Angular momentum conservation:** $\dot{L} = x \times F = 0$ because the for
ce is central (parallel to $x$), so $L = m\,x\times\dot{x}$ is a constant of mot
ion.

3. **Planar motion:** The identity $L \cdot x = 0$ (from the scalar triple produ
ct) forces $x(t)$ to lie in the fixed plane orthogonal to the constant vector $L
$ for all time. Combined with the ODE uniqueness argument (initial data and all
derivatives stay in the plane), the trajectory is confined to this plane. The ca
se $L=0$ gives rectilinear (degenerate planar) motion.
