## Proof

**Setup.** Let $S_u = \{\langle M, x, 1^t\rangle : M \text{ accepts } x \text{ w
ithin } t \text{ steps}\}$ be the universal NP language. For any $L \in \text{NP
}$, there is a nondeterministic machine $M$ and a polynomial $p$ such that $x \i
n L \iff M$ accepts $x$ within $p(|x|)$ steps. The generic reduction (from the p
roof of Theorem 2.19) is

$$f(x) = \langle M,\, x,\, 1^{p(|x|)}\rangle.$$

This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction from $L$ to $S_u$, establishing NP-
completeness of $S_u$ in the worst-case sense.

**Domination condition.** A distributional reduction from $(L, \mu)$ to $(S_u, U
')$ requires, beyond correctness $x \in L \iff f(x) \in S_u$, a **domination** c
ondition: there exists a polynomial $q$ such that for every $y$,

$$\sum_{x\,:\,f(x)=y} \mu(x) \;\le\; q(|y|)\cdot U'(y).$$

Since $f$ is injective (the input $x$ is embedded verbatim in the output), the l
eft-hand side is just $\mu(x)$ for the unique $x$ with $f(x)=y$. So domination r
educes to

$$\mu(x) \;\le\; q(|f(x)|)\cdot U'(f(x)) \quad \text{for all } x. \tag{$\star$}$
$

**Why domination fails.** Under the uniform distribution $U'$, a string of lengt
h $n$ has probability $\Theta(2^{-n})$ (up to the standard $1/n$ length-normaliz
ation, which is polynomial and absorbed into $q$). The output length is

$$|f(x)| = |\langle M, x, 1^{p(|x|)}\rangle| \approx |x| + p(|x|) + |M|,$$

so

$$U'(f(x)) \;\approx\; 2^{-(|x| + p(|x|) + |M|)}.$$

Now take $\mu$ to be the uniform distribution on $\{0,1\}^{n}$, a perfectly vali
d P-computable distribution. Then $\mu(x) = 2^{-|x|}$, and condition ($\star$) d
emands

$$2^{-|x|} \;\le\; q(|f(x)|)\cdot 2^{-(|x| + p(|x|) + |M|)},$$

which simplifies to

$$2^{p(|x|) + |M|} \;\le\; q(|f(x)|).$$

The left-hand side is **exponential** in $|x|$ (since $p(|x|) \geq |x|$ for any
nontrivial verifier, $2^{p(|x|)}$ grows exponentially), while the right-hand sid
e $q(|f(x)|)$ is **polynomial** in $|f(x)|$, hence polynomial in $|x|$. An expon
ential function cannot be bounded by a polynomial, so for all sufficiently large
 $|x|$, domination is violated.

**Fundamental reason.** The generic reduction achieves NP-completeness by encodi
ng the time bound as **polynomial padding** $1^{p(|x|)}$. In the worst-case sett
ing this padding is harmless—it only affects the output length, not membership.
But in the distributional setting, the uniform distribution $U'$ assigns probabi
lity that **decays exponentially** with string length. The polynomial padding in
flates the output length by a polynomial additive term $p(|x|)$, which translate
s to an **exponential multiplicative drop** in probability: $U'(f(x))$ is smalle
r than $\mu(x)$ by a factor of $2^{p(|x|)}$. The domination condition tolerates
only **polynomial** discrepancies (the factor $q$), and no polynomial can absorb
 an exponential gap. Hence the very mechanism that makes the reduction work in t
he worst case—time-padding to let the universal verifier run in polynomial time—
is precisely what breaks the domination condition in the average case.
