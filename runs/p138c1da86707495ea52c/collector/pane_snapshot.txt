## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $M = \{0\}$.

**Step 1: $M$ is finitely generated projective.**

Since $R^{n} \simeq R^{n} \oplus M$, the module $M$ is isomorphic to a direct su
mmand of $R^{n}$. A direct summand of a finitely generated module is finitely ge
nerated, and a direct summand of a free module is projective. Hence $M$ is finit
ely generated and projective.

**Step 2: Localize at every prime ideal.**

Let $\mathfrak{p}$ be any prime ideal of $R$. Localization is exact, so it prese
rves direct sums and isomorphisms. Applying $(-)_{\mathfrak{p}}$ to $R^{n} \sime
q R^{n} \oplus M$ gives
$$R_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n} \simeq R_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n} \oplus M_{\mathfrak{p}}.$$

**Step 3: $M_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is free over the local ring $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$.**

The localization $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is a commutative local ring with maximal ide
al $\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}$. The module $M_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is finitely gen
erated and projective over $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ (projectivity and finite generatio
n are preserved by localization). By the standard theorem that **every finitely
generated projective module over a local ring is free** (Kaplansky), there exist
s $k \geq 0$ with
$$M_{\mathfrak{p}} \simeq R_{\mathfrak{p}}^{k}.$$

**Step 4: Use IBN for local rings to force $k = 0$.**

Substituting into the localized isomorphism:
$$R_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n} \simeq R_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n} \oplus R_{\mathfrak{p}}^{k} \
simeq R_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n+k}.$$

Every commutative local ring has the **Invariant Basis Number (IBN)** property:
$R_{\mathfrak{p}}^{a} \simeq R_{\mathfrak{p}}^{b}$ implies $a = b$. (This follow
s, for instance, from passing to the residue field $R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p
}R_{\mathfrak{p}}$: tensoring the isomorphism with the residue field gives an is
omorphism of vector spaces of dimensions $a$ and $b$, so $a = b$.)

Applying IBN to $R_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n} \simeq R_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n+k}$ yields $n =
 n + k$, hence $k = 0$. Therefore
$$M_{\mathfrak{p}} = 0$$
for every prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$.

**Step 5: $M_{\mathfrak{p}} = 0$ for all primes implies $M = 0$.**

This is a standard fact. Suppose for contradiction that $M \neq 0$, and pick $0
\neq x \in M$. The annihilator $\operatorname{ann}(x)$ is a proper ideal of $R$,
 so it is contained in some maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$. We claim $x/1 \neq 0$
in $M_{\mathfrak{m}}$. Indeed, $x/1 = 0$ in $M_{\mathfrak{m}}$ would mean $sx =
0$ for some $s \in R \setminus \mathfrak{m}$. But $sx = 0$ means $s \in \operato
rname{ann}(x) \subseteq \mathfrak{m}$, contradicting $s \notin \mathfrak{m}$. He
nce $M_{\mathfrak{m}} \neq 0$, contradicting Step 4 (since every maximal ideal i
s prime).

Therefore $M = 0$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on commutativity being essential.** The commutativity hypothesis is nec
essary. Without it, the Leavitt algebra $L_K(1,2)$ over a field $K$ satisfies $R
 \simeq R^{2}$ (hence $R^{n} \simeq R^{n+1}$ for all $n \geq 1$), giving $R^{n}
\simeq R^{n} \oplus R$ with $M = R \neq 0$. Such rings fail IBN, which is the pr
operty the commutative setting guarantees via localization to residue fields.
