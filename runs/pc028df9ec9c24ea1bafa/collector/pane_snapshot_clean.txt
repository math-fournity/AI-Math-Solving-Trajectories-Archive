## Proof

Let
$$B(z)=\frac{z-1/2}{1-z/2}\cdot\frac{z-1/3}{1-z/3}.$$
This is a Blaschke product with simple zeros exactly at $z=1/2$ and $z=1/3$. It
is analytic on a neighborhood of the closed unit disk (its poles are at $z=2$ an
d $z=3$, outside the disk), and for $|z|=1$,
$$|B(z)|=\left|\frac{z-1/2}{1-z/2}\right|\cdot\left|\frac{z-1/3}{1-z/3}\right|=1
\cdot 1=1,$$
since for $|z|=1$ and real $a\in(-1,1)$, $|z-a|=|1-\bar a z|=|1-az|$ (using $\ba
r z = 1/z$). Also $B(0)=\tfrac{-1/2}{1}\cdot\tfrac{-1/3}{1}=\tfrac{1}{6}$.

**Upper bound.** Let $f\in\mathcal F$ satisfy $f(1/2)=f(1/3)=0$ and $|f(z)|\le 1
$ on $|z|=1$. Since $B$ has simple zeros at $1/2$ and $1/3$, and $f$ vanishes th
ere, the quotient
$$g(z)=\frac{f(z)}{B(z)}$$
has removable singularities at $z=1/2,1/3$ and is analytic on a neighborhood of
the closed disk. On $|z|=1$,
$$|g(z)|=\frac{|f(z)|}{|B(z)|}=|f(z)|\le 1.$$
By the maximum modulus principle, $|g(z)|\le 1$ for all $|z|\le 1$. In particula
r,
$$|f(0)|=|B(0)|\cdot|g(0)|\le |B(0)|\cdot 1=\frac{1}{6}.$$
Hence the supremum is at most $1/6$.

**Attainment.** Take $f(z)=B(z)$ (or $f=e^{i\theta}B$ for any $\theta\in\mathbb
R$). Then $f$ is analytic on a neighborhood of the closed disk, $f(1/2)=f(1/3)=0
$, $|f(z)|=|B(z)|=1$ on $|z|=1$, and
$$|f(0)|=|B(0)|=\frac{1}{6}.$$

Therefore the supremum equals $\boxed{\tfrac{1}{6}}$, and it **is attained** (by
 the Blaschke product $B$, up to a unimodular constant).
