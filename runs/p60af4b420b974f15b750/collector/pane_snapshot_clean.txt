## Proof

We work on $X = C[0,1]$ with the supremum norm $\|f\| = \sup_{x\in[0,1]}|f(x)|$.
 The operator is
$$(Tf)(x) = x + \lambda\int_0^x (x-t)f(t)\,dt.$$

**Step 1: $T$ maps $X$ into $X$.** For $f\in C[0,1]$, the integrand $(x-t)f(t)$
is continuous on the triangle $0\le t\le x\le 1$, so $(Tf)(x)$ is continuous in
$x$. Hence $T:X\to X$.

**Step 2: Lipschitz estimate.** For $f,g\in X$ and $x\in[0,1]$,
$$|(Tf)(x)-(Tg)(x)| = \left|\lambda\int_0^x (x-t)(f(t)-g(t))\,dt\right| \le |\la
mbda|\int_0^x (x-t)|f(t)-g(t)|\,dt.$$
Since $|f(t)-g(t)|\le \|f-g\|$ for all $t$,
$$|(Tf)(x)-(Tg)(x)| \le |\lambda|\,\|f-g\|\int_0^x(x-t)\,dt = |\lambda|\,\|f-g\|
\cdot\frac{x^2}{2} \le \frac{|\lambda|}{2}\|f-g\|,$$
using $x^2\le 1$. Taking the supremum over $x\in[0,1]$,
$$\|Tf-Tg\|\le \frac{|\lambda|}{2}\|f-g\|.$$

Thus $T$ is a contraction whenever $\dfrac{|\lambda|}{2}<1$, i.e. $|\lambda|<2$.

**Step 3: Sharpness — the bound $|\lambda|/2$ is attained.** Take $g\equiv 0$ an
d $f\equiv 1$ (so $\|f-g\|=1$). Then
$$(Tf)(x)-(Tg)(x) = \lambda\int_0^x(x-t)\,dt = \lambda\,\frac{x^2}{2}.$$
At $x=1$ this equals $\lambda/2$, so
$$\|Tf-Tg\| = \frac{|\lambda|}{2} = \frac{|\lambda|}{2}\|f-g\|.$$
Hence the Lipschitz constant of $T$ is *exactly* $|\lambda|/2$ (the operator nor
m of the linear part $Kf = \int_0^x(x-t)f(t)\,dt$ on $C[0,1]$ is precisely $1/2$
).

**Step 4: Conclusion.** $T$ is a contraction on $(C[0,1],\|\cdot\|_\infty)$ if a
nd only if its Lipschitz constant $|\lambda|/2$ is strictly less than $1$, i.e.
$$|\lambda|<2 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \lambda\in(-2,\,2).$$

For $|\lambda|=2$ the constant equals $1$ (not a contraction), and for $|\lambda
|>2$ it exceeds $1$.

The range of values of $\lambda$ for which $T$ is a contraction is $\boxed{(-2,\
,2)}$.
