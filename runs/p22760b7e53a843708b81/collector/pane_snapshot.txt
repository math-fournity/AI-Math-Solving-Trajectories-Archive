## Proof

Let $\alpha = \sqrt[7]{2}$ (the real seventh root of $2$). The roots of $x^7 - 2
$ in $\mathbb{C}$ are $\alpha\, z^k$ for $k = 0, 1, \dots, 6$, where $z = e^{2\p
i i/7}$. Hence the splitting field is
$$F = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha, z).$$

**Step 1: $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}] = 7$.**
The polynomial $x^7 - 2$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ by Eisenstein's criter
ion at the prime $2$. Since $\alpha$ is a root, its minimal polynomial over $\ma
thbb{Q}$ has degree $7$.

**Step 2: $[\mathbb{Q}(z):\mathbb{Q}] = 6$.**
Since $7$ is prime, $z$ is a primitive $7$th root of unity, and its minimal poly
nomial is the $7$th cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_7(x) = x^6 + x^5 + \cdots + x +
1$, which has degree $\varphi(7) = 6$.

**Step 3: $\alpha \notin \mathbb{Q}(z)$.**
If $\alpha \in \mathbb{Q}(z)$, then $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(z)$
. By the tower law, $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}]$ would divide $[\mathbb{Q}(
z):\mathbb{Q}]$, i.e. $7 \mid 6$, which is impossible. Hence $\alpha \notin \mat
hbb{Q}(z)$.

**Step 4: Compute $[F:\mathbb{Q}(z)]$.**
Over $\mathbb{Q}(z)$, the element $\alpha$ is a root of $x^7 - 2$, so its minima
l polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}(z)$ divides $x^7 - 2$. Since $7$ is prime, the deg
ree $[\mathbb{Q}(z)(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}(z)] = [F:\mathbb{Q}(z)]$ divides $7$, so
it is either $1$ or $7$.

By Step 3, $\alpha \notin \mathbb{Q}(z)$, so this degree is not $1$. Therefore
$$[F:\mathbb{Q}(z)] = 7.$$
