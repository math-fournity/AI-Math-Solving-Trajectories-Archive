## Primary Decomposition of $(x, y^2)$ in $K[x, y]$

**Claim.** The ideal $I = (x, y^2) \subset K[x, y]$ is already primary. Hence it
s (irredundant) primary decomposition is the trivial one:
$$
(x, y^2) = (x, y^2).
$$

**Proof.**

Recall that an ideal $Q$ in a commutative ring is **primary** if $ab \in Q$ and
$a \notin Q$ imply $b \in \sqrt{Q}$. A standard sufficient condition is:

> *If $\sqrt{Q}$ is a maximal ideal, then $Q$ is primary.*

We verify this condition for $I = (x, y^2)$.

**Step 1: Compute the radical.** We claim $\sqrt{I} = (x, y)$.

- ($\supseteq$): Clearly $x \in I \subseteq \sqrt{I}$. Also $y^2 \in I$, so $y \
in \sqrt{I}$. Hence $(x, y) \subseteq \sqrt{I}$.
- ($\subseteq$): Since $I = (x, y^2) \subseteq (x, y)$ and $(x, y)$ is a prime (
hence radical) ideal, any $f$ with $f^n \in I \subseteq (x, y)$ must satisfy $f
\in (x, y)$. Thus $\sqrt{I} \subseteq (x, y)$.

Therefore $\sqrt{I} = (x, y)$.

**Step 2: $(x, y)$ is maximal.** The quotient $K[x,y]/(x,y) \cong K$ is a field,
 so $(x, y)$ is a maximal ideal.

**Step 3: Conclude $I$ is primary.** Since $\sqrt{I} = (x, y)$ is maximal, $I =
(x, y^2)$ is primary by the criterion above.

**Direct verification (alternative).** Consider the quotient ring
$$
R/I = K[x,y]/(x, y^2) \cong K[y]/(y^2).
$$
In $K[y]/(y^2)$, the element $\bar{y}$ is nilpotent ($\bar{y}^2 = 0$), and the u
nique prime ideal is $(\bar{y}) = (y)/(y^2)$, which equals the nilradical. A rin
g whose nilradical is the unique prime (equivalently, the unique maximal) ideal
has the property that the zero ideal is primary. Pulling back, $I = (x, y^2)$ is
 primary in $K[x, y]$.

**Conclusion.** Since $(x, y^2)$ is itself primary, its irredundant primary deco
mposition is the single-component decomposition:
$$
\boxed{(x, y^2) = (x, y^2), \qquad \sqrt{(x, y^2)} = (x, y).}
$$

The associated prime is $(x, y)$, and the ideal is $(x,y)$-primary.
