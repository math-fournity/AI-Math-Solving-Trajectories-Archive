**Proof.**

Define $k(x) = \#\{n : a_n \leq x\}$. We use the integral representation of the
sum. For each $n$,
$$\frac{1}{a_n} = \int_{a_n}^{\infty} \frac{dt}{t^2}.$$
Since all terms are non-negative, Tonelli's theorem gives
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{a_n} = \int_0^{\infty} \frac{k(t)}{t^2}\,dt. \tag
{1}$$
(The integrand is $0$ for $t < a_1$, so the integral is well-defined.)

Likewise, for the tail sum $S_N := \sum_{n > N} \frac{1}{a_n}$, we have
$$S_N = \int_0^{\infty} \frac{\max(0,\, k(t) - N)}{t^2}\,dt, \tag{2}$$
since $\#\{n > N : a_n \leq t\} = \max(0, k(t) - N)$.

Since $\sum 1/a_n$ converges, $S_N \to 0$ as $N \to \infty$.

**Claim.** For every $T > 0$ with $k(T) > N$,
$$\frac{k(T) - N}{2T} \leq S_N. \tag{3}$$

*Proof of Claim.* Since $k$ is non-decreasing, for $t \in [T, 2T]$ we have $k(t)
 \geq k(T)$, hence $\max(0, k(t)-N) \geq k(T) - N > 0$. Applying (2),
$$S_N \geq \int_T^{2T} \frac{k(t) - N}{t^2}\,dt \geq (k(T) - N)\int_T^{2T}\frac{
dt}{t^2} = (k(T)-N)\cdot\frac{1}{2T}. \qquad \square$$

**Completing the proof.** Let $\varepsilon > 0$. Choose $N$ large enough that $S
_N < \varepsilon/4$. Now for any $T > 2N/\varepsilon$:

- **Case 1:** $k(T) \leq N$. Then $\dfrac{k(T)}{T} \leq \dfrac{N}{T} < \dfrac{N}
{2N/\varepsilon} = \dfrac{\varepsilon}{2} < \varepsilon$.

$$\frac{k(T)}{T} = \frac{k(T) - N}{T} + \frac{N}{T} \leq 2\,S_N + \frac{N}{T} <
2\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{4} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.$$

In both cases, $k(T)/T < \varepsilon$ for all $T > 2N/\varepsilon$. Since $\vare
psilon > 0$ was arbitrary,

$$\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{k(x)}{x} = 0. \qquad \blacksquare$$
