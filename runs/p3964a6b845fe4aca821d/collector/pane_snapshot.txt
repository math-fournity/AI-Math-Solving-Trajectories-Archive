## Solution

**Setup.** Place the circumcircle of $\triangle ABC$ as the unit circle centered
 at the origin $O$. Choose coordinates so that $B$ and $C$ are symmetric about t
he $x$-axis:
$$B = (\cos\beta,\, \sin\beta), \qquad C = (\cos\beta,\, -\sin\beta),$$
where $2\beta$ is the central angle subtending arc $BC$. Since $A$ lies on the m
ajor arc, the inscribed angle is
$$\angle BAC = \beta.$$
Let $A = (\cos\gamma,\, \sin\gamma)$ for some $\gamma$ on the major arc.

**Point $D$ (intersection of tangents at $B$ and $C$).** The tangent to the unit
 circle at $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ is $x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta = 1$. Thus:
$$\text{at } B:\ x\cos\beta + y\sin\beta = 1, \qquad \text{at } C:\ x\cos\beta -
 y\sin\beta = 1.$$
Adding gives $x = \sec\beta$; subtracting gives $y=0$. So
$$D = (\sec\beta,\, 0).$$

**Orthocenter $H$ and nine-point center $N$.** For a triangle inscribed in the u
nit circle with circumcenter at the origin, the orthocenter is the vector sum $H
 = A + B + C$:
$$H = (\cos\gamma + 2\cos\beta,\; \sin\gamma).$$
The nine-point center is the midpoint of $O$ and $H$:
$$N = \frac{H}{2} = \left(\frac{\cos\gamma + 2\cos\beta}{2},\; \frac{\sin\gamma}
{2}\right).$$

**Collinearity condition.** Points $A$, $D$, $N$ are collinear iff the cross pro
duct $(D-A)\times(N-A)=0$:
$$D - A = (\sec\beta - \cos\gamma,\; -\sin\gamma),$$
$$N - A = \left(\frac{2\cos\beta - \cos\gamma}{2},\; -\frac{\sin\gamma}{2}\right
).$$

The $z$-component of the cross product:
$$(\sec\beta - \cos\gamma)\!\left(-\frac{\sin\gamma}{2}\right) - (-\sin\gamma)\!
\left(\frac{2\cos\beta - \cos\gamma}{2}\right) = 0.$$

Factoring out $\frac{\sin\gamma}{2}$ (nonzero since the triangle is nondegenerat
e):
$$\frac{\sin\gamma}{2}\Big[-(\sec\beta - \cos\gamma) + (2\cos\beta - \cos\gamma)
\Big] = 0.$$

Since $\sin\gamma \neq 0$:
$$-\sec\beta + \cos\gamma + 2\cos\beta - \cos\gamma = 0,$$
$$-\sec\beta + 2\cos\beta = 0,$$
$$2\cos\beta = \frac{1}{\cos\beta},$$
$$2\cos^2\beta = 1 \implies \cos^2\beta = \frac{1}{2}.$$

Since $\beta = \angle BAC$ is an angle of an acute triangle, $\cos\beta > 0$, gi
ving $\cos\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, hence
$$\beta = 45°.$$

**Verification of consistency.** The parameter $\gamma$ (position of $A$ on the
major arc) cancelled completely, confirming the condition depends solely on $\an
gle BAC$. An acute-scalene triangle with $\angle A = 45°$ exists (e.g., angles $
45°, 60°, 75°$), so the value is achievable.

$$\boxed{\alpha = 45}$$
