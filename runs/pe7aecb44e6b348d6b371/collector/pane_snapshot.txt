## Proof

Let $f: A \to B$ be a ring homomorphism of commutative rings with units, $I \sub
set A$ an ideal, and let
$$\phi: B \to A/I \otimes_A B, \qquad b \mapsto \overline{1}\otimes_A b$$
be the natural $A$-algebra map (where $\overline{1}$ is the class of $1\in A$ in
 $A/I$). Let $J = \ker\phi$. We claim $J = f(I)\cdot B$, the ideal generated by
$f(I)$ in $B$.

**Step 1: A canonical isomorphism.** Define
$$\Phi: A/I \otimes_A B \longrightarrow B/f(I)B, \qquad \overline{a}\otimes_A b
\longmapsto \overline{f(a)\,b}.$$

*Well-definedness.* The map $A \times B \to B/f(I)B$, $(a,b)\mapsto \overline{f(
a)b}$, is $A$-bilinear (the $A$-action on $B$ is via $f$, and on $A/I$ via the q
uotient). If $a$ is replaced by $a + x$ with $x\in I$, then $f(a+x)b = f(a)b + f
(x)b$, and $f(x)b \in f(I)B$, so the class in $B/f(I)B$ is unchanged. Hence $\Ph
i$ is well-defined. It is clearly a ring homomorphism.

*Inverse.* Define $\Psi: B/f(I)B \to A/I\otimes_A B$ by $\overline{b}\mapsto \ov
erline{1}\otimes_A b$. This is well-defined: if $b$ is replaced by $b + \sum f(x
_i)b_i$ with $x_i\in I$, then
$$\overline{1}\otimes_A\Big(b+\sum f(x_i)b_i\Big) = \overline{1}\otimes b + \sum
 \overline{1}\otimes f(x_i)b_i = \overline{1}\otimes b + \sum \overline{x_i}\oti
mes b_i = \overline{1}\otimes b,$$
since $\overline{x_i}=\overline{0}$ in $A/I$. Clearly $\Phi\circ\Psi = \mathrm{i
d}$ and $\Psi\circ\Phi = \mathrm{id}$, so $\Phi$ is an isomorphism.

**Step 2: Identify $\phi$ under the isomorphism.** Under $\Phi$, the map $\phi$
becomes
$$\Phi\circ\phi: B \to B/f(I)B, \qquad b \mapsto \Phi(\overline{1}\otimes b) = \
overline{f(1)\,b} = \overline{b},$$
which is exactly the canonical quotient map $B \to B/f(I)B$.

**Step 3: Compute the kernel.** The kernel of the quotient map $B \to B/f(I)B$ i
s precisely $f(I)B$. Since $\Phi$ is an isomorphism,
$$J = \ker\phi = \ker(\Phi\circ\phi) = f(I)B.$$

**Conclusion.** Yes, $J$ equals the ideal generated by $f(I)$ in $B$. $\blacksqu
are$
