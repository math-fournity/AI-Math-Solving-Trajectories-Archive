## Problem

Find the solution of
$$\nabla^2 u + k^2 u = -f \;\text{on } D, \qquad u = g \;\text{on } \partial D,$
$
assuming $k^2$ is **not** an eigenvalue of $-\nabla^2$ on $D$ with Dirichlet bou
ndary conditions.

---

## Step 1. Reduction to homogeneous boundary data

Let $G$ be any (sufficiently smooth, e.g. $C^2(\overline D)$) extension of the b
oundary data $g$ into $\overline D$. Set
$$v := u - G, \qquad \text{so that } v|_{\partial D} = 0.$$
Then $v$ satisfies
$$\nabla^2 v + k^2 v = -\bigl(f + \nabla^2 G + k^2 G\bigr) =: -\tilde f, \qquad
v|_{\partial D} = 0,$$
which is a **homogeneous Dirichlet** problem for the Helmholtz operator $L := \n
abla^2 + k^2$. Hence it suffices to solve the case $g \equiv 0$; the general sol
ution is recovered as $u = v + G$.

---

## Step 2. The Dirichlet Green's function for the Helmholtz operator

Let $\{\varphi_n, \lambda_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ be the orthonormal eigenfunctions
and eigenvalues of $-\nabla^2$ on $D$ with Dirichlet boundary conditions:
$$-\nabla^2 \varphi_n = \lambda_n \varphi_n, \qquad \varphi_n|_{\partial D} = 0,
 \qquad 0 < \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \cdots \to \infty.$$
The hypothesis "$k^2$ is not an eigenvalue" means
$$k^2 \ne \lambda_n \quad \text{for all } n \ge 1.$$

Define the **Dirichlet Green's function** for $L = \nabla^2 + k^2$ by the eigenf
unction expansion
$$\boxed{\;G_D(x,\xi) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\varphi_n(x)\,\varphi_n(\xi)}{
k^2 - \lambda_n}.\;}$$

**Convergence.** Since $\lambda_n \to \infty$ and $k^2 - \lambda_n \to -\infty$,
 and the eigenfunctions are bounded in $L^2$, the series converges in $L^2(D \ti
mes D)$ and, by elliptic regularity / Weyl's law, pointwise and uniformly away f
rom $x = \xi$. The denominator never vanishes by hypothesis, so $G_D$ is well de
fined.

**Distributional identity.** Formally,
$$\bigl(\nabla_x^2 + k^2\bigr) G_D(x,\xi) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(\nabla_x^
2 + k^2)\varphi_n(x)\,\varphi_n(\xi)}{k^2 - \lambda_n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \fr
ac{(k^2 - \lambda_n)\,\varphi_n(x)\varphi_n(\xi)}{k^2 - \lambda_n} = \sum_{n=1}^
{\infty}\varphi_n(x)\varphi_n(\xi) = -\delta(x-\xi),$$
where the last equality uses the completeness relation $\sum_n \varphi_n(x)\varp
hi_n(\xi) = \delta(x-\xi)$, and the sign $\nabla^2\varphi_n = -\lambda_n\varphi_
n$. Thus
$$\bigl(\nabla_x^2 + k^2\bigr) G_D(x,\xi) = -\delta(x-\xi), \qquad G_D(x,\xi)\bi
g|_{x\in\partial D} = 0,$$
where the boundary condition follows from $\varphi_n|_{\partial D} = 0$ for ever
y $n$.

---

## Step 3. Representation formula (homogeneous case)

For $g \equiv 0$, set
$$v(x) = \int_D G_D(x,\xi)\,f(\xi)\,d\xi.$$
Then, using the distributional identity from Step 2,
$$\bigl(\nabla_x^2 + k^2\bigr) v(x) = \int_D \bigl(\nabla_x^2 + k^2\bigr) G_D(x,
\xi)\,f(\xi)\,d\xi = \int_D \bigl[-\delta(x-\xi)\bigr] f(\xi)\,d\xi = -f(x),$$
and
$$v\big|_{\partial D} = \int_D G_D(x,\xi)\big|_{x\in\partial D}\, f(\xi)\,d\xi =
 0,$$
since $G_D$ vanishes on the boundary. Hence $v$ solves the homogeneous Dirichlet
 problem.

---

## Step 4. General boundary data via Green's second identity

For **nonhomogeneous** boundary data $g$, apply Green's second identity to $u$ a
nd $G_D(x,\cdot)$ on $D$. Since
$$\nabla_\xi^2 G_D(x,\xi) + k^2 G_D(x,\xi) = -\delta(x-\xi), \qquad \nabla^2 u +
 k^2 u = -f,$$
we obtain
$$\int_D \Bigl[u(\xi)\bigl(\nabla_\xi^2 + k^2\bigr)G_D(x,\xi) - G_D(x,\xi)\bigl(
\nabla^2 + k^2\bigr)u(\xi)\Bigr]d\xi = \oint_{\partial D}\Bigl[u\,\partial_n G_D
 - G_D\,\partial_n u\Bigr]dS.$$
The left-hand side is
$$\int_D\bigl[-u(\xi)\delta(x-\xi) + G_D(x,\xi) f(\xi)\bigr]d\xi = -u(x) + \int_
D G_D(x,\xi) f(\xi)\,d\xi.$$
On the boundary, $G_D|_{\partial D} = 0$ and $u|_{\partial D} = g$, so the bound
ary integral reduces to
$$\oint_{\partial D} g(\xi)\,\partial_{n_\xi} G_D(x,\xi)\,dS_\xi.$$
Equating and solving for $u(x)$:
$$\boxed{\;u(x) = \int_D G_D(x,\xi)\,f(\xi)\,d\xi + \oint_{\partial D} g(\xi)\,\
frac{\partial G_D}{\partial n_\xi}(x,\xi)\,dS_\xi,\;}$$
where $n_\xi$ is the outward unit normal at $\xi \in \partial D$.

## Step 5. Verification

* **PDE.** Applying $\nabla_x^2 + k^2$ to the representation and using $(\nabla_
x^2+k^2)G_D = -\delta(x-\xi)$:
  - The volume term gives $\int_D (-\delta(x-\xi))f(\xi)\,d\xi = -f(x)$.
  - The boundary term: $\partial_{n_\xi}G_D$ is harmonic in $x$ for $x \in D$ (s
ince $G_D$ is Helmholtz-harmonic away from $\xi$, and the normal derivative pres
erves this), so $(\nabla_x^2+k^2)\partial_{n_\xi}G_D = 0$ for $x \in D$. Hence t
he boundary contribution to the PDE is $0$.

  Therefore $(\nabla^2 + k^2)u = -f$. ✓

* **Boundary condition.** As $x \to x_0 \in \partial D$, the volume term $\int_D
 G_D(x,\xi)f\,d\xi \to 0$ because $G_D(x_0,\xi) = 0$. The boundary term tends to
 $g(x_0)$ by the standard jump relation of the double-layer (normal-derivative)
potential with Dirichlet kernel — precisely the property that makes $G_D$ the Di
richlet Green's function. Hence $u|_{\partial D} = g$. ✓

## Step 6. Uniqueness

Suppose $u_1, u_2$ are two solutions and set $w = u_1 - u_2$. Then
$$(\nabla^2 + k^2)w = 0, \qquad w|_{\partial D} = 0.$$
Expand $w = \sum_n c_n \varphi_n$ in the Dirichlet eigenbasis. The PDE gives $(k
^2 - \lambda_n)c_n = 0$ for every $n$. Since $k^2 \ne \lambda_n$ by hypothesis,
every $c_n = 0$, so $w \equiv 0$. Hence the solution is **unique**. ✓

## Conclusion

Under the assumption that $k^2$ is not a Dirichlet eigenvalue of $-\nabla^2$ on
$D$, the unique solution of
$$\nabla^2 u + k^2 u = -f \;\text{ in } D, \qquad u = g \;\text{ on } \partial D
,$$
is given by
$$\boxed{\;u(x) = \int_D G_D(x,\xi)\,f(\xi)\,d\xi \;+\; \oint_{\partial D} g(\xi

where the **Dirichlet Green's function** for the Helmholtz operator is
$$G_D(x,\xi) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\varphi_n(x)\,\varphi_n(\xi)}{k^2 - \la
mbda_n},$$
