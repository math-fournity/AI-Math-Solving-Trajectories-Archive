## Solution

Let $\zeta = e^{2\pi i/5}$, a primitive 5th root of unity, and let $\alpha = \sq
rt[5]{2}$. We seek the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta
)$.

**Step 1: Degrees of the individual extensions.**

The polynomial $x^5 - 2$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ by Eisenstein's criter
ion at $p = 2$, so $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) : \mathbb{Q}] = 5$.

The cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_5(x) = x^4 + x^3 + x^2 + x + 1$ is irreducible o
ver $\mathbb{Q}$, so $[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta) : \mathbb{Q}] = 4$.

**Step 2: The intersection $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) \cap \mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$.**

The field $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) / \mathbb{Q}$ has degree 5 (prime), so its only su
bfields are $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ itself. The intersection $\mat
hbb{Q}(\alpha) \cap \mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$ is a subfield of $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$, he
nce equals $\mathbb{Q}$ or $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$.

If it equaled $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$, then $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) \subseteq \mathbb{Q
}(\zeta)$, which would require $5 \mid 4$ — impossible. Therefore:

$$\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) \cap \mathbb{Q}(\zeta) = \mathbb{Q}.$$

**Step 3: Degree of the compositum.**

Since the two extensions have coprime degrees ($\gcd(5, 4) = 1$) and trivial int
ersection:

$$[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha, \zeta) : \mathbb{Q}] = [\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}] \cd
ot [\mathbb{Q}(\zeta):\mathbb{Q}] = 5 \cdot 4 = 20.$$

**Step 4: The minimal polynomial degree.**

By the tower law:

$$[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha, \zeta) : \mathbb{Q}(\zeta)] = \frac{[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha, \z
eta) : \mathbb{Q}]}{[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta) : \mathbb{Q}]} = \frac{20}{4} = 5.$$

Thus the minimal polynomial of $\alpha = \sqrt[5]{2}$ over $K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta
)$ has degree **5**.

**Step 5: Identifying the minimal polynomial.**

Since $\mathbb{Q} \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$, the polynomial $x^5 - 2$ lies in
 $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)[x]$. It is a monic polynomial of degree 5 with $\alpha$ as a
 root. The minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$ divides $x^5
- 2$ and has degree 5. Since $x^5 - 2$ itself has degree 5 and is monic, the min
imal polynomial must be exactly $x^5 - 2$.

(Equivalently, $x^5 - 2$ remains irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$: it cannot
 factor, since any proper factor would give a minimal polynomial of degree $< 5$
, contradicting Step 4.)

**Answer.** The minimal polynomial of $\sqrt[5]{2}$ over $\mathbb{Q}(e^{2\pi i/5
})$ is:

$$\boxed{x^5 - 2}$$
