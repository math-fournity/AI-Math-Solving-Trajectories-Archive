## Proof

**Claim.** The finite groups having exactly one proper maximal subgroup are prec
isely the cyclic groups $C_{p^k}$ of prime‑power order, where $p$ is prime and $
k\geq 1$.

---

### Necessity ($C_{p^k}$ has a unique maximal subgroup)

The subgroup lattice of $C_{p^k}$ is a chain
$$1 = C_{p^0} < C_{p} < C_{p^2} < \cdots < C_{p^k}.$$
The unique maximal subgroup is $C_{p^{k-1}}$ (of index $p$). Hence every cyclic
$p$‑group has exactly one proper maximal subgroup. (For $k=1$, $C_p$ is simple a
nd its unique maximal subgroup is $\{1\}$.)

---

### Sufficiency (the only such groups are cyclic $p$‑groups)

Let $G$ be a finite group with a unique proper maximal subgroup $M$.

**Step 1 — $G$ is cyclic.** Every proper subgroup of $G$ is contained in some ma
ximal subgroup; since $M$ is the only one, *every* proper subgroup of $G$ lies i
nside $M$. Pick any $g\in G\setminus M$ (non‑empty since $M$ is proper). Then $\
langle g\rangle$ is a subgroup not contained in $M$, so it cannot be proper; thu
s $\langle g\rangle = G$. Therefore $G$ is cyclic, say $G\cong C_n$.

**Step 2 — $n$ is a prime power.** The maximal subgroups of $C_n$ are exactly th
e subgroups of prime index: if $H\le C_n$ is maximal, then $C_n/H$ has no proper
 nontrivial subgroup, hence is cyclic of prime order, so $[C_n:H]$ is prime. Con
versely, for each prime $p\mid n$ there is a unique subgroup of index $p$ (namel
y the subgroup of order $n/p$), and it is maximal. Thus the maximal subgroups of
 $C_n$ are in bijection with the distinct prime divisors of $n$.

Having exactly one maximal subgroup therefore forces $n$ to have exactly one pri
me divisor, i.e. $n=p^k$ for some prime $p$ and $k\ge 1$.

---

Combining both directions, the finite groups with a unique proper maximal subgro
up are exactly
$$\boxed{C_{p^k}\ \text{with }p\text{ prime and }k\ge 1.}$$
