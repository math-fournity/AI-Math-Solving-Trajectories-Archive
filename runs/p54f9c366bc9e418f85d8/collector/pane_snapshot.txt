Looking at this problem, I need to find the volume of the ring-shaped region bou

 both.

## Setup

Place the contact point at the origin, with both sphere centers on the $x$-axis:
 sphere 1 at $(-r, 0)$, sphere 2 at $(R, 0)$. By symmetry, the cone's axis is th
e $x$-axis.

**Finding the cone geometry (2D cross-section):** The upper tangent line $y = m(
x - x_0)$ from apex $(x_0, 0)$ satisfies the tangency conditions:
$$\frac{m(-r - x_0)}{\sqrt{m^2+1}} = r, \qquad \frac{m(R - x_0)}{\sqrt{m^2+1}} =
 R$$

Dividing gives $x_0 = -\frac{2rR}{R-r}$. Substituting back:
$$\frac{m}{\sqrt{m^2+1}} = \frac{R-r}{R+r} \implies m^2 = \frac{(R-r)^2}{4Rr}, \
qquad 1+m^2 = \frac{(R+r)^2}{4Rr}$$

**Tangent points:** $T_1$ on sphere 1 and $T_2$ on sphere 2:
$$x_{T_1} = \frac{m^2 x_0 - r}{1+m^2} = -\frac{2Rr}{R+r}, \qquad x_{T_2} = \frac
{R + m^2 x_0}{1+m^2} = \frac{2Rr}{R+r}$$

## Volume Computation

The buffer zone is the solid of revolution about the $x$-axis. The cross-section
 (upper half) is bounded below by the sphere arcs and above by the cone line, fo
r $x \in [x_{T_1}, 0]$ (sphere 1) and $x \in [0, x_{T_2}]$ (sphere 2):

$$V = \pi\!\int_{x_{T_1}}^{0}\!\bigl[y_{\text{cone}}^2 - y_{s_1}^2\bigr]\,dx + \
pi\!\int_{0}^{x_{T_2}}\!\bigl[y_{\text{cone}}^2 - y_{s_2}^2\bigr]\,dx$$

where $y_{\text{cone}}^2 = m^2(x-x_0)^2$, $\;y_{s_1}^2 = -x^2-2xr$, $\;y_{s_2}^2
 = 2xR - x^2$.

### Cone segment volume
$$V_{\text{cone}} = \frac{\pi m^2}{3}\Bigl[(x_{T_2}-x_0)^3 - (x_{T_1}-x_0)^3\Big
r]$$

Computing: $x_{T_2}-x_0 = \frac{4R^2 r}{R^2-r^2}$, $\;x_{T_1}-x_0 = \frac{4Rr^2}
{R^2-r^2}$, so
$$V_{\text{cone}} = \frac{\pi m^2}{3}\cdot\frac{64R^3 r^3(R^3-r^3)}{(R^2-r^2)^3}
 = \frac{16\pi R^2 r^2(R^2+Rr+r^2)}{3(R+r)^3}$$

### Sphere 1 cap volume
$$V_{s_1} = \pi\!\int_{x_{T_1}}^{0}\!(-x^2-2xr)\,dx = \pi\Bigl[\tfrac{x_{T_1}^3}
{3}+r\,x_{T_1}^2\Bigr] = \frac{4\pi R^2 r^3(R+3r)}{3(R+r)^3}$$

### Sphere 2 cap volume
$$V_{s_2} = \pi\!\int_{0}^{x_{T_2}}\!(2xR-x^2)\,dx = \pi\Bigl[R\,x_{T_2}^2 - \tf
rac{x_{T_2}^3}{3}\Bigr] = \frac{4\pi R^3 r^2(3R+r)}{3(R+r)^3}$$

### Combining
$$V = \frac{\pi R^2 r^2}{3(R+r)^3}\Bigl[16(R^2{+}Rr{+}r^2) - 4r(R{+}3r) - 4R(3R{
+}r)\Bigr]$$

Expanding the bracket:
$$16R^2+16Rr+16r^2 - 4Rr-12r^2 - 12R^2-4Rr = 4R^2+8Rr+4r^2 = 4(R+r)^2$$

Therefore:
$$V = \frac{\pi R^2 r^2 \cdot 4(R+r)^2}{3(R+r)^3} = \boxed{\dfrac{4\pi R^2 r^2}{
3(R+r)}}$$

## Verification

**Limit $r = R$:** The cone degenerates to a cylinder of radius $R$ (apex at inf
inity, $m\to 0$). The formula gives $V = \frac{4\pi R^4}{6R} = \frac{2\pi R^3}{3
}$. Direct check: cylinder segment $= \pi R^2 \cdot 2R = 2\pi R^3$; two hemisphe
res $= \frac{4\pi R^3}{3}$; difference $= \frac{2\pi R^3}{3}$. ✓

**Limit $r \to 0$:** $V \to 0$, consistent with a vanishing buffer zone. ✓

The expression $\frac{4R^2 r^2}{3(R+r)}$ is a rational function of $r$ and $R$.
