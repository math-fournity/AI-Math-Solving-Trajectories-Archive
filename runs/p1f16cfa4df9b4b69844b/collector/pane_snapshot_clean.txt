## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle \int_C \frac{1}{e^z - 1}\,dz$ where $C$ is the circle
 $|z - 2i| = 4$, taken with positive (counterclockwise) orientation.

### Step 1: Identify the singularities

The integrand $f(z) = \frac{1}{e^z - 1}$ has singularities where $e^z = 1$, i.e.
, at
$$z_k = 2\pi i\, k, \qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

### Step 2: Determine which singularities lie inside $C$

For a singularity $z_k = 2\pi i k$, its distance to the center $2i$ is
$$|z_k - 2i| = |2\pi i k - 2i| = 2|\pi k - 1|.$$

We check which of these are $< 4$:

| $k$ | $z_k$ | Distance $2|\pi k - 1|$ | Inside $C$? |
|-----|-------|------------------------|-------------|
| $0$ | $0$ | $2|0 - 1| = 2$ | Yes ($2 < 4$) |
| $1$ | $2\pi i$ | $2(\pi - 1) \approx 4.283$ | No ($> 4$) |
| $-1$ | $-2\pi i$ | $2(\pi + 1) \approx 8.283$ | No ($> 4$) |
| $|k| \geq 2$ | — | $\geq 2(2\pi - 1) \approx 10.57$ | No |

**Only $z_0 = 0$ lies inside the contour.**

### Step 3: Compute the residue at $z = 0$

Since $(e^z - 1)' = e^z$ and $e^0 = 1 \neq 0$, the zero of $e^z - 1$ at $z = 0$
is simple. Hence $z = 0$ is a **simple pole** of $f$. The residue is
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} \frac{1}{e^z - 1} = \lim_{z \to 0} \frac{z}{e^z - 1}
= \frac{1}{(e^z - 1)'\big|_{z=0}} = \frac{1}{e^0} = 1.$$

(Equivalently, expanding $e^z - 1 = z + \tfrac{z^2}{2} + \cdots$ gives $\frac{1}
{e^z-1} = \frac{1}{z} - \frac{1}{2} + \cdots$, confirming the residue is $1$.)

### Step 4: Apply the residue theorem

By the residue theorem,
$$\int_C \frac{1}{e^z - 1}\,dz = 2\pi i \sum \operatorname{Res} = 2\pi i \cdot 1
 = \boxed{2\pi i}.$$
