### Step 1: Reduce to Cauchy's logarithmic equation

Divide both sides of
$$f(xy)=e^{xy-x-y}\bigl(e^{y}f(x)+e^{x}f(y)\bigr)$$
by $e^{xy}$:

$$\frac{f(xy)}{e^{xy}}=e^{-x-y}\bigl(e^{y}f(x)+e^{x}f(y)\bigr)=e^{-x}f(x)+e^{-y}
f(y).$$

Define
$$h(x):=e^{-x}f(x),\qquad x>0.$$

Then the equation becomes
$$h(xy)=h(x)+h(y)\qquad\forall\,x,y>0.\tag{1}$$

### Step 2: Solve (1)

Since $f$ is differentiable at $x=1$ (we are given $f'(1)=e$), $h$ is continuous
 at $x=1$. A function continuous at one point and satisfying (1) on $\mathbb{R}^
{+}$ is of the form
$$h(x)=c\ln x$$
for some constant $c\in\mathbb{R}$.

(Indeed, setting $x=e^{u},y=e^{v}$ and $H(u)=h(e^{u})$ gives $H(u+v)=H(u)+H(v)$
with $H$ continuous, hence $H(u)=ku$, so $h(x)=k\ln x$.)

Thus
$$e^{-x}f(x)=c\ln x\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(x)=c\,e^{x}\ln x.\tag{2}$$

### Step 3: Determine $c$ from $f'(1)=e$

Differentiate (2):
$$f'(x)=c\,e^{x}\!\left(\ln x+\frac{1}{x}\right).$$

At $x=1$:
$$f'(1)=c\,e^{1}\bigl(\ln 1+1\bigr)=c\,e.$$

The condition $f'(1)=e$ gives $c\,e=e$, hence
$$c=1.$$

### Step 4: Verification

With $f(x)=e^{x}\ln x$:

- **LHS:** $f(xy)=e^{xy}\ln(xy)=e^{xy}(\ln x+\ln y)$.
- **RHS:** $e^{xy-x-y}\bigl(e^{y}\cdot e^{x}\ln x+e^{x}\cdot e^{y}\ln y\bigr)=e^
{xy-x-y}\cdot e^{x+y}(\ln x+\ln y)=e^{xy}(\ln x+\ln y).$

LHS $=$ RHS. ✓

Also $f'(1)=e^{1}(\ln 1+1)=e$. ✓

### Result

$$\boxed{\,f(x)=e^{x}\ln x\,}$$
