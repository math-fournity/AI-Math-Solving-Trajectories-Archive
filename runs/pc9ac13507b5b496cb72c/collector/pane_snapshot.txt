The answer is **false**.

## Proof

We exhibit a counterexample: the Hirzebruch surface $\mathbb{F}_1 = \mathbb{P}(\
mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}\oplus \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(1))$ is a $\mathbb{P}
^1$-bundle over $\mathbb{P}^1$ with a section, yet it is not a trivial bundle.

**Setup.** Let $B = \mathbb{P}^1$ (a smooth rational curve). Any $\mathbb{P}^1$-
bundle over $B$ is of the form $\mathbb{P}(E)$ for a rank-2 vector bundle $E$ on
 $B$, with $\mathbb{P}(E)\cong \mathbb{P}(E\otimes L)$ for any line bundle $L$.
By the Birkhoff–Grothendieck theorem, every rank-2 bundle on $\mathbb{P}^1$ spli
ts:
$$E \cong \mathcal{O}(a)\oplus \mathcal{O}(b),\quad a\le b.$$
Tensoring by $\mathcal{O}(-a)$ normalizes to $E\cong \mathcal{O}\oplus \mathcal{
O}(n)$ with $n=b-a\ge 0$. Thus every $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundle over $\mathbb{P}^1$ i
s isomorphic to the Hirzebruch surface $\mathbb{F}_n=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}\oplu
s\mathcal{O}(n))$ for some $n\ge 0$.

**Existence of a section.** Every $\mathbb{F}_n$ admits a section. Indeed, the s
urjection $\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(n)\twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{O}$ define
s a section $S_0\subset \mathbb{F}_n$ with self-intersection $S_0^2 = -n$, and t
he surjection $\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(n)\twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{O}(n)$
 defines another section $S_\infty$ with $S_\infty^2=+n$. Hence the "with a sect
ion" hypothesis is satisfied for **all** $n\ge 0$, including $n=1$.

**Non-triviality of $\mathbb{F}_1$.** The trivial bundle is $\mathbb{F}_0 = \mat
hbb{P}^1\times\mathbb{P}^1$. We show $\mathbb{F}_1\not\cong \mathbb{F}_0$. The s
urface $\mathbb{F}_0$ contains no curve of negative self-intersection: every eff
ective divisor on $\mathbb{P}^1\times\mathbb{P}^1$ has non-negative self-interse
ction (the cone of curves is generated by the two rulings, both of self-intersec
tion $0$). However, $\mathbb{F}_1$ contains the section $S_0$ with $S_0^2 = -1 <
 0$. Therefore $\mathbb{F}_1\not\cong \mathbb{F}_0$.

(More generally, $\mathbb{F}_n\cong \mathbb{F}_m$ iff $n=m$, since the minimal s
elf-intersection of a section is an invariant distinguishing them; in particular
 only $\mathbb{F}_0$ is the trivial bundle.)

**Conclusion.** $\mathbb{F}_1$ is a $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundle over the smooth ration
al curve $\mathbb{P}^1$, it possesses a section, and it is not the trivial bundl
e. Therefore the statement "every $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundle over a smooth rational c
urve with a section is trivial" is **false**.
