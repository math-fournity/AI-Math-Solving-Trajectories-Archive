## Solution

We evaluate
$$I = \int_0^\infty J_0(x)\,\frac{\sin(\pi x)}{\pi x}\,e^{-x}\,dx.$$

**Step 1: Integral representation of sinc.** Since
$$\frac{\sin(\pi x)}{\pi x} = \int_0^1 \cos(\pi x t)\,dt,$$
and the integrand is absolutely integrable ($|J_0(x)|\le 1$, $|\text{sinc}(\pi x
)|\le 1$, $e^{-x}$ decays), we may interchange the order of integration:
$$I = \int_0^1\!\int_0^\infty J_0(x)\,e^{-x}\cos(\pi t x)\,dx\,dt.$$

**Step 2: Laplace transform of $J_0$.** The classical identity
$$\int_0^\infty J_0(x)\,e^{-sx}\,dx = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+s^2}}, \qquad \operatorna
me{Re}(s)>0,$$
with $s = 1 - i\pi t$ (so $\operatorname{Re}(s)=1>0$) and $\cos(\pi tx)=\operato
rname{Re}(e^{i\pi tx})$ gives
$$\int_0^\infty J_0(x)\,e^{-x}\cos(\pi tx)\,dx = \operatorname{Re}\frac{1}{\sqrt
{1+(1-i\pi t)^2}}.$$

**Step 3: Simplify the radicand.** With $u = \pi t$,
$$1+(1-i\pi t)^2 = 2 - \pi^2 t^2 - 2i\pi t = 2 - u^2 - 2iu = 1 - (u+i)^2,$$
since $1-(u+i)^2 = 1 - u^2 - 2iu + 1 = 2-u^2-2iu$. Therefore
$$I = \frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Re}\int_0^\pi \frac{du}{\sqrt{1-(u+i)^2}}.$$

**Step 4: Evaluate via antiderivative.** Substituting $w = u+i$, the path runs f
rom $i$ to $\pi+i$ along the horizontal line $\operatorname{Im}(w)=1$. Along thi
s path $1-w^2$ is never real (it would be real only for real $w$), so it never c
rosses the branch cut of $\sqrt{\cdot}$; the integrand is analytic and
$$I = \frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Re}\Big[\arcsin(w)\Big]_{i}^{\pi+i} = \frac{1}{
\pi}\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\arcsin(\pi+i) - \arcsin(i)\right).$$

Since $\arcsin(i) = i\,\operatorname{arcsinh}(1) = i\ln(1+\sqrt{2})$ is purely i
maginary, its real part is $0$.

**Step 5: Real part of $\arcsin(\pi+i)$.** Write $\arcsin(x+iy) = u + iv$ with $
x=\pi$, $y=1$. From $\sin(u+iv) = \sin u\cosh v + i\cos u\sinh v = x + iy$, squa
ring and eliminating $v$ yields the quadratic in $s = \sin^2 u$:
$$s^2 - s(1+x^2+y^2) + x^2 = 0.$$
With $x=\pi$, $y=1$: $1+x^2+y^2 = \pi^2+2$ and the discriminant is
$$(\pi^2+2)^2 - 4\pi^2 = \pi^4+4.$$
The root with $s \le 1$ (required for real $u$) is
$$\sin^2 u = \frac{\pi^2 + 2 - \sqrt{\pi^4+4}}{2}.$$

(One verifies $\sqrt{\pi^4+4} \approx 10.07$, so $s \approx 0.900 \le 1$; the ot
her root exceeds $1$.)

**Step 6: Assemble the answer.**
$$\operatorname{Re}(\arcsin(\pi+i)) = u = \arcsin\sqrt{\frac{\pi^2+2-\sqrt{\pi^4
+4}}{2}},$$
and therefore
$$\boxed{I = \frac{1}{\pi}\arcsin\sqrt{\frac{\pi^2+2-\sqrt{\pi^4+4}}{2}}.}$$
