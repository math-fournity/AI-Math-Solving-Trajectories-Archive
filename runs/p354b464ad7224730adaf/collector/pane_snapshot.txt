We evaluate
$$I=\int \delta(x)\,\log\!\big(\delta(x-a)\big)\,dx,$$
where $\delta$ is the Dirac delta distribution and $a$ is a constant.

### Step 1 — Apply the sifting property

The defining property of the Dirac delta is that for any test function $g$ (suff
iciently regular at the origin),
$$\int \delta(x)\,g(x)\,dx = g(0).$$

Here the role of $g$ is played by $g(x)=\log\!\big(\delta(x-a)\big)$. Treating t
his formally as the test function and applying the sifting property with the del
ta centered at $x=0$,
$$I = g(0) = \log\!\big(\delta(0-a)\big) = \log\!\big(\delta(-a)\big).$$

### Step 2 — Use evenness of the delta

The Dirac delta is an even distribution: $\delta(-a)=\delta(a)$. Hence
$$I = \log\!\big(\delta(a)\big).$$

### Step 3 — Interpret the result

The value of $\delta(a)$ depends on the constant $a$:

- **If $a\neq 0$:** the delta function vanishes away from its support, so $\delt
a(a)=0$. Therefore
  $$I = \log(0) = -\infty.$$

- **If $a=0$:** the argument of the delta sits at its singular support, where $\
delta(0)\to+\infty$ (in the distributional/regularized sense, e.g. $\delta_\vare
psilon(0)\sim 1/\varepsilon\to\infty$). Therefore
  $$I = \log\!\big(\delta(0)\big)=+\infty.$$

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{\int \delta(x)\,\log\!\big(\delta(x-a)\big)\,dx \;=\; \log\!\big(\delta
(a)\big) \;=\; \begin{cases} -\infty, & a\neq 0,\\[4pt] +\infty, & a=0. \end{cas
es}}$$

The key step is the sifting (sampling) property of the Dirac delta, which collap
ses the integral to the value of the integrand's non-delta factor evaluated at t
he delta's support point $x=0$; the evenness $\delta(-a)=\delta(a)$ then yields
the compact form $\log(\delta(a))$, whose two cases follow directly from the sup
port structure of $\delta$.
