Any number with at most $k$ digits using $\{0,1,2,7\}$ is at most $\underbrace{7
7\cdots7}_{k} < 10^k$, so it satisfies the bound. Every such number can be uniqu

zeros). Thus the count equals the number of length-$k$ strings over $\{0,1,2,7\}

$$0 \to 0, \quad 1 \to 1, \quad 2 \to 2, \quad 7 \to 1.$$

7), and residue 2 has **1** choice (digit 2).

$$\begin{aligned}
a_{k+1} &= a_k + 2\,c_k + b_k, \\
b_{k+1} &= b_k + 2\,a_k + c_k, \\
c_{k+1} &= c_k + 2\,b_k + a_k,
\end{aligned}$$
