## Solution

a-b)^2\sin\gamma$ is degree 2 in lengths (each $(\cdot-\cdot)^2$ is degree 2, si

$$OI^2 = R^2-2Rr = 1-2r.$$

**Expressing $N$ via $s$ and $r$.** Let $s$ be the semiperimeter and $r$ the inr
adius. With $R=1$:
- $a+b+c=2s$, so $x+y+z:=\sin\alpha+\sin\beta+\sin\gamma = s$.
- $xy+yz+zx=\sin\alpha\sin\beta+\cdots=\tfrac{ab+bc+ca}{4}$.
- $xyz=\sin\alpha\sin\beta\sin\gamma=\tfrac{abc}{8}=\tfrac{rs}{2}$ (since $abc=4
R\cdot\Delta=4rs$).

Expanding $N/4=(y-z)^2x+(z-x)^2y+(x-y)^2z$ gives
$$\tfrac{N}{4}=(x+y+z)(xy+yz+zx)-9xyz.$$

Using the identity $ab+bc+ca=s^2+4r+r^2$ (verified below), we obtain
$$N = s\bigl(s^2+r^2-14r\bigr). \tag{1}$$

*Verification of $ab+bc+ca=s^2+4r+r^2$:* From $\sum a^2=8(1+\cos\alpha\cos\beta\
cos\gamma)$ and $\cos\alpha\cos\beta\cos\gamma=\tfrac{s^2-(2+r)^2}{4}$ (with $R=
1$), we get $\sum a^2=2s^2-8r-2r^2$. Then $ab+bc+ca=\tfrac{(2s)^2-\sum a^2}{2}=s
^2+4r+r^2$. ✓

**The key inequality $N\le 8\,OI^2=8(1-2r)$.** By (1) this is
$$s(s^2+r^2-14r)\le 8-16r. \tag{2}$$

*Step 1 — monotonicity in $s$.* Since $N\ge0$ (a sum of nonneg terms) and $s>0$,
 (1) gives $s^2\ge 14r-r^2$. Hence
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\bigl[s(s^2+r^2-14r)\bigr]=3s^2+r^2-14r\ge 3(14r-r^
2)+r^2-14r=2r(14-r)>0$$
for $r\in(0,\tfrac12]$. So the left side of (2) is increasing in $s$.

*Step 2 — apply Gerretsen's upper bound.* $s^2\le 4+4r+3r^2$ (with $R=1$), so
$$s(s^2+r^2-14r)\le \sqrt{4+4r+3r^2}\,\bigl(4-10r+4r^2\bigr). \tag{3}$$

*Step 3 — a one-variable inequality.* We claim
$$\sqrt{4+4r+3r^2}\,(4-10r+4r^2)\le 8-16r,\qquad r\in[0,\tfrac12]. \tag{4}$$
Both sides are nonneg ($4-10r+4r^2=2(2r-1)(r-2)\ge0$ and $8-16r\ge0$ on $[0,\tfr
ac12]$). Squaring and comparing:
$$\text{LHS}^2-\text{RHS}^2=(4+4r+3r^2)(4-10r+4r^2)^2-64(1-2r)^2.$$
A direct expansion yields
$$\text{LHS}^2-\text{RHS}^2=48\,r^3\Bigl(r-\tfrac12\Bigr)^2\Bigl(r-\tfrac83\Bigr
).$$
For $r\in[0,\tfrac12]$: $r^3\ge0$, $(r-\tfrac12)^2\ge0$, and $r-\tfrac83<0$. Thu
s $\text{LHS}^2-\text{RHS}^2\le0$, proving (4). Combining (1), (3), (4):
$$\boxed{\,N\le 8\,OI^2\,},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \frac{N}{OI^2}\le 8. \tag{5}$
$

**The bound 8 is sharp.** Consider the isosceles family $\beta=\gamma=\tfrac{\pi
-\alpha}{2}$ with $\alpha\to0^+$. Then $a=2\sin\alpha\to0$, $b=c=2\cos(\alpha/2)
\to2$, and
$$N=2(a-b)^2\sin\beta=8\cos^3(\alpha/2)\bigl(2\sin(\alpha/2)-1\bigr)^2\to8,$$
while $r=2\sin(\alpha/2)(1-\sin(\alpha/2))\to0$, so $OI^2=1-2r\to1$. Therefore
$$\frac{N}{OI^2}\to 8\qquad(\alpha\to0^+).$$
(The same limit $8\cos^3\delta\le8$ is obtained along any degenerate path $\alph
a\to0,\ \beta\to\tfrac\pi2+\delta,\ \gamma\to\tfrac\pi2-\delta$, with equality i
n the bound only at $\delta=0$.)

**Conclusion.** The expression is bounded above by $8$, and this value is approa
