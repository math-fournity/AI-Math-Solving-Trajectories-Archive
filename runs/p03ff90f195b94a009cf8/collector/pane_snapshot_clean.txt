## Solution

**Answer: Yes.** Such $R, C, n$ exist; in fact one may take $n = 1$.

---

### Setup

Since $f$ is entire and real-valued on $\mathbb{R}$, the Schwarz reflection prin
ciple gives
$$f(\bar z) = \overline{f(z)} \quad \text{for all } z \in \mathbb{C}.$$

Fix $z_0 \in \mathbb{R}$ with $f'(z_0) \neq 0$. Write the Taylor expansion at $z
_0$:
$$f(z_0 + w) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} a_k w^k, \qquad a_k = \frac{f^{(k)}(z_0)}{k!}
.$$
Because $f$ is real on the real axis, each derivative $f^{(k)}(z_0)$ (computed a
long $\mathbb{R}$) is real, so **every $a_k \in \mathbb{R}$**. In particular $a_
0 = f(z_0) \in \mathbb{R}$ and $a_1 = f'(z_0) \neq 0$.

Set $w = u + iv$ with $u, v \in \mathbb{R}$. Since $z_0$ is real,
$$\operatorname{Im}(z) = \operatorname{Im}(z_0 + w) = v.$$

### Factoring out $v$ from $\operatorname{Im} f$

Using reflection ($\overline{z_0 + w} = z_0 + \bar w$ since $z_0 \in \mathbb{R}$
):
$$\operatorname{Im} f(z_0+w) = \frac{f(z_0+w) - \overline{f(z_0+w)}}{2i} = \frac
{f(z_0+w) - f(z_0+\bar w)}{2i} = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} a_k \,\operatorname{Im}(w^k
).$$

For each $k \ge 1$, $\operatorname{Im}(w^k)$ vanishes when $v = 0$ (since $w \in
 \mathbb{R} \Rightarrow w^k \in \mathbb{R}$), so $v$ divides the real polynomial
 $\operatorname{Im}(w^k)$. Concretely
$$\operatorname{Im}(w^k) = v \cdot P_k(u,v), \qquad P_k(u,v) := \sum_{\substack{
j \le k \\ j \text{ odd}}} \binom{k}{j} u^{k-j} v^{j-1} \sin\!\left(\tfrac{j\pi}
{2}\right) \in \mathbb{R}[u,v],$$
with $P_1 \equiv 1$.

Define
$$H(w) := \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} a_k P_k(u,v), \qquad \text{so that} \quad \operato
rname{Im} f(z_0+w) = v \cdot H(w).$$

### $H$ is well-defined and continuous near $0$

The coefficients of $P_k$ sum in absolute value to $\sum_{j \text{ odd}} \binom{
k}{j} = 2^{k-1}$, hence
$$|P_k(u,v)| \le 2^{k-1} |w|^{k-1}.$$
Since $f$ is entire, $\sum_k |a_k| \rho^k$ converges for every $\rho > 0$. Thus
for any $r > 0$,
$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} |a_k|\,|P_k(u,v)| \le \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} |a_k|\, 2^{k-1}
r^{k-1} = \frac{1}{2r}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} |a_k|(2r)^k < \infty \quad (|w| \le r)
,$$
so the series defining $H$ converges absolutely and uniformly on compact sets. T
herefore $H$ is continuous (indeed real-analytic), and
$$H(0) = a_1 P_1(0,0) = a_1 = f'(z_0) \neq 0.$$

### The estimate

By continuity, there exists $R > 0$ such that for all $|w| \le R$,
$$|H(w)| \ge \tfrac{1}{2}|f'(z_0)|.$$
For $z \in B_{\mathbb{C}}(z_0, R)$, writing $w = z - z_0$:
$$|\operatorname{Im} f(z)| = |v|\,|H(w)| \ge \frac{|f'(z_0)|}{2}\,|v| = \frac{|f
'(z_0)|}{2}\,|\operatorname{Im}(z)| = \frac{|f'(z_0)|}{2}\,|\operatorname{Im}(z^
1)|.$$

### Conclusion

Taking
$$n = 1, \qquad C = \frac{|f'(z_0)|}{2} > 0, \qquad R > 0 \text{ as above},$$
we have
$$C\,|\operatorname{Im}(z^n)| \le |\operatorname{Im} f(z)| \quad \text{for all }
 z \in B_{\mathbb{C}}(z_0, R).$$

So **yes**, such $R$, $C$, and $n$ exist. $\blacksquare$
