**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

Let $T$ be a transcendence basis of $\mathbb{R}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, so every fin
ite subset of $T$ is algebraically independent over $\mathbb{Q}$. Let $F = \{A_n
 \subset T : n \in \mathbb{N}\}$ be pairwise disjoint, and $C = \{pA + q : A \in
 F,\; p,q \in \mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}\}$, where $pA+q = \{pa+q : a \in A\}$.

### Part 1: Each set in $C$ is algebraically independent

Fix $A \in F$ and $p, q \in \mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}$. Since $A \subset T$, the
set $A$ is algebraically independent. Suppose $f \in \mathbb{Q}[X_a : a \in A]$
is a polynomial with $f\big((pa+q)_{a\in A}\big) = 0$. Define
$$h(Y) := f\big((p\,Y_a + q)_{a \in A}\big) \in \mathbb{Q}[Y_a : a \in A].$$
Then $h\big((a)_{a\in A}\big) = f\big((pa+q)_{a\in A}\big) = 0$. By algebraic in
dependence of $A$, this forces $h = 0$.

The substitution $X_a \mapsto p\,Y_a + q$ defines an injective $\mathbb{Q}$-alge
bra homomorphism $\mathbb{Q}[X_a] \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Q}[Y_a]$; since $p \ne
q 0$, it is in fact an isomorphism with inverse $Y_a \mapsto (X_a - q)/p$. There
fore $h = 0$ implies $f = 0$.

We have shown: any polynomial vanishing on $pA+q$ must be the zero polynomial. H
ence $pA+q$ is algebraically independent over $\mathbb{Q}$. $\checkmark$

### Part 2: The sets in $C$ are pairwise disjoint

Take two distinct elements of $C$, say $pA+q$ and $p'A'+q'$, with $A, A' \in F$
and $p,q,p',q' \in \mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}$, and $(A,p,q) \neq (A',p',q')$. Sup
pose for contradiction that there exist $a \in A$, $a' \in A'$ with
$$pa + q = p'a' + q', \quad\text{i.e.,}\quad p\,a - p'\,a' - (q'-q) = 0. \tag{$\
star$}$$

**Case 1: $A \neq A'$.** Since $F$ is pairwise disjoint, $a \neq a'$. Both $a, a
'$ are distinct elements of $T$, so $\{a, a'\}$ is algebraically independent. Bu
t $(\star)$ is a nonzero polynomial (of degree $1$) in $a, a'$ with rational coe
fficients — nonzero because the coefficient of $a$ is $p \neq 0$. This contradic
ts algebraic independence.

**Case 2: $A = A'$ (so $(p,q) \neq (p',q')$).**

*Subcase 2a: $a = a'$.* Then $(\star)$ gives $(p - p')\,a = q' - q$. If $p \neq
p'$, then $a = (q'-q)/(p-p') \in \mathbb{Q}$, contradicting that $a \in T$ is tr
anscendental. If $p = p'$, then $q = q'$, contradicting $(p,q) \neq (p',q')$.

*Subcase 2b: $a \neq a'$.* Then $a, a'$ are distinct elements of $A \subset T$,
hence algebraically independent. But $(\star)$ is a nonzero degree-$1$ polynomia
l relation (coefficient of $a$ is $p \neq 0$), contradiction.

In every case we reach a contradiction, so $pA+q \cap p'A'+q' = \emptyset$. $\ch
eckmark$

### Conclusion

Every set in $C$ is algebraically independent over $\mathbb{Q}$, and the sets in
 $C$ are pairwise disjoint. The key mechanism is that any putative overlap $pa +
 q = p'a' + q'$ yields a **nonzero linear polynomial relation** among elements o
f $T$, which is impossible by algebraic independence (algebraic independence for
bids polynomial relations of *any* degree, in particular degree $1$). Likewise,
algebraic independence of $A$ is preserved under the invertible affine substitut
ion $x \mapsto px + q$ (with $p \neq 0$).
