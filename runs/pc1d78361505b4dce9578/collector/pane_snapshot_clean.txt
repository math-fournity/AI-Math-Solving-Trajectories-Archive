$$\frac{\sin x + \cos x}{\cos y} = -\frac{5}{\sqrt{2}}\cot\!\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\

Let $s = \frac{x+y}{2}$.

## Key Step: Multiply the Equations

Multiplying (1) and (2):

$$\frac{(\cos x - \sin x)(\sin x + \cos x)}{\sin y\,\cos y} = \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{5

**Numerator:** $(\cos x - \sin x)(\cos x + \sin x) = \cos^2 x - \sin^2 x = \cos
2x$.

**Denominator:** $\sin y\cos y = \frac{1}{2}\sin 2y$.

**RHS:** $-2 \cdot 1 = -2$.

So:
$$\frac{\cos 2x}{\frac{1}{2}\sin 2y} = -2 \implies \frac{\cos 2x}{\sin 2y} = -1
\implies \cos 2x = -\sin 2y \tag{3}$$
