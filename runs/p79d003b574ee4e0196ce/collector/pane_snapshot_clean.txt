## Solution

**Step 1: Verify the identity for the left-hand side.**

The hint states that the left side equals $a^2 \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial}{\par
tial r}\!\left(r^2 \frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\right)$. Indeed, by the product
 rule:

$$\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left(r^2 \frac{\partial u}{\partial
 r}\right) = \frac{1}{r^2}\!\left(2r\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial r} + r^2\frac{\
partial^2 u}{\partial r^2}\right) = \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial r^2} + \frac{2}
{r}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r},$$

which is exactly the spatial operator in the PDE. So the PDE reads:

$$a^2\,\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left(r^2\frac{\partial u}{\par
tial r}\right) = \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2}. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2: Substitution $v(r,t) = r\,u(r,t)$.**

Set $u = v/r$. Then:

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial r} = \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial v}{\partial r} - \fr
ac{v}{r^2}, \qquad r^2\frac{\partial u}{\partial r} = r\,\frac{\partial v}{\part
ial r} - v.$$

Differentiating once more:

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left(r^2\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\right) = \
frac{\partial v}{\partial r} + r\,\frac{\partial^2 v}{\partial r^2} - \frac{\par
tial v}{\partial r} = r\,\frac{\partial^2 v}{\partial r^2}.$$

Hence

$$\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left(r^2\frac{\partial u}{\partial
r}\right) = \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial^2 v}{\partial r^2}.$$

Also $\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial^2 v}{\partia
l t^2}$. Substituting both into (1) and multiplying through by $r$ (valid for $r
 > 0$):

$$\boxed{\;a^2\,\frac{\partial^2 v}{\partial r^2} = \frac{\partial^2 v}{\partial
 t^2}\;} \tag{2}$$

This is the **one-dimensional wave equation** on $0 < r < c$.

**Step 3: Transform the boundary and initial conditions.**

- **Boundary at $r = c$:** $u(c,t) = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; v(c,t) = c\cdot u(c,t
) = 0$.
- **Boundary at $r = 0$:** Physical regularity requires $u$ bounded at $r=0$, so
 $v(0,t) = \lim_{r\to 0} r\,u(r,t) = 0$.
- **Initial displacement:** $v(r,0) = r\,f(r) \equiv F(r)$.
- **Initial velocity:** $\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}\big|_{t=0} = r\,g(r) \equ
iv G(r)$.

Thus $v$ satisfies the standard wave equation (2) with **homogeneous Dirichlet**
 conditions $v(0,t)=v(c,t)=0$.

**Step 4: Solve by separation of variables.**

Seeking $v(r,t)=R(r)T(t)$, the spatial eigenvalue problem $R''+\lambda R=0$, $R(
0)=R(c)=0$ yields eigenvalues $\lambda_n = \left(\frac{n\pi}{c}\right)^2$ and ei
genfunctions $\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi r}{c}\right)$, $n=1,2,3,\dots$

The temporal equation gives $T_n'' + a^2\lambda_n T_n = 0$, with solutions $\cos
\!\left(\frac{an\pi t}{c}\right)$ and $\sin\!\left(\frac{an\pi t}{c}\right)$. Su
perposing:

$$v(r,t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[A_n\cos\!\left(\frac{an\pi t}{c}\right) + B_
n\sin\!\left(\frac{an\pi t}{c}\right)\right]\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi r}{c}\right).
 \tag{3}$$

**Step 5: Determine the Fourier coefficients.**

From $v(r,0) = F(r) = r\,f(r)$:

$$A_n = \frac{2}{c}\int_0^c r\,f(r)\,\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi r}{c}\right)dr.$$

From $\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}\big|_{t=0} = G(r) = r\,g(r)$, noting $\frac{
\partial v}{\partial t}\big|_{t=0} = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{an\pi}{c}B_n\sin\!\
left(\frac{n\pi r}{c}\right)$:

$$B_n = \frac{2}{an\pi}\int_0^c r\,g(r)\,\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi r}{c}\right)dr.$
$

**Step 6: Recover $u(r,t) = v(r,t)/r$.**

$$\boxed{\;u(r,t) = \frac{1}{r}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[A_n\cos\!\left(\frac{an\
pi t}{c}\right) + B_n\sin\!\left(\frac{an\pi t}{c}\right)\right]\sin\!\left(\fra
c{n\pi r}{c}\right)\;}$$

with

$$A_n = \frac{2}{c}\int_0^c r\,f(r)\,\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi r}{c}\right)dr, \qqu
ad B_n = \frac{2}{an\pi}\int_0^c r\,g(r)\,\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi r}{c}\right)dr.
$$

**Verification.** The substitution $v=ru$ reduces the spherical wave equation to
 the 1D wave equation with fixed-end boundary conditions at $r=0$ and $r=c$. The
 series (3) is the classical Fourier sine-series solution of that standard probl
em, so it satisfies (2), the boundary conditions $v(0,t)=v(c,t)=0$, and the init
ial conditions by construction of $A_n, B_n$. Dividing by $r$ recovers a solutio
n of the original PDE (1) that is bounded at $r=0$ (since $v(0,t)=0$ ensures the
 quotient has a finite limit), satisfies $u(c,t)=0$, and matches $u(r,0)=f(r)$,
$u_t(r,0)=g(r)$. By uniqueness of the wave-equation solution with these data, th
is is the solution.
