## Proof

**Construction.** Fix a base point $(x_0, y_0)$. Define

$$
f(x, y) \;=\; \int_{x_0}^{x} M(t,\, y)\, dt \;+\; \int_{y_0}^{y} N(x_0,\, s)\, d
s.
$$

We claim this $f$ satisfies $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = M$ and $\frac{\part
ial f}{\partial y} = N$.

---

**Step 1: $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = M$.**

The second integral $\int_{y_0}^{y} N(x_0, s)\, ds$ is independent of $x$, so di
fferentiating with respect to $x$ only affects the first term. By the Fundamenta
l Theorem of Calculus,

$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x, y) \;=\; M(x, y).
$$

---

**Step 2: $\frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = N$.**

Differentiate under the integral sign in the first term (justified since $M$ is
$C^1$, which follows from the hypothesis that $\partial M/\partial y$ exists and
 equals $\partial N/\partial x$):

$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(x, y) \;=\; \int_{x_0}^{x} \frac{\partial M}{\part
ial y}(t, y)\, dt \;+\; N(x_0, y).
$$

By the hypothesis $\frac{\partial M}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial N}{\partial x}
$, we substitute:

$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(x, y) \;=\; \int_{x_0}^{x} \frac{\partial N}{\part
ial x}(t, y)\, dt \;+\; N(x_0, y).
$$

Applying the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus to the integrand $\frac{\partial N}
{\partial x}$ with respect to $t$:

$$
\int_{x_0}^{x} \frac{\partial N}{\partial x}(t, y)\, dt \;=\; N(x, y) - N(x_0, y
).
$$

Therefore

$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(x, y) \;=\; \bigl[N(x, y) - N(x_0, y)\bigr] + N(x_
0, y) \;=\; N(x, y).
$$

---

**Conclusion.** The function

$$
f(x, y) = \int_{x_0}^{x} M(t, y)\, dt + \int_{y_0}^{y} N(x_0, s)\, ds
$$

is well-defined and satisfies both $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = M$ and $\fra
c{\partial f}{\partial y} = N$ for all $(x, y)$. This establishes the existence
of such an $f$. $\blacksquare$
