## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. Every $\mathbb{C}$-linear map $f: M_n(\mathbb{C}) \to M_n(\mathb
b{C})$ can be written as $f(X) = \sum_{j=1}^d A_j X B_j$ for some $A_j, B_j \in
M_n(\mathbb{C})$.

**Proof.**

Identify $M_n(\mathbb{C})$ with $\mathbb{C}^{n^2}$ via the column-stacking vecto
rization map $\operatorname{vec}: M_n(\mathbb{C}) \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{C}^
{n^2}$, which stacks the columns of a matrix into a single column vector. This i
s a $\mathbb{C}$-linear isomorphism, so it induces an isomorphism
$$\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(M_n(\mathbb{C})) \cong \operatorname{End}_{\ma
thbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}^{n^2}) = M_{n^2}(\mathbb{C}).$$

The key identity (verified by direct computation on matrix units) is:
$$\operatorname{vec}(AXB) = (B^T \otimes A)\,\operatorname{vec}(X),$$
where $\otimes$ denotes the Kronecker product and $B^T$ is the transpose of $B$.

Therefore, under the isomorphism above, the map $X \mapsto AXB$ corresponds to t
he matrix $B^T \otimes A \in M_{n^2}(\mathbb{C})$. Consequently, a map of the fo
rm $X \mapsto \sum_{j=1}^d A_j X B_j$ corresponds to the matrix $\sum_{j=1}^d B_
j^T \otimes A_j$.

It remains to show that the set $\{B^T \otimes A : A, B \in M_n(\mathbb{C})\}$ s
pans all of $M_{n^2}(\mathbb{C})$.

Let $\{E_{ij}\}_{1 \le i,j \le n}$ be the standard basis of $M_n(\mathbb{C})$ (t
he matrix with $1$ in position $(i,j)$ and $0$ elsewhere). Then $\{E_{ij}\}$ is
a basis of $M_n(\mathbb{C})$, and the Kronecker products
$$\{E_{kl} \otimes E_{ij} : 1 \le i,j,k,l \le n\}$$
form the standard basis of $M_{n^2}(\mathbb{C}) = M_n(\mathbb{C}) \otimes M_n(\m
athbb{C})$ (this is the defining property of the Kronecker product: tensor produ
cts of bases give bases of tensor products).

Now observe that $E_{kl} = (E_{lk})^T$, so
$$E_{kl} \otimes E_{ij} = (E_{lk})^T \otimes E_{ij}.$$
Setting $B = E_{lk}$ and $A = E_{ij}$, this equals $B^T \otimes A$, which is exa
ctly the matrix representing $X \mapsto AXB$ with $A = E_{ij}$, $B = E_{lk}$.

Since every basis element $E_{kl} \otimes E_{ij}$ of $M_{n^2}(\mathbb{C})$ arise
s as $B^T \otimes A$ for suitable $A, B \in M_n(\mathbb{C})$, the set $\{B^T \ot
imes A\}$ spans all of $M_{n^2}(\mathbb{C})$.

Hence every element of $M_{n^2}(\mathbb{C})$ — and therefore every $\mathbb{C}$-
linear endomorphism of $M_n(\mathbb{C})$ — is a finite $\mathbb{C}$-linear combi
nation $\sum_{j=1}^d B_j^T \otimes A_j$. Translating back via the vectorization
isomorphism, every $\mathbb{C}$-linear map $f: M_n(\mathbb{C}) \to M_n(\mathbb{C
})$ admits a representation
$$f(X) = \sum_{j=1}^d A_j X B_j \quad \text{for all } X \in M_n(\mathbb{C}).$$

Explicitly, one may take $d = n^4$ with $\{A_j\} = \{E_{ij}\}$ and $\{B_j\}$ run
ning over $\{E_{lk}\}$ (paired appropriately), though fewer terms suffice in gen
eral. $\blacksquare$
