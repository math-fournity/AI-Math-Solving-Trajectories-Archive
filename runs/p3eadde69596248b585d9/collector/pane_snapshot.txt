## Solution

We evaluate
$$I(k) = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} e^{ik\tan\phi}\,d\phi, \qquad k\in\mathbb{R}.$$

**Step 1: Substitution.** Set $t = \tan\phi$, so $d\phi = \dfrac{dt}{1+t^2}$. As
 $\phi$ ranges over $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$, $t$ ranges over $(-\infty,\infty)$. The in
tegrand $e^{ik\tan\phi}/(1+\tan^2\phi)^{1/2}$... more directly, since $\frac{d\p
hi}{dt}=\frac{1}{1+t^2}$ is integrable and $e^{ikt}$ is bounded, the substitutio
n is justified (the improper integral converges absolutely, since $|e^{ikt}|=1$
and $\int \frac{dt}{1+t^2}<\infty$). Thus

$$I(k) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{ikt}}{1+t^2}\,dt.$$

**Step 2: Contour integration for $k>0$.** Consider $f(z)=\dfrac{e^{ikz}}{1+z^2}
$ and close the contour with a large semicircle in the **upper** half-plane. On
the arc $z=Re^{i\theta}$, $\theta\in[0,\pi]$:
$$|e^{ikz}| = e^{-kR\sin\theta}\le 1 \quad(\text{since }k>0,\ \sin\theta\ge 0),$
$
so the arc contribution vanishes as $R\to\infty$ by Jordan's lemma (the factor $
1/(1+z^2)$ gives $O(1/R^2)$, and even without the oscillation the arc is $O(1/R)
\to 0$). The only pole inside is at $z=i$ (simple pole). The residue is
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=i} f(z) = \left.\frac{e^{ikz}}{z+i}\right|_{z=i} = \frac
{e^{-k}}{2i}.$$
Hence
$$I(k) = 2\pi i\cdot \frac{e^{-k}}{2i} = \pi e^{-k}, \qquad k>0.$$

**Step 3: Contour integration for $k<0$.** Now close in the **lower** half-plane
 (so that $e^{ikz}=e^{i(-|k|)z}$ decays: with $z=Re^{i\theta}$, $\theta\in[\pi,2
\pi]$, $\sin\theta\le 0$, giving $e^{|k|R\sin\theta}\le 1$ and decay via Jordan)
. The contour is traversed **clockwise**, introducing a minus sign. The pole ins
ide is at $z=-i$:
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=-i} f(z) = \left.\frac{e^{ikz}}{z-i}\right|_{z=-i} = \fr
ac{e^{k}}{-2i} = \frac{e^{-|k|}}{-2i}.$$
Therefore
$$I(k) = -2\pi i\cdot \frac{e^{k}}{-2i} = \pi e^{k} = \pi e^{-|k|}, \qquad k<0.$
$

**Step 4: $k=0$.** Directly,
$$I(0)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{1+t^2}=\bigl[\arctan t\bigr]_{-\infty}^{
\infty}=\pi=\pi e^{-|0|}.$$

**Step 5: Unification.** Combining all three cases,

$$\boxed{\,I(k)=\pi\,e^{-|k|}\,},\qquad k\in\mathbb{R}.$$

*Consistency checks.* The result is even in $k$ (as it must be, since $I(-k)=\ov
erline{I(k)}$ and the answer is real and even). It is continuous at $k=0$ with v
alue $\pi$, and decays exponentially as $|k|\to\infty$, consistent with $1/(1+t^
2)$ being smooth and integrable (Riemann–Lebesgue plus the known Fourier pair $\
mathcal{F}\{1/(1+t^2)\}=\pi e^{-|\cdot|}$).
