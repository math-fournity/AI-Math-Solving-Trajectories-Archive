## Proof

**Step 1: Find a closed form for $f(n)$.**

The generating function for $f(n)$ is
$$F(x)=\sum_{n\ge 0}f(n)x^n=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(1+x^{2^k}+x^{2\cdot 2^k}+x
^{3\cdot 2^k}\right)=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1-x^{4\cdot 2^k}}{1-x^{2^k}}=\pro
d_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1-x^{2^{k+2}}}{1-x^{2^k}}.$$

This product **telescopes**. Writing the partial product up to $k=N$:
$$\prod_{k=0}^{N}\frac{1-x^{2^{k+2}}}{1-x^{2^k}}=\frac{(1-x^{2^{N+1}})(1-x^{2^{N
+2}})}{(1-x)(1-x^2)},$$
since each numerator factor $1-x^{2^{k+2}}$ cancels the denominator factor $1-x^
{2^{(k+2)}}$ at index $k+2$, leaving only the two largest numerator factors and
the two smallest denominator factors.

For $|x|<1$, as $N\to\infty$ the terms $x^{2^{N+1}},x^{2^{N+2}}\to 0$, giving
$$F(x)=\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-x^2)}.$$

**Step 2: Extract $f(n)$.**

Since $\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-x^2)}=\frac{1}{1-x}\cdot\frac{1}{1-x^2}=\left(\sum_{a\ge
 0}x^a\right)\left(\sum_{b\ge 0}x^{2b}\right)$, the coefficient of $x^n$ counts
pairs $(a,b)$ with $a+2b=n$, i.e. $b\in\{0,1,\dots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\}$. Hence
$$f(n)=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1.$$

*Verification.* $f(7)=\lfloor 7/2\rfloor+1=4$ ✓ (matches the four representation
s given). Also $f(4)=3$: $\{4,\;2+2,\;2+1+1\}$ ✓.

**Step 3: Construct the polynomial.**

Take $p(x)=\dfrac{x}{2}+1$. For every non-negative integer $n$,
$$\lfloor p(n)\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}+1\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{n
}{2}\right\rfloor+1=f(n),$$
where the second equality holds because $1$ is an integer and $\lfloor u+m\rfloo
r=\lfloor u\rfloor+m$ for integer $m$.

Thus $p(x)=\dfrac{x}{2}+1$ is a real polynomial satisfying $f(n)=\lfloor p(n)\rf
loor$ for all $n\ge 0$.
