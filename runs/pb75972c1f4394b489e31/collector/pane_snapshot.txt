## Solution

We evaluate $\iint_S (x\,dy\,dz + y\,dx\,dz + z\,dx\,dy)$, where $S$ is the uppe
r half of the ellipsoid $\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}+\frac{z^2}{c^2}=1$ (i.e
., $z \geq 0$), with outward orientation.

**Setting up the vector field.** Let $\mathbf{F} = (x,\, y,\, z)$. The integrand
 $x\,dy\,dz + y\,dx\,dz + z\,dx\,dy$ is exactly the flux $\mathbf{F}\cdot d\math
bf{S}$, so the integral is $\iint_S \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$.

**Closing the surface.** The surface $S$ is not closed (it is the upper half-ell
ipsoid). Let $D$ be the base disk in the $xy$-plane: $z = 0$, $\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\
frac{y^2}{b^2}\leq 1$. Then $S \cup D$ is a closed surface bounding the upper ha
lf $V$ of the ellipsoid, with outward normal.

**Applying the divergence theorem.** Since $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = 1+1+1 = 3$
,

$$\iint_{S\cup D} \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iiint_V 3\, dV = 3\,\mathrm{Vol
}(V).$$

The full ellipsoid has volume $\frac{4}{3}\pi abc$, so the upper half has

$$\mathrm{Vol}(V) = \frac{2}{3}\pi abc,$$

giving

$$\iint_{S\cup D} \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = 3\cdot\frac{2}{3}\pi abc = 2\pi
abc.$$

**Flux through the base disk $D$.** On $D$ we have $z=0$, and the outward normal
 points in the $-z$ direction, so $d\mathbf{S} = (0,0,-1)\,dx\,dy$. Thus

$$\iint_D \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iint_D (x,\,y,\,0)\cdot(0,0,-1)\,dx\,dy
 = \iint_D 0\,dx\,dy = 0.$$

**Combining.** Since $\iint_{S\cup D} = \iint_S + \iint_D$,

$$\iint_S \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = 2\pi abc - 0 = \boxed{2\pi abc}.$$
