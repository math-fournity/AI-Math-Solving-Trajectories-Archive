## Factorization of the Dedekind Zeta Function of ℤ[i]

**Claim.** For the ring of Gaussian integers 𝒪 = ℤ[i] (the ring of integers of K
 = ℚ(i)),

$$\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}[i]}(s) \;=\; \zeta(s)\,L\!\left(s,\chi_{-4}\right),$$

where ζ(s) is the Riemann zeta function and L(s, χ_{−4}) is the Dirichlet L-func
tion attached to the unique nontrivial Dirichlet character modulo 4, i.e. the Kr
onecker symbol χ_{−4}(n) = (−4/n).

---

### Proof

The Dedekind zeta function of 𝒪 = ℤ[i] is

$$\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}[i]}(s) = \sum_{\mathfrak{a}\neq 0} \mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{a})^
{-s} = \prod_{\mathfrak{p}} \left(1 - \mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{p})^{-s}\right)^{-1},
$$

the product ranging over nonzero prime ideals of ℤ[i]. Since ℤ[i] is a PID (clas
s number 1 of ℚ(i)), prime ideals correspond to Gaussian primes up to associates
. We classify them by the behavior of the underlying rational prime p in ℤ[i]:

**1. p = 2 (ramified).** Since 2 = −i(1+i)², we have (2) = (1+i)². There is a si
ngle prime ideal above 2, namely (1+i), with norm N(1+i) = 2. The local Euler fa
ctor is

$$\left(1 - 2^{-s}\right)^{-1}.$$

**2. p ≡ 1 (mod 4) (split).** By Fermat's theorem on sums of two squares, such p
 = a² + b² = π\bar{π} for a Gaussian prime π with N(π) = p. Hence (p) = (π)(\bar
{π}) splits into two distinct prime ideals, each of norm p. The local factor is

$$\left(1 - p^{-s}\right)^{-2}.$$

**3. p ≡ 3 (mod 4) (inert).** Such p remains prime in ℤ[i] (it is not a sum of t
wo squares), so (p) is a prime ideal of norm p². The local factor is

$$\left(1 - p^{-2s}\right)^{-1}.$$

Combining,

$$\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}[i]}(s) = \left(1-2^{-s}\right)^{-1}\!\prod_{p\equiv 1\,(4)}\
!\left(1-p^{-s}\right)^{-2}\!\prod_{p\equiv 3\,(4)}\!\left(1-p^{-2s}\right)^{-1}
.$$

Now compute the product ζ(s)·L(s, χ_{−4}). The character χ_{−4} is defined by

$$\chi_{-4}(n) = \begin{cases} 0 & 2\mid n,\\ 1 & n\equiv 1\pmod 4,\\ -1 & n\equ
iv 3\pmod 4.\end{cases}$$

Its L-function has Euler product

$$L(s,\chi_{-4}) = \prod_p \left(1 - \chi_{-4}(p)\,p^{-s}\right)^{-1}.$$

Multiplying by ζ(s) = ∏_p (1 − p^{−s})^{−1} gives, prime by prime:

- **p = 2:** χ_{−4}(2) = 0, so the L-factor is 1. Product with ζ gives (1 − 2^{−
s})^{−1}. ✓ matches the ramified factor.
- **p ≡ 1 (mod 4):** χ_{−4}(p) = 1. Product: (1 − p^{−s})^{−1}·(1 − p^{−s})^{−1}
 = (1 − p^{−s})^{−2}. ✓ matches the split factor.
- **p ≡ 3 (mod 4):** χ_{−4}(p) = −1. Product: (1 − p^{−s})^{−1}(1 + p^{−s})^{−1}
 = (1 − p^{−2s})^{−1}. ✓ matches the inert factor.

Every local factor agrees, so the two Euler products are identical:

$$\boxed{\;\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}[i]}(s) \;=\; \zeta(s)\,L\!\left(s,\chi_{-4}\right).
\;}$$

This is the instance for discriminant D = −4 of the general quadratic-field fact
orization ζ_{ℚ(√d)}(s) = ζ(s)·L(s, χ_D), where χ_D is the Kronecker symbol of th
e field discriminant D.
