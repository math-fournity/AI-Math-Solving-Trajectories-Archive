# Problem

In the coastal town of Arithmos, there are 10 distinct lighthouse beacons numbered 1 through 10. A maritime engineer needs to select a collection $S$ of exactly four different beacons to be part of a new safety grid.

To test the grid's stability, the engineer must determine if the four selected beacons can be placed at the four corners of a square-shaped offshore platform. The placement is valid only if it satisfies the "interference rule": the numerical labels of any two beacons connected by a side of the square must have an absolute difference of at least 4. (Note: This rule does not apply to beacons positioned diagonally across from each other).

How many different subsets $S$ of four beacons can be chosen from the 10 available such that there exists at least one way to arrange them on the corners of the square to satisfy the interference rule?
