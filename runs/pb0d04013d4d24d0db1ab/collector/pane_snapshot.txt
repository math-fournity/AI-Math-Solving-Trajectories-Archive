### 1. The Hall basis is a basis of $L(x,y)$

Fix a total order on generators, say $x < y$. A **Hall set** $\mathcal{H}$ is a
totally ordered subset of the free magma $\mathcal{M}(x,y)$ (the set of all full
y bracketed words in $x,y$) defined recursively:

- The generators $x, y$ belong to $\mathcal{H}$, with $x < y$.
- A bracketed word $[h_1, h_2]$ with $h_1, h_2 \in \mathcal{H}$ belongs to $\mat
hcal{H}$ if and only if:
  - $h_1 < h_2$, and
  - $h_1$ is either a generator, or $h_1 = [h_{11}, h_{12}]$ with $h_{12} \leq h
_2$.

Each Hall word $h \in \mathcal{H}$ maps to a Lie element $\phi(h) \in L(x,y)$ (r
eplacing formal brackets by Lie brackets). The set $\{h_\alpha\} := \{\phi(h) :
h \in \mathcal{H}\}$ is the **Hall basis**.

**Theorem (Hall–Witt).** *The Hall basis $\{h_\alpha\}$ is a basis of $L(x,y)$ a
s a $K$-vector space.*

*Sketch of proof.* There are two things to verify:

**(a) Linear independence.** Via the universal enveloping algebra: $U(L(x,y)) \c
ong K\langle x, y \rangle$, the free associative algebra (this is the PBW theore
m, which for a free Lie algebra gives an isomorphism with the free associative a

Lyndon words** (or more generally Hall words in the associative sense), which fo

,y) \hookrightarrow K\langle x,y\rangle$ is injective and the images of distinct

n/Hall words), the Hall basis elements are linearly independent in $L(x,y)$.

**(b) Spanning (dimension count).** By **Witt's formula**, the dimension of the
homogeneous component $L_n(x,y)$ of degree $n$ is
$$\dim L_n(x,y) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{d \mid n} \mu(d)\, 2^{n/d},$$
where $\mu$ is the Möbius function. The number of Hall words of degree $n$ in tw

ependent and their count equals $\dim L_n$, they span $L_n$. Summing over all de
grees, $\{h_\alpha\}$ spans $L(x,y)$. $\square$

### 2. The algorithm: the collection process

Since $\{h_\alpha\}$ is a basis, every $\alpha \in L(x,y)$ *can* be written as $
\alpha = \sum_\alpha c_\alpha h_\alpha$. We now show this decomposition is **alg
orithmically computable**.

**Input:** An element $\alpha \in L(x,y)$, given as a $K$-linear combination of
Lie monomials (fully bracketed expressions in $x, y$). Any element of $L(x,y)$ a
dmits such a representation, since $L(x,y)$ is generated by $x, y$ under the Lie
 bracket.

**Algorithm (collection / straightening):** Process each Lie monomial $m$ in the
 expression of $\alpha$ independently, rewriting it as a $K$-linear combination
of Hall basis elements, using the following rewriting rules:

**Rule 1 (Anticommutativity).** If $m = [a, b]$ with $a > b$ (in the Hall orderi
ng), replace:
$$[a, b] \;\longrightarrow\; -[b, a].$$

**Rule 2 (Jacobi identity / straightening).** If $m = [a, [b, c]]$ is not a Hall
 element (i.e., the triple $(a, b, c)$ violates the Hall condition), apply the J
acobi identity:
$$[a, [b, c]] \;\longrightarrow\; [[a, b], c] + [b, [a, c]],$$
choosing the decomposition that moves the expression toward Hall form. (The spec
ific variant depends on which Hall condition is violated; the Jacobi identity is
 applied to "re-bracket" so that the outer bracket satisfies the Hall ordering c
ondition.)

**Rule 3 (Self-bracket elimination).** If $m = [a, a]$, replace by $0$.

**Termination.** Define a well-founded order on bracketed words of fixed degree
$n$: order by the Hall word ordering (a total order refining degree-lexicographi
c order). Each application of Rules 1–3 either:
- eliminates the term (Rule 3),
- produces terms that are strictly smaller in the Hall ordering (Rules 1 and 2,
because the rewriting moves toward the "standard" Hall bracketing),

while preserving the total degree. Since there are only finitely many bracketed
words of any given degree $n$, and the ordering is well-founded, the rewriting p
rocess **terminates** after finitely many steps.

**Correctness.** Upon termination, every Lie monomial has been rewritten as a $K
$-linear combination of elements that are Hall basis elements (they satisfy all
Hall conditions and cannot be further rewritten). The transformations preserve t
he Lie algebra element (they only use identities valid in every Lie algebra: ant
icommutativity and the Jacobi identity). Therefore the final expression $\alpha
= \sum_\alpha c_\alpha h_\alpha$ is a valid decomposition of the original elemen
t.

**Collecting coefficients.** After all monomials are processed, combine like ter

m_\alpha c_\alpha\, h_\alpha$ with uniquely determined coefficients $c_\alpha \i
