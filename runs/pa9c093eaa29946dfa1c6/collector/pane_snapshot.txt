## Proof

**Claim:** $\lim_{t \to \infty} X_t = 0$ almost surely.

**Step 1: $X_t$ is a martingale.**

Since $B_t \sim N(0, t)$, the moment generating function gives
$$\mathbb{E}[X_t] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{B_t - t/2}\right] = e^{-t/2}\,\mathbb{E
}\!\left[e^{B_t}\right] = e^{-t/2}\,e^{t/2} = 1.$$
More generally, for $s \le t$, using $B_t = B_s + (B_t - B_s)$ with $B_t - B_s \
sim N(0, t-s)$ independent of $\mathcal{F}_s$,
$$\mathbb{E}[X_t \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = X_s\, e^{-(t-s)/2}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{
B_t - B_s}\right] = X_s.$$
So $(X_t)$ is a non-negative martingale with $\mathbb{E}[X_t] = 1$.

**Step 2: Strong law of large numbers for Brownian motion.**

We use the standard result
$$\frac{B_t}{t} \xrightarrow{a.s.} 0 \quad \text{as } t \to \infty.$$

*Brief justification:* For integers $n$, $B_n = \sum_{k=1}^n (B_k - B_{k-1})$ is
 a sum of i.i.d. $N(0,1)$ random variables, so $B_n/n \to 0$ a.s. by the classic
al SLLN. For $t \in [n, n+1]$, write $B_t/t = B_n/t + (B_t - B_n)/t$. The first
term satisfies $|B_n|/t \le |B_n|/n \to 0$. For the second, $|B_t - B_n|/t \le \
sup_{0 \le u \le 1}|B_{n+u} - B_n|/(n)$. By the reflection principle and Borel–C
antelli (applied to the events $\{\sup_{0\le u\le 1}|B_{n+u}-B_n| > \varepsilon
n\}$, whose probabilities decay like $e^{-\varepsilon^2 n^2/2}$), this supremum
divided by $n$ tends to $0$ a.s. Hence $B_t/t \to 0$ a.s.

**Step 3: The limit.**

Write
$$B_t - \frac{t}{2} = t\!\left(\frac{B_t}{t} - \frac{1}{2}\right).$$
By Step 2, $B_t/t \to 0$ a.s., so $B_t/t - 1/2 \to -1/2$ a.s. Therefore
$$B_t - \frac{t}{2} \xrightarrow{a.s.} -\infty,$$
and consequently
$$X_t = \exp\!\left(B_t - \frac{t}{2}\right) \xrightarrow{a.s.} 0.$$

**Conclusion:** The limit $\lim_{t \to \infty} X_t$ exists almost surely and equ
als $0$.

**Remark.** This is the canonical example of a non-negative martingale with cons
tant expectation $\mathbb{E}[X_t] = 1$ that converges a.s. to $0$, illustrating
that the conditions for the martingale convergence theorem (which guarantees a.s
. convergence of a non-negative supermartingale) do not guarantee $L^1$-converge
nce: $\mathbb{E}[\lim X_t] = 0 \ne 1 = \lim \mathbb{E}[X_t]$. The family $(X_t)$
 is not uniformly integrable.
