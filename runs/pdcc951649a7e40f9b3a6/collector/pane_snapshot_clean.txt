## Proof

A **prime power** is a number of the form $p^k$ where $p$ is prime and $k \geq 1
$. Thus the prime powers less than $x$ are, grouped by exponent:

$$f(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor \log_2 x \rfloor} \pi\!\left(x^{1/k}\right),$$

where $\pi(y)$ denotes the number of primes up to $y$, and the sum is finite sin
ce $x^{1/k} < 2$ (so $\pi(x^{1/k})=0$) once $k > \log_2 x$.

By the **Prime Number Theorem**, $\pi(y) \sim \dfrac{y}{\ln y}$ as $y \to \infty
$.

**Term $k=1$:**
$$\frac{\pi(x)}{x} \sim \frac{1}{\ln x} \to 0 \quad \text{as } x \to \infty.$$

**Term $k \geq 2$:** For each fixed $k \geq 2$,
$$\frac{\pi(x^{1/k})}{x} \sim \frac{x^{1/k}}{x \cdot \ln(x^{1/k})} = \frac{1}{x^
{1-1/k} \cdot \tfrac{1}{k}\ln x} = \frac{k}{x^{1-1/k}\ln x} \to 0,$$
since $1 - 1/k > 0$ for $k \geq 2$.

**Bounding the tail.** We need to ensure the sum of all terms $k\geq 2$ also van
ishes. Using the crude bound $\pi(y) \leq y$:

$$\sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor \log_2 x\rfloor} \frac{\pi(x^{1/k})}{x} \leq \sum_{k=2}^{\
lfloor \log_2 x\rfloor} \frac{x^{1/k}}{x} = \sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor \log_2 x\rfloor}
 x^{1/k - 1}.$$

For $k \geq 2$, the exponent $1/k - 1 \leq -1/2$, so $x^{1/k-1} \leq x^{-1/2}$.
The number of terms is at most $\log_2 x$, giving

$$\sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor \log_2 x\rfloor} \frac{\pi(x^{1/k})}{x} \leq \frac{\log_2
x}{\sqrt{x}} \to 0 \quad \text{as } x \to \infty.$$

**Combining:**
$$\frac{f(x)}{x} = \frac{\pi(x)}{x} + \sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor \log_2 x\rfloor} \frac
{\pi(x^{1/k})}{x} \;\longrightarrow\; 0 + 0 = 0.$$

Therefore,

$$\boxed{\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{f(x)}{x} = 0.}$$
