ignificant). Swapping positions $i$ and $j$ ($i \neq j$) produces

$$N' = N + (d_j - d_i)(10^{k-i} - 10^{k-j}).$$

Since $N \equiv 0 \pmod{7}$, we need $N' \not\equiv 0 \pmod{7}$, i.e.,

$$(d_j - d_i)(10^{k-i} - 10^{k-j}) \not\equiv 0 \pmod{7}$$

for every pair $i \neq j$. A product is nonzero mod 7 iff both factors are nonze
ro mod 7, giving two conditions for every pair:

**(A)** $d_i \not\equiv d_j \pmod{7}$ — all digits are distinct mod 7.

**(B)** $10^{k-i} \not\equiv 10^{k-j} \pmod{7}$ — equivalently $i \not\equiv j \
pmod{6}$, since $\text{ord}_7(10) = 6$.

**Bounding $k$.** Condition (B) requires no two positions to share a residue mod

mod 6. By pigeonhole, if $k \geq 7$, two positions share a residue mod 6, violat
ing (B). (Even if those positions held identical digits, the swap yields $N' = N
 \equiv 0 \pmod{7}$, still a violation.) Thus **$k \leq 6$**.
