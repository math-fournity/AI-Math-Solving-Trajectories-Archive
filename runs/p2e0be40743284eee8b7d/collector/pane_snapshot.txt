## Proof

We find $c = \sqrt{2}$.

**Setup.** Let $X_n \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ be independent. The given tail estima
te is
$$P(X \geq \xi) \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\xi}\,e^{-\xi^2/2}, \quad \xi \to \in
fty.$$

We prove $P\!\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty} \frac{X_n}{\sqrt{\log n}} = \sqrt{2}\rig
ht) = 1$ via two Borel–Cantelli bounds.

---

**Upper bound: $\limsup \frac{X_n}{\sqrt{\log n}} \leq \sqrt{2}$ a.s.**

Fix $\varepsilon > 0$ and set $a_n = (\sqrt{2}+\varepsilon)\sqrt{\log n}$. Then
$$P(X_n \geq a_n) \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,a_n}\,e^{-a_n^2/2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt
{2\pi}(\sqrt{2}+\varepsilon)\sqrt{\log n}}\; n^{-(\sqrt{2}+\varepsilon)^2/2}.$$

Since $\frac{(\sqrt{2}+\varepsilon)^2}{2} = 1 + \sqrt{2}\varepsilon + \frac{\var
epsilon^2}{2} > 1$, there exists $\delta > 0$ with $\frac{(\sqrt{2}+\varepsilon)
^2}{2} = 1+\delta$. Thus for large $n$,
$$P(X_n \geq a_n) \leq \frac{C}{\sqrt{\log n}}\,n^{-(1+\delta)} \leq C'\, n^{-(1
+\delta/2)},$$
so $\sum_n P(X_n \geq a_n) < \infty$. By the **first Borel–Cantelli lemma**,
$$P(X_n \geq a_n \text{ i.o.}) = 0 \implies \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{X_n}{\sqrt
{\log n}} \leq \sqrt{2}+\varepsilon \text{ a.s.}$$
Letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ (countable sequence): $\limsup \frac{X_n}{\sq
rt{\log n}} \leq \sqrt{2}$ a.s.

---

**Lower bound: $\limsup \frac{X_n}{\sqrt{\log n}} \geq \sqrt{2}$ a.s.**

Fix $0 < \varepsilon < \sqrt{2}$ and set $b_n = (\sqrt{2}-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\log
 n}$. Then
$$P(X_n \geq b_n) \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}(\sqrt{2}-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\log n}}\
; n^{-(\sqrt{2}-\varepsilon)^2/2}.$$

Set $\alpha = \frac{(\sqrt{2}-\varepsilon)^2}{2} = 1 - \sqrt{2}\varepsilon + \fr
ac{\varepsilon^2}{2} < 1$ for small $\varepsilon$. For large $n$,
$$P(X_n \geq b_n) \geq \frac{C}{\sqrt{\log n}}\,n^{-\alpha}$$
for some constant $C > 0$. Since $\alpha < 1$, the series $\sum_n n^{-\alpha}$ d
iverges, and $\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log n}}$ is slowly varying (bounded below by, say,
 $\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log n}} \to 0$ but the product still diverges by comparison: f
or any $M$, $\sum_{n=3}^{N} \frac{n^{-\alpha}}{\sqrt{\log n}} \geq \frac{1}{\sqr
t{\log N}}\sum_{n=3}^{N} n^{-\alpha} \to \infty$ since $\sum n^{-\alpha} \sim \f
rac{N^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}$ grows faster than $\sqrt{\log N}$). Hence
$$\sum_n P(X_n \geq b_n) = \infty.$$

Since the $\{X_n\}$ are **independent**, the **second Borel–Cantelli lemma** giv
es
$$P(X_n \geq b_n \text{ i.o.}) = 1 \implies \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{X_n}{\sqrt

---

**Conclusion.** Combining both bounds,
$$P\!\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{X_n}{\sqrt{\log n}} = \sqrt{2}\right) = 1.$
$

Therefore $\boxed{c = \sqrt{2}}$.
