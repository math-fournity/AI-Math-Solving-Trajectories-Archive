## Proof

Let $X$ be an infinite-dimensional Banach space and $T\colon X\to X$ a bounded f
inite-rank operator. We show that $0$ cannot be an accumulation point of $\sigma
(T)$.

**Step 1: $0\in\sigma(T)$.** A finite-rank operator is compact. If $0\notin\sigm
a(T)$, then $T$ would be invertible with bounded inverse $T^{-1}$, and the ident
ity $I = T^{-1}\circ T$ would be compact (a bounded operator composed with a com
pact one is compact). But the identity on an infinite-dimensional Banach space i
s not compact, since the closed unit ball is not relatively compact. Contradicti
on. Hence $0\in\sigma(T)$.

**Step 2: $\sigma(T)\setminus\{0\}$ is finite.** Let $\lambda\neq 0$ lie in $\si
gma(T)$. By the Riesz–Schauder theory of compact operators, every nonzero spectr
al value is an eigenvalue, so there exists $x\neq 0$ with $Tx=\lambda x$. Then $
x=\lambda^{-1}Tx\in\operatorname{range}(T)$. Thus every eigenvector associated w
ith a nonzero eigenvalue lies in $\operatorname{range}(T)$, which is finite-dime
nsional. Eigenvectors corresponding to distinct eigenvalues are linearly indepen
dent, so the number of distinct nonzero eigenvalues is at most $\dim\operatornam
e{range}(T)<\infty$. Hence $\sigma(T)\setminus\{0\}$ is finite.

**Step 3: $0$ is isolated.** Combining the two steps,
$$\sigma(T)=\{0\}\cup\{\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n\}$$
is a finite subset of $\mathbb{C}$. Every finite subset of $\mathbb{C}$ is discr
ete: each point has a neighborhood containing no other point of the set. In part
icular, $0$ is an isolated point of $\sigma(T)$, hence not an accumulation point
.

**Conclusion.** The spectrum of a finite-rank operator on an infinite-dimensiona
l Banach space is always finite, so $0$ is necessarily an isolated point. Theref
ore $0$ **cannot** be an accumulation point of such a spectrum.
