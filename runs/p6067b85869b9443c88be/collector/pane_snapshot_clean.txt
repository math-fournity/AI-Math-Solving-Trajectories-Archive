$$y'(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}x^{-1/2} - \tfrac{1}{2}x^{1/2} = \frac{1-x}{2\sqrt{x}},$$
so
$$1+y'^2 = 1 + \frac{(1-x)^2}{4x} = \frac{4x+(1-x)^2}{4x} = \frac{(x+1)^2}{4x},$
$
hence $\sqrt{1+y'^2} = \frac{x+1}{2\sqrt{x}}$. Therefore
$$s = \int_0^3 \frac{x+1}{2\sqrt{x}}\,dx = \frac12\int_0^3 (x^{1/2}+x^{-1/2})\,d
x = \frac12\Big[\tfrac{2}{3}x^{3/2}+2x^{1/2}\Big]_0^3 = \frac12(2\sqrt3+2\sqrt3)

Expanding the product:
$$(x^{1/2}+x^{-1/2})\Big(x^{1/2}-\tfrac13 x^{3/2}+\lambda\Big) = 1+\tfrac23 x-\t

Integrating over $[0,3]$:
- $\int_0^3 1\,dx = 3$,
- $\int_0^3 \tfrac23 x\,dx = 3$,
- $\int_0^3 -\tfrac13 x^2\,dx = -3$,
- $\int_0^3 x^{1/2}\,dx = 2\sqrt3$,
- $\int_0^3 x^{-1/2}\,dx = 2\sqrt3$.

The constant part sums to $3+3-3 = 3$, and the $\lambda$-part sums to $\lambda(2
\sqrt3+2\sqrt3)=4\sqrt3\,\lambda$. Thus
$$S = \pi\big(3 + 4\sqrt3\,\lambda\big) = 3\pi + 4\pi\sqrt3\,\lambda.$$

**3. Centroid height $h$.** Since $\sqrt{x}(1-x/3)\ge 0$ on $[0,3]$ and $\lambda

$$\frac{S}{hs} = \frac{\pi(3+4\sqrt3\,\lambda)}{2\sqrt3\cdot h} = \frac{\pi(3+4\

- Numerator: $\pi\cdot(4\sqrt3\,\lambda)\cdot(3\lambda) = 12\sqrt3\,\pi\,\lambda

- Denominator: $2\sqrt3\cdot\frac32\lambda^2 = 3\sqrt3\,\lambda^2$.

Therefore
