## Proof

**Claim.** The determinant map $\det: M \to S^1$ is surjective, where $M = U(n)$
 is the group of $n \times n$ unitary matrices ($n > 1$) and $S^1 = \{z \in \mat
hbb{C} : |z| = 1\}$.

**Goal.** For every $z \in S^1$, exhibit a unitary matrix $U \in U(n)$ with $\de
t(U) = z$.

---

**Construction.** Let $z \in S^1$ be arbitrary, so $|z| = 1$. Define the diagona
l matrix

$$
U_z = \operatorname{diag}(z,\, 1,\, 1,\, \dots,\, 1) \in \mathbb{C}^{n \times n}
.
$$

**$U_z$ is unitary.** A matrix is unitary iff $U_z^* U_z = I$. Since $U_z$ is di
agonal,

$$
U_z^* U_z = \operatorname{diag}(\bar{z}z,\, \bar{1}\cdot 1,\, \dots,\, \bar{1}\c
dot 1) = \operatorname{diag}(|z|^2,\, 1,\, \dots,\, 1).
$$

Because $|z| = 1$, we have $|z|^2 = 1$, so $U_z^* U_z = \operatorname{diag}(1, 1
, \dots, 1) = I$. Hence $U_z \in U(n)$.

**The determinant is $z$.** The determinant of a diagonal matrix is the product
of its diagonal entries:

$$
\det(U_z) = z \cdot 1 \cdot 1 \cdots 1 = z.
$$

**Conclusion.** For every $z \in S^1$, we have produced $U_z \in U(n)$ with $\de
t(U_z) = z$. Therefore $\det$ is surjective. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** The hypothesis $n > 1$ is not actually needed for surjectivity — the
 $n = 1$ case works via $U_z = [z]$. The condition $n > 1$ is relevant for other
 properties of $\det$ (e.g., that the kernel $\ker(\det) = SU(n)$ is a nontrivia
l connected Lie group), but surjectivity itself holds for all $n \geq 1$.
