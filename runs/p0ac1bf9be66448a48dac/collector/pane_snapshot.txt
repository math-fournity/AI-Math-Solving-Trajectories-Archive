## Setting Up the Compatibility Condition

**Claim:** Two distinct lattice points $A=(x_1,y_1)$ and $B=(x_2,y_2)$ are $k$-c
ompatible if and only if $\gcd(x_2-x_1,\, y_2-y_1) \mid 2k$.

**Proof of Claim:** Let $\Delta x = x_2-x_1$, $\Delta y = y_2-y_1$, and $g = \gc
d(\Delta x, \Delta y)$. A lattice point $C=(x_3,y_3)$ gives triangle area
$$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2}\left|\Delta x\cdot(y_3-y_1) - \Delta y\cdot(x_3-x_1
)\right|.$$
Setting $u = x_3-x_1$, $v=y_3-y_1$, the expression $\Delta x \cdot v - \Delta y
\cdot u$ ranges over all integer linear combinations of $\Delta x$ and $\Delta y
$, which is exactly the set $g\mathbb{Z}$. So area $= k$ is achievable iff $2k \
in g\mathbb{Z}$, i.e., $g \mid 2k$. When $2k \neq 0$, the area is nonzero so $C$
 is off line $AB$, hence distinct from both. $\square$

## Reformulation

for every pair, $\gcd(\Delta x, \Delta y) \mid D$. We minimize $D$ over even pos
