Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum of the polygonal chain sum $
T = A_1A_2 + A_2A_3 + \cdots + A_{n-1}A_n$ for $n$ points on a circle of radius
$R$.

## Proof

**Setup.** Place $n$ points $A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_n$ on a circle of radius $R$ in
 counterclockwise order. Let $\alpha_i$ denote the central angle (arc) from $A_i
$ to $A_{i+1}$ for $i = 1, \ldots, n-1$, and $\alpha_n$ the arc from $A_n$ back
to $A_1$. Then:

$$\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 + \cdots + \alpha_n = 2\pi, \quad \alpha_i \geq 0.$$

Each chord length is $A_iA_{i+1} = 2R\sin(\alpha_i/2)$, so:

$$T = 2R\sum_{i=1}^{n-1} \sin\!\left(\frac{\alpha_i}{2}\right).$$

**Step 1: Eliminate $\alpha_n$.** Since $\alpha_n$ appears in the constraint but
 not in the objective, we set $\alpha_n = 0$ (i.e., $A_n = A_1$) to maximize the
 budget for the remaining arcs. The constraint becomes:

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n-1} \alpha_i = 2\pi, \quad \alpha_i \geq 0.$$

**Step 2: Apply Jensen's inequality.** The function $h(x) = \sin(x/2)$ is **stri
ctly concave** on $[0, 2\pi]$, since $h''(x) = -\frac{1}{4}\sin(x/2) \leq 0$ (wi
th equality only at $x=0$).

By Jensen's inequality applied to the concave function $h$:

$$\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1} \sin\!\left(\frac{\alpha_i}{2}\right) \leq \sin\
!\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1} \alpha_i}{2(n-1)}\right) = \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{
n-1}\right).$$

Therefore:

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n-1} \sin\!\left(\frac{\alpha_i}{2}\right) \leq (n-1)\sin\!\left(\
frac{\pi}{n-1}\right),$$

with **equality if and only if** $\alpha_1 = \alpha_2 = \cdots = \alpha_{n-1} =
\frac{2\pi}{n-1}$.

**Step 3: Conclusion.** The maximum is:

$$T_{\max} = 2R(n-1)\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{n-1}\right),$$

achieved when $A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_{n-1}$ are equally spaced on the circle (each
 arc $= \frac{2\pi}{n-1}$) and $A_n = A_1$.

Thus:

$$\boxed{f(n, R) = 2R(n-1)\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{n-1}\right)}.$$

**Verification with small cases:**
- $n=3$: $f(3,R) = 2R \cdot 2 \cdot \sin(\pi/2) = 4R$. ✓ (Two diameters from $A_
2$ to $A_1 = A_3$.)
- $n=5$: $f(5,R) = 2R \cdot 4 \cdot \sin(\pi/4) = 4R\sqrt{2}$. ✓ (Four equal arc
s of $\pi/2$.)

**Final computation for $n=13$, $R=10$:**

$$f(13, 10) = 2 \cdot 10 \cdot 12 \cdot \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\right) = 240\
,\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\right).$$

Therefore:

$$\frac{f(13, 10)}{\sin(\pi/12)} = \frac{240\,\sin(\pi/12)}{\sin(\pi/12)} = \box
ed{240}.$$
