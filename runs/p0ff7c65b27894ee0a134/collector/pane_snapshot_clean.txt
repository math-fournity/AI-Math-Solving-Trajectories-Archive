## Theorem

Let $X$ be a Polish topological vector space and $A \subseteq X$ a closed subspa
ce. Then the quotient space $X/A$ equipped with the quotient topology is a Polis
h space.

## Proof

**Step 1 — Reduction to topological groups.** A TVS is, in particular, an abelia
n topological group under addition. A closed subspace $A$ is a closed subgroup.
Thus it suffices to prove:

> *If $G$ is a Polish topological group and $H \le G$ is a closed subgroup, then
 $G/H$ is Polish.*

**Step 2 — A compatible complete invariant metric.** Since $G$ is a metrizable,
completely metrizable topological group, the Birkhoff–Kakutani theorem (together
 with the completion argument of Klee–Kakutani) furnishes a metric $d$ on $G$ th
at is (i) compatible with the topology, (ii) **complete**, and (iii) **left-inva
riant**: $d(gx, gy) = d(x,y)$ for all $g, x, y \in G$. (For an abelian TVS this
is translation-invariance.)

**Step 3 — The quotient metric.** Define
$$\bar d([x], [y]) \;=\; \inf_{a,\, b \,\in\, H} d(xa,\, yb) \;=\; \inf_{h \,\in
\, H} d(x,\, yh),$$
where the second equality uses left-invariance (as $a, b$ range over $H$, so doe
s $b a^{-1}$). One checks:

- $\bar d = 0 \iff [x]=[y]$: if $\inf_{h} d(x, yh) = 0$, then $yh_n \to x$ for s
ome $h_n \in H$; since $H$ is **closed**, $x \in yH$, i.e. $[x]=[y]$. The conver
se is immediate.
- Symmetry and the triangle inequality are straightforward from the correspondin
g properties of $d$.

Hence $\bar d$ is a metric on $G/H$.

**Step 4 — Compatibility with the quotient topology.** The quotient map $\pi: G
\to G/H$ is **open**: for $U \subseteq G$ open,
$$\pi^{-1}(\pi(U)) = UH = \bigcup_{h \in H} Uh,$$
which is open, so $\pi(U)$ is open by definition of the quotient topology. Now o
bserve
$$B_{\bar d}([x], r) \;=\; \bigl\{[y] : \inf_{h \in H} d(x, yh) < r\bigr\} \;=\;
 \pi\bigl(B_d(x, r)\bigr),$$
which is open by openness of $\pi$. Conversely, if $[x] \in \pi(U)$ with $U$ ope
n, pick $\varepsilon > 0$ with $B_d(x,\varepsilon) \subseteq U$; then $B_{\bar d
}([x],\varepsilon) = \pi(B_d(x,\varepsilon)) \subseteq \pi(U)$. Thus the $\bar d
$-topology coincides with the quotient topology.

**Step 5 — Completeness of $\bar d$.** Let $([x_n])$ be $\bar d$-Cauchy. Extract
 a subsequence $([x_{n_k}])$ with $\bar d([x_{n_k}], [x_{n_{k+1}}]) < 2^{-k}$. B
y definition of $\bar d$, for each $k$ choose $h_k \in H$ with
$$d(x_{n_k},\; x_{n_{k+1}} h_k) < 2^{-k}.$$
Set $z_1 = x_{n_1}$ and $z_{k+1} = x_{n_{k+1}}\, h_1 h_2 \cdots h_k \in x_{n_{k+
1}} H = [x_{n_{k+1}}]$. Then
$$d(z_k, z_{k+1}) = d\bigl(x_{n_k},\, x_{n_{k+1}} h_k\bigr) < 2^{-k},$$
so $(z_k)$ is $d$-Cauchy in $G$. By completeness of $d$, $z_k \to z$ for some $z
 \in G$. Since $[z_k] = [x_{n_k}]$,
$$\bar d\bigl([x_{n_k}], [z]\bigr) = \bar d([z_k], [z]) \le d(z_k, z) \to 0.$$
As $([x_n])$ is Cauchy and has a convergent subsequence, $[x_n] \to [z]$. Hence
$\bar d$ is complete.

**Step 6 — Separability.** Let $D \subseteq G$ be countable dense. The continuou
s surjection $\pi$ maps $D$ to a countable set $\pi(D) \subseteq G/H$, and $\ove
rline{\pi(D)} \supseteq \pi(\overline D) = \pi(G) = G/H$. So $G/H$ is separable.

**Conclusion.** $G/H$ is separable and completely metrizable, hence Polish. Appl
ying this to $G = (X, +)$ and $H = A$ yields that $X/A$ is a Polish space. $\bla
cksquare$
