## Proof

Let $\Sigma$ be a closed (compact, without boundary) Riemann surface of genus $g
$, and let $T = T\Sigma$ denote its holomorphic tangent bundle.

**Step 1: Degree of the tangent bundle.**

The tangent bundle $T\Sigma$ is a holomorphic line bundle over $\Sigma$. Its deg
ree equals the Euler characteristic of $\Sigma$:

$$\deg(T\Sigma) = \chi(\Sigma) = 2 - 2g.$$

This is a standard fact: the degree of the tangent bundle equals the integral of
 the first Chern class $c_1(T\Sigma)$ over the fundamental class, and by the Gau
ss–Bonnet theorem this integral is exactly the Euler characteristic $\chi(\Sigma
) = 2 - 2g$.

**Step 2: Triviality forces degree zero.**

If $T\Sigma$ is (holomorphically) trivial, then $T\Sigma \cong \mathcal{O}_\Sigm
a$, the trivial line bundle. The trivial line bundle has degree $0$, hence

$$\deg(T\Sigma) = 0.$$

**Step 3: Solve for the genus.**

Combining the two steps:

$$2 - 2g = 0 \implies g = 1.$$

**Step 4: Consistency check (converse direction).**

For $g = 1$, $\Sigma$ is a complex torus $\mathbb{C}/\Lambda$. The tangent bundl
e of a complex torus is globally trivialized by the constant vector field $\part
ial/\partial z$ descended from $\mathbb{C}$, which is well-defined since transla
tions preserve it. Hence $T\Sigma \cong \mathcal{O}_\Sigma$ when $g = 1$, confir
ming the converse and showing the answer is sharp.

Therefore, the genus of a closed Riemann surface with trivial tangent bundle is

$$\boxed{g = 1}.$$
