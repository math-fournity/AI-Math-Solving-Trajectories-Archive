Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $D^2 \geq 0$ must hold when
 $|D|$ is non-empty and has no fixed component on a smooth complex projective su
rface.

**Answer: Yes, $D^2 \geq 0$ is true.**

**Proof.**

Since $|D|$ is non-empty and has no fixed component, we consider two cases based
 on the dimension of $|D|$.

**Case 1: $\dim |D| = 0$.**

Then $|D| = \{D_0\}$ for a single effective divisor $D_0 \sim D$. Since $|D|$ ha
s no fixed component, no irreducible curve is contained in every member of $|D|$
. But the only member is $D_0$, so every irreducible component of $D_0$ would be
 a fixed component. The hypothesis forces $D_0$ to have no irreducible component
s, meaning $D_0 = 0$. Thus $D \sim 0$, so $D$ is numerically trivial and $D^2 =
0 \geq 0$.

**Case 2: $\dim |D| \geq 1$.**

Choose a general member $D_1 \in |D|$, and let $C_1, \dots, C_k$ be its irreduci
ble components. Since $|D|$ has no fixed component, no $C_i$ is contained in eve
ry member of $|D|$. For each $i$, the subset
$$V_i = \{E \in |D| : C_i \subseteq E\}$$
is a proper linear subspace of $|D| \cong \mathbb{P}^r$. Since the ground field
is $\mathbb{C}$ (infinite), a finite union of proper linear subspaces cannot cov
er $|D|$. Therefore, we can choose $D_2 \in |D|$ lying outside $\bigcup_i V_i$,
meaning $D_2$ contains none of the components $C_1, \dots, C_k$.

Now $D_1$ and $D_2$ are effective Cartier divisors on the smooth surface $S$ wit
h **no common irreducible component**. Their intersection product is computed as
$$D_1 \cdot D_2 = \sum_{p \in D_1 \cap D_2} \operatorname{length}\!\left(\mathca
l{O}_{S,p}/(f_{1,p},\, f_{2,p})\right),$$
where $f_{i,p}$ are local equations. Each summand is a non-negative integer (len
gth of a local Artinian ring), so
$$D_1 \cdot D_2 \geq 0.$$

Since $D_1 \sim D_2 \sim D$, we conclude
$$D^2 = D \cdot D = D_1 \cdot D_2 \geq 0. \qquad \blacksquare$$

**Remark on the necessity of the hypothesis.** The "no fixed component" conditio
n is essential. For example, on $S = \operatorname{Bl}_p(\mathbb{P}^2)$, the exc
eptional divisor $E$ satisfies $|E| = \{E\}$ (which has a fixed component, namel
y $E$ itself), and $E^2 = -1 < 0$.
