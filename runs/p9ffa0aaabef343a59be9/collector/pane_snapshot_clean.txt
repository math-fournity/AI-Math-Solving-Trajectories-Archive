Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the spectral radius is glob
ally Lipschitz on the set of positive matrices.

**Answer: No.** There is no universal constant $C$ such that $|r(A) - r(B)| \le
C\|A - B\|$ for all positive matrices $A, B$.

---

## Proof

We construct a family of positive matrices for which the ratio $|r(A) - r(B)| /
\|A - B\|$ is unbounded.

**The family.** For $\varepsilon > 0$, define the $2 \times 2$ matrices

$$A_\varepsilon = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ \varepsilon & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
 B_\varepsilon = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 2\varepsilon & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Since every entry is strictly positive, both $A_\varepsilon$ and $B_\varepsilon$
 are positive matrices.

**Spectral radii.** The characteristic polynomial of $A_\varepsilon$ is

$$\det(A_\varepsilon - \lambda I) = (1 - \lambda)^2 - \varepsilon = 0,$$

so the eigenvalues are $\lambda = 1 \pm \sqrt{\varepsilon}$, giving

$$r(A_\varepsilon) = 1 + \sqrt{\varepsilon}.$$

Similarly, the characteristic polynomial of $B_\varepsilon$ is $(1-\lambda)^2 -
2\varepsilon = 0$, so

$$r(B_\varepsilon) = 1 + \sqrt{2\varepsilon}.$$

**The difference in spectral radii.**

$$|r(A_\varepsilon) - r(B_\varepsilon)| = \sqrt{2\varepsilon} - \sqrt{\varepsilo
n} = \sqrt{\varepsilon}\,(\sqrt{2} - 1).$$

**The norm of the difference.**

$$A_\varepsilon - B_\varepsilon = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ -\varepsilon & 0 \end
{pmatrix}.$$

This is a rank-one matrix $\varepsilon\, e_2 e_1^\top$. Its operator (spectral)
norm, Frobenius norm, and max-entry norm are all equal to $\varepsilon$:

$$\|A_\varepsilon - B_\varepsilon\| = \varepsilon.$$

**The ratio.**

$$\frac{|r(A_\varepsilon) - r(B_\varepsilon)|}{\|A_\varepsilon - B_\varepsilon\|
} = \frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,(\sqrt{2}-1)}{\varepsilon} = \frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{\sq
rt{\varepsilon}}.$$

As $\varepsilon \to 0^+$, this ratio diverges to $+\infty$.

**Conclusion.** If a constant $C$ satisfying $|r(A) - r(B)| \le C\|A-B\|$ for al
l positive matrices $A, B$ existed, we would need

$$\frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}} \le C \quad \text{for all } \varepsilon
> 0,$$

which is impossible since the left-hand side is unbounded. Therefore **no such c
onstant $C$ exists**.

---

**Remark on why Perron–Frobenius is insufficient here.** The Perron–Frobenius th
eorem guarantees that $r(A)$ is an *algebraically simple* eigenvalue for each po
sitive matrix $A$. Simplicity implies that $r$ is *locally* an analytic function
 of the matrix entries (by standard eigenvalue perturbation theory), so for each
 *fixed* positive matrix $A$, there exists a neighborhood and a local Lipschitz
constant. However, this local constant depends on the eigenvalue condition numbe
r $|u^\top v|^{-1}\|u\|\|v\|$, where $u, v$ are the left and right Perron eigenv
ectors. For the family $A_\varepsilon$ above, the right Perron eigenvector is $v
 \propto (1, \sqrt{\varepsilon})$ and the left is $u \propto (\sqrt{\varepsilon}
, 1)$, giving $u^\top v \propto 2\sqrt{\varepsilon} \to 0$ while $\|u\|\|v\| \to
 1$. The condition number blows up like $1/\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, exactly matching
 the divergence we computed. Thus, simplicity alone does not yield a *uniform* L
ipschitz constant over the entire class of positive matrices.
