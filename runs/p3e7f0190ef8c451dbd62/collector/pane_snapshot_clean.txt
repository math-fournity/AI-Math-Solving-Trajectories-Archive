## Proof

**Yes, such a manifold exists.** We show that $S^2 \times S^1$ is a compact mani
fold with Euler characteristic $0$ that admits no Lie group structure.

---

### Step 1: $S^2 \times S^1$ is compact with Euler characteristic $0$.

Both $S^2$ and $S^1$ are compact manifolds, so $S^2 \times S^1$ is a compact $3$
-manifold. The Euler characteristic is multiplicative:

$$\chi(S^2 \times S^1) = \chi(S^2)\cdot\chi(S^1) = 2 \cdot 0 = 0.$$

### Step 2: Reduction to the connected case.

$S^2 \times S^1$ is connected. Any Lie group homeomorphic to it would therefore
be a **compact connected** Lie group.

### Step 3: Cohomological obstruction — the Hopf theorem.

**Theorem (Hopf, 1940).** If $G$ is a compact connected Lie group, then $H^*(G;\
,\mathbb{Q})$ is an exterior algebra on generators of **odd** degree:
$$H^*(G;\,\mathbb{Q}) \cong \Lambda(x_1,\dots,x_r), \qquad |x_i| \text{ odd for
all } i.$$

*Sketch.* The cohomology $H^*(G;\mathbb{Q})$ carries the structure of a finite-d
imensional graded-commutative **Hopf algebra** (the product is cup product; the
coproduct is induced by the group multiplication $G\times G\to G$). The Hopf–Bor
el structure theorem states that every such Hopf algebra over a field of charact
eristic $0$ is an exterior algebra on odd-degree generators tensored with a poly
nomial algebra on even-degree generators. Finite-dimensionality forces the polyn
omial part to be trivial, leaving only the exterior algebra on odd generators. $
\square$

### Step 4: $H^*(S^2\times S^1;\,\mathbb{Q})$ is **not** an exterior algebra on
odd generators.

By the Künneth theorem:
$$H^*(S^2\times S^1;\,\mathbb{Q}) \cong H^*(S^2;\mathbb{Q})\otimes H^*(S^1;\math
bb{Q}) \cong \frac{\mathbb{Q}[a]}{(a^2)}\otimes \Lambda(b),$$
where $|a|=2$ and $|b|=1$. Hence:

| $k$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $H^k$ | $\mathbb{Q}$ | $\mathbb{Q}\langle b\rangle$ | $\mathbb{Q}\langle a\ran
gle$ | $\mathbb{Q}\langle ab\rangle$ |

In particular $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} H^1 = 1$ and $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} H^2 = 1$.

Suppose for contradiction that this ring were $\Lambda(x_1,\dots,x_r)$ with all
$|x_i|$ odd.

- Since $\dim H^1 = 1$, **exactly one** generator has degree $1$; call it $x_1$.
- In an exterior algebra, $x_1^2 = 0$, and the degree-$2$ component is spanned b
y products $x_i x_j$ with $|x_i|+|x_j|=2$. The only way to get degree $2$ from o
dd-degree generators is a product of two degree-$1$ generators. But there is onl
y **one** degree-$1$ generator, so there are no such products, giving $H^2 = 0$.
- This contradicts $H^2 = \mathbb{Q}\neq 0$.

(If instead no generator had degree $1$, then $H^1=0$, contradicting $H^1=\mathb
b{Q}$.)

### Conclusion.

$S^2\times S^1$ is a compact manifold with $\chi = 0$, yet its rational cohomolo
gy ring cannot be an exterior algebra on odd-degree generators. By the Hopf theo
rem, no compact connected Lie group can have this cohomology ring. Since $S^2\ti
mes S^1$ is connected, it cannot be given **any** Lie group structure.

**Therefore, yes — there exists a compact manifold with Euler characteristic $0$
 that cannot be given a Lie group structure, namely $S^2\times S^1$.**
