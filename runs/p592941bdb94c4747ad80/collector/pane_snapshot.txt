## Proof

We evaluate $\displaystyle \int_C \frac{e^z}{z^3}\,dz$, where $C$ is the boundar
y of the square with corners $\pm 4 \pm 4i$ (traversed once counterclockwise, as
 is standard for positively oriented boundaries).

**Step 1: Identify the singularity.** The integrand $f(z) = \dfrac{e^z}{z^3}$ ha
s a single isolated singularity at $z = 0$, which is a pole of order $3$. Since
$0$ lies strictly inside the square centered at the origin with side length $8$,
 the singularity is enclosed by $C$.

**Step 2: Apply Cauchy's integral formula for derivatives.** For a function $g$
analytic inside and on a simple closed positively oriented contour $C$ enclosing
 a point $a$,

$$g^{(n)}(a) = \frac{n!}{2\pi i}\oint_C \frac{g(z)}{(z-a)^{n+1}}\,dz.$$

Set $g(z) = e^z$ (entire, hence analytic everywhere), $a = 0$, and $n+1 = 3$, i.
e. $n = 2$. Then

$$g^{(2)}(0) = \frac{2!}{2\pi i}\oint_C \frac{e^z}{z^3}\,dz.$$

**Step 3: Compute the derivative.** Since $g(z) = e^z$, we have $g'(z) = e^z$ an
d $g''(z) = e^z$, so

$$g^{(2)}(0) = e^0 = 1.$$

**Step 4: Solve for the integral.** Substituting,

$$1 = \frac{2}{2\pi i}\int_C \frac{e^z}{z^3}\,dz = \frac{1}{\pi i}\int_C \frac{e
^z}{z^3}\,dz.$$

Therefore,

$$\int_C \frac{e^z}{z^3}\,dz = \pi i.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
