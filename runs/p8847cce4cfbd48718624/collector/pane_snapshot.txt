**Claim:** Yes, the holonomy group is contained in $SU_n$.

**Setup.** Let $(M,J,\omega)$ be a Kähler manifold of complex dimension $n$, so
the Riemannian holonomy group $\mathrm{Hol}(M)\subseteq U_n$ (with respect to th

- non-vanishing everywhere, and
- parallel: $\nabla\Omega=0$ (Levi-Civita connection).

$$P_\gamma(T_p)=T_p.$$
Hence every element of $\mathrm{Hol}_p(M)\subseteq U(T_pM)\cong U_n$ fixes $\Ome
ga_p$.

**Step 2 — The $U_n$-representation on $(n,0)$-forms is the determinant characte

$$A\cdot (dz^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dz^n)=\det(A)\,dz^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dz^n,\qqu
ad A\in U_n.$$
This is because the action on the top exterior power of the dual of the defining

**Step 3 — The stabilizer of a non-zero $(n,0)$-form in $U_n$ is $SU_n$.** Since

$$\{A\in U_n : \det(A)=1\}=SU_n.$$

$$\mathrm{Hol}_p(M)\subseteq \mathrm{Stab}_{U_n}(\Omega_p)=SU_n.$$
Since the holonomy group (with its canonical embedding into $U_n$) is contained
in $SU_n$, we conclude
$$\boxed{\mathrm{Hol}(M)\subseteq SU_n.}$$

**Remark on the hypotheses.** The condition $\nabla\Omega=0$ alone already impli
es $\Omega$ is holomorphic: parallelism gives $\nabla^{0,1}\Omega=0$, and on a K

hence vanishes. Thus "holomorphic" is automatic from "parallel"; the essential h

Conversely, this is the standard Calabi–Yau characterization: a Kähler $n$-fold
has holonomy in $SU_n$ if and only if it admits a nowhere-vanishing parallel hol
