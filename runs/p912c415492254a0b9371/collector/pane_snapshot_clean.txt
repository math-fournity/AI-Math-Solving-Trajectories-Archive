The problem, as stated, is **underdetermined**: the number of valid 3-subsets de
pends on how the three language groups overlap, which the problem does not speci
fy. I prove this by exhibiting two configurations, both satisfying all stated co
nditions, that yield different answers.

---

**Setup.** Let the community be $U=\{1,2,\dots,20\}$. Let $E,G,F\subseteq U$ wit
h $|E|=|G|=|F|=10$ be the English, German, and French speakers. We want
$$N=\bigl|\{S\in\binom{U}{3}: S\cap E\neq\varnothing,\; S\cap G\neq\varnothing,\
; S\cap F\neq\varnothing\}\bigr|.$$

By inclusion–exclusion on the complement (a 3-subset *missing* a language lies e
ntirely in that language's complement, of size $20-10=10$):
$$N=\binom{20}{3}-3\binom{10}{3}+\bigl[\tbinom{|E\cap G|}{3}+\tbinom{|E\cap F|}{
3}+\tbinom{|G\cap F|}{3}\bigr]-\tbinom{20-|E\cup G\cup F|}{3},$$
where I used $|E|+|G|=20$ (so $|U\setminus(E\cup G)|=|E\cap G|$, etc.). The brac
keted term depends on the pairwise overlaps, which are **not** specified.

---

**Configuration A.** $E=G=F=\{1,\dots,10\}$ (the same ten people speak all three
 languages; persons $11$–$20$ speak none).
- Conditions satisfied: $|E|=|G|=|F|=10$ in a 20-person community. ✓
- The requirement "at least one English, one German, one French" reduces to "at
least one of $\{1,\dots,10\}$."
- $N_A=\binom{20}{3}-\binom{10}{3}=1140-120=1020.$

**Configuration B.** $E=F=\{1,\dots,10\}$, $G=\{11,\dots,20\}$ (persons $1$–$10$
 speak English and French; persons $11$–$20$ speak German).
- Conditions satisfied: $|E|=|G|=|F|=10$. ✓
- Requirement: at least one from $\{1,\dots,10\}$ (covers $E$ and $F$) **and** a
t least one from $\{11,\dots,20\}$ (covers $G$). Since these two sets partition
$U$, a 3-subset fails iff it lies entirely in one part.
- $N_B=\binom{20}{3}-\binom{10}{3}-\binom{10}{3}=1140-120-120=900.$

(One may also verify Configuration B via the formula: $|E\cap G|=|G\cap F|=0$, $
|E\cap F|=10$, $|E\cup G\cup F|=20$, giving $1140-360+(0+120+0)-0=900$.)

---

**Conclusion.** $N_A=1020\neq 900=N_B$, yet both configurations satisfy every co
ndition stated in the problem. Therefore the requested quantity is **not uniquel
y determined** by the given data; additional information about the overlaps amon
g the three language groups is required. The problem as posed has no single nume
rical answer.
