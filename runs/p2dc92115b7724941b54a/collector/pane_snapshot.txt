## Proof

Sort the twelve numbers so that
$$1 < x_1 \le x_2 \le \cdots \le x_{12} < 12.$$
We will show that some triple among them forms an acute triangle.

**Key fact.** If $a\le b\le c$ are positive and $a^2+b^2>c^2$, then $a,b,c$ are
the sides of an acute triangle. Indeed, $(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2>a^2+b^2>c^2$, so $a
+b>c$ (and the other two triangle inequalities are automatic since $c$ is larges
t); and by the law of cosines the largest angle $\theta$ satisfies $\cos\theta=(
a^2+b^2-c^2)/(2ab)>0$, so every angle is acute.

Thus it suffices to find distinct indices $i,j,k$ with $x_i^2+x_j^2>x_k^2$ (wher
e $x_k$ is the largest of the three).

**Suppose, for contradiction, that no triple forms an acute triangle.** Then for
 every $i<j<k$ we must have
$$x_i^2+x_j^2 \le x_k^2.$$
For a fixed $k$, the most restrictive such inequality is the one with the two la
rgest available smaller elements, namely
$$x_{k-2}^2+x_{k-1}^2 \le x_k^2 \qquad (k\ge 3),$$
since $x_i^2+x_j^2\le x_{k-2}^2+x_{k-1}^2$ for all $i<j<k$. Conversely, this sin
gle inequality for each $k\ge 3$ implies all of them. So the acute-free conditio
n is exactly
$$x_k^2 \ge x_{k-1}^2+x_{k-2}^2 \qquad\text{for every } k=3,4,\dots,12.$$

Set $y_k=x_k^2$. Then $y_k\ge y_{k-1}+y_{k-2}$ for $k\ge 3$, with $y_1>1$ and $y
_2>1$ (because every $x_k>1$). Iterating:

| $k$ | lower bound on $y_k$ |
|---|---|
| 1 | $>1$ |
| 2 | $>1$ |
| 3 | $>2$ |
| 4 | $>3$ |
| 5 | $>5$ |
| 6 | $>8$ |
| 7 | $>13$ |
| 8 | $>21$ |
| 9 | $>34$ |
| 10 | $>55$ |
| 11 | $>89$ |
| 12 | $>144$ |

Each step uses $y_k\ge y_{k-1}+y_{k-2}$ and the (strict) lower bounds already ob
tained, so every bound is strict. In particular
$$y_{12}=x_{12}^2>144=12^2,$$
which forces $x_{12}>12$, contradicting the hypothesis $x_{12}<12$.

(The lower bounds are the Fibonacci numbers $F_1,F_2,\dots,F_{12}=144$, reflecti
ng the Fibonacci recurrence $y_k\ge y_{k-1}+y_{k-2}$.)

The contradiction shows that our assumption was false: some triple $x_i,x_j,x_k$
 satisfies $x_i^2+x_j^2>x_k^2$, and by the key fact these three values are the s
ide lengths of an acute triangle. $\blacksquare$
