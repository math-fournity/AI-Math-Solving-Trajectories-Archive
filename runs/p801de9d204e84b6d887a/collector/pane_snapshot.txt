The set is the **power set of the real numbers**, $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})$.

**Proof.**

Let $\mathfrak{c} = |\mathbb{R}| = 2^{\aleph_0}$ denote the cardinality of the c
ontinuum.

**Step 1: $\mathfrak{c} \geq \aleph_1$.**

By Cantor's theorem, $2^{\aleph_0} > \aleph_0$, so $\mathfrak{c}$ is an uncounta
ble cardinal. The smallest uncountable cardinal is $\aleph_1$, hence $\mathfrak{
c} \geq \aleph_1$.

**Step 2: $|\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})| = 2^{\mathfrak{c}} > \mathfrak{c}$.**

By Cantor's theorem applied to $\mathbb{R}$, the power set $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{
R})$ has cardinality strictly larger than $\mathbb{R}$:
$$|\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})| = 2^{\mathfrak{c}} > \mathfrak{c}.$$

**Step 3: $|\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})| \geq \aleph_2$.**

Since $|\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})|$ is a cardinal strictly greater than $\mathfrak
{c}$, it is at least the successor cardinal $\mathfrak{c}^+$:
$$|\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})| \geq \mathfrak{c}^+.$$

From Step 1, $\mathfrak{c} \geq \aleph_1$, so the successor cardinal satisfies
$$\mathfrak{c}^+ \geq \aleph_1^+ = \aleph_2.$$

Combining,
$$|\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})| \geq \mathfrak{c}^+ \geq \aleph_2.$$

This argument is carried out entirely in **ZFC** and does not depend on the Cont
inuum Hypothesis. Whether $\mathfrak{c} = \aleph_1$ (CH) or $\mathfrak{c} > \ale
ph_1$, the conclusion $|\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})| \geq \aleph_2$ holds.
