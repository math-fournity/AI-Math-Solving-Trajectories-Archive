## Setting Up the Sum

We have $f(n) = 6^n$, so $f(20n) = 6^{20n}$. The $n$-th term is:

$$\frac{x^{6^n}\left(1 - x^{6^{20n}}\right)}{1 - x^{6^n}}$$

Using the geometric series identity $\frac{1-x^M}{1-x^d} = \sum_{k=0}^{M/d - 1}
x^{kd}$ (when $d \mid M$):

$$\frac{1 - x^{6^{20n}}}{1 - x^{6^n}} = \sum_{k=0}^{6^{20n}/6^n - 1} x^{k \cdot
6^n} = \sum_{k=0}^{6^{19n}-1} x^{k \cdot 6^n}$$

Multiplying by $x^{6^n}$, the $n$-th term becomes:

$$\sum_{k=0}^{6^{19n}-1} x^{(k+1)\cdot 6^n} = \sum_{j=1}^{6^{19n}} x^{j \cdot 6^
n}$$

So the full sum is $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{20}\sum_{j=1}^{6^{19n}} x^{j\cdot 6
^n}$, and the coefficient of $x^{20!}$ equals the number of pairs $(n,j)$ with $
j\cdot 6^n = 20!$, $1\le n\le 20$, $1\le j\le 6^{19n}$.
