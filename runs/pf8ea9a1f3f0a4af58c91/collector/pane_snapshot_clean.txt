## Proof

**Goal.** Compute the total Gaussian curvature $\int_S K\,dA$ of the surface
$$S = \{(x,y,z) : x^2 + y^4 + z^6 = 1\}.$$

**Step 1. $S$ is a smooth closed surface.**

Let $F(x,y,z) = x^2 + y^4 + z^6$. Then $S = F^{-1}(1)$. The gradient
$$\nabla F = (2x,\; 4y^3,\; 6z^5)$$
vanishes only at the origin, where $F(0,0,0)=0 \neq 1$. Hence $\nabla F \neq 0$
on $S$, so $S$ is a smooth (codimension-1) submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^3$. Since
$F$ is proper and $1$ is a regular value, $S$ is compact and closed (no boundary
). Being a level surface of a smooth function with non-vanishing gradient, $S$ i
s orientable.

**Step 2. $S$ is homeomorphic to $S^2$ (star-shaped).**

We show $S$ is star-shaped with respect to the origin. Take any direction $v=(a,
b,c)$ with $a^2+b^2+c^2=1$, $v\neq 0$. A ray from the origin in direction $v$ me
ets $S$ at $tv$ where
$$g(t) := t^2 a^2 + t^4 b^4 + t^6 c^6 = 1.$$
For $t>0$, $g$ is strictly increasing (at least one of $a^2,b^4,c^6$ is positive
 and every term $t^{2k}(\cdot)$ is increasing for $t>0$), with $g(0)=0$ and $g(t
)\to\infty$. Thus there is a **unique** $t(v)>0$ solving $g(t)=1$. By evenness (
$g(-t)=g(t)$), the opposite ray gives $-t(v)$.

Therefore the radial projection $\pi:S\to S^2$, $\pi(p) = p/\|p\|$, is a continu
ous bijection with continuous inverse $v \mapsto t(v)\,v$ (continuity of $t(v)$
follows from the implicit function theorem applied to $g(t;v)=1$). Hence $S \con
g S^2$, so
$$\chi(S) = \chi(S^2) = 2, \qquad \text{genus } g = 0.$$

**Step 3. Apply Gauss–Bonnet.**

$S$ is a compact, orientable, boundaryless Riemannian 2-manifold. By the Gauss–B
onnet theorem,
$$\int_S K\,dA = 2\pi\,\chi(S) = 2\pi \cdot 2 = 4\pi.$$

**Conclusion.** The total Gaussian curvature of the surface $x^2+y^4+z^6=1$ is

$$\boxed{4\pi}.$$
