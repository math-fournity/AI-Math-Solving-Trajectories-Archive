## Proof

**Step 1: Branch values on the cut.**

Write $(z^2-1)^{1/2} = |z-1|^{1/2}|z+1|^{1/2}\,e^{i(\arg(z-1)+\arg(z+1))/2}$ wit
h arguments chosen so the cut is $[-1,1]$ and the value is positive for $x>1$.

For $z = x+i0^+$ with $-1<x<1$:
- $z-1 = (x-1)+i0^+$, $\arg \to \pi$; $z+1=(x+1)+i0^+$, $\arg\to 0$.
- $(z^2-1)^{1/2} = \sqrt{1-x^2}\,e^{i\pi/2} = i\sqrt{1-x^2}$.

For $z = x-i0^+$ with $-1<x<1$:
- $z-1 = (x-1)-i0^+$, $\arg\to -\pi$; $z+1=(x+1)-i0^+$, $\arg\to 0$.
- $(z^2-1)^{1/2} = \sqrt{1-x^2}\,e^{-i\pi/2} = -i\sqrt{1-x^2}$.

**Step 2: Contour deformation.**

The integrand is single-valued and analytic in the region $\Omega$ inside $C$ an
d outside the cut $[-1,1]$. By Cauchy's theorem, $\oint_{\partial\Omega} f\,dz=0
$, where $\partial\Omega$ consists of $C$ (counterclockwise) and a dumbbell cont
our $\Gamma$ around the cut (clockwise, so $\Omega$ is on the left). The small c
ircles around $\pm1$ contribute $O(\varepsilon^{3/2})\to 0$. Thus

$$\int_C f\,dz = -\int_{\Gamma,\,\text{CW}} f\,dz.$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the dumbbell integral (clockwise).**

Clockwise around the cut (cut on the right):
- **Top:** $x$ from $-1$ to $1$, $f = i\sqrt{1-x^2}$, $dz=dx$:
$$\int_{\text{top}} = i\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{1-x^2}\,dx = i\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}.$$
- **Bottom:** $x$ from $1$ to $-1$, $f = -i\sqrt{1-x^2}$, $dz=dx$:
$$\int_{\text{bottom}} = \int_{1}^{-1}(-i)\sqrt{1-x^2}\,dx = i\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt
{1-x^2}\,dx = i\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}.$$

So $\int_{\Gamma,\text{CW}} = i\pi$, giving $\int_C = -i\pi$.

**Step 4: Verification via Laurent expansion at infinity.**

For $|z|>1$, with the branch positive on $x>1$:
$$(z^2-1)^{1/2} = z\left(1-\frac{1}{z^2}\right)^{1/2} = z - \frac{1}{2z} - \frac
{1}{8z^3}-\cdots$$

The coefficient of $1/z$ is $-1/2$, so by the residue theorem (applied at infini
ty):

$$\int_C (z^2-1)^{1/2}\,dz = 2\pi i\cdot\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) = -i\pi.$$

Both methods agree. The result is independent of $R$ (for $R>1$), as expected si
nce the only non-analytic feature (the branch cut) lies inside every such circle
.

$$\boxed{\int_C (z^2-1)^{1/2}\,dz = -i\pi}$$
