## Proof that $\pi_1(\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus A) \cong \mathbb{Z}$

**Setup.** Let $A$ be the unit circle $\{(x,y,0):x^2+y^2=1\}$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$.
 Let $T \subset T'$ be two concentric closed tubular neighborhoods of $A$ (solid
 tori), with $T$ contained in the interior of $T'$. Denote by $E = S^3 \setminus
 \operatorname{int}(T)$ the *exterior* solid torus in $S^3 = \mathbb{R}^3 \cup \
{\infty\}$; for the unknotted circle $A$, the exterior $E$ is itself an unknotte
d solid torus, and $\infty$ lies in $\operatorname{int}(E)$.

**Open cover of $\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus A$.** Define
- $U = \mathbb{R}^3 \setminus T = \operatorname{int}(E) \setminus \{\infty\}$,
- $V = \operatorname{int}(T') \setminus A$.

Both are open in $\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus A$ and path-connected. Their union is a
ll of $\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus A$: a point outside $T$ lies in $U$; a point insid
e $\operatorname{int}(T')$ but not on $A$ lies in $V$; and the annular region $\
operatorname{int}(T') \setminus T$ is covered by both. The intersection is
$$U \cap V = \operatorname{int}(T') \setminus T \simeq \partial T \times (0,1) \
simeq T^2,$$
which is path-connected. Van Kampen applies.

**Fundamental groups of the pieces.**

*The intersection $U \cap V$.* It deformation retracts onto the torus $\partial
T$, so
$$\pi_1(U \cap V) \cong \langle m, l \mid [m,l]\rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}^2,$$
where $m$ is the **meridian** of $T$ (a small loop linking $A$ once) and $l$ is
the **longitude** of $T$ (a loop on $\partial T$ parallel to $A$).

*The set $V = \operatorname{int}(T') \setminus A$.* Writing the solid torus as $
\operatorname{int}(T') \cong S^1 \times \operatorname{int}(D^2)$ with core $A =
S^1 \times \{0\}$,
$$V = S^1 \times (\operatorname{int}(D^2)\setminus\{0\}) \simeq S^1 \times S^1 =
 T^2.$$
Thus $\pi_1(V) \cong \langle \mu', \lambda' \mid [\mu',\lambda']\rangle \cong \m
athbb{Z}^2$, with $\mu'$ the meridian and $\lambda'$ the longitude of $T'$.

*The set $U = \operatorname{int}(E) \setminus \{\infty\}$.* The exterior $E$ is
a solid torus, so $\operatorname{int}(E) \simeq S^1$ and $\pi_1(\operatorname{in
t}(E)) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by the core of $E$ — which is exactly the me
ridian $m = \mu_T$ of $T$ (for an unknotted torus, the core of the exterior soli
d torus is the meridian of $T$). Removing a single point from the interior of a
3-manifold does **not** change $\pi_1$: any loop (1-dimensional) and any null-ho
motopy disk (2-dimensional) can be perturbed by general position to avoid a poin
t ($1+0<3$ and $2+0<3$). Hence
$$\pi_1(U) \cong \pi_1(\operatorname{int}(E)) \cong \mathbb{Z} = \langle a \rang
le, \qquad a = \mu_T = m.$$

**Inclusion-induced maps.** The shell $U \cap V$ retracts onto $\partial T$ insi
de $U$ and onto $\partial T'$ inside $V$.

- Inclusion $i: U\cap V \hookrightarrow U$: the meridian $m$ maps to the generat
or $a$ (the core of $E$), while the longitude $l = \lambda_T$ is the meridian of
 $E$, which bounds a meridian disk $\{s\}\times D^2 \subset E$ with $s \neq s_\i
nfty$; this disk avoids $\infty$, so $l$ is null-homotopic in $U$:
$$i_*(m) = a, \qquad i_*(l) = 1.$$

- Inclusion $j: U\cap V \hookrightarrow V$: both generators survive on the torus
 $\partial T'$:
$$j_*(m) = \mu', \qquad j_*(l) = \lambda'.$$

**Van Kampen's theorem.** The fundamental group of $\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus A$ is
 the amalgamated product
$$\pi_1(\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus A) \cong \pi_1(U) *_{\pi_1(U\cap V)} \pi_1(V),$$
with presentation
$$\big\langle a,\ \mu',\ \lambda' \;\big|\; [\mu',\lambda'],\ a = \mu',\ \lambda
' = 1 \big\rangle.$$
The relation $a = \mu'$ identifies the meridian of $T$ with the meridian of $T'$
; the relation $\lambda' = 1$ kills the longitude; and $[\mu',\lambda'] = [\mu',
1]$ is then automatic. What remains is
$$\pi_1(\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus A) \cong \langle a \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z},$$
generated by the meridian — the loop that links the circle $A$ once.
