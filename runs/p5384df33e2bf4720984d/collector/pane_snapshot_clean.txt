Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest $k$ such that any real numb
ers $a_1, \dots, a_d \in [0,1]$ with $\sum a_i = n$ can be partitioned into $k$
groups, each with sum $\le 1$.

**Answer: $k = 2n - 1$.**

## Lower Bound: $k \ge 2n - 1$

Consider $d = 2n - 1$ numbers, each equal to $\frac{n}{2n-1}$.

- **Sum check:** $(2n-1) \cdot \frac{n}{2n-1} = n$. ✓
- **Range check:** $\frac{n}{2n-1} \le 1 \iff n \le 2n-1 \iff n \ge 1$. ✓
- **Key property:** $\frac{n}{2n-1} > \frac{1}{2}$ since $2n > 2n - 1$.

Since every number exceeds $\frac{1}{2}$, no two numbers can share a group (thei
r sum would exceed $1$). Therefore each number requires its own group, giving at
 least $2n - 1$ groups.

## Upper Bound: $k \le 2n - 1$

We show the **First Fit Decreasing (FFD)** algorithm always uses at most $2n - 1
$ bins.

**Algorithm.** Sort the numbers in decreasing order: $a_1 \ge a_2 \ge \cdots \ge
 a_d$. Process them in this order. For each number, place it in the first existi
ng group where it fits (i.e., current group sum $+ a_i \le 1$). If no existing g
roup can accommodate it, open a new group.

Let $b$ denote the number of groups produced, labeled $B_1, B_2, \ldots, B_b$ in
 order of creation. Let $f_j$ denote the first (and hence largest) item placed i
n $B_j$.

**Key property of FFD.** When $f_j$ was processed, it could not fit in any earli
er group $B_i$ ($i < j$). In particular, for each $j \ge 2$:
$$\operatorname{sum}(B_{j-1}) + f_j > 1,$$
where $\operatorname{sum}(B_{j-1})$ is evaluated at the moment $f_j$ is being pl
aced. Since all items in $B_{j-1}$ were placed before $f_j$ (being larger, proce
ssed earlier) and $B_{j-1}$ may only receive additional items afterward, the *fi
nal* sum of $B_{j-1}$ is at least its sum at that moment. Also $\operatorname{su
m}(B_j) \ge f_j$. Therefore, for every $j \ge 2$:
$$\operatorname{sum}(B_{j-1}) + \operatorname{sum}(B_j) > 1. \tag{$\star$}$$

**Counting.** We pair consecutive groups: $(B_1, B_2), (B_3, B_4), \ldots$

- **If $b$ is even:** There are $b/2$ pairs, each satisfying $(\star)$, so the t
otal sum $n > \frac{b}{2}$, giving $b < 2n$, hence $b \le 2n - 1$.

- **If $b$ is odd:** There are $\frac{b-1}{2}$ pairs, each satisfying $(\star)$,
 plus the last group $B_b$ with positive sum. So $n > \frac{b-1}{2}$, giving $b
< 2n + 1$, hence $b \le 2n$. Since $b$ is odd and $2n$ is even, we conclude $b \
le 2n - 1$.

In both cases, $b \le 2n - 1$.

## Conclusion

The lower bound $k \ge 2n - 1$ and upper bound $k \le 2n - 1$ together yield:

$$\boxed{k = 2n - 1}$$
