# Problem

In a remote digital library, a master archivist has organized 30 ancient data crystals into a strictly linear sequence for restoration. However, the exact chronological order of this sequence is hidden. 

The restoration team is permitted to use a specialized scanning device. To operate it, they must select exactly 3 crystals and place them in the scanner. The scanner does not reveal the full relative order of the three; instead, it identifies only two specific crystals: the one that appears earliest in the master sequence and the one that appears latest among those three.

A subset of the crystals is considered "fully sequenced" only if the team can definitively determine the relative chronological order of every crystal within that specific subset, regardless of how the master archivist originally arranged the 30.

Determine the largest integer $n$ such that the team can guarantee they can find the exact relative order of a group of $n$ crystals.
