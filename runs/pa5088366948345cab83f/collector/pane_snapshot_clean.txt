## Proof

**Yes, $f_{*p}$ is surjective.** In fact, it is a linear isomorphism.

**Definition.** $f$ is a local diffeomorphism at $p$ means there exists an open
neighborhood $U \subseteq M$ of $p$ such that $f(U) \subseteq N$ is open and the
 restricted map $f|_U : U \to f(U)$ is a diffeomorphism.

**Step 1: $\dim M = \dim N$.**

Since $U$ is open in $M$, $\dim U = \dim M$. Since $f(U)$ is open in $N$, $\dim
f(U) = \dim N$. A diffeomorphism is in particular a homeomorphism preserving dim
ension, so $\dim M = \dim U = \dim f(U) = \dim N$.

**Step 2: $f_{*p}$ is a linear isomorphism.**

Let $g = (f|_U)^{-1} : f(U) \to U$, the smooth inverse of $f|_U$. By the chain r
ule applied to the identities $g \circ f|_U = \mathrm{id}_U$ and $f|_U \circ g =
 \mathrm{id}_{f(U)}$, we get at the points $p$ and $f(p)$:

$$g_{*f(p)} \circ f_{*p} = \mathrm{id}_{T_p M}, \qquad f_{*p} \circ g_{*f(p)} =
\mathrm{id}_{T_{f(p)} N}.$$

Thus $f_{*p} : T_p M \to T_{f(p)} N$ and $g_{*f(p)} : T_{f(p)} N \to T_p M$ are
mutual inverses as linear maps. Therefore $f_{*p}$ is a linear isomorphism.

**Conclusion.** A linear isomorphism is in particular surjective, so $f_{*p}$ is
 surjective. $\blacksquare$
