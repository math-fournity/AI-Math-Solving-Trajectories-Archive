**Proof of (a).**

Let $n$ be a natural number. We must show that $n$ has a nonzero multiple whose
decimal representation uses only the digits $0$ and $1$.

For each $k \ge 1$, let $R_k$ denote the *repunit* with $k$ digits, all equal to
 $1$:
$$R_k = \underbrace{11\cdots 1}_{k \text{ ones}} = \frac{10^k - 1}{9}.$$

Consider the $n+1$ repunits
$$R_1,\; R_2,\; R_3,\; \dots,\; R_{n+1}.$$

Reduce each of them modulo $n$. There are $n+1$ numbers but only $n$ possible re
sidue classes $\{0, 1, \dots, n-1\}$. By the **pigeonhole principle**, two of th
ese repunits, say $R_j$ and $R_i$ with $j > i$, must lie in the same residue cla
ss modulo $n$. Hence
$$R_j \equiv R_i \pmod{n},$$
so their difference satisfies
$$n \mid (R_j - R_i).$$

Now compute this difference. Since
$$R_j - R_i = \frac{10^j - 1}{9} - \frac{10^i - 1}{9} = \frac{10^j - 10^i}{9} =
10^i \cdot \frac{10^{j-i} - 1}{9} = 10^i \cdot R_{j-i},$$
we see that $R_j - R_i$ is the number consisting of $(j-i)$ ones followed by $i$
 zeros:
$$R_j - R_i = \underbrace{11\cdots 1}_{j-i \text{ ones}}\underbrace{00\cdots 0}_
{i \text{ zeros}}.$$

This number has the following properties:

1. **It is nonzero**, since $j > i$ means it contains at least one digit $1$.
2. **Its decimal representation contains only the digits $0$ and $1$** — a block
 of ones followed by a block of zeros.
3. **It is a multiple of $n$**, as shown above.

Therefore $R_j - R_i$ is a nonzero multiple of $n$ whose base-10 representation
contains only zeroes and ones. $\blacksquare$
