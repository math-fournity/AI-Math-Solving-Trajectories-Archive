## Solution

**Answer: Yes.** The projection of a complex algebraic set onto a lower-dimensio
nal space is always a semi-algebraic set.

### Proof

**Step 1 — Reduction to the real setting.** Identify $\mathbb{C}^n \cong \mathbb
{R}^{2n}$ via $z_j = x_j + i\,y_j$. Let $V \subseteq \mathbb{C}^n$ be a complex
algebraic set, defined by
$$f_1(z)=\cdots=f_k(z)=0,\qquad f_j\in\mathbb{C}[z_1,\dots,z_n].$$
Decompose each coefficient into real and imaginary parts, $f_j = g_j + i\,h_j$ w
ith $g_j,h_j\in\mathbb{R}[x_1,y_1,\dots,x_n,y_n]$. Then
$$f_j(z)=0 \iff g_j(x,y)=0\ \text{and}\ h_j(x,y)=0.$$
Hence $V$, viewed inside $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$, is the common real zero locus of the
 real polynomials $\{g_j,h_j\}_{j=1}^k$. Thus **$V$ is a real algebraic set**, a
nd in particular a **semi-algebraic** subset of $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$.

**Step 2 — The projection is a polynomial map.** A (linear) projection $\pi:\mat
hbb{C}^n\to\mathbb{C}^m$ with $m<n$ corresponds, under the identification $\math
bb{C}^m\cong\mathbb{R}^{2m}$, to a linear — hence polynomial — map $\tilde\pi:\m
athbb{R}^{2n}\to\mathbb{R}^{2m}$. The image $\pi(V)$ is exactly $\tilde\pi(V)\su
bseteq\mathbb{R}^{2m}$.

**Step 3 — Apply Tarski–Seidenberg.** The Tarski–Seidenberg theorem states that
the image of a semi-algebraic set under a polynomial map is semi-algebraic (equi
valently, the projection of a semi-algebraic set in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$ onto a coor
dinate subspace is semi-algebraic). Since $V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2n}$ is semi-al
gebraic (Step 1) and $\tilde\pi$ is polynomial (Step 2),
$$\pi(V)=\tilde\pi(V)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2m}$$
is **semi-algebraic**.

**Remark (why "semi-algebraic" and not "algebraic").** The projection need *not*
 be Zariski-closed, so it is not in general a complex algebraic set. For example
, the projection of the algebraic set $V(xy-1)\subseteq\mathbb{C}^2$ onto the fi
rst coordinate is $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$, which is Zariski-open but not Zari
ski-closed. Chevalley's theorem tells us the image is *constructible* (a finite
union of locally closed sets in the Zariski topology); every constructible set,
when viewed over $\mathbb{R}$, is semi-algebraic (Zariski-closed $\Rightarrow$ r
eal algebraic $\Rightarrow$ semi-algebraic; complements and finite unions preser
ve semi-algebraicity). This is consistent with the Tarski–Seidenberg conclusion
above.

### Conclusion

The projection of a complex algebraic set onto a lower-dimensional space is alwa
ys a semi-algebraic set. The key mechanism is: a complex algebraic set is a real
 algebraic (hence semi-algebraic) set under $\mathbb{C}^n\cong\mathbb{R}^{2n}$,
and the Tarski–Seidenberg theorem guarantees that polynomial images — in particu
lar linear projections — of semi-algebraic sets remain semi-algebraic.
