## Solution

We seek the positive real number $\alpha$ such that

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n^n\,\alpha^n\,\sqrt{2\pi n}}{n!}=1.$$

### Using Stirling's formula

Stirling's approximation gives

$$n! = \sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^n\left(1+\frac{1}{12n}+O(n^{-2})\ri
ght).$$

Substituting this into the expression:

$$\frac{n^n\,\alpha^n\,\sqrt{2\pi n}}{n!} = \frac{n^n\,\alpha^n\,\sqrt{2\pi n}}{
\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^n\left(1+\frac{1}{12n}+O(n^{-2})\right)}.$
$

The $\sqrt{2\pi n}$ factors cancel, and $n^n / (n/e)^n = n^n \cdot e^n / n^n = e
^n$, so

$$= \frac{\alpha^n\, e^n}{1+\frac{1}{12n}+O(n^{-2})} = \frac{(\alpha e)^n}{1+\fr
ac{1}{12n}+O(n^{-2})}.$$

### Analyzing the limit

The denominator $1+\frac{1}{12n}+O(n^{-2}) \to 1$ as $n\to\infty$. Therefore the
 entire limit equals

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}(\alpha e)^n.$$

- If $\alpha e > 1$, then $(\alpha e)^n \to \infty$.
- If $\alpha e < 1$, then $(\alpha e)^n \to 0$.
- If $\alpha e = 1$, then $(\alpha e)^n = 1$ for all $n$, and the limit is $1$.

For the limit to equal $1$, we must have $\alpha e = 1$, i.e.,

$$\boxed{\alpha = \frac{1}{e}}.$$

### Verification

With $\alpha = 1/e$, the expression becomes

$$\frac{n^n\,e^{-n}\,\sqrt{2\pi n}}{n!} = \frac{\sqrt{2\pi n}\,(n/e)^n}{n!}.$$

By Stirling's formula this equals $\dfrac{1}{1+\frac{1}{12n}+O(n^{-2})}\to 1$, c
onfirming the result.
