# Normal Inertial Frame (Normal Geodesic Coordinates) via the Exponential Map

## Definition

Let \(M\) be a smooth \(n\)-manifold with a **symmetric** (torsion‑free) affine
connection \(\nabla\) (so \(\Gamma^{i}{}_{jk}=\Gamma^{i}{}_{kj}\) in any chart),
 and let \(p_{0}\in M\). A **normal coordinate system** (also called a *normal g
eodesic coordinate system* or *normal inertial frame*) at \(p_{0}\) is a chart \
((V,x^{1},\dots,x^{n})\) centered at \(p_{0}\) (i.e. \(x^{i}(p_{0})=0\)) with th
e defining property

\[
\Gamma^{i}{}_{jk}(p_{0})=0\qquad\text{for all }i,j,k .
\]

Equivalently, in this frame the connection coefficients — and hence the "fictiti
ous forces" / geodesic acceleration terms — vanish *at the single point* \(p_{0}
\). This is the differential‑geometric analogue of a freely falling (inertial) r
eference frame in general relativity: at one event one can choose coordinates in
 which gravity is locally eliminated.

## Construction via the Exponential Map

**Step 1 — Pick a linear frame at \(p_{0}\).** Choose a basis \(\{e_{1},\dots,e_
{n}\}\) of the tangent space \(T_{p_{0}}M\). It identifies \(T_{p_{0}}M\cong\mat
hbb{R}^{n}\) via \(v=v^{i}e_{i}\mapsto (v^{1},\dots,v^{n})\).

**Step 2 — Define the exponential map.** For each \(v\in T_{p_{0}}M\), let \(\ga
mma_{v}(t)\) be the unique geodesic satisfying
\[
\gamma_{v}(0)=p_{0},\qquad \dot\gamma_{v}(0)=v .
\]
By the existence/uniqueness theorem for the geodesic ODE, there is an open neigh
borhood \(U\subset T_{p_{0}}M\) of the origin, which we may take **star‑shaped**
 (closed under \(v\mapsto tv\) for \(|t|\le 1\)), on which every \(\gamma_{v}(1)
\) is defined. The **exponential map** is
\[
\exp_{p_{0}}:U\longrightarrow M,\qquad \exp_{p_{0}}(v):=\gamma_{v}(1).
\]

**Step 3 — It is a local diffeomorphism at the origin.** Compute its differentia
l at \(0\). For \(v=te_{i}\),
\[
\exp_{p_{0}}(te_{i})=\gamma_{e_{i}}(t)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(d\exp_{p_{0}})_{0}(e_{i})=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\gamma_{e_{i}}(t)=e_{i
}.
\]
Thus \((d\exp_{p_{0}})_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{T_{p_{0}}M}\), which is invertible. By t
he inverse function theorem, shrinking \(U\) if necessary, \(\exp_{p_{0}}:U\to V
:=\exp_{p_{0}}(U)\) is a diffeomorphism onto a neighborhood \(V\) of \(p_{0}\) i
n \(M\).

**Step 4 — Pull back the linear coordinates.** Define the chart on \(V\) by
\[
x^{i}:V\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\qquad x^{i}(q):=v^{i}\quad\text{where }q=\exp_
{p_{0}}(v^{i}e_{i}),
\]
i.e. \((x^{1},\dots,x^{n})=(\text{linear coords on }T_{p_{0}}M)\circ(\exp_{p_{0}
}|_{U})^{-1}\). This is the **normal coordinate system** at \(p_{0}\) associated
 to the frame \(\{e_{i}\}\).

## Verification: the Connection Vanishes at \(p_{0}\)

We now show \(\Gamma^{i}{}_{jk}(p_{0})=0\), which is the property that earns the
 name "inertial frame."

**Geodesics through the origin are straight lines.** For any \(v=v^{i}e_{i}\in U
\), the reparametrized curve \(t\mapsto\exp_{p_{0}}(tv)=\gamma_{v}(t)\) is a geo
desic. In normal coordinates its coordinate expression is, by definition,
\[
x^{i}(\gamma_{v}(t))=tv^{i},
\]
i.e. every geodesic through \(p_{0}\) is a **radial straight line** \(x^{i}(t)=t
v^{i}\) through the origin.

**Plug into the geodesic equation.** The geodesic equation in coordinates is
\[
\frac{d^{2}x^{i}}{dt^{2}}+\Gamma^{i}{}_{jk}(x(t))\,\frac{dx^{j}}{dt}\frac{dx^{k}
}{dt}=0.
\]
For \(x^{i}(t)=tv^{i}\) we have \(\ddot x^{i}=0\) and \(\dot x^{j}=v^{j}\), henc
e
\[
\Gamma^{i}{}_{jk}(t v)\,v^{j}v^{k}=0\qquad\text{for all }v\in U\text{ and }|t|\l
e 1.
\]
Setting \(t=0\) (so \(x=p_{0}\)):
\[
\Gamma^{i}{}_{jk}(p_{0})\,v^{j}v^{k}=0\qquad\text{for all }v\in T_{p_{0}}M. \tag
{$\ast$}
\]

**Use symmetry to polarize.** Equation \((\ast)\) says the quadratic form \(Q^{i
}(v):=\Gamma^{i}{}_{jk}(p_{0})v^{j}v^{k}\) vanishes identically. Because the con
nection is **symmetric**, \(\Gamma^{i}{}_{jk}(p_{0})=\Gamma^{i}{}_{kj}(p_{0})\),
 so \(Q^{i}\) is a genuine quadratic form with symmetric coefficient matrix. Pol
arizing:
\[
0=Q^{i}(v+w)=Q^{i}(v)+Q^{i}(w)+2\,\Gamma^{i}{}_{jk}(p_{0})\,v^{j}w^{k},
\]
so \(\Gamma^{i}{}_{jk}(p_{0})\,v^{j}w^{k}=0\) for all \(v,w\), giving
\[
\boxed{\;\Gamma^{i}{}_{jk}(p_{0})=0\quad\forall\,i,j,k.\;}
\]

*Remark on the role of symmetry.* Without torsion‑freeness, \((\ast)\) only forc
es the *symmetric part* \(\Gamma^{i}{}_{(jk)}(p_{0})\) to vanish; the antisymmet
ric part \(\Gamma^{i}{}_{[jk]}(p_{0})=\tfrac12 T^{i}{}_{jk}(p_{0})\) (the torsio
n) survives. Thus a symmetric connection is exactly what is needed to kill **all
** connection coefficients at a single point — the precise sense in which a norm
al frame is "inertial."

## Summary of Properties

1. **Origin:** \(x^{i}(p_{0})=0\).
2. **Radial geodesics are linear:** geodesics emanating from \(p_{0}\) have coor
dinate form \(x^{i}(t)=tv^{i}\).
3. **Vanishing connection at the point:** \(\Gamma^{i}{}_{jk}(p_{0})=0\) (uses s
ymmetry of \(\nabla\)).
4. **First‑order metric property (Riemannian case):** if \(\nabla\) is the Levi‑
Civita connection, additionally \(\partial_{k}g_{ij}(p_{0})=0\), i.e. the metric
 is Euclidean to first order at \(p_{0}\) — the "Einstein equivalence principle"
 form.
5. **Curvature is not eliminated:** only the *first* derivatives of the metric /
 connection vanish; second derivatives encode the Riemann tensor, so tidal effec
ts remain. The frame is inertial at \(p_{0}\) but not in a neighborhood.
