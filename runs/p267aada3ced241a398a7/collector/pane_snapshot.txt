## Solution

**Setup.** Let $a_k^{(r)}$ denote the number of packets with exactly $k$ priorit
y bits after round $r$. Initially $a_0^{(0)} = 2^n + 6$ and all others are $0$.

**Recurrence.** In each round, packets at level $k$ pair up: $\lfloor a_k^{(r)}/
2 \rfloor$ pairs form, and $a_k^{(r)} \bmod 2$ packets skip. Each pair sends one
 packet to level $k+1$ (winner) and keeps one at level $k$ (loser). Skippers sta
y at $k$. Thus:

$$a_k^{(r+1)} = \left\lceil \frac{a_k^{(r)}}{2}\right\rceil + \left\lfloor \frac
{a_{k-1}^{(r)}}{2}\right\rfloor$$

**Decomposition.** Write $a_k^{(r)} = \binom{r}{k}\, 2^{n-r} + e_k^{(r)}$, where
 the first term is the "ideal" distribution (valid when $N = 2^n$, all counts ev
en) and $e_k^{(r)}$ is the perturbation from the extra $6$ packets. Since $n > 7
 \geq r$, every $\binom{r}{k}\,2^{n-r}$ is even, so the parity of $a_k^{(r)}$ is
 governed by $e_k^{(r)}$.

Define $s_k^{(r)} = e_k^{(r)} \bmod 2 \in \{0,1\}$. Using $\lceil a/2\rceil = (a
 + s)/2$ and $\lfloor a/2\rfloor = (a - s)/2$ where $s = a \bmod 2$, and the Pas
cal identity $\binom{r}{k}+\binom{r}{k-1} = \binom{r+1}{k}$, we obtain:

$$e_k^{(r+1)} = \frac{e_k^{(r)} + e_{k-1}^{(r)} + s_k^{(r)} - s_{k-1}^{(r)}}{2}$
$

The numerator is always even (since $e_k + s_k$ and $e_{k-1} - s_{k-1}$ are each
 even), so $e_k^{(r+1)}$ is always an integer.

**Tracking the perturbation.** Starting from $e_0^{(0)} = 6$, all others $0$:

| $r$ | $e_0$ | $e_1$ | $e_2$ | $e_3$ | $e_4$ | $e_5$ | $e_6$ | $e_7$ | Sum |
|-----|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-----|
| 0   | 6     | 0     | 0     | 0     | 0     | 0     | 0     | 0     | 6   |
| 1   | 3     | 3     | 0     | 0     | 0     | 0     | 0     | 0     | 6   |
| 2   | 2     | 3     | 1     | 0     | 0     | 0     | 0     | 0     | 6   |
| 3   | 1     | 3     | 2     | 0     | 0     | 0     | 0     | 0     | 6   |
| 4   | 1     | 2     | 2     | 1     | 0     | 0     | 0     | 0     | 6   |
| 5   | 1     | 1     | 2     | 2     | 0     | 0     | 0     | 0     | 6   |
| 6   | 1     | 1     | 1     | 2     | 1     | 0     | 0     | 0     | 6   |
| 7   | 1     | 1     | 1     | 1     | **2** | 0     | 0     | 0     | 6   |

Each row sums to $6$ (conservation of packets), confirming correctness.

**Verification with small case.** For $n=3$ ($N=14$), the formula gives $a_0^{(3
)}=2, a_1^{(3)}=6, a_2^{(3)}=5, a_3^{(3)}=1$, which matches direct simulation of
 the pairing process, validating the decomposition.

**Final answer.** After $7$ rounds, the number of packets with exactly $4$ prior
ity bits is:

$$a_4^{(7)} = \binom{7}{4}\, 2^{n-7} + e_4^{(7)} = 35 \cdot 2^{n-7} + 2$$

The total number of data packets with exactly $4$ priority bits is $\boxed{35 \c
dot 2^{n-7} + 2}$.
