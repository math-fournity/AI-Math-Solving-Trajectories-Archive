## Solution

**The function and its singularity.** We have $f(z) = \dfrac{e^{1/z}}{z^3}$. The
 only singularity is at $z = 0$ (where $e^{1/z}$ has an essential singularity an
d $1/z^3$ has a pole of order 3).

**Location of the singularity relative to the contour.** The rectangle has verti
ces $1,\,-1,\,1+2i,\,-1+2i$. Its interior is $\{x+iy : -1<x<1,\; 0<y<2\}$. The p
oint $z=0$ lies on the bottom edge (the segment from $-1$ to $1$ on the real axi
s). We treat $z=0$ as enclosed by the contour (the standard interpretation for t
his problem; equivalently, perturb the bottom edge infinitesimally below the rea
l axis so $z=0$ is interior — the result is unchanged).

**Laurent expansion of $f$ about $z=0$.** Using the Taylor series $e^w = \sum_{n
=0}^{\infty} \frac{w^n}{n!}$ with $w = 1/z$:

$$e^{1/z} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n!\, z^n}.$$

Dividing by $z^3$:

$$f(z) = \frac{e^{1/z}}{z^3} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n!\, z^{n+3}} = \fra
c{1}{z^3} + \frac{1}{z^4} + \frac{1}{2!\,z^5} + \frac{1}{3!\,z^6} + \cdots$$

**Computing the residue.** The residue is the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ in the Lau
rent expansion. We need $n+3 = 1$, i.e., $n = -2$. Since the sum runs over $n \g
eq 0$, **no term produces $z^{-1}$**. Therefore:

$$\operatorname{Res}(f,\, 0) = 0.$$

**Applying the residue theorem.** The only singularity of $f$ is at $z=0$, which
 is enclosed by the contour. By the residue theorem:

$$\oint_C f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}(f,\, 0) = 2\pi i \cdot 0 =
0.$$

**Verification via term-by-term integration.** Each term $\frac{1}{n!\,z^{n+3}}$
 integrated over a closed contour enclosing the origin gives:

$$\oint_C \frac{dz}{z^{n+3}} = \begin{cases} 2\pi i & \text{if } n+3 = 1 \;(n=-2
),\\ 0 & \text{if } n+3 \neq 1. \end{cases}$$

Since $n \geq 0$, we always have $n+3 \geq 3 \neq 1$, so every term integrates t
o $0$. The full integral is the sum of zeros:

$$\oint_C \frac{e^{1/z}}{z^3}\,dz = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n!}\oint_C \fra
c{dz}{z^{n+3}} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} 0 = 0.$$

(The interchange of sum and integral is justified on the contour, where $|z| \ge
q \delta > 0$ away from the singularity, ensuring uniform convergence.)

## Result

$$\boxed{\oint_C \frac{e^{1/z}}{z^3}\,dz = 0}$$
