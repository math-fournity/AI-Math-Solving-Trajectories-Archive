## Proof

We evaluate the contour integral

$$\int_{\partial D_1(0)} \frac{e^{z}}{z}\,dz,$$

where $\partial D_1(0)$ is the positively oriented boundary of the unit disk cen
tered at the origin.

**Cauchy's Integral Formula.** Let $f$ be holomorphic on a simply connected doma
in $\Omega$, and let $D_r(a)\subset \Omega$ be a disk with positively oriented b
oundary $\partial D_r(a)$. Then for every $a\in D_r(a)$,

$$f(a)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial D_r(a)}\frac{f(z)}{z-a}\,dz.$$

**Application.** Set $f(z)=e^{z}$, which is entire (holomorphic on all of $\math
bb{C}$), and take $a=0$, $r=1$. The hypotheses are satisfied since $D_1(0)\subse
t \mathbb{C}$ and $f$ is holomorphic there. Cauchy's Integral Formula gives

$$f(0)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial D_1(0)}\frac{e^{z}}{z}\,dz.$$

Since $f(0)=e^{0}=1$, we obtain

$$\int_{\partial D_1(0)}\frac{e^{z}}{z}\,dz = 2\pi i\cdot f(0)=2\pi i\cdot 1 = 2
\pi i.$$

**Verification via direct parametrization.** Parametrize $\partial D_1(0)$ by $\
gamma(t)=e^{it}$, $t\in[0,2\pi]$, so $\gamma'(t)=ie^{it}$. Then

$$\int_{\partial D_1(0)}\frac{e^{z}}{z}\,dz=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{e^{e^{it}}}{e^{
it}}\,ie^{it}\,dt=i\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{e^{it}}\,dt.$$

Expanding $e^{e^{it}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{int}}{n!}$ and integrating ter
mwise (uniform convergence on $[0,2\pi]$),

$$i\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{int}}{n!}\,dt=i\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
\frac{1}{n!}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{int}\,dt.$$

For $n\ge 1$, $\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{int}\,dt=0$; for $n=0$, $\int_{0}^{2\pi}1\,dt=2
\pi$. Hence only the $n=0$ term survives:

$$i\cdot\frac{1}{0!}\cdot 2\pi = 2\pi i.$$

Both methods agree.

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{\partial D_1(0)}\frac{e^{z}}{z}\,dz = 2\pi i.}$$
