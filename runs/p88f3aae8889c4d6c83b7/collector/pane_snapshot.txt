## Solution

**Setup.** Let $k = \lfloor \sqrt[3]{n} \rfloor$, so $k^3 \le n \le (k+1)^3 - 1$
. We need:
1. $k^5 \mid n^2$ (Processable), and
2. $n$ is not a perfect cube.

**Key reduction.** Write $d = \gcd(n,k)$, $n = dm$, $k = ds$ with $\gcd(m,s)=1$.
 Then $k^5 \mid n^2$ becomes $d^5 s^5 \mid d^2 m^2$, i.e., $d^3 s^5 \mid m^2$. S
ince $\gcd(m,s)=1$, we need $s^5 \mid m^2$, forcing $s=1$. **Therefore $k \mid n
$.**

Write $n = kq$. Then $k^5 \mid k^2 q^2$ gives $k^3 \mid q^2$.

**Factoring $k$.** Write $k = u v^2$ with $u$ squarefree. For each prime $p^a \|
 k$, we need $v_p(q) \ge \lceil 3a/2 \rceil$. The minimal such $q$ is $q_0 = u^2
 v^3$, so $q = u^2 v^3 \cdot t$ and:
$$n = k \cdot q = u v^2 \cdot u^2 v^3 \cdot t = u^3 v^5 \, t.$$

**Range constraint.** From $k^2 \le q < (k+1)^3/k$:
$$v \le t \le \left\lfloor \frac{(uv^2+1)^3 - 1}{u^3 v^5} \right\rfloor = \left\
lfloor v + \frac{3}{uv} + \frac{3}{u^2 v^3} \right\rfloor =: t_{\max}.$$

For $uv \ge 4$: the fractional part is $< 1$, so $t_{\max} = v$, forcing $t = v$
. But then $n = u^3 v^6 = (uv^2)^3 = k^3$, a perfect cube — **excluded**. So no
valid $n$ exists when $uv \ge 4$.

**Only $uv \le 3$ can work.** The pairs $(u,v)$ with $u$ squarefree and $uv \le
3$:

| $u$ | $v$ | $k=uv^2$ | $t$ range | $t_{\max}$ | Non-cube $t$ (exclude $t=vw^3$
) | Best $n=u^3v^5t$ |
|-----|-----|-----------|-----------|------------|------------------------------
----|-------------------|
| 1 | 1 | 1 | $[1,7]$ | 7 | $t \neq 1,8$: best $t=7$ | $7$ |
| 2 | 1 | 2 | $[1,3]$ | 3 | $t \neq 1,8$: best $t=3$ | $24$ |
| 3 | 1 | 3 | $[1,3]$ | 3 | $t \neq 1,8$: best $t=3$ | $81$ |
| 1 | 2 | 4 | $[2,3]$ | 3 | $t \neq 2,16$: best $t=3$ | $96$ |
| 1 | 3 | 9 | $[3,4]$ | 4 | $t \neq 3,24$: best $t=4$ | $972$ |

**Verification of $n = 972$:**
- $\lfloor \sqrt[3]{972} \rfloor = 9$ (since $9^3 = 729 \le 972 < 1000 = 10^3$).
 ✓
- $9^5 = 59049$ and $972^2 = 944784 = 59049 \times 16$. So $k^5 \mid n^2$. ✓
- $972$ is not a perfect cube ($9^3 = 729$, $10^3 = 1000$). ✓

**No larger $n$ exists** because for every $k \ge 5$ with $k \neq 9$, we have $u
v \ge 4$, which forces $n = k^3$ (a perfect cube, excluded). The only $k$ values
 yielding valid non-cube $n$ are $k \in \{1,2,3,4,9\}$, giving candidates $n \in
 \{7, 24, 81, 96, 972\}$.

The largest possible integer quality score is $\boxed{972}$.
