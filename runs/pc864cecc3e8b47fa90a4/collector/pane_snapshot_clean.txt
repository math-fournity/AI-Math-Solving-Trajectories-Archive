Looking at this problem, I need to find all non-empty sets $S$ of positive integ
ers where $\frac{i+j}{\gcd(i,j)} \in S$ for all $i,j \in S$, then compute the re
quired sum.

## Key Observation

For any $i = j \in S$: $\frac{i+j}{\gcd(i,j)} = \frac{2i}{i} = 2 \in S$. So **ev
ery** $S \in \mathcal{F}$ contains 2, meaning $m(S) \in \{1, 2\}$.

## Case 1: $1 \in S$

From $1, 1 \to 2$; from $1, 2 \to 3$; from $1, 3 \to 4$; by induction $S = \math
bb{Z}^+$. This is valid since $\frac{i+j}{\gcd(i,j)}$ is always a positive integ
er.

## Case 2: $S = \{2\}$

Check: $\frac{2+2}{\gcd(2,2)} = 2 \in S$. ✓ Valid.

## Case 3: $1 \notin S$, $S$ contains some element $n > 2$

**Subcase 3a: $n$ is odd, $n \geq 3$.** From $2, n$ (gcd = 1): $n+2 \in S$. Repe
ating gives all odd numbers $\geq n$ in $S$. Since $3n$ is odd and $3n \geq 9 \g
eq n$, we have $3n \in S$. Then:
$$n, 3n \to \frac{n + 3n}{\gcd(n, 3n)} = \frac{4n}{n} = 4 \in S$$
$$2, 4 \to \frac{6}{2} = 3 \in S$$

**Subcase 3b: $n = 2k$ is even, $k \geq 2$.** From $2, 2k$ (gcd = 2): $k+1 \in S
$. If $k+1$ is odd and $\geq 3$, reduce to Subcase 3a. If $k+1$ is even, set $k_
1 = (k+1)/2$ and repeat. The sequence $k_i$ is strictly decreasing (for $k_i \ge
q 3$) and stays $\geq 2$, terminating when some $k_i$ is even, yielding the odd
number $k_i + 1 \geq 3$. This always reaches an odd number $\geq 3$, reducing to
 Subcase 3a.

**From $\{2, 3\}$, all integers $\geq 2$ are generated.** Direct computation ver
ifies $\{2, 3, \ldots, 14\} \subset S$:
- $2,3 \to 5$; $3,5 \to 8$; $2,5 \to 7$; $2,7 \to 9$; $3,9 \to 4$; $2,4 \to 3$;
$3,7 \to 10$; $2,10 \to 6$; $2,9 \to 11$; $5,7 \to 12$; $4,9 \to 13$; $5,9 \to 1
4$.

**Induction for $n \geq 15$:** Assume $\{2, \ldots, n-1\} \subset S$.
- **$n$ odd:** $2, (n{-}2) \to n$ since $\gcd(2, n{-}2) = 1$ and $n{-}2 \geq 13
\in S$.
- **$n$ even ($n \geq 16$):** Choose $a \in \{2, \ldots, n{-}2\}$ with $\gcd(a,
n) = 1$. Then $a, (n{-}a) \to n$ since $\gcd(a, n{-}a) = \gcd(a, n) = 1$, with b
oth $a, n{-}a \in S$ by induction. Such $a$ exists because $\phi(n) \geq 6$ for
all even $n \geq 16$ (verified: minimum is $\phi(18)=6$), so at least $\phi(n)-2
 \geq 4$ integers in $\{2, \ldots, n{-}2\}$ are coprime to $n$.

Therefore $S \supseteq \{2, 3, 4, \ldots\}$. Since $1 \notin S$, we get $S = \{2
, 3, 4, \ldots\}$. This set is valid: for $i, j \geq 2$, $\frac{i+j}{\gcd(i,j)}
\geq 2$ always.

## Exhaustiveness

Since $2 \in S$ always, $m(S) \in \{1, 2\}$. If $m = 1$: $S = \mathbb{Z}^+$. If
$m = 2$: either $S = \{2\}$, or $S$ contains some $n > 2$ forcing $S = \{2, 3, 4
, \ldots\}$. No other sets exist.
