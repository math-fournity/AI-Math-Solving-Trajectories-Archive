## Solution

Consider the Volterra integral equation of the second kind

$$
\varphi(x)=f(x)+\lambda\int_{a}^{x}K(x,t)\,\varphi(t)\,dt,
$$

with kernel $K(x,t)\equiv 1$. The **resolvent kernel** $R(x,t;\lambda)$ is defin
ed so that the solution can be written as

$$
\varphi(x)=f(x)+\lambda\int_{a}^{x}R(x,t;\lambda)\,f(t)\,dt.
$$

It is given by the Neumann series (iterated kernels):

$$
R(x,t;\lambda)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{\,n-1}K_{n}(x,t),
$$

where $K_{1}(x,t)=K(x,t)$ and the iterated kernels satisfy the recursion

$$
K_{n+1}(x,t)=\int_{t}^{x}K(x,s)\,K_{n}(s,t)\,ds,\qquad t\le x.
$$

### Computing the iterated kernels

Since $K(x,t)\equiv 1$:

- $K_{1}(x,t)=1$.
- $K_{2}(x,t)=\displaystyle\int_{t}^{x}1\cdot 1\,ds=x-t$.
- $K_{3}(x,t)=\displaystyle\int_{t}^{x}1\cdot(s-t)\,ds=\frac{(x-t)^{2}}{2}$.
- $K_{4}(x,t)=\displaystyle\int_{t}^{x}1\cdot\frac{(s-t)^{2}}{2}\,ds=\frac{(x-t)
^{3}}{3!}$.

By induction, assume $K_{n}(x,t)=\dfrac{(x-t)^{\,n-1}}{(n-1)!}$. Then

$$
K_{n+1}(x,t)=\int_{t}^{x}\frac{(s-t)^{\,n-1}}{(n-1)!}\,ds
=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\cdot\frac{(x-t)^{n}}{n}
=\frac{(x-t)^{n}}{n!}.
$$

Hence for all $n\ge 1$,

$$
K_{n}(x,t)=\frac{(x-t)^{\,n-1}}{(n-1)!}.
$$

### Summing the series

Substituting into the Neumann series:

$$
R(x,t;\lambda)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{\,n-1}\frac{(x-t)^{\,n-1}}{(n-1)!}
=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\bigl(\lambda(x-t)\bigr)^{m}}{m!}
=e^{\,\lambda(x-t)}.
$$

The series converges for every finite $\lambda$ (entire function), so the resolv
ent is well defined for all $\lambda$.

### Verification

The resolvent must satisfy the resolvent equation

$$
R(x,t;\lambda)=K(x,t)+\lambda\int_{t}^{x}K(x,s)\,R(s,t;\lambda)\,ds.
$$

With $K\equiv 1$ and $R=e^{\lambda(x-t)}$:

$$
\text{RHS}=1+\lambda\int_{t}^{x}e^{\lambda(s-t)}\,ds
=1+\lambda\cdot\frac{e^{\lambda(x-t)}-1}{\lambda}
=1+e^{\lambda(x-t)}-1
=e^{\lambda(x-t)}=\text{LHS}. \checkmark
$$

The identity holds, confirming the result.

### Conclusion

The resolvent kernel of the Volterra integral equation with kernel $K(x,t)\equiv
 1$ is

$$
\boxed{R(x,t;\lambda)=e^{\,\lambda(x-t)}}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{e^{\,\lambda(x-t)}}$
