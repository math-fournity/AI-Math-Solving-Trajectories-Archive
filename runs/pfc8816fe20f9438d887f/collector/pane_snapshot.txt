## Proof

**Claim:** $P\!\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty} C_n\right) = 1$.

**Step 1: The events $C_n$ are independent.**

Since $(X_n)$ is a sequence of independent random variables and $C_n = \{|X_n| \
geq nK\}$ is determined by $X_n$ alone, the events $(C_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ are
independent.

**Step 2: A tail-sum identity for non-negative random variables.**

For any non-negative random variable $Y$,
$$E[Y] = \int_0^\infty P(Y \geq t)\,dt.$$
Moreover, by comparing the integral with Riemann sums over $[n-1,n]$ and $[n,n+1
]$:
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P(Y \geq n) \;\leq\; \int_0^\infty P(Y \geq t)\,dt \;\leq\
; \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} P(Y \geq n) = 1 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P(Y \geq n).$$
(The left inequality uses $P(Y\geq t)\geq P(Y\geq n)$ for $t\in[n-1,n]$; the rig
ht uses $P(Y\geq t)\leq P(Y\geq n)$ for $t\in[n,n+1]$.)

Consequently,
$$E[Y] < \infty \iff \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P(Y \geq n) < \infty.$$

**Step 3: Apply to $Y = |X_1|/K$.**

Since $K > 0$ is a fixed finite constant and $E[|X_1|] = \infty$ (the variables
are not integrable),
$$E[Y] = \frac{E[|X_1|]}{K} = \infty.$$
By the equivalence from Step 2 (contrapositive),
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P(Y \geq n) = \infty.$$
But $P(Y \geq n) = P(|X_1| \geq nK) = P(C_n)$ (the $X_n$ are identically distrib
uted, so $P(|X_n|\geq nK)=P(|X_1|\geq nK)$). Hence
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P(C_n) = \infty.$$

**Step 4: Conclude via the second Borel–Cantelli lemma.**

The events $(C_n)$ are independent (Step 1) and $\sum_{n} P(C_n) = \infty$ (Step
 3). By the second Borel–Cantelli lemma,
$$P\!\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty} C_n\right) = 1. \qquad\blacksquare$$
