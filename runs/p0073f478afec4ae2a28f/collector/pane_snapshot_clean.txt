**Answer: No.**

**Proof.** We provide a counterexample.

Let $G = \mathbb{T} = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ be the circle group, which is a com
pact (hence Hausdorff) topological group under addition modulo $1$. Choose $b =
\alpha \in \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ where $\alpha$ is irrational (e.g., $\alpha =
\sqrt{2} \bmod 1$).

The cyclic subgroup generated by $b$ is
$$\langle b \rangle = \{n\alpha \bmod 1 : n \in \mathbb{Z}\}.$$

By **Kronecker's theorem** (equidistribution of $\{n\alpha\}$ modulo $1$ for irr
ational $\alpha$), the set $\langle b \rangle$ is **dense** in $\mathbb{T}$.

Now let $U$ be **any** open neighborhood of $b$ in $\mathbb{T}$. Since $\langle
b \rangle$ is dense in $\mathbb{T}$, the intersection $U \cap \langle b \rangle$
 is dense in $U$, hence in particular **infinite** (a nonempty open set in a Hau
sdorff space without isolated points cannot have a finite dense subset, and $\ma
thbb{T}$ has no isolated points).

Therefore, there exists **no** open neighborhood $U$ of $b$ such that $U \cap \l
angle b \rangle$ is finite.

**Remark.** The answer would be "Yes" when $b$ has finite order (since $\langle
b\rangle$ is then finite, and any neighborhood works), or more generally when $\
langle b \rangle$ is discrete in the subspace topology. But the question asks wh
ether this holds for **every** $b$ in **every** Hausdorff topological group, and
 the irrational-rotation example on the circle group shows it does not.
