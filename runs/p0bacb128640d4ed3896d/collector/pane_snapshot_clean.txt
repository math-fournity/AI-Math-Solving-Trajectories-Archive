## Proof

Define $Q(x) = xP(x) - 1$. Since $\deg P = 1996$, we have $\deg Q = 1997$.

For each $n = 1, 2, \ldots, 1997$:
$$Q(n) = n \cdot P(n) - 1 = n \cdot \frac{1}{n} - 1 = 0.$$

So $Q$ has roots $1, 2, \ldots, 1997$ (1997 roots, matching its degree), hence
$$Q(x) = c\,(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-1997)$$
for some constant $c$.

**Finding $c$.** Evaluate at $x = 0$:
$$Q(0) = 0 \cdot P(0) - 1 = -1,$$
$$Q(0) = c\,(-1)(-2)\cdots(-1997) = c\,(-1)^{1997}\cdot 1997! = -c\cdot 1997!.$$
Thus $-c\cdot 1997! = -1$, giving $c = \dfrac{1}{1997!}$.

**Evaluating $P(1998)$.**
$$Q(1998) = 1998\cdot P(1998) - 1,$$
$$Q(1998) = c\,(1997)(1996)\cdots(1) = c\cdot 1997! = \frac{1}{1997!}\cdot 1997!
 = 1.$$

Therefore
$$1998\cdot P(1998) - 1 = 1 \implies 1998\cdot P(1998) = 2 \implies P(1998) = \f
rac{2}{1998} = \frac{1}{999}.$$

$$\boxed{P(1998) = \dfrac{1}{999}}$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
