## Solution

We want $\mathbb{E}[X_t] = \mathbb{E}[\sin(B_t)]$, where $B_t$ is a standard Bro
wnian motion.

**Key fact.** For a standard Brownian motion, $B_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, t)$, i.e.
 $B_t$ is Gaussian with mean $0$ and variance $t$. Its density is

$$
p_t(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{2t}\right).
$$

**Computing the expectation.**

$$
\mathbb{E}[\sin(B_t)] = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \sin(x)\, p_t(x)\, dx = \frac{1}
{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \sin(x)\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{2t}\right)
 dx.
$$

Now observe the symmetry of the integrand:

- $\sin(x)$ is an **odd** function: $\sin(-x) = -\sin(x)$.
- $\exp(-x^2/(2t))$ is an **even** function.

Therefore $\sin(x)\exp(-x^2/(2t))$ is an **odd** function of $x$. The integral o
f an odd function over the symmetric interval $(-\infty, \infty)$ is zero:

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \sin(x)\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{2t}\right) dx = 0.
$$

Hence

$$
\mathbb{E}[\sin(B_t)] = 0.
$$

**Verification via the characteristic function (optional cross-check).** The cha
racteristic function of $B_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,t)$ is

$$
\mathbb{E}[e^{iuB_t}] = e^{-t u^2/2}.
$$

Since $\sin(x) = \operatorname{Im}(e^{ix})$,

$$
\mathbb{E}[\sin(B_t)] = \operatorname{Im}\!\left(\mathbb{E}[e^{iB_t}]\right) = \
operatorname{Im}\!\left(e^{-t/2}\right) = 0,
$$

because $e^{-t/2}$ is purely real. This confirms the result.

**Conclusion.** For all $t \geqslant 0$,

$$
\boxed{\mathbb{E}[X_t] = \mathbb{E}[\sin(B_t)] = 0.}
$$
