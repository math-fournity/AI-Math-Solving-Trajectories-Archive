Looking at this problem, I need to find the probability that the white flare in
Hangar B is never selected before the process terminates.

## Proof

**Setup.** Hangar A has 5 red flares; Hangar B has 4 red + 1 white. At each step
, a hangar is chosen with probability 1/2, then a flare is drawn uniformly at ra
ndom from that hangar. The process ends when one hangar is emptied.

**Key observation.** The white flare is in B, so for it to *never* be selected,
Hangar A must be the one that empties first (if B empties, all 5 of its flares—i
ncluding white—were drawn). Moreover, every draw from B during the process must
pick a red flare.

**Decomposition.** The hangar-selection process is a sequence of independent fai
r coin flips (A or B). The process stops when either A or B accumulates 5 select
ions. If A is emptied first, then exactly $k$ draws from B occurred, where $0 \l
e k \le 4$ (B never reached 5). The probability that the 5th A-selection occurs
at step $5+k$ (with exactly $k$ B-selections among the first $5+k-1$ steps, and
A selected at step $5+k$) is:

$$\binom{5+k-1}{k}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{5+k}$$

This is the negative binomial probability. Since $k \le 4$, B never reaches 5, s
o A is indeed emptied first.

**Conditional probability of avoiding white.** Given that B is selected exactly
$k$ times, the probability that all $k$ draws from B are red (not white) is the
probability of drawing $k$ red flares without replacement from B's 5 flares (4 r
ed, 1 white):

$$\frac{\binom{4}{k}}{\binom{5}{k}} = \frac{4!\,(5-k)!}{5!\,(4-k)!} = \frac{5-k}
{5}$$

This elegant simplification holds for $0 \le k \le 4$ (and gives 0 for $k=5$, co
nsistent with the fact that emptying B requires drawing white).

**Combining.** The hangar choices and the flare choices within B are independent
, so:

$$P(\text{white never selected}) = \sum_{k=0}^{4} \binom{5+k-1}{k}\left(\frac{1}
{2}\right)^{5+k} \cdot \frac{5-k}{5}$$

**Computing each term:**

| $k$ | $\binom{4+k}{k}$ | $(1/2)^{5+k}$ | $\frac{5-k}{5}$ | Product |
|-----|------------------|----------------|-----------------|---------|
| 0 | 1 | $1/32$ | $1$ | $1/32 = 8/256$ |
| 1 | 5 | $1/64$ | $4/5$ | $4/64 = 1/16 = 16/256$ |
| 2 | 15 | $1/128$ | $3/5$ | $45/640 = 9/128 = 18/256$ |
| 3 | 35 | $1/256$ | $2/5$ | $70/1280 = 7/128 = 14/256$ |
| 4 | 70 | $1/512$ | $1/5$ | $70/2560 = 7/256$ |

**Sum:**

$$\frac{8 + 16 + 18 + 14 + 7}{256} = \frac{63}{256}$$

**Verification via dynamic programming.** Define $P(a,r)$ as the probability of
success from state where A has $a$ flares and B has $r$ red flares (white still
present). The recurrence is:

$$P(a,r) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,P(a-1,r) + \tfrac{r}{2(r+1)}\,P(a,r-1)$$

with $P(0,r)=1$ and $P(a,0) = (1/2)^a$. Computing the full table yields $P(5,4)
= 63/256$, confirming the result.

## Answer

$$\boxed{\dfrac{63}{256}}$$
