## Proof

We derive the stated solution by separation of variables and Fourier–Bessel expa
nsion.

### 1. Separation of variables

Seek $u(r,t)=R(r)T(t)$. Substituting into
$$u_{tt}=a^{2}\!\left(u_{rr}+\tfrac1r u_r\right)$$
gives
$$\frac{T''}{a^{2}T}=\frac{R''+\frac1r R'}{R}=-\lambda,\qquad \lambda>0,$$
where the sign is forced by the boundary condition $R(r_0)=0$ (a positive $\lamb
da$ yields oscillatory, non-trivial $R$; $\lambda\le 0$ gives only the trivial s
olution compatible with $R(r_0)=0$ and regularity at $r=0$).

**Radial (Bessel) equation.**
$$R''+\frac1r R'+\lambda R=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (rR')'+\lambda r R=0.$
$
This is Bessel's equation of order $0$; the solution regular at $r=0$ is
$$R(r)=J_0(\sqrt{\lambda}\,r).$$
The boundary condition $R(r_0)=0$ requires
$$J_0(\sqrt{\lambda}\,r_0)=0\;\Longrightarrow\;\sqrt{\lambda}\,r_0=\mu_n,$$
where $\mu_n$ ($n=1,2,\dots$) are the positive zeros of $J_0$. Hence
$$\lambda_n=\left(\frac{\mu_n}{r_0}\right)^{\!2},\qquad R_n(r)=J_0\!\left(\frac{
\mu_n r}{r_0}\right).$$

**Time equation.**
$$T''+a^{2}\lambda_n T=0\;\Longrightarrow\;T''+\left(\frac{a\mu_n}{r_0}\right)^{
\!2}T=0.$$
The initial condition $u_t(r,0)=0$ forces $T'_n(0)=0$, so
$$T_n(t)=\cos\!\left(\frac{a\mu_n t}{r_0}\right).$$

**General solution.**
$$u(r,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} c_n\,J_0\!\left(\frac{\mu_n r}{r_0}\right)\cos\!\le
ft(\frac{a\mu_n t}{r_0}\right).$$

### 2. Fourier–Bessel expansion of the initial profile

At $t=0$,
$$u(r,0)=A\!\left(1-\frac{r^2}{r_0^2}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} c_n\,J_0\!\left
(\frac{\mu_n r}{r_0}\right).$$

The functions $\{J_0(\mu_n r/r_0)\}$ are orthogonal on $[0,r_0]$ with weight $r$
:
$$\int_0^{r_0} r\,J_0\!\left(\frac{\mu_n r}{r_0}\right)J_0\!\left(\frac{\mu_m r}
{r_0}\right)dr=\frac{r_0^{2}}{2}\,J_1(\mu_n)^2\,\delta_{nm}.$$
Therefore
$$c_n=\frac{2}{r_0^{2}J_1(\mu_n)^2}\int_0^{r_0} r\,A\!\left(1-\frac{r^2}{r_0^2}\
right)J_0\!\left(\frac{\mu_n r}{r_0}\right)dr.$$

Set $x=r/r_0$ (so $r=r_0 x$, $dr=r_0\,dx$):
$$c_n=\frac{2A}{J_1(\mu_n)^2}\int_0^{1} x(1-x^2)\,J_0(\mu_n x)\,dx.$$

### 3. Evaluating the integral

Denote $\mu=\mu_n$ (so $J_0(\mu)=0$) and
$$\mathcal J(\mu)=\int_0^{1} x(1-x^2)J_0(\mu x)\,dx=\int_0^{1}xJ_0(\mu x)\,dx-\i
nt_0^{1}x^3 J_0(\mu x)\,dx.$$

**First term.** Using $\frac{d}{dx}\bigl[xJ_1(\mu x)\bigr]=\mu x J_0(\mu x)$,
$$\int_0^{1}xJ_0(\mu x)\,dx=\frac{J_1(\mu)}{\mu}.$$

**Second term.** From $\frac{d}{dx}\bigl[x^3 J_1(\mu x)\bigr]=2x^2 J_1(\mu x)+\m
u x^3 J_0(\mu x)$,
$$\mu\int_0^{1}x^3 J_0(\mu x)\,dx=\Bigl[x^3 J_1(\mu x)\Bigr]_0^{1}-2\int_0^{1}x^
2 J_1(\mu x)\,dx=J_1(\mu)-2\int_0^{1}x^2 J_1(\mu x)\,dx.$$
And from $\frac{d}{dx}\bigl[x^2 J_2(\mu x)\bigr]=\mu x^2 J_1(\mu x)$,
$$\int_0^{1}x^2 J_1(\mu x)\,dx=\frac{J_2(\mu)}{\mu}.$$
Hence
$$\int_0^{1}x^3 J_0(\mu x)\,dx=\frac{J_1(\mu)}{\mu}-\frac{2J_2(\mu)}{\mu^2}.$$

**Combine.**
$$\mathcal J(\mu)=\frac{J_1(\mu)}{\mu}-\left(\frac{J_1(\mu)}{\mu}-\frac{2J_2(\mu
)}{\mu^2}\right)=\frac{2J_2(\mu)}{\mu^2}.$$

**Use $J_0(\mu)=0$.** The recurrence $J_2(z)=\frac{2}{z}J_1(z)-J_0(z)$ gives, at
 $z=\mu$,
$$J_2(\mu)=\frac{2J_1(\mu)}{\mu}.$$
Therefore
$$\mathcal J(\mu_n)=\frac{2}{\mu_n^2}\cdot\frac{2J_1(\mu_n)}{\mu_n}=\frac{4J_1(\
mu_n)}{\mu_n^3}.$$

### 4. The coefficients

$$c_n=\frac{2A}{J_1(\mu_n)^2}\cdot\frac{4J_1(\mu_n)}{\mu_n^3}=\frac{8A}{\mu_n^3\
,J_1(\mu_n)}.$$

### 5. Final solution

Substituting back,
$$\boxed{\,u(r,t)=8A\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{J_0\!\left(\frac{\mu_n r}{r_0}\righ
t)}{\mu_n^3\,J_1(\mu_n)}\cos\!\left(\frac{a\mu_n t}{r_0}\right)\,},$$
where $\mu_n$ are the positive roots of $J_0(\mu)=0$.

**Verification of the conditions.**
- *PDE:* Each term $J_0(\mu_n r/r_0)\cos(a\mu_n t/r_0)$ satisfies $u_{tt}=a^2(u_
{rr}+\frac1r u_r)$ because $J_0$ solves Bessel's equation of order $0$ with $\la
mbda_n=(\mu_n/r_0)^2$ and $\cos$ solves $T''+(a\mu_n/r_0)^2T=0$.
- *Boundary:* $u(r_0,t)=\sum c_n J_0(\mu_n)\cos(\cdot)=0$ since $J_0(\mu_n)=0$.
- *Initial velocity:* $u_t(r,0)=\sum c_n J_0(\cdot)\cdot(-\frac{a\mu_n}{r_0})\si
n 0=0$.
- *Initial displacement:* $u(r,0)=\sum c_n J_0(\mu_n r/r_0)=A(1-r^2/r_0^2)$ by t
he Fourier–Bessel expansion just computed.

This is exactly the stated formula. $\blacksquare$
