## Part (a): Solution of the heat problem

**Setup.** The temperature \(u(r,\theta,\phi,t)\) in the unit ball with \(c=1\),
 no internal sources, surface held at 0°, satisfies
\[
u_t=\Delta u,\qquad 0\le r<1,\ t>0,
\]
\[
u(1,\theta,\phi,t)=0,\qquad u(r,\theta,\phi,0)=f(r,\theta,\phi)=r^2,
\]
with \(u\) bounded at the origin.

Since the initial data \(f=r^2\) depends only on \(r\), the solution is radial:
\(u=u(r,t)\). The Laplacian of a radial function in 3D is
\[
\Delta u=u_{rr}+\frac{2}{r}u_r.
\]

**Reduction to a 1D problem.** Set \(v(r,t)=r\,u(r,t)\). Then
\[
v_t=r\,u_t=r\,\Delta u=v_{rr},
\]
because \(v_{rr}=(ru)_{rr}=r\,u_{rr}+2u_r=r\bigl(u_{rr}+\tfrac{2}{r}u_r\bigr)=r\
,\Delta u\). The boundary and regularity conditions become
\[
v(0,t)=0\quad(\text{since }u\text{ bounded}),\qquad v(1,t)=0\quad(\text{since }u
(1,t)=0),
\]
and the initial condition is
\[
v(r,0)=r\cdot r^2=r^3,\qquad 0\le r\le 1.
\]

Thus \(v\) solves the standard one-dimensional heat equation on \([0,1]\) with h
omogeneous Dirichlet data.

**Eigenfunction expansion.** The eigenfunctions of \(-\partial_{rr}\) on \([0,1]
\) with Dirichlet conditions are \(\sin(n\pi r)\) with eigenvalues \((n\pi)^2\),
 \(n=1,2,\dots\). Hence
\[
v(r,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n\,e^{-(n\pi)^2 t}\sin(n\pi r),
\]
where
\[
b_n=2\int_0^1 r^3\sin(n\pi r)\,dr.
\]

**Computing \(b_n\).** With \(\alpha=n\pi\), repeated integration by parts gives
\[
\int_0^1 r^3\sin(\alpha r)\,dr
=\Bigl[-\frac{r^3\cos(\alpha r)}{\alpha}+\frac{3r^2\sin(\alpha r)}{\alpha^2}+\fr
ac{6r\cos(\alpha r)}{\alpha^3}-\frac{6\sin(\alpha r)}{\alpha^4}\Bigr]_0^1.
\]
Using \(\sin(n\pi)=0\) and \(\cos(n\pi)=(-1)^n\), the upper endpoint contributes
\[
-\frac{(-1)^n}{\alpha}+\frac{6(-1)^n}{\alpha^3}=(-1)^n\!\left(-\frac{1}{n\pi}+\f
rac{6}{(n\pi)^3}\right)=\frac{(-1)^n\bigl(6-(n\pi)^2\bigr)}{(n\pi)^3},
\]
and the lower endpoint contributes \(0\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{\,b_n=\frac{2(-1)^n\bigl(6-(n\pi)^2\bigr)}{(n\pi)^3}\,}.
\]

**Solution.** Recovering \(u=v/r\) (for \(0<r<1\)):
\[
\boxed{\;u(r,t)=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2(-1)^n\bigl(6-(n\pi)^2\bigr
)}{(n\pi)^3}\,e^{-(n\pi)^2 t}\sin(n\pi r),\qquad 0<r<1,\ t>0.\;}
\]

One may verify the initial condition: as \(t\to0^+\) the series is the Fourier s
ine series of \(r^3\), so \(v(r,0)=r^3\) and \(u(r,0)=r^3/r=r^2=f(r)\). The boun
dary condition \(u(1,t)=0\) holds since \(\sin(n\pi)=0\), and regularity at \(r=
0\) follows because \(v(0,t)=0\) so \(u=v/r\) has a finite limit there.

## Part (b): Temperature at the center

At the center we take \(r\to0\). Since \(v(0,t)=0\),
\[
u(0,t)=\lim_{r\to0}\frac{v(r,t)}{r}=v_r(0,t).
\]
Differentiating the series termwise (justified by uniform convergence for \(t>0\
)),
\[
v_r(0,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n\,(n\pi)\,e^{-(n\pi)^2 t}\cos(0)=\sum_{n=1}^{\in
fty} b_n\,(n\pi)\,e^{-(n\pi)^2 t}.
\]
Substituting \(b_n\):
\[
\boxed{\;u(0,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2(-1)^n\bigl(6-(n\pi)^2\bigr)}{(n\pi)^2
}\,e^{-(n\pi)^2 t},\qquad t>0.\;}
\]

**Behavior / plot description.**

- **At \(t=0\):** the series equals \(v_r(0,0)=(r^3)'|_{r=0}=0\), consistent wit
h \(f(0)=0^2=0\).
- **For small \(t>0\):** the dominant term is \(n=1\),
  \[
  \frac{2(-1)(6-\pi^2)}{\pi^2}e^{-\pi^2 t}=\frac{2(\pi^2-6)}{\pi^2}e^{-\pi^2 t}\
approx 0.784\,e^{-\pi^2 t},
  \]
  which is positive. Heat diffuses inward from the warmer outer region (\(r^2\)
increases with \(r\)), so the center temperature **rises rapidly from 0**, reach
es a single maximum, and thereafter decays.
- **As \(t\to\infty\):** every term decays exponentially; the slowest is the \(n
=1\) mode, so
  \[
  u(0,t)\sim \frac{2(\pi^2-6)}{\pi^2}\,e^{-\pi^2 t}\to 0.
  \]

Thus the plot of \(u(0,t)\) versus \(t>0\) is a curve starting at \(0\), rising
to a positive peak, then decaying monotonically to \(0\) with asymptotic rate \(
e^{-\pi^2 t}\).
