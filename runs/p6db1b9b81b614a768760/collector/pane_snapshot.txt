## Derivation of the Series Expansion of $E(m)$

**Setup.** We start from
$$E(m) = \int_0^{\pi/2} (1 - m\sin^2\theta)^{1/2}\,d\theta.$$

**Step 1: Binomial expansion of the integrand.**

The generalized binomial theorem gives, for $|x| \le 1$,
$$(1-x)^{1/2} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{1/2}{n}(-x)^n,$$
where
$$\binom{1/2}{n} = \frac{(1/2)(1/2-1)\cdots(1/2-n+1)}{n!}.$$

Setting $x = m\sin^2\theta$:
$$(1-m\sin^2\theta)^{1/2} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{1/2}{n}(-1)^n\, m^n \sin^{
2n}\theta.$$

**Step 2: Justification of term-by-term integration.**

For $|m|<1$, we have $|m\sin^2\theta|\le|m|<1$ for all $\theta\in[0,\pi/2]$. The
 binomial series converges absolutely and uniformly on $[0,\pi/2]$ by the Weiers
trass $M$-test (since $\left|\binom{1/2}{n}(-1)^n m^n\sin^{2n}\theta\right|\le\l
eft|\binom{1/2}{n}\right||m|^n$ and $\sum\left|\binom{1/2}{n}\right||m|^n<\infty
$). Hence we may integrate term by term:
$$E(m) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{1/2}{n}(-1)^n\, m^n \int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^{2n}\t
heta\,d\theta.$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the Wallis integral.**

The classical Wallis formula gives
$$\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^{2n}\theta\,d\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{(2n)!}{2^{2n}
(n!)^2} = \frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^n}, \qquad n\ge 0.$$

**Step 4: Simplify the binomial coefficient.**

For $n\ge 1$, the product in $\binom{1/2}{n}$ is
$$(1/2)(-1/2)(-3/2)\cdots\bigl((3-2n)/2\bigr) = \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{2^n}\cdot 1\cd
ot 1\cdot 3\cdots(2n-3) = \frac{(-1)^{n-1}(2n-3)!!}{2^n},$$
so
$$\binom{1/2}{n}(-1)^n = \frac{(-1)^{n-1}(2n-3)!!}{2^n\, n!}\cdot(-1)^n = -\frac
{(2n-3)!!}{2^n\, n!}.$$

Using $(2n-2)! = 2^{n-1}(n-1)!\,(2n-3)!!$, this becomes
$$\boxed{\binom{1/2}{n}(-1)^n = -\frac{(2n-2)!}{2^{2n-1}\, n!\, (n-1)!}} = -\fra
c{1}{2^{2n-1}\, n}\binom{2n-2}{n-1}.$$

(One checks: $n=1$ gives $-1/2$; $n=2$ gives $-1/8$; both correct.)

**Step 5: Combine and reduce to central binomial coefficients.**

For $n\ge 1$, the coefficient of $m^n$ in $E(m)$ is
$$c_n = \binom{1/2}{n}(-1)^n\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^n} = -
\frac{(2n-2)!}{2^{2n-1}\,n!\,(n-1)!}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{(2n)!}{2^{2n}(n
!)^2}.$$

Using the identity $\displaystyle\binom{2n}{n}=\frac{2(2n-1)}{n}\binom{2n-2}{n-1
}$, i.e. $\binom{2n-2}{n-1}=\frac{n}{2(2n-1)}\binom{2n}{n}$, we substitute:

$$c_n = -\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2^{2n-1}\,n}\cdot\frac{n}{2(2n-1)}\binom{2n}
{n}\cdot\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^n} = -\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{(2n-1)}\cdot\fra
c{\binom{2n}{n}^2}{2^{2n}\cdot 4^n\cdot 2}.$$

Since $2^{2n}\cdot 4^n = 4^n\cdot 4^n = 4^{2n}$ and the extra factor of $2$ give
s $2\cdot 4^{2n} = 2^{4n+1}$:

$$c_n = -\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2n-1}\cdot\frac{\binom{2n}{n}^2}{2^{4n}} = \
frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{1-2n}\left(\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^n}\right)^2.$$

**Step 6: Unify with the $n=0$ term.**

For $n=0$: $\binom{1/2}{0}(-1)^0 = 1$ and $\int_0^{\pi/2}d\theta = \pi/2$, givin
g $c_0 = \pi/2$. The formula $\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{1-0}\left(\frac{\binom{
0}{0}}{4^0}\right)^2 = \frac{\pi}{2}$ agrees. So the single formula covers all $
n\ge 0$.

**Result.**

$$\boxed{E(m) = \frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-2n}\left(\frac{\binom
{2n}{n}}{4^n}\right)^2 m^n, \qquad |m|<1.}$$

**Equivalent forms.** Using $\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^n} = \frac{(2n-1)!!}{(2n)!!}
$ (with $(-1)!!=1$):

$$E(m) = \frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-2n}\left(\frac{(2n-1)!!}{(2n

t(\frac{(2n-1)!!}{(2n)!!}\right)^2 m^n\right].$$
