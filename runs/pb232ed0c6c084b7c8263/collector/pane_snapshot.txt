## Analysis

Let $|G| = p^n q^m$ with $p < q$ primes, and suppose $\operatorname{ord}_q(p) >
n$.

**Sylow $q$-subgroups.** The number $n_q$ of Sylow $q$-subgroups satisfies $n_q
\mid p^n$ and $n_q \equiv 1 \pmod{q}$. Since $n_q \mid p^n$, we have $n_q = p^k$
 for some $0 \le k \le n$. The congruence $p^k \equiv 1 \pmod{q}$ requires $\ope
ratorname{ord}_q(p) \mid k$. But $\operatorname{ord}_q(p) > n \ge k$, so the onl
y possibility is $k = 0$, giving $n_q = 1$. **The Sylow $q$-subgroup is normal.*

**Sylow $p$-subgroups.** The number $n_p$ satisfies $n_p \mid q^m$ and $n_p \equ
iv 1 \pmod{p}$. So $n_p = q^j$ for some $0 \le j \le m$, with $q^j \equiv 1 \pmo
d{p}$, i.e., $\operatorname{ord}_p(q) \mid j$.

The condition $\operatorname{ord}_q(p) > n$ constrains the order of $p$ modulo $
q$, but $n_p > 1$ depends on $\operatorname{ord}_p(q)$ (the order of $q$ modulo
$p$). These two orders are generally independent — there is no logical implicati
on from $\operatorname{ord}_q(p) > n$ to $\operatorname{ord}_p(q) > m$.

## Counterexample: $S_3$

Take $G = S_3$, the symmetric group on 3 letters, with $|S_3| = 6 = 2^1 \cdot 3^
1$.

- $p = 2$, $q = 3$, $n = 1$, $m = 1$, and $p < q$. ✓
- $\operatorname{ord}_q(p) = \operatorname{ord}_3(2)$: since $2^1 = 2 \not\equiv
 1 \pmod{3}$ and $2^2 = 4 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$, we get $\operatorname{ord}_3(2) =
2 > 1 = n$. ✓ The condition is satisfied.

Now examine the Sylow $p$-subgroups (Sylow 2-subgroups):
- $n_2 \mid 3$ and $n_2 \equiv 1 \pmod{2}$, so $n_2 \in \{1, 3\}$.
- $S_3$ has three elements of order 2: $(12)$, $(13)$, $(23)$, each generating a
 distinct subgroup of order 2.
- Therefore $n_2 = 3 > 1$, and **the Sylow 2-subgroup is not normal**.

(Consistent with the analysis above, the Sylow $q$-subgroup $A_3 = \{e, (123), (
132)\}$ is normal: $n_3 = 1$.)
