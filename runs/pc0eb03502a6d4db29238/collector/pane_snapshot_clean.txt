## Solution

We find all functions $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ satisfying $f(f(x)+y)=2x+f(f(y
)-x)$ for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$.

**Claim.** The solutions are exactly $f(x)=x+c$ for an arbitrary constant $c\in\
mathbb{R}$.

### Verification

For $f(x)=x+c$ (with $c=f(0)$): LHS $=f(f(x)+y)=f(x+c+y)=x+y+2c$; RHS $=2x+f(f(y
)-x)=2x+f(y+c-x)=2x+y+c-x+c=x+y+2c$. ✓

### Necessity

Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion, and set $c=f(0)$.

**Step 1: Two key identities.**

$P(0,y)$: $f(c+y)=f(f(y))$ for all $y$. — (1)

$P(x,0)$: $f(f(x))=2x+f(c-x)$. Combined with (1) (with $y=x$): $f(c+x)=2x+f(c-x)
$. — (2)

**Step 2: A symmetry relation.**

From (2), substituting $t=c+x$ (so $x=t-c$, $c-x=2c-t$):
$$f(t)=2(t-c)+f(2c-t),\quad\text{i.e.,}\quad f(t)-f(2c-t)=2t-2c=t-(2c-t).\tag{3}
$$

Defining $g(t)=f(t)-t$, (3) gives $g(t)=g(2c-t)$: $g$ is symmetric about $c$. Eq
uivalently,
$$f(2c-s)=f(s)+2c-2s\quad\text{for all }s.\tag{4}$$

**Step 3: $f$ is surjective.**

Set $y=-f(x)$ in the original equation: $f(0)=2x+f(f(-f(x))-x)$, i.e.,
$$f\bigl(f(-f(x))-x\bigr)=c-2x.$$
As $x$ ranges over $\mathbb{R}$, $c-2x$ ranges over all of $\mathbb{R}$, so $f$
is **surjective**.

**Step 4: $f$ is injective.**

Suppose $f(a)=f(b)$; put $d=a-b$. Subtracting $P(b,y)$ from $P(a,y)$ (the LHS ar
e equal since $f(a)=f(b)$):
$$2a+f(f(y)-a)=2b+f(f(y)-b)\quad\text{for all }y,$$
so $f(f(y)-a)-f(f(y)-b)=2(b-a)=-2d$. Since $f$ is surjective, $f(y)$ ranges over
 all $\mathbb{R}$, giving
$$f(s)-f(s+d)=-2d,\quad\text{i.e.,}\quad f(s+d)=f(s)+2d\quad\text{for all }s.\ta
g{5}$$

Now combine (5) with (4). Applying (5) to $2c-s$:
$$f(2c-s+d)=f(2c-s)+2d\stackrel{(4)}{=}\bigl(f(s)+2c-2s\bigr)+2d=f(s)+2c-2s+2d.$
$
Applying (4) to $s-d$ (noting $2c-(s-d)=2c-s+d$):
$$f(2c-s+d)=f(s-d)+2c-2(s-d)=f(s-d)+2c-2s+2d.$$
Equating the two expressions: $f(s)=f(s-d)$ for all $s$. But (5) with $s\to s-d$
 gives $f(s)=f(s-d)+2d$, so $f(s-d)=f(s)-2d$. Hence $f(s)=f(s)-2d$, yielding $d=
0$, i.e., $a=b$. Therefore $f$ is **injective**.

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

Since $f$ is bijective, apply injectivity to (1): $f(c+y)=f(f(y))$ implies $c+y=
f(y)$ for all $y$. Thus
$$f(y)=y+c\quad\text{for all }y\in\mathbb{R},$$
where $c=f(0)$ is an arbitrary real constant.
