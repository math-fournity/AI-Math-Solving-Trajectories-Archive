**Key observation.** A non-empty square can cover *any subset* of its 4 edges: o
ne reflector covers 2 adjacent edges; two reflectors at opposite corners cover a

**Boundary squares are forced.** Each perimeter segment is adjacent to exactly o
ne square (a boundary square), so all boundary squares must be in $S$. The bound
ary ring of a $100 \times 100$ grid has $4(100) - 4 = 396$ squares.

**Interior reduction to vertex cover.** After selecting all 396 boundary squares
, every segment adjacent to a boundary square is covered. The remaining uncovere

interior squares so that every edge of this $98 \times 98$ grid graph has at lea
st one selected endpoint — this is a **vertex cover** problem.

**Minimum vertex cover of the $98 \times 98$ grid.** The $98 \times 98$ grid gra
ph is bipartite (checkerboard coloring). Since both dimensions are even, it has
a perfect matching: pair $(i, 2k)$ with $(i, 2k+1)$ for each row $i$ and $k = 1,
 \ldots, 49$, giving $49 \times 98 = 4802$ matched edges. By König's theorem, th
e minimum vertex cover equals the maximum matching, so the minimum vertex cover
has size $4802$.

**Lower bound.** Any valid configuration requires all 396 boundary squares non-e
mpty, plus a vertex cover of the $98 \times 98$ interior grid (size $\geq 4802$)
. Thus at least $396 + 4802 = 5198$ squares must be non-empty, giving $B(100) \l
eq 10000 - 5198 = 4802$.

**Upper bound (construction).** Select all 396 boundary squares and a minimum ve

 even among interior squares). For each selected square, place reflectors to cov

**Verification of the construction.** For each grid segment $e$, at least one ad

**Conclusion.** $B(100) = 10000 - 396 - 4802 = \boxed{4802}$.
