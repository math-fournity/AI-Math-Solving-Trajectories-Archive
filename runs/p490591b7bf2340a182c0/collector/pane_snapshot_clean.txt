**Step 2: The condition for distinct $x \neq y$ also forces $m(E) = 0$.**

Suppose for contradiction that $m(E) > 0$ and the midpoint condition holds for a

By the **Lebesgue Density Theorem**, there exists a point $x_0 \in E$ that is a
density point of $E$. Translating $E$, assume $x_0 = 0$.

Since $0$ is a density point, for any $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $r_0 > 0$ s

$$m(E \cap (-r, r)) > (1 - \varepsilon) \cdot 2r.$$

This gives us two bounds. First:
$$m(E \cap (0, r)) \geq m(E \cap (-r,r)) - r > (1-\varepsilon)\cdot 2r - r = (1
- 2\varepsilon)\, r.$$

Second, applying the density property with $r/2$ in place of $r$:
$$m(E \cap (0, r/2)) > (1 - 2\varepsilon)\cdot \frac{r}{2}.$$

**Step 3: The midpoint condition forces midpoints out of $E$.**

Since $0 \in E$, for any $c \in E \cap (0, r)$ we have $c \neq 0$ and both $0, c

$$\frac{c}{2} = \frac{0 + c}{2} \notin E.$$
