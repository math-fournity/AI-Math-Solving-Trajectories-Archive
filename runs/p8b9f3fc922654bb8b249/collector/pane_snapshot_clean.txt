## Efficient Method for the Extended GCD of $a_1, \ldots, a_k \in \mathbb{Z}[X]$

### Key Observations

$\mathbb{Z}[X]$ is a UFD, so $\gcd(a_1, \ldots, a_k)$ exists. However, $\mathbb{
Z}[X]$ is **not** a Bézout domain, so a Bézout identity $g = \sum u_i a_i$ with
$u_i \in \mathbb{Z}[X]$ need not hold (e.g., $\gcd(2, X) = 1$, yet $1 \neq 2p +
Xq$ for any $p, q \in \mathbb{Z}[X]$). We therefore perform the extended computa
tion over $\mathbb{Q}[X]$, which is a Euclidean domain, and recover the $\mathbb
{Z}[X]$-gcd via **Gauss's Lemma**:

$$\gcd_{\mathbb{Z}[X]}(a_1, \ldots, a_k) = \operatorname{pp}\!\bigl(\gcd_{\mathb
b{Q}[X]}(a_1, \ldots, a_k)\bigr),$$

where $\operatorname{pp}(f) = f / \operatorname{cont}(f)$ is the primitive part
and $\operatorname{cont}(f)$ is the gcd of the coefficients of $f$.

### Step 1 — Reduction to Binary Extended GCD

We reduce the $k$-ary problem to $k - 1$ binary extended GCD computations over $
\mathbb{Q}[X]$. Set $g^{(1)} = a_1$ with cofactor vector $(1)$. For $i = 2, \ldo
ts, k$:

1. Compute the binary extended GCD of $g^{(i-1)}$ and $a_i$ in $\mathbb{Q}[X]$:
$$g^{(i)} = s_i\, g^{(i-1)} + t_i\, a_i, \qquad s_i, t_i \in \mathbb{Q}[X].$$

2. Update the cofactor vector. If $g^{(i-1)} = \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} c_j^{(i-1)} a_j$
, then
$$g^{(i)} = \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \bigl(s_i\, c_j^{(i-1)}\bigr)\, a_j \;+\; t_i\, a_i
.$$

After $k-1$ steps we obtain $g^{(k)} = \gcd_{\mathbb{Q}[X]}(a_1, \ldots, a_k)$ t
ogether with a full Bézout representation
$$g^{(k)} = \sum_{j=1}^{k} c_j\, a_j, \qquad c_j \in \mathbb{Q}[X].$$

### Step 2 — Recover the $\mathbb{Z}[X]$-gcd

Let $d = \operatorname{cont}(g^{(k)}) \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$. Output

$$g = \operatorname{pp}(g^{(k)}) = \frac{g^{(k)}}{d} = \sum_{j=1}^{k} \frac{c_j}
{d}\, a_j.$$

By Gauss's Lemma, $g = \gcd_{\mathbb{Z}[X]}(a_1, \ldots, a_k)$ (up to sign), and
 $g = \sum_j u_j\, a_j$ with $u_j = c_j / d \in \mathbb{Q}[X]$.

### Step 3 — Efficient Binary Extended GCD over $\mathbb{Q}[X]$

The core subroutine is the binary extended GCD in $\mathbb{Q}[X]$.

- **Naive Euclidean algorithm:** $O(n^2)$ field operations for polynomials of de
gree $\le n$.
- **Half-GCD (fast Euclidean) algorithm:** $O(M(n) \log n)$ field operations, wh
ere $M(n)$ is the multiplication cost (e.g., $M(n) = O(n \log n \log \log n)$ vi
a FFT). This uses a divide-and-conquer scheme that computes the transformation m
atrix of the Euclidean algorithm from the top half of the quotient sequence, rec
ursing on half-sized inputs.

### Step 4 — Managing Coefficient Growth

Working directly over $\mathbb{Q}$, the numerators and denominators of intermedi
ate coefficients can grow **exponentially** in the degree, destroying bit-comple
xity efficiency. Two standard remedies:

**(a) Subresultant pseudo-remainder sequences.** Replace ordinary division by **
pseudo-division** in $\mathbb{Z}[X]$: to divide $f$ by $g$ (with $\deg f = m$, $
\deg g = n$, leading coefficient $\operatorname{lc}(g)$), compute
$$\operatorname{prem}(f, g) = \operatorname{lc}(g)^{m-n+1} f \bmod g \in \mathbb
{Z}[X],$$
which clears all denominators. The **subresultant algorithm** organizes the pseu
do-remainder sequence so that intermediate coefficients have $O(d \cdot \tau)$ b
its (where $d$ is the degree bound and $\tau$ is the input coefficient bit-size)
, giving a polynomial-time algorithm working entirely over $\mathbb{Z}$. The sub
resultant coefficients are proportional to the corresponding remainders, so the
Bézout cofactors can be recovered from the subresultant chain.

**(b) Modular / Hensel methods.** Compute the extended GCD modulo a **lucky prim
e** $p$ (one not dividing any relevant leading coefficient or resultant). This y
ields the degree of the true GCD and a modular Bézout representation. Then eithe
r:
- **Hensel lift** the modular GCD and cofactors to sufficient $p$-adic precision
 and reconstruct via rational recovery (e.g., via continued fractions / Moenck),
 or
- Use **multiple primes** with the Chinese Remainder Theorem and reconstruct the
 rational coefficients.

Both approaches avoid intermediate coefficient swell and achieve softly-linear b
it complexity.

### Complexity Summary

Using the half-GCD algorithm over $\mathbb{Q}[X]$ with modular coefficient arith
metic (or the subresultant algorithm over $\mathbb{Z}$), each binary extended GC
D for polynomials of degree $\le n$ with coefficient bit-size $\le \tau$ costs $
\tilde{O}(n \cdot \tau)$ bit operations (where $\tilde{O}$ hides polylogarithmic
 factors). The $k - 1$ pairwise reductions contribute a factor of $k$, giving **
total complexity** $\tilde{O}(k \cdot n \cdot \tau)$ bit operations.

### Optional: Divide-and-Conquer for the $k$-ary Case

For better parallelism and balanced intermediate degrees, replace the sequential
 chain by a **balanced binary tree**: split $\{a_1, \ldots, a_k\}$ into two halv
es, recursively compute the extended GCD of each half, then combine via one bina
ry extended GCD. This still performs $k - 1$ binary operations but reduces the d
epth to $O(\log k)$, enabling parallel execution and keeping intermediate degree
s more balanced.
