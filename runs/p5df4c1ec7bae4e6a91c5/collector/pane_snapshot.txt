**Setup.** Given $\sin b = \frac{3}{5}$, we take $b$ acute so $\cos b = \frac{4}
{5}$ (the standard convention; the problem asks for a unique value of $a \bmod 5
$). Then
$$
\cos b + i\sin b = \frac{4+3i}{5},
$$
and by De Moivre's theorem,
$$
\sin(1000b) = \operatorname{Im}\!\left(\frac{(4+3i)^{1000}}{5^{1000}}\right).
$$
Therefore
$$
a = 5^{1000}\sin(1000b) = \operatorname{Im}\!\left((4+3i)^{1000}\right).
$$

**Reduction mod 5.** Since $4\equiv -1$ and $3\equiv -2\pmod{5}$,
$$
(4+3i)^{1000} \equiv (-1-2i)^{1000} \pmod{5}.
$$

**Key factorization.** Observe $-1-2i = -(1+2i)$ and $1+2i = i(2-i)$ (since $i(2
-i)=2i-i^2=2i+1$). Hence
$$
(-1-2i)^{1000} = \bigl(-i(2-i)\bigr)^{1000} = (-i)^{1000}(2-i)^{1000}.
$$
Since $(-i)^4=1$ and $4\mid 1000$, we get $(-i)^{1000}=1$, so
$$
(-1-2i)^{1000} = (2-i)^{1000}.
$$

**CRT in $\mathbb{Z}[i]/(5)$.** Since $5=(2+i)(2-i)$ in $\mathbb{Z}[i]$, the Chi
nese Remainder Theorem gives
$$
\mathbb{Z}[i]/(5)\cong \mathbb{Z}[i]/(2+i)\times \mathbb{Z}[i]/(2-i)\cong \mathb
b{F}_5\times\mathbb{F}_5,
$$
where $i\mapsto -2$ in the first factor and $i\mapsto 2$ in the second. A Gaussi
an integer $A+Bi$ maps to $(A-2B,\; A+2B)$.

For $(2-i)^{1000}$:
- **Mod $(2+i)$:** $i\equiv -2$, so $2-i\equiv 2-(-2)=4\equiv -1$, giving $(2-i)
^{1000}\equiv (-1)^{1000}=1$.
- **Mod $(2-i)$:** $i\equiv 2$, so $2-i\equiv 0$, giving $(2-i)^{1000}\equiv 0$.

Writing $(2-i)^{1000}=A+Bi$, the CRT system is:
$$
A-2B\equiv 1,\qquad A+2B\equiv 0 \pmod{5}.
$$
From the second equation, $A\equiv -2B$. Substituting: $-2B-2B\equiv 1$, i.e., $
-4B\equiv 1$. Since $-4\equiv 1\pmod{5}$, this gives $B\equiv 1\pmod{5}$.

**Conclusion.** Since $a = \operatorname{Im}((4+3i)^{1000})$ and $\operatorname{
Im}((4+3i)^{1000})\equiv \operatorname{Im}((2-i)^{1000})\equiv 1\pmod{5}$,
