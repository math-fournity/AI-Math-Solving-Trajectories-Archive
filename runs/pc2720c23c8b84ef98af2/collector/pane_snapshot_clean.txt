## Proof

$$\operatorname{cov}\!\left[W(1),\ \int_0^1 r\,W(r)\,dr\right].$$

n, it is a zero-mean process: $\mathbb{E}[W(t)]=0$ for every $t$. Hence

$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^1 r\,W(r)\,dr\right]=\int_0^1 r\,\mathbb{E}[W(r)]\,dr
=0,$$

and $\mathbb{E}[W(1)]=0$. Therefore the covariance equals the second mixed momen
t:

$$\operatorname{cov}\!\left[W(1),\ \int_0^1 r\,W(r)\,dr\right]=\mathbb{E}\!\left
[W(1)\cdot\int_0^1 r\,W(r)\,dr\right].$$

**Step 2: Exchange expectation and integration.** By Fubini's theorem (justified
 since $\int_0^1 r\,\mathbb{E}|W(1)W(r)|\,dr<\infty$),

$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[W(1)\cdot\int_0^1 r\,W(r)\,dr\right]=\int_0^1 r\,\mathbb{E}\
!\left[W(1)\,W(r)\right]\,dr.$$

**Step 3: Apply the covariance kernel of Brownian motion.** For standard Brownia
n motion, $\mathbb{E}[W(s)W(t)]=\min(s,t)$. With $s=1$ and $t=r\in[0,1]$,

$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[W(1)\,W(r)\right]=\min(1,r)=r.$$

**Step 4: Evaluate the integral.** Substituting,

$$\int_0^1 r\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[W(1)\,W(r)\right]\,dr=\int_0^1 r\cdot r\,dr=\int
_0^1 r^2\,dr=\left[\frac{r^3}{3}\right]_0^1=\frac{1}{3}.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\operatorname{cov}\!\left[W(1),\ \int_0^1 r\,W(r)\,dr\right]=\frac{1}{3
}.}$$
