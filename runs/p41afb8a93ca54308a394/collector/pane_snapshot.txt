## Answer

**Yes.** The Rado graph (the countable random graph) is an example: removing any
 single edge leaves a graph isomorphic to the original.

### Proof

Let $R = (V, E)$ be the **Rado graph**, the unique (up to isomorphism) countable
 simple undirected graph satisfying the **extension property**:

> (*) For any two disjoint finite sets $A, B \subseteq V$, there exists a vertex
 $w \notin A \cup B$ adjacent to every vertex of $A$ and to no vertex of $B$.

It is standard (and follows by iterating (*)) that in fact there are **infinitel
y many** such witnesses $w$ for each pair $(A, B)$: having found witnesses $w_1,
 \dots, w_k$, apply (*) with the "forbidden" set $B \cup \{w_1, \dots, w_k\}$ to
 obtain a new witness distinct from all of them.

Fix any edge $e = \{u, v\} \in E$ and set $R' = (V, E \setminus \{e\})$. We clai
m $R'$ also satisfies (*). Since $R'$ is countable and the extension property ch
aracterizes the Rado graph up to isomorphism, this yields $R' \cong R$.

Let $A, B \subseteq V$ be disjoint finite sets. In $R$ there are infinitely many
 witnesses $w$ adjacent to all of $A$ and none of $B$. Choose such a $w$ with $w
 \notin \{u, v\}$ (possible since only two vertices are excluded and infinitely
many witnesses exist). Removing the single edge $\{u, v\}$ changes the adjacency
 relation **only** for the pair $\{u, v\}$. Since $w \neq u$ and $w \neq v$, eve
ry edge incident to $w$ is untouched:

- For $a \in A$: the edge $\{w, a\}$ is present in $R$ and $\{w, a\} \neq \{u, v
\}$ (as $w \notin \{u,v\}$), so it remains in $R'$.
- For $b \in B$: $\{w, b\}$ was absent in $R$ and removing an edge cannot create
 one, so it is still absent in $R'$.

Hence $w$ is adjacent in $R'$ to every vertex of $A$ and to no vertex of $B$, wi
th $w \notin A \cup B$. Thus $R'$ satisfies (*).

Therefore $R'$ is a countable graph satisfying the extension property, so by uni
queness of the Rado graph,
$$R' = (V, E \setminus \{e\}) \cong R = (V, E).$$

This holds for **every** edge $e$ of $R$. Since $R$ is infinite (indeed countabl
y infinite), it is the desired infinite simple undirected graph. $\blacksquare$
