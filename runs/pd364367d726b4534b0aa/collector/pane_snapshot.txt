## Solution

Let
$$F(x)=\int_x^1 t^{-t}\,dt,\qquad L=\lim_{x\to 0^+}F(x)^{1/x}.$$
(The limit must be taken from the right, since $t^{-t}$ is not real-valued for $
t<0$.)

**Step 1. The limiting value of $F(x)$.**
Set
$$C=\int_0^1 t^{-t}\,dt.$$
For $t\in(0,1]$ we have $\ln t\le 0$, hence $-t\ln t\ge 0$, so
$$t^{-t}=e^{-t\ln t}\ge 1,$$
with strict inequality on $(0,1)$ (where $t\ln t<0$). Therefore
$$C=\int_0^1 t^{-t}\,dt>\int_0^1 1\,dt=1.$$
In particular $\ln C>0$. The integral $C$ is finite because $t^{-t}\to 1$ as $t\
to 0^+$, so the integrand is bounded near $0$.

**Step 2. Decompose $F(x)$.**
Write
$$F(x)=C-r(x),\qquad r(x)=\int_0^x t^{-t}\,dt.$$
Then $r(x)\to 0$ as $x\to 0^+$, and since $t^{-t}\to 1$ as $t\to 0^+$,
$$\frac{r(x)}{x}=\frac{1}{x}\int_0^x t^{-t}\,dt\to 1.$$

**Step 3. Logarithmic expansion.**
$$\ln F(x)^{1/x}=\frac{1}{x}\ln(C-r(x))=\frac{\ln C}{x}+\frac{1}{x}\ln\!\left(1-
\frac{r(x)}{C}\right).$$

For the second term, since $r(x)/C\to 0$, we use $\ln(1-u)=-u+O(u^2)$:
$$\frac{1}{x}\ln\!\left(1-\frac{r(x)}{C}\right)=-\frac{r(x)}{Cx}+O\!\left(\frac{
r(x)^2}{x}\right).$$
Because $r(x)/x\to 1$, we have $r(x)^2/x = x\cdot(r(x)/x)^2\to 0$, so
$$\frac{1}{x}\ln\!\left(1-\frac{r(x)}{C}\right)\to -\frac{1}{C}.$$

For the first term, since $C>1$ we have $\ln C>0$, and thus
$$\frac{\ln C}{x}\to +\infty\qquad (x\to 0^+).$$

Combining,
$$\ln F(x)^{1/x}=\underbrace{\frac{\ln C}{x}}_{\to+\infty}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{
x}\ln\!\left(1-\frac{r(x)}{C}\right)}_{\to -1/C}\;\longrightarrow\;+\infty.$$
