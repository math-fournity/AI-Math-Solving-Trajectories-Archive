Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest $\lambda > 0$ such that for
 all real sequences $\{x_n\}$ and all positive integers $m$:

$$\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^m x_i^2 \leq \sum_{i=1}^m \lambda^{m-i} y_i^2$$

where $y_1 = x_1$ and $y_{n+1} = x_{n+1} - \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2}$.

**Answer: $\lambda = 2$.**

---

## Necessity ($\lambda \geq 2$)

Consider the sequence $x_1 = 1$, $x_{n+1} = S_n$ where $S_n = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n
 x_i^2}$.

Then $y_1 = 1$ and $y_{n+1} = x_{n+1} - S_n = 0$ for all $n \geq 1$. Also $S_{n+
1}^2 = S_n^2 + S_n^2 = 2S_n^2$, so $S_m^2 = 2^{m-1}$.

The inequality becomes $\frac{2^{m-1}}{m} \leq \lambda^{m-1} \cdot 1$, i.e., $\l
eft(\frac{2}{\lambda}\right)^{m-1} \leq m$.

If $\lambda < 2$, then $2/\lambda > 1$ and the left side grows exponentially whi
le the right side grows linearly, so the inequality fails for sufficiently large
 $m$. Hence $\lambda \geq 2$.

## Sufficiency ($\lambda = 2$ works)

Define $S_k^2 = \sum_{i=1}^k x_i^2$ and $R_k = \sum_{i=1}^k 2^{k-i} y_i^2$. We p
rove by induction that $S_m^2 \leq m R_m$ for all $m \geq 1$.

**Base case** ($m=1$): $S_1^2 = x_1^2 = y_1^2 = R_1$. ✓

**Inductive step**: Assume $S_{m-1}^2 \leq (m-1)R_{m-1}$. Since $R_m = 2R_{m-1}
+ y_m^2 = 2R_{m-1} + (x_m - S_{m-1})^2$ and $S_m^2 = S_{m-1}^2 + x_m^2$, we comp
ute:

$$mR_m - S_m^2 = 2mR_{m-1} + (m-1)x_m^2 - 2mx_m S_{m-1} + (m-1)S_{m-1}^2$$

Using the induction hypothesis $R_{m-1} \geq \frac{S_{m-1}^2}{m-1}$:

$$mR_m - S_m^2 \geq \frac{2m}{m-1}S_{m-1}^2 + (m-1)x_m^2 - 2mx_m S_{m-1} + (m-1)
S_{m-1}^2$$

$$= (m-1)x_m^2 - 2mx_m S_{m-1} + \frac{m^2}{m-1}S_{m-1}^2$$

The discriminant of this quadratic in $x_m$ is:

$$4m^2 S_{m-1}^2 - 4(m-1)\cdot\frac{m^2}{m-1}S_{m-1}^2 = 0$$

So the expression is a perfect square:

$$\left(\sqrt{m-1}\, x_m - \frac{m}{\sqrt{m-1}} S_{m-1}\right)^2 \geq 0$$

Therefore $mR_m \geq S_m^2$, completing the induction. $\blacksquare$

---

The smallest positive real number $\lambda$ is $\boxed{2}$.
