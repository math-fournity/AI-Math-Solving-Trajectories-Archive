**Answer: No.** Not every Borel set of positive Lebesgue measure contains a non-
degenerate closed interval. A counterexample is the **Smith–Volterra–Cantor (SVC
) set**.

---

## Construction of the SVC set

Start with the closed interval $[0,1]$. We iteratively remove open middle subint
ervals:

- **Step 1:** Remove the middle open interval of length $\tfrac{1}{4}$, namely $
\bigl(\tfrac{3}{8},\tfrac{5}{8}\bigr)$. Two closed intervals remain, each of len
gth $\tfrac{3}{8}$.
- **Step 2:** From each of the two remaining intervals, remove its middle open s
ubinterval of length $\tfrac{1}{16}$. Four closed intervals remain.
- **Step $n$:** From each of the $2^{n-1}$ remaining closed intervals, remove it
s middle open subinterval of length $\dfrac{1}{2^{2n}}$.

Define
$$
S \;=\; [0,1]\;\setminus\;\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{k=1}^{2^{n-1}} I_{n,k},
$$
where $I_{n,k}$ are the open intervals removed at step $n$.

---

## $S$ is Borel

Each $I_{n,k}$ is open, so $\bigcup_{n,k} I_{n,k}$ is open. Its complement $S$ i
s **closed**, hence Borel. $\square$

---

## $S$ has positive Lebesgue measure

The total length removed is
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 2^{n-1}\cdot\frac{1}{2^{2n}}
\;=\;\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}
\;=\;\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{8}+\frac{1}{16}+\cdots
\;=\;\frac{1}{2}.
$$
Therefore
$$
m(S) \;=\; 1 - \tfrac{1}{2} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2} \;>\; 0. \qquad\square
$$

---

## $S$ contains no non-degenerate interval

After step $n$, the remaining set $S_n$ is a union of $2^n$ disjoint closed inte
rvals. Let $L_n$ denote the length of each such interval. We track the recurrenc
e:

- $L_0 = 1$ (the single interval $[0,1]$).
- At step $n$, from each interval of length $L_{n-1}$ we remove a middle open pi
ece of length $1/2^{2n}$, leaving two intervals each of length
$$
L_n \;=\; \frac{L_{n-1} - 2^{-2n}}{2}.
$$

Since $L_{n-1} \geq 2^{-2n}$ for all $n\geq 1$ (easily verified: $L_0=1\geq 1/4$
, and the recurrence keeps $L_n$ positive), the recurrence is valid, and one che
cks that $L_n \to 0$ as $n\to\infty$.

Now $S = \bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty} S_n$. Since every component interval of $S_n$ ha
s length $L_n \to 0$, for any point $x\in S$ and any $\varepsilon>0$, choosing $
n$ with $L_n<\varepsilon$ shows that $S\cap(x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)$ is con
tained in a single component of $S_n$ of length $<\varepsilon$. In particular, $
S$ contains no interval of positive length: $S$ is **nowhere dense** (its interi
or is empty).

Therefore $S$ contains **no** closed interval $[a,b]$ with $a<b$. $\square$

---

## Conclusion

The Smith–Volterra–Cantor set $S$ is a **Borel set** (in fact closed) with **pos
itive Lebesgue measure** ($m(S)=1/2$) that contains **no non-degenerate interval
**. Hence the answer to the question is:

$$\boxed{\text{No.}}$$
