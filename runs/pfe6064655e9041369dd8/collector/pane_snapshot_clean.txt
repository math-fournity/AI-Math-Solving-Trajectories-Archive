**Proof.** This is a classical consequence of Fatou's lemma (a form of Scheffé's
 lemma). We prove that under the hypotheses $f_n \to f$ a.e. and $\int |f_n| \to
 \int |f|$, we have $\int |f_n - f| \to 0$.

Since $f_n \to f$ a.e., we also have $|f_n| \to |f|$ a.e. and $|f_n - f| \to 0$
a.e. By the reverse triangle inequality, the function
$$g_n := |f_n| + |f| - |f_n - f|$$
is non-negative a.e. Moreover, $g_n \to |f| + |f| - 0 = 2|f|$ a.e.

Applying Fatou's lemma to the sequence of non-negative measurable functions $g_n
$:
$$\int \liminf_{n\to\infty} g_n \leq \liminf_{n\to\infty} \int g_n.$$
The left-hand side is $\int 2|f| = 2\int |f|$.

For the right-hand side, since $\int |f_n| \to \int |f|$,
$$\int g_n = \int |f_n| + \int |f| - \int |f_n - f|,$$
so
$$\liminf_{n\to\infty} \int g_n = \lim_{n\to\infty}\int |f_n| + \int |f| - \lims
up_{n\to\infty}\int |f_n - f| = 2\int |f| - \limsup_{n\to\infty}\int |f_n - f|.$
$

Combining:
$$2\int |f| \leq 2\int |f| - \limsup_{n\to\infty}\int |f_n - f|.$$

Since $\int |f| < \infty$ (as $f$ is integrable), we may subtract it, yielding
$$\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int |f_n - f| \leq 0.$$

As $\int |f_n - f| \geq 0$, this forces $\lim_{n\to\infty}\int |f_n - f| = 0$. $
