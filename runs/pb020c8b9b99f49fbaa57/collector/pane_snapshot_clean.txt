## Proof

We compute $\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_t]$ for all $t \in [0,1]$.

**Case 1: $t = 1$.** By definition $\bar{X}_1 = 0$, so $\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_1] =
0$.

**Case 2: $0 \le t < 1$.** Here $\bar{X}_t = X_t = (1-t)\int_0^t \frac{1}{1-s}\,
dW_s$.

First we verify the Itô integral is well-defined. The integrand $f(s) = \frac{1}
{1-s}$ is deterministic and, for every $t < 1$,
$$
\int_0^t f(s)^2\,ds = \int_0^t \frac{1}{(1-s)^2}\,ds = \left[-\frac{1}{1-s}\righ
t]_0^t = \frac{1}{1-t} - 1 < \infty.
$$
Hence $f \in L^2([0,t])$ and the Itô integral $I_t := \int_0^t \frac{1}{1-s}\,dW
_s$ is a well-defined martingale with
$$
\mathbb{E}[I_t] = 0.
$$
(This is the standard property of the Itô integral: for any deterministic square
-integrable integrand, the integral is a centered martingale.)

By linearity of expectation,
$$
\mathbb{E}[X_t] = (1-t)\,\mathbb{E}[I_t] = (1-t)\cdot 0 = 0.
$$

**Conclusion.** Combining both cases,
$$
\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_t] = 0, \qquad \text{for all } t \in [0,1].
$$
