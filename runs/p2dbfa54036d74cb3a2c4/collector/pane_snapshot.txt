## Solution

We compute the flux $\iint_S \mathbf{F}\cdot \mathbf{n}\, dS$ where $\mathbf{F}(
x,y,z)=(xz,\,yz,\,-z^2)$ and $S$ is the upper sheet of the hyperboloid $z^2=1+x^
2+y^2$ with $2\le z\le 3$. "Out of the surface" means the normal pointing away f
rom the $z$-axis.

### Parametrization

Set $R(z)=\sqrt{z^2-1}$ and parametrize by $(z,\theta)$:
$$\mathbf{r}(z,\theta)=\bigl(R(z)\cos\theta,\; R(z)\sin\theta,\; z\bigr),\qquad
2\le z\le 3,\; 0\le\theta\le 2\pi.$$

### Tangent vectors and normal

$$\mathbf{r}_z=\Bigl(\tfrac{z}{R}\cos\theta,\;\tfrac{z}{R}\sin\theta,\;1\Bigr),\
qquad \mathbf{r}_\theta=\bigl(-R\sin\theta,\;R\cos\theta,\;0\bigr).$$

Cross product:
$$\mathbf{r}_z\times\mathbf{r}_\theta=\bigl(-R\cos\theta,\;-R\sin\theta,\;z\bigr
).$$

The radial component along $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta,0)$ is $-R<0$, so this normal
 points **toward** the $z$-axis (inward). The **outward** normal vector area ele
ment is therefore
$$\mathbf{n}\,dS = -(\mathbf{r}_z\times\mathbf{r}_\theta)\,dz\,d\theta = \bigl(R
\cos\theta,\;R\sin\theta,\;-z\bigr)\,dz\,d\theta.$$

### Evaluate $\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{n}$ on $S$

On the surface, $x=R\cos\theta$, $y=R\sin\theta$, so
$$\mathbf{F}=\bigl(Rz\cos\theta,\;Rz\sin\theta,\;-z^2\bigr).$$

Thus
$$\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dS = \bigl(Rz\cos\theta\bigr)(R\cos\theta)+\bigl(Rz
\sin\theta\bigr)(R\sin\theta)+(-z^2)(-z)\;dz\,d\theta$$
$$= R^2 z(\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta)+z^3\;dz\,d\theta = \bigl(R^2 z+z^3\bigr)\,d
z\,d\theta.$$

Since $R^2=z^2-1$:
$$\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dS = \bigl((z^2-1)z+z^3\bigr)\,dz\,d\theta = z(2z^2
-1)\,dz\,d\theta.$$

### Integrate

$$\Phi = \int_2^3\int_0^{2\pi} z(2z^2-1)\,d\theta\,dz = 2\pi\int_2^3 (2z^3-z)\,d
z.$$

$$\int(2z^3-z)\,dz = \frac{z^4}{2}-\frac{z^2}{2}.$$

Evaluating:
$$\left[\frac{z^4}{2}-\frac{z^2}{2}\right]_2^3 = \left(\frac{81}{2}-\frac{9}{2}\
right)-\left(\frac{16}{2}-\frac{4}{2}\right)=36-6=30.$$

$$\Phi = 2\pi\cdot 30 = \boxed{60\pi}.$$

### Verification via divergence theorem

$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F}=\partial_x(xz)+\partial_y(yz)+\partial_z(-z^2)=z+z-2z=0$.

Closing $S$ with the top disk ($z=3$, $r\le\sqrt{8}$, outward normal $(0,0,1)$,
$\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{n}=-9$, flux $=-9\cdot 8\pi=-72\pi$) and bottom disk ($z
=2$, $r\le\sqrt{3}$, outward normal $(0,0,-1)$, $\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{n}=4$, f
lux $=4\cdot 3\pi=12\pi$):

$$0=\Phi_S+(-72\pi)+12\pi \;\Longrightarrow\; \Phi_S=60\pi.\;\checkmark$$
