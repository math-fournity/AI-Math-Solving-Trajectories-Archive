**Answer: Yes.**

**Construction.** Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is countable, fix a bijection $\varphi:\mat
hbb{N}\to\mathbb{Q}$. For each real number $r\in\mathbb{R}$, define
$$B_r \;=\; \{\,n\in\mathbb{N} : \varphi(n) < r\,\}\;\subseteq\;\mathbb{N}.$$
Set $E=\{B_r : r\in\mathbb{R}\}\subseteq 2^{\mathbb{N}}$.

**$E$ is a chain under inclusion.** Take two distinct elements $B_r,B_s\in E$ wi
th $r\neq s$; without loss assume $r<s$. By the density of $\mathbb{Q}$ in $\mat
hbb{R}$, there exists $q\in\mathbb{Q}$ with $r<q<s$. Let $n=\varphi^{-1}(q)$. Th
en $\varphi(n)=q<r$ is false, so $n\notin B_r$; but $\varphi(n)=q<s$, so $n\in B
_s$. Hence $B_r\subsetneq B_s$. Thus for any two distinct members of $E$, one is
 a proper subset of the other.

**$E$ is uncountable.** The map $r\mapsto B_r$ is injective: if $r<s$, the ratio
nal $q$ with $r<q<s$ (as above) witnesses $B_r\neq B_s$. Since $\mathbb{R}$ is u
ncountable and $E$ is in bijection with $\mathbb{R}$, $E$ is uncountable.

Therefore such an uncountable collection exists. $\blacksquare$
