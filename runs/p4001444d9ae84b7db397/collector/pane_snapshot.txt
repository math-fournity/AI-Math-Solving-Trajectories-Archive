## Proof

**Setup.** Let $A = \mathbf{Z}_{p^n}$ and $B = \mathbf{Z}_{p^m}$, both viewed as
 permutation groups via their regular action (each acts on itself by translation
). The (regular) wreath product $A \wr B$ is the semidirect product $A^{|B|} \rt
imes B$, where $B$ acts on the direct product $A^{|B|}$ by permuting the $|B|$ c
opies of $A$ according to its regular permutation action.

**Order of the wreath product.** By the standard formula for the order of a wrea
th product,

$$
|A \wr B| = |A|^{|B|} \cdot |B|.
$$

Substituting $|A| = p^n$ and $|B| = p^m$:

$$
|\mathbf{Z}_{p^n} \wr \mathbf{Z}_{p^m}| = (p^n)^{p^m} \cdot p^m = p^{n \cdot p^m
} \cdot p^m = p^{\,n\, p^m + m}.
$$

**Comparison with $p^{m+n}$.** The claimed order is $p^{m+n}$. For these to be e
qual we need

$$
n\, p^m + m = m + n \iff n\, p^m = n \iff p^m = 1 \iff m = 0.
$$

Since $p$ is a prime and $m \geq 1$ in any nontrivial case, $p^m \geq p \geq 2 >
 1$, so $n\,p^m > n$ and therefore

$$
n\, p^m + m > n + m.
$$

**Conclusion.** The order of $\mathbf{Z}_{p^n} \wr \mathbf{Z}_{p^m}$ is $p^{\,n\
,p^m + m}$, which is **strictly greater** than $p^{m+n}$ for every $m \geq 1$ (a
nd any $n \geq 1$). The two are equal only in the degenerate case $m = 0$ (where
 $B$ is trivial and the wreath product reduces to $A$ itself). Hence the answer
is:

$$
\boxed{|\mathbf{Z}_{p^n} \wr \mathbf{Z}_{p^m}| = p^{\,n\,p^m + m} \neq p^{m+n} \
text{ for } m \geq 1.}
$$
