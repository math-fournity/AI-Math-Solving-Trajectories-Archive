Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether equality of stalks at a poi

**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.** Choose an affine open neighborhood $V = \operatorname{Spec} A$ of $x$
. Since $\mathcal{I}$ and $\mathcal{J}$ are coherent, they are of finite type, s
o $\mathcal{I}|_V = \widetilde{I}$ and $\mathcal{J}|_V = \widetilde{J}$ for fini
tely generated ideals $I, J \subseteq A$. Let $x$ correspond to the prime $\math
frak{p} \in \operatorname{Spec} A$.

The hypothesis $\mathcal{I}_x = \mathcal{J}_x$ means $I_\mathfrak{p} = J_\mathfr
ak{p}$ as ideals of $A_\mathfrak{p}$.

**Step 1: Find $s$ with $sI \subseteq J$.** Since $I$ is finitely generated, wri
te $I = (a_1, \ldots, a_m)$. The inclusion $I_\mathfrak{p} \subseteq J_\mathfrak
{p}$ means that for each generator $a_i$, there exists $s_i \in A \setminus \mat
hfrak{p}$ with $s_i a_i \in J$. Set $s = s_1 \cdots s_m \in A \setminus \mathfra
k{p}$. Then $s \cdot a_i = \left(\prod_{j \neq i} s_j\right) \cdot s_i a_i \in J
$ for each $i$, so $s \cdot I \subseteq J$.

**Step 2: Find $t$ with $tJ \subseteq I$.** By the same argument applied to the
inclusion $J_\mathfrak{p} \subseteq I_\mathfrak{p}$, using finite generation of
$J$, there exists $t \in A \setminus \mathfrak{p}$ with $t \cdot J \subseteq I$.

**Step 3: Conclude on $D(st)$.** Set $U = D(st) \subseteq V$. Since $s, t \notin
 \mathfrak{p}$, we have $st \notin \mathfrak{p}$, so $x \in U$. In the localizat
ion $A_{st}$, both $s$ and $t$ are units. From $sI \subseteq J$ and $s$ being a
unit in $A_{st}$, every $a \in I$ satisfies $a = s^{-1}(sa) \in J_{st}$, giving
$I_{st} \subseteq J_{st}$. From $tJ \subseteq I$ and $t$ being a unit in $A_{st}
$, similarly $J_{st} \subseteq I_{st}$. Therefore $I_{st} = J_{st}$, which gives
 $\mathcal{I}|_U = \mathcal{J}|_U$. $\square$

**Remark.** The argument only uses that $\mathcal{I}$ and $\mathcal{J}$ are of f
inite type (finite generation of the corresponding ideals on affines); full cohe
rence and the integrality of $X$ are not needed. The key mechanism is that finit
e generation lets us "clear denominators uniformly": instead of needing a differ
ent multiplier for each element of the ideal, a single multiplier $s$ (resp. $t$
) works for all generators simultaneously.
