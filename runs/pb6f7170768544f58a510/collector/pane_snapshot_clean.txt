## Proof

Let $G = (\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}) \star (\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z})$. W
rite $A = \langle a, b \mid [a,b]=1 \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ and $B = \langle
 c, d \mid [c,d]=1 \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$, so
$$G = A \star B = \langle a, b, c, d \mid [a,b]=1,\; [c,d]=1 \rangle.$$
Let $F = [G, G]$.

**Step 1: $F$ is a free group (Kurosh's theorem).**

$G = A \star B$ acts on its Bass–Serre tree $T$ with vertex stabilizers being co
njugates of $A$ or $B$, and trivial edge stabilizers. The abelianization map $\a
lpha: G \to G_{\mathrm{ab}} \cong \mathbb{Z}^4$ sends $a \mapsto e_1, b \mapsto
e_2, c \mapsto e_3, d \mapsto e_4$. Since $A$ and $B$ are abelian, $\alpha$ rest
ricted to any conjugate $gAg^{-1}$ or $gBg^{-1}$ is injective, so
$$F \cap gAg^{-1} = \ker(\alpha) \cap gAg^{-1} = \{e\}, \qquad F \cap gBg^{-1} =
 \{e\}.$$
By Kurosh's subgroup theorem, $F$ is a free product of conjugates of these trivi
al intersections and a free group — hence $F$ is **free**.

**Step 2: $F$ has rank $\geq 2$.**

$F$ is nontrivial: $a$ and $c$ lie in different factors of the free product, so
the commutator $[a,c] = aca^{-1}c^{-1}$ is a nontrivial element of $F$.

$F$ is non-abelian: Consider $[a,c]$ and $[b,c]$, both in $F$. Their products in
 $G$ have normal forms:
$$[a,c]\,[b,c] = a\cdot c \cdot a^{-1}\cdot c^{-1}\cdot b\cdot c\cdot b^{-1}\cdo
t c^{-1},$$
$$[b,c]\,[a,c] = b\cdot c \cdot b^{-1}\cdot c^{-1}\cdot a\cdot c\cdot a^{-1}\cdo
t c^{-1}.$$
Both are reduced words of length 8 (adjacent letters always lie in different fac
tors), but the first begins with $a \in A$ while the second begins with $b \in A
$. Since these are distinct reduced words, $[a,c]$ and $[b,c]$ do **not** commut
e. Therefore $F$ is non-abelian, hence a free group of rank $\geq 2$.

**Step 3: $N = [F, F]$ is the desired subgroup.**

Set $N = [F, F]$, the commutator subgroup of $F$.

- **Normal in $F$**: $N$ is a commutator subgroup, hence characteristic (and thu
s normal) in $F$.
- **Nontrivial**: $F$ is non-abelian (rank $\geq 2$), so $[F,F] \neq \{e\}$.
- **Proper**: $F/N \cong F_{\mathrm{ab}} \cong \mathbb{Z}^r$ with $r \geq 2$, so
 $N \neq F$.
- **Infinite index**: $[F : N] = |F/N| = |\mathbb{Z}^r| = \infty$.

Therefore $N = [F, F]$ is a proper, nontrivial, normal subgroup of $F$ with infi
nite index. $\blacksquare$
