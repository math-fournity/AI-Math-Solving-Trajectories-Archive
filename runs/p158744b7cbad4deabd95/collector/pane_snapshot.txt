## Proof

Let $A_n = \{x : f(x) \ge n\}$. We must show $\lim_{n\to\infty} n\cdot m(A_n) =
0$.

**Step 1. $f < \infty$ a.e.**

Suppose $m(\{f = \infty\}) > 0$. Then we may choose a Lebesgue measurable subset
 $F \subseteq \{f = \infty\}$ with $0 < m(F) < 1$ (if $m(\{f=\infty\}) \ge 1$, t
ake a subset of measure $\tfrac12$; otherwise take $F = \{f = \infty\}$ itself).
 On $F$ we have $f \equiv \infty$ on a set of positive measure, so $\int_F f\,dm
 = \infty$, contradicting the hypothesis that $\int_E f\,dm < \infty$ for every
measurable $E$ with $m(E) < 1$. Hence
$$m(\{f = \infty\}) = 0. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2. $m(A_n) \to 0$, and $\int_{A_n} f\,dm < \infty$ for all large $n$.**

The sets $A_n$ are nested decreasing, $A_n \searrow \{f = \infty\}$. By (1) and
continuity from above,
$$m(A_n) \to m(\{f=\infty\}) = 0.$$
Thus for all sufficiently large $n$, $m(A_n) < 1$, and the hypothesis gives
$$\int_{A_n} f\,dm < \infty. \tag{2}$$

**Step 3. $\int_{A_n} f\,dm \to 0$.**

Define $h_n = f\cdot \mathbf{1}_{A_n}$. Since $A_n \searrow$, the sequence $\{h_
n\}$ is nonnegative and decreasing. By (1),
$$h_n \searrow f\cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{f=\infty\}} = 0 \quad\text{a.e.}$$
By (2) there exists $N$ with $\int h_N = \int_{A_N} f\,dm < \infty$. Applying th
e downward monotone convergence theorem to the decreasing sequence $\{h_n\}_{n\g
e N}$ (which has finite integral at $n=N$),
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{A_n} f\,dm = \lim_{n\to\infty}\int h_n\,dm = \int 0\,dm
 = 0. \tag{3}$$

**Step 4. Conclusion.**

On $A_n$ we have $f \ge n$, so
$$n\cdot m(A_n) = \int_{A_n} n\,dm \le \int_{A_n} f\,dm.$$
By (3) the right-hand side tends to $0$, and the left-hand side is nonnegative,
so
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} n\cdot m(\{f \ge n\}) = 0.$$
