## Proof

**Setup.** Let $V = \mathbb{F}_p^3$ equipped with the standard dot product $B(u,
v) = u\cdot v = u_1v_1+u_2v_2+u_3v_3$, which is a non-degenerate symmetric bilin
ear form (its matrix is $I_3$, with determinant $1\neq 0$). The vertices of $G_p
$ are the one-dimensional subspaces of $V$, i.e., the points of the projective p
lane $\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{F}_p)$. Two vertices $L, L'$ are adjacent iff $B(u,v)
=0$ for (equivalently, all) representatives $u\in L, v\in L'$.

**Vertex count.** The number of one-dimensional subspaces is
$$\frac{p^3-1}{p-1}=p^2+p+1.$$

**Orthogonal complements.** For a one-dimensional subspace $L=\langle u\rangle$,
 the orthogonal complement $L^\perp=\{w\in V: B(u,w)=0\}$ is a two-dimensional s
ubspace (since $B$ is non-degenerate). A two-dimensional subspace of $\mathbb{F}
_p^3$ contains exactly $\frac{p^2-1}{p-1}=p+1$ one-dimensional subspaces.

**Isotropic vs. non-isotropic.** A subspace $L=\langle u\rangle$ is *isotropic*
if $B(u,u)=u\cdot u=0$, equivalently $L\subseteq L^\perp$. Otherwise $L$ is *non
-isotropic* and $L\not\subseteq L^\perp$.

- If $L$ is isotropic: $L^\perp$ contains $p+1$ one-dimensional subspaces, one o
f which is $L$ itself, so $L$ has **degree $p$**.
- If $L$ is non-isotropic: $L^\perp$ contains $p+1$ one-dimensional subspaces, n
one equal to $L$, so $L$ has **degree $p+1$**.

**Counting isotropic subspaces.** We count the number of solutions to $a^2+b^2+c
^2=0$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ using Gauss sums. Let $\omega=e^{2\pi i/p}$ and $\chi$
 the Legendre symbol. The number of solutions is
$$N=\sum_{a,b,c\in\mathbb{F}_p}\frac{1}{p}\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}_p}\omega^{s(a^2+b
^2+c^2)}=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}_p}\tau(s)^3,$$
where $\tau(s)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_p}\omega^{sa^2}$. We have $\tau(0)=p$ and $\
tau(s)=\chi(s)\,g$ for $s\neq 0$, where $g$ is the Gauss sum satisfying $g^2=\ch
i(-1)\,p$. Thus
$$N=\frac{1}{p}\Big(p^3+g^3\sum_{s\neq 0}\chi(s)^3\Big)=\frac{1}{p}\Big(p^3+g^3\
sum_{s\neq 0}\chi(s)\Big)=\frac{p^3}{p}=p^2,$$
since $\chi(s)^3=\chi(s)$ for $s\neq 0$ and $\sum_{s\neq 0}\chi(s)=0$. The numbe
r of nonzero solutions is $p^2-1$, and since each one-dimensional subspace has $
p-1$ nonzero vectors, the number of isotropic one-dimensional subspaces is
$$\frac{p^2-1}{p-1}=p+1.$$

**Edge count.** The number of non-isotropic vertices is $(p^2+p+1)-(p+1)=p^2$. T
he sum of degrees is
$$(p+1)\cdot p \;+\; p^2\cdot(p+1) \;=\; p(p+1)+p^2(p+1)=(p+1)(p+p^2)=p(p+1)^2.$
$
By the handshaking lemma, the number of edges is
$$\boxed{\dfrac{p(p+1)^2}{2}}.$$

**Verification.** For $p=2$: $7$ vertices, $3$ isotropic (degree $2$), $4$ non-i
sotropic (degree $3$); sum of degrees $= 3\cdot2+4\cdot3=18$, edges $=9=\frac{2\
cdot9}{2}$. ✓ Direct enumeration of all $7$ vertices of $\mathbb{F}_2^3$ confirm
s exactly $9$ edges. For $p=3$: $13$ vertices, $4$ isotropic (degree $3$), $9$ n
on-isotropic (degree $4$); sum $=48$, edges $=24=\frac{3\cdot16}{2}$. ✓
