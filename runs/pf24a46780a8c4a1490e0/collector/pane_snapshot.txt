## Solution

**Setup.** Four couples $(H_i, W_i)$ for $i=1,2,3,4$ are seated in a row of 8 se
ats. The constraint is: each woman's neighbors (if any) must be either her husba
nd or another woman. Equivalently, the only man–woman adjacencies permitted are
between married couples.

**Key structural observation.** If a man $H$ were adjacent to two women, both wo
uld have to be his wife — impossible. Likewise a woman adjacent to two men would
 need both to be her husband — impossible. Therefore in the gender pattern (the
M/W sequence of length 8 with four M's and four W's), no substring `MWM` or `WMW
` may occur. Hence every *interior* run (maximal block of one gender) has length
 $\ge 2$, while end runs may have length $\ge 1$.

**Counting gender patterns by number of runs $t$.** Let the runs have lengths $l
_1,\dots,l_t$ (alternating gender), $\sum l_i = 8$, with $\sum_{\text{M-runs}} l
_i = \sum_{\text{W-runs}} l_i = 4$, interior $l_i \ge 2$, end $l_i \ge 1$.

- **$t=2$** (one M-run, one W-run): `MMMMWWWW`, `WWWWMMMM` — **2 patterns**, $k=
1$ boundary.
- **$t=3$** (M-W-M or W-M-W): middle run has length 4; end runs split as $(1,3),
(2,2),(3,1)$ — $3+3=$ **6 patterns**, $k=2$ boundaries.
- **$t=4$** (M-W-M-W or W-M-W-M): interior runs $\ge 2$; M-split $(l_1,l_3)\in\{
(1,3),(2,2)\}$, W-split $(l_2,l_4)\in\{(2,2),(3,1)\}$, giving $2\cdot2=4$ each —
 **8 patterns**, $k=3$ boundaries.
- **$t=5$** (M-W-M-W-M or W-M-W-M-W): three interior runs $\ge 2$ forces lengths
 $(1,2,2,2,1)$ — **2 patterns**, $k=4$ boundaries.
- **$t\ge 6$**: interior runs alone need $\ge 2(t-2)\ge 8$ seats plus two end ru
ns $\ge 1$ each $\Rightarrow \ge 10 > 8$. Impossible.

Total: $2+6+8+2 = 18$ gender patterns.

**Counting person assignments for a pattern with $k$ boundaries.** Each boundary
 is an M–W adjacency that must be a married couple. The $k$ boundaries use $k$ d
istinct men and $k$ distinct women (no person sits in two boundary seats), paire
d as $k$ married couples. The boundary seats are distinguishable (fixed position
s), so:
- Choose and assign $k$ couples to the $k$ boundary seats: $P(4,k) = \tfrac{4!}{
(4-k)!}$ ways.
- Place the remaining $4-k$ men in the $4-k$ non-boundary male seats: $(4-k)!$ w
ays.
- Place the remaining $4-k$ women in the $4-k$ non-boundary female seats: $(4-k)
!$ ways.

Non-boundary people sit only next to same-gender neighbors (boundary people buff
er them from the opposite gender), so no further constraints arise. The count pe
r pattern is

$$\frac{4!}{(4-k)!}\cdot(4-k)!\cdot(4-k)! = 4!\cdot(4-k)! = 24\cdot(4-k)!.$$

**Summing over all patterns:**

| $t$ | $k=t-1$ | # patterns | count each | subtotal |
|-----|---------|-----------|------------|----------|
| 2 | 1 | 2 | $24\cdot 6 = 144$ | $288$ |
| 3 | 2 | 6 | $24\cdot 2 = 48$ | $288$ |
| 4 | 3 | 8 | $24\cdot 1 = 24$ | $192$ |
| 5 | 4 | 2 | $24\cdot 1 = 24$ | $48$ |

$$\text{Total} = 288 + 288 + 192 + 48 = \boxed{816}.$$

**Verification on a smaller case.** For two couples (4 people), the same method
gives $t=2$: 2 patterns $\times\, 2!\cdot1! = 4$; $t=3$: 2 patterns $\times\, 2!
\cdot0! = 4$; total $8$. Inclusion–exclusion confirms: $24 - 12 - 12 + 8 = 8$. ✓
