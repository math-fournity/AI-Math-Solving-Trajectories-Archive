**Computing the first polynomials:**

- $P_0(x)=x,\; Q_0(x)=x$.
- $P_1(x)=x^2-(-1)^1\cdot 1=x^2+1\ge 1$ → **no real roots**.
- $Q_1(x)=x^2+(-1)^1\cdot 1=x^2-1$ → roots $\pm 1$.
- $P_2(x)=(x^2+1)^2-(-1)^2\cdot 2=(x^2+1)^2-2$. Setting $y=x^2\ge 0$: $y^2+2y-1=
0 \Rightarrow y=-1\pm\sqrt{2}$. Only $y=-1+\sqrt{2}>0$ is valid, giving $x=\pm\s
qrt{\sqrt{2}-1}$ → **two real roots**.
- $Q_2(x)=(x^2-1)^2+2$. With $y=x^2\ge 0$: $y^2-2y+3=(y-1)^2+2\ge 2$ → **no real
 roots**.

**Tracking minimum values to show all higher polynomials are strictly positive:*
*

| $k$ | $P_k$ min | $Q_k$ min |
|-----|-----------|-----------|
| 1 | $1$ | $-1$ |
| 2 | $-1$ | $2$ |
| 3 | $P_2^2+3\ge 3$ | $Q_2^2-3\ge 4-3=1$ |
| 4 | $P_3^2-4\ge 9-4=5$ | $Q_3^2+4\ge 1+4=5$ |
| 5 | $P_4^2+5\ge 30$ | $Q_4^2-5\ge 20$ |
| $\ge 6$ | grows $\to\infty$ | grows $\to\infty$ |

From $P_3, Q_3$ onward every polynomial is $\geq 1 > 0$, and from $k\ge 4$ every
 polynomial is $\geq 5$ with values growing without bound. Hence **no real roots
 exist for $k\ge 3$**.

**Collecting all distinct real roots:**

The only real roots come from $Q_1$ and $P_2$:
$$\left\{\,1,\;-1,\;\sqrt{\sqrt{2}-1},\;-\sqrt{\sqrt{2}-1}\,\right\}$$

These four values are distinct (numerically $\approx 1, -1, 0.644, -0.644$).

**Computing the product:**

$$\prod = (1)(-1)\left(\sqrt{\sqrt{2}-1}\right)\left(-\sqrt{\sqrt{2}-1}\right)=(
-1)\cdot\bigl(-(\sqrt{2}-1)\bigr)=\sqrt{2}-1.$$

$$\boxed{\sqrt{2}-1}$$
