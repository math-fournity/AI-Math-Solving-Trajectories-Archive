## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, the condition implies $E[|X|] < \infty$. In fact, the condition
with $\epsilon = 1$ alone suffices.

**Key identity.** For any non-negative random variable $Y$,
$$E[Y] = \int_0^\infty P[Y > t]\,dt.$$
This follows from Fubini/Tonelli: $E[Y] = E\!\left[\int_0^Y 1\,dt\right] = \int_
0^\infty P[Y > t]\,dt$.

Applying this with $Y = |X|$:
$$E[|X|] = \int_0^\infty P[|X| > t]\,dt.$$

**Comparison between the integral and the sum.** Since $t \mapsto P[|X|>t]$ is n
on-increasing, on each interval $[n, n+1]$ (for $n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$):
$$P[|X| > n+1] \;\leq\; P[|X| > t] \;\leq\; P[|X| > n], \qquad t \in [n, n+1].$$
Integrating over $[n,n+1]$ and summing over $n \geq 0$:
$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} P[|X| > n+1] \;\leq\; \int_0^\infty P[|X|>t]\,dt \;\leq\;
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} P[|X| > n].$$
That is,
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P[|X| > n] \;\leq\; E[|X|] \;\leq\; P[|X|>0] + \sum_{n=1}^
{\infty} P[|X| > n].$$
Since $P[|X|>0] \leq 1 < \infty$, we conclude:

$$E[|X|] < \infty \iff \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P[|X| > n] < \infty.$$

**Applying the hypothesis.** The hypothesis states that $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P[|
X| > n\epsilon] < \infty$ for **every** $\epsilon > 0$. Taking $\epsilon = 1$ (w
hich is permitted) yields
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P[|X| > n] < \infty,$$
and by the equivalence above, $E[|X|] < \infty$.

**Remark.** The full strength of "for any $\epsilon > 0$" is not needed; the sin
gle case $\epsilon = 1$ already forces $E[|X|] < \infty$. The stronger condition
 (all $\epsilon > 0$) is related to almost sure finiteness of $|X|/\epsilon$ via
 Borel–Cantelli, but for the conclusion about $E[|X|]$, the comparison at unit s
pacing is sufficient.
