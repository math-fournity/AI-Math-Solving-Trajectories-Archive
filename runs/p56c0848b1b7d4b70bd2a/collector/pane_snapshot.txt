## Volume Element in a Riemannian Space

**Setup.** Let $(M, g)$ be an $n$-dimensional Riemannian manifold with local coo
rdinates $(y^1, \ldots, y^n)$ and metric tensor $g = g_{ij}\,dy^i \otimes dy^j$,
 where $g_{ij} = g(\partial_i, \partial_j)$ is a symmetric, positive-definite $n
 \times n$ matrix at each point.

**Goal.** Derive the coordinate expression for the Riemannian volume form $dV$.

---

### Step 1. The metric as an inner product on the tangent space

At each point $p \in M$, the metric $g_{ij}(p)$ defines an inner product on $T_p
M$. Choosing the coordinate basis $\{\partial_1, \ldots, \partial_n\}$, the squa
red length of an infinitesimal displacement $d\mathbf{y} = \sum_i dy^i \partial_
i$ is

$$ds^2 = g_{ij}\,dy^i dy^j.$$

### Step 2. Volume of the coordinate parallelepiped

The coordinate differentials $(dy^1, \ldots, dy^n)$ span an infinitesimal parall
elepiped in $T_pM$ with edge vectors $\{\partial_1\,dy^1, \ldots, \partial_n\,dy
^n\}$. Its (Euclidean-computed) volume equals the absolute value of the determin
ant of the matrix whose columns are these edge vectors. But the edges are expres
sed in the coordinate basis itself, so the "geometric" volume must account for t
he distortion introduced by $g$.

Concretely, the Gram matrix of the edge vectors $\{\partial_1, \ldots, \partial_
n\}$ under the inner product $g$ is exactly $G = (g_{ij})$. A standard linear-al
gebra identity gives the volume of the parallelepiped spanned by vectors with Gr
am matrix $G$ as

$$\text{Vol} = \sqrt{\det G}.$$

### Step 3. Deriving $\sqrt{\det G}$ via orthonormalization

To see the factor $\sqrt{\det G}$ rigorously, apply the Gram–Schmidt process to
the coordinate basis. Equivalently, since $G$ is symmetric positive-definite, th
ere exists a matrix $A$ with $G = A^T A$ (e.g. the Cholesky factor). The linear
map $A^{-1}$ sends the coordinate basis to an orthonormal basis, and any orthono
rmal basis has unit parallelepiped volume. The Jacobian of this change-of-basis
is $|\det A^{-1}| = 1/\sqrt{\det G}$. Therefore the coordinate parallelepiped, w
hich has unit coordinate volume $dy^1\cdots dy^n$, has geometric volume

$$dV = \sqrt{\det G}\;dy^1\cdots dy^n.$$

### Step 4. Coordinate-independence check

Under a change of coordinates $y^i \to \tilde y^a$, the metric transforms as

$$\tilde g_{ab} = \frac{\partial y^i}{\partial \tilde y^a}\frac{\partial y^j}{\p
artial \tilde y^b}\,g_{ij},$$

so by the multiplicative property of the determinant,

$$\det \tilde G = \left(\det \frac{\partial y}{\partial \tilde y}\right)^2 \det
G.$$

Meanwhile the coordinate volume element transforms as

$$dy^1\cdots dy^n = \left|\det \frac{\partial y}{\partial \tilde y}\right|\,d\ti
lde y^1\cdots d\tilde y^n.$$

The two Jacobian factors cancel:

$$\sqrt{\det G}\,dy^1\cdots dy^n = \sqrt{\det \tilde G}\,d\tilde y^1\cdots d\til
de y^n,$$

confirming that $dV$ is a well-defined scalar density (the Riemannian volume for
m), independent of the choice of coordinates.

### Step 5. Differential-form viewpoint (consistency check)

The volume form can also be written as the $n$-form

$$dV = \sqrt{\det(g_{ij})}\;dy^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dy^n.$$

One verifies this is the unique $n$-form $\omega$ with $\omega(e_1,\ldots,e_n)=1
$ for every *positively-oriented orthonormal* frame $\{e_a\}$: writing $e_a = E_
a^{\,i}\partial_i$, orthonormality gives $E^T G E = I$, hence $\det E = 1/\sqrt{
\det G}$, and

$$\omega(e_1,\ldots,e_n) = \sqrt{\det G}\,\det E = 1. \checkmark$$

---

### Result

The Riemannian volume element in coordinates $(y^1,\ldots,y^n)$ is

$$\boxed{\,dV = \sqrt{\det(g_{ij})}\;dy^1\cdots dy^n = \sqrt{g}\;dy^1\cdots dy^n
\,}$$

where $g := \det(g_{ij})$. The factor $\sqrt{g}$ is the Jacobian that corrects t
he coordinate volume for the metric distortion, and the expression is invariant
under coordinate changes as shown in Step 4.
