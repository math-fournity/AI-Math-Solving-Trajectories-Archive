### Setup

We want to evaluate
$$S = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta(2k)-1}{k}.$$

Recall $\zeta(2k) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^{-2k}$, so $\zeta(2k)-1 = \sum_{n=2}^{
\infty} n^{-2k}$.

### Interchange of summation

Since every term $\frac{n^{-2k}}{k}$ is non-negative for $n\ge 2, k\ge 1$, Tonel
li's theorem justifies swapping the order of summation:

$$S = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} n^{-2k} = \sum_{n=2}^{\i
nfty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{-2k}}{k}.$$

### Inner sum via the logarithm series

For $|x|<1$, the classical identity $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^k}{k} = -\ln(1-x
)$ holds. Setting $x = n^{-2}\in(0,1)$ for each $n\ge 2$:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{-2k}}{k} = -\ln\!\left(1-\frac{1}{n^2}\right).$$

Hence
$$S = -\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\ln\!\left(1-\frac{1}{n^2}\right) = -\sum_{n=2}^{\inft
y}\ln\!\left(\frac{(n-1)(n+1)}{n^2}\right).$$

### Telescoping product

Exponentiating turns the sum of logs into a log of a product. Consider the parti
al product
$$P_N = \prod_{n=2}^{N}\frac{(n-1)(n+1)}{n^2} = \left(\prod_{n=2}^{N}\frac{n-1}{
n}\right)\!\left(\prod_{n=2}^{N}\frac{n+1}{n}\right).$$

The two factors telescope:
$$\prod_{n=2}^{N}\frac{n-1}{n} = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{3}\cdots\frac{N-1}{N}
= \frac{1}{N},$$
$$\prod_{n=2}^{N}\frac{n+1}{n} = \frac{3}{2}\cdot\frac{4}{3}\cdots\frac{N+1}{N}
= \frac{N+1}{2}.$$

Therefore
$$P_N = \frac{1}{N}\cdot\frac{N+1}{2} = \frac{N+1}{2N} \xrightarrow{N\to\infty}
\frac{1}{2}.$$

### Conclusion

The partial sums satisfy
$$S_N = -\ln P_N = -\ln\!\left(\frac{N+1}{2N}\right) \xrightarrow{N\to\infty} -\
ln\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = \ln 2.$$

Thus
$$\boxed{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta(2k)-1}{k} = \ln 2.}$$
