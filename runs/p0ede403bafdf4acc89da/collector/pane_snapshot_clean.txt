## Answer

**Yes, the bound $M$ can be equal to zero.** When $M=0$, the Phragmén-Lindelöf t
heorem reduces to a non-trivial uniqueness statement: the only analytic function
 satisfying the hypotheses is the identically zero function.

---

## Proof

**Setup.** Consider the standard Phragmén-Lindelöf theorem in a sector. Let $S =
 \{z \in \mathbb{C} : \alpha < \arg z < \beta\}$ be a sector of opening angle $\
theta = \beta - \alpha < \pi/\lambda$, with boundary rays $\partial S = \Gamma_1
 \cup \Gamma_2$. Suppose:

1. $f$ is analytic in $S$ and continuous on $\overline{S}$;
2. $|f(z)| \leq M$ for all $z \in \partial S$;
3. $|f(z)| \leq C\, e^{|z|^{\rho}}$ for some $\rho < \lambda$ and constant $C >
0$.

Then the conclusion is $|f(z)| \leq M$ for all $z \in S$.

**Claim.** The case $M = 0$ is admissible, and the proof of the theorem goes thr
ough without modification.

**Verification that the standard proof handles $M = 0$.**

The classical proof proceeds via an auxiliary function. Set $\theta = \beta - \a
lpha$ and choose $\mu$ with $\rho < \mu < \lambda$, so that $\mu\theta < \pi$. D
efine (after rotating so the sector is symmetric about the positive real axis)

$$g_\varepsilon(z) = f(z)\, e^{-\varepsilon\, z^{\mu}}, \qquad \varepsilon > 0,$
$

where the branch of $z^\mu$ is chosen so that $z^\mu > 0$ on the bisector of $S$
.

*On the boundary rays:* For $z \in \partial S$, we have $\arg z = \alpha$ or $\a
rg z = \beta$, so $\arg(z^\mu) = \mu\alpha$ or $\mu\beta$. Since $\mu\theta = \m
u(\beta - \alpha) < \pi$, the real part $\operatorname{Re}(z^\mu) \geq |z|^\mu \
cos(\mu\theta/2) > 0$ on the boundary rays (after centering). Hence $|e^{-\varep
silon z^\mu}| = e^{-\varepsilon \operatorname{Re}(z^\mu)} \leq 1$, giving

$$|g_\varepsilon(z)| \leq |f(z)| \leq M \quad \text{on } \partial S.$$

*On the circular arc $|z| = R$:* Using the growth condition $|f(z)| \leq C e^{R^
\rho}$ and $|e^{-\varepsilon z^\mu}| = e^{-\varepsilon R^\mu \cos(\mu\theta/2)}$
,

$$|g_\varepsilon(z)| \leq C\, e^{R^\rho - \varepsilon R^\mu \cos(\mu\theta/2)}.$
$

Since $\mu > \rho$, for $R$ sufficiently large the exponent is negative, so $|g_
\varepsilon(z)| \leq M$ on the arc $|z| = R$ as well.

*Applying the maximum principle:* On the truncated sector $S_R = S \cap \{|z| \l
eq R\}$, the function $g_\varepsilon$ is analytic in the interior and $|g_\varep
silon| \leq M$ on $\partial S_R$. By the maximum modulus principle,

$$|g_\varepsilon(z)| \leq M \quad \text{for all } z \in S_R.$$

Letting $R \to \infty$ and then $\varepsilon \to 0^+$ (since $|e^{-\varepsilon z
^\mu}| \to 1$), we obtain

$$|f(z)| \leq M \quad \text{for all } z \in S.$$

**The case $M = 0$.** Every step above remains valid when $M = 0$:

- The boundary estimate $|g_\varepsilon(z)| \leq 0$ on $\partial S$ forces $g_\v
arepsilon \equiv 0$ on $\partial S$.
- The arc estimate $|g_\varepsilon(z)| \leq 0$ on $|z| = R$ (for $R$ large) forc
es $g_\varepsilon \equiv 0$ there.
- The maximum principle gives $|g_\varepsilon(z)| \leq 0$ in $S_R$, hence $g_\va
repsilon \equiv 0$ in $S_R$.
- Since $e^{-\varepsilon z^\mu} \neq 0$, this gives $f \equiv 0$ in $S_R$ for ev
ery $R$, hence $f \equiv 0$ in $S$.

No division by $M$, no reliance on $M > 0$, and no step requires $M$ to be stric
tly positive. The argument is purely an application of the maximum principle to
a non-negative quantity bounded above by $M$, which is equally valid when $M = 0
$.

**Interpretation.** When $M = 0$, the hypothesis $|f| \leq 0$ on $\partial S$ me
ans $f$ vanishes on the boundary rays. The growth condition $|f(z)| \leq C e^{|z
|^\rho}$ with $\rho < \lambda$ is the standard sub-exponential (relative to the
sector opening) growth bound. The conclusion $f \equiv 0$ is then a genuine uniq
ueness theorem:

> *If an analytic function in a sector of opening $< \pi/\lambda$ vanishes on th
e boundary and grows no faster than $e^{|z|^\rho}$ with $\rho < \lambda$, then i
t is identically zero.*

This is non-vacuous: without the growth condition, there exist non-trivial analy
tic functions vanishing on the boundary of a sector (e.g., $\sin(\pi z / \theta)
$ in a half-plane, which grows like $e^{|z|}$). The growth restriction is what f
orces vanishing, and the case $M = 0$ is precisely the statement that this restr
iction is sharp enough to enforce uniqueness.

**Conclusion.** The bound $M$ in the Phragmén-Lindelöf theorem can indeed be equ
al to zero. The standard proof applies without any modification, and the resulti
ng conclusion $|f(z)| \leq 0$, i.e., $f \equiv 0$, is a meaningful and non-trivi
al uniqueness result.
