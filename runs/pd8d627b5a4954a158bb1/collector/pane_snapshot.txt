## Proof

We use the **Principle of Inclusion-Exclusion**.

Let the 12 sets be $A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_{12}$, each with $|A_i| = 8$.

The given conditions are:
- Every pair: $|A_i \cap A_j| = 6$ for all $i \neq j$.
- Every triple: $|A_i \cap A_j \cap A_k| = 4$ for all distinct $i, j, k$.
- Every quadruple (and higher): $|A_i \cap A_j \cap A_k \cap A_l| = 0$ for all d
istinct $i,j,k,l$.

Since no 4 sets share any element, every intersection of 4 or more sets is empty
, so all higher-order terms vanish.

By inclusion-exclusion, the total number of distinct elements is:

$$\left|\bigcup_{i=1}^{12} A_i\right| = \sum_{k=1}^{12}(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{\substack
{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,12\}\\|S|=k}}\left|\bigcap_{i\in S}A_i\right|$$

Computing each term:

**$k=1$ (single sets):**
$$\sum_{|S|=1}\left|\bigcap_{i\in S}A_i\right| = \binom{12}{1}\cdot 8 = 12 \cdot
 8 = 96$$

**$k=2$ (pairs):**
$$\sum_{|S|=2}\left|\bigcap_{i\in S}A_i\right| = \binom{12}{2}\cdot 6 = \frac{12
\cdot 11}{2}\cdot 6 = 66\cdot 6 = 396$$

**$k=3$ (triples):**
$$\sum_{|S|=3}\left|\bigcap_{i\in S}A_i\right| = \binom{12}{3}\cdot 4 = \frac{12
\cdot 11\cdot 10}{6}\cdot 4 = 220\cdot 4 = 880$$

**$k\geq 4$:** Every intersection of 4 or more sets is empty (given), so all the
se terms are $0$.

Substituting with alternating signs:

$$\left|\bigcup_{i=1}^{12}A_i\right| = 96 - 396 + 880 - 0 + 0 - \cdots = 96 - 39
6 + 880 = 580$$

Therefore, the total number of distinct elements in the collection is $\boxed{58
0}$.
