## Proof

**Answer: Yes, $R$ is semiprime.**

We prove that every ideal $I$ with $I^2=0$ must be $I=0$.

---

**Setup.** Let $e$ be a noncentral idempotent (which exists by hypothesis). Then
 $1-e$ is also a noncentral idempotent, since $(1-e)r - r(1-e) = -(er-re)$. Appl
ying the hypothesis to both $e$ and $1-e$:

- $eRe$ and $(1-e)R(1-e)$ are division rings,
- $eR(1-e)Re \neq 0$ and $(1-e)ReR(1-e) \neq 0$.

Both $e\neq 0$ and $1-e\neq 0$ (since $e=0$ or $e=1$ would make $e$ central).

**Step 1: Factoring the idempotents.** From $eR(1-e)Re\neq 0$, there exist $a\in
 eR(1-e)$, $b\in(1-e)Re$ with $ab\neq 0$ in $eRe$. Since $eRe$ is a division rin
g, $ab$ is invertible in $eRe$, so
$$e = (ab)(ab)^{-1} = a\cdot b(ab)^{-1}, \quad a\in eR(1-e),\; b(ab)^{-1}\in(1-e
)Re.$$
Write $e = a_1 b_1$ with $a_1\in eR(1-e)$, $b_1\in(1-e)Re$.

Similarly, from $(1-e)ReR(1-e)\neq 0$, there exist $b'\in(1-e)Re$, $a'\in eR(1-e
)$ with $b'a'\neq 0$ in $(1-e)R(1-e)$ (which is a division ring), giving
$$1-e = b_2 a_2, \quad b_2\in(1-e)Re,\; a_2\in eR(1-e).$$

**Step 2: Diagonal Peirce components of $I$ vanish.** Let $I\trianglelefteq R$ w
ith $I^2=0$. For any $x\in I$: if $exe\neq 0$, then $exe\in eIe\subseteq eRe$ is
 a unit in $eRe$, so $e = exe\cdot(exe)^{-1}\in I\cdot R\subseteq I$, whence $e=
e^2\in I^2=0$, contradiction. Thus $exe=0$. Applying the same argument to $1-e$
gives $(1-e)x(1-e)=0$.

Therefore every $x\in I$ satisfies $x = ex(1-e)+(1-e)xe$.

**Step 3: Off-diagonal components also vanish.** Take $0\neq x\in I$ and a nonce
ntral idempotent $e$. Then $x=ex(1-e)+(1-e)xe$; at least one summand is nonzero.

*Case A: $ex(1-e)\neq 0$.* Using $e=a_1 b_1$:
$$ex(1-e) = e\cdot ex(1-e) = a_1\bigl(b_1\cdot ex(1-e)\bigr).$$
Now $ex(1-e)\in eI(1-e)\subseteq I$ (since $I$ is a two-sided ideal), so $b_1\cd
ot ex(1-e)\in I$ (left ideal). Also $b_1\cdot ex(1-e)\in(1-e)Re\cdot eR(1-e)\sub
seteq(1-e)R(1-e)$. By Step 2 applied to $1-e$, every element of $I$ has zero $(1
-e)$-diagonal, so $b_1\cdot ex(1-e)=0$. Hence $ex(1-e)=a_1\cdot 0=0$, contradict
ion.

*Case B: $(1-e)xe\neq 0$.* Using $1-e=b_2 a_2$:
$$(1-e)xe = (1-e)\cdot(1-e)xe = b_2\bigl(a_2\cdot(1-e)xe\bigr).$$
Now $(1-e)xe\in(1-e)Ie\subseteq I$, so $a_2\cdot(1-e)xe\in I$ (left ideal). Also
 $a_2\cdot(1-e)xe\in eR(1-e)\cdot(1-e)Re\subseteq eRe$. By Step 2 applied to $e$
, every element of $I$ has zero $e$-diagonal, so $a_2\cdot(1-e)xe=0$. Hence $(1-
e)xe=b_2\cdot 0=0$, contradiction.

Both cases are impossible, so $x=0$. Since $x\in I$ was arbitrary, $I=0$.

**Conclusion.** Every ideal $I$ with $I^2=0$ is zero, which is precisely the sta
tement that $R$ is semiprime. $\blacksquare$
