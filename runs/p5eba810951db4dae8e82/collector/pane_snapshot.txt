## Proof

**Setup.** Recall that a measure $\mu$ defined by
$$\mu(E) = \int_{E \cap B(0,1)} \sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}\, d\lambda_3(x,y,z)$$
is absolutely continuous with respect to $\lambda_3$, with Radon–Nikodym derivat
ive
$$\frac{d\mu}{d\lambda_3}(x,y,z) = \sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}\;\cdot\;\mathbf{1}_{B(0,1)
}(x,y,z).$$
Denote $r(x,y,z) = \sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}$.

**Integrability.** The integrand $g(x,y,z) = xy^2 + y^2z$ is a polynomial, hence
 continuous and bounded on the compact set $\overline{B(0,1)}$. The density $r\c
dot\mathbf{1}_{B(0,1)}$ is bounded by $1$ on $B(0,1)$. Therefore $g$ is $\mu$-in
tegrable and $g\cdot r\cdot\mathbf{1}_{B(0,1)}$ is $\lambda_3$-integrable, so al
l integrals below are well-defined and finite.

**Transforming the left-hand side.** By the Radon–Nikodym theorem, for any $\mu$
-integrable function $g$,
$$\int g\, d\mu = \int g\cdot\frac{d\mu}{d\lambda_3}\, d\lambda_3 = \int g\cdot
r\cdot\mathbf{1}_{B(0,1)}\, d\lambda_3.$$

Applying this to $g = (xy^2+y^2z)\cdot\mathbf{1}_{B(0,2)}$ (the integrand restri
cted to the domain of integration $B(0,2)$):
$$\int_{B(0,2)}(xy^2+y^2z)\,d\mu = \int_{\mathbb{R}^3}(xy^2+y^2z)\,\mathbf{1}_{B
(0,2)}\cdot r\cdot\mathbf{1}_{B(0,1)}\,d\lambda_3.$$

**Key observation.** Since $B(0,1)\subset B(0,2)$, we have
$$\mathbf{1}_{B(0,2)}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{B(0,1)} = \mathbf{1}_{B(0,1)}.$$

Therefore
$$\int_{B(0,2)}(xy^2+y^2z)\,d\mu = \int_{B(0,1)}(xy^2+y^2z)\,r\,d\lambda_3 = \in
t_{B(0,1)}(xy^2+y^2z)\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}\,d\lambda_3(x,y,z).$$

**Conclusion.** The left-hand side equals exactly the right-hand side. The equal
ity holds. $\blacksquare$
