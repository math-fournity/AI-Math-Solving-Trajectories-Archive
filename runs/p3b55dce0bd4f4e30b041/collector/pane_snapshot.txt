# Solution

## Setup

A "Standardized Pavilion" is a convex polygon inscribed in a unit circle (radius
 1). Each side is a chord. If a side subtends central angle $\theta_i$, its leng
th is $s_i = 2\sin(\theta_i/2)$, so $s_i^2 = 4\sin^2(\theta_i/2)$.

The constraint requires $s_i^2 \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ for every side. Since $0 < s_i
\leq 2$, we have $s_i^2 \in \{1,2,3,4\}$, giving central angles:

| $s_i^2$ | $\theta_i$ | Label |
|---------|------------|-------|
| 1 | $\pi/3$ | A |
| 2 | $\pi/2$ | B |
| 3 | $2\pi/3$ | C |
| 4 | $\pi$ | D |

## Counting Solutions

Let $a,b,c,d$ = counts of sides of type A,B,C,D. The angles must sum to $2\pi$:

$$\frac{a\pi}{3} + \frac{b\pi}{2} + \frac{2c\pi}{3} + d\pi = 2\pi \implies 2a +
3b + 4c + 6d = 12$$

with $a+b+c+d \geq 3$ (polygon) and $d \leq 1$ (at most one diameter side, since
 two would force $n=2$).

**Case $d=0$:** $2a+3b+4c=12$

| $c$ | $2a+3b$ | Solutions $(a,b,c)$ |
|-----|---------|---------------------|
| 0 | 12 | $(6,0,0),\ (3,2,0),\ (0,4,0)$ |
| 1 | 8 | $(4,0,1),\ (1,2,1)$ |
| 2 | 4 | $(2,0,2)$ |
| 3 | 0 | $(0,0,3)$ |

**Case $d=1$:** $2a+3b+4c=6$, $a+b+c\geq 2$

| $c$ | $2a+3b$ | Solutions $(a,b,c,d)$ |
|-----|---------|----------------------|
| 0 | 6 | $(3,0,0,1),\ (0,2,0,1)$ |
| 1 | 2 | $(1,0,1,1)$ |

**10 solution types total.**

## Counting Non-Congruent Polygons per Type

Two inscribed polygons in the same circle are congruent iff their angle sequence
s match up to cyclic permutation and reversal (dihedral symmetry $D_n$). So we c
ount distinct necklaces for each multiset.

1. **(6,0,0,0)** — AAAAAA: all identical → **1**
2. **(3,2,0,0)** — AAABB (pentagon): B's adjacent or separated → **2**
3. **(0,4,0,0)** — BBBB: all identical → **1**
4. **(4,0,1,0)** — AAAAC: single C, all equivalent → **1**
5. **(1,2,1,0)** — ABBC (quadrilateral): fixing A, C at pos 1 or 3 give one clas
s; C at pos 2 gives another → **2**
6. **(2,0,2,0)** — AACC: A's adjacent (AACC) or opposite (ACAC) → **2**
7. **(0,0,3,0)** — CCC: all identical → **1**
8. **(3,0,0,1)** — AAAD: single D, all equivalent → **1**
9. **(0,2,0,1)** — BBD: single D, two B's identical → **1**
10. **(1,0,1,1)** — ACD: triangle, all distinct, $D_3$-transitive → **1**

## Verification of Convexity

Every polygon with vertices in cyclic order on a circle is convex. All central a
ngles are in $\{\pi/3, \pi/2, 2\pi/3, \pi\}$ (each $\leq \pi$) and sum to $2\pi$
, so all 10 types yield valid convex polygons. The $d=1$ cases have a diameter s
ide with remaining vertices on one semicircle — still convex.

## Final Count

$$1+2+1+1+2+2+1+1+1+1 = \boxed{13}$$
