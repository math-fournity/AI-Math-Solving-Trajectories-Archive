**Claim.** The limit equals $\displaystyle 2\pi i\,\frac{B_{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}$, wh
ere $B_n$ denotes the $n$-th Bernoulli number (with $B_1=-\tfrac{1}{2}$). In par
ticular, for every $k\ge 1$ this is $0$, and for $k=0$ it is $-\pi i$.

---

### Setup

Write the integrand as

$$f(z)=\frac{z}{\exp(z)-1}\cdot\frac{1}{z^{2k+2}}=\frac{1}{z^{2k+1}(\exp z-1)}.$
$

The singularities of $f$ in $\mathbb C$ are:

- **$z=0$:** a pole. Since $\exp z-1=z+\frac{z^2}{2}+\cdots=z\bigl(1+\frac z2+\c
dots\bigr)$, we have $f(z)=\frac{1}{z^{2k+2}}\cdot\frac{1}{1+z/2+\cdots}$, a pol
e of order $2k+2$.
- **$z=2\pi i m,\; m\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$:** simple poles, since $\exp z-1
$ has simple zeros there and $z^{2k+1}\neq 0$.

The circle $C_N$ has radius $R_N=2\pi\bigl(N+\tfrac12\bigr)$, chosen to avoid ev
ery pole: the poles on the imaginary axis sit at $|z|=2\pi|m|$, and $R_N$ lies s
trictly between $2\pi N$ and $2\pi(N+1)$.

---

### Residue at $z=0$

Use the classical generating function (with $B_1=-\tfrac12$):

$$\frac{z}{\exp z-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{B_n}{n!}\,z^n,\qquad |z|<2\pi.$$

Then

$$f(z)=\frac{1}{z^{2k+2}}\cdot\frac{z}{\exp z-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{B_n}{n
!}\,z^{\,n-2k-2}.$$

The residue at $0$ is the coefficient of $z^{-1}$, i.e. the term with $n-2k-2=-1
$, so $n=2k+1$:

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z)=\frac{B_{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}.}$$

---

### Residues at $z=2\pi i m$, $m\neq 0$

At $z_0=2\pi i m$ the pole is simple, so

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_0} f(z)=\frac{1}{z_0^{2k+1}}\cdot\lim_{z\to z_0}\frac{
z-z_0}{\exp z-1}=\frac{1}{z_0^{2k+1}}\cdot\frac{1}{\exp(z_0)}=\frac{1}{(2\pi i\,
m)^{2k+1}},$$

since $\exp(2\pi i m)=1$.

---

### Pairwise cancellation

For each $m\ge 1$, the poles at $+2\pi i m$ and $-2\pi i m$ both lie inside $C_N
$ (for $N\ge m$). Their residues sum to

$$\frac{1}{(2\pi i\,m)^{2k+1}}+\frac{1}{(2\pi i(-m))^{2k+1}}=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{
2k+1}\,m^{2k+1}}\bigl(1+(-1)^{2k+1}\bigr)=0,$$

because $2k+1$ is **odd**, so $(-1)^{2k+1}=-1$.

---

### Applying the residue theorem

For every $N\ge 0$, the poles enclosed by $C_N$ are $z=0$ together with $z=2\pi
i m$ for $1\le |m|\le N$. By the pairwise cancellation above,

$$\oint_{C_N} f(z)\,dz=2\pi i\left(\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}f(z)+\sum_{m=1}^{N}\b
igl[\operatorname{Res}_{z=2\pi i m}f+\operatorname{Res}_{z=-2\pi i m}f\bigr]\rig
ht)=2\pi i\cdot\frac{B_{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}.$$

**The integral is independent of $N$**, so the limit is immediate:

$$\lim_{N\to\infty}\oint_{C_N}\frac{z}{\exp(z)-1}\,\frac{dz}{z^{2k+2}}=2\pi i\,\
frac{B_{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}.$$

---

### Evaluation for $k\ge 1$ and $k=0$

- **$k\ge 1$:** $2k+1\ge 3$ is odd and $\ge 3$, so $B_{2k+1}=0$ (all odd-indexed
 Bernoulli numbers vanish beyond $B_1$). The limit is $\boxed{0}$.

- **$k=0$** (if $0\in\mathbb N$): $B_1=-\tfrac12$, giving $2\pi i\cdot\bigl(-\tf
rac12\bigr)=\boxed{-\pi i}$.
