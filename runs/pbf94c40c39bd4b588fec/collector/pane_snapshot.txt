## Proof

We have 9 unique keys: {a, b, c, p, q, r, x, y, z}. A valid sequence is a permut
ation of all 9 keys in which y appears somewhere between x and z (i.e., either x
 precedes y precedes z, or z precedes y precedes x).

**Total permutations.** There are 9 distinct keys, so the total number of possib
le sequences is

$$9! = 362880.$$

**Symmetry argument.** In any fixed permutation, consider only the relative orde
r of the three keys x, y, z (ignoring the other six keys a, b, c, p, q, r). Ther
e are $3! = 6$ equally likely relative orders of these three keys:

1. x, y, z  ✓ (y between x and z)
2. x, z, y  ✗
3. y, x, z  ✗
4. y, z, x  ✗
5. z, x, y  ✗
6. z, y, x  ✓ (y between z and x)

Exactly 2 of the 6 relative orders place y between x and z.

**Why the orders are equally likely.** For any permutation of all 9 keys, the si
x "other" keys (a, b, c, p, q, r) can be placed in the remaining 6 positions in
$6!$ ways regardless of the relative order of x, y, z. Thus each of the 6 relati
ve orders of {x, y, z} is paired with exactly the same number of completions. Th
e six relative orders are therefore equally frequent.

**Counting valid sequences.** The fraction of permutations satisfying the constr
aint is $\frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}$. Hence the number of valid sequences is

$$\frac{1}{3} \times 9! = \frac{362880}{3} = 120960.$$

**Verification via direct counting.** Choose 3 positions (out of 9) for x, y, z:
 $\binom{9}{3} = 84$ ways. Place y between x and z: 2 valid orders (x-y-z or z-y
-x). Place the remaining 6 keys in the remaining 6 positions: $6! = 720$ ways. T
otal:

$$84 \times 2 \times 720 = 84 \times 1440 = 120960.$$

Both methods agree.

$$\boxed{120960}$$
