Looking at this problem, I need to find the number of unordered pairs $(p(x), q(
x))$ of degree-4 polynomials with positive integer coefficients, $p(1)+q(1)=26$,
 such that $W(x)=(p(x)q(x))^7$ has exactly one odd coefficient.

## Setting Up the Mod 2 Analysis

The parity of coefficients of $W(x)$ depends only on $p(x)$ and $q(x)$ modulo 2.
 Let $\bar{p}(x) = p(x) \bmod 2$ and $\bar{q}(x) = q(x) \bmod 2$ in $\mathbb{F}_
2[x]$. Then:

$$\bar{W}(x) = (\bar{p}(x)\cdot \bar{q}(x))^7$$

$W(x)$ has exactly one odd coefficient iff $\bar{W}(x)$ is a monomial $x^k$.

**Key claim:** $(\bar{p}\cdot\bar{q})^7$ is a monomial iff both $\bar{p}$ and $\
bar{q}$ are monomials.

*Proof:* In $\mathbb{F}_2[x]$ (a UFD), if $f^7 = x^k$, then $f$ must be $x^{k/7}
$ (since $x$ is the only irreducible factor of $x^k$, and $f^7$ having only fact
or $x$ forces $f$ to be a power of $x$). So $\bar{p}\cdot\bar{q}$ is a monomial,
 which in a domain means both $\bar{p}$ and $\bar{q}$ are monomials. $\square$

**Conclusion:** Both $p$ and $q$ must have **exactly one odd coefficient** each
(the rest even).

## Counting Polynomials with Exactly One Odd Coefficient

Let $p(x) = a_4x^4 + a_3x^3 + a_2x^2 + a_1x + a_0$ with all $a_i \geq 1$, exactl
y one $a_j$ odd.

- If $a_j$ is the odd coefficient: $a_j = 2v_j + 1$ with $v_j \geq 0$
- For $i \neq j$: $a_i = 2v_i$ with $v_i \geq 1$

Setting $w_j = v_j \geq 0$ and $w_i = v_i - 1 \geq 0$ for $i \neq j$:

$$\sum a_i = 2\sum v_i + 1 = S \implies \sum w_i = \frac{S-9}{2}$$

The minimum sum is $1 + 4\times 2 = 9$ (one odd coeff $=1$, four even coeff $=2$
), so $S \geq 9$.

For each choice of $j \in \{0,1,2,3,4\}$, the number of solutions is $\binom{(S-
9)/2 + 4}{4}$ (stars and bars with 5 non-negative parts). With 5 choices for $j$
:

$$T(S) = 5\cdot\binom{(S-9)/2 + 4}{4}$$

## Determining Valid Values of $S = p(1)$

Since $p(1) + q(1) = 26$ and both must be odd (one odd coefficient makes the sum
 odd) and $\geq 9$:

$$S \in \{9, 11, 13, 15, 17\}, \quad q(1) = 26 - S \in \{17, 15, 13, 11, 9\}$$

## Computing $T(S)$

| $S$ | $(S-9)/2$ | $\binom{(S-9)/2+4}{4}$ | $T(S)$ |
|-----|-----------|------------------------|---------|
| 9   | 0         | $\binom{4}{4}=1$       | 5       |
| 11  | 1         | $\binom{5}{4}=5$       | 25      |
| 13  | 2         | $\binom{6}{4}=15$      | 75      |
| 15  | 3         | $\binom{7}{4}=35$      | 175     |
| 17  | 4         | $\binom{8}{4}=70$      | 350     |

## Counting Unordered Pairs

**Case 1: $p(1) \neq q(1)$** (i.e., $S \in \{9, 11\}$, with $q(1) = 26-S \in \{1
7, 15\}$)

Since $p(1) \neq q(1)$ implies $p \neq q$, each unordered pair is counted once:

- $S=9, q(1)=17$: $T(9)\cdot T(17) = 5 \times 350 = 1750$
- $S=11, q(1)=15$: $T(11)\cdot T(15) = 25 \times 175 = 4375$

**Case 2: $p(1) = q(1) = 13$**

Both $p$ and $q$ are drawn from the same pool of $T(13) = 75$ polynomials. The n
umber of unordered pairs (allowing $p = q$) is:

$$\binom{75+1}{2} = \frac{75 \times 76}{2} = 2850$$

## Final Count

$$1750 + 4375 + 2850 = \boxed{8975}$$
