## Why the counterexample works

The key observation is that the conditions only constrain the *sign* of $g'$ to
match the sign of $f$, not their magnitudes. By choosing $f(x) = x^2$ — which is
 nonnegative everywhere and vanishes only at the origin — we force $g'$ to be no
nnegative everywhere and zero only at the origin. The function $g(x) = x^3/3 + 1

1)$, and returns to the value $1$ only at $x = 0$ where $f'(0) = 0$ holds automa

no mechanism in the hypotheses that forces $g$ to oscillate back to $1$ at addit
