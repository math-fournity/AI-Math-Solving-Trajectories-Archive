## Proof

**Step 1: Integral representation.** We use the identity
$$\frac{e^x - e^y}{x - y} = \int_0^1 e^{tx + (1-t)y}\, dt,$$
which holds for all $x, y$ (at $x = y$ both sides equal $e^x$ by continuity). Si
nce $e^{tx+(1-t)y} \geq 0$ is continuous on the compact set $[0,k]^2 \times [0,1
]$, Fubini's theorem applies:

$$I(k) := \iint_{[0,k]^2} \frac{e^x - e^y}{x - y}\, dx\, dy = \int_0^1 \left(\in
t_0^k e^{tx}\, dx\right)\!\left(\int_0^k e^{(1-t)y}\, dy\right) dt = \int_0^1 \f
rac{(e^{tk} - 1)(e^{(1-t)k} - 1)}{t(1-t)}\, dt.$$

**Step 2: Normalize by $e^k$.** Since $(e^{tk}-1)(e^{(1-t)k}-1)/e^k = (1 - e^{-t
k})(1 - e^{-(1-t)k})$:

$$e^{-k}\, I(k) = \int_0^1 \frac{(1 - e^{-tk})(1 - e^{-(1-t)k})}{t(1-t)}\, dt.$$

**Step 3: Lower bound diverging to $+\infty$.** For $t \in [1/k,\; 1 - 1/k]$, we
 have $tk \geq 1$ and $(1-t)k \geq 1$, so
$$1 - e^{-tk} \geq 1 - e^{-1}, \qquad 1 - e^{-(1-t)k} \geq 1 - e^{-1}.$$

Therefore
$$e^{-k}\, I(k) \;\geq\; (1 - e^{-1})^2 \int_{1/k}^{1-1/k} \frac{dt}{t(1-t)}.$$

Using the partial fraction $\frac{1}{t(1-t)} = \frac{1}{t} + \frac{1}{1-t}$:

$$\int_{1/k}^{1-1/k} \frac{dt}{t(1-t)} = \int_{1/k}^{1-1/k}\frac{dt}{t} + \int_{
1/k}^{1-1/k}\frac{dt}{1-t} = \ln(k-1) + \ln(k-1) = 2\ln(k-1).$$

Hence
$$e^{-k}\, I(k) \;\geq\; 2(1 - e^{-1})^2 \ln(k-1) \;\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\; +
\infty.$$

**Step 4: Upper bound (to confirm the rate, optional).** Using $\frac{1}{t(1-t)}
 = \frac{1}{t} + \frac{1}{1-t}$ and the symmetry $t \leftrightarrow 1-t$:

$$e^{-k}\, I(k) = 2\int_0^1 \frac{(1 - e^{-tk})(1 - e^{-(1-t)k})}{t}\, dt = 2\in
t_0^1 \frac{1 - e^{-tk}}{t}\, dt \;-\; 2\int_0^1 \frac{e^{-(1-t)k} - e^{-k}}{t}\
, dt.$$

The first integral, via $s = tk$, becomes $\int_0^k \frac{1-e^{-s}}{s}\, ds = \l
n k + \gamma + o(1)$ (a classical identity, where $\gamma$ is the Euler–Maschero
ni constant). The second integral is $o(1)$ (substituting $v=(1-t)k$ shows it is
 $O(1/k)$). Thus

$$e^{-k}\, I(k) = 2\ln k + 2\gamma + o(1),$$

confirming divergence to $+\infty$.

**Conclusion.** Since $e^{-k}\, I(k) \geq 2(1-e^{-1})^2 \ln(k-1) \to +\infty$, t
he limit diverges:

$$\boxed{\lim_{k \to \infty} e^{-k} \iint_{[0,k]^2} \frac{e^x - e^y}{x - y}\, dx
\, dy = +\infty.}$$
