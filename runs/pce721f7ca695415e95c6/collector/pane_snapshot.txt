# Solution

We solve the linear system $\dot{\mathbf{u}} = A\mathbf{u}$ with
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -7 & 0 \\ 5 & 10 & 4 \\ 0 & 5 & 2 \end{pmatrix}, \qqua
d \mathbf{u} = (x, y, z)^T.$$

## Eigenvalues

$$\det(A - \lambda I) = \begin{vmatrix} 2-\lambda & -7 & 0 \\ 5 & 10-\lambda & 4
 \\ 0 & 5 & 2-\lambda \end{vmatrix}.$$

Expanding along the first row:

$$\det = (2-\lambda)\bigl[(10-\lambda)(2-\lambda) - 20\bigr] + 7\bigl[5(2-\lambd
a)\bigr].$$

Since $(10-\lambda)(2-\lambda) - 20 = \lambda^2 - 12\lambda$,

$$\det = (2-\lambda)(\lambda^2 - 12\lambda) + 35(2-\lambda) = (2-\lambda)(\lambd
a^2 - 12\lambda + 35).$$

Factoring: $\lambda^2 - 12\lambda + 35 = (\lambda-5)(\lambda-7)$, so

$$\det(A - \lambda I) = (2-\lambda)(\lambda-5)(\lambda-7).$$

The eigenvalues are $\lambda_1 = 2,\; \lambda_2 = 5,\; \lambda_3 = 7$.

## Eigenvectors

**$\lambda_1 = 2$:** $(A - 2I)\mathbf{v} = 0$ gives
$$\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -7 & 0 \\ 5 & 8 & 4 \\ 0 & 5 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\mathbf{v} =
 0 \implies y = 0,\; 5x + 4z = 0 \implies x = -\tfrac{4}{5}z.$$
Taking $z = 5$: $\mathbf{v}_1 = (-4,\, 0,\, 5)^T$.

**$\lambda_2 = 5$:** $(A - 5I)\mathbf{v} = 0$ gives
$$-3x - 7y = 0,\quad 5y - 3z = 0 \implies x = -\tfrac{7}{3}y,\; z = \tfrac{5}{3}
y.$$
Taking $y = 3$: $\mathbf{v}_2 = (-7,\, 3,\, 5)^T$.

**$\lambda_3 = 7$:** $(A - 7I)\mathbf{v} = 0$ gives
$$-5x - 7y = 0,\quad 5y - 5z = 0 \implies x = -\tfrac{7}{5}y,\; z = y.$$
Taking $y = 5$: $\mathbf{v}_3 = (-7,\, 5,\, 5)^T$.

## Verification

- $A\mathbf{v}_1 = (-8,\, 0,\, 10)^T = 2\,\mathbf{v}_1$. ✓
- $A\mathbf{v}_2 = (-35,\, 15,\, 25)^T = 5\,\mathbf{v}_2$. ✓
- $A\mathbf{v}_3 = (-49,\, 35,\, 35)^T = 7\,\mathbf{v}_3$. ✓

## General Solution

Since the three eigenvalues are distinct, the eigenvectors are linearly independ
ent and the general solution is:

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned} x(t) &= -4\,c_1\,e^{2t} - 7\,c_2\,e^{5t} - 7\,c_3\,e^{7
t}, \\ y(t) &= \phantom{-4\,c_1\,e^{2t} -\,} 3\,c_2\,e^{5t} + 5\,c_3\,e^{7t}, \\
 z(t) &= \phantom{-}5\,c_1\,e^{2t} + 5\,c_2\,e^{5t} + 5\,c_3\,e^{7t}, \end{align
ed}}$$

where $c_1, c_2, c_3$ are arbitrary constants determined by initial conditions.
