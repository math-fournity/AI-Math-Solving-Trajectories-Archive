## Solution

The "Harmony Index" of a block $n$ is the count of integers in $\{1, 2, \ldots,
n\}$ coprime to $n$ — this is exactly **Euler's totient function** $\varphi(n)$.
 A block is Perfectly Resonant precisely when $\varphi(n) \mid n$.

### Characterizing all $n$ with $\varphi(n) \mid n$

Write $n = 2^k \cdot m$ where $m$ is odd, $k \ge 0$. Using multiplicativity,

$$\varphi(n) = \varphi(2^k)\cdot \varphi(m) = 2^{k-1}\cdot \varphi(m) \quad (k\g
e 1), \qquad \varphi(2^k)=2^{k-1}.$$

**Case 1: $n = 2^k$ ($k \ge 1$).** Then $\varphi(n) = 2^{k-1}$ and $n/\varphi(n)
 = 2$. So **every power of 2 works.**

**Case 2: $n = 2^k \cdot 3^j$ with $k\ge 1, j\ge 1$.** Then
$$\varphi(n) = 2^{k-1}\cdot \varphi(3^j) = 2^{k-1}\cdot 2\cdot 3^{j-1} = 2^k\cdo
t 3^{j-1},$$
so $n/\varphi(n) = 3$. **All such $n$ work.**

**Case 3: $n$ has a prime factor $p \ge 5$.** Write $n = 2^k\cdot 3^j \cdot p^a
\cdot r$ where $r$ collects any further primes $\ge 5$ and $a\ge 1$. Then
$$\frac{n}{\varphi(n)} = \frac{n}{n\prod_{q\mid n}(1-1/q)} = \prod_{q\mid n}\fra
c{q}{q-1}.$$
For this product to be an integer, every factor $q/(q-1)$ must be "absorbed". Th
e factor for $q=2$ is $2$, for $q=3$ is $3/2$. The factor for $p\ge 5$ is $p/(p-
1)$ in lowest terms (since $\gcd(p,p-1)=1$), with denominator $p-1 \ge 4$. The o
nly available denominators to cancel come from factors $2$ and $3/2$, i.e. only
primes $2$ and $3$ are available, contributing at most a factor of $3$ in the de
nominator. Since $p-1 \ge 4$ and $\gcd(p-1, p)=1$, the denominator $p-1$ cannot
be fully cancelled by $\{2,3\}$ unless $p-1 \mid 6$, i.e. $p-1\in\{1,2,3,6\}$, g
iving $p\in\{2,3,4,7\}$. Only $p=7$ is a prime $\ge 5$ here, with $p-1=6=2\cdot
3$.

For $p=7$: we need **both** $2$ and $3$ as factors of $n$ to cancel the $6$ in t
he denominator. So $n = 2^k\cdot 3^j\cdot 7^a\cdot r$ with $k\ge 1, j\ge 1, a\ge
 1$. But then $r$ must be $1$ (no further primes, else their $q-1$ denominators
reappear uncancelled). Check: $n/\varphi(n) = 2\cdot(3/2)\cdot(7/6) = 7/2$. This
 is **not** an integer! The factor $2$ from $q=2$ cancels the $2$ in $6=2\cdot3$
, and the $3$ from $q=3$ (numerator) cancels the $3$ in $6$, leaving $7/2$... wa
it let me recompute carefully.

$$\frac{n}{\varphi(n)} = \frac{2}{1}\cdot\frac{3}{2}\cdot\frac{7}{6} = \frac{2\c
dot 3\cdot 7}{1\cdot 2\cdot 6} = \frac{42}{12} = \frac{7}{2}.$$

Not an integer. So $p=7$ **fails** as well. Hence **no $n$ with a prime factor $
\ge 5$ works.**

**Case 4: $n = 3^j$ ($j\ge 1$, odd, no factor 2).** $\varphi(3^j)/3^j = 2/3$, so
 $n/\varphi(n) = 3/2$, not an integer. **Fails.**

### Conclusion of characterization

The complete set of $n$ with $\varphi(n)\mid n$ is
$$n = 2^k\cdot 3^j,\quad k\ge 1,\; j\ge 0.$$
