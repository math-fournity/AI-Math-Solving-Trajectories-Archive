## Solution

The three-qubit Hilbert space is $\mathcal{H}_1 \otimes \mathcal{H}_2 \otimes \m
athcal{H}_3$, where $\mathcal{H}_1, \mathcal{H}_2$ are the spaces of the first t
wo qubits (on which $U$ acts) and $\mathcal{H}_3$ is the space of the third qubi
t (left untouched). Grouping the first two qubits together, the space is $(\math
cal{H}_1 \otimes \mathcal{H}_2) \otimes \mathcal{H}_3$.

A unitary $U$ acting on qubits 1 and 2 while qubit 3 is left unchanged is, by de
finition of tensor-product operators, the operator
$$M = U \otimes I_2,$$
where $I_2$ is the $2\times 2$ identity on the third qubit, and $\otimes$ denote
s the Kronecker product.

### Explicit form

Order the 8 basis states as
$$|000\rangle, |001\rangle, |010\rangle, |011\rangle, |100\rangle, |101\rangle,
|110\rangle, |111\rangle,$$
i.e. as $|n,k\rangle$ with $n \in \{0,1,2,3\}$ indexing the first-two-qubit subs
pace (in the order $|00\rangle,|01\rangle,|10\rangle,|11\rangle$) and $k \in \{0
,1\}$ indexing the third qubit. The Kronecker product $U \otimes I_2$ replaces e
ach entry $u_{nm}$ of $U$ by the $2\times 2$ block $u_{nm} I_2$. Hence the entry
 of $M$ at row $(n,k)$ and column $(m,\ell)$ is
$$M_{(n,k),(m,\ell)} = u_{nm}\,\delta_{k\ell}.$$

Writing $M$ out as an $8\times 8$ matrix (rows/columns ordered $(0,0),(0,1),(1,0
),(1,1),(2,0),(2,1),(3,0),(3,1)$):

$$M = \begin{pmatrix}
u_{00} & 0 & u_{01} & 0 & u_{02} & 0 & u_{03} & 0 \\
0 & u_{00} & 0 & u_{01} & 0 & u_{02} & 0 & u_{03} \\
u_{10} & 0 & u_{11} & 0 & u_{12} & 0 & u_{13} & 0 \\
0 & u_{10} & 0 & u_{11} & 0 & u_{12} & 0 & u_{13} \\
u_{20} & 0 & u_{21} & 0 & u_{22} & 0 & u_{23} & 0 \\
0 & u_{20} & 0 & u_{21} & 0 & u_{22} & 0 & u_{23} \\
u_{30} & 0 & u_{31} & 0 & u_{32} & 0 & u_{33} & 0 \\
0 & u_{30} & 0 & u_{31} & 0 & u_{32} & 0 & u_{33}
\end{pmatrix}.$$

### Verification against the given state

The coefficient vector of $\phi$ in this ordering is
$$(c_{000},c_{001},c_{010},c_{011},c_{100},c_{101},c_{110},c_{111}) = (a_0 b_{00
},\, a_0 b_{01},\, a_0 b_{10},\, a_0 b_{11},\, a_1 b_{00},\, a_1 b_{01},\, a_1 b
_{10},\, a_1 b_{11}).$$

Equivalently $c_{n,k} = a_i\, b_{jk}$ with $n = 2i+j$. Acting with $M$ gives
$$c'_{n,k} = \sum_{m=0}^{3} u_{nm}\, c_{m,k} = \sum_{m=0}^{3} u_{nm}\, a_{i_m}\,
 b_{j_m,k},$$
which is exactly the result of applying $U$ to the first-two-qubit amplitudes wh
ile keeping the third-qubit index $k$ fixed — precisely the intended action. The
 third qubit's entanglement structure (encoded in $b_{jk}$) is preserved because
 $I_2$ acts trivially on $k$.

### Unitarity

Since $U$ is unitary ($U^\dagger U = I_4$) and $I_2$ is unitary,
$$(U\otimes I_2)^\dagger(U\otimes I_2) = (U^\dagger U)\otimes(I_2^\dagger I_2) =
 I_4\otimes I_2 = I_8,$$
so $M$ is an $8\times 8$ unitary, as required.

### Conclusion

The overall $8\times 8$ matrix representing the unitary $U$ applied to the first
 two qubits (with the third qubit untouched) is the Kronecker product
$$\boxed{M = U \otimes I_2}$$
with entries $M_{(n,k),(m,\ell)} = u_{nm}\,\delta_{k\ell}$, as displayed explici
tly above.
