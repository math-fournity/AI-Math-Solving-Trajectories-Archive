## Proof

Let $g(x,y) = x^p y^q - x^q y^p = x^q y^q\bigl(x^{p-q}-y^{p-q}\bigr)$. For gener
al real exponents $p,q$ the expression $(f(x))^q$ is well-defined only when $f(x
)\ge 0$, so we maximize over continuous $f:[0,1]\to[0,\infty)$.

**Pointwise optimization.** For each fixed $x\in(0,1]$, consider $g$ as a functi
on of $y\ge 0$. We have $g(x,0)=0$, and as $y\to+\infty$ the term $-x^q y^p$ dom
inates (since $p>q$), so $g(x,y)\to-\infty$. The derivative is
$$
\frac{\partial g}{\partial y}=q\,x^p y^{q-1}-p\,x^q y^{p-1}.
$$
Setting it to zero (for $y>0$) gives
$$
q\,x^{p-q}=p\,y^{p-q}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y^{p-q}=\frac{q}{p}\,x^{p-q},
$$
hence the unique critical point
$$
y = c\,x,\qquad c:=\Bigl(\frac{q}{p}\Bigr)^{\!1/(p-q)}.
$$
Since $0<q/p<1$ and $p-q>0$, we have $0<c<1$. There is exactly one critical poin
t in $(0,\infty)$; combined with $g(x,0)=0$ and $g\to-\infty$, this critical poi
nt is the **global maximum** of $g(x,\cdot)$ on $[0,\infty)$.

**Value at the optimizer.** With $y=cx$ and $c^{p-q}=q/p$:
$$
g(x,cx)=x^p(cx)^q-x^q(cx)^p=(c^q-c^p)\,x^{p+q}.
$$
Moreover
$$
c^q-c^p = c^q\bigl(1-c^{p-q}\bigr)=\Bigl(\frac{q}{p}\Bigr)^{\!q/(p-q)}\!\Bigl(1-
\frac{q}{p}\Bigr)=\Bigl(\frac{q}{p}\Bigr)^{\!q/(p-q)}\frac{p-q}{p}>0.
$$

**Global bound.** Since for every $x\in[0,1]$ and every $y\ge 0$,
$$
g(x,y)\le g(x,cx),
$$
integrating yields
$$
\int_0^1 g(x,f(x))\,dx \le \int_0^1 g(x,cx)\,dx = (c^q-c^p)\int_0^1 x^{p+q}\,dx
= (c^q-c^p)\,\frac{1}{p+q+1}.
$$
Equality holds iff $f(x)=cx$ for (Lebesgue-)a.e. $x\in[0,1]$. Since $f$ is conti
nuous, this is equivalent to $f(x)=cx$ for **all** $x\in[0,1]$.

**Maximum value.**
$$
\boxed{\displaystyle \max_f \int_0^1\!\bigl(x^p(f(x))^q-x^q(f(x))^p\bigr)\,dx \;
=\; \frac{p-q}{p\,(p+q+1)}\Bigl(\frac{q}{p}\Bigr)^{\!\frac{q}{\,p-q\,}}}
$$
achieved uniquely by
$$
\boxed{\displaystyle f(x)=\Bigl(\frac{q}{p}\Bigr)^{\!\frac{1}{p-q}}\,x,\qquad x\
in[0,1].}
$$
