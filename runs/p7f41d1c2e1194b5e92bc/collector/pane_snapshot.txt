## Analysis

The sum is $\sum_{\substack{n \leq [x] \\ n \text{ square-free}}} e^{-\sqrt{\fra
c{\log x}{r}}}$, where the summand depends only on $x$ and $r$, not on $n$.

**Key observation:** Since $e^{-\sqrt{\frac{\log x}{r}}}$ is constant with respe
ct to $n$, the sum factors as:

$$\sum_{\substack{n \leq [x] \\ n \text{ sq-free}}} e^{-\sqrt{\frac{\log x}{r}}}
 = Q([x]) \cdot e^{-\sqrt{\frac{\log x}{r}}}$$

where $Q(x) = \#\{n \leq x : n \text{ is square-free}\}$.

## Proof

**Step 1: Count of square-free integers.** It is a classical result that

$$Q(x) = \frac{6}{\pi^2}x + O(\sqrt{x}),$$

so for $x \geq 2$, $Q(x) \asymp x$.

**Step 2: Simplify the sum.** Since the summand is independent of $n$:

$$S(x) := \sum_{\substack{n \leq [x] \\ n \text{ sq-free}}} e^{-\sqrt{\frac{\log
 x}{r}}} = Q([x]) \cdot e^{-\sqrt{\frac{\log x}{r}}} \asymp x \cdot e^{-\sqrt{\f
rac{\log x}{r}}}.$$

**Step 3: Rewrite the exponent.** Observe that

$$\sqrt{\frac{\log x}{r}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{r}} \cdot \sqrt{\log x}.$$

Therefore:

$$S(x) \asymp x \cdot e^{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\sqrt{\log x}} = \frac{x}{e^{\frac{1
}{\sqrt{r}}\sqrt{\log x}}}.$$

**Step 4: Identify the constant.** Set $c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}$. Since $r$ is a
fixed positive parameter, $c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{r}} > 0$. Then:

$$S(x) \asymp \frac{x}{e^{c\sqrt{\log x}}}.$$

In particular, $S(x) \ll \frac{x}{e^{c\sqrt{\log x}}}$ with $c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{
r}}$.

{\log x}}} \asymp e^{-(\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}} - c)\sqrt{\log x}} \to 0$ as $x \to \i

two sides are of the same order ($\asymp$), so $\ll$ holds. $\blacksquare$
