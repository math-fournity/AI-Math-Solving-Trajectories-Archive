## Proof

Since $A = A^*$ and $\langle Ax, x\rangle > 0$ for all $x \neq 0$, the matrix $A
$ is **positive definite Hermitian**. Hence $A$ is unitarily diagonalizable with
 strictly positive real eigenvalues. Let

$$A = U \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n) U^*,$$

where $U \in U(n)$ and $\lambda_k > 0$ for every $k$.

**Definition of $A^z$.** For $z \in \mathbb{C}$, since each $\lambda_k > 0$, we
may use the real logarithm $\log \lambda_k \in \mathbb{R}$ and define

$$\lambda_k^z := \exp(z \log \lambda_k),$$

which is a well-defined entire function of $z$. Then

$$A^z := U \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1^z, \dots, \lambda_n^z) U^*.$$

This agrees with the usual functional calculus definition and is independent of
the choice of diagonalizing unitary (since $A$ has a unique spectral resolution)
.

**Entry-wise formula.** Writing $U = (u_{ik})$, the $(i,j)$-entry of $A^z$ is

$$(A^z)_{i,j} = \sum_{k=1}^{n} u_{ik}\, \lambda_k^z\, \overline{u_{jk}} = \sum_{
k=1}^{n} u_{ik}\,\overline{u_{jk}}\, \exp(z \log \lambda_k).$$

**Analyticity.** Each summand $z \mapsto u_{ik}\overline{u_{jk}}\exp(z\log\lambd
a_k)$ is an entire function of $z$ (a constant times the exponential of a linear
 function of $z$). A finite sum of entire functions is entire. Therefore

$$z \mapsto (A^z)_{i,j}$$

is an entire function on $\mathbb{C}$, and in particular is analytic everywhere.

**Conclusion.** The map $\mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$, $z \mapsto (A^z)_{i,j}$, is
 analytic (indeed, entire).
