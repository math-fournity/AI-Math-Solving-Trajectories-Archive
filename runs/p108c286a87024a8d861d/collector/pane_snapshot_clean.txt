Looking at this problem, I need to find the game-theoretic value: Chip chooses a
 split to minimize Dale's guaranteed gain, while Dale chooses N to maximize what
 Chip must move.

## Solution

**Setup.** Chip splits 1001 nuts into three piles $(a, b, c)$ with $a+b+c=1001$,
 $a,b,c \ge 0$. Dale sees the split and picks $N \in \{1, \ldots, 1001\}$. Chip
then moves nuts from the original piles to a fourth pile until some pile equals
exactly $N$. Chip minimizes the total moved; Dale maximizes it.

**Chip's optimal response for given piles and $N$.** Chip can either:
- Move $N$ nuts to the fourth pile (making it equal $N$), costing $N$, or
- Move $s_i - N$ nuts from pile $i$ (making pile $i$ equal $N$), costing $s_i -
N$, valid when $s_i \ge N$.

Chip picks the cheapest option, so the cost is $\min\!\big(N,\; \min_{s_i \ge N}
(s_i - N)\big)$.

---

**Chip's strategy (upper bound: Dale gets at most 500).**

Chip splits as $(0, 0, 1001)$. For any $N$:
- The only nonempty original pile has 1001 nuts.
- Option 1: move $N$ nuts to the fourth pile → cost $N$.
- Option 2: move $1001 - N$ nuts from the big pile → cost $1001 - N$.
- Piles of size 0 cannot reach $N \ge 1$ (they only shrink).

Chip's cost $= \min(N,\; 1001 - N) \le \lfloor 1001/2 \rfloor = 500$.

So no matter what $N$ Dale picks, Dale gets at most **500**.

---

**Dale's strategy (lower bound: Dale gets at least 500).**

For any split $(a, b, c)$ with $a + b + c = 1001$, let $c = \max(a,b,c)$.

**Case 1: $c < 1001$.** Dale picks $N = 1001$. Every original pile has size $\le
 c < 1001$ and can only decrease, so no original pile can ever equal 1001. The o
nly way to create a pile of 1001 is to move all 1001 nuts to the fourth pile. Co
st $= 1001 \ge 500$.

**Case 2: $c = 1001$.** Then $a + b = 0$, so $a = b = 0$ (the split is $(0,0,100
1)$ up to ordering). Dale picks $N = 500$. Chip's cost $= \min(500, 1001 - 500)
= \min(500, 501) = 500$.

In every case, Dale gets at least **500**.

---

**Conclusion.** Chip can guarantee Dale receives at most 500 (via the split $(0,
0,1001)$), and Dale can guarantee receiving at least 500 against any split. Ther
efore the maximal number of nuts Dale can get for sure is:

$$\boxed{500}$$
