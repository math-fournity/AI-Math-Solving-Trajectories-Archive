## Key Lemma (Parity Locking)

**Lemma.** In the take-from-ends game on a row of $n$ numbers:

- **If $n$ is even**, the first player can force receiving **all** entries of on
e chosen parity, and the second player can force the first player to receive **o
nly** entries of the parity of her first move. Hence the game value is exactly $
V=\max(\text{odd\_sum},\text{even\_sum})$.

- **If $n$ is odd**, the second player can force the first player to receive **a
ll** odd-position entries (and the second player gets all even-position entries)
. Hence the first player's sum is exactly $\text{odd\_sum}$.

**Proof.**

*Even $n$.* The two ends are positions $1$ (odd) and $n$ (even), so the first pl
ayer's opening move fixes a parity. After that move, both remaining ends share t
he *opposite* parity (e.g. take pos $1$ → ends are $2$ and $n$, both even). The
second player takes one of them; now both exposed ends revert to the first playe
r's parity. This alternation is forced regardless of choices: whenever it is a p
layer's turn, both available ends have the same parity. Thus the first player is
 locked into the parity of her first move, and the second player into the other.
 The first player picks the larger-parity end first, guaranteeing $\max(\text{od
d\_sum},\text{even\_sum})$; the second player's locking strategy prevents exceed
ing it. So $V=\max(\text{odd\_sum},\text{even\_sum})$.

*Odd $n$.* Both ends (positions $1$ and $n$) are odd. The first player must take
 an odd-position entry. The second player then faces one odd and one even end, a
nd **takes the even end**. Now both ends are odd again (first player forced to t
ake odd). Repeating: after each of the second player's moves both ends are odd;
after each of the first player's moves one end is even, which the second player
takes. So the first player receives exactly all odd-position entries (sum $\text
{odd\_sum}$), the second player all even-position entries. $\square$
