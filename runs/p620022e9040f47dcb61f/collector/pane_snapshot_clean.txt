## Splitting Field of $f(x) = x^7 + 3$ over $\mathbb{Q}$

**The roots.** The equation $x^7 + 3 = 0$ gives $x^7 = -3$. Let $\alpha = \sqrt[
7]{-3}$ denote the real seventh root of $-3$, and let $\zeta_7 = e^{2\pi i/7}$ b
e a primitive seventh root of unity. The seven roots of $f$ are
$$\alpha,\ \alpha\zeta_7,\ \alpha\zeta_7^2,\ \ldots,\ \alpha\zeta_7^6.$$

**The splitting field.** Since the splitting field must contain $\alpha$ and the
 ratio of any two roots (which gives a power of $\zeta_7$), it must contain both
 $\alpha$ and $\zeta_7$. Conversely, $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha, \zeta_7)$ contains all
seven roots. Hence
$$E = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\, \zeta_7).$$

**Irreducibility of $x^7 + 3$.** We apply Eisenstein's criterion at the prime $p
 = 3$: the leading coefficient is $1$ (not divisible by $3$), every non-leading
coefficient is divisible by $3$ (the constant term is $3$, all other coefficient
s are $0$), and the constant term $3$ is not divisible by $9$. Therefore $x^7 +
3$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, giving
$$[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) : \mathbb{Q}] = 7.$$

**Degree of the cyclotomic part.** The minimal polynomial of $\zeta_7$ over $\ma
thbb{Q}$ is the seventh cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_7(x) = x^6 + x^5 + \cdots +
x + 1$, which has degree $\varphi(7) = 6$. Thus
$$[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_7) : \mathbb{Q}] = 6.$$

**Computing $[E:\mathbb{Q}]$.** We use the tower law in two ways:

- $[E:\mathbb{Q}] = [E:\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)]\cdot[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}]$,
 so $7 \mid [E:\mathbb{Q}]$.
- $[E:\mathbb{Q}] = [E:\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_7)]\cdot[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_7):\mathbb{Q}]
$, so $6 \mid [E:\mathbb{Q}]$.

Since $\gcd(7, 6) = 1$, the degree $[E:\mathbb{Q}]$ is divisible by $\operatorna
me{lcm}(7,6) = 42$.

On the other hand, $[E:\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)] \leq [\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_7):\mathbb{Q}]
 = 6$ (adjoining $\zeta_7$ to $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ contributes at most the degre
e of $\zeta_7$'s minimal polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}$), so
$$[E:\mathbb{Q}] \leq 7 \times 6 = 42.$$

Combining the lower bound $42 \mid [E:\mathbb{Q}]$ with the upper bound $[E:\mat
hbb{Q}] \leq 42$, we conclude
$$[E:\mathbb{Q}] = 42.$$

**Conclusion.** The splitting field of $x^7 + 3$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is
$$E = \mathbb{Q}\!\left(\sqrt[7]{-3},\, \zeta_7\right),$$
and its degree over $\mathbb{Q}$ is
$$\boxed{[E:\mathbb{Q}] = 42}.$$
