## Proof

**Claim.** For every $n > 2$, $\pi_1(SO(n)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.

We prove this in two stages: (A) compute the base case $\pi_1(SO(3))$, and (B) s
how $\pi_1(SO(n)) \cong \pi_1(SO(n-1))$ for all $n \ge 4$ by using the long exac
t sequence of the standard fibration, then induct.

### Stage A: $\pi_1(SO(3)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$

Consider $SU(2)$, the group of $2\times 2$ unitary matrices with determinant $1$
. Topologically $SU(2) \cong S^3$, hence $SU(2)$ is simply connected.

$SU(2)$ acts on its Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ (a $3$-dimensional real vecto
r space) by the adjoint action (conjugation): for $g \in SU(2)$, the map $X \map
sto gXg^{-1}$ is a real-linear automorphism of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$. The Killing f
orm $\langle X, Y\rangle = -\operatorname{tr}(XY)$ on $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ is a po
sitive-definite inner product, and the adjoint action preserves it (since $\oper
atorname{tr}(gXg^{-1}\cdot gYg^{-1}) = \operatorname{tr}(XY)$). Choosing an orth
onormal basis of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, this yields a homomorphism

$$\Phi: SU(2) \to SO(3).$$

**$\Phi$ is surjective.** Both $SU(2)$ and $SO(3)$ are connected compact Lie gro
ups of the same dimension $3$. The image $\Phi(SU(2))$ is a connected closed sub
group of $SO(3)$ of dimension $3$ (one checks the differential $d\Phi$ is inject
ive, hence an isomorphism on Lie algebras since both have dimension $3$). A conn
ected $3$-dimensional closed subgroup of the connected $3$-dimensional group $SO
(3)$ must be all of $SO(3)$.

**The kernel is $\{\pm I\}$.** If $g \in \ker\Phi$, then $gXg^{-1} = X$ for all
$X \in \mathfrak{su}(2)$, i.e., $g$ is in the center of $SU(2)$. The center of $
SU(2)$ is $\{I, -I\}$. Conversely $\pm I$ act trivially by conjugation. So $\ker
\Phi = \{I, -I\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.

Thus $\Phi: SU(2) \to SO(3)$ is a $2$-to-$1$ covering map. Since $SU(2) \cong S^
3$ is simply connected, $\Phi$ is the **universal cover**, and by covering space
 theory

$$\pi_1(SO(3)) \cong \ker\Phi = \{I, -I\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.$$

### Stage B: $\pi_1(SO(n)) \cong \pi_1(SO(n-1))$ for $n \ge 4$

There is a fiber bundle (the standard fibration of the orthogonal group)

$$SO(n-1) \hookrightarrow SO(n) \xrightarrow{\;p\;} S^{n-1},$$

where $p$ sends a matrix $A \in SO(n)$ to its last column $Ae_n \in S^{n-1}$. Th
e fiber over $e_n$ consists of matrices fixing $e_n$, which is exactly $SO(n-1)$
 acting on the orthogonal complement $\langle e_n\rangle^\perp \cong \mathbb{R}^
{n-1}$. The total space $SO(n)$ acts transitively on $S^{n-1}$ (any unit vector
can be sent to any other by a rotation), so $p$ is surjective, and this is indee
d a fiber bundle.

Applying the long exact sequence of homotopy groups to this fibration:

$$\cdots \to \pi_2(S^{n-1}) \to \pi_1(SO(n-1)) \to \pi_1(SO(n)) \to \pi_1(S^{n-1
}) \to \pi_0(SO(n-1)) \to \cdots$$

For $n \ge 4$, the sphere $S^{n-1}$ has dimension $n-1 \ge 3$. Spheres of dimens
ion $\ge 2$ are simply connected, and spheres of dimension $\ge 3$ satisfy $\pi_
2 = 0$. Hence

$$\pi_1(S^{n-1}) = 0 \quad\text{and}\quad \pi_2(S^{n-1}) = 0 \qquad (n \ge 4).$$

Substituting, the long exact sequence gives

$$0 \to \pi_1(SO(n-1)) \to \pi_1(SO(n)) \to 0,$$

so the middle map is an isomorphism:

$$\pi_1(SO(n)) \cong \pi_1(SO(n-1)), \qquad n \ge 4.$$

### Induction

$$\pi_1(SO(n)) \cong \pi_1(SO(n-1)) \cong \cdots \cong \pi_1(SO(3)) \cong \mathb

for every $n \ge 3$, i.e., for every $n > 2$.
