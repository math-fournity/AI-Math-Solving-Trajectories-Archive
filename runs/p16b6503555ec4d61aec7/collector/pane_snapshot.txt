**Notation.** Let $s(n)$ denote the sum of decimal digits of $n$, and let $f(k)$
 denote the smallest positive integer with digit sum $k$. For $1 \le k \le 9$, $
f(k) = k$.

**Necessary conditions.** Suppose $N$ works with digit sum $p$ (for the 2002 rep
resentation) and digit sum $q$ (for the 2003 representation), where $p, q \ge 1$
.

- **Lower bound:** Each summand is $\ge f(p)$ (resp. $f(q)$), so $N \ge 2002\,f(
p)$ and $N \ge 2003\,f(q)$.
- **Modular constraint:** Since $s(n) \equiv n \pmod{9}$, every summand with dig
it sum $p$ is $\equiv p \pmod{9}$. Thus $N \equiv 2002p \equiv 4p \pmod{9}$ and
$N \equiv 2003q \equiv 5q \pmod{9}$. Compatibility requires $4p \equiv 5q \pmod{
9}$, i.e., $p + q \equiv 0 \pmod{9}$.

Since $p, q \ge 1$ and $p + q \equiv 0 \pmod 9$, we have $p + q \ge 9$.

**Minimizing the lower bound.** For $p + q = 9$ (with $1 \le p \le 8$, $q = 9-p$
), since $f(k) = k$ for $k \le 9$:

$$\max(2002p,\; 2003(9-p)).$$

This is minimized near $2002p = 2003(9-p)$, i.e., $p \approx 4.5$:
- $p = 4$: $\max(8008, 10015) = 10015$
- $p = 5$: $\max(10010, 8012) = \mathbf{10010}$

For $p + q \ge 18$, the minimum occurs at $p = q = 9$: $\max(18018, 18027) = 180
27 > 10010$. All larger sums give even bigger bounds.

Therefore $N \ge 10010$ for all valid $(p, q)$.
