## Proof

**Claim.** The entire functions satisfying $|f(z)| \ge \dfrac{1}{|z|+1}$ for all
 $z\in\mathbb{C}$ are exactly the constant functions $f(z)\equiv c$ with $|c|\ge
 1$.

---

**Step 1. $f$ has no zeros.** Since $\frac{1}{|z|+1}>0$ for every $z$, we have $
|f(z)|>0$ everywhere, so $f$ never vanishes.

**Step 2. Logarithmic factorization.** Because $f$ is entire and zero-free, ther
e exists an entire function $g$ with $f=e^{g}$ (the logarithm exists globally on
 $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$, and composing with the covering $\exp:\mathbb{C}\to
\mathbb{C}^{*}$ gives an entire lift since $\mathbb{C}$ is simply connected).

The hypothesis becomes
$$e^{\operatorname{Re} g(z)}=|f(z)|\ge \frac{1}{|z|+1},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \
operatorname{Re} g(z)\ge -\log(|z|+1).$$

**Step 3. An auxiliary entire function of linear growth.** Set $\varphi(z)=e^{-g
(z)}$. Then $\varphi$ is entire, never zero, and
$$|\varphi(z)|=e^{-\operatorname{Re}g(z)}\le |z|+1.$$

For $|z|\ge 1$ we have $|\varphi(z)|\le |z|+1\le 2|z|$. By the generalized Liouv
ille theorem (Cauchy estimates applied to $z^{-2}\varphi(z)$ at infinity, or equ
ivalently comparing Taylor coefficients: $|\varphi^{(n)}(0)|\le n!\,R^{-n}\cdot
2R$ for large $R$, giving $0$ for $n\ge 2$), $\varphi$ is a polynomial of degree
 at most $1$:
$$\varphi(z)=az+b,\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{C}.$$

**Step 4. $\varphi$ is constant.** Since $\varphi=e^{-g}$ never vanishes, the li
near polynomial $az+b$ has no zero in $\mathbb{C}$. If $a\ne 0$, then $z=-b/a$ i
s a zero — contradiction. Hence $a=0$ and $\varphi\equiv b$ with $b\ne 0$.

**Step 5. $f$ is constant.** From $e^{-g(z)}\equiv b$ (constant), differentiatin
g gives $-g'(z)e^{-g(z)}=0$, so $g'\equiv 0$ and $g\equiv g_{0}$ is constant. Th
us
$$f(z)\equiv e^{g_{0}}=:c,\qquad c\ne 0.$$

**Step 6. Determining the admissible constants.** For a constant $f\equiv c$, th
e condition $|c|\ge \frac{1}{|z|+1}$ for all $z$ is equivalent to $|c|\ge \max_{
z\in\mathbb{C}}\frac{1}{|z|+1}=\frac{1}{0+1}=1$.

So $|c|\ge 1$.

**Verification.** Conversely, every constant $c$ with $|c|\ge 1$ satisfies $|c|\
ge 1\ge \frac{1}{|z|+1}$ for all $z$. ✓

---

**Conclusion.** The solutions are precisely
$$\boxed{\,f(z)\equiv c\quad\text{with }|c|\ge 1\,}.$$
