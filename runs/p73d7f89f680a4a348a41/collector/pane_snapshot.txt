## Proof

**Setup and notation.** Let $R$ be a ring with distinct minimal prime ideals $p_
1, \dots, p_n$, and let $D = R/p_1 \times \cdots \times R/p_n$. Since each $p_i$
 is prime, each $R/p_i$ is an integral domain. We interpret the element $(1+p_1,
\, p_2,\, \dots,\, p_n)$ as $e_1 = (1+p_1,\, 0+p_2,\, \dots,\, 0+p_n)$, the unit
 of the first factor.

**Claim.** Any element $x \in (p_2 \cap \cdots \cap p_n) \setminus p_1$ satisfie
s $\operatorname{ann}_D(x+p_1,\dots,x+p_n) = \operatorname{ann}_D(e_1)$.

**Step 1: Compute $\operatorname{ann}_D(e_1)$.** An element $(a_1+p_1,\dots,a_n+
p_n) \in D$ annihilates $e_1$ iff
$$(a_1+p_1,\dots,a_n+p_n)\cdot(1+p_1,0,\dots,0) = (a_1+p_1,0,\dots,0) = 0,$$
which holds iff $a_1 \in p_1$. Hence
$$\operatorname{ann}_D(e_1) = 0 \times R/p_2 \times \cdots \times R/p_n.$$

**Step 2: Compute $\operatorname{ann}_D(\bar{x})$ for $\bar{x}=(x+p_1,\dots,x+p_
n)$.** An element $(a_1+p_1,\dots,a_n+p_n)$ annihilates $\bar{x}$ iff $a_i x \in
 p_i$ for every $i$, i.e.,
$$\operatorname{ann}_D(\bar{x}) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} \operatorname{ann}_{R/p_i}(x+p
_i).$$
Since each $R/p_i$ is a domain, $\operatorname{ann}_{R/p_i}(x+p_i) = 0$ if $x \n
otin p_i$, and $\operatorname{ann}_{R/p_i}(x+p_i) = R/p_i$ if $x \in p_i$.

**Step 3: Choose $x \in (p_2\cap\cdots\cap p_n)\setminus p_1$.** For such $x$: $
x \notin p_1$ gives $\operatorname{ann}_{R/p_1}(x+p_1)=0$, and $x \in p_i$ for $
i\ge 2$ gives $\operatorname{ann}_{R/p_i}(x+p_i)=R/p_i$. Therefore
$$\operatorname{ann}_D(\bar{x}) = 0 \times R/p_2 \times \cdots \times R/p_n = \o
peratorname{ann}_D(e_1).$$

**Step 4: Existence of such $x$.** It remains to show $(p_2\cap\cdots\cap p_n)\s
etminus p_1 \neq \emptyset$, i.e., $p_2\cap\cdots\cap p_n \not\subseteq p_1$.

Suppose for contradiction that $p_2\cap\cdots\cap p_n \subseteq p_1$. Since $p_1
$ is prime, a standard result gives $p_i \subseteq p_1$ for some $i \in \{2,\dot
s,n\}$. But $p_i$ is a *minimal* prime, so any prime ideal contained in $p_i$ eq
uals $p_i$; in particular $p_i \subseteq p_1$ with both minimal and prime forces
 $p_i = p_1$, contradicting the distinctness of the minimal primes.

Hence $p_2\cap\cdots\cap p_n \not\subseteq p_1$, and the desired $x$ exists. $\b
lacksquare$
