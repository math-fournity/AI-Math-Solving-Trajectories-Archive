## Proof

Let $k$ be an odd integer with $k \geq 1$. We determine the cardinality of
$$F = \{ f \text{ entire} : f(x) = |x^k| \text{ for all } x \in (-1,1)\}.$$

**Step 1. Simplify $|x^k|$ on the two halves of $(-1,1)$.**

Since $k$ is odd, $x^k$ has the same sign as $x$:
- For $x \in (0,1)$: $x^k > 0$, so $|x^k| = x^k$.
- For $x \in (-1,0)$: $x^k < 0$, so $|x^k| = -x^k$.

Thus the condition becomes $f(x) = x^k$ on $(0,1)$ and $f(x) = -x^k$ on $(-1,0)$
.

**Step 2. Apply the identity theorem on $(0,1)$.**

Suppose $f \in F$. Then $f$ is entire and agrees with the entire function $g(z)
= z^k$ on the open interval $(0,1) \subset \mathbb{R} \subset \mathbb{C}$. The i
nterval $(0,1)$ contains a limit point in $\mathbb{C}$ (indeed every point of it
 is a limit point). By the **identity theorem** for holomorphic functions,
$$f(z) = z^k \quad \text{for all } z \in \mathbb{C}.$$

**Step 3. Check consistency on $(-1,0)$.**

Take $x = -\tfrac{1}{2} \in (-1,0)$. The defining condition requires
$$f\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right) = \left|\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^k\right| = \l
eft|-\tfrac{1}{2^k}\right| = \frac{1}{2^k}.$$

But Step 2 forces
$$f\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right) = \left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^k = -\frac{1}{2^k}.
$$

Equating: $-\dfrac{1}{2^k} = \dfrac{1}{2^k}$, i.e. $-1 = 1$, a contradiction.

**Step 4. Conclusion.**

No entire function can simultaneously equal $z^k$ (forced by the identity theore
m from the values on $(0,1)$) and equal $-x^k$ on $(-1,0)$. The two entire candi
dates $z^k$ and $-z^k$ are distinct (since $k \geq 1$), and the identity theorem
 permits only one. Hence
$$F = \varnothing, \qquad |F| = 0.$$

The cardinality of $F$ is $\boxed{0}$.
