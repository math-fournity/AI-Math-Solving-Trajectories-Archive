Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the surface $S$ with metric
 $E = G = \frac{4}{(1-u^2-v^2)^4}$, $F = 0$ on the unit disk $\mathbb{D}$ is geo
desically complete at some point $p$.

## Analysis

**The metric.** We have $ds^2 = E\,du^2 + 2F\,du\,dv + G\,dv^2 = \frac{4}{(1-u^2
-v^2)^4}(du^2 + dv^2)$, which is conformal to the Euclidean metric with conforma
l factor $\lambda = \frac{2}{(1-u^2-v^2)^2}$.

**Key tool — Hopf–Rinow.** On a connected Riemannian manifold, geodesic complete
ness at a single point implies geodesic completeness at every point, and is equi
valent to metric (Cauchy) completeness. Therefore, either $S$ is geodesically co
mplete at **every** point or at **no** point. The question "does there exist $p$
" reduces to: "is $S$ complete?"

**Testing metric completeness via radial distance to the boundary.** Consider th
e radial geodesic from the origin toward the boundary along $v = 0$, $u = s$ for
 $s \in [0,1)$. The Riemannian distance from the origin to a point at Euclidean
radius $r$ is

$$d(0, r) = \int_0^r \lambda\, ds = \int_0^r \frac{2}{(1-s^2)^2}\, ds.$$

Substitute $s = \tanh t$, so $1 - s^2 = \operatorname{sech}^2 t$ and $ds = \oper
atorname{sech}^2 t\, dt$:

$$d(0, r) = \int_0^{\operatorname{arctanh} r} \frac{2}{\operatorname{sech}^4 t}\
cdot \operatorname{sech}^2 t\, dt = \int_0^{\operatorname{arctanh} r} 2\operator
name{sech}^2 t\, dt = 2\tanh t\,\Big|_0^{\operatorname{arctanh} r} = 2r.$$

As $r \to 1^-$, the distance approaches $2$. Thus the boundary of the disk lies
at **finite** Riemannian distance ($= 2$) from the origin.

**Consequence.** The sequence of points $p_n = (1 - 1/n, 0)$ is Cauchy in the Ri
emannian metric (the distances $d(p_n, p_m) \to 0$ as $n,m \to \infty$ since bot
h approach distance $2$ from the origin), yet it has no limit in $\mathbb{D}$ (i
t converges to the boundary, which is not part of the manifold). Hence $S$ is **
not metrically complete**.

By the Hopf–Rinow theorem, incompleteness of the metric implies that $S$ is not
geodesically complete — and since geodesic completeness at one point on a connec
ted manifold would imply completeness everywhere, there is **no** point $p \in \
mathbb{D}$ at which $S$ is geodesically complete.

## Answer

**no**
