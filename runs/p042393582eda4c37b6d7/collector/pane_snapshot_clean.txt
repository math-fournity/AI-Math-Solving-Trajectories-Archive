# The q-uple (Veronese) Embedding and Hypersurfaces of Degree q

## Setup

Let $\mathbb{P}^n$ have homogeneous coordinates $(X_0 : \cdots : X_n)$, and let
$M_0, \ldots, M_N$ be **all** the monomials of degree $q$ in $n+1$ variables. Th
e number of such monomials is
$$N+1 = \binom{n+q}{q}, \qquad \text{so } N = \binom{n+q}{q}-1.$$

The **q-uple (Veronese) embedding** is the map
$$\nu_q : \mathbb{P}^n \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}^N, \qquad (X_0:\cdots:X_n) \;\
longmapsto\; \bigl(M_0(X) : \cdots : M_N(X)\bigr).$$

## Key observation: a degree-$q$ form is a linear form in the $M_i$

A hypersurface $V \subset \mathbb{P}^n$ of degree $q$ is, by definition, the zer
o locus
$$V = V(F) = \{P \in \mathbb{P}^n : F(P) = 0\}$$
of a homogeneous polynomial $F \in k[X_0,\ldots,X_n]$ of degree $q$. Since $F$ i
s homogeneous of degree $q$, it expands uniquely as
$$F(X_0,\ldots,X_n) = \sum_{i=0}^{N} a_i\, M_i(X_0,\ldots,X_n)$$
for some scalars $a_0,\ldots,a_N \in k$ (not all zero), because $\{M_0,\ldots,M_
N\}$ is a **$k$-basis** of the vector space of degree-$q$ homogeneous forms in $
n+1$ variables.

Now define the **linear form** on $\mathbb{P}^N$:
$$L(Y_0,\ldots,Y_N) = \sum_{i=0}^{N} a_i\, Y_i.$$

Then for every point $P = (X_0:\cdots:X_n) \in \mathbb{P}^n$:
$$F(P) = 0 \;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\; \sum_{i} a_i\, M_i(P) = 0 \;\;\Longleftri
ghtarrow\;\; L\bigl(\nu_q(P)\bigr) = 0.$$

## The correspondence

The equivalence above establishes a **bijection** between:

| Degree-$q$ hypersurface in $\mathbb{P}^n$ | Hyperplane in $\mathbb{P}^N$ |
|---|---|
| $V(F)$, with $F = \sum a_i M_i$ | $H = V(L)$, with $L = \sum a_i Y_i$ |

concretely given by
$$\nu_q\bigl(V(F)\bigr) \;=\; \nu_q(\mathbb{P}^n) \cap H, \qquad \text{where } H
 = V\!\left(\textstyle\sum a_i Y_i\right).$$

**Why this is well-defined.** The map $F \mapsto L$ sends the degree-$q$ form $F
 = \sum a_i M_i$ to the linear form $L = \sum a_i Y_i$. This is a linear isomorp
hism between the vector space of degree-$q$ forms on $\mathbb{P}^n$ and the dual
 space $(k^{N+1})^*$ of linear forms on $\mathbb{P}^N$. Two forms $F, F'$ define
 the same hypersurface iff they are scalar multiples, and likewise two linear fo
rms define the same hyperplane iff they are scalar multiples — so the isomorphis
m descends to a bijection on projective classes:
$$\{\text{degree-}q\text{ hypersurfaces in }\mathbb{P}^n\} \;\xleftrightarrow{\;
1:1\;}\; \{\text{hyperplanes in }\mathbb{P}^N\}.$$

## Geometric statement

Under the Veronese embedding $\nu_q$, every hypersurface of degree $q$ in $\math
bb{P}^n$ is sent to the **intersection of the Veronese variety $\nu_q(\mathbb{P}
^n)$ with a hyperplane** of $\mathbb{P}^N$:

$$\boxed{\;\nu_q\bigl(V(F)\bigr) \;=\; \nu_q(\mathbb{P}^n) \,\cap\, V(L) \;\subs
et\; \mathbb{P}^N,\;}$$

where $F = \sum a_i M_i$ and $L = \sum a_i Y_i$. Conversely, every hyperplane $H
 \subset \mathbb{P}^N$ cuts out on $\nu_q(\mathbb{P}^n)$ the image of a unique d
egree-$q$ hypersurface of $\mathbb{P}^n$.

## Why this works — the conceptual reason

The Veronese embedding is designed precisely so that **degree-$q$ polynomial con
ditions on $\mathbb{P}^n$ become linear conditions on $\mathbb{P}^N$**. The coor
dinates of $\mathbb{P}^N$ are *by construction* all the degree-$q$ monomials in
the coordinates of $\mathbb{P}^n$. Therefore:

- A degree-$q$ homogeneous polynomial $F = \sum a_i M_i$ is nothing but a **line
ar combination of the coordinates** $Y_i$ of $\mathbb{P}^N$.
- The vanishing condition $F = 0$ on $\mathbb{P}^n$ is *exactly* the vanishing o
f the corresponding linear form $L = \sum a_i Y_i$ on the image $\nu_q(\mathbb{P
}^n)$.
- Hence the zero locus $V(F) \subset \mathbb{P}^n$ is the **preimage under $\nu_
q$** of the hyperplane $V(L) \subset \mathbb{P}^N$; equivalently, $\nu_q(V(F))$
is the intersection $\nu_q(\mathbb{P}^n) \cap V(L)$.

This is the defining property of the Veronese embedding: it **linearizes** degre
e-$q$ equations. The space of degree-$q$ hypersurfaces in $\mathbb{P}^n$ is the
projective space $\mathbb{P}(H^0(\mathbb{P}^n, \mathcal{O}(q))) \cong \mathbb{P}
^N$, which is naturally identified with the dual projective space $(\mathbb{P}^N
)^\vee$ parametrizing hyperplanes of $\mathbb{P}^N$. The Veronese embedding real
izes $\mathbb{P}^n$ as a subvariety of $\mathbb{P}^N$ so that the restriction of
 $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^N}(1)$ to $\nu_q(\mathbb{P}^n)$ is $\mathcal{O}_{\math
bb{P}^n}(q)$, making hyperplane sections of $\nu_q(\mathbb{P}^n)$ correspond exa
ctly to degree-$q$ divisors on $\mathbb{P}^n$.

## Summary

The q-uple embedding $\nu_q : \mathbb{P}^n \to \mathbb{P}^N$ uses the degree-$q$
 monomials as coordinates. A degree-$q$ hypersurface $V(F) \subset \mathbb{P}^n$
 is cut out by $F = \sum a_i M_i$; writing $L = \sum a_i Y_i$ gives a hyperplane
 $V(L) \subset \mathbb{P}^N$, and
$$\nu_q(V(F)) = \nu_q(\mathbb{P}^n) \cap V(L).$$
Thus the Veronese embedding converts the nonlinear (degree-$q$) equation definin
g a hypersurface in $\mathbb{P}^n$ into a **linear** equation (hyperplane) in $\
mathbb{P}^N$, establishing a bijection between degree-$q$ hypersurfaces of $\mat
hbb{P}^n$ and hyperplanes of $\mathbb{P}^N$.
