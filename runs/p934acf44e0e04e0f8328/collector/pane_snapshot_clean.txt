## Answer

**The number of field extensions of degree $n$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, up to $\mathbb
{Q}$-isomorphism, is:**
- **Exactly 1** if $n = 1$,
- **Countably infinite ($\aleph_0$)** if $n \geq 2$.

---

## Proof

### Case $n = 1$

The only extension of degree 1 is $\mathbb{Q}$ itself. So there is exactly **1**
 such extension.

### Case $n \geq 2$: Lower bound (infinitely many)

We construct infinitely many pairwise non-isomorphic extensions of degree $n$.

**Construction.** For each prime $p$ with $p \nmid n$, consider the polynomial
$$f_p(x) = x^n - p \in \mathbb{Q}[x].$$
This polynomial is **Eisenstein at $p$** (since $p \mid p$, $p \mid 0$ for all l
ower coefficients, and $p^2 \nmid p$), hence irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$. Let
$\alpha_p = p^{1/n}$ and $K_p = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha_p)$. Then $_p : \mathbb{Q}]
= n$.

**Non-isomorphism.** We show that $K_{p_1} \not\cong_{\mathbb{Q}} K_{p_2}$ for d
istinct primes $p_1, p_2$ with $p_1, p_2 \nmid n$.

Since $f_p$ is Eisenstein at $p$, the prime $p$ is **totally ramified** in $K_p$
: $p\,\mathcal{O}_{K_p} = \mathfrak{p}^n$. The discriminant of the polynomial $f
_p$ is
$$\operatorname{disc}(f_p) = (-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\, n^n\, p^{n-1}.$$
The field discriminant $d(K_p)$ satisfies $d(K_p) = \operatorname{disc}(f_p) / [
\mathcal{O}_{K_p} : \mathbb{Z}[\alpha_p]]^2$. The index $[\mathcal{O}_{K_p} : \m
athbb{Z}[\alpha_p]]$ can only be divisible by primes dividing $n$ (by Dedekind's
 criterion: the only primes where $\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_p]$ may fail to be the full
 ring of integers are those dividing $\operatorname{disc}(f_p)$, and at $p$ the
Eisenstein condition ensures $\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_p]$ is $p$-maximal). Since $p \n
mid n$, the exact power of $p$ dividing $d(K_p)$ is $p^{n-1}$.

Therefore:
- $p_1^{n-1} \mid d(K_{p_1})$ but $p_1 \nmid d(K_{p_2})$ (the only primes dividi
ng $d(K_{p_2})$ are $p_2$ and primes dividing $n$, and $p_1 \neq p_2$, $p_1 \nmi
d n$).

Since isomorphic number fields have equal discriminants, $K_{p_1} \not\cong K_{p
_2}$.

As there are **infinitely many primes** $p$ with $p \nmid n$, we obtain infinite
ly many pairwise non-isomorphic degree-$n$ extensions of $\mathbb{Q}$.

### Case $n \geq 2$: Upper bound (countably many)

Every degree-$n$ extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ is of the form $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ w
here $\alpha$ is algebraic of degree $n$, hence a root of an irreducible polynom
ial of degree $n$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. The set $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ is countable, so th
e set of irreducible polynomials of degree $n$ is countable. Each such polynomia
l has at most $n$ roots, giving at most $n$ extensions (some possibly isomorphic
). Thus the total number of degree-$n$ extensions is **at most countable**.

### Conclusion for $n \geq 2$

Combining the lower and upper bounds, the number of field extensions of degree $
n$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ up to isomorphism is exactly **$\aleph_0$** (countably infi
nite).

### Remark on other base fields

The answer depends on the base field $K$:
- **Algebraically closed field** (e.g., $\mathbb{C}$): $0$ for $n \geq 2$, $1$ f
or $n=1$ (no nontrivial algebraic extensions).
- **Finite field $\mathbb{F}_q$**: exactly **1** for every $n$ (the unique exten
sion is $\mathbb{F}_{q^n}$, the splitting field of $x^{q^n}-x$).
- **$\mathbb{R}$**: $1$ for $n=1$, $1$ for $n=2$ (namely $\mathbb{C}$), $0$ for
$n \geq 3$ (by the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra applied to $\mathbb{C}$ being
algebraically closed, so $\mathbb{R}$ has no finite extensions of degree $\geq 3
$).
- **$\mathbb{Q}$**: as proved above, $1$ for $n=1$, $\aleph_0$ for $n \geq 2$.
