## Proof

**Claim:** $D(|\Delta|^{1/2}) = H^1_0(U)$ (Dirichlet realization).

### Step 1: Self-Adjoint Realization and $|\Delta| = -\Delta$

The Laplacian $\Delta$ is negative semi-definite: for $u \in H^2(U) \cap H^1_0(U
)$,
$$\langle \Delta u, u\rangle_{L^2} = -\int_U |\nabla u|^2\,dx \leq 0.$$

The operator $A := -\Delta_D$ with domain $D(A) = H^2(U) \cap H^1_0(U)$ (Dirichl
et boundary conditions) is a **positive self-adjoint** operator on $L^2(U)$. In
the spectral sense, $|\Delta| = -\Delta_D = A$, so $|\Delta|^{1/2} = A^{1/2}$.

### Step 2: Domain of $A^{1/2}$ = Form Domain of $A$

By the spectral theorem for positive self-adjoint operators, $A^{1/2}$ is well-d
efined via
$$A^{1/2} = \int_0^\infty \lambda^{1/2}\,dE(\lambda),$$
where $E$ is the spectral resolution of $A$. A fundamental result (Kato, *Pertur
bation Theory for Linear Operators*, Ch. VI, Thm. 2.23) states:

$$D(A^{1/2}) = D(q_A),$$

where $q_A(u) = \langle Au, u\rangle$ is the **closed quadratic form** associate
d with $A$, and $D(q_A)$ is its **form domain** — the completion of $D(A)$ with
respect to the form norm $\|u\|_q^2 = \|u\|_H^2 + q_A(u)$.

### Step 3: The Quadratic Form of $-\Delta_D$

For $u \in D(A) = H^2(U) \cap H^1_0(U)$, integration by parts (Green's formula)
with the Dirichlet condition $u|_{\partial U} = 0$ gives:

$$q(u) = \langle -\Delta u, u\rangle_{L^2} = \int_U |\nabla u|^2\,dx.$$

The form norm is:
$$\|u\|_q^2 = \|u\|_{L^2}^2 + \|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^2 = \|u\|_{H^1(U)}^2.$$

### Step 4: The Form Domain is $H^1_0(U)$

The form domain is the completion of $D(A) = H^2(U) \cap H^1_0(U)$ with respect
to $\|\cdot\|_q = \|\cdot\|_{H^1}$.

- **Density:** $C_c^\infty(U) \subset H^2(U) \cap H^1_0(U) \subset H^1_0(U)$, an
d $C_c^\infty(U)$ is dense in $H^1_0(U)$ by definition. Hence $H^2(U) \cap H^1_0
(U)$ is dense in $H^1_0(U)$.

- **Completeness:** $H^1_0(U)$ is a closed subspace of $H^1(U)$, hence complete
with respect to $\|\cdot\|_{H^1}$.

- **Closedness of the form:** By Poincaré's inequality (valid since $U$ is bound
ed), there exists $C > 0$ such that $\|u\|_{L^2} \leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}$ for a
ll $u \in H^1_0(U)$. Thus $q(u)^{1/2} = \|\nabla u\|_{L^2}$ is equivalent to $\|
u\|_{H^1}$ on $H^1_0(U)$, confirming the form is closed.

Therefore, the form domain is $H^1_0(U)$.

### Step 5: Verification via Interpolation (Alternative Argument)

By the Kato–Heinz theorem, for a positive self-adjoint operator $A$:
$$D(A^{1/2}) = [H, D(A)]_{1/2} \quad \text{(complex interpolation)}.$$

For $A = -\Delta_D$ with $D(A) = H^2(U) \cap H^1_0(U)$, and using that $[L^2(U),
 H^2(U) \cap H^1_0(U)]_{1/2} = H^1_0(U)$ (a standard interpolation result for So
bolev spaces with Dirichlet conditions), we recover:

$$D(|\Delta|^{1/2}) = H^1_0(U).$$

### Conclusion

The domain of $|\Delta|^{1/2}$ is:

$$\boxed{D(|\Delta|^{1/2}) = H^1_0(U)}$$

This is the form domain of the Dirichlet Laplacian — the Sobolev space $H^1(U)$
with zero trace on $\partial U$.

elta_N$ with domain $\{u \in H^2(U) : \partial_\nu u|_{\partial U} = 0\}$, the a
