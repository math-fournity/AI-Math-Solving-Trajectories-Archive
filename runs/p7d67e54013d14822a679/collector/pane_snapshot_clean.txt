## Proof

Let $F$ be a field with $1+1\neq 0$ (so $2\neq 0$ in $F$). We prove that the sol
ution set of
$$x^2+y^2=1,\qquad x,y\in F,$$
is exactly
$$\{(1,0)\}\;\cup\;\left\{\left(\frac{r^2-1}{r^2+1},\,\frac{2r}{r^2+1}\right):r\
in F,\;r^2\neq -1\right\}.$$

---

### Step 1. The listed points are solutions.

- $(1,0)$: $\;1^2+0^2=1$. ✓
- For $r\in F$ with $r^2\neq -1$ (so $r^2+1\neq 0$ and the fractions are defined
), set $x=\frac{r^2-1}{r^2+1}$, $y=\frac{2r}{r^2+1}$. Then
$$x^2+y^2=\frac{(r^2-1)^2+(2r)^2}{(r^2+1)^2}=\frac{r^4-2r^2+1+4r^2}{(r^2+1)^2}=\
frac{r^4+2r^2+1}{(r^2+1)^2}=\frac{(r^2+1)^2}{(r^2+1)^2}=1.\;\checkmark$$

### Step 2. Every solution other than $(1,0)$ arises from some $r$ with $r^2\neq
 -1$.

Let $(x,y)\in F^2$ satisfy $x^2+y^2=1$ and $(x,y)\neq (1,0)$.

**$1-x\neq 0$.** If $x=1$, then $y^2=1-x^2=0$, so $y=0$, giving $(x,y)=(1,0)$, c
ontradicting our assumption. Hence $1-x\neq 0$.

Define
$$r:=\frac{y}{1-x}\in F.$$
The line through $(1,0)$ with this parameter is $y=r(1-x)$, i.e. $y=-r(x-1)$.

**$r^2\neq -1$.** Using $y^2=1-x^2=(1-x)(1+x)$,
$$r^2=\frac{y^2}{(1-x)^2}=\frac{(1-x)(1+x)}{(1-x)^2}=\frac{1+x}{1-x}.$$
If $r^2=-1$, then $\frac{1+x}{1-x}=-1$, so $1+x=-(1-x)=-1+x$, giving $1=-1$, i.e
. $2=0$, contradicting $1+1\neq 0$. Thus $r^2\neq -1$, and in particular $r^2+1\
neq 0$.

**Recovering $(x,y)$ from $r$.** Substituting $y=r(1-x)$ into $x^2+y^2=1$:
$$x^2+r^2(1-x)^2=1.$$
Expanding: $(1+r^2)x^2-2r^2 x+(r^2-1)=0$. Since $(1,0)$ lies on both the circle
and the line, $x=1$ is a root. Factoring:
$$(x-1)\bigl((1+r^2)x-(r^2-1)\bigr)=0.$$
(The expansion checks: $(1+r^2)x^2-(r^2-1)x-(1+r^2)x+(r^2-1)=(1+r^2)x^2-2r^2 x+(
r^2-1)$.) ✓

Since $(x,y)\neq(1,0)$, we have $x\neq 1$, so the other root applies:
$$x=\frac{r^2-1}{r^2+1},\qquad y=r(1-x)=r\!\left(1-\frac{r^2-1}{r^2+1}\right)=r\
cdot\frac{2}{r^2+1}=\frac{2r}{r^2+1}.$$

Thus every solution $(x,y)\neq(1,0)$ is of the required form.

### Step 3. Conclusion.

Combining Steps 1 and 2: the solution set consists precisely of $(1,0)$ together
 with all points $\left(\frac{r^2-1}{r^2+1},\frac{2r}{r^2+1}\right)$ as $r$ rang
es over $\{r\in F:r^2\neq -1\}$. $\blacksquare$
