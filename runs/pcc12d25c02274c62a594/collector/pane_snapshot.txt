## Solution

We evaluate
$$L = \lim_{x \to \infty} e^{-x^2} \int_x^{x + \ln(x)/x} e^{-t^2} \, dt.$$

**Bounding the integrand.** For $t \in [x,\, x+\ln x/x]$ and $x \geq 1$, we have
 $t \geq x > 0$, hence $t^2 \geq x^2$, so
$$e^{-t^2} \leq e^{-x^2}.$$

**Bounding the integral.** The interval has length $\ln x / x$, so
$$0 \leq \int_x^{x+\ln x/x} e^{-t^2}\,dt \leq \frac{\ln x}{x}\, e^{-x^2}.$$

**Bounding the whole expression.** Multiplying by $e^{-x^2} > 0$,
$$0 \leq e^{-x^2}\int_x^{x+\ln x/x} e^{-t^2}\,dt \leq \frac{\ln x}{x}\, e^{-2x^2
}.$$

**The upper bound tends to 0.** We have $\ln x / x \to 0$ and $e^{-2x^2} \to 0$
as $x\to\infty$, so their product tends to $0$:
$$\frac{\ln x}{x}\, e^{-2x^2} \longrightarrow 0.$$

**Squeeze theorem.** Since the expression is squeezed between $0$ and a quantity
 tending to $0$,
$$L = 0.$$
