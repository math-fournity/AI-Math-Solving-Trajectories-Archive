Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the given conditions force
$I$ to be 2-generated.

**Answer: No.**

**Proof.**

The key observation is that the condition "$\varphi(I)$ is a prime ideal of $S$"
 is essentially vacuous—it can always be satisfied by choosing the right surject
ion, regardless of how $I$ is generated.

**Claim:** For any prime ideal $I$ of any ring $R$, there exists a surjective ri
ng homomorphism $\varphi: R \to S$ such that $\varphi(I)$ is prime in $S$.

*Proof of claim:* Take $S = R/I$ and let $\varphi: R \to R/I$ be the canonical q
uotient map. Then $\ker\varphi = I$, so $\varphi(I) = 0$ in $S = R/I$. Since $I$
 is prime, $R/I$ is an integral domain, so the zero ideal is prime in $S$. Thus
$\varphi(I) = 0$ is a prime ideal of $S$. $\square$

This means the hypothesis of the problem places no constraint on the number of g
enerators of $I$—it is satisfied by *every* prime ideal. So the question reduces
 to: "Is every prime ideal generated by two elements?" which is false.

**Explicit counterexample.** Let $k$ be a field and let $R = k[x_1, x_2, x_3, \l
dots]$ be the polynomial ring in countably many variables. Let $I = (x_1, x_2, x
_3, \ldots)$, the ideal generated by all the variables.

1. **$I$ is prime:** $R/I \cong k$, which is a field (hence an integral domain),
 so $I$ is prime (indeed maximal).

2. **$I$ is not generated by two elements:** $I$ is not even finitely generated.
 Any finite set of generators involves only finitely many variables, say $x_{i_1
}, \ldots, x_{i_n}$, and cannot generate any variable $x_j$ with $j \notin \{i_1
, \ldots, i_n\}$.

3. **The surjection condition is satisfied:** Take $S = k$ and $\varphi: R \to k
$ the map evaluating every variable at $0$. This is surjective with $\ker\varphi
 = I$, and $\varphi(I) = 0$ is prime in the field $k$.

All hypotheses are satisfied, yet $I$ is not generated by two elements.

**Remark.** Even in the Noetherian setting, counterexamples abound. For instance
, in $R = k[x,y,z]$, the maximal ideal $I = (x,y,z)$ is prime but requires $3$ g
enerators (since $\dim_k I/I^2 = 3$, the minimal number of generators is $3$). T
he quotient map $R \to R/I \cong k$ again satisfies the hypothesis with $\varphi
(I) = 0$ prime in $k$.
