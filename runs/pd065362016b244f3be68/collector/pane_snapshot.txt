## Answer

**No**, it does not follow in general. The inequality on a generating π-system d
oes **not** propagate to all Borel sets, even when the π-system contains the who
le space. Below is an explicit counterexample.

---

### Counterexample

Take the compact interval $[a,b]=[0,1]$, and consider the π-system

$$\mathcal{P}=\{\varnothing\}\cup\{[0,t]:0\le t\le 1\}.$$

This is a π-system: $[0,s]\cap[0,t]=[0,\min(s,t)]\in\mathcal{P}$. It contains $[
0,1]$ (take $t=1$), and it generates $\mathcal{B}([0,1])$, since every interval
$(s,t]\subseteq[0,1]$ can be written as $[0,t]\setminus[0,s]$, and the half-open
 intervals generate the Borel σ-algebra.

Define two finite measures on $([0,1],\mathcal{B}([0,1]))$:

- $\nu=$ Lebesgue measure restricted to $[0,1]$ (total mass $1$),
- $\mu=\delta_{1}$, the Dirac point mass at $1$ (total mass $1$).

Both are finite measures.

**Check the hypothesis.** For every $[0,t]\in\mathcal{P}$:

- If $t<1$: $\mu([0,t])=\delta_{1}([0,t])=0\le t=\nu([0,t])$. ✓
- If $t=1$: $\mu([0,1])=1\le 1=\nu([0,1])$. ✓
- For $\varnothing$: $0\le 0$. ✓

So $\mu(B)\le\nu(B)$ for every $B\in\mathcal{P}$.

**But $\mu\not\le\nu$.** Consider the Borel set $\{1\}$:

$$\mu(\{1\})=\delta_{1}(\{1\})=1,\qquad \nu(\{1\})=\lambda(\{1\})=0.$$

Thus $\mu(\{1\})=1>0=\nu(\{1\})$, so $\mu\not\le\nu$.

---

### Why the standard argument fails

The classical π-λ (Dynkin) theorem states that if two **finite** measures *agree
* on a π-system generating the σ-algebra and containing the whole space, then th
ey agree everywhere. The proof works because the class $\{B:\mu(B)=\nu(B)\}$ is
a λ-system, and the smallest λ-system containing $\mathcal{P}$ is $\sigma(\mathc
al{P})$.

For **inequality**, one would need the class

$$\mathcal{L}=\{B\in\mathcal{B}([0,1]):\mu(B)\le\nu(B)\}$$

to be a λ-system. But it is not:

- **Not closed under relative complements.** If $A\subseteq B$ with $A,B\in\math
cal{L}$, then $\mu(B\setminus A)=\mu(B)-\mu(A)$ and $\nu(B\setminus A)=\nu(B)-\n
u(A)$. The desired inequality $\mu(B)-\mu(A)\le\nu(B)-\nu(A)$ is equivalent to $
\mu(B)-\nu(B)\le\mu(A)-\nu(A)$, which is **not** guaranteed by $\mu\le\nu$ on $A
$ and $B$ alone.

In the counterexample above, $A=[0,t]$ (with $t<1$) and $B=[0,1]$ both lie in $\
mathcal{L}$, but $B\setminus A=(t,1]$, and $\mu((t,1])=1>\nu((t,1])=1-t$, so $(t
,1]\notin\mathcal{L}$.

Since $\mathcal{L}$ is not a λ-system, the π-λ theorem cannot be applied, and th
e inequality does not extend from $\mathcal{P}$ to all of $\mathcal{B}([0,1])$.

---

### Conclusion

The implication is **false**. The π-λ theorem guarantees uniqueness under *equal
ity* on a generating π-system, but *inequality* on a generating π-system does no
t propagate to the full σ-algebra, because the class of sets where the inequalit
y holds is not a λ-system. The Dirac-vs-Lebesgue example on $[0,1]$ with the π-s
ystem $\{[0,t]\}$ provides an explicit witness.
