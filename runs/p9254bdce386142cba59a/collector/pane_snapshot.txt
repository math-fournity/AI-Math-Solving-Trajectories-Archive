## Proof

**Key tool: the Cauchy–Green (Pompeiu) solution and its Laurent expansion at inf
inity.**

Let $g \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{C})$. A particular solution to $\dfrac{\partial \p
si}{\partial \bar z} = g$ is given by the Cauchy–Green integral

$$
\psi_0(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{C}} \frac{g(\zeta)}{\zeta - z}\,d\zeta
\wedge d\bar\zeta .
$$

The general solution is $\psi = \psi_0 + h$ with $h$ entire (since $\bar\partial
 h = 0$ on all of $\mathbb{C}$).

---

**Laurent expansion at infinity.** Suppose $\operatorname{supp}(g)\subset B(0,R)
$. For $|z|>R$ we expand

$$
\frac{1}{\zeta-z} = -\frac{1}{z}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\zeta/z} = -\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\
frac{\zeta^n}{z^{n+1}}, \qquad |\zeta|<|z|,
$$

so

$$
\psi_0(z) = -\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{z^{n+1}}\int_{\mathbb{C
}} \zeta^n\, g(\zeta)\,d\zeta\wedge d\bar\zeta
= -\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_n}{z^{n+1}}, \qquad a_n:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\ma
thbb{C}}\zeta^n g(\zeta)\,d\zeta\wedge d\bar\zeta .
$$

Since $d\zeta\wedge d\bar\zeta=-2i\,dA$, we have $a_n = -\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mat
hbb{C}}\zeta^n g(\zeta)\,dA(\zeta)$.

---

**Criterion for a compactly supported solution.**

*Claim.* A compactly supported solution $\psi$ exists **if and only if**

$$
\int_{\mathbb{C}} \zeta^n\, g(\zeta)\,dA(\zeta)=0 \qquad \text{for all } n\ge 0.
 \tag{$\star$}
$$

*Proof of Claim.*

$(\Rightarrow)$ Suppose $\psi=\psi_0+h$ has compact support, so $\psi(z)=0$ for
$|z|>R'$. Then $h(z)=-\psi_0(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{a_n}{z^{n+1}}\to 0$ as $z\to\i
nfty$. Thus $h$ is entire and bounded, hence $h\equiv 0$ by Liouville. Then $\ps
i_0\equiv 0$ for $|z|>R'$, forcing every $a_n=0$, i.e. $(\star)$.

$(\Leftarrow)$ If $(\star)$ holds, every $a_n=0$, so $\psi_0(z)=0$ for $|z|>R$;
thus $\psi_0$ itself is compactly supported. $\square$

---

**The example.** Let $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^2)$ be the standard bump
with $\operatorname{supp}(\varphi)\subset\overline{B(0,1)}$, $\varphi\ge 0$, and
 $\int_{\mathbb{C}}\varphi\,dA=1$. Set

$$
g := \varphi .
$$

Then $g$ is smooth with compact support, and

$$
\int_{\mathbb{C}} g(\zeta)\,dA(\zeta)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\varphi\,dA = 1 \neq 0.
$$

So the $n=0$ moment in $(\star)$ is nonzero. By the Claim, **no** solution $\psi
$ of $\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial\bar z}=g$ can have compact support. $\blacks
quare$
