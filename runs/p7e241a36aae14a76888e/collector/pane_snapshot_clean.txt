## Solution

**Problem.** Solve $\nabla^2 u = 0$ in the upper half-plane $y>0$, with boundary
 data
$$u(x,0) = \begin{cases} -1, & x<0 \\ 1, & x>0 \end{cases}$$
subject to the boundedness condition $|u|<M$.

### Step 1. Poisson integral representation

For the Dirichlet problem in the upper half-plane with bounded boundary data $f$
, the bounded harmonic solution is given by the Poisson integral
$$u(x,y) = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{y\, f(t)}{(x-t)^2+y^2}\,dt
, \qquad y>0.$$
Here $f(t) = \operatorname{sgn}(t)$ (the value at $t=0$ is irrelevant to the int
egral). Uniqueness of the bounded solution is guaranteed by the maximum principl
e for harmonic functions on the half-plane.

### Step 2. Evaluate the integral

Split at $t=0$:
$$u(x,y) = \frac{1}{\pi}\!\left[\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{-y}{(x-t)^2+y^2}\,dt + \
int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{y}{(x-t)^2+y^2}\,dt\right].$$

Using $\displaystyle\int \frac{y}{(x-t)^2+y^2}\,dt = -\arctan\!\frac{x-t}{y}$:

- $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{y}{(x-t)^2+y^2}\,dt = \Big[-\arctan\frac{
x-t}{y}\Big]_{0}^{\infty} = -\arctan(-\infty)+\arctan\frac{x}{y} = \frac{\pi}{2}
+\arctan\frac{x}{y}.$

- $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{y}{(x-t)^2+y^2}\,dt = \Big[-\arctan\frac
{x-t}{y}\Big]_{-\infty}^{0} = -\arctan\frac{x}{y}+\arctan(\infty) = \frac{\pi}{2
}-\arctan\frac{x}{y}.$

Hence the first (negative) piece contributes $-\bigl(\frac{\pi}{2}-\arctan\frac{
x}{y}\bigr)$, and summing:
$$u(x,y) = \frac{1}{\pi}\!\left[-\frac{\pi}{2}+\arctan\frac{x}{y} + \frac{\pi}{2
}+\arctan\frac{x}{y}\right] = \frac{2}{\pi}\arctan\frac{x}{y}.$$

### Step 3. Verification

**Harmonicity.** $u = \frac{2}{\pi}\arctan(x/y) = \frac{2}{\pi}\operatorname{Im}
(\log(x+iy))$ up to constants; equivalently $\arctan(x/y)$ is the imaginary part
 of the analytic function $\log z$ (after rotation), hence harmonic for $y>0$. D
irectly, writing $u = \frac{2}{\pi}\arg(x+iy)$ in the form $\theta$-coordinate,
one checks $u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0$ for $y>0$.

**Boundary values.** As $y\to 0^{+}$:
- $x>0$: $x/y\to +\infty$, $\arctan\to \pi/2$, so $u\to 1$. ✓
- $x<0$: $x/y\to -\infty$, $\arctan\to -\pi/2$, so $u\to -1$. ✓
- $x=0$: $u(0,y)=0$ for all $y>0$ (the transition value).

**Boundedness.** $|\arctan|\le \pi/2$, so $|u|\le 1 < M$. ✓

**Uniqueness** follows from the maximum principle: any two bounded harmonic func
tions agreeing a.e. on the boundary differ by a bounded harmonic function with z
ero boundary data, which must vanish identically.

### Step 4. Physical interpretation

The upper half-plane models a semi-infinite conductor/plate/medium. The boundary
 $y=0$ is held at two different constant "potentials": $-1$ on the negative half
 and $+1$ on the positive half, with a jump at the origin. The solution
$$\boxed{\,u(x,y) = \tfrac{2}{\pi}\arctan(x/y)\,}$$
is the steady-state **temperature** (equivalently, electrostatic potential, or v
elocity potential of an irrotational flow) inside the medium.

Geometrically, $\arctan(x/y)=\text{const}$ are **rays emanating from the origin*
*, so the **equipotential lines** are straight rays through the origin. The orth
ogonal trajectories — **semicircles** $x^2+y^2=R^2$, $y>0$ — are the **streamlin
es / heat-flow lines**, along which energy flows from the hot side ($x>0$) to th
e cold side ($x<0$).

The origin is a **boundary singularity**: the boundary data jumps there, and eve
ry equipotential ray (for values strictly between $-1$ and $1$) emanates from th
is single point. Near the origin the gradients become unbounded ($|\nabla u|\sim
 1/\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$), reflecting the concentrated "source/sink" effect of the dis
continuity, while $u$ itself remains bounded and varies smoothly through all int
ermediate values in the interior — a classic example of harmonic smoothing of di
scontinuous boundary data.
