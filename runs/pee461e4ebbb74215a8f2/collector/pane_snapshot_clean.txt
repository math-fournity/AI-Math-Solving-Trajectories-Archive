## Proof

**Setup.** Let $q = 1009$, which is prime (checked: no prime divisor up to $\sqr
t{1009} \approx 31.8$). Write $N = q^v \cdot M$ with $\gcd(M, q) = 1$ and $v = v
_q(N) \geq 0$.

**Divisor–residue structure.** A divisor $d \mid N$ is $\equiv 0 \pmod{q}$ iff $
v_q(d) \geq 1$. Divisors with $v_q(d) = 0$ are exactly the divisors of $M$. The
Synchronized condition requires every nonzero residue mod $q$ to be hit by exact
ly one divisor, so the divisors of $M$ (all $\not\equiv 0$) must form a **comple
te residue system** of the nonzero classes mod $q$. In particular:
$$\tau(M) = q - 1 = 1008 = 2^4 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 7.$$

**Cyclic decomposition.** Write $M = \prod_{i=1}^{s} r_i^{a_i}$ with distinct pr
imes $r_i \neq q$. The residue map
$$(b_1,\dots,b_s) \;\mapsto\; \prod_i r_i^{b_i} \pmod{q}, \qquad 0 \le b_i \le a
_i,$$
must be a bijection onto $(\mathbb{Z}/q)^*$, a cyclic group of order $1008$. Set
ting $H_i = \langle r_i \rangle \leq (\mathbb{Z}/q)^*$ and $m_i = |H_i|$:

- Injectivity forces $r_i^0, r_i^1, \dots, r_i^{a_i}$ to be distinct, so $m_i \g
eq a_i + 1$.
- The image is $\prod_i H_i$, of size $\leq \prod m_i$; bijectivity onto a group
 of size $1008 = \prod(a_i+1)$ forces $\prod m_i = 1008$ and $m_i = a_i + 1$ for
 every $i$.
- The $H_i$ form an **internal direct product** of the cyclic group $(\mathbb{Z}
/q)^*$, so the orders $m_i$ are **pairwise coprime** with $\prod m_i = 1008$.

**Coprime factorizations of 1008.** Since $1008 = 2^4 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 7$, a copr
ime factorization assigns each prime-power block $\{16, 9, 7\}$ entirely to one
part. The possible multisets $\{m_i\}$ (each $m_i \geq 2$) are:
$$\{1008\},\quad \{144, 7\},\quad \{112, 9\},\quad \{63, 16\},\quad \{16, 9, 7\}
.$$
The smallest entry appearing in any factorization is **7**. In particular, **no
$m_i$ equals 2**.

**Consequence for exponent-1 primes.** A prime $p \neq q$ with $v_p(N) = 1$ must
 divide $M$ with $v_p(M) = 1$, i.e. $p = r_i$ for some $i$ with $a_i = 1$, givin
g $m_i = a_i + 1 = 2$. Since $m_i = 2$ never appears in any valid coprime factor
ization, **no such prime $p \neq q$ exists**. (Equivalently: the only element of
 order 2 is $-1 \bmod q$, but order-2 cannot be an isolated factor since the ful
l $2^4$-part must stay together.)

**The remaining case $p = q$.** Taking $v = v_q(N) = 1$ gives $v_q(N) = 1$, so $
p = q = 1009$ is admissible provided a valid $M$ exists. Such $M$ do exist: pick
 a primitive root $r$ mod $1009$ (which exists since $1009$ is prime) and set $M
 = r^{1007}$; then $m_1 = 1008$, $\tau(M) = 1008$, and the divisors $1, r, \dots
, r^{1007}$ are exactly all nonzero residues mod $1009$. Then $N = 1009 \cdot r^
{1007}$ is Synchronized with $v_{1009}(N) = 1$.

If instead $v \geq 2$, then $v_q(N) \neq 1$, and no prime $p \neq q$ can supply
$v_p(N) = 1$ (shown above), so no valid $p$ exists in that case.

**Conclusion.** The only prime $p$ with $v_p(N) = 1$ for a Synchronized $N$ is $
p = 1009$, yielding the single remainder
$$p \bmod 1009 = 0.$$

The number of possible remainders is $\boxed{1}$.
