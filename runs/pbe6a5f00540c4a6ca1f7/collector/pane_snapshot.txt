## Solution

Let $G$ be the given graph on $n$ vertices, and let $H = \overline{G}$ be its co
mplement (so an edge is in $G$ iff it is not in $H$).

The condition says: for every pair $\{u,v\}$ of vertices, there exists a vertex
$w$ (possibly $w\in\{u,v\}$) adjacent to **neither** $u$ nor $v$ in $G$, i.e. ad
jacent to **both** $u$ and $v$ in $H$ (when $w\notin\{u,v\}$).

**Claim.** $H$ is connected and has diameter at most $2$.

*Connectedness.* Suppose $H$ is disconnected, and let $u,v$ lie in different com
ponents. Then $\{u,v\}$ is a non-edge of $H$, hence an edge of $G$. The conditio
n provides a vertex $w$ non-adjacent to both $u,v$ in $G$. Since $u,v$ are adjac
ent in $G$, we have $w\neq u$ and $w\neq v$; thus $w$ is a third vertex adjacent
 to both $u$ and $v$ in $H$. But a vertex adjacent to $u$ lies in $u$'s componen
t and a vertex adjacent to $v$ lies in $v$'s component — impossible for distinct
 components. Contradiction. So $H$ is connected.

*Diameter $\le 2$.* Take any non-adjacent pair $\{u,v\}$ in $H$ (equivalently, a
n edge of $G$). The condition gives $w$ non-adjacent to both $u,v$ in $G$. As ab
ove $w\notin\{u,v\}$, so $w$ is adjacent to both $u$ and $v$ in $H$, giving a pa
th $u\!-\!w\!-\!v$ of length $2$. Adjacent pairs in $H$ have distance $1$. Hence
 $\operatorname{diam}(H)\le 2$.

**Upper bound.** A connected graph on $n$ vertices has at least $n-1$ edges, so
$e(H)\ge n-1$. Therefore
$$
e(G)=\binom{n}{2}-e(H)\le \binom{n}{2}-(n-1)=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}=\binom{n-1}{2}
.
$$

**Construction attaining the bound.** Let $G = K_{n-1}\cup\{w\}$, i.e. a complet
e graph on $n-1$ vertices together with one isolated vertex $w$. Then $e(G)=\bin
om{n-1}{2}$, and the condition holds:
- If $\{u,v\}\subseteq V(K_{n-1})$: the isolated vertex $w$ is adjacent to neith
er $u$ nor $v$. ✓
- If one of them is $w$, say $\{w,u\}$: this is a non-edge, and $w$ itself is ad
jacent to neither $w$ (no self-loop) nor $u$. ✓

Thus the bound is achieved.

The greatest possible number of edges is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}}.$$
