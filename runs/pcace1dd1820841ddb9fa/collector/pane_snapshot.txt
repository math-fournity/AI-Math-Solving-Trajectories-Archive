## Characterization of the coordinate ring of $Z(x^2+y^2-1)$

Let $k$ be algebraically closed with $\operatorname{char}(k)\neq 2$, and let
$$A = k[x,y]\big/\bigl(x^2+y^2-1\bigr)$$
be the coordinate ring of the affine circle $C = Z(x^2+y^2-1)$.

**Claim.** $A \cong k[t,t^{-1}]$.

### Step 1. $-1$ is a square in $k$

Since $k$ is algebraically closed and $\operatorname{char}(k)\neq 2$, the polyno
mial $T^2+1$ splits in $k$. Fix $i\in k$ with $i^2=-1$.

### Step 2. The two maps

Define
$$\varphi : k[t,t^{-1}] \longrightarrow A, \qquad t\longmapsto \bar x + i\,\bar
y,\quad t^{-1}\longmapsto \bar x - i\,\bar y,$$
where $\bar x,\bar y$ are the images of $x,y$ in $A$. This is well-defined as a
$k$-algebra map because in $A$
$$(\bar x + i\bar y)(\bar x - i\bar y) = \bar x^2 + \bar y^2 = 1,$$
so the image of $t$ is a unit and $t^{-1}$ is sent to its inverse.

Define conversely
$$\psi : A \longrightarrow k[t,t^{-1}], \qquad \bar x\longmapsto \tfrac{t+t^{-1}
}{2},\quad \bar y\longmapsto \tfrac{t-t^{-1}}{2i}.$$
(Division by $2$ and by $i$ is legitimate since $\operatorname{char}(k)\neq 2$ a
nd $i\neq 0$.) To see $\psi$ is well-defined, verify the defining relation vanis
hes:
$$\psi(\bar x)^2+\psi(\bar y)^2-1 = \frac{(t+t^{-1})^2}{4}+\frac{(t-t^{-1})^2}{4
i^2}-1 = \frac{(t+t^{-1})^2-(t-t^{-1})^2}{4}-1 = \frac{4}{4}-1 = 0,$$
using $i^2=-1$. Hence $\psi$ descends to $A$.

### Step 3. The maps are inverse

Compute the two compositions.

**$\varphi\circ\psi = \mathrm{id}_A$:**
$$\varphi\!\left(\tfrac{t+t^{-1}}{2}\right)=\tfrac{(\bar x+i\bar y)+(\bar x-i\ba
r y)}{2}=\bar x,\qquad \varphi\!\left(\tfrac{t-t^{-1}}{2i}\right)=\tfrac{(\bar x
+i\bar y)-(\bar x-i\bar y)}{2i}=\bar y.$$

**$\psi\circ\varphi = \mathrm{id}_{k[t,t^{-1}]}$:**
$$\psi(\bar x+i\bar y)=\tfrac{t+t^{-1}}{2}+i\cdot\tfrac{t-t^{-1}}{2i}=\tfrac{t+t
^{-1}}{2}+\tfrac{t-t^{-1}}{2}=t,$$
$$\psi(\bar x-i\bar y)=\tfrac{t+t^{-1}}{2}-\tfrac{t-t^{-1}}{2}=t^{-1}.$$

Since the generators match, both composites are the identity. Therefore $\varphi
$ and $\psi$ are mutually inverse $k$-algebra isomorphisms.

### Step 4. Geometric interpretation (why char $\neq 2$ is necessary)

The projective closure of $C$ is $\widetilde C : X^2+Y^2=Z^2\subset\mathbb P^1_k
$-... in $\mathbb P^2_k$. Its points at infinity ($Z=0$) satisfy $X^2+Y^2=0$, i.
e. $(X/Y)^2=-1$. Over algebraically closed $k$ with $\operatorname{char}(k)\neq
2$ this yields **two distinct** $k$-rational points at infinity, $[1:\pm i:0]$.
A smooth conic is $\mathbb P^1$, and removing two $k$-points from $\mathbb P^1$
yields $\mathbb G_m=\operatorname{Spec}k[t,t^{-1}]$. This recovers the isomorphi
sm above and shows exactly why the hypotheses matter:

- If $\operatorname{char}(k)=2$, then $x^2+y^2-1=(x+y-1)^2$, so $A$ is **non-red
uced** (has nilpotents) and cannot equal the reduced ring $k[t,t^{-1}]$.
- If $k$ were not algebraically closed and $-1$ were not a square, the two point
s at infinity would form a single conjugate degree-$2$ closed point; removing it
 from $\mathbb P^1$ gives a form of $\mathbb G_m$ that is *not* split, with coor
dinate ring strictly smaller than $k[t,t^{-1}]$.

Under the given hypotheses (algebraically closed, $\operatorname{char}\neq 2$) n
either obstruction occurs.

### Conclusion

Over an algebraically closed field $k$ with $\operatorname{char}(k)\neq 2$, the
coordinate ring of $Z(x^2+y^2-1)$ is
$$A \;=\; k[x,y]\big/\bigl(x^2+y^2-1\bigr)\;\cong\; k[t,t^{-1}],$$
with the isomorphism given explicitly by $t\mapsto x+iy$, $t^{-1}\mapsto x-iy$,
where $i^2=-1\in k$. **Yes**, the coordinate ring is isomorphic to $k[t,t^{-1}]$
.
