## Solution

**Recall the definitions.**

The **Dirichlet function** $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ is
$$
f(x)=\begin{cases}1,& x\in\mathbb{Q},\\[2pt]0,& x\notin\mathbb{Q}.\end{cases}
$$

The **characteristic function of the rationals** $\chi_{\mathbb{Q}}:\mathbb{R}\t
o\mathbb{R}$ is
$$
\chi_{\mathbb{Q}}(x)=\begin{cases}1,& x\in\mathbb{Q},\\[2pt]0,& x\notin\mathbb{Q
}.\end{cases}
$$

These are in fact the **same function**: $f = \chi_{\mathbb{Q}}$.

**Construction of $\beta$.**

We need $\beta\circ f = \chi_{\mathbb{Q}}$. Since $f=\chi_{\mathbb{Q}}$, this be
comes
$$
\beta\circ f = f.
$$

The function $f$ takes only the two values $0$ and $1$. Therefore the condition
$\beta\circ f = f$ is equivalent to requiring
$$
\beta(0)=0 \quad\text{and}\quad \beta(1)=1,
$$
since for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$, $f(x)\in\{0,1\}$, and we need $\beta(f(x))=f(x
)$.

**Choose the identity function.** Let
$$
\beta(t)=t,\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.
$$

**Verification.** For every $x\in\mathbb{R}$:
$$
(\beta\circ f)(x)=\beta(f(x))=f(x)=\chi_{\mathbb{Q}}(x).
$$

Hence $\beta\circ f = \chi_{\mathbb{Q}}$, as required.

**Remark (generality).** The identity is the simplest choice, but it is not uniq
ue. Any function $\beta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ satisfying $\beta(0)=0$ and $\b
eta(1)=1$ works, since $f$ never attains any value other than $0$ or $1$, so the
 behavior of $\beta$ on $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0,1\}$ is irrelevant. For instance
 $\beta(t)=t^2$ restricted to the relevant values also works ($\beta(0)=0$, $\be
ta(1)=1$), as does $\beta(t)=\chi_{\{1\}}(t)$.
