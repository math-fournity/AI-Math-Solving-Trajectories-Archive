## Proof

We derive the explicit form of $K_{1/2}(x)$ using the integral representation of
 the modified Bessel function of the third kind.

### Setup: Integral Representation

For $\nu \in \mathbb{R}$ and $x > 0$, the modified Bessel function of the third
kind admits the integral representation

$$K_\nu(x) = \int_0^\infty e^{-x\cosh t}\,\cosh(\nu t)\,dt.$$

### Step 1: Evaluate $K_{1/2}(x)$

Setting $\nu = 1/2$:

$$K_{1/2}(x) = \int_0^\infty e^{-x\cosh t}\,\cosh\!\left(\tfrac{t}{2}\right)dt.$
$

**Substitution.** Let $u = \cosh t$, so $du = \sinh t\,dt$ and $t = \operatornam
e{arcosh} u$. As $t$ ranges over $[0,\infty)$, $u$ ranges over $[1,\infty)$. We
use the half-angle identity

$$\cosh\!\left(\tfrac{t}{2}\right) = \sqrt{\frac{\cosh t + 1}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{
u+1}{2}},$$

and $\sinh t = \sqrt{\cosh^2 t - 1} = \sqrt{u^2-1} = \sqrt{(u-1)(u+1)}$. Thus $d
t = du/\sqrt{(u-1)(u+1)}$, and

$$K_{1/2}(x) = \int_1^\infty e^{-xu}\,\sqrt{\frac{u+1}{2}}\,\frac{du}{\sqrt{(u-1
)(u+1)}}.$$

The factors $\sqrt{u+1}$ cancel:

$$K_{1/2}(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_1^\infty e^{-xu}\,(u-1)^{-1/2}\,du.$$

**Second substitution.** Let $v = u - 1$, $dv = du$:

$$K_{1/2}(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,e^{-x}\int_0^\infty e^{-xv}\,v^{-1/2}\,dv.$$

**Evaluating the integral.** This is the Gamma integral $\int_0^\infty e^{-av}v^
{s-1}\,dv = \Gamma(s)/a^s$ with $s = 1/2$ and $a = x$:

$$\int_0^\infty e^{-xv}\,v^{-1/2}\,dv = \frac{\Gamma(1/2)}{x^{1/2}} = \frac{\sqr
t{\pi}}{\sqrt{x}}.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{K_{1/2}(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,e^{-x}\,\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{x}} =
\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2x}}\,e^{-x}.}$$

### Step 2: Show $K_{-1/2}(x) = K_{1/2}(x)$

Using the same integral representation with $\nu = -1/2$:

$$K_{-1/2}(x) = \int_0^\infty e^{-x\cosh t}\,\cosh\!\left(-\tfrac{t}{2}\right)dt
.$$

Since $\cosh$ is an even function, $\cosh(-t/2) = \cosh(t/2)$. Hence the integra
nd is identical to that of $K_{1/2}(x)$:

$$K_{-1/2}(x) = \int_0^\infty e^{-x\cosh t}\,\cosh\!\left(\tfrac{t}{2}\right)dt
= K_{1/2}(x) = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2x}}\,e^{-x}.$$

### Conclusion

For all $x \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}$,

$$K_{1/2}(x) = K_{-1/2}(x) = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2x}}\,e^{-x}. \qquad\blacksquare$$
