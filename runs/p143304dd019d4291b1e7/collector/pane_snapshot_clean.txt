## Proof

**Answer: Yes, such fields exist.**

**Construction.** Let $p$ be a prime, let $K = \mathbb{F}_p(t)$ be the rational
function field over $\mathbb{F}_p$, and let $F = K(\alpha)$ where $\alpha = t^{1
/p}$, i.e., $\alpha$ is a root of $X^p - t \in K[X]$. Then $F = \mathbb{F}_p(\al
pha) \cong \mathbb{F}_p(t^{1/p})$, and $F/K$ is a purely inseparable extension o
f degree $p$.

**Why purely inseparable.** Over $K$, the polynomial $X^p - t = X^p - \alpha^p =
 (X-\alpha)^p$ has $\alpha$ as its only root (with multiplicity $p$). Hence the
minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $K$ is $X^p - t$, which is irreducible over
$K$ (by Eisenstein at the prime $t$, or by degree considerations: $t$ is not a $
p$-th power in $\mathbb{F}_p(t)$). So $[F:K]=p$ and the extension is purely inse
parable.

**Trace computation.** We use the definition $\operatorname{Tr}_{F/K}(\beta) = \
operatorname{tr}(m_\beta)$, the trace of the $K$-linear multiplication map $m_\b
eta: F \to F,\ x \mapsto \beta x$.

Take the $K$-basis $\{1, \alpha, \alpha^2, \dots, \alpha^{p-1}\}$ of $F$.

- **Multiplication by $1$:** the identity matrix, trace $= p \cdot 1 = 0$ in $K$
 (since $\operatorname{char} K = p$).
- **Multiplication by $\alpha^i$** for $1 \le i \le p-1$: the map $x \mapsto \al
pha^i x$ sends $\alpha^j \mapsto \alpha^{i+j}$, reducing modulo the relation $\a
lpha^p = t$ when $i+j \ge p$. In all cases the diagonal entries of the represent
ing matrix are zero (each basis element $\alpha^j$ is sent to a basis element $\
alpha^{i+j}$ with $i+j \not\equiv j \pmod{p}$, since $i \not\equiv 0$). Hence $\
operatorname{tr}(m_{\alpha^i}) = 0$.

Since the trace is $K$-linear, for any $\beta = c_0 + c_1\alpha + \cdots + c_{p-
1}\alpha^{p-1} \in F$ with $c_j \in K$:
$$\operatorname{Tr}_{F/K}(\beta) = c_0\,\operatorname{Tr}(1) + c_1\,\operatornam
e{Tr}(\alpha) + \cdots + c_{p-1}\,\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha^{p-1}) = c_0 \cdot 0
+ c_1 \cdot 0 + \cdots + c_{p-1}\cdot 0 = 0.$$

**Conclusion.** Every element of $F$ has zero trace over $K$. The key mechanism
is that $\operatorname{Tr}_{F/K}(1) = [F:K]\cdot 1 = p \cdot 1 = 0$ in character
istic $p$, and the purely inseparable nature of the extension forces all other t
races to vanish as well. $\\blacksquare$
