## Proof

Let $H$ be a connected graph with $h = |V(H)|$ vertices and $e_H = |E(H)|$ edges
. Let $G \sim G_{n,m}$ with $m = cn/2$, $c > 1$.

**Setup.** The giant component of $G$ is its largest connected component. For it
 to be isomorphic to $H$, three conditions must hold simultaneously:

1. **Isolation:** Some set $S \subseteq V(G)$, $|S| = h$, forms a connected comp
onent whose internal edge set is exactly (a labeled copy of) $E(H)$, with no edg
es from $S$ to $V \setminus S$.
2. **Largest:** Every connected component in $V \setminus S$ has strictly fewer
than $h$ vertices.
3. **Edge budget:** The remaining $m - e_H$ edges all lie within $V \setminus S$
.

**Counting favorable configurations for a fixed $S$ and fixed labeling.** Fix a
set $S$ of $h$ vertices and a specific labeled copy of $H$ on $S$ (i.e., a speci
fic injection of $H$'s edges into $\binom{S}{2}$). The total number of possible
edges in $G$ is $N = \binom{n}{2}$. These partition as:

$$\binom{n}{2} = \underbrace{\binom{h}{2}}_{\text{within } S} + \underbrace{h(n-
h)}_{\text{between } S \text{ and } V\setminus S} + \underbrace{\binom{n-h}{2}}_
{\text{within } V\setminus S}.$$

For the fixed labeled copy of $H$ to be an isolated component, the $m$ edges of
$G$ must consist of:
- Exactly the $e_H$ edges of $H$ (from the $\binom{h}{2}$ within $S$): forced.
- Zero edges between $S$ and $V \setminus S$: forced.
- The remaining $m - e_H$ edges chosen from $\binom{n-h}{2}$ edges within $V \se
tminus S$.

The number of such edge sets is $\binom{\binom{n-h}{2}}{m - e_H}$, out of $\bino
m{N}{m}$ total edge sets. So the probability (for this fixed $S$ and labeling) t
hat $S$ is an isolated component isomorphic to $H$ is:

$$\frac{\binom{\binom{n-h}{2}}{m - e_H}}{\binom{\binom{n}{2}}{m}}.$$

**Number of labeled copies.** The number of ways to choose $S$ is $\binom{n}{h}$
. For each $S$, the number of distinct labeled copies of $H$ (i.e., the number o
f distinct subgraphs of the complete graph $K_h$ on $S$ that are isomorphic to $
H$) is $\frac{h!}{|\operatorname{Aut}(H)|}$, where $\operatorname{Aut}(H)$ is th

**Ensuring $H$ is the giant (largest) component.** Given that $S$ is an isolated

uted as $G_{n-h,\, m - e_H}$ (since the remaining $m - e_H$ edges are chosen uni
formly from $\binom{n-h}{2}$ edges). For $S$ to be the **giant** component, we n
eed every component of this induced subgraph to have fewer than $h$ vertices. De
note this event:

$$\mathcal{E}_{n-h,\, m-e_H}^{(<h)} := \{\text{largest component of } G_{n-h,\,
m-e_H} \text{ has } < h \text{ vertices}\}.$$

**Combining (independence).** The event "$S$ is an isolated copy of $H$" and the
 event "$V \setminus S$ has no component of size $\geq h$" depend on disjoint ed
ge sets (edges within $S$ are fixed; edges within $V\setminus S$ are free). Unde

th $e_H$ edges, the remaining $m - e_H$ edges are uniformly distributed over $\b
inom{n-h}{2}$ possibilities—exactly the $G_{n-h, m-e_H}$ model. Hence the two fa
ctors multiply.

**Disjointness of events for different $S$.** Two different sets $S_1, S_2$ cann

nt (which requires $2h \leq n$). In the regime $c > 1$ with $h \sim \rho n$ (the

are mutually exclusive when we require uniqueness (if two copies of size $h$ exi

**Final formula.** Combining all factors:

$$\boxed{P\!\left(\text{giant component of } G_{n,m} \cong H\right) = \binom{n}{
h}\,\frac{h!}{|\operatorname{Aut}(H)|}\;\frac{\displaystyle\binom{\binom{n-h}{2}
}{m - e_H}}{\displaystyle\binom{\binom{n}{2}}{m}}\;\;P\!\left(\mathcal{E}_{n-h,\
,m-e_H}^{(<h)}\right)}$$

where $h = |V(H)|$, $e_H = |E(H)|$, $m = \frac{cn}{2}$, and the probability is z
ero if $m < e_H$ or $m - e_H > \binom{n-h}{2}$.

**Asymptotic behavior ($c > 1$, $n \to \infty$).** The giant component of $G_{n,
m}$ with $m = cn/2$ has $\sim \rho n$ vertices w.h.p., where $\rho \in (0,1)$ is

- If $H$ is **fixed** (i.e., $h = O(1)$ independent of $n$), then $h \ll \rho n$
