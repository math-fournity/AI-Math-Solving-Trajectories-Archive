## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, such integer functions $f(n)$ and $g(n)$ exist.

**Key facts:**

1. $\zeta(2) = \frac{\pi^2}{6}$ is irrational (Euler's classical result).

2. **Dirichlet's Approximation Theorem:** For any irrational number $\alpha$ and
 any positive integer $N$, there exist integers $p, q$ with $1 \le q \le N$ such
 that
$$|q\alpha - p| < \frac{1}{N+1}.$$

**Construction:**

For each positive integer $n$, apply Dirichlet's theorem with $\alpha = \zeta(2)
$ and $N = n$. We obtain integers $p_n, q_n$ with $1 \le q_n \le n$ such that
$$|q_n\,\zeta(2) - p_n| < \frac{1}{n+1}.$$

Define:
$$f(n) = q_n, \qquad g(n) = -p_n.$$

**Verification of the limit:**
$$|f(n)\,\zeta(2) + g(n)| = |q_n\,\zeta(2) - p_n| < \frac{1}{n+1} \xrightarrow{n
\to\infty} 0.$$

Hence $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty} f(n)\,\zeta(2) + g(n) = 0.$

**Verification of non-vanishing:**

Since $\zeta(2) = \pi^2/6$ is irrational and $q_n \ge 1$ is a positive integer,
the quantity $q_n\,\zeta(2)$ is irrational, hence $q_n\,\zeta(2) - p_n \ne 0$ fo
r every $n$. Therefore
$$f(n)\,\zeta(2) + g(n) \ne 0 \quad \text{for all positive integers } n.$$

**Conclusion:** The integer functions $f(n) = q_n$ and $g(n) = -p_n$ constructed
 above satisfy both required conditions. $\blacksquare$
