## Proof

**Lemma 1 (Obtuse ⟺ open semicircle).** Three points on a circle form an obtuse
triangle if and only if they all lie in some open semicircle (an arc of length s
trictly less than 180°).

*Proof.* Three points divide the circle into arcs of lengths α, β, γ with α+β+γ
= 360°. The inscribed angle opposite arc γ equals γ/2. The triangle is obtuse if
f some angle > 90°, iff some arc > 180°, iff the complementary two arcs sum to <
 180°, iff all three points lie in an arc of length < 180°. ∎

**Lemma 2 (Any 5 points have 3 in an open semicircle).** Among any 5 points on a
 circle, some 3 lie in an open semicircle.

*Proof.* Let the 5 points have consecutive gaps g₁,…,g₅ > 0, summing to 360°. Th
e five consecutive-pair sums g₁+g₂, g₂+g₃, g₃+g₄, g₄+g₅, g₅+g₁ sum to 720°. If a
ll were ≥ 180°, their sum would be ≥ 900° > 720° — contradiction. So some gᵢ+gᵢ₊
₁ < 180°, meaning three consecutive points lie in an arc of length < 180°. ∎

**Lemma 3 (4 points can avoid 3-in-a-semicircle).** The four vertices of an insc
ribed square have no 3 points in any open semicircle.

*Proof.* Points at 0°, 90°, 180°, 270° have all gaps equal to 90°. Every pair of
 consecutive gaps sums to exactly 180°, so no three consecutive points lie in an
 arc < 180°. For any triple, one of the three determined arcs always equals 180°
, so no triple lies in an *open* semicircle. (All triples form right triangles,
never obtuse.) ∎

**Upper bound: n = 13 suffices.** For any 3-coloring of the circle (all three co
lors appearing) and any choice of 13 points, by the pigeonhole principle at leas
t ⌈13/3⌉ = 5 points share one color. By Lemma 2, three of these five lie in an o
pen semicircle. By Lemma 1, they form a monochromatic obtuse triangle.

**Lower bound: n = 12 fails.** Place 12 points as three inscribed squares:
- Red: 0°, 90°, 180°, 270°
- Yellow: 45°, 135°, 225°, 315°
- Blue: 10°, 100°, 190°, 280°

Color the remainder of the circle arbitrarily (all three colors already appear).
 Each color class is a square, so by Lemma 3 no three same-colored points form a
n obtuse triangle. Thus 12 points can avoid the property.

**Conclusion.** Since n = 12 can fail and n = 13 always succeeds, the minimum va
lue of n is $\boxed{13}$.
