## Translation of Arithmetic into Z-Arithmetic by Relativization

### Setup

Let **Arithmetic** denote the complete theory of the structure $\mathcal{N} = (\
mathbb{N}; =, <, +, \cdot, 0, 1)$, and let **$\mathbb{Z}$-Arithmetic** denote th
e complete theory of the structure $\mathcal{Z} = (\mathbb{Z}; =, <, +, \cdot, 0
, 1)$. Both are in the same language $L(=, <, +, \cdot, 0, 1)$.

The goal is a translation $\varphi \mapsto \varphi^{\mathbb{Z}}$ from $L$-senten
ces (about $\mathbb{N}$) to $L$-sentences (about $\mathbb{Z}$) such that
$$\mathcal{N} \models \varphi \iff \mathcal{Z} \models \varphi^{\mathbb{Z}}.$$

### Defining $\mathbb{N}$ inside $\mathbb{Z}$-Arithmetic

The natural numbers are precisely the non-negative integers. In the language $L(
=, <, +, \cdot, 0, 1)$ this is definable by the formula

$$\mathrm{Nat}(x) \;:\!\!\iff\; \neg(x < 0),$$

equivalently $\mathrm{Nat}(x) \iff 0 \leq x \iff (0 < x \vee x = 0)$.

This uses only the symbol $<$ and the constant $0$, both of which are in the lan
guage of $\mathbb{Z}$-Arithmetic. Clearly, for $z \in \mathbb{Z}$:
$$\mathcal{Z} \models \mathrm{Nat}(z) \iff z \in \mathbb{N}.$$

### The Translation

The translation $\varphi \mapsto \varphi^{\mathbb{Z}}$ is defined recursively on
 the structure of $\varphi$. **Terms are left unchanged** — the function symbols
 $+$, $\cdot$ and constants $0, 1$ are interpreted identically in both structure
s.

**Atomic formulas.** For terms $t, s$:
$$(t = s)^{\mathbb{Z}} \;:=\; t = s, \qquad (t < s)^{\mathbb{Z}} \;:=\; t < s.$$

**Boolean connectives.**
$$(\neg \varphi)^{\mathbb{Z}} \;:=\; \neg\, \varphi^{\mathbb{Z}},$$
$$(\varphi \wedge \psi)^{\mathbb{Z}} \;:=\; \varphi^{\mathbb{Z}} \wedge \psi^{\m
athbb{Z}},$$
and similarly for $\vee, \rightarrow, \leftrightarrow$.

**Quantifiers — relativized to $\mathbb{N}$.**
$$(\exists x\, \varphi)^{\mathbb{Z}} \;:=\; \exists x\, \bigl(\mathrm{Nat}(x) \w
edge \varphi^{\mathbb{Z}}\bigr),$$
$$(\forall x\, \varphi)^{\mathbb{Z}} \;:=\; \forall x\, \bigl(\mathrm{Nat}(x) \r
ightarrow \varphi^{\mathbb{Z}}\bigr).$$

### Correctness Proof

**Claim.** For every $L$-formula $\varphi$ and every assignment $\sigma : \mathr
m{FV}(\varphi) \to \mathbb{N}$,
$$\mathcal{N}, \sigma \models \varphi \iff \mathcal{Z}, \sigma \models \varphi^{
\mathbb{Z}}.$$

(For sentences, $\sigma$ is empty and this gives the desired equivalence.)

**Proof by induction on $\varphi$.**

*Terms.* Since $\mathbb{N}$ is a substructure of $\mathbb{Z}$ (it contains $0, 1
$ and is closed under $+$ and $\cdot$: the sum and product of two non-negative i
ntegers is non-negative), every term $t$ with variables assigned in $\mathbb{N}$
 evaluates to the same value in $\mathcal{N}$ and in $\mathcal{Z}$, and that val
ue lies in $\mathbb{N}$.

*Atomic.* $(t = s)$: by the term observation, $t$ and $s$ take the same values i
n both structures, so $t = s$ has the same truth value. Likewise $(t < s)$: the
order $<$ on $\mathbb{N}$ is the restriction of the order on $\mathbb{Z}$, so th
e comparison gives the same result.

*Negation and connectives.* Immediate from the induction hypothesis, since the t
ranslation commutes with the connectives.

*Existential.* We have $(\exists x\, \varphi)^{\mathbb{Z}} = \exists x\,(\mathrm
{Nat}(x) \wedge \varphi^{\mathbb{Z}})$.
$$\mathcal{Z}, \sigma \models \exists x\,(\mathrm{Nat}(x) \wedge \varphi^{\mathb
b{Z}})$$
$$\iff \exists z \in \mathbb{Z}:\; \mathcal{Z} \models \mathrm{Nat}(z) \text{ an
d } \mathcal{Z}, \sigma[x \mapsto z] \models \varphi^{\mathbb{Z}}$$
$$\iff \exists z \in \mathbb{N}:\; \mathcal{Z}, \sigma[x \mapsto z] \models \var
phi^{\mathbb{Z}} \qquad (\text{since } \mathrm{Nat}(z) \iff z \in \mathbb{N})$$
$$\iff \exists z \in \mathbb{N}:\; \mathcal{N}, \sigma[x \mapsto z] \models \var
phi \qquad (\text{by IH, as } \sigma[x\mapsto z] \text{ maps into } \mathbb{N})$
$
$$\iff \mathcal{N}, \sigma \models \exists x\, \varphi.$$

*Universal.* $(\forall x\, \varphi)^{\mathbb{Z}} = \forall x\,(\mathrm{Nat}(x) \
rightarrow \varphi^{\mathbb{Z}})$.
$$\mathcal{Z}, \sigma \models \forall x\,(\mathrm{Nat}(x) \rightarrow \varphi^{\
mathbb{Z}})$$
$$\iff \forall z \in \mathbb{Z}:\; \mathcal{Z} \models \mathrm{Nat}(z) \Rightarr
ow \mathcal{Z}, \sigma[x\mapsto z] \models \varphi^{\mathbb{Z}}$$
$$\iff \forall z \in \mathbb{N}:\; \mathcal{Z}, \sigma[x\mapsto z] \models \varp
hi^{\mathbb{Z}} \qquad (\text{only } z \in \mathbb{N} \text{ matter})$$
$$\iff \forall z \in \mathbb{N}:\; \mathcal{N}, \sigma[x\mapsto z] \models \varp
hi \qquad (\text{by IH})$$
$$\iff \mathcal{N}, \sigma \models \forall x\, \varphi.$$

This completes the induction. $\square$

### Summary

The translation consists of three ingredients:

1. **Definition of $\mathbb{N}$ in $\mathbb{Z}$-Arithmetic:** $\mathrm{Nat}(x) :
\!\!\iff \neg(x < 0)$, i.e., $x \geq 0$.

2. **Terms and atomic formulas:** translated identically (the language is shared
 and $\mathbb{N} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}$ is a substructure closed under $+$ and $\
cdot$).

3. **Quantifier relativization:**
   - $(\exists x\, \varphi)^{\mathbb{Z}} = \exists x\,(\mathrm{Nat}(x) \wedge \v
arphi^{\mathbb{Z}})$
   - $(\forall x\, \varphi)^{\mathbb{Z}} = \forall x\,(\mathrm{Nat}(x) \rightarr
ow \varphi^{\mathbb{Z}})$

   with connectives commuting with the translation.

This yields a faithful interpretation: $\mathcal{N} \models \varphi \iff \mathca
l{Z} \models \varphi^{\mathbb{Z}}$ for every sentence $\varphi$ of Arithmetic.
