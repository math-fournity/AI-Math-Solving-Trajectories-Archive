## Proof

**Setup.** A cuboid has 8 vertices and 6 faces, each face containing 4 vertices.
 Each vertex lies on exactly 3 faces. Place the numbers 1–8 at the vertices (one
 per vertex). The constraint is: on every face, the sum of any 3 of its 4 number
s is ≥ 10. We seek the smallest possible value of the sum of the 4 numbers on a
face.

**Key observation.** For a face with numbers $a<b<c<d$, "any 3 sum to ≥ 10" is e
quivalent to the three smallest summing to ≥ 10, i.e., $a+b+c \ge 10$, i.e., $\t
ext{sum} - d \ge 10$, i.e., $\text{sum} \ge d+10$.

**Lower bound: no face can have sum ≤ 15.** Suppose a face has sum $S \le 15$. T
hen $d = S-(a+b+c) \le S-10 \le 5$, so all four numbers are at most 5, hence dra
wn from $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$. The largest possible sum of 4 distinct numbers from thi
s set is $2+3+4+5=14<15$, a contradiction. Hence every face has sum $\ge 16$.

**Construction achieving 16.** Label the vertices as $\{0,1\}^3$; the six faces
are obtained by fixing one coordinate. Assign:

| Vertex | Value |
|---|---|
| $(0,0,0)$ | 1 |
| $(0,1,0)$ | 6 |
| $(1,0,0)$ | 4 |
| $(1,1,0)$ | 5 |
| $(0,0,1)$ | 7 |
| $(0,1,1)$ | 3 |
| $(1,0,1)$ | 8 |
| $(1,1,1)$ | 2 |

The six faces and their checks:

| Face | Numbers | Smallest 3 sum | Face sum |
|---|---|---|---|
| $z=0$ | $\{1,4,5,6\}$ | $1+4+5=10\ge10$ ✓ | **16** |
| $z=1$ | $\{2,3,7,8\}$ | $2+3+7=12\ge10$ ✓ | 20 |
| $x=0$ | $\{1,3,6,7\}$ | $1+3+6=10\ge10$ ✓ | 17 |
| $x=1$ | $\{2,4,5,8\}$ | $2+4+5=11\ge10$ ✓ | 19 |
| $y=0$ | $\{1,4,7,8\}$ | $1+4+7=12\ge10$ ✓ | 20 |
| $y=1$ | $\{2,3,5,6\}$ | $2+3+5=10\ge10$ ✓ | **16** |

Every face satisfies the constraint, and the minimum face sum is 16.

**Conclusion.** The lower bound is 16 and it is attained, so the smallest possib
le sum of the 4 numbers on a side is $\boxed{16}$.
