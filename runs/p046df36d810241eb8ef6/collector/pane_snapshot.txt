# Maximal Ideals of the Ring of Continuous Functions on $[0,1]$

Let $R = C([0,1], \mathbb{R})$ be the ring of continuous real-valued functions o
n $[0,1]$, with pointwise addition and multiplication.

**Claim.** The maximal ideals of $R$ are exactly the ideals
$$M_p = \{f \in R : f(p) = 0\}$$
for $p \in [0,1]$.

---

## Proof

### Step 1. Each $M_p$ is a maximal ideal.

For each $p \in [0,1]$, define the evaluation map
$$\varphi_p : R \to \mathbb{R}, \qquad \varphi_p(f) = f(p).$$

This is a ring homomorphism: $\varphi_p(f+g) = (f+g)(p) = f(p)+g(p) = \varphi_p(
f)+\varphi_p(g)$, and $\varphi_p(fg) = f(p)g(p) = \varphi_p(f)\varphi_p(g)$, and
 $\varphi_p(1) = 1$.

It is **surjective**: the constant function $f \equiv c$ satisfies $\varphi_p(f)
 = c$ for any $c \in \mathbb{R}$.

Its kernel is $\ker \varphi_p = \{f : f(p) = 0\} = M_p$.

By the First Isomorphism Theorem, $R/M_p \cong \mathbb{R}$. Since $\mathbb{R}$ i
s a field, $M_p$ is a maximal ideal. $\checkmark$

### Step 2. Every maximal ideal is of the form $M_p$.

Let $\mathfrak{m} \subseteq R$ be a maximal ideal. We show $\mathfrak{m} = M_p$
for some $p$.

**Key lemma.** $\mathfrak{m}$ is a *proper* ideal, so $1 \notin \mathfrak{m}$.

**Sub-step 2a.** For every $f \in \mathfrak{m}$, $f$ has a zero in $[0,1]$.

Suppose for contradiction that $f \in \mathfrak{m}$ and $f$ has no zero on $[0,1
]$. Since $f$ is continuous on the compact set $[0,1]$, $|f|$ attains a positive
 minimum: there exists $\epsilon > 0$ with $|f(x)| \geq \epsilon$ for all $x \in
 [0,1]$. Then $g = 1/f$ is a well-defined continuous function on $[0,1]$, i.e.,
$g \in R$. Since $\mathfrak{m}$ is an ideal and $f \in \mathfrak{m}$, we have $g
f = 1 \in \mathfrak{m}$, contradicting $\mathfrak{m}$ being proper. $\checkmark$

**Sub-step 2b.** There exists a single point $p \in [0,1]$ at which *every* $f \
in \mathfrak{m}$ vanishes.

Consider the family of closed sets $\{Z_f\}_{f \in \mathfrak{m}}$ where $Z_f = f
^{-1}(0) \subseteq [0,1]$. By Step 2a, each $Z_f$ is nonempty. We claim this fam
ily has the **finite intersection property**.

Take any finite subfamily $f_1, \dots, f_n \in \mathfrak{m}$. Define
$$h = f_1^2 + f_2^2 + \cdots + f_n^2 \in \mathfrak{m}$$
(since $\mathfrak{m}$ is an ideal, each $f_i^2 \in \mathfrak{m}$, and their sum
is in $\mathfrak{m}$). By Step 2a, $h$ has a zero $p \in [0,1]$. But $h(p) = 0$
with $h = \sum f_i^2 \geq 0$ forces $f_i(p) = 0$ for every $i$. Thus
$$p \in Z_{f_1} \cap \cdots \cap Z_{f_n},$$
so the finite intersection is nonempty. The family $\{Z_f\}$ has the finite inte
rsection property. $\checkmark$

Since $[0,1]$ is compact and each $Z_f$ is closed (as the preimage of the closed
 set $\{0\}$ under a continuous function), the **finite intersection property**
implies
$$\bigcap_{f \in \mathfrak{m}} Z_f \neq \emptyset.$$

Choose $p \in \bigcap_{f \in \mathfrak{m}} Z_f$. Then every $f \in \mathfrak{m}$
 satisfies $f(p) = 0$, so
$$\mathfrak{m} \subseteq M_p.$$

**Sub-step 2c.** Since $\mathfrak{m}$ is maximal and $M_p$ is a proper ideal (fr
om Step 1, $M_p = \ker\varphi_p$ with $1 \notin M_p$), the inclusion $\mathfrak{
m} \subseteq M_p \subsetneq R$ forces
$$\mathfrak{m} = M_p.$$

$\checkmark$

### Conclusion

Combining Steps 1 and 2, the maximal ideals of $R = C([0,1], \mathbb{R})$ are pr
ecisely
$$\boxed{M_p = \{f \in R : f(p) = 0\}, \quad p \in [0,1].}$$

The correspondence $p \mapsto M_p$ is in fact a bijection between $[0,1]$ and $\
operatorname{MaxSpec}(R)$, reflecting that $[0,1]$ is compact (every maximal ide
al comes from a point) and Hausdorff (distinct points give distinct maximal idea
ls, since $M_p = M_q$ with $p \neq q$ is impossible: a continuous function vanis
hing at $p$ but not at $q$ exists, e.g. $f(x)=x-p$).
