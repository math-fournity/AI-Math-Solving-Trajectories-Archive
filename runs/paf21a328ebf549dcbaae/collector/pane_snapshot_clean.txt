# Solution

**Answer:** The possible values of $n$ are exactly the positive multiples of 4:
$n \in \{4, 8, 12, 16, \dots\}$.

## Proof

We work on the integer lattice $\mathbb{Z}^2$. A bishop and knight are on the sa
me diagonal iff $|\Delta x| = |\Delta y|$, and are at distance $\sqrt{5}$ iff $(
\Delta x, \Delta y) \in \{(\pm1,\pm2),(\pm2,\pm1)\}$.

Let $B$ be the set of bishops, $K$ the set of knights. Define $N(k) = \{b \in B
: \text{dist}(b,k)=\sqrt5\}$ and $D(b) = \{k \in K : k \text{ on diagonal of } b
\}$.

**Step 1: $|B|,|K| \ge 2$.**

If $|B|=1$, every knight must be at distance $\sqrt5$ from the sole bishop (cond
ition C2), but a $\sqrt5$-displacement has $|\Delta x| \ne |\Delta y|$, so no kn
ight lies on the bishop's diagonal — violating C1. Similarly $|K|=1$ is impossib
le. So $|B|,|K| \ge 2$.

**Step 2: Minimality forces $|B|=|K|$ and a cycle decomposition.**

*Claim (M1):* Every bishop $b$ is the **unique** element of $N(k)$ for some knig
ht $k$. Otherwise, removing $b$ leaves every knight with another $\sqrt5$-bishop
, and $b$'s own condition is moot — contradicting minimality.

*Claim (M2):* Every knight $k$ is the **unique** element of $D(b)$ for some bish
op $b$. (Analogous.)

By (M1), the map $\sigma: B \to K$ sending $b$ to a knight $k$ with $N(k)=\{b\}$
 is injective, so $|B| \le |K|$. By (M2), $\tau: K \to B$ is injective, so $|K|
\le |B|$. Hence $|B|=|K|=:m$, and $\sigma,\tau$ are bijections.

The permutation $\pi = \tau \circ \sigma$ on $B$ decomposes into cycles. Each cy
cle $b_1 \to b_2 \to \cdots \to b_\ell \to b_1$ corresponds to the geometric cyc
le:
$$b_1 \xleftrightarrow{\sqrt5} \sigma(b_1) \xleftrightarrow{\text{diag}} b_2 \xl
eftrightarrow{\sqrt5} \sigma(b_2) \xleftrightarrow{\text{diag}} \cdots \xleftrig
htarrow{\sqrt5} \sigma(b_\ell) \xleftrightarrow{\text{diag}} b_1.$$

**Step 3: Parity — every cycle has even length.**

Classify each lattice point by parity: **same-type** if $x \equiv y \pmod{2}$ (c
lasses $(0,0),(1,1)$), **mixed-type** otherwise ($(1,0),(0,1)$).

- A $\sqrt5$-displacement $(\Delta x,\Delta y) \equiv (1,0)$ or $(0,1) \pmod 2$
**swaps** same-type $\leftrightarrow$ mixed-type.
- A diagonal displacement $(s, \pm s) \equiv (s,s) \pmod 2$ **preserves** the ty
pe.

Tracking bishop types around the cycle: $b_1$ has type $C$; after $\sqrt5$ then
diagonal, $b_2$ has type $\neg C$; $b_3$ has type $C$; etc. In general, $b_i$ ha
s type $C$ iff $i$ is odd. For the cycle to close ($b_{\ell+1} = b_1$ with type
$C$), we need $\ell+1$ odd, i.e., **$\ell$ is even**.

**Step 4: $4 \mid n$.**

Since $m = \sum \ell_i$ with each $\ell_i$ even, $m$ is even. Thus $n = 2m \equi
v 0 \pmod{4}$. With $m \ge 2$, we get $n \ge 4$.

**Step 5: Every $n = 4j$ ($j \ge 1$) is achievable.**

*Base case ($n=4$):* Place $K_1=(0,0)$, $B_1=(1,2)$, $K_2=(2,3)$, $B_2=(1,1)$.
- **C1:** $B_1$ has $K_2$ on its diagonal ($|2{-}1|=|3{-}2|=1$); $B_2$ has $K_1$
 on its diagonal ($|0{-}1|=|0{-}1|=1$). ✓
- **C2:** $K_1$ has $B_1$ at $\sqrt5$ ($1^2{+}2^2=5$); $K_2$ has $B_2$ at $\sqrt
5$ ($1^2{+}2^2=5$). ✓
- **C3:** Checking all pairs: $D(B_1)=\{K_2\}$, $D(B_2)=\{K_1\}$, $N(K_1)=\{B_1\
}$, $N(K_2)=\{B_2\}$ — no extra edges. Removing any piece breaks the unique witn
ess for its partner. ✓

*General case ($n=4j$):* Take $j$ copies of the above, with copy $i$ shifted by
$(100i, 0)$. All within-copy coordinates are bounded by 3, so cross-copy distanc
es exceed $\sqrt5$, and cross-copy diagonal alignment is impossible ($|\Delta x|
 \ge 97$ vs $|\Delta y| \le 3$). Each copy is isolated, preserving all condition
s and minimality. Total: $n = 4j$. ✓

**Conclusion:** $n$ can be any positive multiple of 4.

$$\boxed{n \in \{4,\, 8,\, 12,\, 16,\, \dots\} = \{4j : j \in \mathbb{Z}^+\}}$$
