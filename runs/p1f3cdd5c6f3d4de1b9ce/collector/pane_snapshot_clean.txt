The group $\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$ is the Prüfer $p$-group $C_{p^\infty}$, eq

The group $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ decomposes as a direct sum of its primary comp
onents:
$$\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \;=\; \bigoplus_{\ell \text{ prime}} \mathbb{Q}_\ell/\ma
thbb{Z}_\ell \;=\; (\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p) \;\oplus\; \left(\bigoplus_{\ell

Under this decomposition, there is a canonical inclusion $i: \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathb
b{Z}_p \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$.

**The induced map.**

The inclusion $i$ induces a map by post-composition:
$$i_*: \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(G,\, \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p) \;\lon
grightarrow\; \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(G,\, \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).$$

We claim $i_*$ is a bijection.

**Injectivity.** If $i \circ \varphi = i \circ \psi$ for continuous homomorphism
s $\varphi, \psi: G \to \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$, then since $i$ is injective,
 $\varphi = \psi$.

**Surjectivity — the key step.**

Let $\varphi: G \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ be a continuous homomorphism. We show

*Step 1: The image $\varphi(G)$ is finite.*

The group $G$ is pro-$p$, hence compact. The group $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ is Ha
usdorff (whether given the discrete topology, or the subspace topology inherited
 from $S^1 = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$). A continuous image of a compact space in a
 Hausdorff space is compact, hence closed. So $\varphi(G)$ is a compact subgroup
 of $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$.

- If $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ is discrete, compactness forces $\varphi(G)$ to be
finite.
- If $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ carries the subspace topology from $S^1$, then any
infinite subgroup of $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ is dense in $S^1$ (or in some close
d arc), hence not closed in $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$, hence not compact. So again
 $\varphi(G)$ is finite.

In either case, $\varphi(G)$ is a **finite** subgroup of $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$
.

*Step 2: The image $\varphi(G)$ is a $p$-group.*

Since $\varphi$ is a homomorphism, $\varphi(G) \cong G / \ker(\varphi)$ is a fin
ite quotient of $G$. Because $G$ is pro-$p$, every finite quotient of $G$ is a f
inite $p$-group. Therefore $\varphi(G)$ is a finite $p$-group.

*Step 3: The image lands in the $p$-primary component.*

A finite $p$-group has no elements of order $\ell$ for any prime $\ell \neq p$.
Under the decomposition $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} = \bigoplus_\ell (\mathbb{Q}_\ell
/\mathbb{Z}_\ell)$, the projection of $\varphi(G)$ onto each $\ell$-primary comp
onent ($\ell \neq p$) is a $p$-group that is also an $\ell$-group, hence trivial
. Therefore:
$$\varphi(G) \;\subseteq\; \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p.$$

*Step 4: Factorization.*

Since $\varphi(G) \subseteq \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$, the map $\varphi$ factor
s uniquely through $i$: there exists a unique homomorphism $\widetilde{\varphi}:

Both $\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$ and $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ are discrete, so $\
widetilde{\varphi}$ is continuous (the preimage of any point is the same as unde

b{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p)$ and $i_*(\widetilde{\varphi}) = \varphi$.

This proves surjectivity.
