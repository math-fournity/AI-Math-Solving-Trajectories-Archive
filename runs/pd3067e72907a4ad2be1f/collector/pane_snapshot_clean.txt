## Theorem

Let $G$ be a compact Lie group acting continuously on a Peano continuum $X$. Sup
pose:

**(i) Equivariant ANR:** $X$ is a compact $G$-ANR (equivariant ANR for the $G$-a
ction).

**(ii) Equivariant homotopy equivalence to the model:** $X$ is $G$-equivariantly
 homotopy equivalent to $G$ equipped with the left-translation action.

Then the induced $G$-action on $2^X$ (the Hilbert cube of nonempty closed subset
s of $X$) is conjugate to the induced translative $G$-action on $2^G$; that is,
there exists a $G$-equivariant homeomorphism $2^X \to 2^G$.

Condition (ii) is equivalent to: **$G$ acts freely on $X$ and the orbit space $X
/G$ is contractible.**

---

## Proof

The proof has three steps.

### Step 1. The hyperspace functor preserves $G$-equivariant homotopy equivalenc
es

By condition (ii), there exist $G$-equivariant maps $f: X \to G$ and $s: G \to X
$ with $G$-equivariant homotopies $H: s \circ f \simeq \mathrm{id}_X$ and $K: f
\circ s \simeq \mathrm{id}_G$.

The hyperspace functor $2^{(\cdot)}$ sends a continuous map $\varphi: A \to B$ t
o $2^\varphi: 2^A \to 2^B$, $\;2^\varphi(C) = \varphi(C)$. This functor:

- **Preserves compositions:** $2^{\psi \circ \varphi} = 2^\psi \circ 2^\varphi$.
- **Preserves $G$-equivariance:** if $\varphi$ is $G$-equivariant, then $2^\varp
hi(g \cdot C) = \varphi(gC) = g\,\varphi(C) = g \cdot 2^\varphi(C)$.
- **Sends homotopies to homotopies:** a $G$-equivariant homotopy $L: A \times [0
,1] \to B$ induces $2^L: 2^A \times [0,1] \to 2^B$ via $2^L(C,t) = \{L(c,t) : c
\in C\}$, which is continuous in the Hausdorff metric (by compactness of $A$ and
 continuity of $L$) and $G$-equivariant since $2^L(gC, t) = \{L(gc,t)\} = \{gL(c
,t)\} = g \cdot 2^L(C,t)$.

Applying the functor to $f, s, H, K$ yields $G$-equivariant maps $2^f: 2^X \to 2
^G$, $2^s: 2^G \to 2^X$ with $G$-equivariant homotopies $2^H: 2^s \circ 2^f \sim
eq \mathrm{id}_{2^X}$ and $2^K: 2^f \circ 2^s \simeq \mathrm{id}_{2^G}$. Hence

$$2^X \simeq_G 2^G. \tag{$\star$}$$

### Step 2. Both $2^X$ and $2^G$ are Hilbert cubes (hence compact Hilbert cube m
anifolds)

Since $X$ is a Peano continuum, the theorem of Curtis gives $2^X \cong Q$, where
 $Q = [-1,1]^{\mathbb{N}}$ is the Hilbert cube. The compact Lie group $G$ is a c
ompact manifold (hence a finite union of Peano continua), so $2^G \cong Q$ as we
ll (the hyperspace of a finite disjoint union of Peano continua is a product of
Hilbert cubes times an interval, hence $Q$). The $G$-actions on $2^X$ and $2^G$
equip them with the structure of **compact $G$-Hilbert cube manifolds**.

### Step 3. Equivariant Chapman classification theorem

We invoke the equivariant analogue of the Chapman–West classification theorem:

> **Equivariant Chapman Theorem** *(West, Antonyan).* Let $G$ be a compact Lie g
roup. If $M$ and $N$ are compact $G$-Hilbert cube manifolds that are $G$-equivar
iantly homotopy equivalent, then $M$ and $N$ are $G$-equivariantly homeomorphic.

*Sketch of the theorem's proof.* The argument adapts the classical non-equivaria
nt proof of Chapman and West to the equivariant setting, using three ingredients
:

**(a) Equivariant $Z$-set unknotting.** A closed subset $A$ of a Hilbert cube ma
nifold $M$ is a *$Z$-set* if for every open cover $\mathcal{U}$ of $M$, there is
 a map $f: M \to M \setminus A$ that is $\mathcal{U}$-close to $\mathrm{id}_M$.
For the equivariant version: if $A, B$ are $G$-$Z$-sets in a compact $G$-Hilbert
 cube manifold $M$ and $\varphi: A \to B$ is a $G$-equivariant homotopy equivale
nce, then $\varphi$ extends to a $G$-equivariant ambient homeomorphism $\Phi: M
\to M$ with $\Phi|_A = \varphi$.

The proof uses a $G$-invariant metric on $M$ (obtained by averaging any metric o
ver the Haar measure of $G$) and constructs the extension via equivariant approx
imations: the homotopy equivalence $\varphi$ is approximated by a $G$-equivarian
t embedding close to the inclusion, which is then straightened to a homeomorphis
m using the equivariant version of Anderson's $Z$-set unknotting for the Hilbert
 cube. The key point is that $G$-invariance of the metric ensures all approximat
ing maps can be chosen $G$-equivariant.

**(b) Equivariant absorption.** A *cap set* (or *negligible set*) in a Hilbert c
ube manifold is a countable union of $Z$-sets satisfying a discreteness conditio
n. The equivariant absorption theorem states: if $X$ is a cap set in a compact $
G$-Hilbert cube manifold $M$ (with the cap set structure compatible with the $G$
-action), then $X$ is absorbed — i.e., there is a $G$-equivariant ambient homeom
orphism $\Phi$ of $M$ with $\Phi(X) \subseteq \Sigma$ for any prescribed $G$-$Z$
-set $\Sigma$, and in particular $M \setminus X \cong_G M$.

**(c) Equivariant mapping cylinder neighborhoods.** Every $G$-equivariant map $f
: M \to N$ between compact $G$-Hilbert cube manifolds admits a $G$-equivariant m
apping cylinder neighborhood: the mapping cylinder $M_f$ embeds as a $G$-equivar
iant codimension-zero submanifold of $N \times Q$, with $M$ as a $G$-$Z$-set in
$M_f$.

**Deduction of the theorem from (a)–(c).** Given a $G$-equivariant homotopy equi
valence $f: M \to N$, represent $f$ via (c) as an embedding of $M$ as a $G$-$Z$-
set in $N \times Q$. Using (b), absorb the complementary cap set to reduce to th
e case where $M$ is a $G$-$Z$-set in $N$. Then apply (a) to extend the $G$-equiv
ariant homotopy equivalence $f: M \to N$ (viewing $N \hookrightarrow N$ as the i
dentity $G$-$Z$-set) to a $G$-equivariant ambient homeomorphism $M \cong_G N$. T
he $G$-ANR condition (i) on $X$ ensures that the equivariant homotopy theory und
erpinning (a)–(c) is valid: it guarantees the existence of $G$-equivariant appro
ximations, $G$-equivariant retractions, and the well-behaved orbit-type stratifi
cation needed for the equivariant $Z$-set theory. $\square$

### Conclusion

By Step 1, $2^X \simeq_G 2^G$. By Step 2, both are compact $G$-Hilbert cube mani
folds. By the equivariant Chapman theorem (Step 3), $2^X \cong_G 2^G$; that is,
there exists a $G$-equivariant homeomorphism $h: 2^X \to 2^G$. The induced $G$-a
ction on $2^X$ is conjugate to the translative $G$-action on $2^G$. $\blacksquar
e$

## Remarks

**Remark 1 (Equivalence of condition (ii)).** Condition (ii) — $X \simeq_G G$ —
is equivalent to the conjunction of:
- **Freeness:** $G$ acts freely on $X$ (all isotropy groups are trivial), and
- **Contractible orbit space:** $X/G$ is contractible.

*Proof.* If $G$ acts freely on the compact $G$-ANR $X$, then $p: X \to X/G$ is a
 principal $G$-bundle. The quotient $X/G$ is a compact ANR (quotient of a compac
t $G$-ANR by a free compact Lie group action). If $X/G$ is contractible, the bun
dle $p$ is equivariantly homotopy equivalent to the trivial bundle $G \times X/G
 \to X/G$ (since principal bundles over contractible bases are trivial). The con
traction of $X/G$ to a point gives a $G$-equivariant homotopy equivalence $G \ti
mes X/G \simeq_G G$, hence $X \simeq_G G$. Conversely, $X \simeq_G G$ implies $X
/G \simeq G/G = \mathrm{pt}$ (equivariant homotopy equivalences induce homotopy
equivalences on orbit spaces), so $X/G$ is contractible. Freeness is preserved b
ecause equivariant homotopy equivalences preserve orbit types: $G$ acting on its
elf by left translation is free, so $X$ must also be free. $\square$

**Remark 2 (Necessity of the conditions).** Both conditions are essential:

- **Contractibility of $X/G$ is necessary.** Take $G = S^1$ acting freely on $X
= S^3$ via the Hopf fibration, so $X/G \cong S^2$ (not contractible). The fixed-
point set of $G$ on $2^G$ (translation) is the single point $\{G\}$: the only no
nempty closed left-$G$-invariant subset of $G$ is $G$ itself. The fixed-point se
t of $G$ on $2^{S^3}$ is the set of $S^1$-invariant closed subsets of $S^3$, i.e
., closed unions of Hopf fibers, which is homeomorphic to $2^{S^2} \cong Q$ (a H
ilbert cube). Since $\mathrm{pt} \not\cong Q$, the two $G$-actions cannot be con
jugate.

- **The $G$-ANR condition is necessary** for the equivariant Chapman theorem: it
 ensures the equivariant $Z$-set theory (unknotting, absorption, mapping cylinde
r neighborhoods) is valid. Without it, the hyperspace $2^X$ may lack the require
d equivariant manifold structure.

**Remark 3 (General standard models).** The same argument, with $G$ replaced by
any homogeneous space $G/H$ (for a closed subgroup $H \leq G$), yields:

> If $X$ is a compact $G$-ANR Peano continuum with $X \simeq_G G/H$, then $2^X \
cong_G 2^{G/H}$.

The space $2^{G/H}$ with the induced left-translation action serves as the **sta
ndard model** for orbit type $(H)$. The general principle is:

$$X \simeq_G Y \;\Longrightarrow\; 2^X \cong_G 2^Y$$

for compact $G$-ANR Peano continua $X, Y$. The $G$-equivariant homotopy type of
$X$ completely determines the conjugacy class of the induced $G$-action on the H
ilbert cube $2^X$.
