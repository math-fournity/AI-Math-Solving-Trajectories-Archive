## Finding L (Maximum Distance)

**Setup:** The path is 1 → a₁ → a₂ → ... → a₁₀ → 1, where {a₁,...,a₁₀} = {2,...,
11}. Total distance = Σ|consecutive differences|.

**Key decomposition:** In any cycle, each vertex v has degree 2. For each edge,
assign +value to the higher endpoint and −value to the lower. Then vertex v's ne
t contribution is v·(h_v − l_v), where h_v + l_v = 2, so (h_v − l_v) ∈ {−2, 0, +
2}:

- **+2 (peak):** both neighbors are lower → v is a local maximum
- **−2 (valley):** both neighbors are higher → v is a local minimum
- **0 (transition):** one neighbor higher, one lower

Total distance = Σ v·(h_v − l_v). Also Σ(h_v − l_v) = 0 (each edge contributes +
1 and −1).

**Maximizing:** With k peaks and k valleys (and 11−2k transitions):

Max total = 2·(sum of k largest) − 2·(sum of k smallest)

- k=5: 2·(7+8+9+10+11) − 2·(1+2+3+4+5) = 2·45 − 2·15 = **60**
- k=4: 2·(8+9+10+11) − 2·(1+2+3+4) = 76 − 20 = 56 < 60
- k=6: impossible (12 > 11 vertices)

So **L = 60**, uniquely achieved with peaks = {7,8,9,10,11}, valleys = {1,2,3,4,
5}, transition = {6}.

**Verification:** Cycle 7-1-8-2-9-3-10-4-11-5-6-7: distance = 6+7+6+7+6+7+6+7+6+
1+1 = 60 ✓

## Finding N (Number of Maximum Paths)

**Cycle structure:** Every edge connects a high vertex to a low vertex (peak↔val
ley, peak↔6, or 6↔valley). Since 6 is the unique transition (one peak neighbor,
one valley neighbor), removing 6 from the cycle yields a unique path of 10 verti
ces: 5 peaks and 5 valleys, strictly alternating, starting with a peak and endin
g with a valley (or vice versa). Vertex 6 reconnects the two endpoints.

Fixing orientation (path starts with peak, ends with valley):

**Path:** P₁-V₁-P₂-V₂-P₃-V₃-P₄-V₄-P₅-V₅, with 6 connecting V₅ back to P₁.

**Distance is arrangement-independent:** Computing the sum, each peak Pᵢ appears
 with coefficient +2, each valley Vⱼ with coefficient −2, and 6 with coefficient
 0. So total = 2·(7+8+9+10+11) − 2·(1+2+3+4+5) = 60 regardless of how peaks/vall
eys are assigned to positions. ✓

**Counting undirected cycles:** The 5 peaks can be assigned to positions (P₁,...

hors the path (removing 6 gives a unique path, and we fix direction by requiring
 "start with peak"), each undirected cycle is counted exactly once:

Undirected cycles = 5! × 5! = 120 × 120 = **14,400**

**No overcounting:** Unlike the even-n case (where no transition vertex exists a

**Counting directed sequences from dock 1:** Each undirected cycle, traversed st

intermediate values are distinct, no sequence is palindromic, so both directions

N = 2 × 14,400 = **28,800**
