## Group Structure

The maps $f(a)=1/a$ and $g(a)=1-a$ generate a group isomorphic to $S_3$ (both ha
ve order 2; their composition $f\circ g$ has order 3). The six elements are:
$$a,\quad \frac{1}{a},\quad 1-a,\quad \frac{1}{1-a},\quad \frac{a-1}{a},\quad \f
rac{a}{a-1}$$

**Excluded values:** $a=0$ (since $1/0$ undefined) and $a=1$ (since $1/(1-1)$ un
defined) cannot be roots.

## Orbit Analysis

The root set must be a union of orbits under this $S_3$-action. Special orbits (
size $<6$) arise from points with nontrivial stabilizers:

**Stabilizer of order 2** (fixed by some involution):
- Fixed by $f$: $a=\pm1$. Orbit of $-1$: $\{-1,\, 2,\, \tfrac{1}{2}\}$ (size 3).
 Orbit of $1$ involves $0$ → excluded.
- Fixed by $g$: $a=\tfrac{1}{2}$ → same orbit $\{-1, \tfrac{1}{2}, 2\}$.
- Fixed by $fgf$: $a=0$ (excluded) or $a=2$ → same orbit.

**Stabilizer of order 3** (fixed by $f\circ g$): $a = \frac{1}{1-a} \Rightarrow
a^2-a+1=0 \Rightarrow a = e^{\pm i\pi/3}$. Orbit: $\{e^{i\pi/3},\, e^{-i\pi/3}\}
$ (size 2, a conjugate pair ✓ for real coefficients).

**Generic orbits** have size 6 — impossible for degree 5 (whether real or comple
x, since a complex generic orbit would need its conjugate orbit too, giving 12 r
oots).

## Valid Polynomials

Degree 5 requires orbit sizes summing to 5. The only possibilities:

**Case 1:** Zero set $\{-1, \tfrac{1}{2}, 2\}$ with multiplicities $(a,b,c)$, $a
+b+c=5$, $a,b,c\geq 1$. All roots real → real coefficients automatic. There are
$\binom{4}{2}=6$ such polynomials.

**Case 2:** Zero set $\{-1, \tfrac{1}{2}, 2, e^{i\pi/3}, e^{-i\pi/3}\}$, all mul
tiplicity 1. The complex pair gives factor $x^2-x+1$. Exactly 1 polynomial. (The
 orbit $\{e^{\pm i\pi/3}\}$ alone requires equal multiplicities → $2m=5$, imposs
ible.)
