Looking at this problem, I need to find the largest rank $k$ of a free abelian s
ubgroup of $\mathcal{GL}(n, \mathbb{Z})$.

**Answer: $k = \lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor$.**

## Proof

### Lower Bound: Construction

**Case $n = 2m$ (even).** Consider matrices of the form $I_{2m} + N$ where
$$N = \begin{pmatrix} 0_m & B \\ 0_m & 0_m \end{pmatrix}, \quad B \in M_m(\mathb
b{Z}).$$
Since $N^2 = 0$, the group law is $(I+N_1)(I+N_2) = I + N_1 + N_2$, so this subg
roup is isomorphic to $(M_m(\mathbb{Z}), +) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{m^2} = \mathbb{Z}^
{n^2/4}$. Each such matrix has determinant 1 and integer entries, so lies in $\m
athcal{GL}(n,\mathbb{Z})$.

**Case $n = 2m+1$ (odd).** Take matrices $I + N$ with
$$N = \begin{pmatrix} 0_{m} & B \\ 0_{m+1} & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad B \in M_{m,\
, m+1}(\mathbb{Z}).$$
Again $N^2 = 0$, giving a subgroup isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}^{m(m+1)} = \mathbb{
Z}^{\lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor}$.

Thus $k \geq \lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor$.

### Upper Bound

Let $G \cong \mathbb{Z}^k$ be a free abelian subgroup of $\mathcal{GL}(n, \mathb
b{Z})$, and let $R = \mathbb{Q}[G] \subseteq M_n(\mathbb{Q})$ be the $\mathbb{Q}
$-algebra generated by $G$. Since $G$ is abelian, $R$ is a **commutative** subal
gebra of $M_n(\mathbb{Q})$.

**Step 1 (Dimension bound).** By Gerstenhaber's theorem (1961), the maximum dime
nsion of a commutative subalgebra of $M_n(F)$ over any field $F$ is $\lfloor n^2
/4 \rfloor + 1$. Viewing $R \subseteq M_n(\mathbb{Q}) \hookrightarrow M_n(\overl
ine{\mathbb{Q}})$, we get
$$\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} R \leq \lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor + 1.$$

**Step 2 (Unit rank bound).** As a commutative Artinian $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra, $R
$ decomposes as
$$R \cong \prod_{i=1}^{s} R_i, \quad R_i \text{ local with residue field } K_i \
text{ and nilpotent radical } N_i,$$
where $s \geq 1$, each $K_i$ is a number field of degree $d_i = _i:\mathbb{Q}]
$, and $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} R = \sum_i (d_i + \dim_{\mathbb{Q}} N_i)$.

The unit group $R^\times = \prod_i R_i^\times$, where $R_i^\times \cong K_i^\tim
es \times (1 + N_i)$ (semidirect, but commutative since $R_i$ is). The free abel
ian rank of the arithmetic subgroup $R^\times \cap M_n(\mathbb{Z})$ is:

- **Semisimple part:** By Dirichlet's unit theorem, the unit group of any order
in $K_i$ has free abelian rank $r_{1,i} + r_{2,i} - 1$, where $r_{1,i} + 2r_{2,i
} = d_i$.
- **Unipotent part:** Since $N_i$ is commutative and nilpotent, the exponential
map $\exp: (N_i, +) \to (1+N_i, \cdot)$ is a group isomorphism (the Baker–Campbe
ll–Hausdorff series terminates and all commutator terms vanish). The integer poi
nts $1 + (N_i \cap M_n(\mathbb{Z}))$ form a finitely generated abelian group who
se rank equals $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} N_i$ (the logarithm is a $\mathbb{Q}$-rational
 bijection mapping a full-rank lattice to a full-rank lattice).

Therefore:
$$k \leq \sum_{i=1}^{s}\bigl((r_{1,i} + r_{2,i} - 1) + \dim_{\mathbb{Q}} N_i\big
r).$$

Since $r_{1,i} + r_{2,i} \leq d_i$ (with equality iff $K_i$ is totally real), we
 obtain:
$$k \leq \sum_{i=1}^{s}\bigl((d_i - 1) + \dim_{\mathbb{Q}} N_i\bigr) = \sum_{i=1
}^{s}(d_i + \dim_{\mathbb{Q}} N_i) - s = \dim_{\mathbb{Q}} R - s.$$

Since $s \geq 1$:
$$k \leq \dim_{\mathbb{Q}} R - 1 \leq \lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor + 1 - 1 = \lfloor n^
2/4 \rfloor.$$

### Conclusion

Combining both bounds:
$$\boxed{k = \left\lfloor \frac{n^2}{4} \right\rfloor}.$$
