Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether convergence in probability
of the conditional expectation onto a finer σ-field implies convergence in proba
bility onto a coarser nested σ-field.

## Analysis

By the **tower property** (law of iterated expectations), since $\mathcal{F}_n \
subseteq \mathcal{F}_n'$:

$$\mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{F}_n] = \mathbb{E}\!\Big[\mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mat
hcal{F}_n'] \;\Big|\; \mathcal{F}_n\Big].$$

Setting $Y_n := \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{F}_n']$ and $Z_n := \mathbb{E}[X_n
\mid \mathcal{F}_n]$, we have $Z_n = \mathbb{E}[Y_n \mid \mathcal{F}_n]$. The qu
estion reduces to: **if $Y_n \to c$ in probability, does $\mathbb{E}[Y_n \mid \m
athcal{F}_n] \to c$ in probability?**

The conditional expectation operator is a contraction on $L^p$ for $p \geq 1$, b
ut it is **not** continuous with respect to convergence in probability (without
uniform integrability). This suggests the answer is **no**, and a counterexample
 exists.

## Counterexample

Work on the probability space $\big([0,1],\, \mathcal{B}([0,1]),\, \text{Lebesgu
e}\big)$.

Define:
- $A_n := [0,\, 1/n]$, so $P(A_n) = 1/n$.
- $X_n := n \cdot \mathbf{1}_{A_n}$.
- $\mathcal{F}_n := \{\emptyset,\, [0,1]\}$ (the trivial σ-field).
- $\mathcal{F}_n' := \sigma(A_n) = \{\emptyset,\, A_n,\, A_n^c,\, [0,1]\}$.

**Verification of the setup:**

1. **Integrability:** $\mathbb{E}[|X_n|] = n \cdot P(A_n) = n \cdot \tfrac{1}{n}
 = 1 < \infty$. ✓

2. **Nesting:** $\mathcal{F}_n = \{\emptyset, [0,1]\} \subseteq \mathcal{F}_n'$.
 ✓

3. **Measurability:** $X_n = n\cdot\mathbf{1}_{A_n}$ is $\mathcal{F}_n'$-measura
ble since $A_n \in \mathcal{F}_n'$.

**Conditional expectation onto $\mathcal{F}_n'$:**

Since $X_n$ is $\mathcal{F}_n'$-measurable:
$$\mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{F}_n'] = X_n = n\cdot\mathbf{1}_{A_n}.$$

For any $\varepsilon > 0$:
$$P\!\big(|\mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{F}_n']| > \varepsilon\big) = P(X_n > \va
repsilon) = P(A_n) = \frac{1}{n} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.$$

Hence $\mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{F}_n'] \to 0$ in probability, so $c = 0$.

**Conditional expectation onto $\mathcal{F}_n$:**

Since $\mathcal{F}_n$ is trivial:
$$\mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{F}_n] = \mathbb{E}[X_n] = n \cdot \frac{1}{n} = 1
 \quad \text{for all } n.$$

Therefore:
$$P\!\big(|\mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{F}_n] - 0| > \varepsilon\big) = P(1 > \v
arepsilon) = 1 \quad \text{for all } 0 < \varepsilon < 1,$$

which does **not** converge to $0$.

## Conclusion

$\mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{F}_n'] \to c = 0$ in probability, but $\mathbb{E}[
X_n \mid \mathcal{F}_n] \equiv 1 \not\to 0$ in probability.

**The answer is no.** Convergence in probability of $\mathbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathca
l{F}_n']$ to a constant $c$ does **not** imply convergence in probability of $\m
athbb{E}[X_n \mid \mathcal{F}_n]$ to $c$.

The essential reason is that the tower property rewrites the problem as $\mathbb
{E}[Y_n \mid \mathcal{F}_n] \to c$ where $Y_n \to c$ in probability, but conditi
onal expectation is only a contraction in $L^p$ (not in probability). The counte
rexample exploits a sequence $Y_n = n\mathbf{1}_{A_n}$ with $P(A_n)=1/n$ that co
nverges to $0$ in probability yet retains constant $L^1$-mass $1$; projecting on
to the trivial σ-field recovers this mass as the constant $1$, preventing conver
gence to $0$. Uniform integrability (or boundedness) of $\{Y_n\}$ would be neede
d to make the implication hold.
