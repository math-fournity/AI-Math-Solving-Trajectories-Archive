## Proof

**Claim.** The residue $\mathrm{Res}_s(\eta)$ is an element of the residue field
 $k(s)$, not (in general) of $k$.

---

**Setup and local computation.**

Let $\bar{C}$ be a smooth projective curve over $k$, $S = \bar{C}\setminus C$ a
reduced effective divisor defined over $k$, and $\eta$ a non-zero rational secti
on of $\Omega^1_{\bar{C}/k}(\log S)$ defined over $k$. Let $s\in S$ be a closed
point.

Since $S$ is reduced, $s$ is a simple pole of $\eta$. Let $\mathcal{O}_{\bar{C},
s}$ be the local ring at $s$, with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}_s$ and residue fi
eld $k(s)=\mathcal{O}_{\bar{C},s}/\mathfrak{m}_s$. Since $S$ is reduced and $s$
lies on $S$, a local equation $t$ for $S$ at $s$ is a uniformizer of $\mathcal{O
}_{\bar{C},s}$ (i.e., $t$ generates $\mathfrak{m}_s$).

A section of $\Omega^1(\log S)$ has at most a simple pole along $S$. Locally at
$s$, every such form can be written as

$$\eta = a\,\frac{dt}{t} + \omega_{\mathrm{hol}},$$

where $a\in\mathcal{O}_{\bar{C},s}$ and $\omega_{\mathrm{hol}}$ is holomorphic a
t $s$. The **residue** of $\eta$ at $s$ is defined as

$$\mathrm{Res}_s(\eta) \;=\; a \bmod \mathfrak{m}_s \;\in\; \mathcal{O}_{\bar{C}
,s}/\mathfrak{m}_s \;=\; k(s).$$

This is the standard residue map for logarithmic differentials, and it lands in
$k(s)$ by construction: the coefficient $a$ lives in the local ring, and reducin
g modulo the maximal ideal places the residue in the residue field of the point.

---

**The residue is in $k(s)$, not necessarily in $k$.**

The residue field $k(s)$ is a finite extension of $k$, equal to $k$ if and only
if $s$ is $k$-rational. In general, a closed point $s$ of $\bar{C}$ need not be
$k$-rational, so $k(s)$ can be a proper extension of $k$.

**Concrete example.** Let $k=\mathbb{R}$, $\bar{C}=\mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{R}}$, a
nd $S$ the reduced divisor cut out by $x^2+1=0$, i.e., $S$ is the single closed
point $s$ with residue field $k(s)=\mathbb{R}[x]/(x^2+1)\cong\mathbb{C}$. Take t
he logarithmic differential

$$\eta = \frac{dx}{x^2+1} \in \Omega^1(\log S).$$

A local equation for $S$ at $s$ is $t = x^2+1$, which is a uniformizer of $\math
cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1,s}$. Since $dt = 2x\,dx$, we have $dx = dt/(2x)$, hence

$$\eta = \frac{dx}{t} = \frac{1}{2x}\,\frac{dt}{t}.$$

The residue is $\frac{1}{2x}\bmod (x^2+1)$. In $k(s)\cong\mathbb{C}$, the class
of $x$ is $i$, so

$$\mathrm{Res}_s(\eta) = \frac{1}{2i} = -\frac{i}{2}\in\mathbb{C}=k(s),$$

which is **not** an element of $k=\mathbb{R}$. This confirms that the residue ne
ed not lie in $k$.

---

**Galois compatibility (why $k$ appears at all).**

Although each individual residue $\mathrm{Res}_s(\eta)$ lives in $k(s)$, the fac
t that $\eta$ is defined over $k$ imposes a Galois-equivariance: for every $\sig
ma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{k}/k)$,

$$\mathrm{Res}_{\sigma(s)}(\eta) = \sigma\!\bigl(\mathrm{Res}_s(\eta)\bigr).$$

Consequently, the **trace** $\mathrm{Tr}_{k(s)/k}\!\bigl(\mathrm{Res}_s(\eta)\bi
gr)$ lies in $k$, and the residue theorem for logarithmic differentials over $k$
 reads

$$\sum_{s\in S}\mathrm{Tr}_{k(s)/k}\!\bigl(\mathrm{Res}_s(\eta)\bigr)=0\in k.$$

But this is a statement about traces, not about the individual residues. Each $\
mathrm{Res}_s(\eta)$ itself is an element of $k(s)$.

---

**Conclusion.**

The residue $\mathrm{Res}_s(\eta)$ of a rational section $\eta$ of $\Omega^1(\lo
g S)$ at a point $s\in S$ is naturally an element of the **residue field $k(s)$*
*. It lies in $k$ only in the special case that $s$ is $k$-rational (i.e., $k(s)
=k$), or if the particular value happens to fall in $k$ for other reasons. In ge
neral, $\mathrm{Res}_s(\eta)\in k(s)$ and $\mathrm{Res}_s(\eta)\notin k$.
