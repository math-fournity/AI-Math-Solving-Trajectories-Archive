## Proof

We minimize $\int_0^1 [f''(x)]^2\,dx$ subject to $f(0)=f(1)=0$ and $f'(0)=a$, ov
er $f\in C^2([0,1])$.

**Reduction to a constrained quadratic minimization.** Set $g(x)=f''(x)$. Then
$$f'(x)=a+\int_0^x g(t)\,dt,\qquad f(x)=ax+\int_0^x (x-t)\,g(t)\,dt.$$
The boundary condition $f(1)=0$ becomes the single linear constraint
$$\int_0^1 (1-t)\,g(t)\,dt = -a. \tag{$\ast$}$$
Conversely, any continuous $g$ satisfying $(\ast)$ produces, via the formulas ab
ove, an $f\in C^2([0,1])$ with $f(0)=f(1)=0$, $f'(0)=a$, and $f''=g$. Thus the p
roblem is equivalent to:

> Minimize $\displaystyle\int_0^1 g(t)^2\,dt$ over continuous $g$ subject to $\d
isplaystyle\int_0^1 (1-t)\,g(t)\,dt=-a$.

**Solving the constrained problem.** By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,
$$\left|\int_0^1 (1-t)\,g(t)\,dt\right|^2 \le \int_0^1 (1-t)^2\,dt\;\cdot\;\int_
0^1 g(t)^2\,dt.$$
Since $\int_0^1 (1-t)^2\,dt = \tfrac13$, the constraint $(\ast)$ gives
$$a^2 \le \tfrac13 \int_0^1 g(t)^2\,dt \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \int_0^1 g(t)^2
\,dt \ge 3a^2.$$
Equality in Cauchy–Schwarz holds iff $g$ is a scalar multiple of $(1-t)$, i.e. $
g(t)=\lambda(1-t)$. Substituting into $(\ast)$:
$$\lambda\int_0^1 (1-t)^2\,dt = \frac{\lambda}{3} = -a \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \lambda = -3a.$$
Hence the unique minimizer is $f''(x)=-3a(1-x)$, and the minimum value is
$$\int_0^1 9a^2(1-x)^2\,dx = 9a^2\cdot\frac13 = 3a^2.$$

**Recovering $f$.** Integrating $f''(x)=-3a(1-x)=3a(x-1)$:
$$f'(x)=a+\int_0^x 3a(t-1)\,dt = a+3a\!\left(\tfrac{x^2}{2}-x\right)=a-\tfrac{3a
}{2}x^2\cdot\!\tfrac{2}{1}\,\text{...}$$
More cleanly, $f'(x)=a-3ax+\tfrac{3a}{2}x^2$, and integrating again from $0$:
$$f(x)=ax-\tfrac{3a}{2}x^2+\tfrac{a}{2}x^3=\frac{a}{2}\,x(x-1)(x-2).$$

**Verification.**
- $f(0)=0$ and $f(1)=\tfrac{a}{2}\cdot1\cdot0\cdot(-1)=0$. ✓
- $f'(x)=\tfrac{a}{2}(3x^2-6x+2)$, so $f'(0)=\tfrac{a}{2}\cdot2=a$. ✓
- $f''(x)=\tfrac{a}{2}(6x-6)=3a(x-1)=-3a(1-x)$. ✓
- $\int_0^1 [f'']^2 dx = \int_0^1 9a^2(1-x)^2\,dx = 9a^2\cdot\tfrac13 = 3a^2$. ✓

**Conclusion.** The minimum value of $\displaystyle\int_0^1 [f''(x)]^2\,dx$ is $
\boxed{3a^2}$, achieved uniquely by
$$\boxed{f(x)=\frac{a}{2}\,x(x-1)(x-2)}.$$
