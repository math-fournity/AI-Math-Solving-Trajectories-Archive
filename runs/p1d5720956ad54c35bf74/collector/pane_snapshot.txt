## Answer

**No.** A subalgebra of a finitely generated algebra over a Noetherian ring need
 not be finitely generated. Below is a counterexample.

---

### Counterexample

Let $A = k$ be a field (which is Noetherian). Let $C = k[x, y]$, a finitely gene
rated $k$-algebra. Define

$$B = k\big[x,\; xy,\; xy^{2},\; xy^{3},\; \ldots\big] \subseteq k[x,y] = C.$$

This is a $k$-subalgebra of $C$ (it is closed under addition and multiplication,
 and contains $k$ and the products of its generators, all of which lie in $k[x,y
]$).

### Claim: $B$ is not finitely generated as a $k$-algebra.

Suppose for contradiction that $B = k[f_1, f_2, \ldots, f_n]$ for some $f_i \in
B$.

**Step 1.** Each $f_i$ is a polynomial in finitely many of the generators $\{x,
xy, xy^2, \ldots\}$. Hence there exists an integer $N \geq 0$ such that every $f
_i$ lies in the subalgebra

$$B_N := k\big[x,\; xy,\; xy^{2},\; \ldots,\; xy^{N}\big].$$

Consequently $B = k[f_1, \ldots, f_n] \subseteq B_N$.

**Step 2.** We show $xy^{N+1} \notin B_N$, contradicting the fact that $xy^{N+1}
 \in B$.

A monomial of $B_N$ has the form

$$x^{a_0}(xy)^{a_1}(xy^2)^{a_2}\cdots(xy^N)^{a_N} = x^{a_0+a_1+\cdots+a_N}\, y^{
a_1 + 2a_2 + \cdots + Na_N},$$

where $a_0, \ldots, a_N \geq 0$. Thus a monomial $x^p y^q$ belongs to $B_N$ only
 if there exist non-negative integers $a_0, \ldots, a_N$ with

$$a_0 + a_1 + \cdots + a_N = p, \qquad a_1 + 2a_2 + \cdots + N a_N = q. \tag{$\a
st$}$$

For the monomial $xy^{N+1}$ we have $p = 1$ and $q = N+1$. The first equation in
 $(\ast)$ forces exactly one $a_j = 1$ and all others $= 0$. The second equation
 then gives $j = N+1$, which is impossible since $j \in \{0, 1, \ldots, N\}$.

Therefore $xy^{N+1} \notin B_N$, yet $xy^{N+1} \in B$. This contradicts $B \subs
eteq B_N$.

### Conclusion

The assumption that $B$ is finitely generated over $k$ leads to a contradiction.
 Hence $B$ is **not** finitely generated as a $k$-algebra, even though:

- $A = k$ is Noetherian,
- $C = k[x,y]$ is finitely generated over $A$,
- $B$ is a subalgebra of $C$.

Therefore the answer to the question is **no**: $B$ is not necessarily finitely
generated over $A$.
