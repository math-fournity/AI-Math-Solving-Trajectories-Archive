Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum $n$ such that every graph on
 $3m$ vertices containing $n$ vertices with degrees $1, 2, \dots, n$ must contai
n a triangle.

## Proof

**Answer: $n = 2m + 1$.**

### Part 1: $n = 2m$ does not suffice (triangle-free construction exists)

**Construction.** Build a bipartite graph with parts $A = \{a_1, \dots, a_m\}$ a
nd $B = \{b_1, \dots, b_{2m}\}$ (total $3m$ vertices). Define edges as follows:

- Each $b_j$ for $j = m+1, \dots, 2m$ connects to **all** vertices in $A$.
- For $j = 1, \dots, m$: $a_i$ connects to $b_j$ if and only if $i + j \geq m +
1$.

Since the graph is bipartite, it is triangle-free.

**Degree computation.**

- **Vertices $a_i$ ($i = 1, \dots, m$):** Connected to all $m$ vertices $b_{m+1}
, \dots, b_{2m}$, plus those $b_j$ ($j \leq m$) with $j \geq m+1-i$, giving $i$
additional edges. Total degree: $m + i$. So $a_i$ has degree $m + i$, yielding d
egrees $m+1, m+2, \dots, 2m$.

- **Vertices $b_j$ ($j = 1, \dots, m$):** Connected to those $a_i$ with $i \geq
m+1-j$, giving exactly $j$ neighbors. Degree: $j$, yielding degrees $1, 2, \dots
, m$.

- **Vertices $b_j$ ($j = m+1, \dots, 2m$):** Connected to all of $A$. Degree: $m
$.

The $2m$ vertices $\{a_1, \dots, a_m, b_1, \dots, b_m\}$ have degrees $\{m+1, \d
ots, 2m\} \cup \{1, \dots, m\} = \{1, 2, \dots, 2m\}$. This is a triangle-free g
raph that is Level-$2m$ optimized. $\square$

Since any Level-$2m$ optimized graph is also Level-$n$ optimized for all $n \leq
 2m$ (take the subset with degrees $1, \dots, n$), the property **fails for all
$n \leq 2m$**.

### Part 2: $n = 2m + 1$ suffices (no triangle-free graph can be Level-$(2m+1)$
optimized)

**Proof by contradiction.** Suppose $G$ is a triangle-free graph on $3m$ vertice
s that is Level-$(2m+1)$ optimized. Then there exist $2m+1$ vertices with degree
s $1, 2, \dots, 2m+1$.

Let $v$ be the vertex with degree $2m+1$. Since $G$ is triangle-free, the neighb
orhood $N(v)$ is an **independent set**. Set:
- $S = N(v)$, so $|S| = 2m + 1$ and $S$ is independent.
- $T = V(G) \setminus (\{v\} \cup S)$, so $|T| = 3m - 1 - (2m+1) = m - 2$.

**Key observations:**

1. Every vertex in $S$ has degree between $1$ (the edge to $v$) and $1 + |T| = m
 - 1$ (since $S$ is independent, the only additional edges from a vertex in $S$
go to $T$).

2. Among our $2m+1$ special vertices, one is $v$ (degree $2m+1$). The remaining
$2m$ special vertices lie in $S \cup T$ and must have degrees $1, 2, \dots, 2m$.

3. The degrees $m, m+1, \dots, 2m$ (there are $m + 1$ such values) cannot be rea
lized by any vertex in $S$ (max degree in $S$ is $m - 1$). Therefore, all $m + 1
$ of these distinct degree values must be realized by vertices in $T$.

4. But $|T| = m - 2 < m + 1$ for all $m \geq 2$. A set of $m - 2$ vertices canno
t realize $m + 1$ distinct degree values.

This is a contradiction. Therefore, no triangle-free graph on $3m$ vertices can
be Level-$(2m+1)$ optimized. $\square$

### Conclusion

- For $n \leq 2m$: a triangle-free Level-$n$ optimized graph exists (property fa
ils).
- For $n = 2m + 1$: no triangle-free Level-$(2m+1)$ optimized graph exists (prop
erty holds).

The minimum value of $n$ is $\boxed{2m+1}$.
