In fact, properness of $f$ is a formal consequence of $X$ being projective, and
does not depend on the specific contraction conditions (the extremal face

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Key fact. If $g \circ f$ is proper and $g$ is separated, then $f$ is proper.

This is a standard theorem in scheme theory (see, e.g., Hartshorne, Algebraic
Geometry, Corollary II.4.8(e), or Stacks Project, Tag 01W6). It follows from the
characterization of proper morphisms as those that are separated, of finite
type, and universally closed, together with the stability of these properties
under base change and composition.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Application. Since $X$ is a projective variety, $X$ is proper over $
\operatorname{Spec} k$. Let us write the structure morphism of $X$ as $$\pi_X :
X \longrightarrow \operatorname{Spec} k,$$ which is proper.

The morphism $f : X \to Y$ is a morphism of $k$-schemes (in the context of the
cone theorem and extremal contractions, $Y$ is a projective variety, hence in
particular a separated $k$-scheme of finite type). Let $g : Y \to
\operatorname{Spec} k$ denote the structure morphism of $Y$. Then we have a
factorization: $$\pi_X = g \circ f : X \xrightarrow{;f;} Y \xrightarrow{;g;}
\operatorname{Spec} k.$$

Now:

  • $\pi_X = g \circ f$ is proper (since $X$ is projective, hence proper over
  $k$).
  • $g : Y \to \operatorname{Spec} k$ is separated (since $Y$ is a variety,
  hence separated over $k$).

By the key fact above, we conclude: $$f : X \longrightarrow Y \quad \text{is
proper.} \qquad \blacksquare$$

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Remark. The two defining conditions of the contraction — (1) that $f$ contracts
exactly the curves whose classes lie in the extremal face $F$, and (2) that
$f_*(\mathcal{O}_X) = \mathcal{O}_Y$ (i.e., $f$ has connected fibers, by Stein
factorization) — are not needed for the properness conclusion. Properness holds
for any morphism from a projective (or more generally, proper) $k$-scheme to a
separated $k$-scheme. The contraction conditions serve to uniquely characterize
$f$ as the morphism associated to the extremal face $F$, but the properness is
automatic from the projectivity of $X$.
