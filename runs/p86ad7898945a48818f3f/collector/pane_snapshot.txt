## Proof

We evaluate

$$I \;=\; \int_{0}^{2\pi} \ln\!\bigl(2R\sin(\theta/2)\bigr)\,d\theta, \qquad R>0
.$$

**Step 1 — Reduction to a contour integral.**

For $\theta\in(0,2\pi)$ we have $\sin(\theta/2)>0$ and the identity

$$\bigl|1-e^{i\theta}\bigr| \;=\; \sqrt{(1-\cos\theta)^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta} \;=\;
 \sqrt{2-2\cos\theta} \;=\; 2\sin(\theta/2).$$

Hence

$$\ln\!\bigl(2R\sin(\theta/2)\bigr) \;=\; \ln R \;+\; \ln\bigl|1-e^{i\theta}\big
r|,$$

so that

$$I \;=\; 2\pi\ln R \;+\; \int_{0}^{2\pi}\ln\bigl|1-e^{i\theta}\bigr|\,d\theta.
\tag{1}$$

It remains to show the last integral vanishes.

**Step 2 — A logarithm on the unit circle.**

Define $\ln(1-z)$ with the branch cut along $[1,+\infty)$, so that $\ln(1-z)$ is
 analytic on $\mathbb{C}\setminus[1,+\infty)$. For $\theta\in(0,2\pi)$,

$$1-e^{i\theta} \;=\; -2i\,e^{i\theta/2}\sin(\theta/2) \;=\; 2\sin(\theta/2)\,e^
{i(\theta/2-\pi/2)},$$

with argument $\theta/2-\pi/2\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$, which lies in the chosen branch
. Therefore

$$\ln\!\bigl(1-e^{i\theta}\bigr) \;=\; \ln\!\bigl(2\sin(\theta/2)\bigr) \;+\; i\
Bigl(\frac{\theta}{2}-\frac{\pi}{2}\Bigr), \qquad \theta\in(0,2\pi). \tag{2}$$

In particular $\operatorname{Re}\ln(1-e^{i\theta})=\ln|1-e^{i\theta}|=\ln(2\sin(
\theta/2))$.

**Step 3 — Contour integration.**

Consider

$$J \;=\; \oint_{C}\frac{\ln(1-z)}{z}\,dz,$$

where $C$ is the unit circle traversed counterclockwise but **indented inward**
by a semicircle of radius $\varepsilon$ around the branch point $z=1$. The integ
rand $g(z)=\ln(1-z)/z$ is analytic on and inside $C$ (the branch cut $[1,\infty)
$ leaves the disk at $z=1$, and $g$ is regular at $z=0$ since $\ln(1-z)/z\to -1$
). By Cauchy's theorem,

$$J=0. \tag{3}$$

Parametrize the circular part by $z=e^{i\theta}$, $\theta\in[\varepsilon,\,2\pi-
\varepsilon]$:

$$\frac{\ln(1-z)}{z}\,dz \;=\; \frac{\ln(1-e^{i\theta})}{e^{i\theta}}\,i e^{i\th
eta}\,d\theta \;=\; i\,\ln\!\bigl(1-e^{i\theta}\bigr)\,d\theta.$$

On the indentation, write $z=1-\varepsilon e^{i\phi}$ with $\phi:\pi/2\to -\pi/2
$ (clockwise, through the interior). Then $1-z=\varepsilon e^{i\phi}$, $\ln(1-z)
=\ln\varepsilon+i\phi$, $dz=-i\varepsilon e^{i\phi}d\phi$, and

$$\Bigl|\int_{\text{indent}}\frac{\ln(1-z)}{z}\,dz\Bigr| \;\le\; \int_{-\pi/2}^{
\pi/2}\frac{|\ln\varepsilon+i\phi|}{|1-\varepsilon e^{i\phi}|}\,\varepsilon\,d\p
hi \;\le\; \frac{C\,|\ln\varepsilon|\,\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\;\xrightarrow[
\varepsilon\to0]{}\;0.$$

Letting $\varepsilon\to0$ in (3) and using (2):

$$0 \;=\; i\int_{0}^{2\pi}\ln\!\bigl(1-e^{i\theta}\bigr)\,d\theta \;=\; i\int_{0
}^{2\pi}\ln\!\bigl(2\sin(\theta/2)\bigr)\,d\theta \;-\;\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Bigl(\fra
c{\theta}{2}-\frac{\pi}{2}\Bigr)\,d\theta.$$

The second integral vanishes:

$$\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Bigl(\frac{\theta}{2}-\frac{\pi}{2}\Bigr)\,d\theta \;=\; \Bigl
[\frac{\theta^{2}}{4}-\frac{\pi\theta}{2}\Bigr]_{0}^{2\pi} \;=\; \pi^{2}-\pi^{2}
 \;=\; 0.$$

Hence

$$\int_{0}^{2\pi}\ln\!\bigl(2\sin(\theta/2)\bigr)\,d\theta \;=\; 0 \;=\; \int_{0
}^{2\pi}\ln\bigl|1-e^{i\theta}\bigr|\,d\theta. \tag{4}$$

(The integral converges since $\ln(2\sin(\theta/2))\sim\ln\theta$ is integrable
near $0$ and $2\pi$.)

**Step 4 — Conclusion.**

Substituting (4) into (1):

$$I \;=\; 2\pi\ln R \;+\; 0 \;=\; 2\pi\ln R.$$

$$\boxed{\,\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\ln\!\bigl(2R\sin(\theta/2)\bigr)\,d\thet
a \;=\; 2\pi\ln R\,}$$
