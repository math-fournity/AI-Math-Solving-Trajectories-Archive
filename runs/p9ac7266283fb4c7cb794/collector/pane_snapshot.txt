# Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $P$ is an integral polyhedron.

## Step 1: $P$ is the projection of $Q$ onto the $x$-space

For any $x \in \mathbb{R}^m$, there exists $y$ with $(x,y) \in Q$ if and only if
 $Ax = b$, $x \geq 0$, and $y = d - Bx \geq 0$, i.e., $Bx \leq d$. This is exact
ly the condition $x \in P$. Hence $P = \operatorname{proj}_x(Q)$.

## Step 2: Every extreme point of $P$ is the unique optimizer of some linear obj
ective

Let $\bar{x}$ be an extreme point of $P$. Being an extreme point, $\bar{x}$ is a
 zero-dimensional face of $P$, so there exists $c \in \mathbb{R}^m$ such that $\
bar{x}$ is the **unique** maximizer of $c^\top x$ over $P$:
$$c^\top \bar{x} > c^\top x \quad \text{for all } x \in P,\; x \neq \bar{x}.$$

## Step 3: The same objective over $Q$ achieves the same optimal value

Since $P = \operatorname{proj}_x(Q)$:
$$\max\{c^\top x : (x,y) \in Q\} = \max\{c^\top x : x \in P\} = c^\top \bar{x}.$
$

This maximum is attained over $Q$ (at any point $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$ with $\bar{
y} = d - B\bar{x} \geq 0$).

## Step 4: An extreme point of $Q$ attains the optimum

Since $Q$ is an integral polyhedron, it possesses integral extreme points, hence
 $Q$ is pointed (contains no line). The set of optimal solutions to $\max\{c^\to
p x : (x,y) \in Q\}$ is a non-empty face of the pointed polyhedron $Q$, and ther
efore contains an extreme point $(\hat{x}, \hat{y})$ of $Q$.

## Step 5: The projected extreme point coincides with $\bar{x}$

Since $(\hat{x}, \hat{y})$ is optimal, $c^\top \hat{x} = c^\top \bar{x}$, and $\
hat{x} \in P$. By the **uniqueness** of the maximizer over $P$ (Step 2), we conc
lude:
$$\hat{x} = \bar{x}.$$

## Step 6: Integrality transfers

Since $Q$ is integral, the extreme point $(\hat{x}, \hat{y})$ is integral, so $\
hat{x} = \bar{x}$ is integral.

Since $\bar{x}$ was an arbitrary extreme point of $P$, every extreme point of $P
$ is integral. Therefore $P$ is an integral polyhedron. $\blacksquare$
