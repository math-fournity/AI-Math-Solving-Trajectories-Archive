## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, such $c$ exists. In particular, $c = k!\,(s-1)^k + 1$ suffices.

This is the **Erdős–Rado Sunflower Lemma**. A *sunflower with $s$ petals* is a c
ollection of $s$ sets $A_1,\dots,A_s$ such that $A_i \cap A_j = A_p \cap A_q$ fo
r all $i\neq j,\; p\neq q$ (the common intersection is the *kernel*). We prove b
y induction on $k$:

> **($\star$)** Any family $\mathbb{F}$ of $k$-element sets with $|\mathbb{F}| >
 k!\,(s-1)^k$ contains a sunflower with $s$ petals.

**Base case $k=1$.** Each member of $\mathbb{F}$ is a singleton. Since $|\mathbb
{F}| > (s-1)$, we have $|\mathbb{F|\geq s}$, so there are at least $s$ distinct
singletons. Any $s$ of them have pairwise intersection $\emptyset$, forming a su
nflower with empty kernel. ✓

**Inductive step.** Assume ($\star$) holds for $k-1$ (with $k\geq 2$). Let $\mat
hbb{F}$ be a family of $k$-element sets with $|\mathbb{F}| > k!\,(s-1)^k$.

Let $\mathbb{G}\subseteq\mathbb{F}$ be a **maximal** subfamily of pairwise disjo
int sets, and set $Y = \bigcup_{G\in\mathbb{G}} G$.

**Case 1: $|\mathbb{G}| \geq s$.** Any $s$ sets from $\mathbb{G}$ are pairwise d
isjoint, so all pairwise intersections equal $\emptyset$. Done.

**Case 2: $|\mathbb{G}| \leq s-1$.** By maximality, every $A\in\mathbb{F}$ inter
sects some member of $\mathbb{G}$ (otherwise $\mathbb{G}\cup\{A\}$ would be a la
rger pairwise disjoint family). Hence $A\cap Y\neq\emptyset$ for every $A\in\mat
hbb{F}$.

Since $|Y|\leq k\,|\mathbb{G}|\leq k(s-1)$, by the pigeonhole principle there ex
ists $x\in Y$ belonging to at least
$$\frac{|\mathbb{F}|}{|Y|} > \frac{k!\,(s-1)^k}{k(s-1)} = (k-1)!\,(s-1)^{k-1}$$
members of $\mathbb{F}$.

Define $\mathbb{F}_x = \{A\in\mathbb{F}: x\in A\}$, so $|\mathbb{F}_x| > (k-1)!\
,(s-1)^{k-1}$. Consider the family
$$\mathbb{F}_x' = \{A\setminus\{x\} : A\in\mathbb{F}_x\},$$
which consists of $(k-1)$-element sets. By the inductive hypothesis, $\mathbb{F}
_x'$ contains a sunflower $B_1,\dots,B_s$ with common kernel $K$:
$$B_i\cap B_j = K \quad\text{for all } i\neq j.$$

Set $A_i = B_i\cup\{x\}\in\mathbb{F}$ for $i=1,\dots,s$. Then for $i\neq j$:
$$A_i\cap A_j = (B_i\cup\{x\})\cap(B_j\cup\{x\}) = (B_i\cap B_j)\cup\{x\} = K\cu
p\{x\}.$$

This is independent of $i,j$, so $A_1,\dots,A_s$ form a sunflower with kernel $K
\cup\{x\}$. ✓

This completes the induction. Taking $c = k!\,(s-1)^k + 1$, whenever $|\mathbb{F
}|\geq c$ (i.e., $|\mathbb{F}|>k!\,(s-1)^k$), the family $\mathbb{F}$ contains $
s$ sets $A_1,\dots,A_s$ with $A_i\cap A_j = A_p\cap A_q$ for all $i\neq j$, $p\n
eq q$. $\blacksquare$
