## Proof that $\kappa\mathbb{N}$ is NOT zero-dimensional

### Setup: The topology of $\kappa\mathbb{N}$

The underlying set is $X = \mathbb{N} \cup \mathcal{F}$, where $\mathcal{F}$ den
otes the set of all free ultrafilters on $\mathbb{N}$. The Katetov extension top
ology is generated by:

- **Isolated points:** $\{n\}$ for each $n \in \mathbb{N}$.
- **Ultrafilter neighborhoods:** $\{p\} \cup A$ for each free ultrafilter $p$ an
d each $A \in p$.

A set $U \subseteq X$ is open if and only if for every free ultrafilter $p \in U
$, we have $U \cap \mathbb{N} \in p$.

**Remark (contrast with $\beta\mathbb{N}$):** This topology is *strictly finer*
than the Stone topology on $\beta\mathbb{N}$. In $\beta\mathbb{N}$, the basic cl
open set determined by $A \in p$ is $\widehat{A} = A \cup \{q \in \mathcal{F} :
A \in q\}$, which contains *all* free ultrafilters extending $A$. In $\kappa\mat
hbb{N}$, the basic open set $\{p\}\cup A$ contains only the single ultrafilter $
p$. The set $\{p\}\cup A$ is open in $\kappa\mathbb{N}$ but **not** open in $\be
ta\mathbb{N}$ (since in $\beta\mathbb{N}$, openness at $p$ would require some $\
widehat{B}\subseteq \{p\}\cup A$, forcing $\{q: B\in q\}\subseteq\{p\}$, which i
s impossible for infinite $B$).

### Step 1: Characterize all clopen subsets of $\kappa\mathbb{N}$

**Claim.** The clopen subsets of $\kappa\mathbb{N}$ are exactly the sets
$$\widehat{S} \;=\; S \cup \{q \in \mathcal{F} : S \in q\}, \qquad S \subseteq \
mathbb{N}.$$

*Proof of claim.* Let $C$ be clopen and set $S = C \cap \mathbb{N}$.

- **$C$ open:** For every $p \in C \cap \mathcal{F}$, there exists $A_p \in p$ w
ith $\{p\}\cup A_p \subseteq C$, so $A_p \subseteq S$, hence $S \in p$. Thus $C
\cap \mathcal{F} \subseteq \{q : S \in q\}$.

- **$C$ closed (complement open):** For every $q \in \mathcal{F} \setminus C$, t
he complement $X\setminus C$ is open at $q$, so $(X\setminus C)\cap\mathbb{N} =
\mathbb{N}\setminus S \in q$, i.e., $S \notin q$. Thus $\{q : S \in q\} \subsete
q C$.

Combining: $C \cap \mathcal{F} = \{q : S \in q\}$, so $C = \widehat{S}$.

Conversely, $\widehat{S}$ is open (at each $p$ with $S \in p$, take $A = S$) and
 its complement is $\widehat{\mathbb{N}\setminus S}$, which is also open. So $\w
idehat{S}$ is clopen. $\square$

### Step 2: No clopen neighborhood fits inside $\{p\}\cup A$

Fix a free ultrafilter $p$ and $A \in p$. Since $p$ is free, $A$ is infinite.

Suppose $\widehat{S}$ is a clopen neighborhood of $p$ with $\widehat{S} \subsete
q \{p\}\cup A$. Then:

1. $p \in \widehat{S}$, so $S \in p$, hence $S$ is **infinite** (free ultrafilte
rs contain no finite sets).
2. $S \subseteq A$ (from $\widehat{S}\cap\mathbb{N} \subseteq A$).
3. $\{q \in \mathcal{F} : S \in q\} \subseteq \{p\}$ (from $\widehat{S}\cap\math
cal{F} \subseteq \{p\}$).

Condition (3) says $S$ belongs to **exactly one** free ultrafilter, namely $p$.
But this is impossible:

> **Lemma.** Every infinite subset $S\subseteq\mathbb{N}$ belongs to $2^{2^{\ale
ph_0}}$ free ultrafilters.

*Proof.* Since $S$ is infinite, it admits a free ultrafilter (extend the Fréchet
 filter on $S$). In fact, the free ultrafilters on $S$ are in bijection with the
 free ultrafilters on $\mathbb{N}$ that contain $S$, and there are $2^{2^{\aleph
_0}}$ of these. In particular, there are at least two distinct free ultrafilters
 containing $S$. $\square$

By the lemma, $\{q \in \mathcal{F} : S \in q\}$ has more than one element, contr
adicting condition (3).

The set $\{p\}\cup A$ (for any $A\in p$) is an open neighborhood of the free ult

Since free ultrafilter points fail to have clopen neighborhood bases, $\kappa\ma
thbb{N}$ is **not zero-dimensional**.

**Remark.** The isolated points $n\in\mathbb{N}$ trivially have clopen neighborh
