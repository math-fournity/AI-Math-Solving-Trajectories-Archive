## Proof

By Gelfand duality, every unital commutative C*-algebra $A$ is isometrically *-i
somorphic to $C(X)$, the algebra of continuous complex-valued functions on a com
pact Hausdorff space $X$.

**Projections in $C(X)$.** A projection $p \in C(X)$ satisfies $p = p^* = p^2$,
so $p$ takes values in $\{0, 1\}$. Since $p$ is continuous, $p^{-1}(\{1\})$ is b
oth closed and open in $X$ — a **clopen** subset. Conversely, the characteristic
 function $\chi_U$ of any clopen set $U \subseteq X$ is continuous and is a proj
ection. Thus projections in $C(X)$ are in bijection with clopen subsets of $X$.

**The clopen sets form a Boolean algebra.** The collection $\mathcal{C}(X)$ of c
lopen subsets of $X$ is closed under complement, finite union, and finite inters
ection, with $\emptyset$ and $X$ as the bottom and top. Hence $\mathcal{C}(X)$ i
s a Boolean algebra, and the lattice of projections of $C(X)$ is isomorphic (as
a Boolean algebra) to $\mathcal{C}(X)$.

**Counting.** The trivial projections $0$ and $1$ correspond to $\emptyset$ and
$X$. If $C(X)$ had exactly five non-trivial projections, then $\mathcal{C}(X)$ w
ould have exactly $5 + 2 = 7$ elements total, i.e. $\mathcal{C}(X)$ would be a *
*finite Boolean algebra with 7 elements**.

**Finite Boolean algebras have cardinality $2^n$.** Every finite Boolean algebra
 $B$ is isomorphic to the power set $\mathcal{P}(S)$ of its set $S$ of atoms (th
e minimal non-zero elements). If $|S| = n$, then $|B| = 2^n$. In particular, the
 cardinality of any finite Boolean algebra is a power of $2$.

**Contradiction.** The number $7$ is not a power of $2$. Therefore no finite Boo
lean algebra can have exactly $7$ elements, so no compact Hausdorff space $X$ ca
n have exactly $7$ clopen subsets, and consequently no unital commutative C*-alg
ebra can have exactly five non-trivial projections.

More generally, the number of non-trivial projections in a unital commutative C*
-algebra, when finite, must be $2^n - 2$ for some $n \geq 0$. The sequence of at
tainable values is $0, 2, 6, 14, 30, \dots$; the value $5$ does not appear.

### Conclusion

A unital commutative C*-algebra **cannot** be constructed with exactly five non-
trivial projections.
