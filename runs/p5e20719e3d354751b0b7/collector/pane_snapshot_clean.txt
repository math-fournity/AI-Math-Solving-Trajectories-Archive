We determine the maximum length of a sequence $f_1, f_2, f_3, \ldots$ of binary
operations on $\mathbb{R}$, where each $f_i \in \{+, \times\}$, each $f_i$ is as
sociative, and $f_i$ distributes over $f_{i+1}$ (denoted $f_i \leadsto f_{i+1}$)
 for every consecutive pair.

### Step 1: Associativity is automatic

Both addition and multiplication on $\mathbb{R}$ are associative. Hence the asso
ciativity condition imposes no restriction on the sequence — any sequence of $+$
 and $\times$ satisfies it.

### Step 2: Check all four distributivity transitions

Distributivity $f_i \leadsto f_{i+1}$ means:
$$a \; f_i \; (b \; f_{i+1} \; c) \;=\; (a \; f_i \; b) \; f_{i+1} \; (a \; f_i
\; c) \quad \text{for all } a,b,c \in \mathbb{R}.$$

We check all four cases:

**(a) $+ \leadsto +$:** $a + (b+c) = (a+b) + (a+c)$, i.e., $a+b+c = 2a+b+c$. Fai
ls (take $a=1$). ✗

**(b) $\times \leadsto +$:** $a(b+c) = ab + ac$. This is the standard distributi
ve law. ✓

**(c) $+ \leadsto \times$:** $a + (bc) = (a+b)(a+c) = a^2 + a(b+c) + bc$. Requir
es $a = a^2 + a(b+c)$ for all $a,b,c$, i.e., $a(1 - a - b - c) = 0$ for all $a,b
,c$. Fails (take $a=1, b=0, c=0$). ✗

**(d) $\times \leadsto \times$:** $a(bc) = (ab)(ac)$, i.e., $abc = a^2bc$. Requi
res $a = a^2$ for all $a$, i.e., $a \in \{0,1\}$ for all $a$. Fails (take $a=2$)
. ✗

### Step 3: The only valid transition is $\times \leadsto +$

From Step 2, the unique valid consecutive pair is $(\times, +)$.

### Step 4: Bound the sequence length

- **Length 1:** Any single operation works (no distributivity condition to check
). Both $(+)$ and $(\times)$ are valid.

- **Length 2:** The pair $(\times, +)$ satisfies $\times \leadsto +$. So a seque
nce of length 2 exists. No other pair works.

- **Length 3:** We would need $f_1 \leadsto f_2 \leadsto f_3$. By Step 3, the on
ly valid transition forces $f_1 = \times$ and $f_2 = +$. But then we need $+ \le
adsto f_3$, and by Step 2, $+$ distributes over neither $+$ nor $\times$. **No v
alid $f_3$ exists.**

- **Length $\geq 3$:** Impossible, since any such sequence would contain a lengt
h-3 subsequence at its start, which we just showed cannot exist.

### Conclusion

The longest sequence satisfying all conditions has length **2** (namely $f_1 = \
times,\; f_2 = +$). Since $2$ is finite, the longest such sequence is **finite**
.
