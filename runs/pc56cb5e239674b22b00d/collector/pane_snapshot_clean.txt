# Morse Lemma — Normal Form Near a Nondegenerate Critical Point

## Statement

Let $\phi: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ be a smooth ($C^\infty$) function, and l
et $x_0 \in \mathbb{R}^n$ be a **nondegenerate critical point**, i.e.

$$\nabla \phi(x_0) = 0, \qquad \det\!\big(H_\phi(x_0)\big) \neq 0,$$

where $H_\phi(x_0) = \left(\dfrac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial x_i \partial x_j}(x_
0)\right)$ is the Hessian matrix of $\phi$ at $x_0$.

## The Normal Form

The **Morse Lemma** asserts that there exists a neighborhood $U$ of $x_0$ and a
smooth diffeomorphism $\psi: U \to V \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ (with $\psi(x_0) =
0$) such that, writing $y = \psi(x)$, the function $\phi$ takes the **quadratic
normal form**

$$\boxed{\;\phi(x) \;=\; \phi(x_0) \;-\; y_1^2 - \cdots - y_\mu^2 \;+\; y_{\mu+1
}^2 + \cdots + y_n^2\;}$$

where:
- $y = (y_1, \dots, y_n)$ are the local coordinates induced by $\psi$,
- $\mu \in \{0, 1, \dots, n\}$ is the **Morse index** of $x_0$ — the number of n
egative eigenvalues of the Hessian $H_\phi(x_0)$ (equivalently, the maximal dime
nsion of a subspace on which $H_\phi(x_0)$ is negative definite).

Equivalently, in matrix notation:

$$\phi(x) = \phi(x_0) + y^\top \, D \, y, \qquad D = \operatorname{diag}(\underb
race{-1,\dots,-1}_{\mu},\underbrace{+1,\dots,+1}_{n-\mu}).$$

## Proof Sketch

**Step 1 — Reduction to $x_0 = 0$ and $\phi(x_0) = 0$.**
By translating coordinates ($x \mapsto x - x_0$) and subtracting the constant $\
phi(x_0)$, we may assume without loss of generality that $x_0 = 0$ and $\phi(0)
= 0$.

**Step 2 — Use the critical point condition.**
Since $\nabla \phi(0) = 0$, we apply the **Hadamard lemma**: there exist smooth
functions $g_{ij}(x)$ with $g_{ij}(0) = \dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\partial^2 \phi}{\par
tial x_i \partial x_j}(0)$ such that

$$\phi(x) = \sum_{i,j=1}^{n} g_{ij}(x)\, x_i x_j.$$

This is obtained by integrating twice: $\phi(x) = \int_0^1 (1-t)\, x^\top H_\phi
(tx)\, x \, dt$, and setting $g_{ij}(x) = \int_0^1 (1-t)\,\dfrac{\partial^2 \phi
}{\partial x_i \partial x_j}(tx)\,dt$.

**Step 3 — Diagonalize via completing the square (Morse's inductive argument).**

Because $H_\phi(0)$ is nondegenerate, at least one $g_{ij}(0) \neq 0$. By a line
ar change of coordinates we may assume $g_{11}(0) \neq 0$. Completing the square
 in $x_1$:

$$\phi(x) = \pm\, \big(y_1\big)^2 + \sum_{i,j \geq 2} h_{ij}(x)\, x_i x_j,$$

where $y_1 = |g_{11}|^{1/2}\!\left(x_1 + \sum_{j\geq 2} \frac{g_{1j}}{g_{11}} x_
j\right)$ is a valid new coordinate (since $\frac{\partial y_1}{\partial x_1}\Bi
g|_0 \neq 0$, the map $x \mapsto y$ is a local diffeomorphism by the inverse fun
ction theorem).

The remaining $(n-1)\times(n-1)$ block has Hessian at the origin equal to the Sc
hur complement of $g_{11}(0)$ in $H_\phi(0)$, which is again **nondegenerate**.
Repeating inductively $n$ times yields

$$\phi(x) = \pm y_1^2 \pm y_2^2 \cdots \pm y_n^2,$$

where the number of minus signs is exactly $\mu$, the number of negative eigenva
lues of $H_\phi(0)$.

**Step 4 — Sign pattern is invariant (Sylvester's law of inertia).**
The number $\mu$ of negative squares is an invariant of the quadratic form defin
ed by $H_\phi(0)$ under smooth changes of coordinates (since the Jacobian of a d
iffeomorphism at the origin is a nonsingular linear map, and Sylvester's law of
inertia guarantees the signature is preserved). Hence $\mu$ equals the Morse ind
ex.

## Key Consequences

1. **Nondegenerate critical points are isolated** — the quadratic normal form ha
s an isolated critical point at $y=0$, and diffeomorphisms preserve isolation.
2. **The local topology of sublevel sets $\{\phi \leq \phi(x_0) \pm \varepsilon\
}$ changes only by attaching a $\mu$-handle** — this is the foundation of **Mors
e theory**, relating the critical point structure of $\phi$ to the topology of $
\mathbb{R}^n$ (or more generally, of a manifold).
3. **The Morse index $\mu$ is a complete local invariant** of a nondegenerate cr
itical point up to smooth equivalence.

## Summary

The Morse Lemma provides the local normal form

$$\phi(x) = \phi(x_0) - y_1^2 - \cdots - y_\mu^2 + y_{\mu+1}^2 + \cdots + y_n^2,
$$

via a smooth local change of coordinates $y = \psi(x)$ with $\psi(x_0)=0$, where
 $\mu$ is the Morse index (the number of negative eigenvalues of the Hessian at
$x_0$). The proof proceeds by translating to the origin, applying the Hadamard l
emma to extract the quadratic factor, and inductively completing the square — th
e nondegeneracy of the Hessian guarantees each inductive step produces a valid l
ocal diffeomorphism via the inverse function theorem.
