## Proof

**Setup and notation.** Let $f\colon X\to Y$ be a morphism of $k$-schemes. We ha
ve field extensions $k\subseteq K\subseteq L\subseteq \bar K$ with $K\subseteq L
$ finite Galois over $k$ (in particular $L\subseteq \bar K$ after choosing an em
bedding). A point $x\in X(L)$ has image $y:=f(x)\in Y(K)$. Denote by $\bar x\in
X(\bar K)$ and $\bar y\in Y(\bar K)$ the $\bar K$-points obtained from $x$ and $
y$ via the chosen embeddings $L\hookrightarrow\bar K$, $K\hookrightarrow\bar K$.
 The absolute Galois group $G=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar K/K)$ acts on $X(\bar K)$
and $Y(\bar K)$ on the right by precomposition: for $\sigma\in G$, $\bar x^\sigm
a := \bar x\circ\operatorname{Spec}(\sigma)$ (this is the $\sigma^\star\circ\bar
 x$ of the problem).

**Key fact (Galois equivariance).** Since $f$ is a morphism of $k$-schemes (henc
e of $K$-schemes, as $k\subseteq K$), it is defined over $K$ and therefore commu
tes with the $G$-action:
$$f(\bar x^\sigma)=f(\bar x)^\sigma=\bar y^\sigma\qquad\text{for all }\sigma\in
G.$$
Indeed, $f\circ\bar x = \bar y$ as morphisms $\operatorname{Spec}\bar K\to Y$, a
nd precomposing both sides with $\operatorname{Spec}(\sigma)$ gives $f\circ\bar
x^\sigma = \bar y^\sigma$.

**The point $y$ is $G$-fixed.** Since $y\in Y(K)$, the morphism $\bar y\colon\op
eratorname{Spec}\bar K\to Y$ factors through $\operatorname{Spec}K\xrightarrow{y
}Y$:
$$\bar y = y\circ(\operatorname{Spec}\bar K\to\operatorname{Spec}K).$$
For $\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\bar K/K)$, the automorphism $\operatorname{Spe
c}(\sigma)$ fixes $\operatorname{Spec}K$ (as $\sigma|_K=\mathrm{id}$), so the co
mposition $\operatorname{Spec}\bar K\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Spec}(\sigma)}\op
eratorname{Spec}\bar K\to\operatorname{Spec}K$ equals the structural map $\opera
torname{Spec}\bar K\to\operatorname{Spec}K$. Hence
$$\bar y^\sigma = y\circ(\operatorname{Spec}\bar K\to\operatorname{Spec}K)\circ\
operatorname{Spec}(\sigma)=y\circ(\operatorname{Spec}\bar K\to\operatorname{Spec
}K)=\bar y.$$
So $\bar y$ is $G$-invariant.

**Conclusion.** Combining the two steps,
$$f(\bar x^\sigma)=\bar y^\sigma=\bar y\qquad\text{for every }\sigma\in G.$$
Under the identification $Y(K)=Y(\bar K)^G$, the $G$-fixed $\bar K$-point $\bar
y$ corresponds exactly to $y\in Y(K)$. Therefore the image of $\sigma^\star\circ
\bar x$ under $f$ is again $y$.

**Answer: Yes.** For each $\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\bar K/K)$, the image $f(
\sigma^\star\circ\bar x)$ equals $y$.

**Remark.** The étaleness of $f$ is not needed for this forward direction; it is
 used for the converse (descent): that the geometric fiber $f^{-1}(\bar y)$ is a
 finite étale $K$-scheme on which $G$ acts, and that $K$-rational lifts of $y$ c
orrespond to $G$-orbits of geometric lifts. The statement proved above holds for
 any morphism of $k$-schemes.
