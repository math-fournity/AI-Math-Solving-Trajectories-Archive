## Proof

Let $G$ be a finite-by-cyclic group, so there exists a finite normal subgroup $N
 \trianglelefteq G$ with $G/N$ cyclic. We show that $G$ has only finitely many f
inite subgroups.

Since $G/N$ is cyclic, it is either infinite cyclic or finite cyclic.

**Case 1: $G/N$ is infinite cyclic.**

An infinite cyclic group has no nontrivial finite subgroups. Let $H \leq G$ be a
ny finite subgroup. The image $\pi(H) \leq G/N$ (where $\pi: G \to G/N$ is the q
uotient map) is a finite subgroup of $G/N$, hence trivial. Therefore $H \leq \ke
r\pi = N$.

Thus every finite subgroup of $G$ is contained in $N$. Since $N$ is finite, it h
as only finitely many subgroups, and consequently $G$ has only finitely many fin
ite subgroups.

**Case 2: $G/N$ is finite cyclic.**

Here $N$ is finite and $G/N$ is finite, so $G$ itself is finite (with $|G| = |N|
\cdot|G/N|$). A finite group has only finitely many subgroups, so in particular
$G$ has only finitely many finite subgroups.

**Conclusion.** In both cases, $G$ contains only finitely many finite subgroups.
 $\blacksquare$
