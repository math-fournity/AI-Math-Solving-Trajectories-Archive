## Proof

The set in question is
$$S = \left\{\frac{m^3}{n^3} : m,n\in\mathbb{N}\right\} = \left\{q^3 : q\in\math
bb{Q}_{>0}\right\},$$
since every positive rational $q$ can be written as $m/n$ with $m,n\in\mathbb{N}
$, and conversely $m^3/n^3 = (m/n)^3$.

We claim $S$ is everywhere dense in $\mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ (indeed, dense in $\mathbb
{R}_{>0}$).

**Key observation.** The map $f:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}$, $f(x)=x^3$,
is a homeomorphism: it is continuous, strictly increasing, surjective, and its i
nverse $f^{-1}(y)=y^{1/3}$ is also continuous on $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$.

**Density argument.** Let $r\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ be an arbitrary positive rationa
l and let $\varepsilon>0$. We must find $q\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ with $|q^3 - r|<\v
arepsilon$.

Since $f^{-1}$ is continuous at $r$, there exists $\delta>0$ such that for all $
y\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}$,
$$|y - r| < \delta \implies |y^{1/3} - r^{1/3}| < \eta,$$
for any chosen $\eta>0$. Equivalently (using continuity of $f$ directly): there
exists $\eta>0$ such that
$$|x - r^{1/3}| < \eta \implies |x^3 - r| < \varepsilon.$$

Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}$, we may pick $q\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ w
ith $|q - r^{1/3}|<\eta$. (Choose $\eta$ small enough, e.g. $\eta < r^{1/3}$, to
 ensure $q>0$.) Then by construction
$$|q^3 - r| < \varepsilon.$$

Writing $q = m/n$ with $m,n\in\mathbb{N}$, we have $q^3 = m^3/n^3 \in S$.

**Conclusion.** For every positive rational $r$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, there
 exists an element of $S$ within $\varepsilon$ of $r$. Hence $S$ is dense in $\m
athbb{Q}_{>0}$.

Equivalently, the argument shows that the image of a dense subset under a contin
uous surjection is dense in the image of the whole space; here $\mathbb{Q}_{>0}$
 is dense in $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ and $x\mapsto x^3$ is a continuous surjection $\m
athbb{R}_{>0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}$, so $S$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$, and a fo
rtiori in $\mathbb{Q}_{>0}$.
