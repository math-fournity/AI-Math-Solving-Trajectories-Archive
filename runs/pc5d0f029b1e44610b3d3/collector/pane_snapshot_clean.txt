identity $\sum_{k} (k+a)^{-2} = \pi^2/\sin^2(\pi a)$.

---

**Problem.** Evaluate
$$L = \lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\gamma_n} \frac{\cot(\pi z)}{(z+a)^2}\,dz,$$
where $\gamma_n$ is the positively oriented rectangle with vertices
$$n+\tfrac12+ni,\quad -n-\tfrac12+ni,\quad -n-\tfrac12-ni,\quad n+\tfrac12-ni,\q
uad n+\tfrac12+ni.$$

We assume $a\notin\mathbb Z$ (and $a$ fixed), so the pole of $(z+a)^{-2}$ at $z=
-a$ does not coincide with a pole of $\cot(\pi z)$.

---

**Step 1 — Poles enclosed by $\gamma_n$.**

The contour has vertical sides at $\operatorname{Re} z=\pm(n+\tfrac12)$ (half-in
tegers) and horizontal sides at $\operatorname{Im} z=\pm n$. Hence it encloses e
xactly the integers $k=-n,-n+1,\dots,n$ — the simple poles of $\cot(\pi z)$ — an
d, for $n$ large enough, the point $z=-a$ (a double pole of $(z+a)^{-2}$), since
 $a$ is fixed.

Set $f(z)=\dfrac{\cot(\pi z)}{(z+a)^2}$.

---

**Step 2 — Residues.**

*At an integer $z=k$:* Since $\sin(\pi z)$ has a simple zero at $k$ with derivat
ive $\pi(-1)^k$,
$$\cot(\pi z)\sim\frac{1}{\pi(z-k)},\qquad\text{so}\qquad \operatorname{Res}_{z=
k} f=\frac{1}{\pi(k+a)^2}.$$

*At $z=-a$ (double pole):* $\cot(\pi z)$ is analytic near $-a$ (as $a\notin\math
bb Z$). The residue of $\dfrac{g(z)}{(z+a)^2}$ at $z=-a$ is $g'(-a)$, with $g(z)
=\cot(\pi z)$:
$$g'(z)=-\pi\csc^2(\pi z),\qquad g'(-a)=-\pi\csc^2(\pi a)=-\frac{\pi}{\sin^2(\pi
 a)}.$$

---

**Step 3 — Residue theorem.**

$$\int_{\gamma_n} f(z)\,dz
=2\pi i\left[\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\frac{1}{\pi(k+a)^2}-\frac{\pi}{\sin^2(\pi a)}\right
].\tag{1}$$

---

**Step 4 — The integral tends to $0$.**

We bound the integrand on $\gamma_n$.

*Boundedness of $\cot(\pi z)$.* Using
$$|\cot(\pi z)|^2=\frac{\cos^2(\pi x)+\sinh^2(\pi y)}{\sin^2(\pi x)+\sinh^2(\pi
y)},\qquad z=x+iy,$$
one checks $|\cot(\pi z)|\leq M$ uniformly on $\gamma_n$ for all large $n$ (e.g.
 $M=2$). Concretely:
- On the vertical sides $z=\pm(n+\tfrac12)+iy$: $\cot(\pi(n+\tfrac12+iy))=-i\tan
h(\pi y)$, so $|\cot|\leq 1$.
- On the horizontal sides $z=x\pm in$: $|\cot(\pi z)|\leq\coth(\pi n)\leq\coth\p
i<2$.

*Lower bound on $|z+a|$.* Since $-a$ is fixed and lies strictly inside $\gamma_n
$ for large $n$, the distance from $-a$ to the contour is at least $n-C$ for som
e constant $C=C(a)$. Thus
$$|z+a|^{-2}\leq (n-C)^{-2}\quad\text{on }\gamma_n.$$

*Length.* $|\gamma_n|=2(2n+1)+2(2n)=8n+2=O(n)$.

Combining,
$$\left|\int_{\gamma_n} f\,dz\right|\leq M\cdot(n-C)^{-2}\cdot(8n+2)=O\!\left(\t
frac1n\right)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.$$

---

**Step 5 — Conclusion.**

Letting $n\to\infty$ in (1), the left side tends to $0$, and the sum tends to $\
displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+a)^2}$. Hence
$$0=2\pi i\left[\frac1\pi\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+a)^2}-\frac{\pi}{\
sin^2(\pi a)}\right],$$
yielding the classical identity
$$\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+a)^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{\sin^2(\pi a)}.$$

In particular, the requested limit is

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\gamma_n}\frac{\cot(\pi z)}{(z+a)^2
}\,dz=0.}$$
