## Proof

Let $\epsilon > 0$ and $n \geq 2$ (for $n = 1$ the claim is vacuous, requiring $
0$ vectors). We construct $\mathcal{L}$ explicitly.

**Construction.** Let $B$ be the $n \times n$ diagonal matrix

$$B = \operatorname{diag}\!\bigl(\underbrace{\epsilon,\, \epsilon,\, \dots,\, \e
psilon}_{n-1 \text{ entries}},\; \epsilon^{-(n-1)}\bigr),$$

and define the lattice $\mathcal{L} = B\,\mathbb{Z}^n \subset \mathbb{R}^n$, wit
h basis vectors $b_1, \dots, b_n$ given by the columns of $B$.

**Unimodularity.** The covolume of $\mathcal{L}$ equals $|\det B|$. We compute:

$$|\det B| = \epsilon^{n-1} \cdot \epsilon^{-(n-1)} = \epsilon^{0} = 1.$$

Hence $\mathcal{L}$ is a unimodular $\mathbb{Z}$-lattice in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

**Short vectors.** For $i = 1, \dots, n-1$, the $i$-th basis vector is $b_i = \e
psilon\, e_i$, so

$$\|b_i\| = \epsilon \leq \epsilon.$$

**Linear independence.** The vectors $b_1, \dots, b_{n-1}$ are positive scalar m
ultiples of the distinct standard basis vectors $e_1, \dots, e_{n-1}$, hence are
 linearly independent.

**Primitivity.** Each $b_i$ (for $i = 1, \dots, n-1$) is a basis vector of $\mat
hcal{L}$, and every basis vector of a lattice is primitive. To verify directly:
suppose $b_i = k\, w$ for some $w \in \mathcal{L}$ and integer $k \geq 1$. Writi
ng $w = \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_j\, b_j$ with $a_j \in \mathbb{Z}$, the equation $b_i =
 k \sum_j a_j b_j$ yields $k\, a_i = 1$ and $k\, a_j = 0$ for $j \neq i$. Since
$a_i \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $k\, a_i = 1$, we must have $k = 1$. Thus $b_i$ is prim
itive. $\checkmark$

**Conclusion.** The lattice $\mathcal{L} = B\,\mathbb{Z}^n$ is unimodular and co
ntains the $n-1$ linearly independent primitive vectors $b_1, \dots, b_{n-1}$, e
ach of norm $\epsilon \leq \epsilon$. This construction works for every $\epsilo
n > 0$. $\blacksquare$
