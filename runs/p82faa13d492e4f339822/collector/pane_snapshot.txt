## Proof

### 1. The full unit hypersphere (literal reading)

Let $S^{n-1} = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : \|x\|=1\}$ be the unit hypersphere centere
d at the origin $O$. Its centroid (center of mass under the uniform surface meas
ure $\sigma$) is

$$G = \frac{1}{|S^{n-1}|}\int_{S^{n-1}} x\, d\sigma.$$

For every point $x \in S^{n-1}$, the antipodal point $-x$ also lies on $S^{n-1}$
, and the contributions $x + (-x) = 0$ cancel. Hence $\int_{S^{n-1}} x\,d\sigma
= 0$, so $G = O$ and

$$d(G, O) = 0 \quad \text{for all } n \ge 1.$$

The same holds for the unit ball $B^n$ by the same symmetry argument. So the lit
eral answer is the constant formula $d(n) = 0$.

### 2. The meaningful general formula: centroid of a unit hemispherical hypersur
face

The interesting "general formula in $n$" is obtained for the **upper hemisphere*
* $S^{n-1}_+ = \{x \in S^{n-1} : x_n \ge 0\}$, whose centroid is *not* the cente
r. By symmetry the centroid lies on the $x_n$-axis at height

$$\bar z = \frac{\displaystyle\int_{S^{n-1}_+} x_n\, d\sigma}{\displaystyle\int_
{S^{n-1}_+} d\sigma}.$$

**Slicing formula.** Slicing $S^{n-1}$ by hyperplanes $x_n = t$ ($t \in [-1,1]$)
 gives, for any integrable $f$,

$$\int_{S^{n-1}} f(x_n)\, d\sigma = |S^{n-2}|\int_{-1}^{1} f(t)(1-t^2)^{(n-3)/2}
\, dt,$$

where $|S^{n-2}| = \dfrac{2\pi^{(n-1)/2}}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)}$ i
s the area of the unit $(n{-}2)$-sphere.

**Numerator.** With $f(t) = t\cdot\mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}(t)$ and the substitution $u
 = 1-t^2$, $du = -2t\,dt$:

$$\int_{S^{n-1}_+} x_n\, d\sigma = |S^{n-2}|\int_0^1 t(1-t^2)^{(n-3)/2}\, dt = |
S^{n-2}|\cdot\frac{1}{2}\int_0^1 u^{(n-3)/2}\, du = \frac{|S^{n-2}|}{n-1}.$$

**Denominator** (hemisphere area, half of $|S^{n-1}|$):

$$\int_{S^{n-1}_+} d\sigma = \tfrac12|S^{n-1}| = \frac{\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(n/2)}.$
$

**Ratio.** Therefore

$$\bar z = \frac{|S^{n-2}|/(n-1)}{\pi^{n/2}/\Gamma(n/2)} = \frac{1}{n-1}\cdot\fr
ac{2\pi^{(n-1)/2}}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(n/2)}{\p
i^{n/2}} = \frac{2\,\Gamma(n/2)}{(n-1)\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\ri
ght)}.$$

### 3. The general formula

$$\boxed{\,d(n) = \bar z = \dfrac{2\,\Gamma\!\left(\dfrac{n}{2}\right)}{(n-1)\,\
sqrt{\pi}\;\Gamma\!\left(\dfrac{n-1}{2}\right)}\,}, \qquad n \ge 2.$$

### 4. Verification

- **$n=2$** (semicircle arc, unit radius): $\bar z = \dfrac{2\,\Gamma(1)}{1\cdot
\sqrt{\pi}\cdot\Gamma(1/2)} = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}\cdot\sqrt{\pi}} = \dfrac{2}{\
pi}$. ✓ (classical centroid of a semicircular arc)
- **$n=3$** (hemispherical surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$): $\bar z = \dfrac{2\,\Gamm
a(3/2)}{2\cdot\sqrt{\pi}\cdot\Gamma(1)} = \dfrac{2\cdot\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}}{2\s
qrt{\pi}} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. ✓ (classical centroid of a hemispherical shell)
- **$n=4$**: $\bar z = \dfrac{2\,\Gamma(2)}{3\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(3/2)} = \dfrac{2
}{3\sqrt{\pi}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}} = \dfrac{4}{3\pi}$, which matches the di
rect computation $\bar z = \frac{|S^2|/3}{|S^2|\cdot\pi/4} = \frac{4}{3\pi}$. ✓

### 5. Limiting behavior

As $n \to \infty$, using Stirling's approximation $\Gamma(x+1/2)/\Gamma(x) \sim
\sqrt{x}$, we get $\bar z \sim \sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\pi n}} \to 0$: in high dimension
s the mass of a hemisphere concentrates near the equator, so its centroid approa
ches the center — consistent with the well-known concentration of measure on hig
h-dimensional spheres.
