**Claim:** $BT = TB$.

**Setup.** Let $\{T\}' = \{S \in \mathcal{B}(H) : ST = TS\}$ be the commutant of
 $T$, and $\{T\}'' = \{S \in \mathcal{B}(H) : SA = AS \text{ for all } A \in \{T
\}'\}$ the double commutant. The hypothesis says precisely that
$$B \in \{T\}'', \qquad T^+ \in \{T\}'', \qquad T^- \in \{T\}'' .$$
We only need the first inclusion.

**Step 1: Apply the double commutant theorem.** Since $T$ is self-adjoint, the s
ingleton $\{T\}$ is a self-adjoint subset of $\mathcal{B}(H)$. By von Neumann's
double commutant theorem,
$$\{T\}'' = W^*(T),$$
the von Neumann algebra generated by $T$, i.e. the smallest strongly closed $*$-
subalgebra of $\mathcal{B}(H)$ containing $T$ and $I$.

**Step 2: $W^*(T)$ is abelian.** Because $T = T^*$, the algebra generated by $T$
 and $I$ consists of polynomials $p(T)$ in the single operator $T$. Any two poly
nomials in $T$ commute: $p(T)\,q(T) = q(T)\,p(T) = (pq)(T)$. Thus the polynomial
 algebra is abelian. Its strong closure remains abelian: if $p_n(T) \to A$ and $
q_n(T) \to B$ strongly, then $(p_n(T))$ is uniformly bounded (uniform boundednes
s principle), so $p_n(T)\,q_n(T) \to AB$ and $q_n(T)\,p_n(T) \to BA$ strongly; s
ince $p_n(T)\,q_n(T) = q_n(T)\,p_n(T)$ for all $n$, we get $AB = BA$. Hence ever
y pair of elements in $W^*(T)$ commutes.

**Step 3: Both $B$ and $T$ lie in $W^*(T)$.** The hypothesis gives $B \in \{T\}'
' = W^*(T)$. Also $T \in \{T\}''$, since $T$ commutes with every operator that c
ommutes with $T$ (trivially: $ST = TS \implies TS = ST$).

**Conclusion.** Since $W^*(T)$ is abelian and both $B, T \in W^*(T)$, we obtain
$$BT = TB.$$
