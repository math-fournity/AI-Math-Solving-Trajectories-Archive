## Proof

We claim the limit holds, i.e.,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\varphi_{1/n}*f_n - f_n\|_{L^p(\mathbb{R}^N)} = 0.$$

**Setup.** Let $1 \le p < \infty$ (the case $p=\infty$ is addressed at the end).
 The standard mollifier satisfies $\varphi \in C_c^\infty$, $\varphi \ge 0$, $\o
peratorname{supp}\varphi \subset B(0,1)$, $\int \varphi = 1$, and $\varphi_\vare
psilon(x) = \varepsilon^{-N}\varphi(x/\varepsilon)$. In particular $\|\varphi_\v
arepsilon\|_{L^1}=1$ for every $\varepsilon > 0$.

**Key decomposition.** By the triangle inequality,
$$
\|\varphi_{1/n}*f_n - f_n\|_{L^p}
\le \underbrace{\|\varphi_{1/n}*f_n - \varphi_{1/n}*f\|_{L^p}}_{(A)}
+ \underbrace{\|\varphi_{1/n}*f - f\|_{L^p}}_{(B)}
+ \underbrace{\|f - f_n\|_{L^p}}_{(C)}.
$$

**Term (A): mollification is an $L^p$-contraction.** By Young's convolution ineq
uality,
$$
\|\varphi_{1/n}*(f_n - f)\|_{L^p} \le \|\varphi_{1/n}\|_{L^1}\,\|f_n - f\|_{L^p}
 = \|f_n - f\|_{L^p},
$$
since $\|\varphi_{1/n}\|_{L^1} = 1$. Thus $(A) \le \|f_n - f\|_{L^p}$.

**Term (C):** Directly $\|f - f_n\|_{L^p} \to 0$ by the hypothesis $f_n \to f$ i
n $L^p$.

**Term (B): standard mollification of a fixed $L^p$ function.** For a fixed $f \
in L^p(\mathbb{R}^N)$ with $1 \le p < \infty$, the classical result gives
$$
\lim_{\varepsilon \to 0^+}\|\varphi_\varepsilon * f - f\|_{L^p} = 0.
$$
*Proof sketch for completeness:* Since $C_c(\mathbb{R}^N)$ is dense in $L^p$, fo
r any $\delta > 0$ pick $g \in C_c$ with $\|f - g\|_{L^p} < \delta$. Then
$$
\|\varphi_\varepsilon * f - f\|_{L^p} \le \|\varphi_\varepsilon*(f-g)\|_{L^p} +
\|\varphi_\varepsilon * g - g\|_{L^p} + \|g - f\|_{L^p} \le 2\delta + \|\varphi_
\varepsilon * g - g\|_{L^p}.
$$
Since $g$ is uniformly continuous and compactly supported, $\varphi_\varepsilon
* g \to g$ uniformly on compact sets, and the supports stay in a common compact
set, giving $\|\varphi_\varepsilon * g - g\|_{L^p} \to 0$. Hence $\limsup_{\vare
psilon\to 0}\|\varphi_\varepsilon * f - f\|_{L^p} \le 2\delta$, and $\delta$ is
arbitrary. Setting $\varepsilon = 1/n \to 0$ yields $(B) \to 0$.

**Combining.** We have shown
$$
\|\varphi_{1/n}*f_n - f_n\|_{L^p} \le 2\|f_n - f\|_{L^p} + \|\varphi_{1/n}*f - f
\|_{L^p}.
$$
Both terms on the right tend to $0$ as $n \to \infty$ (the first by hypothesis,
the second by the standard mollification theorem applied to the fixed function $
f$). Therefore
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\varphi_{1/n}*f_n - f_n\|_{L^p(\mathbb{R}^N)} = 0.
$$

**Remark on $p = \infty$.** The same argument applies when $p = \infty$, since Y
oung's inequality $\|\varphi_{1/n}*(f_n-f)\|_{L^\infty} \le \|f_n-f\|_{L^\infty}
$ still holds, and $\|f_n - f\|_{L^\infty} \to 0$ by hypothesis. The only subtle
ty is term $(B)$: for a general $f \in L^\infty$, mollification converges to $f$
 only in the $L^\infty_{\text{loc}}$ sense (or a.e. along subsequences), not in
$L^\infty$. However, since $(A) + (C) \to 0$ already, and $(B) \ge 0$, the bound
 is not directly closed for $p=\infty$ without additional regularity on $f$. Thu
s the clean statement with the proof above is for $1 \le p < \infty$, which is t
he standard setting for the hypothesis $f_n \to f \in L^p$.
