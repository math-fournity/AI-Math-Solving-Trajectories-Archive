## Proof: Euler–Lagrange Equation for a Function of \(n\) Independent Variables

### Setup

Let the functional be
\[
I[y]=\int_{\Omega} F\!\left(y,\,\frac{\partial y}{\partial x_1},\ldots,\frac{\pa
rtial y}{\partial x_n},\,x_1,\ldots,x_n\right)\,dx_1\cdots dx_n,
\]
where \(\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n\) is a fixed domain with boundary \(\partial
\Omega\). We write \(p_i=\frac{\partial y}{\partial x_i}\) for brevity, so \(F=F
(y,p_1,\ldots,p_n,x_1,\ldots,x_n)\).

Let \(\hat y\) be an extremal and consider a variation
\[
y_\varepsilon = \hat y + \varepsilon\,\eta,
\]
where \(\eta=\eta(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\) is an arbitrary smooth function vanishing on
 the boundary:
\[
\eta\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0.
\]

### First variation

A necessary condition for \(\hat y\) to extremize \(I\) is
\[
\left.\frac{dI[y_\varepsilon]}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{\varepsilon=0}=0
\quad\text{for all admissible }\eta.
\]

Computing:
\[
\frac{dI[y_\varepsilon]}{d\varepsilon}
=\int_{\Omega}\left[
\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}\,\eta
+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial F}{\partial p_i}\,\frac{\partial \eta}{\partial x_
i}
\right]dx_1\cdots dx_n.
\]

Setting \(\varepsilon=0\) (so \(y=\hat y\), \(p_i=\partial\hat y/\partial x_i\))
:
\[
0=\int_{\Omega}\left[
F_y\,\eta+\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_{p_i}\,\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial x_i}
\right]dx_1\cdots dx_n. \tag{$\ast$}
\]

### Integration by parts

Apply the divergence identity to each term in the sum:
\[
F_{p_i}\,\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial x_i}
=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\!\left(F_{p_i}\,\eta\right)-\eta\,\frac{\partial}
{\partial x_i}\!\left(F_{p_i}\right).
\]

Substituting into \((\ast)\):
\[
0=\int_{\Omega}\left[
F_y\,\eta+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\!\left(F_{p_i}\,\eta\right
)-\eta\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial F_{p_i}}{\partial x_i}
\right]dx_1\cdots dx_n.
\]

The middle term is a total divergence:
\[
\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\!\left(F_{p_i}\,\eta\ri
ght)dx_1\cdots dx_n
=\int_{\partial\Omega}\eta\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_{p_i}\,n_i\,dS,
\]
by the divergence theorem, where \(n_i\) are the components of the outward unit
normal. Since \(\eta\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0\), this boundary integral **vanishe
s**.

Therefore:
\[
0=\int_{\Omega}\eta\left[
F_y-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial F_{p_i}}{\partial x_i}
\right]dx_1\cdots dx_n. \tag{$\ast\ast$}
\]

### Fundamental lemma

The function \(\eta\) is arbitrary (subject to smoothness and vanishing on \(\pa
rtial\Omega\)). By the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations, the brac
keted expression must vanish identically on \(\Omega\):
\[
F_y-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial F_{p_i}}{\partial x_i}=0.
\]

### Result

Restoring \(p_i=\partial y/\partial x_i\), the **Euler–Lagrange equation** for t
he functional \(I\) is:

\[
\boxed{\;\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_
i}\!\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\,(\partial y/\partial x_i)}\right)=0\;}
\]

or equivalently, in expanded form,
\[
\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}
-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_1}\!\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial y_{x_1}}\right)
-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_2}\!\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial y_{x_2}}\right)
-\cdots-
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_n}\!\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial y_{x_n}}\right)=
0.
\]

**Note on the total derivatives.** The derivatives \(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_
i}(\cdot)\) here are *total* (material) derivatives: when differentiating \(F_{p
_i}\) with respect to \(x_i\), one must account for the implicit dependence of \
(y\) and every \(p_j=\partial y/\partial x_j\) on \(x_i\). Explicitly,
\[
\frac{\partial F_{p_i}}{\partial x_i}
=F_{p_i\,y}\,y_{x_i}
+\sum_{j=1}^{n}F_{p_i\,p_j}\,y_{x_j x_i}
+F_{p_i\,x_i}.
\]

### Verification (special cases)

- **\(n=1\)** (single independent variable): the equation reduces to the classic
al one-dimensional Euler–Lagrange equation
  \[
  F_y-\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(F_{y'}\right)=0.
  \]
- **\(F=\tfrac12\sum_i y_{x_i}^2\)** (Dirichlet energy): \(F_y=0\), \(F_{y_{x_i}
}=y_{x_i}\), giving \(-\sum_i y_{x_i x_i}=0\), i.e. \(\Delta y=0\) — Laplace's e
quation, as expected.

Both special cases confirm the general formula.
