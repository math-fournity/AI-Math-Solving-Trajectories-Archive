## Evaluation of $\displaystyle I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\cos x}{x(b^{2
}+x^{2})}\,dx$ by contour integration

Assume $b>0$. The integrand has a simple pole at $x=0$ on the real axis and deca
ys like $|x|^{-3}$ as $|x|\to\infty$, so the integral is understood as a **Cauch
y principal value**, and it is absolutely convergent at infinity.

### Step 1. Symmetry observation

$\cos x$ is even, $x$ is odd, $b^{2}+x^{2}$ is even, hence
$$
\frac{\cos x}{x(b^{2}+x^{2})}\ \text{is odd.}
$$
Therefore, *if* the principal value exists, it must vanish. We now **prove** thi
s existence and the value $0$ rigorously by residues, which also yields the comp
anion (non‑trivial) sine integral.

### Step 2. Complexification

Set
$$
f(z)=\frac{e^{iz}}{z(b^{2}+z^{2})}=\frac{e^{iz}}{z(z-ib)(z+ib)}.
$$
Poles: $z=0$ (simple, on $\mathbb R$), $z=ib$ (simple, upper half‑plane), $z=-ib
$ (simple, lower half‑plane).

### Step 3. Residues

$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z)=\frac{e^{i\cdot 0}}{b^{2}+0}=\frac{1}{b^{2}}.
$$

$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=ib} f(z)=\frac{e^{i(ib)}}{(ib)\,(ib-(-ib))}=\frac{e^{-b}}{
(ib)(2ib)}=\frac{e^{-b}}{2i^{2}b^{2}}=-\frac{e^{-b}}{2b^{2}}.
$$

### Step 4. Contour with indentation above the origin

Take the standard keyhole‑free contour $C$: the segment $[-R,-\varepsilon]$, a s
mall semicircle $C_{\varepsilon}$ of radius $\varepsilon$ in the **upper** half‑
plane from $-\varepsilon$ to $+\varepsilon$ (traversed clockwise, i.e. above the
 pole), the segment $[\varepsilon,R]$, and the large semicircle $C_{R}$ of radiu
s $R$ in the upper half‑plane (counterclockwise).

Only the pole $z=ib$ lies inside $C$, so by the residue theorem
$$
\oint_{C} f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i\,\operatorname{Res}_{z=ib}f(z)=2\pi i\!\left(-\frac{
e^{-b}}{2b^{2}}\right)=-\frac{\pi i\,e^{-b}}{b^{2}}.
$$

### Step 5. Estimate the large arc

On $C_{R}$, $z=Re^{i\theta}$ with $\theta\in[0,\pi]$, so $|e^{iz}|=e^{-R\sin\the
ta}\le 1$ and
$$
|f(z)|\le \frac{1}{R\,(R^{2}-b^{2})}\sim R^{-3},
$$
hence $\displaystyle\int_{C_{R}}f(z)\,dz\to 0$ as $R\to\infty$ by Jordan's lemma
 (in fact by a direct $ML$‑estimate).

### Step 6. Small‑arc contribution

On $C_{\varepsilon}$ (clockwise semicircle above $0$), the standard indentation
lemma gives
$$
\int_{C_{\varepsilon}} f(z)\,dz \;\longrightarrow\; -\,i\pi\,\operatorname{Res}_
{z=0}f(z)=-\frac{i\pi}{b^{2}}\qquad(\varepsilon\to 0).
$$
The minus sign is because the indentation is traversed clockwise (half‑residue,
negative orientation).

### Step 7. Assemble and take real/imaginary parts

Letting $R\to\infty$ and $\varepsilon\to 0$,
$$
\operatorname{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{ix}}{x(b^{2}+x^{2})}\,dx \;-\
;\frac{i\pi}{b^{2}} \;=\; -\frac{\pi i\,e^{-b}}{b^{2}},
$$
so
$$
\operatorname{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{ix}}{x(b^{2}+x^{2})}\,dx
=\frac{i\pi}{b^{2}}\bigl(1-e^{-b}\bigr).
$$

Taking the **real part** (using $e^{ix}=\cos x+i\sin x$):
$$
\boxed{\;\operatorname{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos x}{x(b^{2}+x^{2})}
\,dx \;=\; \operatorname{Re}\!\left[\frac{i\pi}{b^{2}}(1-e^{-b})\right]=0.\;}
$$

Taking the **imaginary part** (a useful by‑product confirming the computation is
 non‑vacuous):
$$
\operatorname{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x(b^{2}+x^{2})}\,dx=\fra
c{\pi}{b^{2}}\bigl(1-e^{-b}\bigr),
$$
which is the standard non‑trivial result and is consistent with differentiating
the classical identity $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx
=\pi$ against the parameter $b$.

### Step 8. Consistency check

The result $I=0$ agrees with the oddness argument of Step 1: the principal value
 of an odd, integrable‑at‑infinity function over the symmetric interval $(-\inft
y,\infty)$ is exactly $0$. The contour computation independently confirms this a
nd, importantly, **establishes existence** of the principal value (the cancellat
ion of the logarithmic singularity at the origin is exactly what the indentation
 lemma certifies).

### Conclusion

For $b>0$,
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\cos x}{x(b^{2}+x^{2})}\,dx \;=\; 0
\quad\text{(Cauchy principal value).}
$$
