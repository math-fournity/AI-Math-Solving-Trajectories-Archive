## Inverse Function Theorem

**Statement.** Let $U \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ be an open set, let $p_0 \in U$, and
 let $F : U \to \mathbb{R}^n$ be a $C^k$ map ($k \ge 1$). Suppose that the deriv
ative (Jacobian) $DF(p_0) : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$ is invertible, i.e. $
\det DF(p_0) \neq 0$. Then there exist:

- an open neighborhood $V \subset U$ of $p_0$ (with $p_0 \in V \subseteq U$),
- an open neighborhood $W \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ of $F(p_0)$,

such that the restriction
$$F\big|_V : V \to W$$
is a **$C^k$ diffeomorphism**. Concretely:

1. **Bijectivity.** $F\big|_V : V \to W$ is one-to-one and onto $W$, so it has a
n inverse
$$G : W \to V, \qquad G = \big(F\big|_V\big)^{-1}.$$

2. **Regularity of the inverse.** The inverse $G$ is of class $C^k$ on $W$. In p
articular, the local regularity of $F$ is inherited by its inverse: if $F$ is $C
^k$, then $G$ is $C^k$; if $F$ is $C^\infty$ (smooth), then $G$ is $C^\infty$.

3. **Derivative of the inverse.** For every $q = F(p) \in W$ (with $p \in V$), t
he derivative of $G$ at $q$ exists and is given by the inverse of the derivative
 of $F$ at $p$:
$$DG(q) = \big[DF(p)\big]^{-1} = \big[DF\big(G(q)\big)\big]^{-1}.$$
In particular, at the base point:
$$DG\big(F(p_0)\big) = \big[DF(p_0)\big]^{-1}.$$

4. **Openness.** $F(V) = W$ is open, so $F$ is an open mapping locally around $p
_0$.

5. **Persistence of invertibility.** By continuity of $p \mapsto \det DF(p)$ (si
nce $F$ is $C^k$ with $k \ge 1$) and the fact that $\det DF(p_0) \neq 0$, there
is a (possibly smaller) neighborhood of $p_0$ on which $\det DF(p) \neq 0$; one
takes $V$ inside this neighborhood, so $DF(p)$ is invertible for every $p \in V$
.

**Conclusion about $F$ locally around $p_0$.** Locally around $p_0$, the map $F$
 behaves like its linear approximation $DF(p_0)$: it is a bijective $C^k$ map be
tween open sets, with a $C^k$ inverse whose derivative is the pointwise inverse
of $DF$. In other words, $F$ is a **local $C^k$ diffeomorphism** at $p_0$. The s
ingle hypothesis "$DF(p_0)$ is invertible" — a condition checked at one point —
guarantees that $F$ is invertible with a regular inverse on a whole neighborhood
 of that point.
