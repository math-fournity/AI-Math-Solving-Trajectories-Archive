## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** The series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n f_n$ converges in the norm of
 $C(K)$.

Let $S_n = \operatorname{supp}(f_n) = \overline{\{x \in K : f_n(x) \neq 0\}}$ de
note the (closed) support of $f_n$. By hypothesis the $S_n$ are pairwise disjoin
t.

**Key observation.** Since the $S_n$ are pairwise disjoint, for every $x \in K$
there is at most one index $n$ with $f_n(x) \neq 0$ (namely, if $f_n(x)\neq 0$ t
hen $x \in S_n$, and $x$ belongs to at most one $S_n$). Consequently, for any fi
nite set of indices $I$ and any scalars $b_n$,

$$\Big\|\sum_{n\in I} b_n f_n\Big\|_\infty = \max_{n\in I} |b_n|\,\|f_n\|_\infty
.$$

Indeed, at each $x$ the sum reduces to a single term $b_n f_n(x)$ (or $0$), so t
he supremum over $x$ is the maximum over $n\in I$ of $\sup_{x\in S_n}|b_n f_n(x)
| = |b_n|\|f_n\|_\infty$; the maximum is attained since each $S_n$ is compact (c
losed in compact $K$) and $|b_n f_n|$ is continuous.

**Cauchy criterion.** Set $M_0 := \sup_n \|f_n\|_\infty < \infty$. For $N > M$,

$$\|S_N - S_M\|_\infty = \Big\|\sum_{n=M+1}^{N} a_n f_n\Big\|_\infty = \max_{M+1
\le n\le N} |a_n|\,\|f_n\|_\infty \le M_0 \cdot \max_{n\ge M+1} |a_n|.$$

Since $(a_n)\in c_0$, we have $\max_{n\ge M+1}|a_n| \to 0$ as $M\to\infty$. Henc
e $\|S_N-S_M\|_\infty\to 0$; the partial sums are Cauchy in $(C(K),\|\cdot\|_\in
fty)$.

**Conclusion.** $C(K)$ with the sup norm is a Banach space ($K$ compact Hausdorf
f), so every Cauchy sequence converges. Therefore $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n f_n$ co
nverges in the norm of $C(K)$ to a continuous function.

*Remark.* The hypothesis $\inf_n\|f_n\|>0$ is not needed for this (forward) dire
ction; it is the condition ensuring the converse — that the coefficient map $c_0
\to C(K)$, $(a_n)\mapsto\sum a_n f_n$, is an isomorphic embedding with lower bou
nd controlled by $\inf\|f_n\|$.
