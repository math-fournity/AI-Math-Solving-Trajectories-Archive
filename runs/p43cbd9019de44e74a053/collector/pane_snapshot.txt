## Solution

**Setup.** The red ball is equally likely to be in any of the $N+1$ positions am
ong the $N$ green balls. If the red ball is in position $k$ (with $k$ green ball
s to its left and $N-k$ to its right), where $k \in \{0,1,\dots,N\}$, then "at l
east $\frac{3}{5}$ of the green balls are on the same side" means
$$\max(k,\, N-k) \ge \tfrac{3N}{5},$$
equivalently $k \ge \tfrac{3N}{5}$ or $k \le \tfrac{2N}{5}$.

**Counting favorable positions.** Since $N$ is a multiple of $5$, both $\tfrac{2
N}{5}$ and $\tfrac{3N}{5}$ are integers, and $\tfrac{2N}{5} < \tfrac{3N}{5}$ (fo
r $N>0$), so the two ranges are disjoint:
- $k \le \tfrac{2N}{5}$: values $k=0,1,\dots,\tfrac{2N}{5}$ → $\tfrac{2N}{5}+1$
positions.
- $k \ge \tfrac{3N}{5}$: values $k=\tfrac{3N}{5},\dots,N$ → $N-\tfrac{3N}{5}+1 =
 \tfrac{2N}{5}+1$ positions.

Total favorable $= 2\!\left(\tfrac{2N}{5}+1\right) = \tfrac{4N}{5}+2$, giving
$$P(N) = \frac{\tfrac{4N}{5}+2}{N+1}.$$

**Verification of given values.**
- $P(5) = \frac{4+2}{6} = 1.$ ✓
- $\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty} P(N) = \frac{4N/5}{N} = \frac{4}{5}.$ ✓

**Solving the inequality.** We need
$$\frac{\tfrac{4N}{5}+2}{N+1} < \frac{321}{400}.$$
Cross-multiplying (both sides positive):
$$400\!\left(\tfrac{4N}{5}+2\right) < 321(N+1)$$
$$320N + 800 < 321N + 321$$
$$479 < N.$$

**Least qualifying $N$.** The least multiple of $5$ strictly greater than $479$
is $N = 480$.

**Sum of digits:** $4 + 8 + 0 = 12$.
