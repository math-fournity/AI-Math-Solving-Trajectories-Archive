Looking at this problem, I need to determine the maximum number of servers in a
circle such that no two non-adjacent servers can equalize their token and key co
unts.

## Analysis

**Setting up the parity framework.**

Each server $i$ has a pair $(s_i, e_i)$ of Type-S tokens and Type-E keys. Two no
n-adjacent servers $i, j$ can redistribute their combined pool equally iff each
gets half of each type, requiring:
- $s_i + s_j$ is even, i.e., $s_i \equiv s_j \pmod{2}$
- $e_i + e_j$ is even, i.e., $e_i \equiv e_j \pmod{2}$

The protocol demands that for every non-adjacent pair, redistribution is **impos
sible**, so at least one of these parity conditions must **fail**. Equivalently:

> **Any two non-adjacent servers must differ in at least one of the two parities
 (token-parity or key-parity).**

**Classifying servers by type.**

Each server's parity pair $(s_i \bmod 2,\; e_i \bmod 2)$ gives one of 4 types:
$$A=(0,0),\quad B=(0,1),\quad C=(1,0),\quad D=(1,1).$$

The condition becomes: **no two non-adjacent servers share the same type.** Equi
valently, for each type, all servers of that type must be **pairwise adjacent**
in the circle.

**Bounding the clique size in a cycle.**

For $n \geq 4$, the cycle graph $C_n$ has clique number 2: any set of pairwise-a
djacent vertices has size at most 2 (since three mutually adjacent vertices woul
d require a triangle, which $C_n$ lacks for $n \geq 4$). Therefore, each type ca
n appear **at most 2 times**, and if it appears twice, those two servers must be
 adjacent.

With 4 types, each appearing at most twice:
$$n \leq 4 \times 2 = 8.$$

**Constructing the achievement of $n = 8$.**

Arrange 8 servers in a circle with consecutive pairs sharing a type:
$$A, A, B, B, C, C, D, D$$

Verification:
- The two $A$-servers (positions 1, 2) are adjacent ✓
- The two $B$-servers (positions 3, 4) are adjacent ✓
- The two $C$-servers (positions 5, 6) are adjacent ✓
- The two $D$-servers (positions 7, 8) are adjacent ✓
- Every non-adjacent pair has distinct types (since each type appears only on an
 adjacent pair) ✓

For instance, we can assign concrete values:
| Position | Type | $(s_i, e_i)$ |
|----------|------|---------------|
| 1 | $A$ | $(0, 0)$ |
| 2 | $A$ | $(2, 4)$ |
| 3 | $B$ | $(0, 1)$ |
| 4 | $B$ | $(4, 3)$ |
| 5 | $C$ | $(1, 0)$ |
| 6 | $C$ | $(3, 6)$ |
| 7 | $D$ | $(1, 1)$ |
| 8 | $D$ | $(5, 7)$ |

Each type's two members share both parities (so they *could* redistribute, but t
hey're adjacent, so the protocol doesn't restrict them). Every non-adjacent pair
 differs in at least one parity, making equalization impossible. ✓

**Confirming $n = 9$ is impossible.**

For $n = 9 \geq 4$, each of the 4 types appears at most 2 times, giving $n \leq
8 < 9$. By pigeonhole, some type must appear at least $\lceil 9/4 \rceil = 3$ ti
mes, but three servers of the same type cannot be pairwise adjacent in $C_9$ (cl
ique number is 2), so two of them are non-adjacent with identical parities — vio
lating the protocol.

## Answer

The maximum number of servers is $\boxed{8}$.
