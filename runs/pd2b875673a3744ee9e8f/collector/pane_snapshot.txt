## Solution

**Answer:** The only positive integer $n \geq 3$ for which a regular $n$-gon can
 be placed with all vertices at integer lattice points is $n = 4$.

---

### Proof

**Necessity ($n = 4$ is the only possibility).**

Suppose a regular $n$-gon ($n \geq 3$) has all vertices at points of $\mathbb{Z}
^2$. Let $a$ be its side length. Since consecutive vertices are lattice points,

$$a^2 = (\Delta x)^2 + (\Delta y)^2 \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}.$$

The area of a regular $n$-gon with side length $a$ is

$$A = \frac{n\, a^2}{4}\cot\!\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right).$$

On the other hand, by the **shoelace formula**, the (signed) area of any polygon
 whose vertices are lattice points is a half-integer, hence rational. Therefore
$A \in \mathbb{Q}$.

Since $\frac{n\,a^2}{4}$ is a positive rational number, we conclude

$$\cot\!\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right) \in \mathbb{Q}. \tag{$\star$}$$

Set $\theta = \pi/n$. From $(\star)$, $\cot\theta$ is rational, so

$$\cos(2\theta) = \frac{\cot^2\theta - 1}{\cot^2\theta + 1} \in \mathbb{Q}.$$

Thus $\cos(2\pi/n)$ is rational. Since $2\pi/n$ is a rational multiple of $\pi$,
 **Niven's theorem** applies: the only rational values of $\cos$ at rational mul
tiples of $\pi$ are $0, \pm\tfrac12, \pm 1$. Hence

$$\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right) \in \left\{0,\ \pm\tfrac12,\ \pm 1\right\}.$
$

We examine each case for $n \geq 3$:

| $\cos(2\pi/n)$ | $2\pi/n$ | $n$ | $\cot(\pi/n)$ | Rational? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1$ | $0$ | — | impossible ($n\geq 3$) | — |
| $-1$ | $\pi$ | $2$ | excluded | — |
| $0$ | $\pi/2$ | $4$ | $1$ | ✓ |
| $1/2$ | $\pi/3$ | $6$ | $\sqrt{3}$ | ✗ |
| $-1/2$ | $2\pi/3$ | $3$ | $1/\sqrt{3}$ | ✗ |

The cases $n=3$ and $n=6$ are eliminated because $\cot(\pi/3)=1/\sqrt3$ and $\co
t(\pi/6)=\sqrt3$ are both irrational, contradicting $(\star)$. The only survivin
g case is $n=4$.

**Sufficiency ($n=4$ works).**

The unit square with vertices $(0,0),\ (1,0),\ (1,1),\ (0,1)$ is a regular $4$-g
on with all vertices at lattice points. $\square$
