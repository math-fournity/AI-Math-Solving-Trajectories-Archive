## Solution

**Answer leak check:** No leaked answers, solutions, or proof sketches found in
the problem statement. Proceeding.

**Setup.** Encode each sequence of 10 chips as a lattice path starting at height
 0, where "Descend" is a step $+1$ and "Ascend" is a step $-1$. With 5 of each,
every path has 5 up-steps and 5 down-steps, ending at height $0$ after 10 steps.
 The safety condition "never moves more than 2 units deeper than the starting po
sition" means the running partial sum
$$S_k = (\text{# Descend in first } k) - (\text{# Ascend in first } k)$$
must satisfy $S_k \le 2$ for all $k = 0,1,\dots,10$.

**Total (unrestricted) paths.** Choosing the positions of the 5 Descend chips am
ong 10 slots:
$$\binom{10}{5} = 252.$$

**Counting the bad paths via the reflection principle.** A path is *bad* if it e
ver reaches height $3$ (i.e., exceeds the margin of 2). Let $k=3$ be the forbidd
en level. By André's reflection principle, the number of paths from $(0,0)$ to $
(10,0)$ that touch or cross level $3$ equals the number of unrestricted paths fr
om the reflected starting point $(0, 2k) = (0,6)$ to $(10,0)$.

A path from height $6$ to height $0$ in 10 steps needs net displacement $-6$, so
 with $u$ up-steps and $d$ down-steps:
$$u + d = 10, \quad u - d = -6 \implies d = 8,\; u = 2.$$
The count is
$$\binom{10}{2} = 45.$$

**Why reflection works.** Take any bad path and let $t$ be the first time it rea
ches height $3$. Reflect every step *up to and including time $t$* across the li
ne $y=3$ (swapping $+1 \leftrightarrow -1$). This maps the starting point $(0,0)
$ to $(0,6)$ while preserving the endpoint $(10,0)$, and the map is a bijection
between bad paths from $(0,0)$ and unrestricted paths from $(0,6)$. Hence the co
unt of bad paths is exactly $\binom{10}{2}=45$.

**Good paths.**
$$\binom{10}{5} - \binom{10}{2} = 252 - 45 = 207.$$

**Sanity checks of the formula.** For $n$ Descend and $n$ Ascend chips with ceil
ing $h$, the count is $\binom{2n}{n} - \binom{2n}{n-(h+1)}$.
- $n=1, h=0$: $\binom{2}{1}-\binom{2}{0}=2-1=1$ (only `AD`), correct.
- $n=2, h=0$: $\binom{4}{2}-\binom{4}{1}=6-4=2$ (the two Catalan paths `ADAD`, `
AADD`), correct.

The formula is verified.

**Answer.** The number of distinct valid sequences is $\boxed{207}$.
