## Proof

We show that the interpolation problem is not always solvable by exhibiting a co
ncrete obstruction, and then explain the underlying principle via the Schwarz–Pi
ck lemma.

### A concrete counterexample

Take
$$a_1 = 0,\quad a_2 = \tfrac{1}{2},\quad b_1 = 0,\quad b_2 = \tfrac{3}{4}.$$
All four points lie in $D(0,1)$. Suppose, for contradiction, that a holomorphic
$f:D(0,1)\to D(0,1)$ exists with $f(0)=0$ and $f(\tfrac12)=\tfrac34$.

Since $f(0)=0$ and $f$ maps the disc to itself, the **Schwarz lemma** applies: $
|f(z)|\le |z|$ for all $z\in D(0,1)$. In particular,
$$\tfrac34 = \left|f\!\left(\tfrac12\right)\right| \le \left|\tfrac12\right| = \
tfrac12,$$
a contradiction. Hence no such holomorphic self-map of the disc exists for these
 four points.

### The general obstruction: Schwarz–Pick

The example above is a special case of a general necessary condition. The **Schw
arz–Pick lemma** states that every holomorphic $f:D(0,1)\to D(0,1)$ is a contrac
tion for the *pseudohyperbolic metric*:
$$\rho(f(z),f(w)) \;\le\; \rho(z,w),\qquad \rho(z,w):=\left|\frac{z-w}{1-z\overl
ine{w}}\right|.$$

Therefore, a necessary condition for the existence of $f$ with $f(a_1)=b_1$, $f(
a_2)=b_2$ is
$$\rho(b_1,b_2)\;\le\;\rho(a_1,a_2).$$

This condition is not automatic: the four points $a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2$ are arbitrary
, and one can easily choose them so that $\rho(b_1,b_2)>\rho(a_1,a_2)$. In the c
ounterexample above,
$$\rho(b_1,b_2)=\rho\!\left(0,\tfrac34\right)=\tfrac34 \;>\; \tfrac12 = \rho\!\l
eft(0,\tfrac12\right)=\rho(a_1,a_2),$$
violating the inequality.

Geometrically, the Schwarz–Pick lemma says that holomorphic self-maps of the dis
c can only *decrease* (never increase) pseudohyperbolic distances. So if the tar
get pair $b_1,b_2$ is "farther apart" in this metric than the source pair $a_1,a
_2$, the interpolation is impossible. Since the problem statement places no cons
traint relating the pair $(a_1,a_2)$ to the pair $(b_1,b_2)$, such obstructions
can occur, and consequently a holomorphic $f:D(0,1)\to D(0,1)$ with the prescrib
ed values need not exist.
