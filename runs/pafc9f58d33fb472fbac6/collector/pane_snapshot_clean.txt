## Proof

Let $\mathscr{F}$ be a locally free sheaf of rank $n$ on a scheme $X$.

### The map

Define the **wedge pairing**
$$
\Phi : \wedge^{r}\mathscr{F} \times \wedge^{n-r}\mathscr{F} \longrightarrow \wed
ge^{n}\mathscr{F}, \qquad (\alpha,\beta)\longmapsto \alpha\wedge\beta,
$$
where $\alpha\wedge\beta$ is the exterior product, using the canonical multiplic
ation $\wedge^{r}\mathscr{F}\otimes\wedge^{n-r}\mathscr{F}\to\wedge^{n}\mathscr{
F}$. This is well-defined globally since exterior powers and the wedge product a
re functorial constructions on locally free sheaves (they commute with localizat
ion and pullback).

Being $\mathscr{O}_X$-bilinear, $\Phi$ corresponds by the universal property of
the tensor product to an $\mathscr{O}_X$-linear map
$$
\varphi : \wedge^{r}\mathscr{F}\otimes\wedge^{n-r}\mathscr{F}\longrightarrow \we
dge^{n}\mathscr{F}, \qquad \alpha\otimes\beta\longmapsto\alpha\wedge\beta.
$$

Currying (i.e., using the adjunction $\mathscr{H}om(-,-)\dashv -\otimes-$ for lo
cally free sheaves), $\varphi$ induces
$$
\psi : \wedge^{r}\mathscr{F} \longrightarrow \mathscr{H}om\!\bigl(\wedge^{n-r}\m
athscr{F},\,\wedge^{n}\mathscr{F}\bigr) = \bigl(\wedge^{n-r}\mathscr{F}\bigr)^{\
vee}\otimes\wedge^{n}\mathscr{F},
$$
given on sections by $\alpha\mapsto\bigl(\beta\mapsto\alpha\wedge\beta\bigr)$.

### $\psi$ is an isomorphism

It suffices to check locally. Since $\mathscr{F}$ is locally free of rank $n$, w
e may reduce to the affine case where $\mathscr{F}$ is a free module $M$ of rank
 $n$ over a ring $A$, with basis $e_1,\dots,e_n$. Then $\wedge^{r}M$ is free wit
h basis $\{e_I : |I|=r\}$ and $\wedge^{n-r}M$ is free with basis $\{e_J:|J|=n-r\
}$, where $I,J$ range over increasing multi-indices.

For each $I$ with $|I|=r$, let $I^{c}=\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus I$ (with its induce
d increasing order) be the complementary index set, $|I^{c}|=n-r$. Then
$$
e_I\wedge e_{I^{c}} = \varepsilon(I)\,e_1\wedge\cdots\wedge e_n,
$$
where $\varepsilon(I)=\operatorname{sgn}(I,I^{c})\in\{\pm1\}$ is the sign of the
 permutation reordering $(I,I^{c})$ to $(1,\dots,n)$. For $J\neq I^{c}$, the mul
ti-indices $I$ and $J$ share a common index, so $e_I\wedge e_J=0$.

Hence the dual basis element $e_{I^{c}}^{\vee}\in(\wedge^{n-r}M)^{\vee}$ (define
d by $e_{I^{c}}^{\vee}(e_J)=\delta_{J,I^{c}}$) satisfies
$$
\psi(e_I) : e_J \longmapsto e_I\wedge e_J = \varepsilon(I)\,\delta_{J,I^{c}}\,e_
1\wedge\cdots\wedge e_n.
$$
Therefore
$$
\psi(e_I) = \varepsilon(I)\,e_{I^{c}}^{\vee}\otimes(e_1\wedge\cdots\wedge e_n).
$$

The set $\{\varepsilon(I)\,e_{I^{c}}^{\vee}\otimes(e_1\wedge\cdots\wedge e_n)\}_
{|I|=r}$ is, up to the harmless signs $\varepsilon(I)$, exactly the basis of $(\
wedge^{n-r}M)^{\vee}\otimes\wedge^{n}M$ indexed by the complementary multi-indic
es $I^{c}$ (which biject with the $I$'s). Thus $\psi$ sends a basis to a basis,
so it is an isomorphism of free $A$-modules.

r)^{\vee}\otimes\wedge^{n}\mathscr{F}.
$$
