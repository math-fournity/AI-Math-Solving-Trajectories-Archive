## Problem

Find the mean distance between two points $P, Q$ chosen uniformly at random in a
 ball of radius $a$.

## Setup and scaling

By homogeneity, the answer is $a$ times the answer for the **unit** ball. So com
pute $E[d]$ for the unit ball and multiply by $a$.

For a uniform point in the unit ball, write it in spherical coordinates. The rad
ial variable $r\in[0,1]$ has density $f(r)=3r^{2}$ (since $\frac{4\pi r^{2}\,dr}
{4\pi/3}=3r^{2}\,dr$), and the direction is uniform on the sphere, independent o
f $r$. For two independent uniform directions, $u=\cos\theta\in[-1,1]$ has densi
ty $\tfrac12$.

With $P$ at radius $r$, $Q$ at radius $s$, and relative angle $\theta$ ($u=\cos\
theta$),

$$d=\sqrt{r^{2}+s^{2}-2rs\,u}.$$

## The mean

$$E[d]=\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{r^{2}+s^{2}-2rsu}\;\bi
gl(3r^{2}\bigr)\bigl(3s^{2}\bigr)\tfrac12\,du\,ds\,dr=\frac{9}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\!\
!\int_{0}^{1}r^{2}s^{2}\,I(r,s)\,ds\,dr,$$

where $I(r,s)=\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{r^{2}+s^{2}-2rsu}\,du$.

### Inner angular integral

With $A=r^{2}+s^{2}$, $B=2rs$,

$$I(r,s)=\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{A-Bu}\,du=\frac{2}{3B}\bigl[(A+B)^{3/2}-(A-B)^{3/2}\
bigr].$$

Since $A+B=(r+s)^{2}$ and $A-B=(r-s)^{2}$,

$$I(r,s)=\frac{1}{3rs}\bigl[(r+s)^{3}-|r-s|^{3}\bigr].$$

### Reduce to a radial double integral

$$E[d]=\frac{9}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{1}r^{2}s^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{3rs}\bigl[
(r+s)^{3}-|r-s|^{3}\bigr]\,ds\,dr=\frac{3}{2}\,J,$$

with $J=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{1}rs\bigl[(r+s)^{3}-|r-s|^{3}\big
r]\,ds\,dr$.

### Evaluate $J$

By symmetry in $r,s$, split the square into the two triangles:

$$J=2\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{r}rs\bigl[(r+s)^{3}-(r-s)^{3}\bigr]\,ds\,dr.$$

Expand: $(r+s)^{3}-(r-s)^{3}=6r^{2}s+2s^{3}=2s(3r^{2}+s^{2})$. Hence

$$J=2\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{r}rs\cdot 2s(3r^{2}+s^{2})\,ds\,dr=4\int_{0}^{1}r
\!\!\int_{0}^{r}s^{2}(3r^{2}+s^{2})\,ds\,dr.$$

Inner integral:

$$\int_{0}^{r}s^{2}(3r^{2}+s^{2})\,ds=\Bigl[r^{2}s^{3}+\tfrac{s^{5}}{5}\Bigr]_{0
}^{r}=r^{5}+\tfrac{r^{5}}{5}=\tfrac{6r^{5}}{5}.$$

So

$$J=4\int_{0}^{1}r\cdot\frac{6r^{5}}{5}\,dr=\frac{24}{5}\int_{0}^{1}r^{6}\,dr=\f
rac{24}{5}\cdot\frac{1}{7}=\frac{24}{35}.$$

### Final value for the unit ball

$$E[d]=\frac{3}{2}\cdot\frac{24}{35}=\frac{72}{70}=\frac{36}{35}.$$

## Rescale to radius $a$

Every linear dimension scales by $a$, so distances scale by $a$:

$$\boxed{E[|P-Q|]=\frac{36}{35}\,a}.$$

### Sanity check

The diameter is $2a$, and $\frac{36}{35}a\approx 1.0286\,a$, which is just over
the radius $a$ — a sensible mean (slightly larger than the center-to-surface mea
n distance, consistent with two random interior points typically being moderatel
y far apart). The classic result $\tfrac{36}{35}a$ is recovered.
