## Proof

The holomorphic automorphisms of the unit disk $\mathbb{D}=\{z:|z|<1\}$ are exac
tly the Möbius transformations

$$
f(z)=e^{i\theta}\,\frac{z-a}{1-\overline{a}\,z},\qquad |a|<1,\ \theta\in\mathbb{
R}.
$$

We determine which of these map the set $\{-1,1\}$ onto itself. There are two ca
ses.

**Case 1: $f(1)=1$ and $f(-1)=-1$.**

From $f(1)=1$:
$$
e^{i\theta}\,\frac{1-a}{1-\overline{a}}=1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad e^{i\theta}=
\frac{1-\overline{a}}{1-a}. \tag{1}
$$

From $f(-1)=-1$:
$$
e^{i\theta}\,\frac{-1-a}{1+\overline{a}}=-1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad e^{i\theta
}=\frac{1+\overline{a}}{1+a}. \tag{2}
$$

Equating (1) and (2):
$$
\frac{1-\overline{a}}{1-a}=\frac{1+\overline{a}}{1+a}
\;\Longrightarrow\; (1-\overline{a})(1+a)=(1+\overline{a})(1-a)
\;\Longrightarrow\; a-\overline{a}=\overline{a}-a
\;\Longrightarrow\; a=\overline{a}.
$$

Hence $a\in\mathbb{R}$. Substituting back into (1) gives $e^{i\theta}=1$, i.e. $
\theta\equiv 0\pmod{2\pi}$. Thus

$$
f(z)=\frac{z-a}{1-az},\qquad a\in\mathbb{R},\ |a|<1. \tag{F1}
$$

**Case 2: $f(1)=-1$ and $f(-1)=1$.**

From $f(1)=-1$:
$$
e^{i\theta}\,\frac{1-a}{1-\overline{a}}=-1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad e^{i\theta}
=-\,\frac{1-\overline{a}}{1-a}. \tag{3}
$$

From $f(-1)=1$:
$$
e^{i\theta}\,\frac{-1-a}{1+\overline{a}}=1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad e^{i\theta}
=-\,\frac{1+\overline{a}}{1+a}. \tag{4}
$$

Equating (3) and (4) yields the same equation $\frac{1-\overline{a}}{1-a}=\frac{
1+\overline{a}}{1+a}$, so again $a\in\mathbb{R}$. Substituting back gives $e^{i\
theta}=-1$, i.e. $\theta\equiv\pi\pmod{2\pi}$. Thus

$$
f(z)=-\,\frac{z-a}{1-az}=\frac{a-z}{1-az},\qquad a\in\mathbb{R},\ |a|<1. \tag{F2
}
$$

**Verification.** For (F1): $f(1)=\frac{1-a}{1-a}=1$, $f(-1)=\frac{-1-a}{1+a}=-1
$. ✓
For (F2): $f(1)=\frac{a-1}{1-a}=-1$, $f(-1)=\frac{a+1}{1+a}=1$. ✓
Both are disk automorphisms since $|a|<1$. Note (F2) with $a=0$ gives $f(z)=-z$,
 confirming the expected involution.

**Group structure.** Let $\varphi_a(z)=\frac{z-a}{1-az}$. A direct computation g
ives

$$
\varphi_a\circ\varphi_b=\varphi_{\frac{a+b}{1+ab}},
$$

the hyperbolic-tangent addition law, so $\{\varphi_a:a\in\mathbb{R},|a|<1\}$ is
a subgroup (isomorphic to $(\mathbb{R},+)$ via $a=\tanh t$). Writing $\iota(z)=-
z$ (which is $\varphi$-family member $\psi_0$ of type F2), every element of $S$
is either $\varphi_a$ or $\iota\circ\varphi_a$. Since

$$
\iota\circ\varphi_a\circ\iota(z)=-\varphi_a(-z)=\frac{z+a}{1+az}=\varphi_{-a}(z)
,
$$

the subgroup $\{\varphi_a\}$ is normal, and

$$
S\cong (\mathbb{R},+)\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.
$$

Equivalently, $S$ is the subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{D})$ consisting
 of all Möbius transformations with **real coefficients** that preserve $\mathbb
{D}$: any real-coefficient Möbius transformation preserving $\mathbb{D}$ maps $\
mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}$ to itself and $\partial\mathbb{D}$ to itself, hence map
s $\partial\mathbb{D}\cap\mathbb{R}=\{-1,1\}$ onto itself; conversely every elem
ent found above has real coefficients.

---

**Conclusion.** The group $S$ consists of exactly the Möbius transformations

$$
\boxed{\,f(z)=\varepsilon\,\frac{z-a}{1-az},\qquad \varepsilon\in\{1,-1\},\ a\in
\mathbb{R},\ |a|<1\,}
$$

i.e. the two one-parameter families $f(z)=\dfrac{z-a}{1-az}$ (fixing $1$ and $-1
$) and $f(z)=\dfrac{a-z}{1-az}$ (swapping $1$ and $-1$), with real parameter $a$
 satisfying $|a|<1$. This group is isomorphic to $(\mathbb{R},+)\rtimes\mathbb{Z
}/2\mathbb{Z}$, and coincides with the group of real-coefficient Möbius transfor
mations preserving the unit disk.
