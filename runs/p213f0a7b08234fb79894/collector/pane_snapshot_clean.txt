## Derivation of the first-order Euler-Lagrange equations on $J^1(X,Y)$

### 1. Setup and notation

Let $\pi_{XY}:Y\to X$ be a fiber bundle with $\dim X = n$, $\operatorname{rank}
Y = m$. Local coordinates on $X$: $(x^\alpha)$, $\alpha=1,\dots,n$. Adapted coor
dinates on $Y$: $(x^\alpha,y^i)$, $i=1,\dots,m$. The first jet manifold $J^1(X,Y
)$ carries adapted coordinates $(x^\alpha,y^i,y^i_\alpha)$, where $y^i_\alpha$ r
epresents $\partial y^i/\partial x^\alpha$.

The Lagrangian density is
$$L = \mathcal{L}(x^\alpha,y^i,y^i_\alpha)\,\omega,\qquad \omega = dx^1\wedge\cd
ots\wedge dx^n.$$

A section $\sigma:X\to Y$ (i.e. $\pi_{XY}\circ\sigma=\operatorname{id}_X$) has f
irst jet prolongation
$$j^1\sigma:X\to J^1(X,Y),\qquad (y^i_\alpha\circ j^1\sigma)(x)=\partial_\alpha\
sigma^i(x).$$

A section of $J^1(X,Y)\to X$ is *holonomic* iff it is of the form $j^1\sigma$ fo
r some $\sigma:X\to Y$. The variational principle is imposed over holonomic sect
ions, so the unknown is really $\sigma$, and the action functional is
$$S[\sigma]=\int_X (j^1\sigma)^*L=\int_X \mathcal{L}\bigl(x,\sigma^i(x),\partial
_\alpha\sigma^i(x)\bigr)\,d^nx.$$

### 2. Variations

Let $\sigma_\varepsilon$ be a smooth one-parameter family of sections with $\sig
ma_0=\sigma$, defined for $\varepsilon$ near $0$. The *variation field* (vertica

$$v^i(x)=\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{\varepsilon=0}\sigma_\varepsilon^i
(x),\qquad v=\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{0}j^1\sigma_\varepsilon = v^i\
frac{\partial}{\partial y^i}+(\partial_\alpha v^i)\frac{\partial}{\partial y^i_\

We take $v$ compactly supported in the interior of $X$ (so $v$ and all its deriv
atives vanish on $\partial X$, or $X$ is taken without boundary).

### 3. First variation of the action

Differentiating under the integral,
$$\left.\frac{dS[\sigma_\varepsilon]}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{0}=\int_X\left[\frac
{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial y^i}\,v^i+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial y^i
_\alpha}\,\partial_\alpha v^i\right]\omega,$$
where $\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial y^i$ and $\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial y^i_\

### 4. Integration by parts (the divergence theorem on $X$)

For the second term, use the identity on the $n$-form
$$\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial y^i_\alpha}\,\partial_\alpha v^i\,\omega =

$$\int_X \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial y^i_\alpha}\,\partial_\alpha v^i\,d

^1\wedge\cdots\widehat{dx^\alpha}\cdots\wedge dx^n \;-\;\int_X \partial_\alpha\!

The boundary integral vanishes because $v$ has compact support in $\operatorname

$$\delta S[\sigma]\cdot v = \int_X\left[\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial y^i}

### 5. The total derivative

Along $\sigma$, the function $\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial y^i_\alpha$ is a func

$$D_\alpha\!\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial y^i_\alpha}\right)=\frac{\

evaluated at $j^1\sigma$ (so $y^j_\alpha=\partial_\alpha\sigma^j$, $y^j_{\beta\a
lpha}=\partial_\beta\partial_\alpha\sigma^j$). The operator $D_\alpha$ is the fo

### 6. Fundamental lemma and the Euler-Lagrange equations

By the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations (the integrand is continu

gma]\cdot v=0$ for all such $v$ iff
$$\boxed{\;\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial y^i}-D_\alpha\!\left(\frac{\parti

with summation over $\alpha=1,\dots,n$. In fully expanded form, evaluated along

$$\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial y^i}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x^\alpha}\!\

### 7. Coordinate-free form (Poincaré-Cartan / Euler-Lagrange form)

The same equations are encoded by the *Euler-Lagrange form*
$$\mathcal{E}(L)=\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial y^i}-D_\alpha\!\frac{
\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial y^i_\alpha}\right)dy^i\wedge\omega\in\Omega^{n+1}_
{J^1}(Y),$$
a contact-horizontal $(n+1)$-form on $J^1(X,Y)$. A holonomic section $j^1\sigma$

$$(j^1\sigma)^*\,i_\xi\,\mathcal{E}(L)=0\quad\text{for every vertical vector fie
ld }\xi\text{ on }Y\to X,$$
equivalently $(j^1\sigma)^*\mathcal{E}(L)=0$ on vertical contractions, which rep

### 8. Summary

The first-order Euler-Lagrange equations for sections $\sigma:X\to Y$ (equivalen
