## Proof

**Setup.** Let $x = \frac{2\pi}{13}$. Using the identity $\cos^2\theta = \frac{1
+\cos 2\theta}{2}$:

$$S = \sum_{k\in\{1,2,4,5,6\}} \cos^2(kx) = \frac{5}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\
{1,2,4,5,6\}} \cos(2kx).$$

**Step 1: Reduce the doubled arguments.** The doubled arguments $2kx$ for $k \in
 \{1,2,4,5,6\}$ are:

$$2x,\; 4x,\; 8x,\; 10x,\; 12x = \frac{4\pi}{13},\; \frac{8\pi}{13},\; \frac{16\
pi}{13},\; \frac{20\pi}{13},\; \frac{24\pi}{13}.$$

Reducing modulo $2\pi$ (using $\cos\theta = \cos(2\pi - \theta)$):

$$\cos\frac{16\pi}{13} = \cos\frac{10\pi}{13},\quad \cos\frac{20\pi}{13} = \cos\
frac{6\pi}{13},\quad \cos\frac{24\pi}{13} = \cos\frac{2\pi}{13}.$$

So the cosine sum becomes:

$$\cos\frac{2\pi}{13} + \cos\frac{4\pi}{13} + \cos\frac{6\pi}{13} + \cos\frac{8\
pi}{13} + \cos\frac{10\pi}{13}.$$

**Step 2: Use the full sum identity.** Since $\sum_{k=0}^{12} e^{2\pi i k/13} =
0$ (sum of all 13th roots of unity), taking real parts:

$$\sum_{k=0}^{12} \cos\frac{2\pi k}{13} = 0.$$

By the symmetry $\cos\frac{2\pi k}{13} = \cos\frac{2\pi(13-k)}{13}$, this gives:

$$1 + 2\sum_{k=1}^{6} \cos\frac{2\pi k}{13} = 0 \implies \sum_{k=1}^{6} \cos\fra
c{2\pi k}{13} = -\frac{1}{2}.$$

**Step 3: Extract our sum.** Our sum from Step 1 is the full sum $\sum_{k=1}^{6}
$ **minus** the $k=3$ term (i.e., $\cos\frac{12\pi}{13}$):

$$\sum_{k\in\{1,2,4,5,6\}} \cos\frac{2\pi k}{13} = \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) - \
cos\frac{12\pi}{13}.$$

Since $\cos\frac{12\pi}{13} = \cos\!\left(\pi - \frac{\pi}{13}\right) = -\cos\fr
ac{\pi}{13}$:

$$\sum_{k\in\{1,2,4,5,6\}} \cos\frac{2\pi k}{13} = -\frac{1}{2} + \cos\frac{\pi}
{13}.$$

**Step 4: Combine.** Substituting back:

$$S = \frac{5}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\!\left(-\frac{1}{2} + \cos\frac{\pi}{13}\right)
= \frac{5}{2} - \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{2}\cos\frac{\pi}{13} = \frac{9}{4} + \fra
c{1}{2}\cos\frac{\pi}{13}.$$

**Numerical verification.** $\cos\frac{\pi}{13} \approx 0.97094$, so $S \approx
2.25 + 0.48547 = 2.73547$. Direct computation of the five squared cosines confir

$$\boxed{S = \dfrac{9}{4} + \dfrac{1}{2}\cos\dfrac{\pi}{13}}$$
