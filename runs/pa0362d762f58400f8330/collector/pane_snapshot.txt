Let $a$ be a $k$-cycle in $S_n$. By relabeling (conjugating by a fixed permutati
on), we may assume without loss of generality that
$$a = (1\ 2\ 3\ \cdots\ k).$$

The centralizer of $a$ in $S_n$ is
$$C_{S_n}(a) = \{\sigma \in S_n : \sigma a \sigma^{-1} = a\}.$$

**Step 1: Use the conjugation formula.** For any $\sigma \in S_n$, conjugation o
f a cycle acts entrywise:
$$\sigma(1\ 2\ \cdots\ k)\sigma^{-1} = (\sigma(1)\ \sigma(2)\ \cdots\ \sigma(k))
.$$

Thus $\sigma \in C_{S_n}(a)$ if and only if
$$(\sigma(1)\ \sigma(2)\ \cdots\ \sigma(k)) = (1\ 2\ \cdots\ k) \quad \text{as c
ycles.}$$

**Step 2: Determine the constraint on $\{1,\ldots,k\}$.** Two cyclic notations $
(\sigma(1)\ \cdots\ \sigma(k))$ and $(1\ 2\ \cdots\ k)$ represent the same cycle
 if and only if $(\sigma(1), \sigma(2), \ldots, \sigma(k))$ is a **cyclic rotati
on** of $(1, 2, \ldots, k)$. Hence there exists $r \in \{0, 1, \ldots, k-1\}$ su
ch that
$$\sigma(i) \equiv i + r \pmod{k}, \qquad i = 1, \ldots, k$$
(interpreting residues in $\{1,\ldots,k\}$). In particular $\sigma$ maps the set
 $\{1,\ldots,k\}$ onto itself, and the restriction $\sigma|_{\{1,\ldots,k\}}$ eq
uals the power $a^r$.

**Step 3: Determine the action on the complement.** Since $\sigma$ is a bijectio
n of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ and $\sigma(\{1,\ldots,k\}) = \{1,\ldots,k\}$, we have $\s
igma(\{k+1,\ldots,n\}) = \{k+1,\ldots,n\}$. The restriction $\sigma|_{\{k+1,\ldo
ts,n\}}$ is an **arbitrary** permutation of the remaining $n-k$ symbols.

**Step 4: Combine.** Every centralizing $\sigma$ decomposes uniquely as
$$\sigma = a^r \cdot \tau, \qquad r \in \{0,\ldots,k-1\},\quad \tau \in S_{\{k+1
,\ldots,n\}},$$
where $a^r$ acts on $\{1,\ldots,k\}$ (cyclically) and fixes $\{k+1,\ldots,n\}$,
while $\tau$ acts on $\{k+1,\ldots,n\}$ and fixes $\{1,\ldots,k\}$. These two fa
ctors commute since they have disjoint supports. Conversely, every such product
centralizes $a$ (the cyclic part commutes with $a$ since $\langle a \rangle$ is
abelian; the disjoint part commutes with $a$ since supports are disjoint).

**Conclusion.** The centralizer of a $k$-cycle $a$ in $S_n$ is
$$\boxed{C_{S_n}(a) = \langle a \rangle \times S_{\{k+1,\ldots,n\}} \;\cong\; \m
athbb{Z}_k \times S_{n-k},}$$
a direct product of the cyclic group generated by $a$ (order $k$) and the symmet
ric group on the $n-k$ symbols not moved by $a$. Its order is
$$|C_{S_n}(a)| = k \cdot (n-k)!.$$
