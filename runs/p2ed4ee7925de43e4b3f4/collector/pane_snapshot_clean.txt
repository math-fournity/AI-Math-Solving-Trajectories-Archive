## Proof

**Yes**, $\lim_{t\to\infty} e^{kt}|f(t)|^2 = 0$.

**Step 1: Reduction to a Sobolev embedding problem.**

Define
$$h(t) := e^{kt/2}\,f(t), \qquad t > 0.$$
Since $f$ is locally $H^1$ on $(0,\infty)$ and $e^{kt/2}$ is smooth, $h$ is loca
lly absolutely continuous on $(0,\infty)$, with
$$h'(t) = \tfrac{k}{2}\,e^{kt/2}f(t) + e^{kt/2}f'(t) = \tfrac{k}{2}\,h(t) + e^{k
t/2}f'(t).$$

The conclusion $\lim_{t\to\infty} e^{kt}|f(t)|^2 = 0$ is equivalent to $\lim_{t\
to\infty} |h(t)|^2 = 0$, i.e., $h(t)\to 0$.

**Step 2: $h \in H^1(0,\infty)$.**

By hypothesis,
$$\int_0^\infty |h(t)|^2\,dt = \int_0^\infty |f(t)|^2\,e^{kt}\,dt < \infty,$$
so $h \in L^2(0,\infty)$. Moreover,
$$\int_0^\infty |e^{kt/2}f'(t)|^2\,dt = \int_0^\infty |f'(t)|^2\,e^{kt}\,dt < \i
nfty,$$
so $e^{kt/2}f' \in L^2(0,\infty)$. Since $h' = \tfrac{k}{2}h + e^{kt/2}f'$ is a
sum of two $L^2(0,\infty)$ functions,
$$\int_0^\infty |h'(t)|^2\,dt \leq 2\Big(\tfrac{k^2}{4}\int_0^\infty |h|^2 + \in
t_0^\infty |e^{kt/2}f'|^2\Big) < \infty.$$
Hence $h \in H^1(0,\infty) := \{u \in L^2(0,\infty) : u' \in L^2(0,\infty)\}$.

**Step 3: Sobolev embedding $H^1(0,\infty) \hookrightarrow C_0([0,\infty))$.**

We show every $u \in H^1(0,\infty)$ satisfies $u(t)\to 0$ as $t\to\infty$.

*Continuity.* For $0 < s < t$,
$$|u(t)-u(s)| = \Big|\int_s^t u'(\tau)\,d\tau\Big| \leq \sqrt{t-s}\,\|u'\|_{L^2(
s,t)} \leq \sqrt{t-s}\,\|u'\|_{L^2(0,\infty)}.$$
Thus $u$ is (Hölder-$1/2$) continuous, in particular uniformly continuous on $(0
,\infty)$.

*Vanishing at infinity.* Suppose for contradiction that $u(t)\not\to 0$. Then th
ere exist $\varepsilon > 0$ and a sequence $t_n \to \infty$ with $|u(t_n)| \geq
\varepsilon$. By uniform continuity, there exists $\delta > 0$ such that $|u(t)-
u(s)| < \varepsilon/2$ whenever $|t-s|<\delta$. Hence $|u(t)| \geq \varepsilon/2
$ for all $t \in [t_n, t_n+\delta]$. Choosing a subsequence with $t_{n+1} > t_n
+ \delta$ (possible since $t_n\to\infty$), the intervals $[t_n, t_n+\delta]$ are
 disjoint, giving
$$\int_0^\infty |u(t)|^2\,dt \geq \sum_n \int_{t_n}^{t_n+\delta} |u(t)|^2\,dt \g
eq \sum_n \delta\cdot \frac{\varepsilon^2}{4} = \infty,$$
contradicting $u \in L^2(0,\infty)$. Therefore $u(t)\to 0$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Applying Step 3 to $h \in H^1(0,\infty)$ gives $h(t)\to 0$ as $t\to\infty$. Cons
equently
$$e^{kt}|f(t)|^2 = |h(t)|^2 \longrightarrow 0 \qquad (t\to\infty).$$

$\blacksquare$
