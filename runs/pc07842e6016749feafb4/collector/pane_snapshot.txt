## Proof

**Claim:** The series $\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} E_k(x)$ diverges for every $x \in \ma
thbb{R}$. Equivalently, the set of values of $x$ for which it converges is $\emp
tyset$.

**Key tool:** A necessary condition for any series $\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} a_k$ to
converge is that $a_k \to 0$ as $k \to \infty$. We show this necessary condition
 is **never** satisfied.

**Argument by contradiction.** Suppose there exists $x \in \mathbb{R}$ such that
 $E_k(x) \to 0$ as $k \to \infty$.

Since $E_{k+1}(x) = e^{E_k(x)}$ and the exponential function $e^t$ is continuous
 on $\mathbb{R}$, we apply the continuous mapping theorem:

$$E_{k+1}(x) = e^{E_k(x)} \longrightarrow e^{0} = 1 \quad \text{as } k \to \inft
y. \tag{1}$$

On the other hand, the sequence $\{E_{k+1}(x)\}_{k=0}^{\infty}$ is simply $\{E_k
(x)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}$—the original sequence with its first term removed. This is
 a subsequence of $\{E_k(x)\}_{k=0}^{\infty}$. Since we assumed $E_k(x) \to 0$,
every subsequence must converge to the same limit, so:

$$E_{k+1}(x) \longrightarrow 0 \quad \text{as } k \to \infty. \tag{2}$$

Combining (1) and (2): $0 = 1$, a contradiction.

**Conclusion:** For every $x \in \mathbb{R}$, the sequence $E_k(x)$ does **not**
 tend to $0$. Since $E_k(x) \to 0$ is a necessary condition for the convergence
of $\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} E_k(x)$, the series diverges for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

**Remark.** The same argument applies to complex $x$ as well: $e^z$ is continuou
s on $\mathbb{C}$ and $e^0 = 1$, so the identical contradiction arises.

The set of values of $x$ for which the series converges is $\boxed{\emptyset}$.
