## Proof

We determine the **global dimension** (projective dimension of the ring) of $A =
 \mathbb{Z}_l[T, T^{-1}]$.

### Step 1: Global dimension of $\mathbb{Z}_l$

$\mathbb{Z}_l$, the ring of $l$-adic integers, is a **complete discrete valuatio
n ring (DVR)**. Its unique maximal ideal is $l\mathbb{Z}_l$, and it is a princip
al ideal domain (every ideal is $l^n \mathbb{Z}_l$ for some $n \geq 0$). Since $
\mathbb{Z}_l$ is a PID but not a field, every module has a projective resolution
 of length $\leq 1$, and there exist modules (e.g., the residue field $\mathbb{F
}_l = \mathbb{Z}_l/l\mathbb{Z}_l$) with projective dimension exactly 1. Thus:

$$\operatorname{gl.dim}(\mathbb{Z}_l) = 1.$$

### Step 2: Upper bound via the Laurent extension theorem

$\mathbb{Z}_l$ is commutative Noetherian with finite global dimension. The class
ical theorem on global dimension of Laurent polynomial extensions (see McConnell
–Robson, *Noncommutative Noetherian Rings*, or Rotman, *An Introduction to Homol
ogical Algebra*, §8) states:

> **Theorem.** If $R$ is a (right) Noetherian ring with $\operatorname{gl.dim}(R
) = d < \infty$, then $\operatorname{gl.dim}(R[T, T^{-1}]) = d + 1$.

Applying this with $R = \mathbb{Z}_l$ and $d = 1$:

$$\operatorname{gl.dim}(A) = \operatorname{gl.dim}(\mathbb{Z}_l[T, T^{-1}]) = 1
+ 1 = 2.$$

### Step 3: Direct verification of the lower bound (change of rings)

For completeness, we verify $\operatorname{gl.dim}(A) \geq 2$ directly, without
appealing to the full theorem.

Let $k = \mathbb{Z}_l / l\mathbb{Z}_l \cong \mathbb{F}_l$ be the residue field.
Since $l$ is a non-zero-divisor in $A$ (as $A$ is an integral domain), we have t
he short exact sequence of $A$-modules:

$$0 \to A \xrightarrow{\cdot\, l} A \to A/lA \to 0.$$

Since $A$ is free (hence projective) over itself and $lA$ is not a direct summan
d of $A$ (as $l$ is not a unit), $\operatorname{pd}_A(A/lA) = 1$.

Now $A/lA \cong k[T, T^{-1}]$, which is a **principal ideal domain** (it is a lo
calization of the PID $k[T]$). Hence $\operatorname{gl.dim}(k[T, T^{-1}]) = 1$,
and there exist $k[T,T^{-1}]$-modules of projective dimension 1 — for instance,
$k \cong k[T,T^{-1}]/(T-1)k[T,T^{-1}]$.

By the **change of rings theorem** (Cartan–Eilenberg, or see Rotman §8): if $x$
is a non-zero-divisor in $R$ and $M$ is an $R/xR$-module with $\operatorname{pd}
_{R/xR}(M) < \infty$, then

$$\operatorname{pd}_R(M) = \operatorname{pd}_{R/xR}(M) + 1.$$

Applying this with $R = A$, $x = l$, and $M = k$ (viewed as an $A/lA$-module of
projective dimension 1):

$$\operatorname{pd}_A(k) = \operatorname{pd}_{A/lA}(k) + 1 = 1 + 1 = 2.$$

Therefore $\operatorname{gl.dim}(A) \geq 2$.

### Step 4: Upper bound via localization

$A = \mathbb{Z}_l[T, T^{-1}]$ is the localization of $\mathbb{Z}_l[T]$ at the mu
ltiplicative set $S = \{1, T, T^2, \ldots\}$. By the polynomial extension theore
m (Hilbert syzygy, Noetherian version):

$$\operatorname{gl.dim}(\mathbb{Z}_l[T]) = \operatorname{gl.dim}(\mathbb{Z}_l) +
 1 = 2.$$

Localization can only decrease or preserve global dimension, so:

$$\operatorname{gl.dim}(A) \leq \operatorname{gl.dim}(\mathbb{Z}_l[T]) = 2.$$

### Conclusion

Combining Steps 3 and 4:

$$\operatorname{gl.dim}(\mathbb{Z}_l[T, T^{-1}]) = 2.$$

The projective dimension (global dimension) of $A = \mathbb{Z}_l[T, T^{-1}]$ is
$\boxed{2}$.
