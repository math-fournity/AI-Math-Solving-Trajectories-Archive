## Proof

Consider the system
$$
\dot x = x - y - x(x^2+y^2), \qquad \dot y = x + y - y(x^2+y^2).
$$

**Step 1: Convert to polar coordinates.** Let $x = r\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\thet
a$, so $r^2 = x^2+y^2$.

**Step 2: Radial equation.** Using $r\dot r = x\dot x + y\dot y$:
$$
x\dot x + y\dot y = x(x-y-xr^2)+y(x+y-yr^2)
= x^2+y^2 - r^2(x^2+y^2)
= r^2 - r^4 = r^2(1-r^2).
$$
Hence
$$
\dot r = r(1-r^2).
$$

**Step 3: Angular equation.** Using $\dot\theta = \dfrac{x\dot y - y\dot x}{r^2}
$:
$$
x\dot y - y\dot x = x(x+y-yr^2)-y(x-y-xr^2)
= x^2+xy-xyr^2-xy+y^2+xyr^2
= x^2+y^2 = r^2.
$$
Hence
$$
\dot\theta = 1.
$$

**Step 4: Analyze the decoupled polar system**
$$
\dot r = r(1-r^2), \qquad \dot\theta = 1.
$$

The radial equation has equilibria at $r=0$ and $r=1$.

- At $r=0$: for small $r>0$, $\dot r \approx r > 0$, so $r=0$ is **unstable** (t
he origin is an unstable spiral since $\dot\theta=1\neq 0$).
- At $r=1$: for $r$ slightly less than $1$, $\dot r > 0$; for $r$ slightly great
er than $1$, $\dot r < 0$. Thus $r=1$ is a **stable** equilibrium of the radial
equation.

Since $\dot\theta = 1$ is constant and nonzero, every trajectory with $r>0$ rota
tes with constant angular velocity while $r(t)\to 1$ as $t\to\infty$.

**Step 5: Identify the limit cycle.** The set $r=1$, i.e. the unit circle $x^2+y
^2=1$, is a **periodic orbit**: $r(t)\equiv 1$ and $\theta(t) = t+\theta_0$, giv
ing closed trajectories of period $2\pi$.

It is **isolated**: the only equilibria of the radial equation are $r=0$ (a fixe
d point, not a periodic orbit) and $r=1$. No other value of $r$ yields a closed
orbit, so there is no periodic orbit in any punctured neighborhood of $r=1$.

Moreover, every trajectory starting with $r_0\in(0,\infty)$, $r_0\neq 1$, spiral
s toward the unit circle as $t\to\infty$, confirming it attracts all nontrivial
orbits.

**Conclusion.** The system possesses a (stable) limit cycle, namely the unit cir
cle
$$
\boxed{x^2+y^2=1.}
$$
