**Claim.** The solutions are exactly $f(x) = k\,\overline{x}$ for a constant $k\
in\mathbb{C}$ with $|k|=1$.

---

### Proof

Denote the equation by $P(x,y):\ f(f(x)+yf(y))=x+|y|^{2}$.

**Step 1 — Surjectivity.** Fix $y$. As $x$ ranges over $\mathbb{C}$, the right s
ide $x+|y|^{2}$ ranges over all of $\mathbb{C}$, so $f$ is surjective. Pick $a$
with $f(a)=0$; then $P(a,y)$ gives $f(yf(y))=a+|y|^{2}$.

**Step 2 — Involution.** $P(x,0):\ f(f(x))=x$ for all $x$. Hence $f$ is bijectiv
e and $f^{-1}=f$.

**Step 3 — A shifted relation.** Apply $f$ to both sides of $P(x,y)$ and use the
 involution on the argument $f(x)+yf(y)$:
$$f\bigl(x+|y|^{2}\bigr)=f\bigl(f(f(x)+yf(y))\bigr)=f(x)+yf(y). \tag{E}$$

**Step 4 — Determining $f(0)$.** Set $x=0$ in (E): $f(|y|^{2})=f(0)+yf(y)$. Let
$c:=f(0)$ and $g:=f-c$, so $g(0)=0$ and
$$yf(y)=f(|y|^{2})-c=g(|y|^{2}). \tag{1}$$
Equation (E) becomes $g(x+|y|^{2})=g(x)+g(|y|^{2})$, i.e.
$$g(x+t)=g(x)+g(t)\qquad(x\in\mathbb{C},\ t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}). \tag{2}$$
Taking $x=-t$ gives $g(-t)=-g(t)$ for $t\ge0$, so (2) extends to all real $t$:
$$g(x+t)=g(x)+g(t)\qquad(x\in\mathbb{C},\ t\in\mathbb{R}). \tag{2'}$$

Write $x=a+bi$ with $a,b\in\mathbb{R}$. By (2'), $g(a+bi)=g(bi)+g(a)$. For pure
imaginary arguments, use (1) with $y=it$ and with $y=t$ (both give $|y|^{2}=t^{2
}$):
$$it\,g(it)+c\,it=t\,g(t)+ct\ \Longrightarrow\ g(it)=-i\,g(t)-c(1+i).$$
Therefore
$$g(a+bi)=g(a)-i\,g(b)-c(1+i).$$
Setting $a=b=0$ and using $g(0)=0$ yields $c(1+i)=0$, hence
$$c=f(0)=0,\qquad\text{so }g=f.$$

**Step 5 — $f$ is conjugation up to a constant.** With $c=0$, let $\alpha:\mathb
b{R}\to\mathbb{C}$ be $\alpha(t)=f(t)$; by (2') $\alpha$ is additive on $\mathbb
{R}$, and
$$f(a+bi)=\alpha(a)-i\alpha(b). \tag{3}$$
Equation (1) (now $yf(y)=f(|y|^{2})$) with $y=a+bi$ gives, using $|y|^{2}=a^{2}+
b^{2}$ and additivity $\alpha(a^{2}+b^{2})=\alpha(a^{2})+\alpha(b^{2})$:
$$(a+bi)\bigl(\alpha(a)-i\alpha(b)\bigr)=\alpha(a^{2})+\alpha(b^{2}).$$
Expanding the left side:
$$\bigl(a\alpha(a)+b\alpha(b)\bigr)+i\bigl(b\alpha(a)-a\alpha(b)\bigr)=\alpha(a^
{2})+\alpha(b^{2}).$$
Taking $b=0$ gives $\alpha(a^{2})=a\alpha(a)$ for all $a$, so the real parts mat
ch automatically. The imaginary part forces
$$b\,\alpha(a)=a\,\alpha(b)\qquad\forall\,a,b\in\mathbb{R}.$$
For $a,b\neq0$ this says $\alpha(a)/a=\alpha(b)/b$ is constant; call it $k\in\ma
thbb{C}$. Then $\alpha(t)=kt$ for all $t\in\mathbb{R}$, and (3) becomes
$$f(a+bi)=ka-ikb=k\,\overline{a+bi}.$$
Thus $f(x)=k\,\overline{x}$ for some constant $k\in\mathbb{C}$.

**Step 6 — Pinning down $k$.** The involution $f(f(x))=x$ gives
$$f(k\overline{x})=k\,\overline{k\overline{x}}=k\overline{k}\,x=|k|^{2}x=x\qquad
\forall x,$$
so $|k|^{2}=1$, i.e. $|k|=1$.

**Step 7 — Verification.** For $f(x)=k\overline{x}$ with $|k|=1$:
$$f\bigl(f(x)+yf(y)\bigr)=f\bigl(k\overline{x}+yk\overline{y}\bigr)=f\bigl(k(\ov
erline{x}+|y|^{2})\bigr)=k\,\overline{k}\,(\overline{\overline{x}+|y|^{2}})=|k|^
{2}(x+|y|^{2})=x+|y|^{2},$$
since $|y|^{2}\in\mathbb{R}$. ✓

---

**Conclusion.** All functions $f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}$ satisfying the equatio
n are
$$\boxed{f(x)=k\,\overline{x}\quad\text{for any }k\in\mathbb{C}\text{ with }|k|=
1.}$$
