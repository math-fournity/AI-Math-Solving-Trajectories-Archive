## Statement

If $R_P$, the localization of a ring $R$ at $P \in \operatorname{Spec}(R)$, is a
 regular local ring, then $R$ is a regular local ring.

## Verdict: **False**

## Counterexample

Let $k$ be a field and set

$$
A = k[x,y]/(x^2,\,xy), \qquad R = A_{(x,y)}.
$$

Then $R$ is a local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m = (x,y)R$. I claim:

1. $R$ is **not** a regular local ring, yet
2. there exists a prime $P \in \operatorname{Spec}(R)$, namely $P = (x)R$, such
that $R_P$ **is** a regular local ring.

This disproves the statement.

---

### Step 1. The prime spectrum of $A$ (and hence of $R$)

In $k[x,y]$, the ideal $(x^2, xy) = x\cdot(x,y)$ has radical $(x)$. Therefore th
e primes of $A = k[x,y]/(x^2,xy)$ correspond to primes of $k[x,y]$ containing $(
x)$. These are

$$
(x) \quad\text{and}\quad (x,\,y-a)\ \text{for } a\in k .
$$

The primes of $R = A_{(x,y)}$ are those primes of $A$ contained in $(x,y)$, name
ly

$$
(x)R \;\subset\; \mathfrak m = (x,y)R .
$$

In particular $\dim R = 1$ (the longest chain is $(x)R \subset \mathfrak m$).

### Step 2. $R$ is not regular

Compute $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$ as a vector space over the residue field $\k
appa = R/\mathfrak m \cong k$.

Since $x^2 = 0$ and $xy = 0$ in $R$,

$$
\mathfrak m^2 = (x^2,\, xy,\, y^2)R = (y^2)R .
$$

Hence $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$ is spanned by the images of $x$ and $y$, and t
hese are linearly independent over $k$ (no nontrivial $k$-linear combination of
$x, y$ lies in $(y^2)$). Thus

$$
\dim_k \mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2 = 2 .
$$

A local ring is regular iff $\dim_k \mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2 = \dim R$. Here $2
 \neq 1$, so **$R$ is not a regular local ring**.

### Step 3. $R_P$ is regular local for $P = (x)R$

Localize $R$ at $P = (x)R$. The element $y \in R$ does **not** lie in $P$ (since
 $y \notin (x)$ in $A$), so $y$ becomes a unit in $R_P$. The relation $xy = 0$ t
hen forces $x = 0$ in $R_P$. Consequently

$$
R_P \;\cong\; \bigl(k[y]\bigr)_{(0)} \;=\; k(y),
$$

the fraction field of $k[y]$. A field is a regular local ring (it has dimension
$0$ and maximal ideal $(0)$, generated by $0$ elements).

### Step 4. Conclusion

We have exhibited a local ring $R$ and a prime $P \in \operatorname{Spec}(R)$ su
ch that $R_P$ is a regular local ring while $R$ itself is **not** a regular loca
l ring. Therefore the statement

> "If $R_P$ is a regular local ring, then $R$ is a regular local ring"

is **false**. $\blacksquare$
