### Key Lemma

**Lemma.** *In $M = L[G]$, for no set $s \in M$ does there exist a definable (fr
om parameter $s$) well-order of all of $V^M$.*

**Proof of Lemma.** We establish two facts and derive a contradiction.

**Fact 1.** *If there exists a definable well-order $<$ of $V$ from set paramete

*Proof of Fact 1.* Suppose $<$ is definable from $s$. Then every set $x \in V$ i
s the $\alpha$-th element of $<$ for some ordinal $\alpha$, so $x$ is ordinal-de
finable from $s$: $x \in \mathrm{OD}[s]$. Moreover, every element of $x$ is like
wise the $\beta$-th element of $<$ for some $\beta$, so it too lies in $\mathrm{
OD}[s]$. By induction on the transitive closure, $x \in \mathrm{HOD}[s]$. Hence
$V \subseteq \mathrm{HOD}[s]$, and since $\mathrm{HOD}[s] \subseteq V$ always, w
e get $V = \mathrm{HOD}[s]$. $\square$

**Fact 2.** *For every set $s \in M$, $V^M \neq \mathrm{HOD}[s]^M$.*

*Proof of Fact 2.* Since $\mathbb{P} = \mathrm{Add}(\omega,\omega_1)$ is ccc, ev
ery set in $M = L[G]$ is determined by countably many coordinates of $G$. More p
recisely, for every $s \in M$ there exists a countable $A \subseteq \omega_1$ su

Fix such an $s$ and $A$. Pick $\alpha \in \omega_1 \setminus A$. We claim the Co
hen real $G(\alpha) \notin \mathrm{OD}[s]^M$.

Consider the factorization $\mathbb{P} \cong \mathrm{Add}(\omega, A) \times \mat
hrm{Add}(\omega, \omega_1 \setminus A)$. Over the ground model $N := L[G \upharp

tminus A)$ is a **homogeneous** forcing (it is a product of Cohen forcings, and
the automorphism group acts transitively on generics: for any two coordinates $\
alpha, \beta \in \omega_1 \setminus A$, there is an automorphism of $\mathbb{Q}$

Suppose for contradiction that $G(\alpha) \in \mathrm{OD}[s]^M$, i.e., there is
a formula $\varphi$ and ordinals $\gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_n$ such that $G(\alpha

By the homogeneity of $\mathbb{Q}$ over $N$, the automorphism $\pi$ of $\mathbb{
Q}$ that swaps coordinates $\alpha$ and $\beta$ (for any $\beta \in \omega_1 \se

$$M \models \varphi(G(\alpha), s, \vec\gamma) \iff M \models \varphi(G(\beta), s
, \vec\gamma).$$

But $\varphi(\cdot, s, \vec\gamma)$ uniquely defines a set, so $G(\alpha) = G(\b
eta)$. This is a contradiction, since $G(\alpha)$ and $G(\beta)$ are distinct Co
hen reals (they are generic for independent coordinates). Hence $G(\alpha) \noti
n \mathrm{OD}[s]^M$, and therefore $G(\alpha) \notin \mathrm{HOD}[s]^M$, giving
$V^M \neq \mathrm{HOD}[s]^M$. $\square$

**Combining Facts 1 and 2:** If a definable well-order from $s$ existed, Fact 1
would give $V = \mathrm{HOD}[s]$, contradicting Fact 2. So no definable well-ord

### Building the GB Model

Let $\mathrm{Def}(M)$ denote the collection of all subclasses of $M$ that are fi
rst-order definable over $M$ with set parameters. A standard result in class the
ory (see, e.g., Felgner's work on GB) states:

**Theorem.** *If $M \models \mathsf{ZFC}$, then $(M, \mathrm{Def}(M)) \models \m
athsf{GB}$.*

The class comprehension axioms hold because definable classes are closed under f
irst-order operations; the set axioms hold because $M \models \mathsf{ZFC}$; cla
ss replacement and class foundation follow from their set-level counterparts.

Now consider the structure $\mathcal{M} := (M, \mathrm{Def}(M))$.

- **$\mathcal{M} \models \mathsf{GB} + \mathsf{AC}$:** Since $M \models \mathsf{
ZFC} + \mathsf{AC}$, the set-level axioms including AC hold, and $(M, \mathrm{De
f}(M)) \models \mathsf{GB}$ by the theorem above.

- **$\mathcal{M} \not\models \mathsf{Global\ Choice}$:** Global Choice in $\math
cal{M}$ asserts the existence of a *class* $F \in \mathrm{Def}(M)$ that bijects
$V$ with $\mathrm{ORD}$. But every class in $\mathrm{Def}(M)$ is definable from
set parameters. A class bijection $V \leftrightarrow \mathrm{ORD}$ would in part
icular yield a definable well-order of $V$ from some set parameter $s$. By the K
ey Lemma, no such definable well-order exists. Hence Global Choice fails.

The structure $\mathcal{M} = (L[G], \mathrm{Def}(L[G]))$, where $G$ is generic f
