## Proof

We consider the diffusion (heat) equation

$$u_t = \kappa\, u_{xx}, \qquad u(x,0)=F(x), \qquad -\infty < x < \infty,$$

with $\kappa > 0$ a fixed constant. The solution is given by convolution with th
e Gauss–Weierstrass (heat) kernel:

$$u(x,t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} G(x-y,\,t)\,F(y)\,dy, \qquad G(z,t)=\frac{1}{
\sqrt{4\pi\kappa t}}\,e^{-z^{2}/(4\kappa t)}, \quad t>0. \tag{1}$$

The key property of the kernel is:

$$G(z,t) > 0 \quad \text{for every } z\in\mathbb{R} \text{ and every } t>0. \tag
{2}$$

---

### (a) $u(x,t)$ depends on all the initial data $F(x)$, $-\infty < x < \infty$.

Fix $t>0$ and $x\in\mathbb{R}$. From (1) the value $u(x,t)$ is a weighted averag
e of $F$ over the entire real line, with weight density $G(x-y,t)$ at the point
$y$. By (2) this weight density is **strictly positive at every** $y$.

To show that $u(x,t)$ genuinely depends on the data at an arbitrary point $y_0$,
 suppose we alter the initial data only on a small open interval $I=(y_0-\delta,
\,y_0+\delta)$ surrounding $y_0$, replacing $F$ by $\tilde F$ with $\tilde F = F
$ outside $I$ and $\tilde F \neq F$ on a subset of $I$ of positive measure. The
corresponding solutions satisfy

$$\tilde u(x,t)-u(x,t) = \int_{I} G(x-y,t)\bigl[\tilde F(y)-F(y)\bigr]\,dy.$$

Since $G(x-y,t)>0$ for all $y\in I$ (by (2)) and $\tilde F - F$ is nonzero on a
set of positive measure, the integral is (in absolute value) strictly positive:

$$\bigl|\tilde u(x,t)-u(x,t)\bigr| > 0.$$

Thus changing the data on **any** open interval—no matter how far from $x$ or ho
w small—changes $u(x,t)$. Equivalently, the support of the kernel $G(x-\cdot,t)$
 is all of $\mathbb{R}$, so every point of the initial line contributes to $u(x,
t)$. Hence for each $t>0$ and every $x$, the value $u(x,t)$ depends on **all** t
he initial data $F(x)$, $-\infty<x<\infty$. $\quad\blacksquare$

---

### (b) For the data (4), $u(x,t)>0$ for all $x$ and every $t>0$, no matter how
small $\varepsilon>0$.

With

$$F(x)=\begin{cases}1,&|x|<\varepsilon,\\[2pt]0,&|x|\ge\varepsilon,\end{cases}$$

formula (1) reduces to

$$u(x,t)=\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon} G(x-y,t)\,dy. \tag{3}$$

The domain of integration is the interval $(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$, which ha
s measure $2\varepsilon>0$. By property (2), the integrand $G(x-y,t)$ is **stric
tly positive** for every $y\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$ and every $t>0$. A con
tinuous (in $y$) strictly positive function integrated over a set of positive me
asure yields a strictly positive result:

$$u(x,t)=\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\underbrace{G(x-y,t)}_{>\,0}\,dy \;>\;
 0, \qquad \forall\,x\in\mathbb{R},\;\forall\,t>0.$$

This holds for **every** $x$, no matter how large $|x|$ is: although $G(x-y,t)$
becomes exponentially small when $|x-y|$ is large, it is never zero, and the int
egral over the fixed positive-measure interval $(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$ rema
ins strictly positive. It also holds for **every** $\varepsilon>0$, however smal
l, since the only requirement is that the integration set have positive measure,
 which $2\varepsilon>0$ guarantees. $\quad\blacksquare$

---

**Interpretation.** Parts (a) and (b) together exhibit the **infinite speed of p
ropagation** characteristic of diffusion: an initial disturbance confined to an
arbitrarily small neighborhood $(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$ is felt **instantane
ously** at **every** point of the line—$u(x,t)>0$ for all $x$ the moment $t>0$.
There is no finite wavefront beyond which the disturbance has not yet arrived; t
he heat kernel has full support for every $t>0$, in contrast to the wave equatio
n where signals propagate at finite speed.
