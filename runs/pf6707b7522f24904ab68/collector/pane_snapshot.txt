# Proof

**Claim.** The supremum of all ordinals admitting an order-preserving injection
into $[0,1]$ is $\omega_1$, the first uncountable ordinal.

---

## Step 1. Only countable ordinals embed into $[0,1]$.

Let $\alpha$ be an ordinal and $f:\alpha\to[0,1]$ a strictly increasing (hence o
rder-preserving) injection. The image $W:=f[\alpha]\subseteq[0,1]$ is well-order
ed by the usual order on $\mathbb{R}$, since $\alpha$ is well-ordered and $f$ is
 an order-isomorphism onto its image.

**Lemma.** Every well-ordered subset $W\subseteq[0,1]$ is countable.

*Proof of lemma.* In a well-ordered set, every element except the maximum has an
 immediate successor. For each $w\in W$ that is not the maximum, let $w^+$ denot
e its successor in $W$, and consider the open interval $(w,w^+)\subseteq[0,1]$.
These intervals are pairwise disjoint (they sit between consecutive elements of
$W$). Since $\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]$ is dense, each $(w,w^+)$ contains a rational $
q_w$. The map $w\mapsto q_w$ is injective (disjoint intervals give distinct rati
onals), so $W\setminus\{\max W\}$ injects into the countable set $\mathbb{Q}$. H
ence $W$ is countable. $\square$

Since $|W|=|\alpha|$ and $W$ is countable, $\alpha$ is countable.

## Step 2. Every countable ordinal embeds into $[0,1]$.

Every countable linear order embeds into $\mathbb{Q}$ (a classical theorem of Ca
ntor). Every countable ordinal is a countable linear order, so it embeds order-p
reservingly into $\mathbb{Q}\subset[0,1]$. Hence every countable ordinal admits
an order-preserving injection into $[0,1]$.

## Step 3. The supremum is $\omega_1$.

From Steps 1 and 2, the ordinals admitting an order-preserving injection into $[
0,1]$ are **exactly** the countable ordinals, i.e., the ordinals $\alpha<\omega_
1$.

- $\omega_1$ is an upper bound: every countable ordinal is strictly less than $\
omega_1$ by definition.
- $\omega_1$ is the *least* upper bound: if $\beta<\omega_1$, then $\beta$ is co
untable, so $\beta+1$ is also countable and $<\omega_1$; thus $\beta$ is not an
upper bound.

Therefore $\sup\{\alpha:\exists\text{ order-preserving injection }\alpha\hookrig
htarrow[0,1]\}=\omega_1$.

## Step 4. Comparison with the alternatives.

- **First uncountable ordinal** = $\omega_1$. This is the answer.
- **Initial ordinal of $2^{\aleph_0}$**: this is the least ordinal of cardinalit
y $2^{\aleph_0}$. It equals $\omega_1$ only under the Continuum Hypothesis; in g
eneral it is $\geq\omega_1$ and may be strictly larger. It is not the correct an
swer in ZFC.

The key structural reason the answer is $\omega_1$ and not the initial ordinal o
f $2^{\aleph_0}$: the obstruction to embedding is **separability** of $[0,1]$ (c
ountable dense subset), which bounds well-ordered subsets by countability — not
the cardinality $2^{\aleph_0}$ of the continuum. A linear order of size $2^{\ale
ph_0}$ can still contain no uncountable well-ordered suborder, precisely because
 it is separable.

---

**Conclusion.** The supremum is $\omega_1$, the **first uncountable ordinal** —
not the initial ordinal of $2^{\aleph_0}$ (unless CH happens to hold, in which c
ase they coincide), and not anything else.
