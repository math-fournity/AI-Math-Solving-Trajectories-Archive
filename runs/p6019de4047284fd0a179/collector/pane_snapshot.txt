## Proof

**Setup.** Place $n$ points $P_1, \dots, P_n$ independently and uniformly on a c
ircle. Let $a_i$ be the arc length (as a fraction of the full circumference) fro
m $P_i$ to $P_{i+1}$ traveling around the perimeter, with indices taken mod $n$.
 Then $a_i \geq 0$ and $\sum_{i=1}^n a_i = 1$.

The joint distribution of $(a_1, \dots, a_n)$ is $\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1, \d
ots, 1)$, i.e., uniform on the $(n{-1)$-simplex.

**Interior angle formula.** The interior angle at vertex $P_i$ is the inscribed
angle $\angle P_{i-1} P_i P_{i+1}$, which by the inscribed angle theorem equals
half the central angle subtended by the arc from $P_{i-1}$ to $P_{i+1}$ *not con
taining* $P_i$. That arc has measure $2\pi(1 - a_{i-1} - a_i)$, so

$$\theta_i = \pi(1 - a_{i-1} - a_i).$$

This angle is acute ($< \pi/2$) if and only if

$$a_{i-1} + a_i > \tfrac{1}{2}.$$

**Defining events.** Let $A_i$ be the event that the angle at $P_i$ is acute, i.
e., $a_{i-1} + a_i > 1/2$. We seek $P\!\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^n A_i\right)$.

**Key observation: mutual exclusivity of non-adjacent events.** If $A_i$ and $A_
j$ involve *disjoint* pairs of arc variables — meaning $\{i{-}1, i\} \cap \{j{-}
1, j\} = \emptyset$ — then $a_{i-1}+a_i > 1/2$ and $a_{j-1}+a_j > 1/2$ force fou
r distinct variables to sum to $> 1$, contradicting $\sum a_k = 1$. Hence $A_i \
cap A_j = \emptyset$.

For $n \geq 4$, the pairs $\{i{-}1, i\}$ and $\{j{-}1, j\}$ are disjoint wheneve
r $|i - j| \geq 2$ (mod $n$), since the cyclic wraparound doesn't cause overlap.
 Thus the **only** non-empty pairwise intersections are $A_i \cap A_{i+1}$ (adja
cent events sharing one variable).

**No triple intersections.** Any three events $A_i, A_j, A_k$ with $n \geq 4$ mu
st include at least two with disjoint variable pairs (among three consecutive-pa
ir events on a cycle of length $\geq 4$, at least two pairs are disjoint), forci
ng a contradiction. So all triple-and-higher intersections are empty.

**Inclusion–exclusion** therefore stops at second order:

$$P\!\left(\bigcup A_i\right) = \sum_i P(A_i) - \sum_i P(A_i \cap A_{i+1}).$$

**Computing $P(A_i)$.** The marginal $(a_i, a_{i+1}, 1 - a_i - a_{i+1}) \sim \op
eratorname{Dirichlet}(1,1,n{-}2)$, giving density $(n{-}1)(n{-}2)(1-s)^{n-3}$ fo
r $s = a_i + a_{i+1}$. Then

$$P(A_i) = (n{-}1)(n{-}2)\int_{1/2}^{1} s(1-s)^{n-3}\,ds = (n{-}1)(n{-}2)\left[\
frac{(1/2)^{n-2}}{n-2} - \frac{(1/2)^{n-1}}{n-1}\right] = \frac{n}{2^{n-1}}.$$

**Computing $P(A_i \cap A_{i+1})$.** We need $P(a_{i-1}+a_i > 1/2,\; a_i + a_{i+
1} > 1/2)$. Set $u = a_{i-1}+a_i$, $v = a_i + a_{i+1}$, $w = a_i$ (Jacobian $= 1
$). The marginal of $(a_{i-1}, a_i, a_{i+1})$ is $\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1,1,1
,n{-}3)$ with density $(n{-}1)(n{-}2)(n{-}3)(1{-}u{-}v{+}w)^{n-4}$. Over the reg
ion $u, v \in (1/2, 1]$, $\max(0, u{+}v{-}1) \leq w \leq \min(u,v)$:

$$P(A_i \cap A_{i+1}) = (n{-}1)(n{-}2)(n{-}3)\int_{1/2}^{1}\!\!\int_{1/2}^{1}\in
t_{u+v-1}^{\min(u,v)}(1{-}u{-}v{+}w)^{n-4}\,dw\,dv\,du.$$

The inner integral evaluates to $\frac{(1-\max(u,v))^{n-3}}{n-3}$. By symmetry:

$$= 2(n{-}1)(n{-}2)\int_{1/2}^{1}(u - \tfrac12)(1-u)^{n-3}\,du = 2(n{-}1)(n{-}2)
\left[\frac{(1/2)^{n-2}}{2(n-2)} - \frac{(1/2)^{n-1}}{n-1}\right] = \frac{1}{2^{
n-2}}.$$

**Assembling.** There are $n$ events and $n$ adjacent pairs, so

$$P\!\left(\bigcup A_i\right) = n \cdot \frac{n}{2^{n-1}} - n \cdot \frac{1}{2^{
n-2}} = \frac{n^2}{2^{n-1}} - \frac{2n}{2^{n-1}} = \frac{n(n-2)}{2^{n-1}}.$$

**Verification.**
- $n = 4$: $\frac{4 \cdot 2}{8} = 1$. A cyclic quadrilateral has opposite angles
 summing to $180°$; if all angles $\geq 90°$ then all equal $90°$ (a rectangle),
 which occurs with probability $0$. So $P = 1$. ✓
- $n = 5$: $\frac{15}{16} < 1$. ✓
- $n \to \infty$: $\sim n^2/2^{n-1} \to 0$, consistent with angles approaching $
\pi$. ✓

$$\boxed{P(\text{at least one acute interior angle}) = \dfrac{n(n-2)}{2^{n-1}}}$
$
