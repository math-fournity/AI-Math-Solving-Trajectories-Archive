## Proof

We find the highest power of $2$ dividing $3^{1024}-1$, i.e. we compute $v_2(3^{
1024}-1)$.

We use the **Lifting the Exponent Lemma** for $p=2$:

> If $a,b$ are odd integers and $n$ is a positive even integer, then
> $$v_2(a^n - b^n) = v_2(a-b) + v_2(a+b) + v_2(n) - 1.$$

**Verification of hypotheses.** Take $a=3$, $b=1$, $n=1024$. Both $a$ and $b$ ar
e odd, and $n=1024$ is even. So the lemma applies.

**Computing each term.**

- $v_2(a-b) = v_2(3-1) = v_2(2) = 1$.
- $v_2(a+b) = v_2(3+1) = v_2(4) = 2$.
- $v_2(n) = v_2(1024) = v_2(2^{10}) = 10$.

**Applying the lemma.**

$$v_2(3^{1024}-1) = 1 + 2 + 10 - 1 = 12.$$

**Sanity checks.**

- $n=2$: $3^2-1=8=2^3$, and $1+2+1-1=3$. ✓
- $n=4$: $3^4-1=80=2^4\cdot 5$, and $1+2+2-1=4$. ✓
- $n=8$: $3^8-1=6560=2^5\cdot 205$, and $1+2+3-1=5$. ✓

The pattern $v_2(3^{2^k}-1)=k+2$ holds, and for $n=2^{10}=1024$ we get $10+2=12$
.

**Conclusion.** The highest power of $2$ dividing $3^{1024}-1$ is $\boxed{2^{12}
}$.
