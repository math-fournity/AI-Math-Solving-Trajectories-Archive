**Answer: Yes.** $C(K,\mathbb{R})$ is separable whenever $K$ is a compact metric
 space (equivalently, a compact separable metric space). No additional hypothese
s on $K$ are needed.

---

**Proof.**

Since $K$ is a compact metric space, it is separable: there exists a countable d
ense subset $S=\{s_1,s_2,\dots\}\subseteq K$.

**Step 1. A countable family of functions separating points.**

For each $n\in\mathbb{N}$ define the distance function
$$
\rho_n(x) := d(x,s_n),\qquad x\in K.
$$
Each $\rho_n\in C(K,\mathbb{R})$ (distance to a fixed point is continuous). The
family $\{\rho_n:n\ge 1\}$ separates points of $K$: if $x\neq y$, pick $s_n\in S
$ with $d(y,s_n)<\tfrac12 d(x,y)$ (possible since $S$ is dense); then $\rho_n(x)
\ge d(x,y)-d(y,s_n)>\tfrac12 d(x,y)>d(y,s_n)=\rho_n(y)$, so $\rho_n(x)\neq\rho_n
(y)$.

**Step 2. Build a countable subalgebra containing constants and separating point
s.**

Let $\mathcal{A}_0$ be the $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra generated by the constant functi
on $\mathbf{1}$ and the family $\{\rho_n:n\ge 1\}$. Concretely, $\mathcal{A}_0$
consists of all finite $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combinations of finite products of th
e $\rho_n$'s (including the empty product $\mathbf{1}$). Because the generators
are countably many and we use rational coefficients and finite products, $\mathc
al{A}_0$ is countable.

Let $\mathcal{A}:=\overline{\mathcal{A}_0}^{\,\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty}$ be its
uniform closure in $C(K,\mathbb{R})$.

**Step 3. $\mathcal{A}$ is a closed subalgebra containing constants and separati
ng points; apply Stone–Weierstrass.**

- $\mathcal{A}$ is a closed subalgebra of $C(K,\mathbb{R})$ (uniform limit of po
lynomials in continuous functions is again a polynomial-type limit; closure of a
n algebra is an algebra).
- $\mathbf{1}\in\mathcal{A}_0\subseteq\mathcal{A}$, so $\mathcal{A}$ contains th
e constants.
- $\mathcal{A}$ separates points because $\{\rho_n\}\subseteq\mathcal{A}$ does (
Step 1).
- $\mathcal{A}$ is a subalgebra of the *real*-valued functions, so the Stone–Wei
erstrass theorem (real version) applies with no self-adjointness condition neede
d.

By the Stone–Weierstrass theorem, $\mathcal{A}=C(K,\mathbb{R})$.

**Step 4. Separability.**

$\mathcal{A}_0$ is countable and dense in $\mathcal{A}=C(K,\mathbb{R})$. Hence $
C(K,\mathbb{R})$ admits a countable dense subset, i.e., it is separable. $\black
square$

---

**Remark on the necessity of hypotheses.** Compactness of $K$ is used to invoke
Stone–Weierstrass (which requires a compact space) and to identify "compact metr
ic" with "compact separable." Separability of $K$ alone, without compactness, do
es *not* suffice in general: for example $C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})$ with the uni
form norm is not separable. The argument above uses exactly the hypotheses given
 — $K$ compact and separable (metric) — and nothing more.
