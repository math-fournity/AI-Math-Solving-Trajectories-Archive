$$\nabla^2 g(x) = H(x) + S.$$

The matrix $S$ is a random symmetric $n \times n$ matrix whose entries are mutua
lly independent with continuous distributions: $S_{ii} = 2A_{ii} \sim \text{Unif
orm}[-2,2]$ and $S_{ij} = A_{ij} + A_{ji}$ (triangular distribution on $[-2,2]$)
 for $i < j$. Thus $S$ admits a density with respect to Lebesgue measure on $\ma
thrm{Sym}(n) \cong \mathbb{R}^{d}$ where $d = n(n+1)/2$.

**Per-point analysis.** For any *fixed* $x$, the map $S \mapsto \det(H(x) + S)$
is a polynomial in the entries of $S$. This polynomial is not identically zero (
one can choose $S$ so that $H(x)+S$ is invertible). Since $S$ has a continuous d
istribution, $\Pr[\det(H(x)+S)=0]=0$ for each fixed $x$.

**The "for all $x$" question is strictly stronger.** The bad event is:

$$B = \{S \in \mathrm{Sym}(n) : \exists\, x \in \mathbb{R}^n,\; \det(H(x)+S) = 0
\} = -H(\mathbb{R}^n) + \mathrm{Sing},$$

where $\mathrm{Sing} = \{M \in \mathrm{Sym}(n) : \det(M) = 0\}$ is the variety o
f singular symmetric matrices (codimension 1 in $\mathrm{Sym}(n)$), and $+$ deno
tes Minkowski sum. The question is whether $B$ has measure zero.

**Counterexample ($n=2$).** Take $f(x_1, x_2) = \tfrac{1}{2}x_1^2 x_2^2$, which
is $C^\infty$ (hence $C^2$), with $n=2>1$.

The Hessian is:
$$H(x_1, x_2) = \begin{pmatrix} x_2^2 & 2x_1 x_2 \\ 2x_1 x_2 & x_1^2 \end{pmatri
x}.$$

Evaluating along the line $x_2 = 0$:
$$H(t, 0) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & t^2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \nabla^2 g(t,
0) = \begin{pmatrix} S_{11} & S_{12} \\ S_{12} & t^2 + S_{22} \end{pmatrix},$$

where $S_{11}=2A_{11}$, $S_{22}=2A_{22}$, $S_{12}=A_{12}+A_{21}$. The determinan
t is:

$$\det(\nabla^2 g(t,0)) = S_{11}\, t^2 + \det(S), \qquad \det(S) = S_{11}S_{22}
- S_{12}^2.$$

Setting this to zero gives $t^2 = -\det(S)/S_{11}$, which has a real solution $t
$ if and only if $\det(S)/S_{11} \leq 0$ (with $S_{11} \neq 0$).

**The bad event has positive probability.** Consider the open set:
$$U = \{S \in \mathrm{Sym}(2) : \det(S) < 0,\; S_{11} > 0\} = \{S : S_{12}^2 > S
_{11}S_{22},\; S_{11} > 0\}.$$

This set is nonempty (e.g., $S_{11}=0.2, S_{22}=0.2, S_{12}=1$) and open, hence
has positive Lebesgue measure in $\mathbb{R}^3$. Since $S$ has a density, $\Pr[S
 \in U] > 0$.

Whenever $S \in U$, setting $t = \sqrt{-\det(S)/S_{11}}$ yields $\det(\nabla^2 g
(t, 0)) = 0$, so the Hessian of $g$ is singular at $(t, 0)$.

$$\Pr\big[\exists\, x \in \mathbb{R}^n : \nabla^2 g(x) \text{ is singular}\big]
\;\geq\; \Pr[S \in U] \;>\; 0.$$

Therefore:
$$\Pr\big[\forall\, x \in \mathbb{R}^n : \nabla^2 g(x) \text{ is invertible}\big
] \;<\; 1.$$

The Hessian of $g$ is **not** invertible for all $x$ with probability one in gen

terminant is a nonzero polynomial evaluated at a continuously distributed random

se the Hessian image $H(\mathbb{R}^n)$ sweeps out a family of translates of the
singular variety whose Minkowski sum can have positive measure — as demonstrated
