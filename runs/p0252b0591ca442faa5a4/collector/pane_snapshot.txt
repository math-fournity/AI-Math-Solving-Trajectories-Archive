## Solution

**Notation.** Let the triangle be $ABC$ with base $a = BC$, sides $b = CA$, $c =
 AB$, angles $A, B, C$, circumcenter $O$, circumradius $R$, and $M$ the midpoint
 of $BC$ (so $AM = m_a$). Set $\delta = B - C$.

---

### Key Lemma

**In triangle $AOM$, we have $OA = R$, $AM = m_a$, and $\angle AOM = \pi - \delt
a$.**

*Proof.* Place $O$ at the origin with $A$ at central angle $0$, i.e., $A = (R, 0
)$. On the circumcircle, the arc $BC$ not containing $A$ has central angle $2A$,
 so we may place:
- $B$ at central angle $2C$,
- $C$ at central angle $2C + 2A = 2(\pi - B)$.

The midpoint of chord $BC$ is:
$$M = \frac{B+C}{2}\text{ (as vectors)} = \left(\frac{R\cos 2C + R\cos 2(\pi-B)}
{2},\; \frac{R\sin 2C + R\sin 2(\pi-B)}{2}\right).$$

Using sum-to-product:
$$M_x = R\cos(B+C)\cos(B-C) = R\cos(\pi - A)\cos\delta = -R\cos A\cos\delta,$$
$$M_y = R\cos(B+C)\sin(C-B) = -R\cos(\pi-A)\sin\delta = R\cos A\sin\delta.$$

So $M = R\cos A\cdot(-\cos\delta,\;\sin\delta)$, meaning $\overrightarrow{OM}$ p
oints in direction $(-\cos\delta, \sin\delta) = (\cos(\pi-\delta), \sin(\pi-\del
ta))$, i.e., at angle $\pi - \delta$ from the positive $x$-axis (the direction o
f $OA$).

Therefore $\angle AOM = \pi - \delta$. $\square$

---

### Construction

**Given:** $R$, $m_a$, and $\delta = B - C$ (with $|\delta| < \pi$).

**Step 1.** Draw a segment $OA$ of length $R$.

**Step 2.** At $O$, draw a line $\ell$ making angle $\pi - \delta$ with $OA$.

**Step 3.** With center $A$ and radius $m_a$, draw a circle $\Gamma$. Let $M$ be
 an intersection point of $\Gamma$ with $\ell$.

- *Existence condition:* The perpendicular distance from $A$ to $\ell$ is $R\sin
(\pi-\delta) = R\sin\delta$, so intersections exist iff $m_a \geq R|\sin\delta|$
.
- If $m_a > R|\sin\delta|$, there are **two** intersection points, giving (in ge
neral) **two** solution triangles.
- If $m_a = R|\sin\delta|$, there is **one** (tangent) solution.

**Step 4.** Through $M$, draw the line perpendicular to $OM$. This line meets th
e circumcircle (center $O$, radius $R$) at two points; call them $B$ and $C$.

**Step 5.** Triangle $ABC$ is the desired triangle. Repeat Steps 4–5 for each va
lid intersection point $M$ from Step 3 to obtain all solutions.

---

### Verification

1. **Circumradius $R$:** $B$ and $C$ lie on the circle of center $O$ and radius
$R$, and $A$ is at distance $R$ from $O$. So the circumcircle has radius $R$. ✓

2. **Median $m_a$:** Since $OM \perp BC$ (constructed perpendicular) and $O$ is
the circumcenter, $M$ is the midpoint of chord $BC$. Thus $AM$ is the median to
the base, and $AM = m_a$ by construction (Step 3). ✓

3. **Angle difference $B - C = \delta$:** By the Lemma, $\angle AOM = \pi - \del
ta$. The central angle $\angle BOC = 2A$ (since $BC$ is a chord at distance $|OM
| = R|\cos A|$ from center, giving $\cos A = OM/R$). With $A$ at central angle $
0$, $B$ and $C$ lie at central angles $\pi - A + \delta = 2B$ and $\pi - A - \de
lta = 2C$ respectively (as computed above). The inscribed angles are therefore $
B$ and $C$ with $B - C = \delta$. ✓

4. **Validity check:** For each candidate, verify $A > 0$, $B > 0$, $C > 0$, i.e
., $A < \pi - |\delta|$. Discard any candidate failing this.

---

### Analysis of Solutions

From Step 3, the two intersection points give:
$$OM = -R\cos\delta \pm \sqrt{m_a^2 - R^2\sin^2\delta},$$
$$\cos A = \frac{OM}{R} = -\cos\delta \pm \sqrt{\frac{m_a^2}{R^2} - \sin^2\delta
}.$$

- **No solution** if $m_a < R|\sin\delta|$.
- **One solution** if $m_a = R|\sin\delta|$ (the two values coincide).
- **Two solutions** if $m_a > R|\sin\delta|$, provided both yield valid angles (
$0 < A < \pi - |\delta|$). One solution typically has $A$ acute and the other $A
$ obtuse.
