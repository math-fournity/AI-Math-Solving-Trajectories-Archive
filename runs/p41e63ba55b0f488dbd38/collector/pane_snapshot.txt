## Proof

The number of distinct conjugates of a subgroup $H$ in $G$ is given by the index
 of its normalizer:

$$\#\{gHg^{-1} : g \in G\} = [G : N_G(H)].$$

We determine $N_G(H)$.

**Step 1: $N_G(H) \neq G$.**
If $N_G(H) = G$, then $H \trianglelefteq G$. Since $H$ is a *maximal proper* sub
group, $H \neq G$. Also $H \neq 1$: for if $H = 1$ were maximal, $G$ would have
no nontrivial proper subgroups, forcing $G$ to be cyclic of prime order, hence a
belian — contradicting that $G$ is non-abelian (it is minimal *non-abelian*). Th
us $H$ would be a nontrivial proper normal subgroup of $G$, contradicting the si
mplicity of $G$. Hence $N_G(H) \neq G$.

**Step 2: $N_G(H) = H$ by maximality.**
We always have $H \leq N_G(H) \leq G$. From Step 1, $N_G(H) < G$. Since $H$ is m
aximal among proper subgroups of $G$, the chain $H \leq N_G(H) < G$ forces $N_G(
H) = H$.

**Step 3: Compute the number of conjugates.**
Therefore

$$\#\{\text{conjugates of } H\} = [G : N_G(H)] = [G : H] = \frac{|G|}{|H|}.$$

**Remark on the hypotheses.** The "minimal non-abelian" assumption guarantees th
at every proper subgroup — in particular the maximal subgroup $H$ — is abelian,
but the count of conjugates depends only on the index $[G:H]$. The two key ingre
dients used are: simplicity of $G$ (which rules out $N_G(H) = G$, i.e. rules out
 $H$ being normal) and maximality of $H$ (which then forces $N_G(H) = H$).

$$\boxed{\text{The number of distinct conjugates of } H \text{ in } G \text{ is
} [G:H] = \dfrac{|G|}{|H|}.}$$
