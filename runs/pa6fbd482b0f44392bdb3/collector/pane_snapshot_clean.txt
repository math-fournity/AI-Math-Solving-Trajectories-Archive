## Proof

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field with $\operatorname{char} k \neq 2, 3$,
 and let $(E, O)$ be an elliptic curve over $k$. We determine $\operatorname{Aut
}(E, O)$, the group of automorphisms of the curve $E$ fixing the origin $O$, in
the cases $j(E) = 0$ and $j(E) = 1728$.

### Step 1. Reduction to Weierstrass form

Since $\operatorname{char} k \neq 2, 3$, we may put $E$ in short Weierstrass for
m
$$E: y^2 = x^3 + ax + b, \qquad \Delta = -16(4a^3 + 27b^2) \neq 0,$$
with $O$ the point at infinity and
$$j(E) = 1728 \cdot \frac{4a^3}{4a^3 + 27b^2}.$$

### Step 2. Form of an automorphism fixing $O$

Let $\varphi \in \operatorname{Aut}(E, O)$. The divisor of $x$ is $(x) = (x)_0 -
 2(O)$ and $(y) = (y)_0 - 3(O)$. Since $\varphi$ fixes $O$, it preserves the lin
ear systems $L(2O) = \langle 1, x\rangle$ and $L(3O) = \langle 1, x, y\rangle$.
As $\dim L(2O)/L(O) = 1$ and $\dim L(3O)/L(2O) = 1$, we must have
$$\varphi^*(x) = \lambda\, x, \qquad \varphi^*(y) = \mu\, y$$
for some $\lambda, \mu \in k^\times$.

### Step 3. Constraints from the Weierstrass equation

Pulling back $y^2 = x^3 + ax + b$ gives
$$\mu^2 y^2 = \lambda^3 x^3 + a\lambda\, x + b.$$
Substituting $y^2 = x^3 + ax + b$ and collecting powers of $x$:
$$(\mu^2 - \lambda^3)\,x^3 + a(\mu^2 - \lambda)\,x + (\mu^2 - 1)\,b = 0 \quad \t
ext{on } E.$$
Since $[k(E):k(x)] = 2$, a polynomial in $x$ of degree $\le 3$ that vanishes on
$E$ is identically zero. Hence
$$\mu^2 = \lambda^3, \qquad a(\mu^2 - \lambda) = 0, \qquad (\mu^2 - 1)b = 0. \ta
g{$\ast$}$$

### Step 4. Case $j(E) = 0$: $a = 0$, $b \neq 0$

Here $E: y^2 = x^3 + b$ and $j = 0$. The system $(\ast)$ becomes
$$\mu^2 = \lambda^3, \qquad (\lambda^3 - 1)\,b = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \lambda^3
 = 1.$$
So $\lambda \in \mu_3 = \{1, \omega, \omega^2\}$ (cube roots of unity, $\omega =
 e^{2\pi i/3}$), and for each such $\lambda$, $\mu^2 = 1$ gives $\mu \in \{+1, -
1\}$. All six pairs $(\lambda, \mu) \in \mu_3 \times \{\pm 1\}$ satisfy $(\ast)$
.

The group law is $(\lambda_1, \mu_1)\cdot(\lambda_2, \mu_2) = (\lambda_1\lambda_
2, \mu_1\mu_2)$, so
$$\operatorname{Aut}(E, O) \cong \mu_3 \times \{\pm 1\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathb
b{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}.$$

Concretely, a generator is $(\omega, -1)$: it sends $(x, y) \mapsto (\omega\, x,
 -y)$, and one checks $(\omega, -1)^6 = (1, 1)$ with no smaller positive power b
eing trivial.

### Step 5. Case $j(E) = 1728$: $a \neq 0$, $b = 0$

Here $E: y^2 = x^3 + ax$ and $j = 1728$. The system $(\ast)$ becomes
$$\mu^2 = \lambda^3, \qquad a(\lambda^3 - \lambda) = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \lamb
da^3 = \lambda, \qquad (\mu^2 - 1)\cdot 0 = 0.$$
Since $\lambda \neq 0$ and $\operatorname{char} k \neq 2$, we get $\lambda \in \
{1, -1\}$.
- $\lambda = 1$: $\mu^2 = 1$, so $\mu = \pm 1$.
- $\lambda = -1$: $\mu^2 = -1$, so $\mu = \pm i$ (where $i^2 = -1$, which exists
 in $k$ since $k$ is algebraically closed).

This gives four automorphisms: $(1,1),\; (1,-1),\; (-1, i),\; (-1, -i)$.

Let $\sigma = (-1, i)$. Then
$$\sigma^2 = (1, i^2) = (1, -1), \quad \sigma^3 = (-1, -i), \quad \sigma^4 = (1,
 1),$$
and no smaller positive power is trivial. Hence $\sigma$ has order $4$ and
$$\operatorname{Aut}(E, O) \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}.$$

Concretely, $\sigma$ sends $(x, y) \mapsto (-x, iy)$.

### Step 6. Consistency with the endomorphism-ring viewpoint

Every $\varphi \in \operatorname{Aut}(E, O)$ is a group homomorphism (it preserv
es linear equivalence of divisors and fixes $O$, hence preserves the relation $P
 + Q + R \sim 3O$ defining the group law). Thus $\operatorname{Aut}(E, O) = \ope
ratorname{End}(E)^\times$, the unit group of the endomorphism ring.

- For $j = 0$, $\operatorname{End}(E) \cong \mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ (CM by $\omega =
 e^{2\pi i/3}$), whose units are $\{\pm 1, \pm \omega, \pm \omega^2\} \cong \mat
hbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$.
- For $j = 1728$, $\operatorname{End}(E) \cong \mathbb{Z}[i]$ (CM by $i$), whose
 units are $\{\pm 1, \pm i\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$.

These agree with the explicit computations above.

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Aut}(E, O) \cong \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} & \
text{if } j(E) = 0,\\[4pt] \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} & \text{if } j(E) = 1728. \end
{cases}}$$

(For all other values of $j$, $\operatorname{Aut}(E, O) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathb
b{Z}$, generated by $[-1]: P \mapsto -P$.)
