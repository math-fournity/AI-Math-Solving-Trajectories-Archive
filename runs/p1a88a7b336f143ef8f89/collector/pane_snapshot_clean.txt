**Key Claim:** For all $n \geq 0$,
$$x_n = \prod_{\substack{i \geq 0 \\ \text{bit}_i(n)=1}} p_{i+1}$$
where $\text{bit}_i(n)$ is the $i$-th bit of $n$ in binary (0-indexed from LSB),
 and the empty product (at $n=0$) equals 1.

**Proof by strong induction on $n$.**

*Base case:* $n=0$: $x_0 = 1$ (given) = empty product. ✓

*Inductive step:* Assume the formula holds for $n$; prove it for $n+1$ (with $n
\geq 0$).

By hypothesis, $x_n = \prod_{i:\,\text{bit}_i(n)=1} p_{i+1}$. The primes dividin
g $x_n$ are $\{p_{i+1} : \text{bit}_i(n)=1\}$.

Let $k$ = position of the lowest 0-bit of $n$. Then bits $0,1,\ldots,k-1$ of $n$
 are all 1, and bit $k$ is 0. (For $n=0$, $k=0$.)

- $p(x_n) = p_{k+1}$ (smallest prime not dividing $x_n$, since $p_1,\ldots,p_k$
all divide $x_n$ but $p_{k+1}$ does not).
- $q(x_n) = \prod_{j=1}^{k} p_j = \prod_{i=0}^{k-1} p_{i+1}$ (product of all pri
mes below $p_{k+1}$).

Therefore:
$$x_{n+1} = \frac{x_n \cdot p_{k+1}}{q(x_n)} = \frac{\left(\prod_{i=0}^{k-1} p_{
i+1}\right)\left(\prod_{i>k:\,\text{bit}_i(n)=1} p_{i+1}\right) \cdot p_{k+1}}{\
prod_{i=0}^{k-1} p_{i+1}} = p_{k+1} \cdot \prod_{i>k:\,\text{bit}_i(n)=1} p_{i+1
}$$

Now examine $n+1$ in binary. Since bits $0,\ldots,k-1$ of $n$ are 1 and bit $k$
is 0, adding 1 carries through: bits $0,\ldots,k-1$ become 0, bit $k$ becomes 1,
 bits above $k$ are unchanged. So:
$$\text{bit}_i(n+1) = \begin{cases} 0 & i < k \\ 1 & i = k \\ \text{bit}_i(n) &
i > k \end{cases}$$

Thus $\prod_{i:\,\text{bit}_i(n+1)=1} p_{i+1} = p_{k+1} \cdot \prod_{i>k:\,\text
{bit}_i(n)=1} p_{i+1} = x_{n+1}$. ✓

This completes the induction. $\square$
