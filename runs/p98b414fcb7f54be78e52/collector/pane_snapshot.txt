## Proof

We claim that the optimal constant is **K = 1**, i.e., for all $a, b > 0$ and $p
 > 1$,
$$|a-b|^p \leq |a^p - b^p|.$$

### Reduction to one variable

By symmetry and homogeneity, assume $a \geq b > 0$ and set $t = a/b \geq 1$. Div
iding both sides by $b^p > 0$, the inequality becomes
$$(t-1)^p \leq t^p - 1, \qquad t \geq 1,\ p > 1.$$

### Proving $(t-1)^p \leq t^p - 1$ for $t \geq 1$, $p > 1$

Define $f(t) = t^p - 1 - (t-1)^p$. We have:
- $f(1) = 1 - 1 - 0 = 0$.
- $f'(t) = p\,t^{p-1} - p\,(t-1)^{p-1} = p\bigl(t^{p-1} - (t-1)^{p-1}\bigr)$.

Since $p > 1$, the exponent $p-1 > 0$, so $x \mapsto x^{p-1}$ is strictly increa
sing on $(0,\infty)$. For $t > 1$, $t > t-1 > 0$, hence $t^{p-1} > (t-1)^{p-1}$,
 giving $f'(t) > 0$.

Therefore $f$ is strictly increasing on $[1,\infty)$ with $f(1)=0$, so $f(t) \ge
q 0$ for all $t \geq 1$. This proves
$$(t-1)^p \leq t^p - 1,$$
and consequently $|a-b|^p \leq |a^p - b^p|$ with $K = 1$.

### Optimality: no smaller constant works

We show $\inf\{K : |a-b|^p \leq K|a^p-b^p|\ \forall a,b>0\} = 1$, so $K=1$ is sh
arp.

Take $a = t$, $b = 1$ with $t \to \infty$. The required inequality forces
$$K \geq \frac{(t-1)^p}{t^p - 1} = \frac{(1-1/t)^p}{1 - 1/t^p} \xrightarrow{t\to
\infty} \frac{1}{1} = 1.$$

Hence any valid $K$ must satisfy $K \geq 1$. Since $K=1$ is valid, it is the opt
imal (smallest) constant.

### Conclusion

There exists a constant $K$ depending on $p$ (in fact independent of $p$) such t
hat $|a-b|^p \leq K|a^p-b^p|$ holds for all $a,b>0$ and $p>1$. The optimal value
 is

$$\boxed{K = 1}.$$
