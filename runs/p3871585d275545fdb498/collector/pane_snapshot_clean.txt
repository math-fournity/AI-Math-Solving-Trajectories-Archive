Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a 1-dimensional Noetherian
domain in which all prime ideals are invertible must be a Dedekind domain.

**The statement is TRUE.**

Here is the proof.

---

## Preliminary observations

First, note that the zero ideal $(0)$ is never invertible in a domain (since $0
\cdot J = 0 \neq A$ for any fractional ideal $J$). Thus the hypothesis "all prim
e ideals of $A$ are invertible" is necessarily interpreted as **all nonzero prim
e ideals of $A$ are invertible**.

Recall that a **Dedekind domain** is a Noetherian, integrally closed domain of K
rull dimension $1$. We are already given that $A$ is Noetherian and $1$-dimensio
nal. So it suffices to prove that $A$ is **integrally closed**.

---

## Key facts used

**Fact 1.** *An invertible ideal in a local ring is principal.*

*Proof.* An invertible ideal $I$ is a projective $R$-module of rank $1$. By Kapl
ansky's theorem, every projective module over a local ring is free. Since $I \ne
q 0$ and has rank $1$, we get $I \cong R$, i.e., $I$ is principal. $\square$

**Fact 2.** *A Noetherian local domain whose maximal ideal is principal and nonz
ero is a DVR (hence integrally closed).*

*Proof.* Let $(R, \mathfrak{m})$ be a Noetherian local domain with $\mathfrak{m}
 = (\pi)$, $\pi \neq 0$. Then $\mathfrak{m}^n = (\pi^n)$. The map $R/\mathfrak{m
} \to \mathfrak{m}^n/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}$, $\bar{r} \mapsto r\pi^n \bmod \mathfra
k{m}^{n+1}$, is an isomorphism of $R/\mathfrak{m}$-vector spaces:
- *Well-defined:* if $r \in \mathfrak{m}$, then $r\pi^n \in \mathfrak{m}^{n+1}$.
 ✓
- *Injective:* if $r\pi^n \in (\pi^{n+1})$, then $r\pi^n = s\pi^{n+1}$, so $r =
s\pi$ (since $R$ is a domain and $\pi \neq 0$), giving $r \in \mathfrak{m}$. ✓
- *Surjective:* $\mathfrak{m}^n/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}$ is generated by $\pi^n \bmod
 \mathfrak{m}^{n+1}$. ✓

By the Krull intersection theorem, $\bigcap_{n \geq 0} \mathfrak{m}^n = 0$. So e
very nonzero $x \in R$ lies in $\mathfrak{m}^n \setminus \mathfrak{m}^{n+1}$ for
 a unique $n \geq 0$, meaning $x = u\pi^n$ for some unit $u$. This shows $R$ is
a DVR. $\square$

**Fact 3.** *For any Noetherian domain $A$ with fraction field $K$,* $$A = \bigc
ap_{\mathrm{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) = 1} A_{\mathfrak{p}}.$$

*Proof.* The inclusion $A \subseteq \bigcap A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is clear. For the
reverse, let $x \in K \setminus A$, write $x = a/b$ with $a, b \in A$, $b \neq 0
$. Then $a \notin (b)$. By a standard lemma, there exists a prime $\mathfrak{p}$
 minimal over $(b)$ with $a \notin \mathfrak{p}$. By Krull's principal ideal the
orem, $\mathrm{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) \leq 1$; since $b \neq 0$ and $A$ is a domain,
$\mathfrak{p} \neq (0)$, so $\mathrm{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) = 1$. Now $b \in \mathfra
k{p}$ and $a \notin \mathfrak{p}$, so $x = a/b \notin A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ (if $x =
 c/s$ with $s \notin \mathfrak{p}$, then $as = bc \in \mathfrak{p}$, but $a, s \
notin \mathfrak{p}$, contradicting that $\mathfrak{p}$ is prime). $\square$

---

## Proof that $A$ is integrally closed

Since $A$ has Krull dimension $1$, every nonzero prime ideal is maximal, so the
height-$1$ primes are exactly the nonzero (maximal) primes.

**Step 1: Each localization $A_{\mathfrak{m}}$ is a DVR.**

Let $\mathfrak{m}$ be a nonzero prime (hence maximal) ideal of $A$. By hypothesi
s, $\mathfrak{m}$ is invertible, so $\mathfrak{m} \cdot \mathfrak{m}^{-1} = A$.
Localizing at $\mathfrak{m}$:
$$(\mathfrak{m} A_{\mathfrak{m}}) \cdot (\mathfrak{m}^{-1} A_{\mathfrak{m}}) = A
_{\mathfrak{m}},$$
so $\mathfrak{m} A_{\mathfrak{m}}$ is invertible in the local ring $A_{\mathfrak
{m}}$. By **Fact 1**, $\mathfrak{m} A_{\mathfrak{m}}$ is principal. Since $A_{\m
athfrak{m}}$ is a Noetherian local domain (localization of a Noetherian domain)
with principal nonzero maximal ideal, **Fact 2** implies $A_{\mathfrak{m}}$ is a
 DVR, hence integrally closed.

**Step 2: $A$ is an intersection of integrally closed rings.**

By **Fact 3** and the fact that height-$1$ primes $=$ maximal ideals (dimension
$1$):
$$A = \bigcap_{\mathfrak{m} \in \mathrm{MaxSpec}(A)} A_{\mathfrak{m}}.$$

Each $A_{\mathfrak{m}}$ is a DVR (hence integrally closed in $K$) by Step 1.

**Step 3: The intersection of integrally closed subrings of $K$ is integrally cl
osed.**

Let $x \in K$ be integral over $A$. Then $x$ is integral over each $A_{\mathfrak
{m}}$ (since $A \subseteq A_{\mathfrak{m}}$). Each $A_{\mathfrak{m}}$ is integra
lly closed, so $x \in A_{\mathfrak{m}}$ for every $\mathfrak{m}$. Thus $x \in \b
igcap A_{\mathfrak{m}} = A$. So $A$ is integrally closed.

---

## Conclusion

We have shown $A$ is:
- **Noetherian** (given),
- **integrally closed** (proved above),
- **Krull dimension $1$** (given).

Therefore $A$ is a **Dedekind domain**. The statement is **true**. $\blacksquare
