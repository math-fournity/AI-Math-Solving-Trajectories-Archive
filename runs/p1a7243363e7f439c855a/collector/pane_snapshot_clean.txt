## Proof

Let the torus be parametrized by
$$\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\theta,\varphi)=\bigl((a+b\cos\theta)\cos\varphi,\;(a+b\co
s\theta)\sin\varphi,\;b\sin\theta\bigr),\qquad a>b>0.$$

This is a surface of revolution generated by the circle $r(\theta)=a+b\cos\theta
$, $z(\theta)=b\sin\theta$ revolved about the $z$-axis.

**First fundamental form.** Compute the partial derivatives:
$$\boldsymbol{\sigma}_\theta=(-b\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\;-b\sin\theta\sin\varphi,
\;b\cos\theta),$$
$$\boldsymbol{\sigma}_\varphi=(-(a+b\cos\theta)\sin\varphi,\;(a+b\cos\theta)\cos
\varphi,\;0).$$

Hence
$$E=\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_\theta\|^2=b^2\sin^2\theta+b^2\cos^2\theta=b^2,$$
$$F=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_\theta\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}_\varphi=0,$$
$$G=\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_\varphi\|^2=(a+b\cos\theta)^2.$$

**Unit normal.**
$$\boldsymbol{\sigma}_\theta\times\boldsymbol{\sigma}_\varphi=-b(a+b\cos\theta)(
\cos\theta\cos\varphi,\;\cos\theta\sin\varphi,\;\sin\theta),$$
so $\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_\theta\times\boldsymbol{\sigma}_\varphi\|=b(a+b\cos\th
eta)>0$ (since $a>b$). Taking the normal pointing toward the center of the gener
ating tube,
$$\mathbf{n}=-(\cos\theta\cos\varphi,\;\cos\theta\sin\varphi,\;\sin\theta).$$

**Second fundamental form.** The second partials are
$$\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\theta\theta}=(-b\cos\theta\cos\varphi,\;-b\cos\theta\sin
\varphi,\;-b\sin\theta),$$
$$\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\theta\varphi}=(b\sin\theta\sin\varphi,\;-b\sin\theta\cos
\varphi,\;0),$$
$$\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\varphi\varphi}=(-(a+b\cos\theta)\cos\varphi,\;-(a+b\cos\
theta)\sin\varphi,\;0).$$

Therefore
$$L=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\theta\theta}\cdot\mathbf{n}=b\cos^2\theta\cos^2\varphi
+b\cos^2\theta\sin^2\varphi+b\sin^2\theta=b,$$
$$M=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\theta\varphi}\cdot\mathbf{n}=-b\sin\theta\cos\theta\si
n\varphi\cos\varphi+b\sin\theta\cos\theta\sin\varphi\cos\varphi=0,$$
$$N=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\varphi\varphi}\cdot\mathbf{n}=(a+b\cos\theta)\cos\thet
a\cos^2\varphi+(a+b\cos\theta)\cos\theta\sin^2\varphi=(a+b\cos\theta)\cos\theta.
$$

**Principal curvatures.** Since $F=M=0$, the coordinate curves are lines of curv
ature, and the shape operator is diagonal. The principal curvatures are
$$\kappa_1=\frac{L}{E}=\frac{b}{b^2}=\frac{1}{b},\qquad \kappa_2=\frac{N}{G}=\fr
ac{(a+b\cos\theta)\cos\theta}{(a+b\cos\theta)^2}=\frac{\cos\theta}{a+b\cos\theta
}.$$

With the opposite (outward) choice of normal, both signs are reversed.

$$\boxed{\kappa_1=\frac{1}{b},\qquad \kappa_2=\frac{\cos\theta}{\,a+b\cos\theta\
,}}$$

(up to a common sign depending on the choice of unit normal). The first is the c
urvature of the meridian circle (constant, equal to $1/b$); the second varies wi
th $\theta$, being positive on the outer half ($\cos\theta>0$), vanishing on the
 top and bottom circles ($\theta=\pm\pi/2$), and negative on the inner half ($\c
os\theta<0$).
