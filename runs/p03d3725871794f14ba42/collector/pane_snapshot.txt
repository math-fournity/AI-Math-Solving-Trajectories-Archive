The pushout of the diagram

\[
\begin{array}{} A \longrightarrow * \\ \big\downarrow {\iota} \\ X \end{array}
\]

in the category **Top** of topological spaces is the **quotient space $X/A$**, o
btained from $X$ by collapsing the subspace $A$ to a single point.

---

## Proof

**Construction.** Define $P = X / A$, the quotient space where we identify all p
oints of $A$ to a single point. Concretely, let $\sim$ be the equivalence relati
on on $X$ generated by $a \sim a'$ for all $a, a' \in A$ (so every point of $A$
is equivalent to every other, and points outside $A$ are equivalent only to them
selves). Set $P = X/{\sim}$, equipped with the quotient topology, and let $q: X
\to P$ be the quotient map. Denote the collapsed point by $[A] \in P$.

Define the map $j: * \to P$ by $j(*) = [A]$. Define the map $q: X \to P$ as abov
e (the quotient map). We must verify:

1. **The square commutes:** The map $A \to * \to P$ sends every $a \in A$ to $[A
]$. The map $A \xrightarrow{\iota} X \xrightarrow{q} P$ sends every $a \in A$ to
 $q(a) = [A]$. So $q \circ \iota = j \circ (!)$, where $!: A \to *$ is the uniqu
e map. ✓

2. **Universal property:** Suppose we are given a topological space $Y$ and cont
inuous maps $f: X \to Y$, $g: * \to Y$ (i.e., a point $y_0 = g(*) \in Y$) such t
hat $f \circ \iota = g \circ (!$, i.e., $f$ is constant on $A$ with value $y_0$.
 We must show there exists a unique continuous map $h: P \to Y$ with $h \circ q
= f$ and $h \circ j = g$.

   - **Uniqueness:** Any such $h$ must satisfy $h(q(x)) = f(x)$ for all $x \in X
$. Since $q$ is surjective, this determines $h$ uniquely on all of $P$.

   - **Existence:** Define $h: P \to Y$ by $h([x]) = f(x)$ for $x \notin A$, and
 $h([A]) = y_0$. This is well-defined: if $[x] = [x']$, then either $x = x' \not
in A$ (so $f(x) = f(x')$), or both $x, x' \in A$ (so $f(x) = y_0 = f(x')$ by the
 constancy condition). By construction $h \circ q = f$ and $h \circ j = g$.

   - **Continuity of $h$:** By the universal property of the quotient topology,
$h: P \to Y$ is continuous if and only if $h \circ q: X \to Y$ is continuous. Bu
t $h \circ q = f$, which is continuous by hypothesis. ✓

Since $P = X/A$ satisfies the universal property of the pushout, and pushouts in
 **Top** are unique up to unique homeomorphism, we conclude:

$$\boxed{X/A}$$

is the pushout of the given diagram. ∎
