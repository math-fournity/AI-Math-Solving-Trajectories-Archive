$$v+w=\sin^2\alpha,\quad w+u=\sin^2\beta,\quad u+v=\sin^2\gamma$$

$$u=\tfrac{1}{2}(\sin^2\beta+\sin^2\gamma-\sin^2\alpha),\quad v=\tfrac{1}{2}(\si

The intersection is nonempty iff $u,v,w\geq 0$, i.e., the triangle inequalities

## Simplifying via Trigonometric Identity

$$\sin^2\alpha+\sin^2\beta-\sin^2(\alpha+\beta) = 2\sin\alpha\sin\beta\bigl(\sin

By the cyclic symmetry:
$$\sin^2\beta+\sin^2\gamma-\sin^2\alpha = 2\sin\beta\sin\gamma\cos\alpha$$
$$\sin^2\alpha+\sin^2\gamma-\sin^2\beta = 2\sin\alpha\sin\gamma\cos\beta$$

## Describing the Region $A$

The region $A$ is the set of $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ with $\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0

## Cutting Off Three Corners

The constraint $\alpha\leq\pi/2$ cuts off the corner at $(\pi,0,0)$. The line $\
alpha=\pi/2$ meets the simplex edges at:
- $(\pi/2,\,0,\,\pi/2)$ (on $\beta=0$)

This forms a triangle with vertices $(\pi,0,0),\,(\pi/2,0,\pi/2),\,(\pi/2,\pi/2,
