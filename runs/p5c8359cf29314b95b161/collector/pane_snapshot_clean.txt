## Solution

**Claim.** Yes. In fact, *every* point of $K^n$ is closed in the Zariski topolog
y (the hypothesis that $K$ is algebraically closed is more than sufficient; the
argument works over any field).

---

### Proof

Recall that in the Zariski topology on $K^n$, the closed sets are precisely the
*algebraic sets*
$$V(I)=\{p\in K^n : f(p)=0\ \text{for all } f\in I\},$$
where $I\subseteq K[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ is an ideal. A point $w\in K^n$ is closed ex
actly when the singleton $\{w\}$ is an algebraic set.

Let $w=(a_1,\dots,a_n)\in K^n$ be **arbitrary**, and consider the ideal
$$I_w \;=\; (x_1-a_1,\ \dots,\ x_n-a_n)\ \subseteq\ K[x_1,\dots,x_n].$$

We compute its vanishing set. A point $p=(b_1,\dots,b_n)\in K^n$ lies in $V(I_w)
$ iff every generator of $I_w$ vanishes at $p$, i.e. iff
$$b_i-a_i = 0 \quad\text{for all } i=1,\dots,n.$$
This holds iff $b_i=a_i$ for every $i$, i.e. iff $p=w$. Hence
$$V(I_w)=\{w\}.$$

Since $I_w$ is an ideal of $K[x_1,\dots,x_n]$, the set $V(I_w)=\{w\}$ is Zariski
-closed. Therefore $w$ is a closed point.

Because $w$ was chosen arbitrarily, **every** point of $K^n$ is closed in the Za
riski topology. In particular, a closed point certainly exists — for instance th
e origin $(0,\dots,0)$, with $V(x_1,\dots,x_n)=\{(0,\dots,0)\}$.

$\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** The algebraic-closedness of $K$ is not needed for the *existence* of
 a closed point here; it becomes essential in the converse direction — that over
 an algebraically closed field these $K$-rational points are exactly the closed
points of $\operatorname{Spec} K[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ (via the Nullstellensatz, which
 identifies maximal ideals with points of $K^n$).
