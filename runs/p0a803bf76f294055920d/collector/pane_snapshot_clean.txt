**Proof.**

**Setup.** Each card is a triple $(s,\ell,n)$ with $s\in\{0,1,2\}$ (shape), $\el
l\in\{0,1,2\}$ (letter), $n\in\{0,1,2\}$ (number), giving $27$ distinct cards. C
all a pair of cards **bad** if the two cards agree on exactly two attributes (eq
uivalently, differ in exactly one attribute). The problem asks for unordered tri
ples in which **no** pair is bad, i.e., every pair agrees on at most one attribu
te.

**Total triples.** $\binom{27}{3}=2925$.

We count the complement (triples containing at least one bad pair) and subtract.

**Counting bad pairs.** Fix the first card ($27$ ways), choose which single attr
ibute differs ($3$ ways), choose the differing value ($2$ ways): $27\cdot3\cdot2
=162$ ordered, hence $\mathbf{81}$ unordered bad pairs.

**Classifying triples by their number of bad pairs.** Fix a bad pair $\{a,b\}$ d
iffering only in attribute $X$; WLOG $a=(0,0,0)$, $b=(1,0,0)$. For each of the $
25$ remaining cards $c$:

- $c$ makes $(a,c)$ bad $\iff$ $c$ differs from $a$ in exactly one attribute: $6
$ cards, minus $b$ $\Rightarrow$ $5$.
- $c$ makes $(b,c)$ bad $\iff$ $c$ differs from $b$ in exactly one attribute: $6
$ cards, minus $a$ $\Rightarrow$ $5$.
- $c$ makes **both** bad: must differ from $a$ only in $X$ and from $b$ only in
$X$, so $c=(2,0,0)$: **$1$** card (all three pairs bad).
- $c$ makes $(a,c)$ bad only: $5-1=4$.
- $c$ makes $(b,c)$ bad only: $5-1=4$.
- $c$ makes **neither** bad: $25-5-5+1=16$.

So per bad pair: $16$ triples with exactly $1$ bad pair, $8$ with exactly $2$, a
nd $1$ with exactly $3$.

Let $T_k$ = number of triples with exactly $k$ bad pairs. Each triple with $k$ b
ad pairs is counted $k$ times (once per bad pair), so:

$$T_1 = 81\cdot 16 = 1296,\qquad T_2 = \frac{81\cdot 8}{2}=324,\qquad T_3=\frac{
81\cdot 1}{3}=27.$$

**Consistency check for $T_3=27$:** a triple with all three pairs bad has all th
ree cards sharing two attributes and differing in the third. Choose the differin
g attribute ($3$) and the fixed values of the other two ($3\cdot3=9$): $3\cdot9=
27$. ✓

Also verify the pair-count identity: $T_1+2T_2+3T_3 = 1296+648+81=2025=81\cdot25
$. ✓

**Bad triples:** $T_1+T_2+T_3 = 1296+324+27 = 1647$.

**Good triples:**

$$2925 - 1647 = \boxed{1278}.$$
