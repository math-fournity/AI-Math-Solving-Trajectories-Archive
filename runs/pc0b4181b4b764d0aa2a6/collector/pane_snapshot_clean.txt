## Analysis

This is **Erdős Problem 410**, formally an open research problem (marked `@[cate
gory research open]`), originating from Erdős, Granville, Pomerance, Spiro (1990
).

**The conjecture:** For every $n > 1$, defining $\sigma_1(n) = \sigma(n)$ and $\
sigma_k(n) = \sigma(\sigma_{k-1}(n))$, we have
$$\lim_{k \to \infty} \sigma_k(n)^{1/k} = \infty.$$

### What can be established

**Basic growth:** For $n > 1$, $\sigma(n) \geq n + 1 > n$, so $(\sigma_k(n))$ is
 strictly increasing. Hence $\sigma_k(n) \geq n + k - 1$, giving $\sigma_k(n)^{1
/k} \geq (n+k-1)^{1/k} \to 1$. This only shows the limit is $\geq 1$ (if it exis
ts).

**Better lower bound for even iterates:** If $m$ is even, then $m$ and $m/2$ are
 divisors, so $\sigma(m) \geq m + m/2 = \frac{3}{2}m$. After the first step from
 any $n > 1$: if $n$ is prime, $\sigma(n) = n+1$ which is even (for $n > 2$); if
 $n$ is composite and even, we get the $3/2$ factor directly; if $n$ is odd comp
osite, $\sigma(n) \geq n + 1$ and $\sigma(n)$ is even when $n$ is an odd prime p
ower or has specific forms. In many cases the iterate becomes even, and then we
get at least a $(3/2)^{k/2}$-type growth, yielding $\sigma_k(n)^{1/k} \geq (3/2)
^{1/2} > 1$.

**The core difficulty:** To prove $\sigma_k(n)^{1/k} \to \infty$, one needs $\lo
g \sigma_k(n) / k \to \infty$, i.e., **super-exponential** growth. This requires
 showing that the iterates eventually and repeatedly land on numbers with $\sigm
a(m)/m$ large (i.e., numbers with many small prime factors).

The obstacle is that the iteration could in principle hit long runs of primes or
 near-primes, where $\sigma(m)/m \approx 1 + 1/m \approx 1$, giving only linear
growth during those stretches. Whether this can happen often enough to keep $\lo
g \sigma_k / k$ bounded is exactly the open question.

**Known related results (Erdős–Granville–Pomerance–Spiro):** The iterates of $\s
igma$ (and related functions like $\varphi$) have been studied extensively. For
$\varphi$, it is known that $\varphi_k(n) \to 0$ in a suitable sense. For $\sigm
a$, the average order and normal order of $\sigma(n)/n$ are understood ($\sigma(
n)/n$ has average $\pi^2/6$ and normal order tending to constants), but **the be
havior along specific deterministic iteration trajectories is not controlled** b
y these analytic results, because the iterates form a zero-density, highly corre
lated sequence.

**Why standard tools fail:**
- The average/normal order of $\sigma(n)/n$ applies to random $n$, not to the de
terministic sequence $\sigma_k(n)$.
- No known mechanism forces the iterates to repeatedly encounter numbers with ma
ny small prime factors.
- The problem is sensitive to the multiplicative structure of each iterate, whic
h is determined by the previous iterate in a way no current technique can track
over long ranges.
