**Answer: Yes.** In fact, for *every* entire function $f$ (constant or not), $|f
|$ is subharmonic on $\mathbb{C}$. Hence any non-constant entire function, e.g.
$f(z)=z$, is an example.

---

**Proof.**

Recall that an upper semicontinuous function $u:\Omega\to[-\infty,\infty)$ is *s
ubharmonic* if for every closed disk $\overline{D(a,r)}\subset\Omega$,

$$u(a)\le \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}u(a+re^{i\theta})\,d\theta.$$

We use two standard facts:

**Fact 1.** If $g$ is holomorphic on $\Omega$, then $\log|g|$ is subharmonic on
$\Omega$ (with value $-\infty$ at the zeros of $g$).

*Proof of Fact 1.* $\log|g|$ is upper semicontinuous (it is continuous off the d
iscrete zero set and equals $-\infty$ at zeros). At a point $a$ with $g(a)\neq 0
$, $\log|g|$ is harmonic in a neighborhood of $a$, so the sub-mean-value propert
y holds with equality. At a zero $a$ of order $m$, write $g(z)=(z-a)^m h(z)$ wit
h $h(a)\neq 0$. Then
$$\log|g(a+re^{i\theta})|=m\log r+\log|h(a+re^{i\theta})|,$$
so
$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|g(a+re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta = m\log r + \frac{
1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|h(a+re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta = m\log r+\log|h(a)| \ge -
\infty = \log|g(a)|,$$
where we used that $\log|h|$ is harmonic near $a$. $\square$

**Fact 2.** If $u$ is subharmonic and $\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ is conve
x and increasing, then $\varphi\circ u$ is subharmonic (where $\varphi(-\infty):
=\lim_{t\to-\infty}\varphi(t)$).

*Proof of Fact 2.* Convexity of $\varphi$ gives, by Jensen's inequality,
$$\varphi\!\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int u\right)\le \frac{1}{2\pi}\int \varphi\circ
u.$$
Since $\varphi$ is increasing and $u(a)\le \frac{1}{2\pi}\int u$, applying $\var
phi$ yields
$$\varphi(u(a))\le \varphi\!\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int u\right)\le \frac{1}{2\pi}\
int \varphi\circ u.$$
Upper semicontinuity is preserved. $\square$

**Conclusion.** Let $f$ be entire. By Fact 1, $u=\log|f|$ is subharmonic. The fu
nction $\varphi(t)=e^t$ is convex and increasing on $\mathbb{R}$, with $\varphi(
-\infty)=0$. By Fact 2,
$$|f|=e^{\log|f|}=\varphi\circ u$$
is subharmonic on $\mathbb{C}$.

Taking $f(z)=z$ (non-constant, entire) gives the desired example. $\blacksquare$
