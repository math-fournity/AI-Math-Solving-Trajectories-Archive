Each arrow represents one application of $T$. Counting every transition from 27

### Comparison with the Logarithmic Trend

We compute $(\ln 27)^3$:

$$\ln 27 = \ln(3^3) = 3\ln 3 \approx 3 \times 1.09861 = 3.29584$$

$$(\ln 27)^3 = (3.29584)^3 \approx 35.80$$

### Verification

The Collatz sequence length of 27 is:

$$L(27) = 111$$

The logarithmic trend with $C = 3$ is:

$$(\ln 27)^3 \approx 35.80$$

Since:

$$111 > 35.80$$

we have $L(27) > (\ln 27)^3$, i.e., the Collatz sequence length of 27 **exceeds*
* the logarithmic trend $(\ln x)^C$ with $C = 3$.

### Conclusion

The natural number $\boxed{27}$ satisfies the requirement: its Collatz sequence
