## Solution: Klein-Gordon Equation via Fourier Transform

**Setup.** Consider the Klein-Gordon equation
$$u_{tt} - \Delta u + u = 0, \quad x \in \mathbb{R}^d,\ t>0,$$
with initial data $u(x,0)=f(x)$, $u_t(x,0)=g(x)$.

We assume $f,g$ are sufficiently nice (e.g., Schwartz class) so that all Fourier
 transforms and integrals below are justified; the final formula extends by dens
ity to broader function classes.

---

### Step 1. Apply the spatial Fourier transform

Define the Fourier transform and its inverse (with the convention we use through
out):
$$\hat{h}(\xi) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^d} h(x)\,e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,dx, \qquad h(x) = (2
\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^d} \hat{h}(\xi)\,e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi.$$

Set $\hat{u}(\xi,t) = \int u(x,t)\,e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,dx$. Under the Fourier trans
form, $\Delta u \mapsto -|\xi|^2 \hat{u}$, so the PDE becomes, for each fixed $\
xi\in\mathbb{R}^d$, an **ordinary differential equation in $t$**:

$$\boxed{\;\hat{u}_{tt}(\xi,t) + \bigl(|\xi|^2+1\bigr)\,\hat{u}(\xi,t) = 0.\;}$$

The initial data transform to
$$\hat{u}(\xi,0)=\hat{f}(\xi), \qquad \hat{u}_t(\xi,0)=\hat{g}(\xi).$$

---

### Step 2. Solve the ODE for each $\xi$

Define the **dispersion relation** (frequency)
$$\omega(\xi) := \sqrt{|\xi|^2+1} > 0.$$

The ODE $\hat{u}_{tt} + \omega^2 \hat{u}=0$ is the simple harmonic oscillator eq
uation, with general solution
$$\hat{u}(\xi,t) = A(\xi)\cos\!\bigl(\omega(\xi)\,t\bigr) + B(\xi)\,\frac{\sin\!
\bigl(\omega(\xi)\,t\bigr)}{\omega(\xi)}.$$

Matching initial conditions:
- $\hat{u}(\xi,0)=A(\xi)=\hat{f}(\xi)$,
- $\hat{u}_t(\xi,0)=\omega(\xi)\,B(\xi)=\hat{g}(\xi)\;\Longrightarrow\; B(\xi)=\
hat{g}(\xi)/\omega(\xi)$.

Hence
$$\boxed{\;\hat{u}(\xi,t) = \hat{f}(\xi)\cos\!\bigl(\omega(\xi)\,t\bigr) + \frac
{\hat{g}(\xi)}{\omega(\xi)}\,\sin\!\bigl(\omega(\xi)\,t\bigr),\qquad \omega(\xi)
=\sqrt{|\xi|^2+1}.\;}$$

---

### Step 3. Invert the Fourier transform

Applying the inverse Fourier transform gives the **representation of the solutio
n**:

$$\boxed{\;u(x,t) = (2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}\left[\hat{f}(\xi)\cos\!\bigl(
\omega(\xi)\,t\bigr) + \frac{\hat{g}(\xi)}{\omega(\xi)}\,\sin\!\bigl(\omega(\xi)
\,t\bigr)\right]e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi,\quad \omega(\xi)=\sqrt{|\xi|^2+1}.\;}$$

---

### Step 4. Operator form (Duhamel / propagator structure)

Using $\cos(\omega t) = \partial_t\!\bigl[\sin(\omega t)/\omega\bigr]$, we may w
rite the solution as
$$u(x,t) = \partial_t\bigl(\mathcal{S}(t)f\bigr)(x) + \bigl(\mathcal{S}(t)g\bigr
)(x),$$
where the **Klein-Gordon propagator** $\mathcal{S}(t)$ is the operator defined b
y
$$\bigl(\mathcal{S}(t)h\bigr)(x) := (2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}\frac{\sin\!\b
igl(\omega(\xi)\,t\bigr)}{\omega(\xi)}\,\hat{h}(\xi)\,e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi, \qqu
ad \omega(\xi)=\sqrt{|\xi|^2+1}.$$

Equivalently, $\mathcal{S}(t)$ is the Fourier multiplier with symbol $\dfrac{\si
n(\omega(\xi)t)}{\omega(\xi)}$.

---

### Step 5. Verification

We verify directly that the formula satisfies the PDE and initial data.

**Initial position.** At $t=0$: $\cos(0)=1$, $\sin(0)=0$, so
$$u(x,0)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int \hat{f}(\xi)\,e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi = f(x).\;\checkmark$
$

**Initial velocity.** Differentiating under the integral:
$$u_t(x,t)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int\left[-\hat{f}(\xi)\,\omega(\xi)\sin(\omega t)+\hat{g}
(\xi)\cos(\omega t)\right]e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi.$$
At $t=0$: $u_t(x,0)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int \hat{g}(\xi)\,e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi = g(x).\;
\checkmark$

**PDE.** Differentiating twice:
$$u_{tt}(x,t)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int\left[-\hat{f}(\xi)\,\omega^2\cos(\omega t)-\hat{g}
(\xi)\,\omega\sin(\omega t)\right]e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi.$$
Also $\Delta u$ corresponds to multiplying the integrand by $-|\xi|^2$, and $u$
contributes $+1$ times the integrand. Therefore
$$u_{tt}-\Delta u+u = (2\pi)^{-d}\int\Bigl[-\omega^2+|\xi|^2+1\Bigr]\bigl[\hat{f
}\cos(\omega t)+\hat{g}\sin(\omega t)/\omega\bigr]e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi.$$
Since $\omega^2=|\xi|^2+1$, the bracket $-\omega^2+|\xi|^2+1=0$, so
$$u_{tt}-\Delta u+u=0.\;\checkmark$$

---

### Summary

The solution to the Klein-Gordon equation $u_{tt}-\Delta u+u=0$ with data $u(x,0
)=f$, $u_t(x,0)=g$ is

$$u(x,t)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}\left[\hat{f}(\xi)\cos\!\bigl(t\sqrt{|\xi
|^2+1}\bigr)+\frac{\hat{g}(\xi)}{\sqrt{|\xi|^2+1}}\sin\!\bigl(t\sqrt{|\xi|^2+1}\
bigr)\right]e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi,$$

or equivalently $u(x,t)=\partial_t(\mathcal{S}(t)f)(x)+(\mathcal{S}(t)g)(x)$ wit
h the propagator $\mathcal{S}(t)$ carrying the multiplier $\sin\!\bigl(t\sqrt{|\
xi|^2+1}\bigr)/\sqrt{|\xi|^2+1}$. The dispersion relation $\omega(\xi)=\sqrt{|\x
i|^2+1}$ encodes the massive (rest-mass $1$) nature of the field, distinguishing
 the Klein-Gordon case from the wave equation ($\omega=|\xi|$) and producing the
 dispersive smoothing and propagation properties characteristic of massive relat
ivistic fields.
