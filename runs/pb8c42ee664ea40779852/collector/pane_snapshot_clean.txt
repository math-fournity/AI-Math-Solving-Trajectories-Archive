## Modulus of Continuity of f(x) = 1/x on (0,1)

**Definition.** The modulus of continuity of a function f on an interval I is

$$\omega(\delta) = \sup\{|f(x) - f(y)| : x, y \in I,\ |x - y| \le \delta\}, \qqu
ad \delta > 0.$$

**Claim.** For $f(x) = 1/x$ on $I = (0,1)$, we have $\omega(\delta) = +\infty$ f
or every $\delta > 0$.

**Proof.**

Fix any $\delta > 0$. We show the supremum is $+\infty$ by exhibiting, for every
 $M > 0$, a pair $x, y \in (0,1)$ with $|x - y| \le \delta$ and $|f(x) - f(y)| >
 M$.

Choose $x = \varepsilon$ and $y = \varepsilon + \tfrac{\delta}{2}$, where $\vare
psilon > 0$ is small enough that $y < 1$ (any $\varepsilon < \min(1 - \delta/2,\
, \delta/2)$ works; in particular $\varepsilon < 1/2$ suffices for $\delta < 1$,
 and for $\delta \ge 1$ we may use $y = \varepsilon + \delta'$ with any $\delta'
 \in (0,\delta)$, say $\delta' = \min(\delta/2, 1/2)$). Then $x, y \in (0,1)$ an
d

$$|x - y| = \frac{\delta}{2} \le \delta.$$

Now

$$|f(x) - f(y)| = \left|\frac{1}{\varepsilon} - \frac{1}{\varepsilon + \delta/2}
\right| = \frac{\delta/2}{\varepsilon\,(\varepsilon + \delta/2)}.$$

As $\varepsilon \to 0^+$, the denominator $\varepsilon(\varepsilon + \delta/2) \
to 0$ while the numerator $\delta/2 > 0$ is fixed, so

$$\frac{\delta/2}{\varepsilon\,(\varepsilon + \delta/2)} \longrightarrow +\infty
.$$

Hence for any prescribed $M > 0$ we can pick $\varepsilon$ sufficiently small so
 that $|f(x) - f(y)| > M$. Since $M$ was arbitrary,

$$\omega(\delta) = \sup\{|f(x)-f(y)| : x,y\in(0,1),\ |x-y|\le\delta\} = +\infty.
$$

**Remark on the underlying reason.** A finite modulus of continuity at some $\de
lta_0 > 0$ would imply that $f$ is bounded on $I$ (since covering $I$ by finitel
y many $\delta_0$-intervals would give a global bound). The function $f(x) = 1/x
$ is unbounded on $(0,1)$ — $\lim_{x\to 0^+} 1/x = +\infty$ — so no finite modul
us of continuity can exist. The computation above makes this explicit: the singu
larity at $0$ lets us extract arbitrarily large oscillations from arbitrarily sm
all pairs of points near the origin.

**Conclusion.** The modulus of continuity of $f(x) = 1/x$ on $(0,1)$ is

$$\boxed{\omega(\delta) = +\infty \quad \text{for all } \delta > 0.}$$
