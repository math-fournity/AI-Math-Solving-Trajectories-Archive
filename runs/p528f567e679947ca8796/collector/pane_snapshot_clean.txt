## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. There exists an infinite non-abelian group in which every non-id
entity element has infinite order.

**Construction:** Let $F_2 = \langle a, b \rangle$ be the free group on two gene
rators. We show $F_2$ is (i) infinite, (ii) non-abelian, and (iii) torsion-free
(every non-identity element has infinite order).

Recall that elements of $F_2$ are **reduced words** over the alphabet $\{a, a^{-
1}, b, b^{-1}\}$, i.e., finite sequences in which no generator appears adjacent
to its inverse. Multiplication is concatenation followed by reduction (cancellat
ion of adjacent inverse pairs). The identity is the empty word $\varepsilon$.

**(i) $F_2$ is infinite.** The words $a, a^2, a^3, \ldots$ are all distinct redu
ced words, so $F_2$ contains infinitely many elements.

**(ii) $F_2$ is non-abelian.** The word $ab$ is reduced, and the word $ba$ is re
duced. They are distinct reduced words, so $ab \neq ba$. Hence $a$ and $b$ do no
t commute, and $F_2$ is non-abelian.

**(iii) $F_2$ is torsion-free.** We show that no non-identity element has finite
 order.

Call a reduced word $w$ **cyclically reduced** if its first and last letters are
 not inverses of each other (i.e., the word does not begin with $x$ and end with
 $x^{-1}$ for any generator $x$).

*Fact 1 (cyclic reduction):* Every reduced word $w$ can be written as $w = c v c
^{-1}$ where $v$ is cyclically reduced and $c$ is a (possibly empty) reduced wor
d. This is achieved by repeatedly peeling off a matching pair: if $w$ begins wit
h $x$ and ends with $x^{-1}$, write $w = x\, w'\, x^{-1}$ and repeat on $w'$. Th
e process terminates because the word is finite, yielding $w = c v c^{-1}$ with
$v$ cyclically reduced.

*Fact 2:* If $v$ is a non-empty cyclically reduced word, then $v^n$ is reduced f
or every $n \geq 1$, hence $v^n \neq \varepsilon$.

*Proof of Fact 2:* Since $v$ is cyclically reduced, the last letter of $v$ and t
he first letter of $v$ are not inverses. Therefore, when we concatenate $v$ with
 $v$ to form $v^2$, no cancellation occurs at the junction. The same holds at ev
ery junction in $v^n$. Since $v$ itself is reduced, $v^n$ is reduced. A non-empt
y reduced word is not $\varepsilon$, so $v^n \neq \varepsilon$.

*Conclusion of (iii):* Let $w \in F_2$, $w \neq \varepsilon$. By Fact 1, write $
w = c v c^{-1}$ with $v$ cyclically reduced. Since $w \neq \varepsilon$, we have
 $v \neq \varepsilon$ (conjugation preserves being non-identity). Suppose for co
ntradiction that $w^n = \varepsilon$ for some $n \geq 1$. Then
$$v^n = c^{-1} w^n c = c^{-1} \varepsilon\, c = \varepsilon,$$
contradicting Fact 2. Therefore $w$ has infinite order.

**Conclusion.** The free group $F_2$ is an infinite, non-abelian, torsion-free g
roup. Hence there exists an infinite non-abelian group in which every non-identi
ty element has infinite order. $\blacksquare$
