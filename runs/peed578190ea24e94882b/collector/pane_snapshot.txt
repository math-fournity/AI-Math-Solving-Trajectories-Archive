No. A full and faithful functor need not be injective on objects. Here is a coun
terexample.

**Construction.** Let $D$ be the terminal category: a single object $*$ with onl
y the identity morphism $\mathrm{id}_*$.

Let $C$ be the "free isomorphism" category with two distinct objects $A$ and $B$
, and morphisms
$$\mathrm{Hom}_C(A,A)=\{\mathrm{id}_A\},\quad \mathrm{Hom}_C(B,B)=\{\mathrm{id}_
B\},\quad \mathrm{Hom}_C(A,B)=\{f\},\quad \mathrm{Hom}_C(B,A)=\{g\},$$
where $f:A\to B$ and $g:B\to A$ are mutually inverse isomorphisms, i.e. $g\circ
f=\mathrm{id}_A$ and $f\circ g=\mathrm{id}_B$. (So $C$ is a category equivalent
to the terminal category, but with two distinct objects.)

Define $F:C\to D$ by
$$F(A)=*,\quad F(B)=*,\quad F(\mathrm{id}_A)=F(\mathrm{id}_B)=F(f)=F(g)=\mathrm{
id}_*.$$
This is a functor: it preserves identities and composition, since every composab
le pair in $C$ composes to an identity, and $F$ sends every morphism to $\mathrm
{id}_*$, with $\mathrm{id}_*\circ\mathrm{id}_*=\mathrm{id}_*$.

**$F$ is faithful.** For each pair of objects $(X,Y)\in\{A,B\}^2$, the map
$$F_{X,Y}:\mathrm{Hom}_C(X,Y)\to\mathrm{Hom}_D(FX,FY)=\mathrm{Hom}_D(*,*)=\{\mat
hrm{id}_*\}$$
must be injective. But each $\mathrm{Hom}_C(X,Y)$ has exactly one element, so ea
ch $F_{X,Y}$ is an injection (a map from a singleton).

**$F$ is full.** For each pair $(X,Y)$, $\mathrm{Hom}_D(*,*)=\{\mathrm{id}_*\}$
is a singleton, and each $\mathrm{Hom}_C(X,Y)$ is nonempty (it is a singleton),
so each $F_{X,Y}$ is surjective.

Thus $F$ is full and faithful.

**$F$ is not injective on objects.** We have $A\neq B$ in $C$, yet $F(A)=*=F(B)$
.

**Conclusion.** Fullness and faithfulness are conditions on the maps between hom
-sets $\mathrm{Hom}_C(X,Y)\to\mathrm{Hom}_D(FX,FY)$; they say nothing about whet
her $F$ separates distinct objects. Whenever two objects of $C$ are isomorphic,
a full and faithful functor may collapse them to a single object of $D$ (as abov
e). Hence a full and faithful functor does **not** imply that $F:\mathrm{Ob}(C)\
to\mathrm{Ob}(D)$ is injective.
