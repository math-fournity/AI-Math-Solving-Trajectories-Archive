## Proof

We analyze the Fourier series of the $2\pi$-periodic function $f$ at $x = 0$.

**Decomposition.** Write $f(x) = 1 + g(x)$ where $g(x) = \sin(\pi^2/x)$ for $x \
neq 0$ and $g(0) = 0$.

**Key observation: $g$ is odd.** For $x \neq 0$:
$$g(-x) = \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi^2}{-x}\right) = \sin\!\left(-\frac{\pi^2}{x}\rig
ht) = -\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi^2}{x}\right) = -g(x),$$
and $g(0) = 0$. Hence $g$ is an odd function on $[-\pi, \pi]$.

**Fourier coefficients.** The Fourier coefficients of $f$ are:
$$a_0 = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(x)\,dx, \quad a_k = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{
-\pi}^{\pi} f(x)\cos(kx)\,dx, \quad b_k = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(x)\si
n(kx)\,dx.$$

Since $f = 1 + g$:
- The constant $1$ contributes $a_0^{(1)} = 2$ and $a_k^{(1)} = 0$ for $k \geq 1
$.
- For $g$: since $g$ is odd and $\cos(kx)$ is even, the integrand $g(x)\cos(kx)$
 is **odd**, so
$$a_0^{(g)} = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} g(x)\,dx = 0, \qquad a_k^{(g)} = \f
rac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} g(x)\cos(kx)\,dx = 0 \quad (k \geq 1).$$

Therefore:
$$a_0 = 2, \qquad a_k = 0 \;\;\text{for all } k \geq 1.$$

(The sine coefficients $b_k$ are generally nonzero, but they will not matter at
$x=0$.)

**Partial sum at $x = 0$.** The $n$-th partial sum of the Fourier series is:
$$S_n(0) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{k=1}^{n}\bigl(a_k \cos(0) + b_k \sin(0)\bigr) =
 \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k = \frac{2}{2} + 0 = 1.$$

This holds for **every** $n \geq 0$, so the sequence of partial sums is constant
: $S_n(0) = 1$ for all $n$.

**Conclusion.** The Fourier series of $f$ at $x = 0$ converges, and its sum is
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} S_n(0) = 1 = f(0).$$

The Fourier series converges at $x=0$ to the value $f(0)=1$.
