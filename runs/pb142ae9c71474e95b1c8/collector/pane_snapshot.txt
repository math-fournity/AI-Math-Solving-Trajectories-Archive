## Proof

We work in the Hilbert space $H = L^2[-1,1]$ with inner product $\langle f, g\ra
ngle = \int_{-1}^{1} f(x)g(x)\,dx$.

The subspace $S$ is defined by the constraint
$$\int_{-1}^{1} x\, f(x)\,dx = 0,$$
which is precisely the condition $\langle x, f \rangle = 0$, where we identify $
x$ with the function $t \mapsto t$. Thus
$$S = \{x\}^{\perp},$$
the orthogonal complement of the one-dimensional subspace spanned by the functio
n $x(t)=t$.

**Orthogonal decomposition.** Since $\{x\}$ is a one-dimensional (hence closed)
subspace of $H$, we have the orthogonal decomposition
$$H = \operatorname{span}\{x\} \oplus S.$$
The element of $S$ closest to a given $g \in H$ is the orthogonal projection of
$g$ onto $S$:
$$P_S(g) = g - \frac{\langle g, x\rangle}{\langle x, x\rangle}\, x.$$

**Compute the projection coefficient.**
$$\langle g, x\rangle = \int_{-1}^{1} x^2 \cdot x\, dx = \int_{-1}^{1} x^3\, dx.
$$
The integrand $x^3$ is an odd function, and the interval $[-1,1]$ is symmetric a
bout $0$, so
$$\int_{-1}^{1} x^3\, dx = 0.$$

Therefore
$$\frac{\langle g, x\rangle}{\langle x, x\rangle} = 0,$$
and
$$P_S(g) = g - 0 \cdot x = g = x^2.$$

**Verification.** We confirm $g \in S$ directly:
$$\int_{-1}^{1} x \cdot x^2\, dx = \int_{-1}^{1} x^3\, dx = 0. \checkmark$$

Since $g = x^2$ already lies in $S$, it is its own closest point in $S$ (distanc
e $0$).

**Answer:** The element of $S$ closest to $g(x) = x^2$ is

$$\boxed{x^2}.$$
