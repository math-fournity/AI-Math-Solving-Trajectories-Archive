## Proof

Let $X = C([0,1])$ equipped with the supremum norm $\|\cdot\|_\infty$. Define
$$F(u)(x) = \int_0^1 K(x,y)\, f(u(y))\, dy.$$

### Claim (Candidate derivative)

For $u, h \in X$, define the linear operator $L_u : X \to X$ by
$$L_u h(x) = \int_0^1 K(x,y)\, f'(u(y))\, h(y)\, dy.$$
We claim $DF(u) = L_u$, i.e. $L_u$ is the Fréchet derivative of $F$ at $u$.

### Step 1. $L_u$ is a bounded linear operator $X \to X$.

Linearity in $h$ is immediate. For boundedness, since $K$ is continuous on the c
ompact set $[0,1]^2$ and $u$ is continuous on $[0,1]$, the function $(x,y) \maps
to K(x,y) f'(u(y))$ is continuous on $[0,1]^2$, hence bounded. Let $M_u = \sup_{
x,y} |K(x,y) f'(u(y))|$. Then
$$|L_u h(x)| \le M_u \int_0^1 |h(y)|\, dy \le M_u \|h\|_\infty,$$
so $\|L_u h\|_\infty \le M_u \|h\|_\infty$. Continuity of $x \mapsto L_u h(x)$ f
ollows by dominated convergence (or uniform continuity of $K$). Thus $L_u \in \m
athcal{L}(X)$.

### Step 2. Fréchet differentiability.

We must show
$$F(u+h) - F(u) - L_u h = o(\|h\|_\infty) \quad \text{as } \|h\|_\infty \to 0.$$

By the mean value theorem applied to $f$ pointwise at each $y$,
$$f(u(y)+h(y)) - f(u(y)) = f'(u(y))\, h(y) + R(y),$$
where the remainder satisfies
$$R(y) = \big[f'(\xi(y)) - f'(u(y))\big] h(y)$$
for some $\xi(y)$ between $u(y)$ and $u(y)+h(y)$.

Therefore
$$\big(F(u+h) - F(u) - L_u h\big)(x) = \int_0^1 K(x,y)\, R(y)\, dy.$$

Set $K_{\max} = \sup_{x,y}|K(x,y)| < \infty$. Then
$$\|F(u+h) - F(u) - L_u h\|_\infty \le K_{\max} \sup_{y \in [0,1]} |R(y)| \le K_
{\max} \sup_{y} |f'(\xi(y)) - f'(u(y))|\cdot \|h\|_\infty.$$

Now $f'$ is continuous (since $f \in C^1$), and $u([0,1])$ is a compact interval
. Given $\varepsilon > 0$, by uniform continuity of $f'$ on a compact interval c
ontaining $u([0,1])$, there exists $\delta > 0$ such that $|s - t| < \delta \Rig
htarrow |f'(s)-f'(t)| < \varepsilon/K_{\max}$ (if $K_{\max}>0$; trivial otherwis
e). For $\|h\|_\infty < \delta$, we have $|\xi(y) - u(y)| \le |h(y)| \le \|h\|_\
infty < \delta$ for all $y$, hence
$$\|F(u+h) - F(u) - L_u h\|_\infty \le \varepsilon\, \|h\|_\infty.$$

This proves $F$ is Fréchet differentiable at $u$ with
$$\boxed{DF(u)\, h(x) = \int_0^1 K(x,y)\, f'(u(y))\, h(y)\, dy.}$$

### Step 3. Continuity of $u \mapsto DF(u)$.

We show the map $u \mapsto DF(u) = L_u \in \mathcal{L}(X)$ is continuous in the
operator norm. It suffices to show $\|L_{u} - L_{v}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \to 0$ as $\
|u - v\|_\infty \to 0$.

For any $h \in X$ with $\|h\|_\infty \le 1$:
$$\big|(L_u - L_v)h(x)\big| \le \int_0^1 |K(x,y)|\, |f'(u(y)) - f'(v(y))|\, |h(y
)|\, dy \le K_{\max}\, \sup_{y}|f'(u(y)) - f'(v(y))|.$$

Hence
$$\|L_u - L_v\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le K_{\max}\, \sup_{y \in [0,1]} |f'(u(y)) - f'(v
(y))|.$$

Since $f'$ is continuous and $u, v$ range over the compact set $[0,1]$ with valu
es in a compact interval (when $\|u-v\|_\infty$ is small, both take values in a
common compact interval), $f'$ is uniformly continuous there. Given $\varepsilon
 > 0$, choose $\delta > 0$ such that $|s-t|<\delta \Rightarrow |f'(s)-f'(t)| < \
varepsilon/K_{\max}$. Then $\|u-v\|_\infty < \delta$ implies
$$\|L_u - L_v\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \varepsilon.$$

Therefore $u \mapsto DF(u)$ is continuous (in fact $F$ is of class $C^1$).

### Summary

The Fréchet derivative of $F$ at $u \in X$ is the bounded linear operator
$$DF(u): X \to X, \qquad \big(DF(u)\, h\big)(x) = \int_0^1 K(x,y)\, f'(u(y))\, h
(y)\, dy,$$
and the map $u \mapsto DF(u)$ is continuous from $X$ into $\mathcal{L}(X)$ (oper
ator norm), so $F$ is a $C^1$-map on $X$.
