## Proof

**Setup.** Let $x_{i,j} \in \{0,1\}$ for $i,j \in \{0,1,2,3,4\}$, where $x_{i,j}
=1$ means red. Each unit square at position $(i,j)$ (for $i,j \in \{0,1,2,3\}$)
satisfies:
$$x_{i,j} + x_{i+1,j} + x_{i,j+1} + x_{i+1,j+1} = 2.$$

**Key reduction.** Define $s_j(i) = x_{i,j} + x_{i,j+1}$ (sum of two vertically
adjacent vertices in column $i$, row-pair $j$). The square constraint at $(i,j)$
 becomes $s_j(i) + s_j(i+1) = 2$.

From consecutive squares in a row: $s_j(i) + s_j(i+1) = 2$ and $s_j(i+1) + s_j(i
+2) = 2$ give $s_j(i) = s_j(i+2)$. So **$s_j$ is periodic with period 2** in the
 column index:
$$s_j(0) = s_j(2) = s_j(4) = a_j, \quad s_j(1) = s_j(3) = 2 - a_j,$$
where $a_j \in \{0,1,2\}$ for each $j \in \{0,1,2,3\}$.

**Column independence.** Since the square constraints are fully encoded by the $
s_j$ values, and $s_j(i)$ depends only on column $i$, the columns are **independ
ent** once $(a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3)$ is fixed. The total count is:
$$\sum_{a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3 \in \{0,1,2\}} N_{\text{even}}^3 \cdot N_{\text{odd}}^2,
$$
where $N_{\text{even}}$ (resp. $N_{\text{odd}}$) is the number of valid assignme
nts for an even (resp. odd) column.

**Per-column count.** Define $d_0 = a_0,\ d_1 = a_1 - a_0,\ d_2 = a_2 - a_1 + a_
0,\ d_3 = a_3 - a_2 + a_1 - a_0$.

For an **even column** ($s_j = a_j$), choosing $c = x_{i,0} \in \{0,1\}$ determi
nes all entries:
$$x_{i,1} = d_0 - c,\ x_{i,2} = d_1 + c,\ x_{i,3} = d_2 - c,\ x_{i,4} = d_3 + c.
$$
- $c=0$ valid iff $d_k \in \{0,1\}$ for all $k$ — call this **condition A**.
- $c=1$ valid iff $d_0 \in \{1,2\},\ d_1 \in \{-1,0\},\ d_2 \in \{1,2\},\ d_3 \i
n \{-1,0\}$ — call this **condition B**.

For an **odd column** ($s_j = 2-a_j$), a similar computation gives:
- $c=0$ valid iff **condition B** holds.
- $c=1$ valid iff **condition A** holds.

Therefore $N_{\text{even}} = N_{\text{odd}} = N(d) := [\text{A}] + [\text{B}]$,
and the total becomes $\sum N(d)^5$.

**Enumeration.** The inverse map is $a_0 = d_0,\ a_1 = d_0+d_1,\ a_2 = d_1+d_2,\
 a_3 = d_2+d_3$. One verifies that **all** vectors satisfying A or B give $a_k \
in \{0,1,2\}$:

- **Condition A:** $(d_0,d_1,d_2,d_3) \in \{0,1\}^4$ — $16$ vectors.
- **Condition B:** $d_0 \in \{1,2\},\ d_1 \in \{-1,0\},\ d_2 \in \{1,2\},\ d_3 \
in \{-1,0\}$ — $16$ vectors.
- **Overlap** (both A and B): $d_0=1,\ d_1=0,\ d_2=1,\ d_3=0$ — exactly $1$ vect
or.

For this overlap vector, $N = 2$. For the other $15+15=30$ vectors, $N = 1$. All
 remaining valid $d$-vectors have $N = 0$.

**Final count:**
$$\sum N(d)^5 = 30 \cdot 1^5 + 1 \cdot 2^5 = 30 + 32 = \boxed{62}.$$

- **1×1 grid** (4 vertices, 1 square): formula gives $1^2+2^2+1^2 = 6 = \binom{4
}{2}$. ✓
