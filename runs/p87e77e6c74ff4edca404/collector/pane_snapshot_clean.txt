## Proof

**Construction.** For $n \geq 10$, start with the transitive tournament $T_0$ on
 vertex set $[n] = \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}$, where $i \to j$ whenever $i < j$. Then
flip the single edge between $1$ and $n$: replace $1 \to n$ with $n \to 1$. Call
 the resulting tournament $T$.

**Condition (2): No vertex has outdegree $n-1$.**

In $T_0$, vertex $1$ has outdegree $n-1$ (it beats every other vertex). After fl
ipping the edge $1 \to n$ to $n \to 1$, vertex $1$'s outdegree becomes $n-2$. Ev
ery other vertex $i$ with $2 \leq i \leq n-1$ has its edges to $1$ and $n$ uncha
nged (since $1 < i < n$, we still have $1 \to i$ and $i \to n$), so its outdegre
e remains $n - i \leq n - 2$. Vertex $n$ now has outdegree $1$ (beats only verte
x $1$). Thus the maximum outdegree is $n - 2 < n - 1$. ✓

**Condition (1): Every subtournament of size $\geq \sqrt{n}$ is rigid.**

Since $n \geq 10$, we have $\sqrt{n} > 3$, so every subtournament of size $\geq
\sqrt{n}$ has size $\geq 4$.

Let $V' \subseteq [n]$ with $|V'| = k \geq 4$, and consider the induced subtourn
ament $T[V']$. We split into cases based on whether $V'$ contains both $1$ and $
n$.

**Case A: $V'$ does not contain both $1$ and $n$.**

The only edge of $T$ that differs from $T_0$ is $n \to 1$ (instead of $1 \to n$)
. Since at least one of $1, n$ is absent from $V'$, this edge is not present in
$T[V']$. Therefore $T[V']$ is a subtournament of the transitive tournament $T_0$
, hence is itself transitive. A transitive tournament admits a unique topologica
l ordering, so its only automorphism is the identity. Thus $T[V']$ is rigid. ✓

**Case B: $V'$ contains both $1$ and $n$.**

Write $V' = \{1, n\} \cup S$ where $S \subseteq \{2, \ldots, n-1\}$ and $|S| = m
 \geq k - 2 \geq 2$. Enumerate $S = \{s_1 < s_2 < \cdots < s_m\}$.

The edges of $T[V']$ are:
- $n \to 1$ (the flipped edge),
- $1 \to s_i$ for all $i$ (since $1 < s_i$),
- $s_i \to n$ for all $i$ (since $s_i < n$),
- $s_i \to s_j$ for $i < j$ (transitive within $S$).

**Outdegrees in $T[V']$:**
- $\deg^+(n) = 1$ (beats only $1$),
- $\deg^+(1) = m$ (beats all $s_i$),
- $\deg^+(s_i) = (m - i) + 1 = m - i + 1$ (beats $s_{i+1}, \ldots, s_m$ and $n$)
.

So the outdegree sequence is: $1, m, m, m{-}1, m{-}2, \ldots, 2, 1$, correspondi
ng to vertices $n, 1, s_1, s_2, \ldots, s_m$.

Since $m \geq 2$, there are exactly two pairs of vertices sharing the same outde
gree:
- $n$ and $s_m$ both have outdegree $1$,
- $1$ and $s_1$ both have outdegree $m$.

All other vertices have distinct outdegrees. Any automorphism $\sigma$ of $T[V']
$ must preserve outdegrees, so $\sigma$ must either fix or swap each of these tw
o pairs, and must fix all other vertices. This gives at most $4$ candidates. We
check each:

1. **Identity:** trivially works. ✓

2. **Swap $n \leftrightarrow s_m$, fix all others:** The edge $n \to 1$ must map
 to $s_m \to 1$. But $1 < s_m$, so $1 \to s_m$, meaning $s_m \to 1$ is **not** a
n edge. Contradiction. ✗

3. **Swap $1 \leftrightarrow s_1$, fix all others:** The edge $1 \to s_1$ must m
ap to $s_1 \to 1$. But $1 < s_1$ gives $1 \to s_1$, so $s_1 \to 1$ is **not** an
 edge. Contradiction. ✗

4. **Swap $n \leftrightarrow s_m$ and $1 \leftrightarrow s_1$, fix all others:**
 The edge $n \to 1$ must map to $s_m \to s_1$. But $s_1 < s_m$ gives $s_1 \to s_
m$, so $s_m \to s_1$ is **not** an edge. Contradiction. ✗

No nontrivial automorphism exists. Thus $T[V']$ is rigid. ✓

**Conclusion.** Both cases confirm that every subtournament of size $\geq 4$ (he
nce $\geq \sqrt{n}$ for $n \geq 10$) is rigid, and no vertex has outdegree $n -
1$. Therefore, for all $n \geq 10$ (and thus for sufficiently large $n$), such a
 tournament exists.
