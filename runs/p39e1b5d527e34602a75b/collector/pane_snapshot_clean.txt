## Derivation of the Geodesic Equation from the Energy Functional

### Setup

Let $(M, g)$ be a Riemannian manifold with metric $g = g_{ij}\,dx^i \otimes dx^j
$ in local coordinates. Let $\gamma: [a,b] \to M$ be a smooth curve with coordin
ate representation $x^i(t) = \gamma^i(t)$, and denote $\dot{x}^i = dx^i/dt$.

The **energy functional** is

$$E[\gamma] = \frac{1}{2}\int_a^b g_{ij}(x(t))\,\dot{x}^i(t)\,\dot{x}^j(t)\,dt.$
$

A geodesic is a stationary point of $E$ under variations that fix the endpoints.

### Step 1: Introduce a variation

Let $\gamma_s$ be a one-parameter family of curves with $\gamma_0 = \gamma$, $\g
amma_s(a) = \gamma(a)$, $\gamma_s(b) = \gamma(b)$ for all $s$. Define the variat
ion field

$$V^i(t) = \left.\frac{\partial x^i_s(t)}{\partial s}\right|_{s=0}, \qquad V^i(a
) = V^i(b) = 0.$$

Note that $\partial \dot{x}^i/\partial s = \partial/\partial s\,(dx^i/dt) = d/dt
\,(\partial x^i/\partial s) = \dot{V}^i$.

### Step 2: Compute the first variation

$$\frac{dE}{ds}\bigg|_{s=0} = \frac{1}{2}\int_a^b \Big[\partial_k g_{ij}\, V^k\,
 \dot{x}^i \dot{x}^j + g_{ij}\,\dot{V}^i\,\dot{x}^j + g_{ij}\,\dot{x}^i\,\dot{V}
^j\Big]\,dt.$$

Using the symmetry $g_{ij} = g_{ji}$, the last two terms combine:

$$\frac{dE}{ds}\bigg|_{s=0} = \frac{1}{2}\int_a^b \partial_k g_{ij}\, V^k\, \dot
{x}^i \dot{x}^j\,dt + \int_a^b g_{ij}\,\dot{x}^i\,\dot{V}^j\,dt.$$

### Step 3: Integrate by parts

Integrate the second term by parts:

$$\int_a^b g_{ij}\,\dot{x}^i\,\dot{V}^j\,dt = \Big[g_{ij}\,\dot{x}^i\, V^j\Big]_
a^b - \int_a^b \frac{d}{dt}\!\big(g_{ij}\,\dot{x}^i\big)\, V^j\,dt.$$

The boundary term vanishes because $V^j(a) = V^j(b) = 0$. Thus

$$\frac{dE}{ds}\bigg|_{s=0} = \int_a^b \left[\frac{1}{2}\,\partial_j g_{ik}\,\do
t{x}^i \dot{x}^k - \frac{d}{dt}\!\big(g_{ij}\,\dot{x}^i\big)\right] V^j\,dt,$$

where in the first term we relabeled the dummy index $k \to j$ (the index contra
cted with $V$).

### Step 4: Apply the fundamental lemma

For $\frac{dE}{ds}\big|_{s=0} = 0$ to hold for **all** variations $V^j$ vanishin
g at the endpoints, the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations gives th
e **Euler–Lagrange equation**:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\!\big(g_{ij}\,\dot{x}^i\big) - \frac{1}{2}\,\partial_j g_{ik}\,\d
ot{x}^i \dot{x}^k = 0.$$

### Step 5: Expand and identify the Christoffel symbols

Expand the total derivative:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\!\big(g_{ij}\,\dot{x}^i\big) = \partial_k g_{ij}\,\dot{x}^k \dot{
x}^i + g_{ij}\,\ddot{x}^i.$$

Since $\dot{x}^i \dot{x}^k$ is symmetric in $(i,k)$, we symmetrize the first pie
ce:

$$\partial_k g_{ij}\,\dot{x}^i \dot{x}^k = \frac{1}{2}\big(\partial_k g_{ij} + \
partial_i g_{kj}\big)\,\dot{x}^i \dot{x}^k.$$

Substituting:

$$g_{ij}\,\ddot{x}^i + \frac{1}{2}\big(\partial_k g_{ij} + \partial_i g_{kj} - \
partial_j g_{ik}\big)\,\dot{x}^i \dot{x}^k = 0.$$

The bracketed expression is precisely the **Christoffel symbol of the first kind
**:

$$[ik, j] \;=\; \Gamma_{j,ik} \;=\; \frac{1}{2}\big(\partial_i g_{kj} + \partial
_k g_{ij} - \partial_j g_{ik}\big).$$

Hence

$$g_{ij}\,\ddot{x}^i + [ik, j]\,\dot{x}^i \dot{x}^k = 0.$$

### Step 6: Raise the index

Multiply through by $g^{jl}$ and sum over $j$:

$$g^{jl}g_{ij}\,\ddot{x}^i + g^{jl}\,[ik, j]\,\dot{x}^i \dot{x}^k = 0.$$

Using $g^{jl}g_{ij} = \delta^l_i$ and the definition of the **Christoffel symbol
 of the second kind** $\Gamma^l_{ik} = g^{jl}\,[ik, j]$:

$$\ddot{x}^l + \Gamma^l_{ik}\,\dot{x}^i \dot{x}^k = 0.$$

### Result

The geodesic equation for a general Riemannian metric $g_{ij}$ is

$$\boxed{\;\ddot{x}^l + \Gamma^l_{ik}\,\dot{x}^i \dot{x}^k = 0\;}$$

where

$$\Gamma^l_{ik} = \frac{1}{2}\,g^{lj}\!\left(\partial_i g_{kj} + \partial_k g_{i
j} - \partial_j g_{ik}\right)$$

are the Levi-Civita connection coefficients. Equivalently, in coordinate-free fo
rm, a geodesic satisfies $\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma} = 0$, where $\nabla$
 is the Levi-Civita connection of $g$.
