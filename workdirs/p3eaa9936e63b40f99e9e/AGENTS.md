# Problem

A specialized acoustics lab is testing sound wave interference patterns generated by digital synthesizers. For each synthesizer model identified by a positive integer power level $r$, engineers calibrate a signal sequence $\{ a_n \}_{n\ge 1}$. Each term in the sequence is defined such that the next frequency $a_{n+1}$ is exactly $r$ times the previous frequency $a_n$.

For a signal to be considered "pure" at a specific step $n$, its frequency $a_n$ must avoid falling too close to any whole-number harmonic. Specifically, the deviation of the frequency from the nearest integer harmonic is measured as $\| a_n \|$. A synthesizer is rated by a stability constant $C(r)$, which is defined as the maximum possible value such that an initial frequency $a_1$ can be chosen to ensure that every single frequency in the infinite sequence $\{ a_n \}_{n\ge 1}$ maintains a distance of at least $C(r)$ from its nearest integer harmonic.

Determine the exact value of the total stability sum for the first ten power levels:
$$\sum_{r=1}^{10} C(r)$$
