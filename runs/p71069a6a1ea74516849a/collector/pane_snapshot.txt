**Proof.**

Let $(X, d)$ be a metric space, and let $U \subseteq X$ be an open set. We show
that $U$ can be written as a union of open balls.

If $U = \emptyset$, then $U$ is the empty union (the union of an empty collectio
n of open balls), so the claim holds trivially.

Assume $U \neq \emptyset$. For every point $x \in U$, since $U$ is open, there e
xists a radius $r_x > 0$ such that the open ball
$$B(x, r_x) = \{y \in X : d(x, y) < r_x\}$$
is entirely contained in $U$; that is, $B(x, r_x) \subseteq U$. This is precisel
y the definition of openness in a metric space.

Now consider the collection of open balls
$$\mathcal{B} = \{B(x, r_x) : x \in U\}.$$

We claim that
$$U = \bigcup_{x \in U} B(x, r_x).$$

We verify this by showing containment in both directions:

- **($\subseteq$)** For every $x \in U$, we have $x \in B(x, r_x)$ (since $d(x,
x) = 0 < r_x$), and $B(x, r_x)$ is one of the sets in the union. Hence $x \in \b
igcup_{x \in U} B(x, r_x)$. This shows $U \subseteq \bigcup_{x \in U} B(x, r_x)$
.

- **($\supseteq$)** Every ball $B(x, r_x)$ in the collection satisfies $B(x, r_x
) \subseteq U$ by construction. Therefore their union is also contained in $U$:
$\bigcup_{x \in U} B(x, r_x) \subseteq U$.

Both inclusions hold, so $U = \bigcup_{x \in U} B(x, r_x)$.

Thus every open set $U$ in the metric space $(X, d)$ is expressible as a union o
f open balls. $\blacksquare$
