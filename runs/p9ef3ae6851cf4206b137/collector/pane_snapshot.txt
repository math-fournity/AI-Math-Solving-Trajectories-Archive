Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a holomorphic $f$ on the un
it disk $D$, with $|f(z)|\to 1$ as $|z|\to 1$ and $f$ nonzero on $D$, extends co
ntinuously to $\partial D$.

**Answer: Yes. In fact, $f$ must be a constant of modulus 1, which extends trivi
ally.**

**Proof.**

Since $D$ is simply connected and $f$ is holomorphic and nonzero on $D$, there e
xists a holomorphic function $g: D \to \mathbb{C}$ such that
$$f(z) = e^{g(z)}, \quad z \in D.$$
(This is the holomorphic logarithm, guaranteed by simple connectivity of $D$ and
 nonvanishing of $f$.)

Set $u(z) = \operatorname{Re} g(z) = \log|f(z)|$. Then $u$ is harmonic on $D$. T
he boundary condition $|f(z)| \to 1$ as $|z| \to 1$ gives
$$u(z) = \log|f(z)| \to \log 1 = 0 \quad \text{as } |z| \to 1.$$

**Claim:** $u \equiv 0$ on $D$.

*Proof of claim.* Let $\varepsilon > 0$. By the boundary condition, there exists
 $\delta > 0$ such that $|u(z)| < \varepsilon$ whenever $1-\delta < |z| < 1$. Fo
r any $r$ with $1-\delta < r < 1$, the maximum principle for harmonic functions
yields
$$\max_{|z| \le r}|u(z)| = \max_{|z|=r}|u(z)| \le \varepsilon,$$
since every point on $|z|=r$ satisfies $1-\delta < |z| < 1$. Letting $r \to 1^-$
, we obtain $|u(z)| \le \varepsilon$ for all $z \in D$. As $\varepsilon > 0$ was
 arbitrary, $u \equiv 0$. $\square$

Now $g$ is holomorphic on $D$ with $\operatorname{Re} g \equiv 0$. By the Cauchy
–Riemann equations (or the open mapping theorem—a nonconstant holomorphic functi
on has open image, but $\operatorname{Re} g = 0$ confines the image to the imagi
nary axis, which has empty interior), $g$ must be constant:
$$g(z) = i\theta_0, \quad \theta_0 \in \mathbb{R}.$$

Therefore
$$f(z) = e^{i\theta_0}$$
is a constant of modulus $1$ on all of $D$.

**Continuous extension.** Since $f$ is constant on $D$, it extends to $\overline
{D}$ by setting $f(z) = e^{i\theta_0}$ for $|z| = 1$ as well. This extension is
continuous (indeed constant) on $\overline{D}$.

**Uniqueness.** Any continuous extension of $f$ to $\overline{D}$ is uniquely de
termined: if two continuous functions agree on the dense subset $D \subset \over
line{D}$, they agree on $\overline{D}$ by continuity. Hence the extension is uni
que.

**Conclusion.** A holomorphic function $f$ on $D$ that is nonzero and satisfies
$|f(z)| \to 1$ as $|z| \to 1$ is necessarily a constant of modulus $1$. Such a f
unction extends uniquely and continuously to $\partial D$. $\blacksquare$
