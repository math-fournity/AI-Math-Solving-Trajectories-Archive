Proof

Construction. Let $p_1, p_2, p_3, \ldots$ be an enumeration of the primes. For
each non-empty subset $S \subseteq \mathbb{N}$, define the abelian group

$$G_S = \bigoplus_{n \in S} \left(\mathbb{Z}/

where $\left(\mathbb{Z}/p_n\mathbb{Z}\right)^{(\mathfrak{c})}$ denotes the
direct sum of $\mathfrak{c}$ copies of $\mathbb{Z}/p_n\mathbb{Z}$.

Since $|[0,1]| = \mathfrak{c}$ and the number of non-empty subsets of $
\mathbb{N}$ is $2^{\aleph_0} - 1 = \mathfrak{c}$, there exists a bijection $
\varphi: [0,1] \to {S \subseteq \mathbb{N} : S \neq \emptyset}$. Set $G_s :=
G_{\varphi(s)}$.

Claim 1: Each $G_S$ has cardinality $\mathfrak{c}$.

The group $\left(\mathbb{Z}/p_n\mathbb{Z}\right)^{(\mathfrak{c})}$ consists of
all finitely-supported functions $\mathfrak{c} \to \mathbb{Z}/p_n\mathbb{Z}$.
The number of finite subsets of $\mathfrak{c}$ is $\mathfrak{c}$, and for each
finite subset of size $k$ there are $p_n^k$ choices, so the cardinality is $
\mathfrak{c} \cdot \aleph_0 = \mathfrak{c}$.

The direct sum $G_S = \bigoplus_{n \in S} \left(\mathbb{Z}/
p_n\mathbb{Z}\right)^{(\mathfrak{c})}$ consists of tuples $(x_n)_{n \in S}$ with
$x_n \in \left(\mathbb{Z}/p_n\mathbb{Z}\right)^{(\mathfrak{c})}$ and only
finitely many $x_n \neq 0$. Its cardinality is

$$\sum_{\substack{F \subseteq S \ F \text{ finite}}} \prod_{n \in F}
\mathfrak{c} = \sum_{F \text{ finite}} \mathfrak{c}^{|F|} = \aleph_0 \cdot
\mathfrak{c} = \mathfrak{c},$$

since $S$ is non-empty and countable. $\checkmark$

Claim 2: $G_S \not\cong G_T$ whenever $S \neq T$.

The group $G_S$ is a direct sum of cyclic groups of prime-power order, so it
decomposes uniquely into its $p$-primary components:

$$G_S = \bigoplus_{p \text{ prime}} \left(G_S\right)_p, \quad \text{where }
\left(G_S\right)_p = {x \in G_S : px = 0}.$$

For the prime $p_n$:

  • If $n \in S$, then $\left(G_S\right){p_n} = \left(\mathbb{Z}/
  p_n\mathbb{Z}\right)^{(\mathfrak{c})}$, which is an $\mathbb{F}{p_n}$-vector
  space of dimension $\mathfrak{c}$.
  • If $n \notin S$, then $\left(G_S\right)_{p_n} = 0$.

This primary decomposition is preserved under group isomorphism: any isomorphism
$\phi: G_S \to G_T$ restricts to an isomorphism $\left(G_S\right){p_n} \to
\left(G_T\right){p_n}$ for each $n$.

\setminus T$. Then $\left(G_S\right){p_n} \cong \left(\mathbb{Z}/
p_n\mathbb{Z}\right)^{(\mathfrak{c})}$ is nontrivial (dimension $\mathfrak{c}$
over $\mathbb{F}{p_n}$), while $\left(G_T\right)_{p_n} = 0$. A nontrivial group
cannot be isomorphic to the trivial group, so $G_S \not\cong G_T$. $\checkmark$
