**Answer: No.** Here is a counterexample.

---

**Setup.** Let $\Omega = \mathbb{Q}$ with $\Sigma = \mathscr{B}(\mathbb{Q})$, an
d let $X:\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{R}$ be the inclusion map $X(q)=q$. Then $X$ is mea
surable since $X^{-1}(H)=H\cap\mathbb{Q}$ for every Borel $H\subseteq\mathbb{R}$
.

Let $\mathscr{P} = \{(-\infty, t] : t\in\mathbb{R}\}$, a π-system generating $\m
athscr{B}(\mathbb{R})$.

Let $\nu = \tfrac{1}{2}(\delta_0 + \delta_{\sqrt{2}})$, a Borel probability meas
ure on $\mathbb{R}$.

---

**The assumption is satisfied (vacuously).** We must check: if $(-\infty,t_1], (
-\infty,t_2]\in\mathscr{P}$ with $X^{-1}((-\infty,t_1])=X^{-1}((-\infty,t_2])$,
then $\nu((-\infty,t_1])=\nu((-\infty,t_2])$.

Now $X^{-1}((-\infty,t]) = (-\infty,t]\cap\mathbb{Q} = \{q\in\mathbb{Q}: q\le t\
}$. We claim $(-\infty,t_1]\cap\mathbb{Q} = (-\infty,t_2]\cap\mathbb{Q}$ implies
 $t_1=t_2$.

Indeed, if $t_1 < t_2$, by density of $\mathbb{Q}$ in $\mathbb{R}$ there exists
$q\in\mathbb{Q}$ with $t_1 < q < t_2$, so $q\in(-\infty,t_2]\cap\mathbb{Q}$ but
$q\notin(-\infty,t_1]\cap\mathbb{Q}$, a contradiction. By symmetry $t_1=t_2$.

Therefore the hypothesis $X^{-1}(H_1)=X^{-1}(H_2)$ for $H_1,H_2\in\mathscr{P}$ f
orces $H_1=H_2$, and the conclusion $\nu(H_1)=\nu(H_2)$ holds trivially. The ass
umption is satisfied. ✓

---

**The conclusion fails.** Take $H_1 = \{0, \sqrt{2}\}$ and $H_2 = \{0\}$, both B
orel sets.

- $X^{-1}(H_1) = H_1\cap\mathbb{Q} = \{0\}$ (since $\sqrt{2}\notin\mathbb{Q}$).
- $X^{-1}(H_2) = H_2\cap\mathbb{Q} = \{0\}$.

So $X^{-1}(H_1) = X^{-1}(H_2) = \{0\}$. ✓

Yet:
- $\nu(H_1) = \tfrac{1}{2}(\delta_0(\{0,\sqrt{2}\}) + \delta_{\sqrt{2}}(\{0,\sqr
t{2}\})) = \tfrac{1}{2}(1+1) = 1$.
- $\nu(H_2) = \tfrac{1}{2}(\delta_0(\{0\}) + \delta_{\sqrt{2}}(\{0\})) = \tfrac{
1}{2}(1+0) = \tfrac{1}{2}$.

Thus $\nu(H_1) = 1 \ne \tfrac{1}{2} = \nu(H_2)$, even though $X^{-1}(H_1)=X^{-1}
(H_2)$. ✗

---

**Why it fails.** The image $X(\Omega)=\mathbb{Q}$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}$, so
the π-system $\{(-\infty,t]\}$ cannot produce two *distinct* sets with the same
preimage under $X$—the assumption is vacuous and imposes no constraint on $\nu$.
 Yet Borel sets *can* agree on $\mathbb{Q}$ while differing on irrationals (e.g.
, $\{0,\sqrt{2}\}$ vs $\{0\}$), and $\nu$ has mass at the irrational point $\sqr
t{2}$, detecting this difference.
