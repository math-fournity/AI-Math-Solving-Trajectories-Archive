## Proof

Let $A(X), B(X) \in \mathbb{Z}[X]$ be nonzero polynomials with $A(\alpha) = 0$ a
nd $B(\beta) = 0$. We may assume $A$ is irreducible (the minimal polynomial of $
\alpha$).

### Step 1: An auxiliary polynomial $D(T) \in \mathbb{Z}[T]$

Write $B(X) = B_e(X^2) + X \cdot B_o(X^2)$ where $B_e(U) = \sum_j b_{2j} U^j$ an
d $B_o(U) = \sum_j b_{2j+1} U^j$ are the even and odd parts. Define

$$D(T) = B_e(-T^2)^2 + T^2 \cdot B_o(-T^2)^2 \;\in\; \mathbb{Z}[T].$$

**Key identity:** $D(T) = B(iT)\cdot B(-iT)$. Indeed, $B(iT) = B_e(-T^2) + iT\,
B_o(-T^2)$ and $B(-iT) = B_e(-T^2) - iT\, B_o(-T^2)$, so their product is $B_e(-
T^2)^2 + T^2 B_o(-T^2)^2 = D(T)$. Since $B(iT)$ and $B(-iT)$ are conjugate, $D(T
) \in \mathbb{Z}[T]$ (one also verifies directly: the coefficient of $T^m$ is $i
^m \sum_{k+\ell=m} b_k b_\ell (-1)^\ell$, which vanishes for odd $m$ by the pair
ing $(k,\ell)\leftrightarrow(\ell,k)$, and is an integer for even $m$). Note $D$
 is **even**: $D(-T) = D(T)$.

**Vanishing property:** $D(i\beta) = B(i\cdot i\beta)\cdot B(-i\cdot i\beta) = B
(-\beta)\cdot B(\beta) = 0$, since $B(\beta)=0$. $\checkmark$

### Step 2: Construction of $C(X)$ via resultant

Define

$$\boxed{C(X) = \operatorname{Res}_Y\!\big(A(Y),\; D(X - Y)\big) \;\in\; \mathbb
{Z}[X].}$$

This is a polynomial in $X$ with integer coefficients, being the resultant of $A
(Y) \in \mathbb{Z}[Y]$ and $D(X-Y) \in \mathbb{Z}[X][Y]$.

**$C$ is not identically zero:** If $C \equiv 0$, then $A(Y)$ and $D(X-Y)$ share
 a common factor in $\mathbb{Q}(X)[Y]$. Since $A$ is irreducible in $\mathbb{Q}[
Y]$, this forces $A(Y) \mid D(X-Y)$ in $\mathbb{Q}(X)[Y]$, hence $A(Y) \mid D(X-
Y)$ in $\mathbb{Q}[X,Y]$ (Gauss's lemma). Then for every root $\gamma$ of $A$, $
D(X-\gamma) \equiv 0$ as a polynomial in $X$, so $D \equiv 0$, which implies $B
\equiv 0$—contradiction.

**Vanishing at $\alpha + i\beta$:** Set $z = \alpha + i\beta$. Then $z - \alpha
= i\beta$, so

$$D(z - \alpha) = D(i\beta) = 0.$$

Since also $A(\alpha) = 0$, the value $\alpha$ is a **common root** of $A(Y)$ an
d $D(z - Y)$ (as polynomials in $Y$). Therefore the resultant vanishes:

$$C(\alpha + i\beta) = C(z) = \operatorname{Res}_Y\!\big(A(Y),\, D(z - Y)\big) =
 0. \qquad\checkmark$$

Since $C \in \mathbb{Z}[X] \subset \mathbb{Q}[X]$ is nonzero and $C(\alpha + i\b
eta) = 0$, the number $\alpha + i\beta$ is **algebraic**. $\blacksquare$

### Step 3: Direct construction of $C_0$ and $C_1$

Write $C(X) = \sum_{m} c_m X^m$ with $c_m \in \mathbb{Z}$. Substituting $X = \al
pha + i\beta$ and expanding via the binomial theorem:

$$C(\alpha + i\beta) = \sum_m c_m \sum_{k=0}^{m} \binom{m}{k} \alpha^{m-k}(i\bet
a)^k.$$

Separating even $k = 2j$ (where $i^{2j} = (-1)^j$) and odd $k = 2j+1$ (where $i^
{2j+1} = (-1)^j i$):

$$C(\alpha+i\beta) = \underbrace{\sum_m c_m \sum_j \binom{m}{2j}(-1)^j \alpha^{m
-2j}\beta^{2j}}_{C_0(\alpha,\beta)} + i\underbrace{\sum_m c_m \sum_j \binom{m}{2
j+1}(-1)^j \alpha^{m-2j-1}\beta^{2j+1}}_{C_1(\alpha,\beta)}.$$

Define the **two-variable polynomials**:

$$\boxed{C_0(X, Y) = \frac{C(X+iY) + C(X-iY)}{2} = \sum_m c_m \sum_j \binom{m}{2
j}(-1)^j\, X^{m-2j}\, Y^{2j}}$$

$$\boxed{C_1(X, Y) = \frac{C(X+iY) - C(X-iY)}{2i} = \sum_m c_m \sum_j \binom{m}{
2j+1}(-1)^j\, X^{m-2j-1}\, Y^{2j+1}}$$

**These lie in $\mathbb{Z}[X,Y]$:** In $C(X+iY)+C(X-iY)$, the odd powers of $i$
cancel and the even powers give $i^{2j}+(-i)^{2j} = 2(-1)^j \in \mathbb{Z}$, so
$C_0 \in \mathbb{Z}[X,Y]$. Similarly, in $C(X+iY)-C(X-iY)$, the even powers canc
el and the odd powers give $i^{2j+1}-(-i)^{2j+1} = 2i(-1)^j$, so dividing by $2i
$ yields integer coefficients: $C_1 \in \mathbb{Z}[X,Y]$.

**Vanishing:** Since $C(\alpha + i\beta) = C_0(\alpha,\beta) + i\, C_1(\alpha,\b
eta) = 0$ and $C_0(\alpha,\beta), C_1(\alpha,\beta)$ are **real numbers** (being
 evaluations of integer-coefficient polynomials at real algebraic numbers), we c
onclude:

$$C_0(\alpha, \beta) = 0 \quad\text{and}\quad C_1(\alpha, \beta) = 0. \qquad\che
ckmark$$

### Summary of the direct constructions

| Object | Construction | Ring |
|--------|-------------|------|
| $D(T)$ | $B(iT)\cdot B(-iT) = B_e(-T^2)^2 + T^2 B_o(-T^2)^2$ | $\mathbb{Z}[T]$
 |
| $C(X)$ | $\operatorname{Res}_Y(A(Y),\, D(X-Y))$ | $\mathbb{Z}[X]$ |
| $C_0(X,Y)$ | $\tfrac{1}{2}[C(X+iY)+C(X-iY)]$ | $\mathbb{Z}[X,Y]$ |
| $C_1(X,Y)$ | $\tfrac{1}{2i}[C(X+iY)-C(X-iY)]$ | $\mathbb{Z}[X,Y]$ |

The construction of $D$ avoids $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ entirely: $D$ is built from $B$ b
y splitting into even/odd parts and combining, yielding $C \in \mathbb{Z}[X]$ di
rectly. Then $C_0$ and $C_1$ are obtained by the real/imaginary part extraction
of $C(X+iY)$, which are explicit integer-coefficient polynomials in two variable
s, both vanishing at $(\alpha, \beta)$.
