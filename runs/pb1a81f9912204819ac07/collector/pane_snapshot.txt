## Solution

**Setup.** An 8×8 chessboard has 64 squares. Two different squares are chosen, s
o the total number of unordered pairs is

$$\binom{64}{2} = \frac{64\cdot 63}{2} = 2016.$$

Two queens attack each other iff the two squares share a row, a column, or a dia
gonal. These three cases are **mutually exclusive** for two *distinct* squares:
- Same row and same column ⟹ same square (impossible).
- Same row and same diagonal: a diagonal contains at most one square per row, so
 two squares in the same row cannot lie on the same diagonal. Similarly for colu
mns.
- Same diagonal in both directions: two diagonals of opposite slope intersect in
 at most one square, so two distinct squares cannot share both diagonal directio
ns.

**Same row.** 8 rows, each contributing $\binom{8}{2}=28$ pairs:

$$8 \times 28 = 224.$$

**Same column.** By symmetry:

$$8 \times 28 = 224.$$

**Same diagonal.** Consider one direction (say NW–SE). The 15 diagonals have len
gths

$$1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,7,6,5,4,3,2,1.$$

The number of attacking pairs in one direction is

$$2\!\left[\binom{2}{2}+\binom{3}{2}+\binom{4}{2}+\binom{5}{2}+\binom{6}{2}+\bin
om{7}{2}\right] + \binom{8}{2}$$

$$= 2(1+3+6+10+15+21) + 28 = 2\cdot 56 + 28 = 140.$$

Both diagonal directions give

$$2 \times 140 = 280.$$

**Total attacking pairs.**

$$224 + 224 + 280 = 728.$$

**Probability.**

$$\frac{728}{2016} = \frac{728\div 56}{2016\div 56} = \frac{13}{36},$$

since $\gcd(728,2016)=56$ (checking: $2016=2\cdot728+560$, $728=560+168$, $560=3
\cdot168+56$, $168=3\cdot56$).

$$\boxed{\dfrac{13}{36}}$$
