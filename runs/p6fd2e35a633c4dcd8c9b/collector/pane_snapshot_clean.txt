Looking at this problem, I need to find a monic polynomial $P(x)$ of degree 2023
 satisfying $P(k) = k^{2023} P\!\left(1 - \frac{1}{k}\right)$ for $k = 1, \ldots
, 2023$, then compute $P(-1) \pmod{2027}$.

## Proof

**Step 1: Establish the functional equation.**

Define $R(x) = x^{2023} P\!\left(\frac{x-1}{x}\right)$. Since $P$ has degree 202
3, $R(x)$ is a polynomial. Computing the leading coefficient: the coefficient of
 $x^{2023}$ in $R(x) = \sum_{j=0}^{2023} c_j\, x^{2023-j}(x-1)^j$ is $\sum_{j=0}
^{2023} c_j = P(1)$. Using the $k=1$ condition, $P(1) = P(0) = c_0$.

So the leading coefficient of $P(x) - R(x)$ is $1 - c_0$. Since $P(k) = R(k)$ fo
r $k = 1, \ldots, 2023$ (2023 roots), we get:

$$P(x) - R(x) = (1 - c_0)\prod_{k=1}^{2023}(x - k) = (1-c_0)\,S(x)$$

where $S(x) = \prod_{k=1}^{2023}(x-k)$. (If $c_0 = 1$, then $P - R \equiv 0$, fo
rcing the functional equation $P(x) = x^{2023}P(T(x))$ identically, which yields
 $P \equiv 0$ via the order-3 argument below — contradicting monicity. So $c_0 \
neq 1$.)

**Step 2: Apply the order-3 substitution.**

Let $T(x) = \frac{x-1}{x}$. Then $T^2(x) = \frac{-1}{x-1}$ and $T^3(x) = x$, wit
h $x \cdot T(x) \cdot T^2(x) = -1$.

From $P(x) = x^{2023}P(T(x)) + (1-c_0)S(x)$, substituting $x \to T(x)$ and $x \t
o T^2(x)$ and chaining:

$$P(x) = [x\,T(x)\,T^2(x)]^{2023} P(x) + (1-c_0)\Big[S(x) + x^{2023}S(T(x)) + [x
T(x)]^{2023}S(T^2(x))\Big]$$

Since $[x\,T(x)\,T^2(x)]^{2023} = (-1)^{2023} = -1$:

$$2P(x) = (1-c_0)\,F(x), \quad \text{where } F(x) = S(x) + x^{2023}S(T(x)) + (x-
1)^{2023}S\!\left(\tfrac{-1}{x-1}\right)$$

**Step 3: Determine $c_0$ from the leading coefficient.**

$F(x)$ has degree 2023. Its leading coefficient: $S(x)$ contributes $1$; $x^{202
3}S(T(x)) = -\prod_{j=1}^{2022}(jx+1)$ has degree 2022 (no $x^{2023}$ term); $(x
-1)^{2023}S(T^2(x)) = -x\prod_{k=2}^{2023}(kx-(k-1))$ has leading coefficient $-
2023!$. So the leading coefficient of $F$ is $1 - 2023!$.

From $2P(x) = (1-c_0)F(x)$ with $P$ monic: $2 = (1-c_0)(1 - 2023!)$, giving $1 -
 c_0 = \frac{2}{1-2023!}$.

Thus: $P(x) = \frac{F(x)}{1 - 2023!}$.

**Step 4: Compute $F(-1)$.**

At $x = -1$: $T(-1) = 2$, $T^2(-1) = \frac{1}{2}$.

- $S(-1) = \prod_{k=1}^{2023}(-1-k) = (-1)^{2023}\prod_{k=2}^{2024}k = -2024!$
- $(-1)^{2023}S(2) = -S(2) = 0$ (since $S(2) = 0$, as $k=2$ is a root)
- $[(-1)\cdot 2]^{2023} S(1/2) = -2^{2023}\cdot\frac{-1}{2^{2023}}\prod_{k=1}^{2
023}(2k-1) = \prod_{k=1}^{2023}(2k-1)$

So $F(-1) = -2024! + \prod_{k=1}^{2023}(2k-1)$.

**Step 5: Reduce modulo 2027.**

Since 2027 is prime, by Wilson's theorem: $2026! \equiv -1 \pmod{2027}$.

$2023! = \frac{2026!}{2026 \cdot 2025 \cdot 2024} \equiv \frac{-1}{(-1)(-2)(-3)}
 = \frac{-1}{-6} = \frac{1}{6} \pmod{2027}$

$2024! = 2024 \cdot 2023! \equiv (-3)\cdot\frac{1}{6} = -\frac{1}{2} \pmod{2027}
$

For $\prod_{k=1}^{2023}(2k-1)$: the factor at $k = 1014$ is $2(1014)-1 = 2027 \e
quiv 0 \pmod{2027}$, so the entire product $\equiv 0 \pmod{2027}$.

Therefore: $F(-1) \equiv -(-\tfrac{1}{2}) + 0 = \frac{1}{2} \pmod{2027}$

And: $1 - 2023! \equiv 1 - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{5}{6} \pmod{2027}$

**Step 6: Compute $P(-1) \pmod{2027}$.**

$$P(-1) = \frac{F(-1)}{1 - 2023!} \equiv \frac{1/2}{5/6} = \frac{3}{5} \pmod{202
7}$$

Since $F(-1)$ and $1 - 2023!$ are both not divisible by 2027, the denominator $b

{-1} \equiv P(-1) \equiv \frac{3}{5} \pmod{2027}$.

Compute $5^{-1} \pmod{2027}$: $2027 = 5 \cdot 405 + 2$, $5 = 2 \cdot 2 + 1$, so
$1 = 5 - 2(2027 - 5 \cdot 405) = 811 \cdot 5 - 2 \cdot 2027$. Thus $5^{-1} \equi

$$n \equiv 3 \cdot 811 = 2433 \equiv 2433 - 2027 = 406 \pmod{2027}$$

**Verification with degree-3 analog:** For $d=3$, the formula gives $P(-1) = F(-
1)/(1-3!) = (-9)/(-5) = 9/5$, which matches direct computation of $P(x) = x^3 +
\frac{1}{5}x^2 - \frac{6}{5}x + \frac{7}{5}$, confirming the method.
