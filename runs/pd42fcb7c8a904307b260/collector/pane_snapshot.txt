# Proof

**Claim.** The set $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)} := \left\{\tfrac{a}{b}\;\middle|\; a,b\in\m
athbb{Z},\; p\nmid b\right\}\subset\mathbb{Q}$ is isomorphic (in fact, canonical
ly identified) with the ring of *rational* $p$-adic integers as used in Huppert'
s *Character Theory of Finite Groups*.

---

## 1. Two objects in play

**(A) The localization $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$.**
This is the localization of $\mathbb{Z}$ at the prime ideal $(p)$, i.e., the sub
ring of $\mathbb{Q}$ obtained by inverting every integer coprime to $p$. Equival
ently,
$$\mathbb{Z}_{(p)} = \left\{x\in\mathbb{Q}\;:\; v_p(x)\geq 0\right\}\cap\mathbb{
Q},$$
where $v_p$ is the $p$-adic valuation on $\mathbb{Q}$ (normalized by $v_p(p)=1$)
. It is a discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal $p\,\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ and r
esidue field $\mathbb{F}_p$.

**(B) The $p$-adic integers $\mathbb{Z}_p$ and the "rational" ones.**
The full ring of $p$-adic integers is the completion
$$\mathbb{Z}_p = \varprojlim_n \mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z},$$
a complete DVR with fraction field $\mathbb{Q}_p$. It is **uncountable** and **c
ompact**. The field $\mathbb{Q}$ embeds into $\mathbb{Q}_p$ (since $\mathbb{Z}_p
$ is the completion of $\mathbb{Z}$ w.r.t. the $p$-adic metric, and $\mathbb{Q}_
p$ is its fraction field; the natural map $\mathbb{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_p
$ extends to $\mathbb{Q}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Q}_p$). The **rational $p$-adic i
ntegers** are, by definition,
$$\mathbb{Q}\cap\mathbb{Z}_p \;=\;\left\{x\in\mathbb{Q}\;:\; x\in\mathbb{Z}_p\ri
ght\}\quad\text{(intersection taken inside }\mathbb{Q}_p\text{)}.$$
This is the subring of $\mathbb{Z}_p$ consisting of those $p$-adic integers that
 happen to be rational numbers. It is this object that Huppert denotes and uses
in character theory (where one localizes character values at $p$ without passing
 to the full completion).

---

## 2. The identification $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)} = \mathbb{Q}\cap\mathbb{Z}_p$

Recall that for $x\in\mathbb{Q}_p$,
$$x\in\mathbb{Z}_p \iff v_p(x)\geq 0.$$

**($\subseteq$)** Let $x = \tfrac{a}{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ with $a,b\in\mathbb{
Z}$, $p\nmid b$. Then $v_p(b)=0$, so
$$v_p(x) = v_p(a)-v_p(b) = v_p(a)\geq 0.$$
Hence $x\in\mathbb{Z}_p$. Since also $x\in\mathbb{Q}$, we get $x\in\mathbb{Q}\ca
p\mathbb{Z}_p$.

**($\supseteq$)** Let $x\in\mathbb{Q}\cap\mathbb{Z}_p$. Write $x=\tfrac{a}{b}$ i
n lowest terms ($\gcd(a,b)=1$, $b>0$). Since $x\in\mathbb{Z}_p$, we have $v_p(x)
\geq 0$, i.e.,
$$v_p(a) - v_p(b) \geq 0 \implies v_p(a)\geq v_p(b).$$
Because $\gcd(a,b)=1$, at most one of $v_p(a),\,v_p(b)$ is positive.

- If $v_p(b)>0$, then $v_p(a)=0$ (coprimality), giving $v_p(a)-v_p(b)<0$, contra
dicting $v_p(x)\geq 0$.
- Therefore $v_p(b)=0$, i.e., $p\nmid b$.

So $x=\tfrac{a}{b}$ with $p\nmid b$, which means $x\in\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$.

Thus $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)} = \mathbb{Q}\cap\mathbb{Z}_p$ **as subsets of $\mathbb{Q}
_p$**, and the ring operations (addition, multiplication) agree since both are i
nherited from $\mathbb{Q}\subset\mathbb{Q}_p$.

---

## 3. Conclusion

The map
$$\varphi:\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\longrightarrow \mathbb{Q}\cap\mathbb{Z}_p,\qquad \var
phi(x)=x$$
(the identity, viewing both as subrings of $\mathbb{Q}_p$) is a ring isomorphism
. In fact it is **more than an isomorphism**: the two rings are *canonically equ
al* as subrings of $\mathbb{Q}_p$.

Therefore, **yes**: the subset $\left\{\tfrac{a}{b}\mid a,b\in\mathbb{Z},\,p\nmi
d b\right\}\subset\mathbb{Q}$ is isomorphic to the ring of rational $p$-adic int
egers $\mathbb{Q}\cap\mathbb{Z}_p$ as considered in Huppert's *Character Theory
of Finite Groups*. The former is the localization $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$; the latter
 is the set of rational elements inside the completed DVR $\mathbb{Z}_p$; and th
ese coincide under the natural embedding $\mathbb{Q}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Q}_p$
.

> **Remark (why "rational" matters).** The *full* ring $\mathbb{Z}_p=\varprojlim
\mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$ is uncountable and not a subring of $\mathbb{Q}$, so i
t is certainly *not* isomorphic to the countable ring $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$. The qu
alifier "rational" restricts to $\mathbb{Q}\cap\mathbb{Z}_p$, which is countable
 and exactly recovers $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$. This is the distinction Huppert relies
 on: character-theoretic arguments localize at $p$ but do not require passage to
 the uncountable completion.
