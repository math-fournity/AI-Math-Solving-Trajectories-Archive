**Claim:** Yes. If $A \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ is closed and strictly convex, and $
\mu$ is a probability measure on $\partial A$ that is not a Dirac measure, then
$\bar{x} := \int_{\partial A} x\,\mu(dx) \in A^\circ$.

**Proof.**

Since $A$ is closed and convex, and $\mu$ is supported on $\partial A \subset A$
, the barycenter $\bar{x}$ lies in $A$ (the integral of an $A$-valued random var
iable belongs to the closed convex set $A$). It remains to show $\bar{x} \notin
\partial A$.

**Key fact (strict convexity $\Rightarrow$ exposed points).** For every $b \in \
partial A$ there exists a supporting hyperplane $H = \{x : u \cdot x = u \cdot b
\}$ (with $u \neq 0$) such that
$$u \cdot x \le u \cdot b \quad \forall\, x \in A, \qquad \text{and} \qquad u \c
dot x = u \cdot b \iff x = b.$$

*Proof of the key fact.* A supporting hyperplane $H$ at $b$ exists because $A$ i
s closed and convex with $b \in \partial A$ (by the supporting hyperplane theore
m). Let $u$ be an outward normal, so $u \cdot x \le u \cdot b$ for all $x \in A$
. The set $F := A \cap H$ is a (closed) face of $A$ contained in $\partial A$. I
f $F$ contained a point $y \neq b$, then the entire segment $[b,y] \subset F \su
bset \partial A$, contradicting strict convexity (the boundary contains no nontr
ivial segment). Hence $F = \{b\}$, giving the "equality iff $x=b$" part. $\squar
e$

**Main argument.** Suppose for contradiction that $\bar{x} \in \partial A$. Appl
y the key fact with $b = \bar{x}$: there exists $u \neq 0$ with
$$u \cdot x \le u \cdot \bar{x} \quad \forall\, x \in A, \qquad u \cdot x = u \c
dot \bar{x} \iff x = \bar{x}.$$

Integrating against $\mu$ (supported on $\partial A \subset A$):
$$u \cdot \bar{x} = \int_{\partial A} u \cdot x\,\mu(dx) \le \int_{\partial A} u
 \cdot \bar{x}\,\mu(dx) = u \cdot \bar{x}.$$

Thus $\int_{\partial A} (u \cdot \bar{x} - u \cdot x)\,\mu(dx) = 0$. The integra
nd $u \cdot \bar{x} - u \cdot x$ is non-negative and continuous, and it equals $
0$ only at $x = \bar{x}$. Therefore
$$u \cdot x = u \cdot \bar{x} \quad \mu\text{-a.e.} \implies x = \bar{x} \quad \
mu\text{-a.e.} \implies \mu = \delta_{\bar{x}}.$$

This contradicts the hypothesis that $\mu$ is not a Dirac measure. Hence $\bar{x
} \notin \partial A$, and since $\bar{x} \in A$, we conclude $\bar{x} \in A^\cir
c$. $\blacksquare$
