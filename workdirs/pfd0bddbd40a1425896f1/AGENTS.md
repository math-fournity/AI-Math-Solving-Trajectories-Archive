# Problem

In a remote territory, three survey outposts—A, B, and C—form the vertices of a triangular perimeter. The terrain is such that the triangle ABC is acute and scalene. A central monitoring station, N, is positioned exactly at the center of a circular patrol route that passes through the three points where the altitudes of the triangle meet the opposite boundaries.

To secure the region, two long, straight security fences are constructed. The first fence is tangent to the triangle's circumcircle at outpost B, and the second fence is tangent to the circumcircle at outpost C. These two fences meet at a specific command hub, D.

A logistics expert notices a unique spatial alignment: the main headquarters A, the command hub D, and the monitoring station N lie precisely on a single straight line. Engineering simulations reveal that this perfect collinearity occurs if and only if the internal angle of the perimeter at outpost A (angle BAC) is exactly $\alpha$ degrees.

Find the value of $\alpha$.
