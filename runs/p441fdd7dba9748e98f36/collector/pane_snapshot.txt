## Proof

**Setup.** We are given the functional equation
$$f(xy) = x\,g(y) + y\,g(x), \qquad x,y > 1,$$
with calibration data $f(e)=e$ and $f(e^2)=4e^2$. We seek $g(e^2)$.

**Step 1: Reduce to additive form.** Set $x = e^u,\; y = e^v$ (so $u,v>0$), and
define
$$F(u) := f(e^u), \qquad G(u) := g(e^u).$$
Then $f(xy)=f(e^{u+v})$ becomes
$$F(u+v) = e^u\,G(v) + e^v\,G(u). \tag{1}$$
The calibration data reads $F(1)=e$ and $F(2)=4e^2$.

**Step 2: Derive the ODE for $G$.** Since $F(u+v)$ depends only on $u+v$, the ri
ght side of (1) must be independent of how $u+v$ is split. Fix $s=u+v$ and diffe
rentiate (1) with respect to $u$:
$$e^u\,G(s-u) - e^u\,G'(s-u) - e^{s-u}\,G(u) + e^{s-u}\,G'(u) = 0.$$
Rearranging with $H(t):=G(t)-G'(t)$:
$$e^u\,H(s-u) = e^{s-u}\,H(u) \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \frac{H(s-u)}{e^{s-u}} = \
frac{H(u)}{e^u}.$$
Since $u$ and $s-u$ range freely over all positive values, $K(t):=H(t)/e^t$ is c
onstant: $K(t)=c$. Thus
$$G(t) - G'(t) = c\,e^t \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; G'(t)-G(t) = -c\,e^t.$$

**Step 3: Solve the ODE.** Multiply by the integrating factor $e^{-t}$:
$$\frac{d}{dt}\bigl[G(t)\,e^{-t}\bigr] = -c \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; G(t)\,e^{-t}
 = d - ct,$$
so $G(t) = (d-ct)\,e^t$ for constants $c,d$.

**Step 4: Recover $F$ and apply calibration.** Substituting back into (1):
$$F(u+v) = e^u(d-cv)e^v + e^v(d-cu)e^u = e^{u+v}\bigl[2d - c(u+v)\bigr].$$
Hence $F(s) = e^s(2d - cs)$. Applying the two calibration conditions:
$$F(1)=e\,(2d-c)=e \;\Longrightarrow\; 2d-c=1,$$
$$F(2)=e^2(2d-2c)=4e^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; 2d-2c=4.$$
Subtracting: $c = -3$; then $2d+3=1$ gives $d=-1$. Therefore
$$G(t) = (-1+3t)\,e^t = (3t-1)\,e^t.$$

**Step 5: Compute $g(e^2)$.** Since $g(e^2)=G(2)$:
$$g(e^2) = (3\cdot 2 - 1)\,e^2 = 5e^2.$$

**Verification.** $G(1)=2e$, so $f(e^2)=e\cdot g(e)+e\cdot g(e)=2e\cdot 2e=4e^2$
 ✓. Cross-check with $x=e,\,y=e^2$: $f(e^3)=F(3)=e^3(2d-3c)=e^3(-2+9)=7e^3$, and
 $e\cdot g(e^2)+e^2\cdot g(e)=e\cdot 5e^2+e^2\cdot 2e=7e^3$ ✓.

$$\boxed{g(e^2) = 5e^2}$$
