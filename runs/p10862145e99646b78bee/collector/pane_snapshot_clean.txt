## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. If $V_1 \otimes_K L \cong V_2 \otimes_K L$ as $L$-representation
s of $G$, then $V_1 \cong_V 2$ as $K$-representations.

---

### Setup via the semisimple group algebra

Since $\operatorname{char} K = 0$, Maschke's theorem gives that $A := K[G]$ is a
 finite-dimensional semisimple $K$-algebra. By the Artin–Wedderburn theorem,

$$
A \;=\; K[G] \;\cong\; \prod_{i=1}^{r} A_i, \qquad A_i = M_{n_i}(D_i),
$$

where each $D_i$ is a finite-dimensional division $K$-algebra. The isomorphism c
lasses of irreducible (simple) $K[G]$-modules are in bijection with the simple c
omponents $A_i$: the unique (up to isomorphism) simple $A_i$-module is $S_i := D
_i^{\,n_i}$ (column vectors), viewed as a $K[G]$-module by projecting onto the $
i$-th factor. Concretely, $S_i$ is annihilated by $A_j$ for $j \neq i$.

Extension of scalars gives
$$
L[G] \;=\; K[G]\otimes_K L \;\cong\; \prod_{i=1}^{r}\bigl(A_i\otimes_K L\bigr),
$$
and each $A_i\otimes_K L = M_{n_i}(D_i)\otimes_K L \cong M_{n_i}(D_i\otimes_K L)
$ is a semisimple $L$-algebra (finite-dimensional, $\operatorname{char} L = 0$),
 hence itself a product of simple $L$-algebras. Thus the simple components of $L
[G]$ are a *refinement* of the factors $A_i\otimes_K L$.

### Key observation: distinct $K$-components give disjoint $L$-constituents

For each $i$, the scalar extension $S_i\otimes_K L$ is an $(A_i\otimes_K L)$-mod
ule, and it is annihilated by $A_j\otimes_K L$ for every $j\neq i$ (since $S_i$
is annihilated by $A_j$, and annihilation is preserved by the faithfully flat ba
se change $K\to L$). Therefore every simple $L[G]$-constituent of $S_i\otimes_K
L$ is supported on a simple component of $L[G]$ lying **inside** the central fac
tor $A_i\otimes_K L$.

Because the central factors $A_i\otimes_K L$ ($i=1,\dots,r$) are mutually distin
ct direct factors of $L[G]$, a simple $L[G]$-module supported inside $A_i\otimes
_K L$ cannot be isomorphic to one supported inside $A_j\otimes_K L$ whenever $i\
neq j$ (they are killed by different central idempotents).

Moreover $S_i\otimes_K L \neq 0$, since $L$ is a nonzero free (hence faithfully
flat) $K$-module and $S_i\neq 0$.

### Conclusion

Suppose $V_1\otimes_K L \cong V_2\otimes_K L$ as $L[G]$-modules. Write $V_1 \con
g S_i$ and $V_2\cong S_j$ for unique indices $i,j$. The isomorphism of scalar ex
tensions forces the sets of simple $L[G]$-constituents (with multiplicities) of
$S_i\otimes_K L$ and $S_j\otimes_K L$ to coincide. By the observation above, the
se constituent sets are respectively contained in the disjoint collections belon
ging to the factors $A_i\otimes_K L$ and $A_j\otimes_K L$. Since both sets are n
onempty, they can coincide only if $i = j$.

Hence $V_1\cong S_i = S_j \cong V_2$ as $K[G]$-modules, i.e. as representations
of $G$ over $K$.
