## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, such a function exists. Take $f(x) = \sin x$.

**Verification.** Let $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ be $f(x)=\sin x$. This is non-
constant and continuous. We show that for every compact $K\subset\mathbb{R}$, th
e set $f(\mathbb{R}\setminus K)$ is not open.

**Key observation.** Every compact subset of $\mathbb{R}$ is bounded, so there e
xists $M>0$ with $K\subset[-M,M]$. Hence
$$\mathbb{R}\setminus K \;\supset\; (-\infty,-M)\cup(M,\infty).$$
Choose $c < -M-2\pi$. Then the closed interval $[c,\,c+2\pi]$ is entirely contai
ned in $(-\infty,-M)$. Since $\sin$ has period $2\pi$, on this full period it at
tains every value in $[-1,1]$ (it hits $-1$ and $1$, and by the Intermediate Val
ue Theorem everything in between). Therefore
$$f\bigl((-\infty,-M)\bigr)=[-1,1].$$
Since $(-\infty,-M)\subset\mathbb{R}\setminus K$, we get
$$f(\mathbb{R}\setminus K)\supset f\bigl((-\infty,-M)\bigr)=[-1,1].$$
On the other hand $f(\mathbb{R})=[-1,1]$, so $f(\mathbb{R}\setminus K)\subset[-1
,1]$. Combining,
$$f(\mathbb{R}\setminus K)=[-1,1].$$

The set $[-1,1]$ is **not open** in $\mathbb{R}$ (the point $-1$ is not an inter
ior point).

This covers every nonempty compact $K$. For $K=\varnothing$ (also compact), $\ma
thbb{R}\setminus K=\mathbb{R}$ and $f(\mathbb{R})=[-1,1]$, again not open.

**Conclusion.** The non-constant continuous function $f(x)=\sin x$ satisfies $f(
\mathbb{R}\setminus K)=[-1,1]$ for every compact $K\subset\mathbb{R}$, and $[-1,
1]$ is never open. Hence such a function exists. $\blacksquare$
