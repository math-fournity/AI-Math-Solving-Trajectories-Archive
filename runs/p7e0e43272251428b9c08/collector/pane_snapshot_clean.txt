## Proof

Let $f(A_1,\dots,A_6)=\sum_{i=1}^6 \cos A_i$. The feasible set is
$$D=\{(A_1,\dots,A_6)\in(0,\pi)^6:\textstyle\sum A_i=4\pi\},$$
with closure $\bar D=\{(A_1,\dots,A_6)\in[0,\pi]^6:\sum A_i=4\pi\}$, which is co
mpact. Since $f$ is continuous, it attains its minimum on $\bar D$.

### Step 1: Boundary analysis

On $\partial\bar D$, some $A_i\in\{0,\pi\}$. Note $\cos(0)=1$ and $\cos(\pi)=-1$
.

To minimize, we maximize the number of angles equal to $\pi$. Since $\sum A_i=4\
pi$ and each $A_i\le\pi$, at most $4$ angles can equal $\pi$. If exactly $4$ equ
al $\pi$, the remaining $2$ sum to $0$, so both are $0$, giving $f=4(-1)+2(1)=-2
$.

If exactly $3$ equal $\pi$ (sum $3\pi$), the remaining $3$ sum to $\pi$ with eac
h in $[0,\pi]$. The minimum of $\sum\cos$ over three values in $[0,\pi]$ summing
 to $\pi$ is attained at the boundary (push one to $\pi$, two to $0$), giving $-
1+1+1=1$, so $f=-3+1=-2$.

With fewer angles at $\pi$, the boundary contribution is even higher. Hence:

$$\min_{\partial\bar D} f = -2.$$

### Step 2: Interior critical points

By Lagrange multipliers, at an interior critical point, $-\sin A_i=\lambda$ for
all $i$, so $\sin A_i=c$ (constant) for all $i$. Since $A_i\in(0,\pi)$, we have
$c\in(0,1)$, and each $A_i$ equals either $\alpha$ or $\pi-\alpha$, where $\alph
a=\arcsin c\in(0,\pi/2)$.

Suppose $k$ of the angles equal $\alpha$ and $6-k$ equal $\pi-\alpha$. The const
raint gives:
$$k\alpha+(6-k)(\pi-\alpha)=4\pi \implies (2k-6)\alpha=(k-2)\pi.$$

- **$k=0$:** $\alpha=\pi/3$, all six angles $=2\pi/3$. Then $f=6\cos(2\pi/3)=6\c
dot(-\tfrac12)=-3$.
- **$k=1$:** $\alpha=\pi/4$, one angle $=\pi/4$, five $=3\pi/4$. Then $f=\cos(\p
i/4)+5\cos(3\pi/4)=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}-\frac{5\sqrt2}{2}=-2\sqrt2\approx -2.83$.
- **$k=2$:** $\alpha=0$, degenerate (boundary).
- **$k=3$:** $0=\pi$, impossible.
- **$k=4$:** $\alpha=\pi$, degenerate.
- **$k=5,6$:** by symmetry (swapping $\alpha\leftrightarrow\pi-\alpha$), same as
 $k=1,0$.

So the only interior critical values are $-3$ and $-2\sqrt2$.

### Step 3: Conclusion

Comparing all candidates:
$$-3 \;<\; -2\sqrt2 \;<\; -2 = \min_{\partial\bar D} f.$$

The global minimum on $\bar D$ is $\boxed{-3}$, achieved at $A_1=\cdots=A_6=2\pi
/3$ (i.e., the regular hexagon, which is a valid convex hexagon with all interio
r angles $120°$).

Since the minimum is attained in the interior of the feasible set, the best lowe
r bound over all convex hexagons is:

$$\cos A+\cos B+\cos C+\cos D+\cos E+\cos F \;\geq\; -3,$$

with equality for the regular hexagon. $\blacksquare$
