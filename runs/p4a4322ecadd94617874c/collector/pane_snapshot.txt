## Proof

**Normalization.** Set $x_i = t_i/L \in [0,1]$. The $n$ points are i.i.d. unifor
m on $[0,1]$, producing $n+1$ spacings $G_0, G_1, \dots, G_n$ (from 0 to first p
oint, between consecutive points, and from last point to 1). We seek
$$P\!\left(\max_i G_i \le \tfrac{1}{n}\right).$$

**Distribution of spacings.** The ordered statistics $0 \le X_{(1)} \le \cdots \
le X_{(n)} \le 1$ have joint density $n!$. The spacings $(G_0, \dots, G_n)$ sati
sfy $G_i \ge 0$, $\sum G_i = 1$, and their joint distribution is **Dirichlet$(1,
1,\dots,1)$** — uniform on the $n$-dimensional simplex $\Delta = \{(g_0,\dots,g_
n) : g_i \ge 0,\ \sum g_i = 1\}$, which has volume $1/n!$.

**Inclusion–exclusion.** Let $A_i = \{G_i > 1/n\}$. We want $P\!\left(\bigcap A_
i^c\right)$. By inclusion–exclusion:
$$P\!\left(\bigcap A_i^c\right) = \sum_{k=0}^{n+1} (-1)^k \binom{n+1}{k}\, P(A_0
 \cap \cdots \cap A_{k-1}).$$

**Computing $P(A_0 \cap \cdots \cap A_{k-1})$.** This is the probability that $k
$ specific spacings each exceed $1/n$. Substitute $g_i' = g_i - 1/n$ for $i < k$
; the shifted variables satisfy $g_i' \ge 0$ and $\sum g_i' + \sum_{j \ge k} g_j
 = 1 - k/n$. This is a simplex of total mass $1 - k/n$, whose volume scales as $
(1-k/n)^n$ relative to $\Delta$. Hence
$$P(A_0 \cap \cdots \cap A_{k-1}) = \left(1 - \tfrac{k}{n}\right)^n \quad \text{
when } k \le n, \quad \text{and } 0 \text{ when } k \ge n.$$

(For $k = n$: total mass $1 - n/n = 0$, so volume is $0$. For $k = n+1$: impossi
ble since $n+1$ spacings each $> 1/n$ would sum to $> 1$.)

Therefore:
$$P = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} (-1)^k \binom{n+1}{k}\left(1 - \frac{k}{n}\right)^n = \fr
ac{1}{n^n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} (-1)^k \binom{n+1}{k}(n-k)^n.$$

**Evaluating the sum via finite differences.** Consider the full sum
$$T = \sum_{k=0}^{n+1} (-1)^k \binom{n+1}{k}(n-k)^n.$$
Since $(n-k)^n$ is a polynomial of degree $n$ in $k$, and $\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^k\
binom{m}{k}f(k)$ is the $m$-th forward difference (up to sign) which vanishes fo
r any polynomial of degree $< m$, we get $T = 0$ (here $m = n+1 > n$).

Splitting off the $k=n$ and $k=n+1$ terms:
- $k = n$: $(-1)^n \binom{n+1}{n}\cdot 0^n = 0$ (for $n \ge 1$).
- $k = n+1$: $(-1)^{n+1}\binom{n+1}{n+1}(n-(n+1))^n = (-1)^{n+1}(-1)^n = -1$.

Thus:
$$0 = \underbrace{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^k\binom{n+1}{k}(n-k)^n}_{=:\,S} + 0 + (-1
),$$
giving $S = 1$.

**Conclusion.**
$$P = \frac{S}{n^n} = \frac{1}{n^n}.$$

**Verification with small cases:**
- $n=1$: one point, two spacings summing to 1, both $\le 1$ always. $P = 1 = 1/1
^1$. ✓
- $n=2$: two points, three spacings $\le 1/2$. Direct integration gives $P = 1/4
 = 1/2^2$. ✓
- $n=3$: $P = 1 - 4(2/3)^3 + 6(1/3)^3 = 1 - 32/27 + 6/27 = 1/27 = 1/3^3$. ✓

The probability that every work interval has length at most $L/n$ is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{n^n}}.$$
