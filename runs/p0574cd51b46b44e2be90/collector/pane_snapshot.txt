## Proof

Place coordinates with $A$ at the origin and $B$ on the positive $x$-axis: $A =
(0,0)$, $B = (b, 0)$, where $b^2 = AB^2$ is rational (and $b \neq 0$). Write $C
= (c_1, c_2)$ and $D = (d_1, d_2)$.

**Step 1: $2bc_1$ and $2bd_1$ are rational.**

From $AC^2 - BC^2$:
$$c_1^2 + c_2^2 - \big((c_1-b)^2 + c_2^2\big) = 2bc_1 - b^2 = AC^2 - BC^2.$$
Since $AC^2, BC^2, b^2$ are all rational, $2bc_1 = AC^2 - BC^2 + b^2$ is rationa
l. Likewise $2bd_1 = AD^2 - BD^2 + b^2$ is rational.

**Step 2: $c_1^2, d_1^2, c_2^2, d_2^2$ are rational.**

Since $2bc_1$ and $b^2$ are rational,
$$c_1^2 = \frac{(2bc_1)^2}{4b^2} \in \mathbb{Q}.$$
Then $c_2^2 = AC^2 - c_1^2 \in \mathbb{Q}$. By the identical argument, $d_1^2, d
_2^2 \in \mathbb{Q}$.

**Step 3: $(c_1 - d_1)^2$ and $(c_2 - d_2)^2$ are rational.**

$2b(c_1 - d_1) = 2bc_1 - 2bd_1 \in \mathbb{Q}$, so
$$(c_1 - d_1)^2 = \frac{(2b(c_1-d_1))^2}{4b^2} \in \mathbb{Q}.$$
Then from $CD^2 = (c_1-d_1)^2 + (c_2-d_2)^2 \in \mathbb{Q}$, we get $(c_2 - d_2)
^2 \in \mathbb{Q}$.

**Step 4: $c_2 d_2$ is rational.**

Expanding:
$$(c_2 - d_2)^2 = c_2^2 - 2c_2 d_2 + d_2^2,$$
so
$$2c_2 d_2 = c_2^2 + d_2^2 - (c_2 - d_2)^2 \in \mathbb{Q}.$$
Hence $c_2 d_2 \in \mathbb{Q}$.

**Step 5: Compute the area ratio.**

With $A$ at the origin and $B = (b,0)$ on the $x$-axis:
$$\text{area}(\triangle ABC) = \tfrac{1}{2}|b\,c_2|, \qquad \text{area}(\triangl
e ABD) = \tfrac{1}{2}|b\,d_2|.$$
Since no three of $A, B, D$ are collinear, $D$ is not on line $AB$ (the $x$-axis
), so $d_2 \neq 0$. Therefore
$$\frac{\text{area}(\triangle ABC)}{\text{area}(\triangle ABD)} = \left|\frac{c_
2}{d_2}\right| = \left|\frac{c_2 d_2}{d_2^2}\right|.$$
Both $c_2 d_2$ and $d_2^2$ are rational, with $d_2^2 \neq 0$, so the quotient is
 rational.

Thus $\dfrac{\text{area}(\triangle ABC)}{\text{area}(\triangle ABD)}$ is a ratio
nal number. $\blacksquare$
