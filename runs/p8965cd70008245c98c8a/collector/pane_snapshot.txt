## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, there exists a finite field extension $K/k$ such that $G \times_
k K \cong H \times_k K$.

**Setup.** Since $G$ and $H$ are twists, there is an isomorphism of $\bar{k}$-al
gebraic groups
$$\varphi: G_{\bar{k}} := G \times_k \bar{k} \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} H_{\bar{k}}
:= H \times_k \bar{k}.$$

We work with affine algebraic groups (the general case reduces to this by consid
ering the affine closure / using that any algebraic group embeds into $\mathrm{G
L}_n$, and the argument below applies to the coordinate rings of the underlying
schemes). The isomorphism $\varphi$ is a morphism of schemes over $\bar{k}$, equ
ivalently a $\bar{k}$-algebra homomorphism of Hopf algebras
$$\varphi^*: \bar{k}[H] \longrightarrow \bar{k}[G].$$

**Key observation: finiteness of the data.** The coordinate ring $\bar{k}[H]$ is
 a finitely generated $\bar{k}$-algebra. Choose finitely many $\bar{k}$-algebra
generators $f_1, \dots, f_n$ of $\bar{k}[H]$. The homomorphism $\varphi^*$ is co
mpletely determined by the images $\varphi^*(f_1), \dots, \varphi^*(f_n) \in \ba
r{k}[G]$. Each $\varphi^*(f_i)$ is a finite $\bar{k}$-linear combination of a fi
xed finite set of $\bar{k}$-algebra generators of $\bar{k}[G]$. Thus the entire
homomorphism $\varphi^*$ is specified by a **finite** collection of coefficients
 in $\bar{k}$.

**Constructing $K$.** Let $S \subset \bar{k}$ be the (finite) set of all coeffic
ients appearing in the expressions of $\varphi^*(f_1), \dots, \varphi^*(f_n)$ in
 terms of the generators of $\bar{k}[G]$, together with the coefficients needed
to express the Hopf-algebra structure (comultiplication, counit, antipode) compa
tibly. Since $\bar{k}$ is an algebraic closure of $k$, every element of $S$ is a
lgebraic over $k$. The $k$-subalgebra $k[S] \subset \bar{k}$ is therefore a fini
tely generated $k$-algebra consisting of algebraic elements, hence is a **finite
-dimensional** $k$-vector space. Let
$$K := k[S].$$
Then $K/k$ is a finite field extension (it is a domain that is finite-dimensiona
l over $k$, hence a field).

**Descent to $K$.** By construction, the homomorphism $\varphi^*$ is defined ove
r $K$: there exists a $K$-algebra homomorphism
$$\varphi_K^*: K[H] \longrightarrow K[G]$$
whose base change to $\bar{k}$ recovers $\varphi^*$. Indeed, all the coefficient
s needed to write $\varphi^*$ lie in $K$, so the same polynomial expressions def
ine a map over $K$.

Since $\varphi$ is an isomorphism over $\bar{k}$, its inverse $\varphi^{-1}$ is
likewise specified by finitely many coefficients in $\bar{k}$. Adjoining these (
finitely many additional algebraic elements) to $K$ keeps $K$ finite over $k$. T
hus, after possibly enlarging $K$ (still finite over $k$), both $\varphi$ and $\
varphi^{-1}$ are defined over $K$, making $\varphi_K$ an isomorphism of $K$-alge
braic groups:
$$G \times_k K \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} H \times_k K.$$

**Conclusion.** The isomorphism over $\bar{k}$ involves only finitely many eleme
nts of $\bar{k}$, all algebraic over $k$, and these generate a finite extension
$K/k$ over which the isomorphism (and its inverse) are defined. Hence such a fin
ite extension $K/k$ always exists. $\blacksquare$
