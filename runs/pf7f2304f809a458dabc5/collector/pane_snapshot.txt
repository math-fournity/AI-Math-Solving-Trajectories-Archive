### Step 1: Reformulate using $\operatorname{lcm}(k,n) = \frac{kn}{\gcd(k,n)}$

$$f(n) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{\gcd(k,n)^2}{k^2 n^2} = \frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{k=
1}^{\infty} \frac{\gcd(k,n)^2}{k^2}.$$

### Step 2: Expand $\gcd(k,n)^2$ via the Jordan totient function $J_2$

The Jordan totient function of order 2 satisfies $J_2(d) = d^2\prod_{p\mid d}(1-
p^{-2})$, and has the Dirichlet-series identity:

$$\gcd(k,n)^2 = \sum_{d\mid \gcd(k,n)} J_2(d).$$

(This is Möbius inversion of $\sum_{d\mid m}J_2(d)=m^2$.)

Substituting and swapping the order of summation:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\gcd(k,n)^2}{k^2} = \sum_{d\mid n}J_2(d)\sum_{\substa
ck{k\ge1\\d\mid k}}\frac{1}{k^2} = \sum_{d\mid n}J_2(d)\cdot\frac{1}{d^2}\cdot\f
rac{\pi^2}{6} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}\sum_{d\mid n}\frac{J_2(d)}{d^2}.$$

Since $\frac{J_2(d)}{d^2}=\prod_{p\mid d}(1-p^{-2})$ is multiplicative in $d$, t
he sum $\sum_{d\mid n}\frac{J_2(d)}{d^2}$ is multiplicative in $n$.

### Step 3: Evaluate at prime powers

For $n=p^a$, the divisors are $1,p,\dots,p^a$, contributing $1$ (empty product)
plus $a$ copies of $(1-p^{-2})$:

$$\sum_{d\mid p^a}\frac{J_2(d)}{d^2} = 1 + a\!\left(1-\frac{1}{p^2}\right).$$

### Step 4: Compute $f(10)$ with $10 = 2^1\cdot 5^1$

$$\sum_{d\mid 10}\frac{J_2(d)}{d^2} = \left(1+\left(1-\tfrac{1}{4}\right)\right)
\left(1+\left(1-\tfrac{1}{25}\right)\right) = \frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{24}{25} = \f
rac{18}{25}.$$

Therefore

$$f(10) = \frac{1}{10^2}\cdot\frac{\pi^2}{6}\cdot\frac{18}{25} = \frac{\pi^2}{60
0}\cdot\frac{18}{25} = \frac{3\pi^2}{2500}.$$

### Step 5: Find the smallest $m$ with $m\cdot f(10)$ a square of a rational mul
tiple of $\pi$

We need $m\cdot f(10) = q^2\pi^2$ for some $q\in\mathbb{Q}$, i.e.

$$\frac{3m}{2500} = \frac{3m}{2^2\cdot 5^4} = q^2.$$

For $\frac{3m}{2^2\cdot 5^4}$ to be a rational square, every prime exponent in i
ts factorization must be even:

| prime | exponent in $\frac{3}{2500}$ | requirement on $v_p(m)$ | minimum |
|---|---|---|---|
| $3$ | $+1$ | $1+v_3(m)$ even → $v_3(m)$ odd | $v_3(m)=1$ |
| $2$ | $-2$ | $-2+v_2(m)$ even → $v_2(m)$ even | $v_2(m)=0$ |
| $5$ | $-4$ | $-4+v_5(m)$ even → $v_5(m)$ even | $v_5(m)=0$ |
| other | $0$ | even | $0$ |

The smallest $m$ satisfying all conditions is $m=3$.
