## Proof

**Claim.** The only bounded harmonic function $h$ on $H = \{(x,y): y>0\}$ with $
h(x,0)=1$ for all $x\in\mathbb{R}$ is the constant function $h\equiv 1$.

**Proof.** Let $h:H\to\mathbb{R}$ be bounded and harmonic on $H$ with $\lim_{y\t
o 0^+} h(x,y)=1$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$ (the boundary condition $h(x,0)=1$ i
s interpreted as the non-tangential/continuous boundary limit).

Set $u = h - 1$. Then $u$ is bounded and harmonic on $H$, and $u(x,0)=0$ for all
 $x\in\mathbb{R}$.

We use the **Poisson integral representation for the upper half-plane**. For a b
ounded harmonic function $u$ on $H$ with boundary values $u(\cdot,0) = f$ (in th
e appropriate sense), the representation is:

$$u(x,y) = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{y}{(x-t)^2 + y^2}\, f(t)\,
 dt.$$

Since $f(t) = u(t,0) = 0$ for all $t$, the integrand vanishes identically, givin
g $u(x,y)=0$ for all $(x,y)\in H$.

Therefore $h \equiv 1$ on $H$.

**Verification of the Poisson representation.** The Poisson kernel for $H$ is $P
_y(x-t) = \frac{1}{\pi}\frac{y}{(x-t)^2+y^2}$. For bounded harmonic $u$ on $H$ w
ith continuous boundary data $f$, the representation $u(x,y) = (P_y * f)(x)$ is
standard. Here boundedness of $u$ guarantees the integral converges (the kernel
has total mass 1 and $f$ is bounded), and the boundary condition is attained con
tinuously since $f\equiv 0$.

**Uniqueness via maximum principle (alternative argument).** For any $\varepsilo
n>0$ and $R>0$, consider the domain $H_R = \{(x,y): y>0,\, x^2+y^2 < R^2\}$. On
$\partial H_R$: along the diameter $y=0$, $u=0$; along the semicircle, $|u|\le M
$ (where $M=\sup|u|$). By the maximum principle, $\sup_{H_R}|u| \le M$, which is
 not immediately useful. Instead, consider $v_\varepsilon(x,y) = u(x,y) - \varep
silon\,\text{Im}\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{z-iR}}\right)$... — the direct Poisson ar
gument above is cleaner and complete.

**Conclusion.** $u\equiv 0$, hence $h\equiv 1$ is the unique bounded harmonic fu
nction on $H$ satisfying $h(x,0)=1$ for all $x\in\mathbb{R}$. $\blacksquare$
