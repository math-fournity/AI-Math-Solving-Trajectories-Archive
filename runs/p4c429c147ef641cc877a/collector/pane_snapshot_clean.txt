**Answer: Yes.** $B_{X^*} = \overline{\operatorname{conv}(\pm S)}^{\,w^*}$ for *
any* Banach space $X$ (the infinite-dimensionality is not needed). Here is the p
roof.

---

**Proof.**

Let $C := \overline{\operatorname{conv}(\pm S)}^{\,w^*} \subseteq X^*$.

**Step 1 ($C \subseteq B_{X^*}$).** Since $S \subseteq B_{X^*}$, we have $\pm S
\subseteq B_{X^*}$. The unit ball $B_{X^*}$ is convex and $w^*$-compact (hence $
w^*$-closed) by the Banach–Alaoglu theorem. Therefore it contains the $w^*$-clos
ed convex hull of any of its subsets:
$$C = \overline{\operatorname{conv}(\pm S)}^{\,w^*} \subseteq B_{X^*}.$$

**Step 2 ($B_{X^*} \subseteq C$).** Suppose, for contradiction, that there exist
s $f_0 \in B_{X^*} \setminus C$.

The space $(X^*, w^*)$ is locally convex, and its continuous dual is $X$: every
$w^*$-continuous linear functional on $X^*$ is of the form $g \mapsto g(x)$ for
some $x \in X$.

Since $C$ is $w^*$-closed, convex, and disjoint from $\{f_0\}$, the Hahn–Banach
strict separation theorem (valid in any locally convex space for separating a po
int from a closed convex set) yields an $x \in X$ and a real number $\alpha$ suc
h that
$$f_0(x) > \alpha \geq \sup_{g \in C} g(x).$$

In particular, since $\pm S \subseteq C$,
$$f_0(x) > \sup_{g \in \pm S} g(x) = \sup_{f \in S} |f(x)|.$$

But $S$ is norming, so $\sup_{f \in S}|f(x)| = \|x\|$. Hence
$$f_0(x) > \|x\|.$$

On the other hand $f_0 \in B_{X^*}$, so $|f_0(x)| \leq \|f_0\|\,\|x\| \leq \|x\|
$, giving $f_0(x) \leq \|x\|$ — a contradiction.

Therefore $B_{X^*} \subseteq C$, and combined with Step 1 we conclude
$$B_{X^*} = \overline{\operatorname{conv}(\pm S)}^{\,w^*}. \qquad\blacksquare$$

---

**Remark.** The argument uses only (i) Banach–Alaoglu ($B_{X^*}$ is $w^*$-closed
/compact), (ii) the identification $(X^*,w^*)^* = X$, (iii) Hahn–Banach separati
on, and (iv) the norming property. None of these requires $X$ to be infinite-dim
ensional; the result holds for every Banach space.
