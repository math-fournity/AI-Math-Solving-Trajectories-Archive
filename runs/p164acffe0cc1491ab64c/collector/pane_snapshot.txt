## Szemerédi's Regularity Lemma (Theorem 6.5)

### Definitions

**Density of a pair.** Let $G=(V,E)$ be a graph and let $X,Y\subseteq V$ be disj
oint nonempty vertex sets. The *edge density* of the pair $(X,Y)$ is

$$d(X,Y)=\frac{e(X,Y)}{|X|\,|Y|},$$

where $e(X,Y)$ is the number of edges with one endpoint in $X$ and the other in
$Y$.

**$\epsilon$-regular pair.** A pair $(X,Y)$ is called *$\epsilon$-regular* (for
some $0<\epsilon\le 1$) if for every $X'\subseteq X$ and $Y'\subseteq Y$ with

$$|X'|\ge \epsilon\,|X|,\qquad |Y'|\ge \epsilon\,|Y|,$$

we have

$$\bigl|d(X',Y')-d(X,Y)\bigr|\le \epsilon.$$

In words: all "large enough" sub-pairs of $(X,Y)$ have density close to the dens
ity of the whole pair. The pair behaves pseudo-randomly at every coarse scale.

**$\epsilon$-regular partition.** A partition $\mathcal{P}=\{V_0,V_1,\dots,V_k\}
$ of $V$ is an *$\epsilon$-regular partition* of $G$ if it satisfies:

1. **Exceptional set.** $V_0$ (the *exceptional set* / *garbage set*) satisfies
$|V_0|\le \epsilon\,|V|$.
2. **Equal-sized classes.** The classes $V_1,\dots,V_k$ are pairwise disjoint, h
ave equal size, i.e. $|V_1|=|V_2|=\cdots=|V_k|$, and are disjoint from $V_0$.
3. **Most pairs are regular.** All but at most $\epsilon\binom{k}{2}$ of the pai
rs $(V_i,V_j)$ with $1\le i<j\le k$ are $\epsilon$-regular.

The exceptional set $V_0$ absorbs the "leftover" vertices so that the remaining
$k$ classes can all have exactly the same cardinality (which requires $|V|-|V_0|
$ to be divisible by $k$).

### Statement of the Lemma

**Theorem (Szemerédi's Regularity Lemma).** For every $\epsilon>0$ and every int
eger $m\ge 1$ there exists an integer $M=M(m,\epsilon)$ such that every graph $G
=(V,E)$ admits an $\epsilon$-regular partition $\mathcal{P}=\{V_0,V_1,\dots,V_k\
}$ with

$$m\le k\le M.$$

### Bounds on the Partition Size

The number $k$ of (non-exceptional) classes is bounded both from below and from
above:

- **Lower bound:** $k\ge m$. This is a *user-specified* lower bound: the partiti
on refines any prescribed minimum granularity $m$. The parameter $m$ is needed s
o that one can force the partition to be non-trivially fine (otherwise the trivi
al one-class partition $\{V\}$ would always satisfy the regularity condition vac
uously).

- **Upper bound:** $k\le M=M(m,\epsilon)$. The upper bound depends only on $m$ a
nd $\epsilon$, and **crucially is independent of the graph $G$** — in particular
 independent of $|V|$. This uniformity is the deep content of the lemma: no matt
er how large the graph, a regular partition with a bounded number of classes alw
ays exists.

**Explicit (tower-type) bound.** The standard proof yields

$$M \;\le\; \underbrace{2^{2^{\cdot^{\cdot^{2^{m/\epsilon}}}}}}_{\text{height }
O(1/\epsilon)},$$

i.e. a tower of twos of height $O(1/\epsilon)$ (with constants depending on $m$)
. More precisely, one common formulation gives a tower of height $O(\epsilon^{-1
})$ with $m/\epsilon$ (or a polynomial in $1/\epsilon$) at the top. Concretely,
a typical bound obtained from the standard "energy-increment" proof is

$$k \;\le\; M \;:=\; \operatorname{tower}\!\bigl(\lceil 1/\epsilon\rceil,\; \lce
il m\,\epsilon^{-5}\rceil\bigr),$$

where $\operatorname{tower}(h,t)$ denotes a tower of $h$ twos with $t$ at the to
p. The exact polynomial exponents in the top entry vary by presentation, but the
 qualitative feature — **a tower of twos of height $\Theta(1/\epsilon)$** — is w
hat matters.

**Tightness (Gowers, 1997).** Gowers showed that the tower-type dependence is es
sentially necessary: there exist graphs for which every $\epsilon$-regular parti
tion requires $k$ to be at least a tower of twos of height $\Omega(1/\epsilon)$.
 So the bound coming from the standard proof is, up to constants in the height,
optimal. This rules out any polynomial (or even singly-exponential) bound in $1/
\epsilon$.

### Explanation

**Intuition.** The lemma says that *every* graph, no matter how irregular or str
uctured, can be approximated — at the level of edges between large vertex blocks
 — by a "quasi-random" structure. After passing to a regular partition, the grap
h is described by:

- the $k\times k$ density matrix $(d(V_i,V_j))$, and
- the internal structure within each class $V_i$ (which the lemma does not contr
ol),

while the inter-class edges behave essentially like random bipartite graphs of t
he prescribed densities. This reduces many extremal and Ramsey-type questions to
 questions about *weighted graphs on $k$ vertices*, with $k$ bounded independent
ly of $|V|$.

**Why the exceptional set.** Equal-sized classes are extremely convenient for co
unting and for applying the regularity condition uniformly. Since $|V|$ need not
 be divisible by $k$, one peels off a small exceptional set $V_0$ (of size $\le
\epsilon|V|$) to make the remaining $|V|-|V_0|$ divisible by $k$. The vertices o
f $V_0$ are then redistributed arbitrarily among the classes when applying the l
emma in practice; their contribution is a negligible $\epsilon$-fraction error.

**Why "all but $\epsilon\binom{k}{2}$ pairs."** One cannot demand that *every* p
air $(V_i,V_j)$ be $\epsilon$-regular; some pairs may be pathologically structur
ed. The lemma tolerates an $\epsilon$-fraction of irregular pairs, which is smal
l enough that in applications (e.g. counting copies of a fixed graph $H$, or app
lying the Blow-up Lemma / Counting Lemma) the irregular pairs contribute only a
controlled error.

**Proof idea (energy increment).** Start with an arbitrary partition into $m$ eq
ual classes. Define the *index* (or *energy*) of a partition:

$$\operatorname{ind}(\mathcal{P})=\frac{1}{\binom{|V|}{2}}\sum_{i<j} d(V_i,V_j)^
2\,|V_i|\,|V_j|.$$

This quantity lies in $[0,1]$ and *increases* by at least $\epsilon^{5}/2$ whene
ver one refines the partition by splitting each non-regular class into $\lceil 1
/\epsilon\rceil$ sub-classes (using the witnessing sub-pairs that violate regula
rity). Since the index is bounded above by $1$, the refinement process terminate
s after at most $O(\epsilon^{-5})$ rounds. Each round multiplies the number of c
lasses by $\lceil 1/\epsilon\rceil$, producing the tower of twos of height $O(1/
\epsilon)$. At termination, every pair is either regular or was already accounte
d for, and one checks that at most $\epsilon\binom{k}{2}$ pairs remain irregular
.

**Consequences / why it matters.** The Regularity Lemma is the backbone of the *
graph removal lemma* (and hence of Roth's theorem and its multidimensional gener
alizations), of the *Counting Lemma* / *Blow-up Lemma* machinery, and of numerou
s results in extremal graph theory, additive combinatorics, and property testing
. The price one pays is the tower-type bound on $k$, which makes the lemma non-q
uantitative in practice — it gives existence and structural control, but not eff
icient algorithms (unless one uses the *weak regularity lemma* of Frieze–Kannan,
 which gives a polynomial bound at the cost of a weaker notion of regularity).

### Summary

For every $\epsilon>0$ and $m\ge 1$ there is $M=M(m,\epsilon)$ (a tower of twos
of height $\Theta(1/\epsilon)$) such that every graph $G$ has a partition $V=V_0
\cup V_1\cup\cdots\cup V_k$ with $|V_0|\le\epsilon|V|$, $|V_1|=\cdots=|V_k|$, $m
\le k\le M$, and all but at most $\epsilon\binom{k}{2}$ of the pairs $(V_i,V_j)$
 being $\epsilon$-regular. The bound $M$ is independent of $|V|$, and the tower-
type dependence on $1/\epsilon$ is essentially tight (Gowers, 1997).
