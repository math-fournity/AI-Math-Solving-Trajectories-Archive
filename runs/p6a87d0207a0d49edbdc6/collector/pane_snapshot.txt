## Proof

**Construction.**

Let \(g:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]\) be the **Cantor function** (the devil's staircase),
 extended to all of \(\mathbb{R}\) by
\[
g(t)=0\;\;(t\le 0),\qquad g(t)=1\;\;(t\ge 1),
\]
and defined on \([0,1]\) in the usual way: if \(x\in[0,1]\) has ternary expansio
n \(x=0.a_1a_2a_3\ldots\) (choosing the expansion not ending in all \(2\)'s when
 ambiguous), and \(N=\min\{k:a_k=1\}\) (with \(N=\infty\) if no digit equals \(1
\)), then
\[
g(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{a_k}{2}\cdot 2^{-k}\;+\;2^{-N}\quad(\text{with the se
cond term }0\text{ if }N=\infty).
\]
The Cantor function is continuous, non‑decreasing, constant on each component of
 \([0,1]\setminus\mathcal{C}\) (where \(\mathcal{C}\) is the middle‑third Cantor
 set), and strictly increasing across \(\mathcal{C}\) in the sense that \(g(s)<g
(t)\) whenever \((s,t)\cap\mathcal{C}\neq\varnothing\).

Let \(\{r_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\) be an enumeration of the rationals \(\mathbb{Q}\)
. Define
\[
\boxed{\;f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}\,g(x-r_n),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.\;}
\]

We verify the four required properties.

---

**1. \(f\) is well‑defined and continuous.**

Each summand satisfies \(0\le 2^{-n}g(x-r_n)\le 2^{-n}\), and \(\sum 2^{-n}=1<\i
nfty\). By the Weierstrass \(M\)‑test the series converges **uniformly** on \(\m
athbb{R}\). Each summand is continuous, and a uniform limit of continuous functi
ons is continuous. Hence \(f\) is continuous. ∎

---

**2. \(f\) is monotone non‑decreasing.**

Each \(g(x-r_n)\) is non‑decreasing in \(x\), and every coefficient \(2^{-n}\) i
s positive. A sum of non‑decreasing functions with non‑negative coefficients is
non‑decreasing. Hence \(f\) is non‑decreasing. ∎

---

**3. \(f\) is strictly increasing (in particular, not constant on any interval).
**

We first record a key density fact:

> **Claim.** \(\displaystyle\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}(r_n+\mathcal{C})\) is dense i
n \(\mathbb{R}\).

*Proof of claim.* Let \(I\subset\mathbb{R}\) be any non‑empty open interval and
pick any point \(c\in\mathcal{C}\) (e.g. \(c=0\)). The translate \(I-c=\{t-c:t\i
n I\}\) is a non‑empty open interval, hence contains some rational \(r_n\). Then
 \(r_n+c\in I\cap(r_n+\mathcal{C})\), so \(I\) meets \(\bigcup_n(r_n+\mathcal{C}
)\). ∎

Now let \(x<y\). The open interval \((x,y)\) meets \(\bigcup_n(r_n+\mathcal{C})\
) by the claim, so there exist \(n\) and \(c\in\mathcal{C}\) with \(x<r_n+c<y\),
 i.e. \(x-r_n<c<y-r_n\). Since \((x-r_n,\,y-r_n)\cap\mathcal{C}\neq\varnothing\)
, the Cantor function satisfies
\[
g(x-r_n)<g(y-r_n).
\]
Therefore
\[
f(y)-f(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k}\bigl[g(y-r_k)-g(x-r_k)\bigr]\;\ge\;2^{-n}\bi
gl[g(y-r_n)-g(x-r_n)\bigr]\;>\;0.
\]
Thus \(f(y)>f(x)\) whenever \(x<y\): \(f\) is **strictly increasing**, hence not
 constant on any interval. ∎

---

**4. \(f'(x)=0\) almost everywhere.**

Let \(\nu\) be the **Cantor measure** — the Lebesgue–Stieltjes probability measu
re associated to \(g\), characterized by \(\nu((-\infty,t])=g(t)\). It is the un
ique Borel probability measure supported on \(\mathcal{C}\) with \(\nu(I)>0\) fo
r every open interval \(I\) meeting \(\mathcal{C}\). In particular \(\nu\) is **
singular** with respect to Lebesgue measure (it is supported on the measure‑zero
 set \(\mathcal{C}\)) and **atomless** (so its distribution function \(g\) is co
ntinuous).

For each \(n\), let \(\nu_n\) be the translate of \(\nu\) by \(r_n\), i.e. \(\nu
_n(A)=\nu(A-r_n)\); its distribution function is \(x\mapsto g(x-r_n)\). Define t
he Borel measure
\[
\mu=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}\,\nu_n .
\]
This is a finite measure (\(\mu(\mathbb{R})=\sum 2^{-n}=1\)), and its distributi
on function is
\[
\mu((-\infty,x])=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}\,\nu_n((-\infty,x])=\sum_{n=1}^{\inft
y}2^{-n}\,g(x-r_n)=f(x).
\]

**Singularity of \(\mu\).** Each \(\nu_n\) is supported on \(r_n+\mathcal{C}\),
a set of Lebesgue measure zero. Hence \(\mu\) is supported on
\[
E=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}(r_n+\mathcal{C}),
\]
a countable union of measure‑zero sets, so \(E\) itself has Lebesgue measure zer
o while \(\mu(\mathbb{R}\setminus E)=0\). Thus \(\mu\perp\lambda\) (singular wit
h respect to Lebesgue measure \(\lambda\)).

**Atomlessness of \(\mu\).** Each \(\nu_n\) is atomless, so every countable posi
tive linear combination is atomless; hence \(\mu\) has no atoms, confirming (aga
in) that \(f\) is continuous.

**Conclusion.** By the Lebesgue–Radon–Nikodym decomposition, the derivative of t
he distribution function of a measure equals the Radon–Nikodym derivative of its
 absolutely continuous part. Since \(\mu\) is purely singular (\(\mu_{\mathrm{ac
}}=0\)),
\[
f'(x)=\frac{d\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}}{d\lambda}(x)=0\quad\text{for Lebesgue‑a.e. }x\in
\mathbb{R}.
\]
(Equivalently: by Lebesgue's differentiation theorem the monotone function \(f\)
 is differentiable a.e., and since \(\mu\perp\lambda\), the derivative vanishes
a.e.) ∎

---

**Summary.** The function
\[
f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}\,g(x-r_n),
\]
where \(g\) is the Cantor function and \(\{r_n\}\) enumerates \(\mathbb{Q}\), is
:

- **continuous** (uniform limit of continuous functions),
- **monotone non‑decreasing**, indeed **strictly increasing** (because \(\bigcup
_n(r_n+\mathcal{C})\) is dense, so every interval sees a strict increase from so
me summand),
- **not constant on any interval** (strictly increasing),
- **differentiable with \(f'(x)=0\) a.e.** (it is the distribution function of a
 singular measure \(\mu\perp\lambda\)).

This is the desired continuous monotonic function on \(\mathbb{R}\) that is not
constant on any interval yet has \(f'(x)=0\) almost everywhere.
