## Proof

**Answer:** $\pi_1(X) \cong F_{2n-1}$, the free group on $2n-1$ generators.

---

**Setup.** Let $L_1, \dots, L_n$ be $n$ distinct lines through the origin in $\m
athbb{R}^3$, and let $L = \bigcup_{i=1}^n L_i$. Each line $L_i = \{t v_i : t \in
 \mathbb{R}\}$ for a unit vector $v_i$, and $L_i \cap S^2 = \{v_i, -v_i\}$. Sinc
e the lines are distinct, these $2n$ points on $S^2$ are all distinct. Set $P =
L \cap S^2 = \{v_1, -v_1, \dots, v_n, -v_n\}$, so $|P| = 2n$.

Note $0 \in L_i$ for every $i$, so $0 \notin X = \mathbb{R}^3 \setminus L$; henc
e $|x| > 0$ for all $x \in X$.

**Step 1: Deformation retraction onto $S^2 \setminus P$.**

Define $H: X \times [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}^3$ by
$$H(x, t) = (1-t)\,x + t\,\frac{x}{|x|}.$$

Since $x$ and $x/|x|$ are collinear,
$$H(x,t) = \bigl((1-t)|x| + t\bigr)\,\frac{x}{|x|},$$
which lies on the open ray $\{s\, x/|x| : s > 0\}$ from the origin through $x$.
Because $x \notin L$, this ray is not a ray of any $L_i$, so the ray (excluding
$0$) is disjoint from $L$. The scalar $(1-t)|x| + t > 0$ for all $t \in [0,1]$ (
as $|x|>0$), so $H(x,t) \neq 0$ and $H(x,t) \notin L$. Thus $H$ maps $X \times [
0,1]$ into $X$.

Checking the deformation retract properties:
- $H(x,0) = x$ for all $x \in X$.
- $H(x,1) = x/|x| \in S^2 \setminus P$ (well-defined since $x \notin L$ rules ou
t $x/|x| \in P$).
- For $y \in S^2 \setminus P$, $|y|=1$ so $H(y,t) = (1-t)y + t\,y = y$.

Hence $H$ is a strong deformation retraction of $X$ onto $S^2 \setminus P$, givi
ng
$$\pi_1(X) \cong \pi_1(S^2 \setminus P).$$

**Step 2: Fundamental group of $S^2$ minus $2n$ points.**

The sphere $S^2$ minus $k$ points (for $k \geq 1$) deformation retracts onto a w
edge of $k-1$ circles. Concretely, place the $k$ punctures and connect them by $
k-1$ non-overlapping arcs on $S^2$; a regular neighborhood of this graph is $S^2
$ minus $k$ open disks, which deformation retracts onto the graph (a tree with $
k$ vertices has $k-1$ edges, but here the arcs plus a spanning structure give $k
-1$ independent loops). Equivalently, $S^2 \setminus \{k \text{ points}\} \simeq
 \bigvee_{k-1} S^1$.

Verification on small cases:
- $k=1$: $S^2 \setminus \{\text{pt}\} \cong \mathbb{R}^2$ (contractible), $\pi_1
 = 1 = F_0$. ✓
- $k=2$: $S^2 \setminus \{2\text{ pts}\} \cong S^1 \times \mathbb{R} \simeq S^1$
, $\pi_1 = \mathbb{Z} = F_1$. ✓

With $k = 2n$ punctures,
$$S^2 \setminus P \simeq \bigvee_{2n-1} S^1, \qquad \pi_1(S^2 \setminus P) \cong
 F_{2n-1}.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\pi_1\!\left(\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} L_i\right) \;\con
g\; F_{2n-1},}$$

the free group on $2n-1$ generators. (For $n=1$ this gives $\mathbb{Z}$, matchin
g $\mathbb{R}^3$ minus a single line $\simeq S^1$.)
