## The Extremal Family

**Definition.** For $x \in [n]$ and $I \subseteq [n]$, define
$$S(x, I) = \{\pi \in A_n : \pi(x) \in I \;\wedge\; \pi(I) \cap I = \emptyset\}.
$$

**Observation.** If $x \in I$, then $S(x,I) = \emptyset$: the condition $\pi(x)
\in I$ with $x \in I$ forces $\pi$ to map an element of $I$ into $I$, contradict
ing $\pi(I) \cap I = \emptyset$. Henceforth assume $x \notin I$.

## Theorem 1: $S(x,I)$ is product-free

**Proof.** Suppose for contradiction that $a, b, c \in S(x,I)$ with $ab = c$ (co
mposition $a \circ b$).

- Since $b \in S(x,I)$: $b(x) \in I$.
- Since $a \in S(x,I)$: $a(I) \cap I = \emptyset$, meaning $a$ maps every elemen
t of $I$ outside $I$.
- Since $b(x) \in I$ and $a(I) \cap I = \emptyset$: $a(b(x)) \notin I$.
- But $c(x) = (ab)(x) = a(b(x))$, and $c \in S(x,I)$ requires $c(x) \in I$.

This is a contradiction. Therefore $ab \notin S(x,I)$ for all $a, b \in S(x,I)$.
 $\square$

## Theorem 2: $S(x,I)^{-1}$ is also product-free

**Proof.** We have $S(x,I)^{-1} = \{\sigma \in A_n : x \in \sigma(I),\; \sigma(I

tion $\pi(x) \in I$ becomes $x \in \sigma(I)$, and $\pi(I) \cap I = \emptyset$ b

Suppose $\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_1\sigma_2 \in S(x,I)^{-1}$.

- From $\sigma_1 \in S(x,I)^{-1}$: $x \in \sigma_1(I)$, so $\sigma_1^{-1}(x) \in

- From $\sigma_2 \in S(x,I)^{-1}$: $\sigma_2(I) \cap I = \emptyset$.
- From $\sigma_1\sigma_2 \in S(x,I)^{-1}$: $x \in \sigma_1\sigma_2(I)$, so $\sig

Since $\sigma_1^{-1}(x) \in I$ (first bullet) and $\sigma_2(I) \cap I = \emptyse

## Theorem 3: Size of $S(x,I)$

Let $|I| = k$ with $x \notin I$ and $k \leq n/2$ (needed for nonemptiness).

**Counting in $S_n$:** We count permutations $\pi \in S_n$ with $\pi(x) \in I$ a
nd $\pi(I) \cap I = \emptyset$.

1. **Choose $\pi(x) \in I$:** $k$ choices.
2. **Choose $\pi|_I$:** an injection $I \to [n] \setminus I$ (size $n-k$ codomai
n): $\frac{(n-k)!}{(n-2k)!}$ ways.
3. **Choose $\pi$ on remaining domain** $[n] \setminus (I \cup \{x\})$ (size $n-
k-1$) into remaining images (size $n-k-1$): $(n-k-1)!$ ways.

$$|S(x,I) \cap S_n| = k \cdot \frac{(n-k)!}{(n-2k)!} \cdot (n-k-1)!$$

**Verification ($n=4, k=1, I=\{1\}, x=2$):** $1 \cdot \frac{3!}{2!} \cdot 2! = 6
$ in $S_4$, giving $3$ in $A_4$. Direct enumeration confirms: the even permutati
ons are $(1\,3\,2)$, $(1\,4\,2)$, $(1\,2)(3\,4)$. ✓

**In $A_n$:** For $n \geq 3$ and $S(x,I) \cap S_n \neq \emptyset$, exactly half
lie in $A_n$ (if $\pi$ satisfies the conditions, so does $\pi \circ \tau$ for a
transposition $\tau$ fixing the relevant structure, giving a sign-reversing invo

$$\boxed{|S(x,I)| = \frac{k}{2} \cdot \frac{(n-k)! \cdot (n-k-1)!}{(n-2k)!}}$$

## Theorem 4: Optimization over $k$

The ratio $|S(k)|/|S(k+1)|$ (same $x$, $|I|$ changing from $k$ to $k+1$) equals:

$$\frac{k \cdot (n-k)(n-k-1)}{(k+1) \cdot (n-2k)(n-2k-1)}$$

Setting this to $1$ and solving asymptotically (large $n$, $k = o(n)$):

$$k(n-k)(n-k-1) = (k+1)(n-2k)(n-2k-1)$$

Expanding to leading sub-dominant order: $2k^2 + 4k \approx n$, giving:

$$k_{\text{opt}} \approx \sqrt{n/2}$$

At this optimum, the fraction $|S|/|A_n|$ satisfies:

$$\frac{|S|}{|A_n|} = \frac{k}{n} \prod_{i=0}^{k-1} \frac{n-k-i}{n-1-i} \approx
\frac{k}{n} \exp\!\left(-\frac{k^2}{n}\right) \approx \frac{1}{\sqrt{2n}} \cdot
e^{-1/2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2en}}$$

So the largest extremal family has size $\sim \frac{|A_n|}{\sqrt{2en}} = \frac{n
!}{2\sqrt{2en}}$.

## Theorem 5 (Known result — large $n$): Characterization of maximizers
