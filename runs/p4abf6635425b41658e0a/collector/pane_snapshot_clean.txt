## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $\operatorname{rank}\begin{pmatrix} B \\ D \end{pmatrix} = n-i$.

**Key observation.** The condition $XB + YD = I_{n-i}$ alone suffices; the hypot
hesis $\operatorname{rank}(H) \ge n-i$ is not needed.

**Setup.** Consider the block matrix
$$M := \begin{pmatrix} B \\ D \end{pmatrix} \in M_{n \times (n-i)}(\mathbb{F}),$
$
which is the vertical concatenation of $B \in M_{i \times (n-i)}(\mathbb{F})$ an
d $D \in M_{(n-i)\times(n-i)}(\mathbb{F})$, hence has $n$ rows and $n-i$ columns
. Also form
$$L := \begin{pmatrix} X & Y \end{pmatrix} \in M_{(n-i) \times n}(\mathbb{F}),$$
the horizontal concatenation of $X \in M_{(n-i)\times i}(\mathbb{F})$ and $Y \in
 M_{(n-i)\times(n-i)}(\mathbb{F})$.

**Matrix multiplication.** By block multiplication,
$$L\, M = \begin{pmatrix} X & Y \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} B \\ D \end{pmatri
x} = XB + YD = I_{n-i}.$$

**Rank argument.** Since $LM = I_{n-i}$, the matrix $M$ admits a **left inverse*
*. We show $M$ is injective. Let $v \in \mathbb{F}^{n-i}$ satisfy $Mv = 0$. Then
$$0 = L(Mv) = (LM)v = I_{n-i}\, v = v.$$
Hence $\ker(M) = \{0\}$, so $M$ is injective as a linear map $\mathbb{F}^{n-i} \
to \mathbb{F}^n$. By rank–nullity,
$$\operatorname{rank}(M) = n - i - \dim\ker(M) = n - i.$$

**Upper bound confirmation.** Equivalently, $M$ has $n-i$ columns, so $\operator
name{rank}(M) \le n-i$ always; the left-inverse relation $LM = I_{n-i}$ forces $
\operatorname{rank}(M) \ge \operatorname{rank}(I_{n-i}) = n-i$ (since $\operator
name{rank}(LM) \le \operatorname{rank}(M)$). Combining,
$$\operatorname{rank}\begin{pmatrix} B \\ D \end{pmatrix} = n - i.$$

**Conclusion.** The answer is **yes**. The existence of $X, Y$ with $XB + YD = I
_{n-i}$ already guarantees full column rank of $\begin{pmatrix} B \\ D \end{pmat
rix}$; the additional hypothesis $\operatorname{rank}(H) \ge n-i$ is superfluous
 for this conclusion.
