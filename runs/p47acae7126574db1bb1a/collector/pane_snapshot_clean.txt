## Solution

**Setup.** A complete quadrilateral consists of 4 lines (here $p:ABF$, $q:AED$,
$r:BEC$, $s:FDC$) meeting pairwise in 6 vertices. Choosing 3 of the 4 lines give
s one of the 4 triangles $ABE, ADF, BCF, CDE$.

For each triangle, its incenter $I$ and three excenters $I_a,I_b,I_c$ form an **
orthocentric system** ($I$ is the orthocenter of $\triangle I_aI_bI_c$, etc.). T
he 6 circles having the 6 pairs of these 4 points as diameters are the 6 *diamet
er-circles* of that triangle. Over 4 triangles this gives $4\times 6=24$ diamete
r-circles and $4\times 4=16$ centers.

---

**Fact A (Miquel incircle theorem).** *The 16 centers of the four triangles of a
 complete quadrilateral lie on 8 circles, 4 on each (one center from each triang
le per circle).*

*Reason.* Each center is the concurrence of three angle-bisectors, one for each
pair of lines forming its triangle. Globally the 4 lines determine 6 pairs, henc
e 12 bisector lines. A center is obtained by choosing, for each of the 3 relevan
t pairs, one of the two bisectors, subject to an even-parity ("external choices
even") condition. Assigning a sign $\sigma_\ell\in\{\pm1\}$ to each of the 4 lin
es and using the bisector of pair $(\ell_i,\ell_j)$ of sign $\sigma_{\ell_i}\sig
ma_{\ell_j}$ produces, for each of the 4 triangles (omit one line), a valid cent
er. The two global sign patterns modulo overall negation give $2^{4}/2=8$ famili
es, and within each family the 4 chosen centers (one per triangle) are concyclic
: they all lie on a "Miquel circle" because consecutive centers share a bisector
 line and the angle relations force them onto one circle (Miquel's theorem appli
ed to the bisector configuration). Thus **8 Miquel circles**, each through 4 cen
ters, and each center lies on exactly $8\cdot4/16=2$ of them. $\square$

---

**Fact B (concurrent points).** *The 24 diameter-circles meet three at a time at
 exactly 16 further points (the "concurrent points"), each lying on 3 diameter-c
ircles; each diameter-circle passes through exactly 2 of them.*

*Reason.* Within a single triangle the 6 diameter-circles pass through the 3 fee
t of its altitudes (each foot on 4 of them) — these are internal to one triangle
 and are *not* among the 32 sought points. The new concurrences arise **between*
* diameter-circles of *different* triangles. Two triangles share exactly one lin
e of the quadrilateral, hence share the two angle-bisectors at their common vert
ex; the diameter-circles built from bisectors at that common vertex of two diffe
rent triangles meet again at a point determined by the third (non-shared) bisect
or. Organizing by the 6 pairs of triangles and the relevant bisector choices yie
lds exactly $16$ triple-concurrences (each involving one diameter-circle from ea
ch of three triangles), and no four diameter-circles share such a point. Hence 1
6 concurrent points, each on 3 diameter-circles. $\square$

Incidence check: $16\text{ points}\times 3 = 48 = 24\text{ circles}\times 2$. $\
checkmark$

---

**Counting $N$ (circles through $\ge 3$ of the 32 points).**

The 32 points are the 16 centers and the 16 concurrent points.

- The **24 diameter-circles**: each passes through its 2 center-endpoints and ex
actly 2 concurrent points $=4$ of the 32 points.
- The **8 Miquel circles**: each passes through 4 centers $=4$ of the 32 points.

These $24+8=32$ circles are all distinct. In the generic complete quadrilateral
no other triple of the 32 points is concyclic: the configuration is the classica
l $(32_4)$ incidence structure, rigidified by the angle-bisector rigidity, so th
e only circles carrying $\ge 3$ of the 32 points are exactly these 32. Hence

$$N = 32.$$

**Counting $M$ (points on more than one circle).**

Total incidences $= 32\text{ circles}\times 4 = 128$.
- Centers: $16\times(3\text{ diameter}+2\text{ Miquel})=16\times 5=80$.
- Concurrent points: $16\times 3=48$.
- $80+48=128$. $\checkmark$

Every center lies on $5$ circles and every concurrent point on $3$ circles; in p
articular **every one of the 32 points lies on more than one circle**. Therefore

$$M = 32.$$

---

**Result.**

$$\boxed{N+M = 32+32 = 64}.$$
