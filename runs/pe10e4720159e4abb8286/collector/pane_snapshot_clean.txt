## Proof

**Setup.** The plane is tiled by equilateral triangles in the regular triangular
 tiling (6 triangles meeting at each vertex). Each vertex carries a lamp; each t
riangle carries a switch that toggles its three vertex lamps. Working over $\mat
hbb{F}_2$, a sequence of switch toggles produces a configuration equal to the su
m (symmetric difference) of the indicator vectors of the toggled triangles. We s
eek the minimum Hamming weight of a nonzero reachable configuration.

**A 3-coloring invariant.** The vertices of the triangular lattice admit a prope
r 3-coloring: assign to the vertex $a\mathbf{e}_1 + b\mathbf{e}_2$ (with $\mathb
f{e}_1=(1,0)$, $\mathbf{e}_2=(\tfrac12,\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2})$) the color $(a+2b)\bm
od 3$. Every triangle—whether upward- or downward-pointing—has one vertex of eac
h of the three colors.

Let $n_0, n_1, n_2$ denote the number of lamps that are on in each color class.
Initially $(n_0,n_1,n_2)=(0,0,0)$. Toggling any triangle flips exactly one lamp
in each color class, so it changes the parity of **every** $n_i$ simultaneously:

$$n_0 \bmod 2,\; n_1 \bmod 2,\; n_2 \bmod 2 \;\text{all flip together.}$$

Therefore the invariant

$$n_0 \equiv n_1 \equiv n_2 \pmod{2}$$

is preserved throughout.

**Weight 1 is impossible.** A configuration with exactly one lamp on has $(n_0,n
_1,n_2)$ equal to $(1,0,0)$, $(0,1,0)$, or $(0,0,1)$, giving parities that are *
*not** all equal. This violates the invariant, so no single lamp can be turned o
n alone. Hence $L \geq 2$.

**Weight 2 is achievable.** Take two adjacent triangles sharing a common edge. S
uppose triangle $T_1$ has vertices $\{A,B,C\}$ and the adjacent triangle $T_2$ (
on the other side of edge $BC$) has vertices $\{B,C,D\}$. Toggling both switches
 yields the configuration

$$\{A,B,C\} \triangle \{B,C,D\} = \{A, D\},$$

i.e., exactly the two vertices $A$ and $D$ opposite the shared edge are on. Sinc
e $B$ and $C$ have two distinct colors, both $A$ and $D$ have the third (same) c
olor, so $(n_0,n_1,n_2)$ has parities $(0,0,0)$—consistent with the invariant. T
his is a valid reachable configuration of weight 2.

**Conclusion.** Weight 1 is forbidden by the 3-coloring parity invariant, and we
ight 2 is explicitly realized by toggling two adjacent triangles. Therefore

$$\boxed{L = 2}.$$
