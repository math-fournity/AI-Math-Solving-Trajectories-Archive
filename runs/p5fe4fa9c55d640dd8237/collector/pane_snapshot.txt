Yes. All irreducible polynomials of the same degree appearing in the factorizati
on of $x^{p^n}-x$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ are distinct. Here is the proof.

---

## Proof

Work over $\mathbb{F}_p$, the finite field with $p$ elements.

**Step 1: $x^{p^n}-x$ is squarefree.**

Its formal derivative is
$$\frac{d}{dx}(x^{p^n}-x) = p^n x^{p^n-1} - 1 = 0 - 1 = -1 \neq 0.$$
Since the derivative is a nonzero constant, $\gcd(x^{p^n}-x,\; (x^{p^n}-x)') = \
gcd(x^{p^n}-x, -1) = 1$. Hence $x^{p^n}-x$ has no repeated roots and is squarefr
ee. Consequently, in its factorization into monic irreducibles over $\mathbb{F}_
p$, every irreducible factor appears with multiplicity exactly $1$.

**Step 2: The roots are exactly $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$.**

The splitting field of $x^{p^n}-x$ is $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$. Every element $\alpha
\in \mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ satisfies $\alpha^{p^n} = \alpha$ (Frobenius fixed-point p
roperty), so it is a root; conversely $x^{p^n}-x$ has at most $p^n$ roots, and $
|\mathbb{F}_{p^n}|=p^n$, so the root set is precisely $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$.

**Step 3: Characterization of which irreducibles divide $x^{p^n}-x$.**

Let $f \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ be monic irreducible of degree $d$, and let $\alpha$
 be a root of $f$ in $\mathbb{F}_{p^d}$. Then $f \mid x^{p^n}-x$ iff $\alpha$ is
 a root of $x^{p^n}-x$, i.e., iff $\alpha^{p^n}=\alpha$, i.e., iff $\alpha \in \
mathbb{F}_{p^n}$. Since $\alpha$ generates $\mathbb{F}_{p^d}$ over $\mathbb{F}_p
$, we have $\alpha \in \mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ iff $\mathbb{F}_{p^d} \subseteq \mathbb
{F}_{p^n}$, which holds iff $d \mid n$.

Therefore:
$$x^{p^n} - x = \prod_{\substack{d \mid n \\ f \text{ monic irred.}, \, \deg f =
 d}} f(x),$$
with each such $f$ appearing exactly once.

**Step 4: Distinctness of irreducibles of the same degree.**

Fix a divisor $d$ of $n$. The monic irreducible polynomials of degree $d$ over $
\mathbb{F}_p$ are, by definition, pairwise distinct polynomials (they are differ
ent elements of $\mathbb{F}_p[x]$). By Step 3, *every* monic irreducible of degr
ee $d$ divides $x^{p^n}-x$, and by Step 1 (squarefreeness), each appears with mu
ltiplicity $1$. Hence the set of degree-$d$ irreducible factors of $x^{p^n}-x$ i
s exactly the full set of all monic irreducibles of degree $d$ over $\mathbb{F}_
p$, each occurring once.

Since these are distinct polynomials (no two monic irreducibles of the same degr
ee are equal — they would have to be the same polynomial), **all irreducible pol
ynomials of the same degree in the factorization are distinct.**

**Remark (counting check).** The number of monic irreducibles of degree $d$ over
 $\mathbb{F}_p$ is given by the necklace polynomial
$$N_p(d) = \frac{1}{d}\sum_{e \mid d} \mu(e)\, p^{d/e},$$
and one verifies $\sum_{d \mid n} d\, N_p(d) = p^n$, confirming that the product
 of all these distinct irreducibles (each of degree $d$, contributing $d$ to the
 total degree) has total degree $p^n$, matching $\deg(x^{p^n}-x)$. This is consi
stent with — and confirms — that every irreducible of degree $d\mid n$ appears e
xactly once. $\blacksquare$
