**Claim:** $x = y$.

**Proof.**

Let $z = x - y \in X$. The hypothesis $T(x) = T(y)$ for all $T \in X^*$ is equiv
alent to
$$T(z) = 0 \quad \text{for all } T \in X^*.$$

We show $z = 0$, which gives $x = y$.

Suppose, for contradiction, that $z \neq 0$. Consider the one-dimensional subspa
ce $M = \operatorname{span}\{z\} \subseteq X$. Define a linear functional $f : M
 \to \mathbb{R}$ by
$$f(\alpha z) = \alpha \|z\|.$$
This is well-defined since $z \neq 0$ (so every element of $M$ has a unique repr
esentation $\alpha z$), and $f$ is bounded on $M$ with $\|f\|_{M^*} = 1$, since
$$|f(\alpha z)| = |\alpha|\,\|z\| = \|\alpha z\|.$$

By the Hahn–Banach theorem, $f$ extends to a bounded linear functional $T \in X^
*$ with $\|T\| = \|f\|_{M^*} = 1$ and $T|_M = f$. In particular,
$$T(z) = f(z) = \|z\| \neq 0.$$

But this contradicts the hypothesis that $T(z) = 0$ for all $T \in X^*$. Therefo
re $z = 0$, i.e., $x = y$. $\blacksquare$
