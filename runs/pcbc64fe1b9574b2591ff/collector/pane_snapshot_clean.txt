## Solution

**Setup.** We seek positive integers $(a,b,c)$ satisfying:
1. $ab$ is prime,
2. $bc$ is a product of exactly two primes (not necessarily distinct),
3. $abc$ is square-free,
4. $abc \le 30$.

**Key observation from condition 3.** If $abc$ is square-free, then $a$, $b$, $c
$ are pairwise coprime (any prime dividing two of them would appear squared in $
abc$) and each is itself square-free.

**Analyzing condition 1.** $ab$ prime with $a,b$ positive integers forces one of
 them to be $1$ and the other a prime. This gives two cases.

---

### Case A: $a = 1$, $b = p$ (prime)

- $ab = p$ is prime. ✓
- $bc = p \cdot c$ must be a product of exactly two primes. Since $c$ is coprime
 to $p$ and square-free (condition 3), $c$ must be a single prime $q \neq p$. Th
en $bc = pq$. ✓
- $abc = pq \le 30$, square-free. ✓

So we need **ordered** pairs $(p, q)$ of distinct primes with $pq \le 30$:

| $p$ | valid $q$ | count |
|-----|-----------|-------|
| 2 | 3, 5, 7, 11, 13 | 5 |
| 3 | 2, 5, 7 | 3 |
| 5 | 2, 3 | 2 |
| 7 | 2, 3 | 2 |
| 11 | 2 | 1 |
| 13 | 2 | 1 |

**Total: $5+3+2+2+1+1 = 14$ triples** $(a,b,c) = (1, p, q)$.

---

### Case B: $a = p$ (prime), $b = 1$

- $ab = p$ is prime. ✓
- $bc = c$ must be a product of exactly two primes. By condition 3, $c$ is squar
e-free, so $c = qr$ with $q, r$ **distinct** primes, both different from $p$.
- $abc = p \cdot q \cdot r \le 30$, square-free. ✓

We need three distinct primes $p, q, r$ with $pqr \le 30$:
- $2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 = 30 \le 30$ ✓
- $2 \cdot 3 \cdot 7 = 42 > 30$ ✗

So the only set is $\{2, 3, 5\}$. The prime $p$ (assigned to $a$) can be any of
the three, with $c$ being the product of the other two:

- $p=2,\; c=15$: $(a,b,c) = (2, 1, 15)$
- $p=3,\; c=10$: $(a,b,c) = (3, 1, 10)$
- $p=5,\; c=6$: $(a,b,c) = (5, 1, 6)$

**Total: 3 triples.**

---

### Final count

$$14 \text{ (Case A)} + 3 \text{ (Case B)} = \boxed{17}$$

**Verification of distinctness.** In Case A, $(1, p, q)$ with $p, q \ge 2$ disti
nct gives three distinct values. In Case B, $(p, 1, qr)$ with $p \ge 2$ and $qr
\ge 6$ also gives three distinct values. So all 17 triples have mutually distinc
t capacities, consistent with "unique processing capacities."
