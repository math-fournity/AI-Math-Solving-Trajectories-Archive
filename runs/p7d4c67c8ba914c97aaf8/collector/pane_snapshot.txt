## Solution

Let $a=BC$, $b=CA$, $c=AB$, $s=\frac{a+b+c}{2}$, and $R$ the circumradius.

**Tangent points of the $A$-excircle.** The $A$-excircle touches $BC$ at $A_1$,
line $CA$ (extended beyond $C$) at $B_1$, and line $AB$ (extended beyond $B$) at
 $C_1$. Standard tangent-length computations give:
$$BA_1 = s-c,\quad CA_1 = s-b,\quad CB_1 = s-b,\quad BC_1 = s-c,\quad AB_1=AC_1=
s.$$

**Side lengths of $\triangle A_1B_1C_1$.** Place $B$ at the origin. Then:
$$A_1 = \tfrac{s-b}{a}\,B + \tfrac{s-c}{a}\,C,\quad B_1 = -\tfrac{s-b}{b}\,A + \
tfrac{s}{b}\,C,\quad C_1 = -\tfrac{s-c}{c}\,A + \tfrac{s}{c}\,B.$$

Computing $|B_1-C_1|^2$: after algebra,
$$|B_1-C_1|^2 = \frac{4s^2(s-b)(s-c)}{bc}.$$
By cyclic symmetry:
$$B_1C_1 = 2s\sqrt{\tfrac{(s-b)(s-c)}{bc}},\quad C_1A_1 = 2s\sqrt{\tfrac{(s-c)(s
-a)}{ca}},\quad A_1B_1 = 2s\sqrt{\tfrac{(s-a)(s-b)}{ab}}.$$

**Trigonometric simplification.** Set $x=s-a$, $y=s-b$, $z=s-c$, so $a=y+z$, $b=
x+z$, $c=x+y$. Using $s-a = r\cot\frac{A}{2}$ (where $r$ is the inradius) and th
e identity $\cot\frac{B}{2}+\cot\frac{C}{2}=\frac{\cos\frac{A}{2}}{\sin\frac{B}{
2}\sin\frac{C}{2}}$:

$$\frac{xy}{(y+z)(x+z)} = \frac{\cot\frac{A}{2}\cot\frac{B}{2}}{\left(\cot\frac{
B}{2}+\cot\frac{C}{2}\right)\left(\cot\frac{A}{2}+\cot\frac{C}{2}\right)} = \sin
^2\frac{C}{2}.$$

Therefore $\sqrt{\frac{(s-a)(s-b)}{ab}} = \sin\frac{C}{2}$, and the perimeter is
:
$$P_1 = 2s\!\left(\sin\tfrac{A}{2}+\sin\tfrac{B}{2}+\sin\tfrac{C}{2}\right).$$

**All three perimeters are equal.** The expression $P_1$ is symmetric in $A,B,C$
. By cyclic relabeling, the $B$-excircle and $C$-excircle tangent triangles have
 the same perimeter $P_2 = P_3 = P_1$.

**The ratio.** Sum of perimeters $= 3P_1 = 6s\left(\sin\frac{A}{2}+\sin\frac{B}{
2}+\sin\frac{C}{2}\right)$. Since $a=2R\sin A$, we have $s = R(\sin A+\sin B+\si
n C)$. Thus:

$$\frac{3P_1}{R} = 6\,(\sin A+\sin B+\sin C)\left(\sin\tfrac{A}{2}+\sin\tfrac{B}
{2}+\sin\tfrac{C}{2}\right).$$

**Maximization.** Let $S_1 = \sin A+\sin B+\sin C$ and $S_2 = \sin\frac{A}{2}+\s
in\frac{B}{2}+\sin\frac{C}{2}$, with $A+B+C=\pi$.

- $\sin$ is concave on $(0,\pi)$, so by Jensen: $S_1 \le 3\sin\frac{\pi}{3} = \f
rac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}$.
- $\frac{A}{2}+\frac{B}{2}+\frac{C}{2}=\frac{\pi}{2}$ and $\sin$ is concave on $
(0,\frac{\pi}{2})$, so by Jensen: $S_2 \le 3\sin\frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{3}{2}$.

Since $S_1, S_2 > 0$:
$$S_1 \cdot S_2 \le \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}\cdot\frac{3}{2} = \frac{9\sqrt{3}}{4}.$$

Equality in both Jensen inequalities holds simultaneously iff $A=B=C=\frac{\pi}{
3}$ (equilateral triangle). Therefore:

$$\frac{3P_1}{R} \le 6\cdot\frac{9\sqrt{3}}{4} = \frac{27\sqrt{3}}{2}.$$

The greatest ratio is $\boxed{\dfrac{27\sqrt{3}}{2}}$, achieved when $\triangle
ABC$ is equilateral.
