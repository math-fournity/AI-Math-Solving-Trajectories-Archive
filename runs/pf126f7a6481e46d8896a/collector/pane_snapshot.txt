## Envelope of a Family of Surfaces

For a one-parameter family of surfaces $F(x,y,z,\alpha)=0$, the envelope is obta
ined by eliminating $\alpha$ from the system
$$F=0, \qquad \frac{\partial F}{\partial \alpha}=0.$$

---

### (a)  $\alpha(x-y)-\alpha^{2}z=1$

Set $F(x,y,z,\alpha)=\alpha(x-y)-\alpha^{2}z-1$.

**Step 1.**  $\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial \alpha}=(x-y)-2\alpha z=0$, giving
$$\alpha=\frac{x-y}{2z}\qquad (z\neq 0).$$

**Step 2.**  Substitute into $F=0$:
$$\frac{x-y}{2z}(x-y)-\left(\frac{x-y}{2z}\right)^{2}z-1=0.$$

Simplifying:
$$\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2z}-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4z}-1=0 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \frac{(x
-y)^{2}}{4z}=1.$$

Hence
$$\boxed{(x-y)^{2}=4z.}$$

This is a **parabolic cylinder** with rulings parallel to the line $x=y,\;z=0$.

---

### (b)  $(x-\alpha)^{2}+y^{2}=2\alpha z$

Set $F(x,y,z,\alpha)=(x-\alpha)^{2}+y^{2}-2\alpha z$.

**Step 1.**  $\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial \alpha}=-2(x-\alpha)-2z=0$, giving
$$x-\alpha=-z \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \alpha=x+z.$$

**Step 2.**  Substitute into $F=0$:
$$(x-x-z)^{2}+y^{2}-2(x+z)z=0,$$
$$z^{2}+y^{2}-2xz-2z^{2}=0,$$
$$y^{2}-2xz-z^{2}=0.$$

Hence
$$\boxed{y^{2}=z^{2}+2xz.}$$

This can be written as $y^{2}=z(z+2x)$, a **quadric surface** (a cone with verte
x at the origin, since the equation is homogeneous of degree 2 in $x,y,z$).

---

**Verification of the cone claim for (b).**  The equation $y^{2}-2xz-z^{2}=0$ is
 homogeneous of degree 2, so it defines a cone with vertex at the origin. One ma
y also diagonalize the quadratic form $Q=-2xz-z^{2}+y^{2}$; setting $u=z+x$, $v=
x$ gives $y^{2}=u^{2}-v^{2}$, i.e. $y^{2}+v^{2}=u^{2}$, confirming a right circu
lar cone in the coordinates $(u,v,y)$.
