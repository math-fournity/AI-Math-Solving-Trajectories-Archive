## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. Weak convergence in $H$ implies convergence in the $\Gamma$-norm
.

**Setup.** Let $u_n \rightharpoonup u$ weakly in $H$, i.e. $\langle u_n - u, v\r
angle \to 0$ for every $v \in H$. We must show $\|u_n - u\|_\star \to 0$, where
$$\|u_n - u\|_\star^2 = \sum_{i=1}^\infty \lambda_i\, \langle u_n - u, \phi_i\ra
ngle^2.$$

**Step 1 — Boundedness of the sequence.** Weakly convergent sequences are norm-b
ounded. Indeed, for each $v \in H$, the scalar sequence $\langle u_n, v\rangle$
is convergent (hence bounded). Viewing $u_n$ as elements of $H^{**} = H$ via $\l
angle u_n, \cdot \rangle$, the Uniform Boundedness Principle gives
$$M := \sup_n \|u_n\| < \infty.$$
Consequently $\|u_n - u\| \le \|u_n\| + \|u\| \le M + \|u\| =: C$ for all $n$.

**Step 2 — Pointwise decay of coordinates.** Since each basis vector $\phi_i \in
 H$, weak convergence gives
$$a_i^{(n)} := \langle u_n - u, \phi_i\rangle \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0 \quad \
text{for every fixed } i.$$

**Step 3 — Dominated convergence for the series.** Define $b_i^{(n)} := (a_i^{(n
)})^2 \ge 0$. By Cauchy–Schwarz and Step 1,
$$0 \le \lambda_i\, b_i^{(n)} = \lambda_i\, \langle u_n - u, \phi_i\rangle^2 \le
 \lambda_i\, \|u_n - u\|^2 \le C^2\, \lambda_i.$$
Since $\Gamma$ is nuclear, $\sum_{i=1}^\infty \lambda_i < \infty$, so $(C^2 \lam
bda_i)_{i\ge 1}$ is a summable dominating sequence (independent of $n$). By Step
 2, $\lambda_i\, b_i^{(n)} \to 0$ pointwise in $i$. Applying the Dominated Conve
rgence Theorem on the counting measure space $\mathbb{N}$:
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} \sum_{i=1}^\infty \lambda_i\, b_i^{(n)} = \sum_{i=1}^\infty
\lim_{n\to\infty} \lambda_i\, b_i^{(n)} = \sum_{i=1}^\infty 0 = 0.$$

That is, $\|u_n - u\|_\star^2 \to 0$, hence $\|u_n - u\|_\star \to 0$.

**Step 4 — Equivalent $\varepsilon$-$N$ argument (for completeness).** For any $
\varepsilon > 0$, pick $N$ so that $\sum_{i>N} \lambda_i < \varepsilon/(2C^2)$.
Then
$$\sum_{i>N} \lambda_i\, \langle u_n - u, \phi_i\rangle^2 \le C^2 \sum_{i>N} \la
mbda_i < \varepsilon/2.$$
For the finite head, choose $n_0$ so that $|\langle u_n - u, \phi_i\rangle|^2 <
\varepsilon/(2\sum_{j=1}^N \lambda_j)$ for all $i \le N$ and $n \ge n_0$ (possib
le by Step 2). Then
$$\sum_{i=1}^N \lambda_i\, \langle u_n - u, \phi_i\rangle^2 < \varepsilon/2.$$
Adding both parts: $\|u_n - u\|_\star^2 < \varepsilon$ for all $n \ge n_0$.

**Key reason this works.** The $\Gamma$-norm weights each coordinate by $\lambda
_i$, and the nuclearity condition $\sum \lambda_i < \infty$ makes the tail unifo
rmly small — exactly the summability needed to upgrade pointwise coordinate deca
y (from weak convergence) into decay of the weighted $\ell^2$-sum. This is the s
ame mechanism by which trace-class/nuclear operators map weakly convergent seque
nces to norm-convergent sequences: $\Gamma^{1/2}$ is compact *and* in fact maps
the weakly convergent sequence $u_n - u$ to a norm-convergent one, because $\|\G
amma^{1/2}(u_n-u)\| = \|u_n-u\|_\star \to 0$.
