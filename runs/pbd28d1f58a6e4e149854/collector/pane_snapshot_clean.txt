## Proof

Let $M$ be a complete hyperbolic manifold, so $M \cong \mathbb{H}^n / \Gamma$ wh
ere $\Gamma = \pi_1(M)$ acts freely, properly discontinuously, and isometrically
 on $\mathbb{H}^n$.

### Step 1. Free homotopy classes ↔ conjugacy classes in $\Gamma$

A closed curve $\alpha : S^1 \to M$ lifts (after choosing a basepoint and a lift
 of the basepoint) to a path $\widetilde{\alpha}$ in $\mathbb{H}^n$ from some $\
widetilde{p}$ to $\gamma \widetilde{p}$ for a unique $\gamma \in \Gamma$. Two cl
osed curves are freely homotopic if and only if the corresponding elements of $\
Gamma$ are **conjugate**. Hence free homotopy classes of closed curves in $M$ ar
e in bijection with conjugacy classes $\langle \gamma \rangle$ in $\Gamma$.

### Step 2. Deck transformations are hyperbolic or parabolic (never elliptic)

Since $\Gamma$ acts **freely** on $\mathbb{H}^n$ (the covering of a manifold has
 no fixed points), no non-identity element is elliptic. Every non-identity $\gam
ma \in \Gamma$ is either:

- **Hyperbolic**: has two fixed points on $\partial\mathbb{H}^n$, a unique invar
iant geodesic **axis** $A_\gamma$, and translates along $A_\gamma$ by a positive
 amount $\ell(\gamma) = \inf_{x \in \mathbb{H}^n} d(x, \gamma x) > 0$.
- **Parabolic**: has one fixed point on $\partial\mathbb{H}^n$, no invariant geo
desic, and $\ell(\gamma) = \inf_{x} d(x, \gamma x) = 0$ (infimum not attained).

### Step 3. The translation length is the minimum curve length in the class

For any $\gamma \in \Gamma$ and any closed curve $\alpha$ in the free homotopy c
lass corresponding to $\langle \gamma \rangle$, the lift $\widetilde{\alpha}$ ru
ns from $\widetilde{p}$ to $\gamma\widetilde{p}$ (up to conjugacy), so

$$\operatorname{len}(\alpha) \geq d(\widetilde{p},\, \gamma\widetilde{p}) \geq \
inf_{x \in \mathbb{H}^n} d(x, \gamma x) = \ell(\gamma).$$

Thus $\ell(\gamma)$ is a lower bound for the length of every curve in the class.

### Step 4. Hyperbolic case — the minimum is achieved by a unique closed geodesi
c

If $\gamma$ is **hyperbolic**, the infimum $\ell(\gamma)$ is **attained** exactl
y along the axis $A_\gamma$: for every $x \in A_\gamma$, $d(x, \gamma x) = \ell(
\gamma)$. The segment of $A_\gamma$ between $x$ and $\gamma x$ projects to a **c
losed geodesic** $\bar{A}_\gamma$ in $M$ of length $\ell(\gamma)$, which lies in
 the free homotopy class of $\langle \gamma \rangle$.

By Step 3, this closed geodesic achieves the minimum length in its class. Equali
ty in Step 3 forces $\widetilde{\alpha}$ to be a geodesic segment on $A_\gamma$,
 so $\alpha$ is (up to reparametrisation) the **same** closed geodesic. Hence th
e closed geodesic realising the minimum is **unique**.

Conjugate elements $\gamma' = \delta\gamma\delta^{-1}$ have axes $A_{\gamma'} =
\delta(A_\gamma)$, which project to the **same** closed geodesic in $M$. So the
correspondence is well-defined on conjugacy classes.

### Step 5. Parabolic case — no closed geodesic, infimum not attained

If $\gamma$ is **parabolic**, $\ell(\gamma) = 0$ but no point of $\mathbb{H}^n$
is fixed and no geodesic is invariant, so the infimum is never attained and ther
e is **no closed geodesic** in that free homotopy class. (This occurs for cusp-r
elated classes in finite-volume but non-compact $M$.)

### Step 6. Converse — every closed geodesic arises this way

Any closed geodesic $\bar{c}$ in $M$ lifts to a geodesic $\widetilde{c}$ in $\ma
thbb{H}^n$ that is invariant under some $\gamma \in \Gamma$ (the element corresp
onding to going once around $\bar{c}$). Since $\widetilde{c}$ is a geodesic pres
erved by $\gamma$, the isometry $\gamma$ must be **hyperbolic** with axis $\wide
tilde{c}$. Thus $\bar{c}$ is the projection of an axis, exactly as in Step 4, an
d is the unique shortest curve in its free homotopy class.

### Conclusion

There is a **bijection**

$$\bigl\{\text{closed geodesics in } M\bigr\} \;\longleftrightarrow\; \bigl\{\te
xt{conjugacy classes of hyperbolic elements of } \pi_1(M)\bigr\} \;\subset\; \bi
gl\{\text{non-trivial free homotopy classes of closed curves}\bigr\}.$$

Under this bijection, **each closed geodesic is the unique closed curve of minim
um length in its free homotopy class**, and its length equals the translation le
ngth $\ell(\gamma)$ of the corresponding hyperbolic deck transformation. Equival
ently:

> A non-trivial free homotopy class of closed curves in a complete hyperbolic ma
nifold contains a closed geodesic **if and only if** the infimum of lengths in t
hat class is **attained**; when it is attained, the minimiser is the **unique**
closed geodesic in the class, and its length is the minimum.

Classes corresponding to parabolic elements (cusps) have infimal length $0$, nev
er attained, and contain no closed geodesic.
