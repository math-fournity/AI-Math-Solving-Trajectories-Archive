Let $f$ be a non-constant polynomial satisfying
$$f(x-1)+f(x)+f(x+1)=\frac{f(x)^2}{2013x}\qquad(x\neq 0).$$

**Step 1. $f(0)=0$.** The left-hand side is a polynomial in $x$, so the right-ha
nd side $\frac{f(x)^2}{2013x}$ must also be a polynomial. Hence $2013x\mid f(x)^
2$, which forces $x\mid f(x)$, i.e. $f(0)=0$.

**Step 2. Degree comparison.** Write $\deg f = n$ with leading coefficient $a_n\
neq 0$. Each of $f(x-1),f(x),f(x+1)$ has leading term $a_n x^n$, so
$$\deg\bigl(f(x-1)+f(x)+f(x+1)\bigr)=n,\qquad \text{leading coeff }3a_n\neq 0.$$
On the right, since $f(x)^2$ has degree $2n$ and we divide by $x$ (using $x\mid
f(x)$), $\frac{f(x)^2}{2013x}$ has degree $2n-1$.

Equating degrees: $n=2n-1$, giving $\boxed{n=1}$.

**Step 3. Determine $f$.** Since $n=1$ and $f(0)=0$, write $f(x)=ax$ with $a\neq
 0$. Substituting:
$$a(x-1)+ax+a(x+1)=3ax,\qquad \frac{(ax)^2}{2013x}=\frac{a^2 x}{2013}.$$
So $3a=\frac{a^2}{2013}$, i.e. $a^2=6039a$, hence $a(a-6039)=0$. Since $f$ is no
n-constant, $a=6039$.

Thus $f(x)=6039x$ and $f(1)=6039$.
