## Solution

**Claim:** The minimum number of colors is **2**.

### Step 1: One color is insufficient

With a single color, every element shares the same color. The sequence $1, 2, 3,
 \ldots, 18$ is a nonconstant arithmetic progression with 18 terms (common diffe
rence $d = 1$), and it is monochromatic. Thus one color fails.

### Step 2: Two colors suffice (probabilistic method)

Consider a random 2-coloring of $\{1, 2, \ldots, 2010\}$, where each element is
independently assigned one of two colors with equal probability.

Let $X$ be the number of monochromatic 18-term arithmetic progressions. We compu
te $\mathbb{E}[X]$.

**Counting 18-term APs.** An 18-term AP $a, a+d, a+2d, \ldots, a+17d$ in $\{1, \
ldots, 2010\}$ requires $d \geq 1$ and $a + 17d \leq 2010$, i.e., $a \leq 2010 -
 17d$. For a fixed $d$, the number of valid starting points is $\max(0,\; 2010 -
 17d)$. The maximum $d$ satisfies $17d \leq 2009$, so $d \leq \lfloor 2009/17 \r
floor = 118$.

The total number of 18-term APs is:
$$N = \sum_{d=1}^{118} (2010 - 17d) = 118 \cdot 2010 - 17 \cdot \frac{118 \cdot
119}{2}$$
$$= 237180 - 17 \cdot 7021 = 237180 - 119357 = 117823.$$

**Probability each AP is monochromatic.** For any fixed 18-term AP, all 18 terms
 receive the same color with probability $2 \cdot (1/2)^{18} = 2^{-17} = 1/13107
2$.

**Expected value.** By linearity of expectation:
$$\mathbb{E}[X] = N \cdot 2^{-17} = \frac{117823}{131072}.$$

Since $117823 < 131072$ (the difference is $13249 > 0$), we have:
$$\mathbb{E}[X] = \frac{117823}{131072} \approx 0.8987 < 1.$$

**Applying the first moment method.** By Markov's inequality:
$$\Pr(X \geq 1) \leq \mathbb{E}[X] < 1.$$

Therefore $\Pr(X = 0) > 0$, which means there **exists** at least one 2-coloring
 of $\{1, \ldots, 2010\}$ with zero monochromatic 18-term arithmetic progression
s.

### Conclusion

- One color is insufficient (the trivial AP $1, 2, \ldots, 18$ is monochromatic)
.
- Two colors suffice (by the probabilistic method above, a valid 2-coloring exis
ts).

The minimum number of colors required is $\boxed{2}$.
