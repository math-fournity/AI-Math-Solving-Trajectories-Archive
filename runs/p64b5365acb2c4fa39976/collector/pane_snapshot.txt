## Solution

We compute the limit by expanding the integrand and using the identity $\sin^2\t
heta = \frac{1-\cos 2\theta}{2}$ together with the product-to-sum formula $2\sin
 u\sin v = \cos(u-v)-\cos(u+v)$.

**Expansion.**

$$
(\sin x + \sin ax)^2 = \sin^2 x + 2\sin x\,\sin ax + \sin^2 ax.
$$

Using the identities:

$$
\sin^2 x = \frac{1-\cos 2x}{2},\qquad \sin^2 ax = \frac{1-\cos 2ax}{2},
$$

$$
2\sin x\,\sin ax = \cos\big((1-a)x\big) - \cos\big((1+a)x\big).
$$

Hence

$$
(\sin x+\sin ax)^2 = 1 - \frac{\cos 2x}{2} - \frac{\cos 2ax}{2} + \cos\big((1-a)
x\big) - \cos\big((1+a)x\big).
$$

**Integrating term by term.**

$$
\int_0^T (\sin x+\sin ax)^2\,dx = T - \frac{\sin 2T}{4} - \frac{\sin 2aT}{4a} +
\frac{\sin((1-a)T)}{1-a} - \frac{\sin((1+a)T)}{1+a},
$$

valid when $a\neq 0$ and $a\neq \pm 1$ (so that all denominators are nonzero). D
ividing by $T$:

$$
\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T (\sin x+\sin ax)^2\,dx = 1 - \frac{\sin 2T}{4T} - \frac{\sin
 2aT}{4aT} + \frac{\sin((1-a)T)}{(1-a)T} - \frac{\sin((1+a)T)}{(1+a)T}.
$$

Since $|\sin\theta|\le 1$, every term of the form $\frac{\sin(\alpha T)}{\beta T
}\to 0$ as $T\to\infty$ (with $\beta\neq 0$ constant). Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T(\sin x+\sin ax)^2\,dx = 1}\qquad (a\
neq 0,\ \pm 1).
$$

**Special cases.**

- **$a=0$:** $\sin ax=0$, so the integrand is $\sin^2 x = \frac{1-\cos 2x}{2}$.
Then $\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T\sin^2 x\,dx = \frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sin 2T}{4T}\to \frac{
1}{2}$.

- **$a=1$:** $(\sin x+\sin x)^2 = 4\sin^2 x = 2(1-\cos 2x)$. Then $\frac{1}{T}\i
nt_0^T 4\sin^2 x\,dx = 2 - \frac{\sin 2T}{T}\to 2$.

- **$a=-1$:** $\sin x+\sin(-x)=\sin x-\sin x=0$, so the integrand is identically
 $0$ and the limit is $0$.

**Summary.** The limit, as a function of the real parameter $a$, is

$$
\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T(\sin x+\sin ax)^2\,dx =
\begin{cases}
0, & a=-1,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}, & a=0,\\[6pt]
1, & a\neq 0,\ \pm 1,\\[6pt]
2, & a=1.
\end{cases}
$$
