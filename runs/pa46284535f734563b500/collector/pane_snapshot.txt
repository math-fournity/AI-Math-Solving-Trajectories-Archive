## Proof

**Setup.** Let $(z_n)$ be a sequence of distinct points in $D(0,1)$ with $\lim_{
n\to\infty} z_n = 0$. Since $0 \in D(0,1)$ and the $z_n$ are distinct, the set $
S = \{z_n : n \geq 1\}$ has $0$ as an accumulation point lying inside $D(0,1)$.

We use the **Identity Theorem** for analytic functions: *if $g$ is analytic on a
 domain $\Omega$ and vanishes on a subset of $\Omega$ that has an accumulation p
oint in $\Omega$, then $g \equiv 0$ on $\Omega$.*

---

### Statement P is TRUE

**Existence.** The function $f(z) = \sin z$ is entire, hence analytic on $D(0,1)
$, and satisfies $f(z_n) = \sin(z_n)$ for every $n$.

**Uniqueness.** Suppose $h$ is any analytic function on $D(0,1)$ with $h(z_n) =
\sin(z_n)$ for all $n$. Define $g(z) = h(z) - \sin z$. Then $g$ is analytic on $
D(0,1)$ and $g(z_n) = 0$ for every $n$. Since the $z_n$ are distinct and converg
e to $0 \in D(0,1)$, the set $S$ has accumulation point $0$ inside $D(0,1)$. By
the Identity Theorem, $g \equiv 0$ on $D(0,1)$, i.e., $h = \sin$ on $D(0,1)$.

Hence $f(z) = \sin z$ is the **unique** analytic function on $D(0,1)$ satisfying
 the stated condition. ✓

---

### Statement Q is FALSE

Suppose, for contradiction, that an analytic function $f$ on $D(0,1)$ exists wit
h
$$f(z_n) = \begin{cases} 0, & n \text{ even}, \\ 1, & n \text{ odd}. \end{cases}
$$

Consider the subsequence $(z_{2k})_{k \geq 1}$ of even-indexed points. These are
 distinct (being a subsequence of distinct points) and converge to $0 \in D(0,1)
$. By hypothesis, $f(z_{2k}) = 0$ for every $k$, so $f$ vanishes on the set $\{z
_{2k}\}$, which has accumulation point $0$ inside $D(0,1)$.

By the Identity Theorem, $f \equiv 0$ on $D(0,1)$.

But then for every odd index $n$, we must have $f(z_n) = 0$, contradicting the r
equirement $f(z_n) = 1$ for odd $n$.

This contradiction shows that **no such analytic function exists**. ✗

---

### Conclusion

**Statement P is true; Statement Q is false.**

The key distinction is that P prescribes values consistent with a single analyti
c function ($\sin z$) on a set with an accumulation point in the domain, forcing
 both existence and uniqueness. Q prescribes values ($0$ and $1$) on two intertw
ined subsequences both accumulating at $0$; the values on the even subsequence a
lone already force $f \equiv 0$ by the Identity Theorem, which is incompatible w
ith the values required on the odd subsequence.
