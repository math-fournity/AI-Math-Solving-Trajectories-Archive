## Proof

Let $A$ be a $3\times 3$ integer matrix with $A A^{T} = I$. The condition $A A^{
T}=I$ says that the three **columns** $c_1, c_2, c_3$ of $A$ form an orthonormal
 set:

$$c_i \cdot c_j = \delta_{ij}.$$

In particular each column has unit norm: $c_i \cdot c_i = 1$.

**Step 1: Each column is a signed standard basis vector.**

Write $c_i = (x,y,z)^T$ with $x,y,z\in\mathbb Z$. Then $x^2+y^2+z^2 = 1$. The on
ly integer solutions are those with exactly one coordinate equal to $\pm 1$ and
the other two equal to $0$. Hence each column is of the form $\pm e_k$ for some
$k\in\{1,2,3\}$, where $e_k$ is a standard basis vector.

**Step 2: Distinct columns use distinct basis directions.**

Since $c_i \cdot c_j = 0$ for $i\neq j$, two columns cannot be the same signed b
asis vector, and more specifically they cannot use the **same** basis direction:
 if $c_i = \pm e_k$ and $c_j = \pm e_k$, then $c_i\cdot c_j = (\pm1)(\pm1) = \pm
1 \neq 0$. So the three columns must use three distinct directions $e_{\sigma(1)
}, e_{\sigma(2)}, e_{\sigma(3)}$ for some permutation $\sigma\in S_3$.

**Step 3: Count.**

- Choose the permutation $\sigma\in S_3$ assigning basis directions to columns:
$3! = 6$ choices.
- For each of the 3 columns, independently choose the sign $\pm$: $2^3 = 8$ choi
ces.

Every such choice gives a matrix $A = P_\sigma D$ where $P_\sigma$ is the permut
ation matrix of $\sigma$ and $D$ is a diagonal matrix with $\pm1$ entries; one c
hecks directly that $A A^T = P_\sigma D D^T P_\sigma^T = P_\sigma I P_\sigma^T =
 I$. Conversely, Steps 1–2 show every element of $S$ arises this way.

Therefore

$$|S| = 3!\cdot 2^3 = 6\cdot 8 = 48.$$
