Looking at this problem, I need to find the fundamental group of two copies of $
S^2$ with two pairs of points identified.

## Proof

Let $X$ and $Y$ be copies of $S^2$, with distinguished points $x_1, x_2 \in X$ a
nd $y_1, y_2 \in Y$. The space $Z$ is the quotient of $X \sqcup Y$ by the identi
fications $x_1 \sim y_1$ and $x_2 \sim y_2$. Denote the identified points by $p
= [x_1] = [y_1]$ and $q = [x_2] = [y_2]$.

### CW Structure

Give each copy of $S^2$ a CW structure with:
- **0-cells:** the two distinguished points ($x_1, x_2$ for $X$; $y_1, y_2$ for
$Y$).
- **1-cell:** a single arc connecting the two 0-cells (call it $a$ in $X$, $b$ i
n $Y$).
- **2-cell:** the complementary disk, whose attaching map traverses the 1-cell f
orward and back (i.e., along $a \cdot a^{-1}$ in $X$, and $b \cdot b^{-1}$ in $Y
$).

This is a valid CW decomposition of $S^2$: the 1-skeleton is an arc (contractibl
e), and the 2-cell is attached along a null-homotopic loop, yielding $S^2$ with
$\pi_1 = 0$ as expected.

After identifying $x_1 \sim y_1 = p$ and $x_2 \sim y_2 = q$, the CW structure of
 $Z$ is:
- **0-cells:** $p, q$.
- **1-cells:** $a$ (arc $p \to q$ from $X$) and $b$ (arc $p \to q$ from $Y$).
- **2-cells:** $A$ (from $X$) and $B$ (from $Y$).

### Computing $\pi_1(Z)$

**1-skeleton.** The 1-skeleton $Z^{(1)}$ consists of two arcs $a$ and $b$ from $
p$ to $q$. This is homeomorphic to $S^1$ (a circle formed by traversing $a$ from
 $p$ to $q$ and returning via $b^{-1}$). Thus:
$$\pi_1(Z^{(1)}, p) \cong \mathbb{Z}, \quad \text{generated by } \gamma = a \cdo
t b^{-1}.$$

**Attaching the 2-cell $A$.** In $X$, the 2-cell $A$ is attached along $a \cdot
a^{-1}$, which is the trivial loop in $\pi_1(Z^{(1)}, p)$. Attaching $A$ imposes
 the relation $a \cdot a^{-1} = 1$, which is already trivial. So $\pi_1$ is unch
anged.

**Attaching the 2-cell $B$.** Similarly, $B$ is attached along $b \cdot b^{-1}$,
 also trivial. No new relation is imposed.

### Conclusion

No relations are imposed on the generator $\gamma = a \cdot b^{-1}$, so:
$$\boxed{\pi_1(Z) \cong \mathbb{Z}.}$$

### Verification via Van Kampen (Groupoid Version)

Take open sets $U$ (thickening of $X$) and $V$ (thickening of $Y$) covering $Z$.
 Then $U \simeq S^2$ and $V \simeq S^2$, so $\pi_1(U) = \pi_1(V) = 0$. The inter
section $U \cap V$ has two contractible components (neighborhoods of $p$ and $q$
). By the groupoid version of Van Kampen's theorem, $\pi_1(Z, p)$ is the automor
phism group of $p$ in the pushout groupoid. The unique morphism $\alpha: p \to q
$ in $\pi_1(U)$ and $\beta: p \to q$ in $\pi_1(V)$ generate a free loop $\alpha
\beta^{-1}$ at $p$ with no relations, confirming $\pi_1(Z, p) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
