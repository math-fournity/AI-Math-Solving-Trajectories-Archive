Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $M_2(\Gamma_0(N))$ has posi

## Proof

**Answer: True.** We construct an explicit non-zero element of $M_2(\Gamma_0(N))
$ for every $N > 1$.

**Setup.** Recall the Eisenstein series $E_2(\tau) = 1 - 24\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \
sigma_1(n) q^n$ (with $q = e^{2\pi i \tau}$), which is a *quasi*-modular form of
 weight 2. Its transformation law under $\gamma = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} a &
b \\ c & d \end{smallmatrix}\bigr) \in SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ is:

$$E_2(\gamma\tau) = (c\tau+d)^2\, E_2(\tau) + \frac{12c}{2\pi i}(c\tau+d).$$

**Construction.** Define
$$f(\tau) = E_2(\tau) - N\, E_2(N\tau).$$

**$f \neq 0$:** The $q$-expansion of $E_2(N\tau)$ is $1 - 24\sum_{n \geq 1} \sig
ma_1(n)\, q^{Nn}$, so the coefficient of $q^1$ in $f$ is $-24\sigma_1(1) = -24 \
neq 0$ (since the $q^1$ term of $N\,E_2(N\tau)$ vanishes for $N > 1$). Thus $f$
is non-zero.

**Modularity for $\Gamma_0(N)$:** Let $\gamma = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} a & b
\\ c & d \end{smallmatrix}\bigr) \in \Gamma_0(N)$, so $N \mid c$; write $c = Nc_
0$.

- $E_2(\gamma\tau) = (c\tau+d)^2 E_2(\tau) + \frac{12c}{2\pi i}(c\tau+d)$.

- For $E_2(N\gamma\tau)$: since $N\gamma\tau = \frac{a(N\tau)+Nb}{c_0(N\tau)+d}$
 and $c_0(N\tau)+d = c\tau+d$, we apply the transformation law with the matrix $
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} a & Nb \\ c_0 & d \end{smallmatrix}\bigr) \in SL_2(\ma
thbb{Z})$ (its determinant is $ad - Nb\cdot c_0 = ad - bc = 1$):

$$E_2(N\gamma\tau) = (c\tau+d)^2\, E_2(N\tau) + \frac{12c_0}{2\pi i}(c\tau+d) =
(c\tau+d)^2\, E_2(N\tau) + \frac{12c}{2\pi i\, N}(c\tau+d).$$

Combining:
$$f(\gamma\tau) = (c\tau+d)^2 E_2(\tau) + \frac{12c}{2\pi i}(c\tau+d) - N\!\left
[(c\tau+d)^2 E_2(N\tau) + \frac{12c}{2\pi i\, N}(c\tau+d)\right]$$
$$= (c\tau+d)^2\bigl[E_2(\tau) - N\,E_2(N\tau)\bigr] + \frac{12c}{2\pi i}(c\tau+
d)\underbrace{(1 - 1)}_{=\,0}$$
$$= (c\tau+d)^2\, f(\tau).$$

The anomalous term cancels exactly, so $f$ transforms as a weight-2 modular form
 for $\Gamma_0(N)$.

**Holomorphicity:** $E_2$ is holomorphic on $\mathbb{H}$, hence so is $f$. At th
e cusp $\infty$, the $q$-expansion $f(\tau) = (1-N) - 24\sum_{n\geq 1}\sigma_1(n
)\,q^n + 24N\sum_{n\geq 1}\sigma_1(n)\,q^{Nn}$ has no negative powers of $q$, so
 $f$ is holomorphic at $\infty$. Since $f$ is modular, it admits a $q$-expansion
 at every cusp of $\Gamma_0(N)$, and one verifies (or invokes the standard fact
that such Eisenstein-type combinations are holomorphic at all cusps) that $f$ is
 holomorphic at every cusp.

**Conclusion.** For every integer $N > 1$, the function $f(\tau) = E_2(\tau) - N
\,E_2(N\tau)$ is a non-zero element of $M_2(\Gamma_0(N))$. Therefore $\dim M_2(\
Gamma_0(N)) \geq 1 > 0$ for all $N > 1$.
