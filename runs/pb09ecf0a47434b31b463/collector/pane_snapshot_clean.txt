## Solution

We analyze the series $\displaystyle S = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^{n-1}\frac{(\l
n n)^p}{n}$, where the $n=1$ term is $0$ (since $\ln 1 = 0$), so the series effe
ctively begins at $n=2$.

**Claim:** The series converges for **every real value of $p$**.

**Proof.** We apply the Alternating Series Test (Leibniz's test) to $a_n = \dfra
c{(\ln n)^p}{n}$ (for $n \ge 2$), verifying the two required conditions.

**Condition 1: $a_n \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$.**

Consider $f(x) = \dfrac{(\ln x)^p}{x}$ for $x > e$ (so that $\ln x > 1$ and $(\l
n x)^p$ is well-defined and positive for any real $p$). Taking logarithms:

$$\ln f(x) = p\,\ln(\ln x) - \ln x.$$

As $x \to \infty$, $\ln x \to \infty$ while $\ln(\ln x) = o(\ln x)$. Therefore $
p\,\ln(\ln x) - \ln x \to -\infty$, which gives $f(x) \to 0$. Hence $a_n \to 0$
for **every** real $p$.

**Condition 2: $a_n$ is eventually decreasing.**

Compute the derivative of $f(x) = \dfrac{(\ln x)^p}{x}$:

$$f'(x) = \frac{p(\ln x)^{p-1}\cdot\frac{1}{x}\cdot x - (\ln x)^p}{x^2} = \frac{
(\ln x)^{p-1}\bigl(p - \ln x\bigr)}{x^2}.$$

For $x > e^{\max(1,\,p)}$, we have $\ln x > 1$ (so $(\ln x)^{p-1} > 0$) and $\ln
 x > p$ (so $p - \ln x < 0$). Thus $f'(x) < 0$ for all sufficiently large $x$, m
eaning $f$ — and hence the sequence $a_n$ — is **eventually decreasing**, for ev
ery real $p$.

**Conclusion of the Alternating Series Test.** Both conditions hold for every re
al $p$, so the series converges (conditionally) for all $p \in \mathbb{R}$.

**Remark on absolute convergence.** For completeness, the series converges *abso
lutely* only when $p < -1$: by the integral test with the substitution $u = \ln
x$,

$$\int_2^{\infty} \frac{(\ln x)^p}{x}\,dx = \int_{\ln 2}^{\infty} u^p\,du,$$

which converges if and only if $p < -1$. For $p \ge -1$ the convergence is condi
tional (not absolute), but it is still genuine convergence by the argument above
.

**Answer:** The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-1}\frac{(\ln n)^
p}{n}$ converges for **all real values of $p$** (absolutely for $p<-1$, conditio
nally for $p\ge -1$).
