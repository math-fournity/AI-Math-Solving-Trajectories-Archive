## Fourier-Bessel Series of f(x) = 1 on (0, 1)

**Setup.** The eigenfunctions of the Sturm–Liouville problem
$$ (x y')' + \lambda^2 x\, y = 0, \quad y(1)=0, \quad |y(0)|<\infty $$
are $J_0(\lambda_j x)$, where $\lambda_j$ ($j=1,2,\dots$) are the positive zeros
 of $J_0$. These are orthogonal with weight $w(x)=x$ on $[0,1]$:
$$ \int_0^1 x\, J_0(\lambda_j x) J_0(\lambda_k x)\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\big[J_1(\lam
bda_j)\big]^2 \delta_{jk}. $$

This norm formula follows from the standard identity (with $J_0(\lambda_j)=0$ at
 the upper limit and the regularity $J_1(0)=0$ at the lower limit):
$$ \int_0^1 x\, J_0(\lambda x)^2\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2}\big[J_0(\lambda)^2 + J_1(\la
mbda)^2\big]\Big|_{\lambda=\lambda_j} = \tfrac{1}{2}J_1(\lambda_j)^2. $$

**Expansion form.** We seek
$$ 1 = \sum_{j=1}^{\infty} c_j\, J_0(\lambda_j x), \qquad 0<x<1. $$

**Computing the coefficients.** By orthogonality,
$$ c_j = \frac{\displaystyle\int_0^1 x\cdot 1\cdot J_0(\lambda_j x)\,dx}{\displa
ystyle\int_0^1 x\, J_0(\lambda_j x)^2\,dx} = \frac{2}{J_1(\lambda_j)^2}\int_0^1
x\, J_0(\lambda_j x)\,dx. $$

**Evaluating the integral.** Using the Bessel recurrence identity
$$ \frac{d}{dx}\Big[x\, J_1(\lambda x)\Big] = \lambda\, x\, J_0(\lambda x), $$
we integrate:
$$ \int_0^1 x\, J_0(\lambda_j x)\,dx = \frac{1}{\lambda_j}\Big[x\, J_1(\lambda_j
 x)\Big]_{0}^{1} = \frac{J_1(\lambda_j)}{\lambda_j}, $$
since $J_1(0)=0$ kills the lower endpoint.

**Substituting back:**
$$ c_j = \frac{2}{J_1(\lambda_j)^2}\cdot\frac{J_1(\lambda_j)}{\lambda_j} = \frac
{2}{\lambda_j\, J_1(\lambda_j)}. $$

**Result.**
$$ \boxed{\,1 = \sum_{j=1}^{\infty} \frac{2}{\lambda_j\, J_1(\lambda_j)}\, J_0(\
lambda_j x), \qquad 0<x<1,\,} $$
where $\lambda_1 < \lambda_2 < \cdots$ are the positive zeros of $J_0$.

**Convergence note.** Since $f(x)=1$ is piecewise smooth on $[0,1]$ and satisfie
s the boundary condition $f(1)=1\neq 0$ (a jump-type endpoint mismatch with the
eigenfunctions which vanish at $x=1$), the series converges to $1$ for every $0<
x<1$, with the usual Gibbs-type behavior near $x=1$ (converging to the average $
1/2$ at $x=1$ itself). The coefficients decay as $O(\lambda_j^{-3/2})$, which is
 summable, ensuring absolute and uniform convergence on compact subsets of $(0,1
)$.
