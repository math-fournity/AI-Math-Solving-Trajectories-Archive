## Proof

We evaluate
$$I=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{3}\ln x}{(x^{4}+1)^{3}}\,dx.$$

**Step 1: Reduce to a logarithmic beta-type integral.**

Set $u=x^{4}$. Then $du=4x^{3}\,dx$, so $x^{3}dx=\tfrac{du}{4}$, and $\ln x=\tfr
ac14\ln u$. The limits $x:0\to\infty$ map to $u:0\to\infty$. Hence
$$
I=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\tfrac14\ln u}{(u+1)^{3}}\cdot\frac{du}{4}
=\frac{1}{16}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln u}{(u+1)^{3}}\,du.
$$
Denote $J:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln u}{(u+1)^{3}}\,du$, so $I=J/16$.

**Step 2: Introduce a parameter and use the beta function.**

For $\operatorname{Re}s,\operatorname{Re}(3-s)>0$ (i.e. $0<\operatorname{Re}s<3$
), the beta integral gives
$$
F(s):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{s-1}}{(1+u)^{3}}\,du
=B(s,3-s)=\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma(3-s)}{\Gamma(3)}
=\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma(3-s)}{2},
$$
since $\Gamma(3)=2!=2$. Differentiating under the integral sign (justified by do
minated convergence on compact $s$-subintervals of $(0,3)$),
$$
F'(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{s-1}\ln u}{(1+u)^{3}}\,du.
$$
The desired $J$ is exactly $F'(1)$ (where $u^{s-1}\big|_{s=1}=1$).

**Step 3: Differentiate the closed form.**

Using $\Gamma'(z)=\Gamma(z)\psi(z)$ with $\psi$ the digamma function,
$$
F'(s)=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\Gamma'(s)\Gamma(3-s)-\Gamma(s)\Gamma'(3-s)\Big]
=\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma(3-s)}{2}\Big[\psi(s)-\psi(3-s)\Big].
$$
Evaluate at $s=1$:
- $\Gamma(1)=1$,
- $\Gamma(3-1)=\Gamma(2)=1$,
- $\psi(1)=-\gamma$ (Euler's constant),
- $\psi(2)=\psi(1)+\tfrac{1}{1}=1-\gamma$, using the recurrence $\psi(z+1)=\psi(
z)+\tfrac1z$.

Therefore
$$
J=F'(1)=\frac{1\cdot 1}{2}\big[\psi(1)-\psi(2)\big]
=\frac{1}{2}\big[(-\gamma)-(1-\gamma)\big]
=\frac{1}{2}(-1)=-\frac12.
$$

**Step 4: Conclude.**

$$
I=\frac{J}{16}=\frac{-1/2}{16}=-\frac{1}{32}.
$$

This verifies the claimed value. $\blacksquare$
