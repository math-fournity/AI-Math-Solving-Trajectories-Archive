## Proof

**Step 1: Reformulate.** Since $\pi(k+\tfrac12) = (2k+1)\pi/2$, we write
$$S = \frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k}{(2k+1)\cosh\bigl((k+\tfrac12
)\pi\bigr)}.$$

**Step 2: Fourier series of the Jacobi elliptic function $\operatorname{cn}$.**
The standard Fourier expansion (see Whittaker & Watson, or DLMF §22.11) is:
$$\operatorname{cn}(u,k) = \frac{2\pi}{kK}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n+1/2}}{1+
q^{2n+1}}\cos\frac{(2n+1)\pi u}{2K},$$
where $K=K(k)$ is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind and $q = e^{-
\pi K'(k)/K(k)}$ is the nome, with $K'(k)=K(k')$, $k'=\sqrt{1-k^2}$.

**Step 3: Specialize to $q=e^{-\pi}$ (lemniscatic case).** When $k = k' = 1/\sqr
t{2}$, we have $K'(k)=K(k)$, so $q = e^{-\pi}$. Setting $x=(n+\tfrac12)\pi$:
$$\frac{q^{n+1/2}}{1+q^{2n+1}} = \frac{e^{-x}}{1+e^{-2x}} = \frac{1}{e^x+e^{-x}}
 = \frac{1}{2\cosh x} = \frac{1}{2\cosh\bigl((n+\tfrac12)\pi\bigr)}.$$
Thus
$$\operatorname{cn}(u,k) = \frac{\pi}{kK}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\cos\frac{(2n+
1)\pi u}{2K}}{\cosh\bigl((n+\tfrac12)\pi\bigr)}.$$

**Step 4: Integrate from $0$ to $K$.** Term-by-term integration gives
$$\int_0^K \operatorname{cn}(u,k)\,du = \frac{\pi}{kK}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1
}{\cosh\bigl((n+\tfrac12)\pi\bigr)}\cdot\frac{2K}{(2n+1)\pi}\sin\frac{(2n+1)\pi
u}{2K}\bigg|_0^K.$$
Since $\sin\frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2} = (-1)^n$, this yields
$$\int_0^K \operatorname{cn}(u,k)\,du = \frac{2}{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)
^n}{(2n+1)\cosh\bigl((n+\tfrac12)\pi\bigr)}.$$

**Step 5: Evaluate the integral via substitution.** Using the Jacobi amplitude s
ubstitution $u = F(\phi,k)$, so $\operatorname{cn}(u,k)=\cos\phi$ and $du = d\ph
i/\sqrt{1-k^2\sin^2\phi}$, with limits $\phi: 0\to\pi/2$:
$$\int_0^K \operatorname{cn}(u,k)\,du = \int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\cos\phi\,d\phi}{\sq
rt{1-k^2\sin^2\phi}}.$$
Setting $t=\sin\phi$:
$$= \int_0^1 \frac{dt}{\sqrt{1-k^2 t^2}} = \frac{1}{k}\arcsin(k)\bigg|_0^1 = \fr
ac{\arcsin k}{k}.$$

**Step 6: Combine.** From Steps 4 and 5:
$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n}{(2n+1)\cosh\bigl((n+\tfrac12)\pi\bigr)} = \fr
ac{k}{2}\cdot\frac{\arcsin k}{k} = \frac{\arcsin k}{2}.$$
Therefore
$$S = \frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\frac{\arcsin k}{2} = \frac{\arcsin k}{\pi}.$$
With $k = 1/\sqrt{2}$, $\arcsin(1/\sqrt{2}) = \pi/4$, so
$$S = \frac{\pi/4}{\pi} = \frac{1}{4}.$$

**Numerical verification.** The first few terms give $S \approx \frac{2}{\pi}(0.
39854 - 0.00599 + 0.00016 - \cdots) \approx 0.25000$, confirming the result.

$$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{4}}$$
