## Proof

**Case \(n = 1\).** Take \(F = K = \mathbb{Q}\). Then \(:F]=1\) and there is t
rivially no strictly intermediate field.

**Case \(n \geq 2\).** We use the *generic polynomial* and the Galois correspond
ence.

### Setup

Let \(k = \mathbb{Q}\). Introduce algebraically independent indeterminates \(t_1
, \dots, t_n\) and let
\[
s_1, \dots, s_n \in k(t_1,\dots,t_n)
\]
be the elementary symmetric polynomials in the \(t_i\). Set
\[
F := k(s_1, \dots, s_n), \qquad N := k(t_1, \dots, t_n).
\]
By the fundamental theorem of symmetric functions, \(F = N^{S_n}\) is the fixed
field of \(S_n\) acting on \(N\) by permuting the \(t_i\). Hence \(N/F\) is a Ga
lois extension with
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(N/F) \cong S_n, \qquad [N:F] = |S_n| = n!\,.
\]

### The degree-\(n\) subextension

Define the generic polynomial
\[
f(x) = \prod_{i=1}^{n}(x - t_i) = x^n - s_1 x^{n-1} + s_2 x^{n-2} - \cdots + (-1
)^n s_n \in F[x].
\]
Let \(\alpha = t_1\) and \(K := F(\alpha) = F(t_1)\). The stabilizer of \(t_1\)
in \(S_n\) is
\[
H := \operatorname{Stab}_{S_n}(t_1) \cong S_{\{2,\dots,n\}} \cong S_{n-1},
\]
so by the Galois correspondence
\[
 : F] = [S_n : H] = \frac{n!}{(n-1)!} = n.
\]
Equivalently, \(f\) is the minimal polynomial of \(t_1\) over \(F\) (it vanishes
 at \(t_1\), has degree \(n\), and \([F(t_1):F]=n\)), so \(f\) is irreducible of
 degree \(n\) over \(F\). Thus \(:F] = n\).

### No intermediate fields

By the Galois correspondence for the (Galois) extension \(N/F\), the intermediat
e fields \(L\) with
\[
F \subseteq L \subseteq K
\]
are in bijection with the subgroups \(J\) satisfying
\[
H \leq J \leq G, \qquad G := \operatorname{Gal}(N/F) \cong S_n.
\]
(The field \(K\) corresponds to \(H\), and \(F\) corresponds to \(G\); a subgrou
p \(J\) with \(H \leq J \leq G\) corresponds to the fixed field \(N^J\), which l
ies between \(N^G = F\) and \(N^H = K\).)

Therefore \(K/F\) has a strictly intermediate field if and only if \(H\) is **no
t** a maximal subgroup of \(G = S_n\).

**Claim.** \(S_{n-1}\) is a maximal subgroup of \(S_n\) for every \(n \geq 2\).

*Proof of claim.* The natural action of \(S_n\) on \(\{1, \dots, n\}\) is **2-tr
ansitive** for \(n \geq 2\): given ordered pairs \((i,j)\) and \((k,\ell)\) with
 \(i \neq j\) and \(k \neq \ell\), the permutation sending \(i \mapsto k,\; j \m
apsto \ell\) (and extending arbitrarily) lies in \(S_n\). Every 2-transitive act
ion is **primitive** (a nontrivial block \(B\) containing a point \(i\) must, by
 transitivity, have \(|B|\) dividing \(n\); by 2-transitivity any two points of
\(B\) can be sent to any two points, forcing \(|B| \geq 2\) and then any point o
utside \(B\) together with a point inside \(B\) can be mapped into \(B\), contra
dicting properness). A standard theorem states that a transitive group action is
 primitive if and only if its point stabilizer is a maximal subgroup. Since \(S_
n\) acts primitively on \(\{1,\dots,n\}\), the point stabilizer \(S_{n-1}\) is m
aximal in \(S_n\). \(\square\)

(For concreteness one may verify small cases directly: \(n=2\): \(\{e\}\) is max
imal in \(S_2\); \(n=3\): \(S_2\) is maximal in \(S_3\) since the only larger su
bgroup is \(A_3\), which does not contain the transposition \((12)\); \(n=4\): \
(S_3\) is maximal in \(S_4\) since the only subgroup of order \(12\) is \(A_4\),
 which contains no odd permutations.)

Since \(H \cong S_{n-1}\) is maximal in \(G \cong S_n\), the only subgroups \(J\
) with \(H \leq J \leq G\) are \(J = H\) and \(J = G\). These correspond to \(L
= K\) and \(L = F\) respectively. Hence there is **no** field \(L\) with
\[
F \subsetneq L \subsetneq K.
\]

### Summary of the construction

For each \(n \geq 2\):
\[
F = \mathbb{Q}(s_1, \dots, s_n), \qquad K = F(t_1) = \mathbb{Q}(t_1, s_1, \dots,
 s_n),
\]
where \(t_1, \dots, t_n\) are algebraically independent and \(s_1, \dots, s_n\)
are the elementary symmetric polynomials in them. Then \(:F] = n\) and \(K/F\)
 has no strictly intermediate fields, because the Galois group of the splitting
field over \(F\) is \(S_n\), the subgroup fixing \(K\) is the point stabilizer \
(S_{n-1}\), and \(S_{n-1}\) is a maximal subgroup of \(S_n\).

For \(n = 1\), take \(F = K = \mathbb{Q}\).

This gives the required example for every \(n \in \mathbb{N}\). \(\blacksquare\)
