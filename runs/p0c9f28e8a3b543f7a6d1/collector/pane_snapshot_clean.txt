## Proof

Evaluate
$$I=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{x^{2}-7x+1}{x^{4}+1}\,dx.$$

**Step 1: Split and use parity.**

$$I=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{x^{2}+1}{x^{4}+1}\,dx-7\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty
}\frac{x}{x^{4}+1}\,dx.$$

The integrand $\dfrac{x}{x^{4}+1}$ is odd and $x^{4}+1>0$ everywhere, so the sec
ond integral over the symmetric interval $(-\infty,+\infty)$ vanishes. Hence

$$I=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{x^{2}+1}{x^{4}+1}\,dx.$$

**Step 2: A substitution exploiting self-reciprocity.**

For $x\neq 0$, divide numerator and denominator by $x^{2}$:

$$\frac{x^{2}+1}{x^{4}+1}=\frac{1+\frac{1}{x^{2}}}{x^{2}+\frac{1}{x^{2}}}=\frac{
1+\frac{1}{x^{2}}}{\left(x-\frac{1}{x}\right)^{2}+2}.$$

Set $u=x-\dfrac{1}{x}$, so $du=\left(1+\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}\right)dx$. Split the int
egral at $0$:

- On $(0,+\infty)$: as $x:0^{+}\to+\infty$, $u:-\infty\to+\infty$.
- On $(-\infty,0)$: as $x:-\infty\to 0^{-}$, $u:-\infty\to+\infty$ (since $-1/x\
to+\infty$).

Both halves therefore contribute

$$\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{du}{u^{2}+2}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}.$$

Thus

$$I=2\cdot\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}=\sqrt{2}\,\pi.$$

**Step 3: Verification via the standard Beta-integral values.**

It is well known (and follows from $x^{4}+1=(x^{2}+\sqrt{2}\,x+1)(x^{2}-\sqrt{2}
\,x+1)$, or from $x=t^{1/4}$ and the Beta function) that

$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x^{4}+1}=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{2}},\qquad \int_{0}^{\in
fty}\frac{x^{2}\,dx}{x^{4}+1}=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{2}}$$

(the second follows from the first by $x\mapsto 1/x$). Therefore

$$\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{x^{2}+1}{x^{4}+1}\,dx=2\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt
{2}}+\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{2}}\right)=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot 2=\sqrt{2}\,\pi,$$

confirming the result.

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{x^{2}-7x+1}{x^{4}+1}\,dx=\sq
rt{2}\,\pi.}$$
