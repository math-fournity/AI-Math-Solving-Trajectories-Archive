## Solution

We count ordered 5-tuples $(a,b,c,d,e)$ of **distinct** non-negative integers su
mming to 18, where the five positions correspond to the labeled servers Alpha, B
eta, Gamma, Delta, Epsilon.

### Step 1: Reduce to unordered sets

Since the servers are labeled, every unordered set $\{v_1,\dots,v_5\}$ of 5 dist
inct non-negative integers summing to 18 can be assigned to the 5 servers in $5!
 = 120$ ways. So:

$$\text{Answer} = (\text{number of such sets}) \times 120.$$

### Step 2: Count unordered sets via a bijection to partitions

Let $0 \le v_1 < v_2 < v_3 < v_4 < v_5$ with $v_1+\cdots+v_5 = 18$. Substitute $
v_i = (i-1) + u_i$, so

$$u_1 \le u_2 \le u_3 \le u_4 \le u_5, \qquad u_i \ge 0, \qquad \sum u_i = 18 -
(0+1+2+3+4) = 8.$$

The strict increase of $v_i$ is guaranteed by the $(i-1)$ offset even when $u_i$
 are equal. Thus the number of valid sets equals the number of **non-decreasing
sequences of 5 non-negative integers summing to 8**, which is the number of **pa
rtitions of 8 into at most 5 parts**.

The 22 partitions of 8 are:

This gives $22 - 4 = 18$ partitions with at most 5 parts.

### Step 4: Verify a sample

For instance, partition $2+2+2+2$ gives $u=(0,2,2,2,2)$, hence $v=(0,3,4,5,6)$,
sum $=18$ ✓. Partition $4+1+1+1+1$ gives $u=(1,1,1,1,4)$, hence $v=(1,2,3,4,8)$,
 sum $=18$ ✓. All 18 cases check out.

### Final computation

$$\text{Answer} = 18 \times 5! = 18 \times 120 = \boxed{2160}.$$
