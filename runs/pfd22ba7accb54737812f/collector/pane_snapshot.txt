## Solution

We want to compute the quadratic covariation
$$\langle t^2 B^1_t,\; t^3 B^2_t \rangle_T,$$
where $B^1$ and $B^2$ are **independent** Brownian motions.

### Step 1: Apply Itô's product rule to identify the martingale parts

Let $X_t = t^2 B^1_t$ and $Y_t = t^3 B^2_t$. By Itô's formula:

$$dX_t = 2t\, B^1_t\, dt + t^2\, dB^1_t, \qquad dY_t = 3t^2\, B^2_t\, dt + t^3\,
 dB^2_t.$$

Each process decomposes into a **finite-variation part** (the $dt$ terms) and a
**martingale part** (the $dB^i_t$ terms).

### Step 2: Use the bilinearity property of quadratic covariation

The quadratic covariation $\langle X, Y\rangle$ depends **only on the martingale
 (local-martingale) parts** of $X$ and $Y$. Finite-variation parts contribute no
thing to quadratic covariation. Therefore:

$$d\langle X, Y\rangle_t = t^2 \cdot t^3 \; d\langle B^1, B^2\rangle_t = t^5 \;
d\langle B^1, B^2\rangle_t.$$

### Step 3: Use independence of $B^1$ and $B^2$

Since $B^1$ and $B^2$ are **independent** Brownian motions, their cross-variatio
n vanishes identically:

$$\langle B^1, B^2\rangle_t = 0 \quad \text{for all } t \geq 0.$$

### Step 4: Conclude

$$\langle t^2 B^1_t,\; t^3 B^2_t \rangle_T = \int_0^T t^5 \, d\langle B^1, B^2\r
angle_t = \int_0^T t^5 \cdot 0 \, dt = 0.$$

### Result

$$\boxed{\langle t^2 B^1_t,\; t^3 B^2_t \rangle_T = 0}$$

The quadratic covariation is zero because the two Brownian motions are independe
nt, so their cross-variation is identically zero, and the deterministic prefacto
rs $t^2$ and $t^3$ cannot create covariation where none exists.
