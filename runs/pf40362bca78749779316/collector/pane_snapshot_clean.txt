## Multidimensional Fourier Series on a Box

### Setup

Let \( f: \mathbb{X} \to \mathbb{C} \) where \( \mathbb{X} = [0, X_1]\times\cdot
s\times[0,X_D] \), with \( X_j > 0 \). We use the complex-exponential basis adap
ted to each coordinate. Define the multi-index \( \mathbf{n} = (n_1,\ldots,n_D)\
in\mathbb{Z}^D \) and the basis function

\[
e_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x}) \;=\; \prod_{j=1}^{D} \exp\!\Bigl(2\pi i\, \tfrac{n_j
 x_j}{X_j}\Bigr).
\]

These form an orthogonal system on \( \mathbb{X} \) w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure:

\[
\int_{\mathbb{X}} e_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x})\,\overline{e_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{x
})}\,d\mathbf{x}
= \prod_{j=1}^{D} \int_{0}^{X_j} e^{2\pi i (n_j-m_j)x_j/X_j}\,dx_j
= \begin{cases}\prod_{j=1}^{D} X_j, & \mathbf{n}=\mathbf{m},\\[2pt] 0, & \mathbf
{n}\neq\mathbf{m}.\end{cases}
\]

Writing \( V := \prod_{j=1}^{D} X_j = |\mathbb{X}| \), the Fourier coefficients
are

\[
\boxed{\;\hat f(\mathbf{n}) \;=\; \frac{1}{V}\int_{\mathbb{X}} f(\mathbf{x})\,\o
verline{e_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x})}\,d\mathbf{x}
\;=\; \frac{1}{V}\int_{\mathbb{X}} f(\mathbf{x})\,\exp\!\Bigl(-2\pi i\sum_{j=1}^
{D}\tfrac{n_j x_j}{X_j}\Bigr)\,d\mathbf{x}\;}
\]

and the formal Fourier series is

\[
\boxed{\;f(\mathbf{x}) \;\sim\; \sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}} \hat f(\mathb
f{n})\,e_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x})
\;=\; \sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}} \hat f(\mathbf{n})\,\exp\!\Bigl(2\pi i\
sum_{j=1}^{D}\tfrac{n_j x_j}{X_j}\Bigr).\;}
\]

Equivalently, in real (sine/cosine) form per coordinate one expands each \( x_j
\)-dependence in the standard \( 1 \)-D Fourier series on \( [0,X_j] \); the pro
duct structure of the basis makes the multidimensional coefficients the iterated
 partial integrals above.

### Rectangular partial sums

For a cutoff vector \( \mathbf{N}=(N_1,\ldots,N_D) \) with \( N_j\ge 0 \), defin
e the rectangular partial sum

\[
S_{\mathbf{N}}f(\mathbf{x}) \;=\; \sum_{|n_1|\le N_1}\cdots\sum_{|n_D|\le N_D} \
hat f(\mathbf{n})\,e_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x}).
\]

Because the basis is a tensor product of the one-dimensional bases, \( S_{\mathb
f{N}}f \) is the iterated application of the one-dimensional partial-sum operato
rs:

\[
S_{\mathbf{N}}f \;=\; S_{N_1}^{(1)}\circ S_{N_2}^{(2)}\circ\cdots\circ S_{N_D}^{
(D)}\, f,
\]

where \( S_{N_j}^{(j)} \) acts only on the \( x_j \)-variable. This factorizatio
n is the key structural fact that lets us lift one-dimensional convergence theor
ems to the box.

### Convergence conditions

We state the standard results, all obtainable by lifting the 1-D theory through
the factorization above.

**(A) \( L^{2} \) convergence (always).** If \( f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{X}) \), then
\( \{e_{\mathbf{n}}\}_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}} \) is a *complete* orthonorm
al system (after normalization by \( 1/\sqrt{V} \)), so

\[
\|f - S_{\mathbf{N}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{X})} \xrightarrow[\min_j N_j\to\infty]{}
0,
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}}|\hat f(\mathbf{n})|^{2} \;=\; \frac{1}{V}\|f\
|_{L^{2}}^{2}\quad\text{(Parseval)}.
\]

Completeness follows from density of continuous functions on \( \mathbb{X} \) in
 \( L^{2} \) and the fact that tensor products of the 1-D complete systems are c
omplete on the product domain.

**(B) Pointwise convergence at a point of continuity.** If \( f \) is of bounded
 variation on \( \mathbb{X} \) in the sense of Hardy–Krause (equivalently, each
variable-section is of bounded variation uniformly), and \( f \) is continuous a
t \( \mathbf{x}^{0}\in\mathbb{X} \) (with the natural periodic extension across
the boundary), then

\[
\lim_{\min_j N_j\to\infty} S_{\mathbf{N}}f(\mathbf{x}^{0}) \;=\; f(\mathbf{x}^{0
}).
\]

At a jump discontinuity the rectangular sums converge to the average of the one-
sided limits along each coordinate direction, i.e. the multidimensional analogue
 of the Dirichlet–Jordan theorem:

\[
\lim_{\min_j N_j\to\infty} S_{\mathbf{N}}f(\mathbf{x}^{0}) \;=\; \frac{1}{2^{D}}
\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{+,-\}^{D}} f(\mathbf{x}^{0}_{\varepsilon}),
\]

where \( \mathbf{x}^{0}_{\varepsilon} \) denotes the limit with the \( j \)-th c
oordinate approached from the \( \varepsilon_j \) side.

*Sketch of the lift.* The 1-D Dirichlet kernel satisfies \( S_{N}^{(j)}g(x_j)\to
 \tfrac12(g(x_j^-)+g(x_j^+)) \) for BV \( g \). Iterating over \( j \) via the f
actorization \( S_{\mathbf{N}}=\prod_j S_{N_j}^{(j)} \) and using that a BV sect
ion of a Hardy–Krause BV function is again BV, the rectangular limit equals the
average over all \( 2^{D} \) corner limits.

**(C) Uniform convergence (sufficient conditions).** If \( f \) is continuous on
 \( \mathbb{X} \), periodically extended to a continuous function on \( \mathbb{
T}^{D}_{\mathbf{X}}:=\prod_j \mathbb{R}/X_j\mathbb{Z} \), and the coefficients a
re absolutely summable,

\[
\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}}|\hat f(\mathbf{n})| \;<\;\infty,
\]

then \( S_{\mathbf{N}}f\to f \) uniformly on \( \mathbb{X} \). A concrete suffic
ient condition guaranteeing absolute summability is: \( f \) has continuous mixe
d partial derivatives up to order exceeding \( D \) in total, e.g. \( \partial_{
1}\partial_{2}\cdots\partial_{D} f \) exists and is continuous and the lower-ord
er pure derivatives are continuous; repeated integration by parts in each variab
le then yields \( |\hat f(\mathbf{n})|=O\bigl(\prod_j(1+|n_j|)^{-1}\bigr) \), wh
ich is summable on \( \mathbb{Z}^{D} \).

More generally, if for some \( k\ge 1 \), \( f \) is \( k \) times continuously
differentiable with the periodic extension matching at boundaries, integration b
y parts gives \( \hat f(\mathbf{n})=O(|\mathbf{n}|^{-k}) \); summability of coef
ficients (hence uniform convergence) follows whenever \( k>D \).

**(D) Smoothness via Sobolev (Hilbert-space) criterion.** If \( f\in H^{s}(\math
bb{T}^{D}_{\mathbf{X}}) \) with \( s>D/2 \), then \( \sum_{\mathbf{n}}|\hat f(\m
athbf{n})|<\infty \) by Cauchy–Schwarz and \( \sum(1+|\mathbf{n}|^{2})^{-s}<\inf
ty \) for \( s>D/2 \), so \( S_{\mathbf{N}}f\to f \) uniformly and \( f \) equal
s its Fourier series everywhere.

### Summary

The multidimensional Fourier series of \( f \) on the box \( \mathbb{X}=\prod_{j
}[0,X_j] \) is

\[
f(\mathbf{x}) \;\sim\; \sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}} \hat f(\mathbf{n})\,\e
xp\!\Bigl(2\pi i\sum_{j=1}^{D}\frac{n_j x_j}{X_j}\Bigr),
\qquad
\hat f(\mathbf{n})=\frac{1}{\prod_j X_j}\int_{\mathbb{X}} f(\mathbf{x})\,\exp\!\
Bigl(-2\pi i\sum_{j=1}^{D}\frac{n_j x_j}{X_j}\Bigr)\,d\mathbf{x}.
\]

It converges to \( f \):

- in \( L^{2}(\mathbb{X}) \) for every \( f\in L^{2} \) (rectangular partial sum
s, \( \min_j N_j\to\infty \));
- pointwise at points of continuity (and to the \( 2^{D} \)-corner average at ju
mps) when \( f \) is of Hardy–Krause bounded variation;
- uniformly when \( f \) is continuous with periodic boundary matching and \( \s
um_{\mathbf{n}}|\hat f(\mathbf{n})|<\infty \), which is guaranteed, for example,
 by \( f\in H^{s} \) with \( s>D/2 \), or by \( f \) having continuous mixed par
tial derivatives of total order \( >D \) with periodic matching.
