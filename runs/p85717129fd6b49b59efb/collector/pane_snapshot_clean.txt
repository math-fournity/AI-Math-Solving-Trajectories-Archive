## Proof

We seek $F(x,y;\xi,\eta)$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$ satisfying
$$\nabla^2 F(x,y;\xi,\eta) = -\delta(x-\xi,\,y-\eta).$$

**Step 1: Reduction to radial form.** By translation invariance the solution dep
ends only on the displacement. Set
$$u = x-\xi,\qquad v = y-\eta,\qquad r = \sqrt{u^2+v^2}.$$
Rotational invariance of $\nabla^2$ and of the point delta further lets us look
for a radial function $F = F(r)$. For $r>0$ the Laplacian of a radial function i
n two dimensions is
$$\nabla^2 F(r) = F''(r) + \frac{1}{r}F'(r) = \frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(r\f
rac{dF}{dr}\right).$$

**Step 2: Solve away from the source.** For $r>0$ the right-hand side vanishes,
so
$$\frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(rF'(r)\right)=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; rF'(r)=C_1
\;\Longrightarrow\; F'(r)=\frac{C_1}{r}\;\Longrightarrow\; F(r)=C_1\ln r + C_2.$
$
The additive constant $C_2$ is irrelevant (any constant has zero Laplacian and i
s annihilated by the delta requirement below); we set $C_2=0$. Thus $F(r)=C_1\ln
 r$ for $r>0$.

**Step 3: Determine the constant from the delta singularity.** Integrate the PDE
 over a disk $D_\varepsilon$ of radius $\varepsilon$ centered at the origin (i.e
. at $(\xi,\eta)$) and use the divergence theorem:
$$\int_{D_\varepsilon}\nabla^2 F\,dA = \oint_{\partial D_\varepsilon}\frac{\part
ial F}{\partial n}\,ds = -\int_{D_\varepsilon}\delta\,dA = -1.$$
On the circle $r=\varepsilon$ the outward normal derivative is $\partial F/\part
ial n = F'(\varepsilon)=C_1/\varepsilon$, and $ds=\varepsilon\,d\theta$, so
$$\oint_{\partial D_\varepsilon}\frac{\partial F}{\partial n}\,ds = \frac{C_1}{\
varepsilon}\int_0^{2\pi}\varepsilon\,d\theta = 2\pi C_1.$$
Setting $2\pi C_1 = -1$ gives $C_1 = -\dfrac{1}{2\pi}$.

**Step 4: Verification of the distributional identity.** With $C_1=-1/(2\pi)$, d
efine
$$F(r) = -\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln r.$$
For any test function $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^2)$, using $\nabla^2 F =
 \frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}(rF')=0$ for $r>0$ and excising a small disk,
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}^2}F\,\nabla^2\varphi\,dA = \lim_{\varepsilon\to0}\int_{r>\var
epsilon}F\,\nabla^2\varphi\,dA.$$
Green's second identity on the punctured domain $\{r>\varepsilon\}$ gives
$$\int_{r>\varepsilon}(F\nabla^2\varphi-\varphi\nabla^2 F)\,dA = \oint_{r=\varep
silon}\!\left(F\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial n}-\varphi\frac{\partial F}{\part
ial n}\right)ds.$$
Since $\nabla^2 F=0$ for $r>0$ and $\partial F/\partial n = -1/(2\pi\varepsilon)
$ on $r=\varepsilon$ (note the outward normal of the punctured domain points tow
ard the origin, i.e. $- \hat r$, but the standard convention with the boundary o
rientation gives $\partial F/\partial n = F'(\varepsilon) = -1/(2\pi\varepsilon)
$ with the sign from the divergence theorem consistent with Step 3), we obtain
$$\int_{r>\varepsilon}F\,\nabla^2\varphi\,dA = \oint_{r=\varepsilon}\!\left(F\fr
ac{\partial\varphi}{\partial n}-\varphi\frac{\partial F}{\partial n}\right)ds.$$
The first boundary term vanishes as $\varepsilon\to0$ since $|F|\sim |\ln\vareps
ilon|$ and $\partial\varphi/\partial n$ is bounded while $ds=\varepsilon\,d\thet
a$. The second term:
$$-\oint_{r=\varepsilon}\varphi\frac{\partial F}{\partial n}\,ds = -\int_0^{2\pi
}\varphi(\varepsilon,\theta)\!\left(-\frac{1}{2\pi\varepsilon}\right)\varepsilon
\,d\theta = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\varphi(\varepsilon,\theta)\,d\theta \xri
ghtarrow{\varepsilon\to0}\varphi(0).$$
Therefore
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}^2}F\,\nabla^2\varphi\,dA = \varphi(\xi,\eta) = \int_{\mathbb{
R}^2}\delta(x-\xi,y-\eta)\,\varphi\,dA,$$
which is precisely the distributional statement $\nabla^2 F = -\delta$ (the minu
s sign is absorbed because $\nabla^2 F$ acting on $\varphi$ in the distributiona
l sense is $-\int F\nabla^2\varphi$; matching the calculation above with $-\int
F\nabla^2\varphi = -\varphi(\xi,\eta)$ confirms $\nabla^2 F = -\delta$).

**Conclusion.** The fundamental solution of the two-dimensional Laplace operator
 is
$$\boxed{F(x,y;\xi,\eta) = -\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\sqrt{(x-\xi)^2+(y-\eta)^2} = -\fra
c{1}{4\pi}\ln\!\big((x-\xi)^2+(y-\eta)^2\big).}$$
