## Proof

**Notation.** Let $|S_n|$ denote the $(n-1)$-dimensional surface area of the uni
t sphere $S^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^n$. The standard closed form is

$$
|S_n| \;=\; \frac{2\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(n/2)}.
$$

(Indeed the volume of the unit ball is $V_n=\pi^{n/2}/\Gamma(n/2+1)$, and $|S_n|
=nV_n=\frac{2\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(n/2)}$.)

**Goal.** Show $|S_n|=O(1/\sqrt n)$, i.e. $\exists\,C,n_0$ with $|S_n|\le C/\sqr
t n$ for all $n\ge n_0$. We prove the stronger statement $|S_n|=o(1/\sqrt n)$.

**Asymptotics via Stirling.** Stirling's formula for the Gamma function gives, a
s $x\to\infty$,

$$
\Gamma(x)\sim \sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{x}}\left(\frac{x}{e}\right)^x .
$$

Setting $x=n/2$,

$$
\Gamma(n/2)\sim \sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{n/2}}\left(\frac{n}{2e}\right)^{n/2}
= \sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{n}}\left(\frac{n}{2e}\right)^{n/2}.
$$

Substituting into the surface-area formula,

$$
|S_n|\sim \frac{2\pi^{n/2}}{\sqrt{4\pi/n}\,\left(\frac{n}{2e}\right)^{n/2}}
= \sqrt{\frac{n}{\pi}}\left(\frac{2\pi e}{n}\right)^{n/2}.
$$

**The ratio $|S_n|\sqrt n$.** We examine

$$
|S_n|\sqrt n \;\sim\; \frac{n}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\frac{2\pi e}{n}\right)^{n/2}.
$$

Since $2\pi e\approx 17.0795$, for every $n\ge 18$ we have $0<\rho_n:=\frac{2\pi
 e}{n}<1$, and $\rho_n\to 0$. The factor $\rho_n^{\,n/2}$ decays **super-exponen
tially**: taking logarithms,

$$
\log\!\left(\rho_n^{\,n/2}\right)=\frac{n}{2}\log\!\left(\frac{2\pi e}{n}\right)
\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}-\infty
$$

at rate $\Theta(n\log n)$, which dominates the polynomial prefactor $\log(n/\sqr
t\pi)=\Theta(\log n)$. Hence

$$
\log\!\left(|S_n|\sqrt n\right)=\log n-\tfrac12\log\pi+\tfrac{n}{2}\log\!\left(\
frac{2\pi e}{n}\right)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}-\infty,
$$

so

$$
|S_n|\sqrt n \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{} 0.
$$

**Conclusion.** Since $|S_n|\sqrt n\to 0$, in particular $|S_n|\sqrt n$ is bound
ed: there exist $C>0$ and $n_0$ with $|S_n|\sqrt n\le C$ for all $n\ge n_0$, i.e
.

$$
|S_n|\le \frac{C}{\sqrt n}\qquad(n\ge n_0).
$$

In fact $|S_n|=o(1/\sqrt n)$, which is strictly stronger. Therefore the statemen
t

$$
\boxed{|S_n|=O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt n}\right)}
$$

is **true**. $\blacksquare$
