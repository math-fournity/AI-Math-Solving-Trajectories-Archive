**Claim.** There exists a monotone (increasing) function $f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}$
whose set of discontinuity points has cardinality $\aleph_0$.

**Construction.** Let $\{q_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ be an enumeration of $\mathbb{Q}\
cap(0,1)$. Define the Heaviside function $H:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ by $H(t)=1$
 if $t>0$ and $H(t)=0$ if $t\le 0$. Set
$$
f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^n}\,H(x-q_n),\qquad x\in[0,1].
$$

**The series is well defined and $f$ is bounded.** Since $0\le H(x-q_n)\le 1$ an
d $\sum_{n}2^{-n}=1$, the series converges absolutely and uniformly by the Weier
strass $M$-test, with $0\le f(x)\le 1$.

**$f$ is monotone increasing.** Each summand $x\mapsto 2^{-n}H(x-q_n)$ is non-de
creasing, and a uniformly convergent series of non-decreasing functions is non-d
ecreasing. Hence $f$ is non-decreasing on $[0,1]$.

**$f$ is discontinuous at every $q_k\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)$.** Fix $k$. For any
$x<q_k$ we have $H(x-q_k)=0$, while for any $x>q_k$ we have $H(x-q_k)=1$. Theref

\bigl(0-0\bigr)=0\quad(x<q_k),
$$

$$

$$

>0$. Thus $f$ has a jump of size at least $2^{-k}$ at $q_k$, so $f$ is discontin
