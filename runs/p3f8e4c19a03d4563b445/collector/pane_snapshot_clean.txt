## Solution

We are given:
- $V_n(1) = \dfrac{\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)}$
- $S_n(1) = \dfrac{n\,\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)} = n\,V_n(1)
$

We seek integers $v$ and $s$ maximizing $V_v(1)$ and $S_s(1)$ respectively, then
 compute $v - s$.

### Finding $v$ (maximizing $V_n$)

Using $\Gamma(k+\tfrac12) = \tfrac{(2k)!}{4^k\,k!}\sqrt{\pi}$ and $\Gamma(m) = (
m-1)!$ for integers, I compute $V_n(1)$ for successive $n$:

| $n$ | $V_n(1)$ |
|-----|----------|
| 1 | $2$ |
| 2 | $\pi \approx 3.1416$ |
| 3 | $\frac{4\pi}{3} \approx 4.1888$ |
| 4 | $\frac{\pi^2}{2} \approx 4.9348$ |
| 5 | $\frac{8\pi^2}{15} \approx 5.2638$ |
| 6 | $\frac{\pi^3}{6} \approx 5.1677$ |
| 7 | $\frac{16\pi^3}{105} \approx 4.7248$ |

The volume increases through $n=5$ then decreases: $V_4 < V_5 > V_6$.

**Verification of the peak:** $V_5 = \dfrac{\pi^2}{1.875} = \dfrac{8\pi^2}{15} \
approx 5.2638$, and $V_6 = \dfrac{\pi^3}{6} \approx 5.1677$. Since $5.2638 > 5.1
677$ and $V_5 > V_4 \approx 4.9348$, the maximum is at $\boxed{v = 5}$.

### Finding $s$ (maximizing $S_n = n\,V_n$)

| $n$ | $S_n(1) = n\,V_n(1)$ |
|-----|----------------------|
| 5 | $5 \times 5.2638 \approx 26.319$ |
| 6 | $6 \times 5.1677 \approx 31.006$ |
| 7 | $7 \times 4.7248 \approx 33.073$ |
| 8 | $8 \times \frac{\pi^4}{24} = \frac{\pi^4}{3} \approx 32.470$ |
| 9 | $9 \times 3.2985 \approx 29.687$ |

The surface area increases through $n=7$ then decreases: $S_6 < S_7 > S_8$.

**Verification:** $S_7 = 7 \cdot \dfrac{16\pi^3}{105} = \dfrac{16\pi^3}{15} \app
rox 33.073$ and $S_8 = \dfrac{\pi^4}{3} \approx 32.470$. Since $33.073 > 32.470$
 and $S_7 > S_6 \approx 31.006$, the maximum is at $\boxed{s = 7}$.

### Result

$$v - s = 5 - 7 = -2$$
