Looking at this problem, I need to find the largest $c$ such that $\{n\sqrt{2}\}
 \geq \frac{c}{n}$ for all natural numbers $n$, i.e., $c = \inf_{n \geq 1} n\{n\
sqrt{2}\}$.

## Solution

**Setup.** For any natural number $n$, let $m = [n\sqrt{2}]$. Then $\{n\sqrt{2}\
} = n\sqrt{2} - m > 0$ (since $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational), and $m < n\sqrt{2} < m+
1$, so $m^2 < 2n^2 < (m+1)^2$. Since $m^2$ and $2n^2$ are integers with $m^2 < 2
n^2$, we have:
$$d := 2n^2 - m^2 \geq 1.$$

**Lower bound.** We compute:
$$n\{n\sqrt{2}\} = n(n\sqrt{2} - m) = \frac{n(2n^2 - m^2)}{n\sqrt{2} + m} = \fra
c{nd}{n\sqrt{2} + m}.$$

Since $m < n\sqrt{2}$, we have $n\sqrt{2} + m < 2n\sqrt{2}$, so:
$$n\{n\sqrt{2}\} > \frac{nd}{2n\sqrt{2}} = \frac{d}{2\sqrt{2}} \geq \frac{1}{2\s
qrt{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}.$$

Therefore $\{n\sqrt{2}\} > \frac{\sqrt{2}}{4n}$ for every natural number $n$, wh
ich means $c \geq \frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}$.

**Tightness — the extremal sequence.** Consider the negative Pell equation $m^2
- 2n^2 = -1$, i.e., $d = 1$. Its positive solutions $(m_k, n_k)$ are given by:
$$m_k + n_k\sqrt{2} = (1+\sqrt{2})^{2k+1}, \quad k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$$

yielding $(m_k, n_k) = (1,1),\, (7,5),\, (41,29),\, (239,169),\, \ldots$ with re
currence $n_{k+1} = 6n_k - n_{k-1}$.

For these solutions, $m_k/n_k < \sqrt{2}$ (since $m_k^2 = 2n_k^2 - 1 < 2n_k^2$),
 so $[n_k\sqrt{2}] = m_k$ and:
$$n_k\{n_k\sqrt{2}\} = \frac{n_k}{n_k\sqrt{2} + m_k} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} + m_k/n
_k}.$$

As $k \to \infty$, $m_k/n_k \to \sqrt{2}$ from below (convergents of $\sqrt{2}$)
, so:
$$n_k\{n_k\sqrt{2}\} \to \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{2}} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}} = \
frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}.$$

The sequence $n_k\{n_k\sqrt{2}\}$ is **strictly decreasing** (since $m_k/n_k$ is
 strictly increasing toward $\sqrt{2}$), with values:
- $n=1$: $\quad \sqrt{2}-1 \approx 0.41421$
- $n=5$: $\quad 25\sqrt{2}-35 \approx 0.35534$
- $n=29$: $\quad 841\sqrt{2}-1189 \approx 0.35360$
- $n=169$: $\quad 28561\sqrt{2}-40391 \approx 0.35357$

all approaching $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4} \approx 0.35355$.

**No other $n$ can do better.** For any $n$ with $d = 2n^2 - m^2 \geq 2$:
$$n\{n\sqrt{2}\} > \frac{d}{2\sqrt{2}} \geq \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.707,$$
which is far larger than the convergent values. So only $d=1$ (Pell solutions) m
atter, and among those the values strictly decrease to $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}$.

**Conclusion.** The largest $c$ is:
$$\boxed{c = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}}.$$

The inequality $\{n\sqrt{2}\} \geq \frac{c}{n}$ holds for all natural $n$ (with
strict inequality), and $c$ is optimal since $n\{n\sqrt{2}\}$ can be made arbitr
arily close to $c$ by taking $n$ from the sequence $1, 5, 29, 169, 985, \ldots$
(denominators of even-indexed convergents of $\sqrt{2}$, satisfying $m^2 - 2n^2
= -1$).

**Equality is never achieved** for any natural number $n$ — the infimum $\frac{\
sqrt{2}}{4}$ is a strict lower bound, approached but not attained. The natural n
umbers that approach equality are $n = 1, 5, 29, 169, \ldots$, generated by $n_0
=1, n_1=5, n_{k+1}=6n_k - n_{k-1}$.
