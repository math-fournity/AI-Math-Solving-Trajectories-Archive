## Proof

Let $\alpha_i = dx^{2i-1} \wedge dx^{2i}$ for $i = 1, \dots, n$, so that
$$\omega = \alpha_1 + \alpha_2 + \cdots + \alpha_n.$$

We compute $\omega^n = \underbrace{\omega \wedge \cdots \wedge \omega}_{n \text{
 factors}}$ by expanding using multilinearity of the wedge product:
$$\omega^n = \sum_{(i_1, \dots, i_n) \in \{1,\dots,n\}^n} \alpha_{i_1} \wedge \a
lpha_{i_2} \wedge \cdots \wedge \alpha_{i_n}.$$

**Each $\alpha_i$ is a 2-form (even degree), so the $\alpha_i$ graded-commute wi
th sign $+1$:**
$$\alpha_i \wedge \alpha_j = \alpha_j \wedge \alpha_i \quad \text{for all } i, j
.$$

**Each $\alpha_i$ squares to zero.** Indeed,
$$\alpha_i \wedge \alpha_i = dx^{2i-1} \wedge dx^{2i} \wedge dx^{2i-1} \wedge dx
^{2i}.$$
Moving the third factor $dx^{2i-1}$ left past $dx^{2i}$ gives a sign $-1$, yield
ing $-dx^{2i-1}\wedge dx^{2i-1}\wedge dx^{2i}\wedge dx^{2i} = 0$. Hence $\alpha_
i \wedge \alpha_i = 0$.

Therefore a summand $\alpha_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{i_n}$ vanishes unless
 $i_1, \dots, i_n$ are **all distinct**, i.e. unless $(i_1,\dots,i_n)$ is a perm
utation of $\{1,\dots,n\}$.

For any such permutation $\sigma$, since the $\alpha_i$ commute (sign $+1$),
$$\alpha_{\sigma(1)} \wedge \alpha_{\sigma(2)} \wedge \cdots \wedge \alpha_{\sig
ma(n)} = \alpha_1 \wedge \alpha_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge \alpha_n.$$

Each $\alpha_i = dx^{2i-1}\wedge dx^{2i}$ already has its two factors in increas
ing order, and the blocks $(dx^{2i-1}, dx^{2i})$ appear in order $i=1,\dots,n$,
so
$$\alpha_1 \wedge \alpha_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge \alpha_n = dx^1 \wedge dx^2 \wed
ge dx^3 \wedge dx^4 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^{2n-1} \wedge dx^{2n}.$$

There are exactly $n!$ permutations of $\{1,\dots,n\}$, and every one contribute
s the same term with the same sign $+1$. Thus
$$\omega^n = n!\, dx^1 \wedge dx^2 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^{2n-1} \wedge dx^{2n}
.$$

**The coefficient of $dx^1 \wedge dx^2 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^{2n-1} \wedge dx^
{2n}$ is $\boxed{n!}$.**
