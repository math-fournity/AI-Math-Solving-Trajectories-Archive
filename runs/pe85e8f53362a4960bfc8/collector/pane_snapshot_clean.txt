## Proof

**Setting.** Let $X$ be a locally compact Hausdorff space and $\Lambda$ a positi
ve linear functional on $C_c(X)$. By the Riesz Representation Theorem (Rudin, RC
A, Theorem 2.14), there exists a $\sigma$-algebra $\mathfrak{M}$ containing all
Borel sets $\mathfrak{B}$, and a positive measure $\mu$ on $\mathfrak{M}$ such t
hat $\Lambda f = \int_X f\,d\mu$ for all $f \in C_c(X)$, with the stated regular
ity and completeness properties. Let $\mu|_{\mathfrak{B}}$ denote the restrictio
n of $\mu$ to the Borel $\sigma$-algebra $\mathfrak{B}$.

**Claim.** For every Borel measurable function $f : X \to \overline{\mathbb{R}}$
 (or $X \to \mathbb{C}$),

$$\int_X f\,d\mu \;=\; \int_X f\,d(\mu|_{\mathfrak{B}}),$$

in the sense that one side exists (as a finite number or $\pm\infty$) if and onl
y if the other does, and they are equal.

### Step 1. Non-negative Borel functions

Let $f \geq 0$ be $\mathfrak{B}$-measurable. Since $\mathfrak{B} \subset \mathfr
ak{M}$, $f$ is also $\mathfrak{M}$-measurable, so both integrals are defined.

Construct the standard approximating sequence of $\mathfrak{B}$-measurable simpl
e functions:

$$s_n(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{n2^n - 1} \frac{k}{2^n}\,\chi_{E_{n,k}}(x) + n\,\chi_{F_n
}(x),$$

where $E_{n,k} = f^{-1}\!\bigl([\tfrac{k}{2^n},\tfrac{k+1}{2^n})\bigr) \in \math
frak{B}$ and $F_n = f^{-1}([n,+\infty]) \in \mathfrak{B}$. Then:

- Each $s_n$ is a **$\mathfrak{B}$-measurable** simple function (hence also $\ma
thfrak{M}$-measurable).
- $0 \leq s_1 \leq s_2 \leq \cdots \leq f$ and $s_n \to f$ pointwise.

**In $(X, \mathfrak{M}, \mu)$:** Since $\{s_n\}$ is an increasing sequence of no
n-negative $\mathfrak{M}$-measurable functions converging to $f$, the Monotone C
onvergence Theorem gives

$$\int_X f\,d\mu = \lim_{n\to\infty} \int_X s_n\,d\mu. \tag{1}$$

**In $(X, \mathfrak{B}, \mu|_{\mathfrak{B}})$:** The same sequence $\{s_n\}$ is
an increasing sequence of non-negative $\mathfrak{B}$-measurable functions conve
rging to $f$, so MCT gives

$$\int_X f\,d(\mu|_{\mathfrak{B}}) = \lim_{n\to\infty} \int_X s_n\,d(\mu|_{\math
frak{B}}). \tag{2}$$

**Key equality:** Each $s_n = \sum_j a_j\,\chi_{A_j}$ with $A_j \in \mathfrak{B}
$. Therefore

$$\int_X s_n\,d\mu = \sum_j a_j\,\mu(A_j) = \sum_j a_j\,\mu|_{\mathfrak{B}}(A_j)
 = \int_X s_n\,d(\mu|_{\mathfrak{B}}),$$

since $\mu$ and $\mu|_{\mathfrak{B}}$ agree on Borel sets by definition of restr
iction. Combining with (1) and (2):

$$\boxed{\int_X f\,d\mu = \int_X f\,d(\mu|_{\mathfrak{B}})} \tag{$\ast$}$$

for every non-negative Borel measurable $f$.

### Step 2. General (signed/complex) Borel functions

For a real-valued Borel function $f$, write $f = f^+ - f^-$ with $f^+, f^- \geq
0$ both $\mathfrak{B}$-measurable. Applying $(\ast)$ to each:

$$\int f\,d\mu = \int f^+\,d\mu - \int f^-\,d\mu = \int f^+\,d(\mu|_{\mathfrak{B
}}) - \int f^-\,d(\mu|_{\mathfrak{B}}) = \int f\,d(\mu|_{\mathfrak{B}}),$$

whenever the integrals are defined (i.e., at least one of $\int f^+\,d\mu$, $\in
t f^-\,d\mu$ is finite — and this condition is identical on both sides by $(\ast
)$).

For complex-valued Borel $f = u + iv$, apply the above to $u$ and $v$ separately
.

### Why the larger $\sigma$-algebra $\mathfrak{M}$ is irrelevant for Borel funct
ions

The integral of a measurable function $f$ is determined entirely by the measure
of sets in $\sigma(f) = \{f^{-1}(B) : B \in \mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{R})\}$, the $\s
igma$-algebra generated by $f$. When $f$ is Borel measurable, $\sigma(f) \subset
eq \mathfrak{B}$. The additional sets in $\mathfrak{M} \setminus \mathfrak{B}$ (
which arise from Rudin's construction via local measurability and completion) ne
ver enter the computation of $\int f\,d\mu$, because the approximating simple fu
nctions can always be chosen to use only Borel preimages. The Monotone Convergen
ce Theorem guarantees that this Borel approximation exhausts the integral — no $
\mathfrak{M}$-measurable simple function below $f$ can exceed the limit of the B
orel approximations.

---

**Conclusion.** Yes. For every Borel measurable function $f$, the integral with
respect to the original measure $\mu$ on $\mathfrak{M}$ equals the integral with
 respect to the restricted measure $\mu|_{\mathfrak{B}}$ on $\mathfrak{B}$. The
restriction to Borel sets loses no information about the integrals of Borel func
tions, because such integrals depend only on the values of the measure on Borel
sets.
