Looking at this problem, I need to understand what a "consistent 2-configuration
 of order $n$" is and prove the subset property.

**Interpretation.** A consistent 2-configuration of order $n$ on a finite set $A
$ is a collection $\mathcal{C}$ of 2-element subsets of $A$ such that every elem
ent of $A$ belongs to exactly $n$ members of $\mathcal{C}$, with no repeated 2-e
lement subset (consistency = simplicity). Equivalently, this is a simple $n$-reg
ular graph $G = (A, \mathcal{C})$. A "subset" of the configuration is a subcolle
ction $\mathcal{C}' \subseteq \mathcal{C}$.

---

**Proof.**

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a consistent 2-configuration of order 4 on a finite set $A$
. Then $G = (A, \mathcal{C})$ is a simple 4-regular graph. We must find a subcol
lection $\mathcal{C}' \subseteq \mathcal{C}$ in which every element of $A$ appea
rs in exactly 2 members — i.e., a 2-regular spanning subgraph (a 2-factor).

**Step 1: Eulerian circuits exist.** Since $G$ is 4-regular, every vertex has ev
en degree. Hence every connected component of $G$ is Eulerian: it admits a close
d trail using every edge exactly once.

**Step 2: Each component has an even number of edges.** Let $H$ be a connected c
omponent with $n_H$ vertices. Since $H$ is 4-regular, the handshaking lemma give
s $2m_H = 4n_H$, so $m_H = 2n_H$, which is even.

**Step 3: Alternating 2-coloring of the Eulerian circuit.** Fix a connected comp
onent $H$ and let $e_1, e_2, \dots, e_{m_H}$ be its edges in the order traversed
 by an Eulerian circuit (so $e_{m_H}$ and $e_1$ meet at the start vertex $v_0$).
 Assign edges at odd positions to $F_1$ and edges at even positions to $F_2$.

**Step 4: Each vertex gets exactly 2 edges of each color.** Consider any vertex
$v$ of $H$.

- If $v \neq v_0$: the Eulerian circuit visits $v$ exactly $\deg(v)/2 = 2$ times
 as an intermediate vertex. At each visit, the incoming edge and the outgoing ed
ge are consecutive in the circuit, so they receive different colors. Over 2 visi
ts, $v$ gets exactly 2 edges in $F_1$ and 2 in $F_2$.

- If $v = v_0$ (the start/end vertex): $v_0$ is visited once as an intermediate
vertex (contributing 1 edge to each color) and once as the start/end. At the sta
rt/end, the relevant edges are $e_{m_H}$ (entering $v_0$) and $e_1$ (leaving $v_
0$). Since $m_H$ is even, $e_{m_H}$ is at an even position (color $F_2$) and $e_
1$ is at an odd position (color $F_1$), so they receive different colors. Total:
 2 edges in $F_1$, 2 in $F_2$.

Thus every vertex of $H$ has exactly 2 incident edges in $F_1$ and 2 in $F_2$.

**Step 5: Global 2-factors.** Repeating Steps 3–4 for every connected component
and taking the union, $F_1$ and $F_2$ are each 2-regular spanning subgraphs of $
G$ — i.e., 2-factors.

**Step 6: Conclusion.** Set $\mathcal{C}' = F_1$. Then $\mathcal{C}' \subseteq \
mathcal{C}$, every element of $A$ belongs to exactly 2 members of $\mathcal{C}'$
, and $\mathcal{C}'$ is simple (being a subcollection of a simple graph's edge s
et). Hence $\mathcal{C}'$ is a consistent 2-configuration of order 2.

Therefore every consistent 2-configuration of order 4 on a finite set $A$ has a
subset that is a consistent 2-configuration of order 2. $\blacksquare$
