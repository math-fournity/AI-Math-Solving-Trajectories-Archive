## Proof

**Yes**, such a non-zero element exists.

Let $\{dx_1, dx_2, dx_3, dx_4\}$ be the standard basis of $(\mathbb{R}^4)^*$, an
d let $\omega = dx_1 \wedge dx_2 \wedge dx_3 \wedge dx_4$ be the volume form. Co
nsider

$$z = dx_1 \wedge dx_2 + dx_3 \wedge dx_4 \;\in\; A^2(\mathbb{R}^4).$$

This is clearly non-zero. We compute $z \wedge z$:

$$z \wedge z = (dx_1 \wedge dx_2 + dx_3 \wedge dx_4)\wedge(dx_1 \wedge dx_2 + dx
_3 \wedge dx_4).$$

Expanding:

$$= \underbrace{dx_1 \wedge dx_2 \wedge dx_1 \wedge dx_2}_{=\,0\text{ (repeated
}dx_1)} + dx_1 \wedge dx_2 \wedge dx_3 \wedge dx_4 + dx_3 \wedge dx_4 \wedge dx_
1 \wedge dx_2 + \underbrace{dx_3 \wedge dx_4 \wedge dx_3 \wedge dx_4}_{=\,0\text
{ (repeated }dx_3)}.$$

The first and last terms vanish since any wedge product with a repeated 1-form f
actor is zero. For the cross terms:

- $dx_1 \wedge dx_2 \wedge dx_3 \wedge dx_4 = \omega$.
- $dx_3 \wedge dx_4 \wedge dx_1 \wedge dx_2$: reordering to $dx_1 \wedge dx_2 \w
edge dx_3 \wedge dx_4$ requires moving $dx_3$ past $dx_1, dx_2$ (2 transposition
s) and $dx_4$ past $dx_1, dx_2$ (2 transpositions), for a total of 4 transpositi
ons, giving sign $(-1)^4 = +1$. Hence this also equals $\omega$.

Therefore

$$z \wedge z = \omega + \omega = 2\,\omega \neq 0.$$

Since $z \neq 0$ and $z \wedge z = 2\,\omega \neq 0$, the answer is **yes**: a n
on-zero element $z \in A^2(\mathbb{R}^4)$ with $z \wedge z \neq 0$ exists.
