## Proof

**Claim:** The answer is **No**. The characteristic function of a singular conti
nuous distribution on $[0,1]$ does not always tend to $0$ as $t \to \infty$. We
provide an explicit counterexample: the standard Cantor distribution.

### Construction of the Cantor Measure

Let $X = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} X_n / 3^n$, where $\{X_n\}$ are i.i.d. random varia
bles with $P(X_n = 0) = P(X_n = 2) = 1/2$. The distribution $\mu$ of $X$ is the
**Cantor measure** on $[0,1]$. Its CDF is the Cantor function (devil's staircase
), which is continuous. The measure $\mu$ is supported on the Cantor set, which
has Lebesgue measure zero. Thus $\mu$ is a singular continuous distribution sati
sfying all the hypotheses of the problem.

### Computing the Characteristic Function

By independence of the $X_n$:

$$\varphi(t) = \mathbb{E}[e^{itX}] = \prod_{n=1}^{\infty} \mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{i
tX_n/3^n}\right] = \prod_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2}\left(1 + e^{2it/3^n}\right)
= \prod_{n=1}^{\infty} e^{it/3^n}\cos\!\left(\frac{t}{3^n}\right).$$

The phase factors telescope: $\prod_{n=1}^{\infty} e^{it/3^n} = e^{it \sum_{n=1}
^{\infty} 1/3^n} = e^{it/2}$. Hence:

$$\varphi(t) = e^{it/2} \prod_{n=1}^{\infty} \cos\!\left(\frac{t}{3^n}\right).$$

### Showing $\varphi(t) \not\to 0$

Consider the sequence $t_k = 2\pi \cdot 3^k$ for $k = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$, which te
nds to infinity.

**Phase factor:** $e^{it_k/2} = e^{i\pi \cdot 3^k} = (-1)^{3^k} = -1$ (since $3^
k$ is odd).

**Product factor:** We split the product at $n = k$:

$$\prod_{n=1}^{\infty} \cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi \cdot 3^k}{3^n}\right) = \underbra
ce{\prod_{n=1}^{k} \cos\!\left(2\pi \cdot 3^{k-n}\right)}_{= \, 1} \cdot \prod_{
n=k+1}^{\infty} \cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{3^{n-k}}\right).$$

- For $n \leq k$: $3^{k-n}$ is a non-negative integer, so $\cos(2\pi \cdot 3^{k-
n}) = 1$.
- For $n > k$: setting $m = n - k$, the tail is $\prod_{m=1}^{\infty} \cos(2\pi/
3^m)$.

Therefore:

$$\varphi(t_k) = -\prod_{m=1}^{\infty} \cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{3^m}\right) =: -C
, \qquad \text{independent of } k.$$

### Verifying $C \neq 0$

We check that the infinite product $C = \prod_{m=1}^{\infty} \cos(2\pi/3^m)$ con
verges to a nonzero value.

- $m = 1$: $\cos(2\pi/3) = -1/2 \neq 0$.
- For $m \geq 2$: $\cos(2\pi/3^m) > 0$ (since $2\pi/3^m < \pi/2$ for $m \geq 2$)
, and $1 - \cos(2\pi/3^m) \leq \frac{1}{2}(2\pi/3^m)^2 = \frac{2\pi^2}{9^m}$.

Since $\sum_{m=2}^{\infty} \frac{2\pi^2}{9^m} < \infty$, the product $\prod_{m=2
}^{\infty} \cos(2\pi/3^m)$ converges to a **positive** value $P > 0$ (standard c
riterion: $\prod(1-a_m)$ with $\sum a_m < \infty$ and $0 \leq a_m < 1$ converges
 to a positive limit).

Thus $C = (-1/2) \cdot P$, and $|C| = P/2 > 0$.

### Conclusion

We have shown:

$$|\varphi(t_k)| = |C| = \frac{P}{2} > 0 \qquad \text{for all } k = 1, 2, 3, \ld
ots$$

while $t_k = 2\pi \cdot 3^k \to \infty$. Since $|\varphi(t)|$ is bounded away fr
om zero along a sequence tending to infinity, $\varphi(t)$ does **not** converge
 to $0$ as $t \to \infty$.

The Cantor distribution is a singular continuous distribution on $[0,1]$ with co
ntinuous CDF and growth set (the Cantor set) of Lebesgue measure zero, yet its c
haracteristic function fails to vanish at infinity. Therefore, the characteristi
c function of such a singular distribution does **not** always satisfy $\varphi(
t) \to 0$ as $t \to \infty$.

**Remark.** Measures whose Fourier transforms vanish at infinity are called *Raj
chman measures*. The Riemann–Lebesgue lemma guarantees that every absolutely con
tinuous measure is Rajchman, but the class of singular Rajchman measures is a pr
oper subclass of all singular measures. The Cantor measure is the classical exam
ple of a singular measure that is *not* Rajchman, as demonstrated above.
