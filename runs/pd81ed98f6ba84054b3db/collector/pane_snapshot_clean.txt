## Solution

Let
$$S = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{mnp(m+n+
p+1)}.$$

**Step 1: Integral representation.** For positive integers $m,n,p$,
$$\frac{1}{m+n+p+1} = \int_0^1 x^{m+n+p}\,dx.$$

**Step 2: Interchange sum and integral.** All summands are non-negative, so by T
onelli's theorem we may freely interchange:
$$S = \int_0^1 \left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^m}{m}\right)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\i
nfty}\frac{x^n}{n}\right)\left(\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^p}{p}\right)dx = \int_
0^1 \left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^k}{k}\right)^3 dx.$$

**Step 3: Recognize the logarithm.** For $0 \le x < 1$,
$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^k}{k} = -\ln(1-x).$$

Hence
$$S = \int_0^1 \bigl[-\ln(1-x)\bigr]^3\,dx.$$

**Step 4: Evaluate the integral.** Substitute $u = 1-x$, $du = -dx$:
$$S = \int_0^1 (-\ln u)^3\,du.$$

Now substitute $t = -\ln u$, so $u = e^{-t}$, $du = -e^{-t}\,dt$; as $u: 0\to 1$
, $t: \infty \to 0$:
$$S = \int_0^{\infty} t^3 e^{-t}\,dt = \Gamma(4) = 3! = 6.$$

The interchange is justified throughout by non-negativity (Tonelli), and the fin
al integral converges since $t^3 e^{-t}$ is integrable on $(0,\infty)$.

$$\boxed{S = 6}$$
