## Solution

**Yes, $f$ must be continuous.**

### Proof

Assume $f:X_1\to X_2$ satisfies: for every $E\subseteq X_1$, $f(E)$ is compact i
n $X_2$ iff $E$ is compact in $X_1$. We prove $f$ is continuous by contradiction
.

Suppose $f$ is **not** continuous at some point $a\in X_1$. Since we are in metr
ic spaces, continuity is equivalent to sequential continuity, so there exists a
sequence $x_n\to a$ with $f(x_n)\not\to f(a)$. Hence there is some $\varepsilon>
0$ and a subsequence (relabeled $x_n$) such that
$$d_2\big(f(x_n),\,f(a)\big)\ge \varepsilon \quad\text{for all } n.$$
In particular $x_n\neq a$ for all $n$ (otherwise $f(x_n)=f(a)$, contradicting $d
_2\ge\varepsilon$).

**Step 1 (use the forward direction).** The set
$$E=\{x_n:n\ge 1\}\cup\{a\}$$
is compact in $X_1$ (it is a convergent sequence together with its limit). By th
e forward implication, $f(E)$ is compact in $X_2$.

**Step 2 (extract a convergent subsequence of the images).** Since $f(E)$ is com
pact in the metric space $X_2$, it is sequentially compact. The sequence $\big(f
(x_n)\big)$ lies in $f(E)$, so it has a subsequence $\big(f(x_{n_k})\big)$ conve
rging to some limit $L\in f(E)$. Because $d_2(f(x_{n_k}),f(a))\ge\varepsilon$ fo
r all $k$, taking limits gives $d_2(L,f(a))\ge\varepsilon$, so $L\neq f(a)$. Sin
ce
$$f(E)=\{f(x_n):n\ge 1\}\cup\{f(a)\},$$
and $L\neq f(a)$, we must have $L=f(x_j)$ for some index $j\ge 1$.

**Step 3 (use the backward direction to reach a contradiction).** Consider the s
et
$$S=\{x_{n_k}:k\ge 1\}\cup\{x_j\}.$$
Its image is
$$f(S)=\{f(x_{n_k}):k\ge 1\}\cup\{f(x_j)\}=\{f(x_{n_k})\}\cup\{L\},$$
which is a convergent sequence together with its limit — hence compact in $X_2$.

By the **backward** implication ($f(S)$ compact $\Rightarrow$ $S$ compact), $S$
must be compact, and therefore closed in $X_1$ (compact subsets of a metric spac
e are closed).

But $x_{n_k}\to a$ (as a subsequence of $x_n\to a$), so $a$ is a limit point of
$S$. We showed $x_n\neq a$ for all $n$, hence $a\notin\{x_{n_k}\}$; also $x_j\ne
q a$ (since $j\ge 1$). Thus $a\notin S$, so $S$ does **not** contain all its lim
it points and is **not closed** — contradicting compactness.

This contradiction shows that $f$ must be continuous at every point of $X_1$.
