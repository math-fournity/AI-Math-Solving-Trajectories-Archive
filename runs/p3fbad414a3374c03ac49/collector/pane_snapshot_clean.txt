Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest $k$ such that $4k$ consecut
ive positive integers can be split into two subsets of equal size, equal sum, eq
ual sum of squares, and equal sum of cubes.

## Setup and Reduction

Let $A = \{a, a+1, \ldots, a+4k-1\}$. Assign signs $\varepsilon_i \in \{+1,-1\}$
 to $x_i = a+i$ ($i=0,\ldots,4k-1$), where $+1$ means "in subset $S$" and $-1$ m
eans "in subset $T$". The four equalities become:
$$\sum_{i=0}^{4k-1} \varepsilon_i (a+i)^j = 0, \quad j = 0,1,2,3.$$

Expanding $(a+i)^j$ and using $\sum \varepsilon_i = 0$ (equal sizes) inductively
:
- $j=0$: $\sum \varepsilon_i = 0$
- $j=1$: $a\sum\varepsilon_i + \sum \varepsilon_i i = 0 \Rightarrow \sum \vareps
ilon_i i = 0$
- $j=2$: $\sum \varepsilon_i i^2 = 0$
- $j=3$: $\sum \varepsilon_i i^3 = 0$

**The conditions are independent of $a$**, so WLOG we work with $\{0,1,\ldots,4k
-1\}$.

Define $f(x) = \sum_{i=0}^{4k-1} \varepsilon_i x^i$. The conditions $\sum \varep
silon_i i^j = 0$ for $j=0,1,2,3$ are equivalent to $f(1)=f'(1)=f''(1)=f'''(1)=0$
, i.e., $(x-1)^4 \mid f(x)$ over $\mathbb{Z}$.

## Necessary Condition (mod 2 analysis)

Over $\mathbb{F}_2$, every $\varepsilon_i \equiv 1$, so $f(x) \equiv 1 + x + x^2
 + \cdots + x^{4k-1} = \frac{x^{4k}+1}{x+1} \pmod{2}$.

Since $(x-1)^4 \mid f(x)$ over $\mathbb{Z}$, we need $(x+1)^4 \mid f(x)$ over $\
mathbb{F}_2$, hence $(x+1)^5 \mid (x^{4k}+1)$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$.

In $\mathbb{F}_2[x]$, writing $4k = 2^v \cdot m$ with $m$ odd: $x^{4k}+1 = (x^m+
1)^{2^v}$, and $(x+1)$ divides $x^m+1$ exactly once (since $m$ is odd). So the m
ultiplicity of $(x+1)$ in $x^{4k}+1$ is $2^v$ where $v = v_2(4k) = 2 + v_2(k)$.

We need $2^{2+v_2(k)} \geq 5$, so $v_2(k) \geq 1$, meaning **$k$ must be even**.

This rules out $k=1$ and $k=3$.

## $k=2$ fails (exhaustive check)

For $4k=8$: $\{0,\ldots,7\}$, split into two 4-element sets. Total sum $= 28$ (e
ach part $14$), total sum of squares $= 140$ (each part $70$), total sum of cube
s $= 784$ (each part $392$).

The 4-element subsets of $\{0,\ldots,7\}$ with sum $14$ **and** sum of squares $
70$:
- $\{0,3,5,6\}$: sum of cubes $= 0+27+125+216 = 368 \neq 392$.
- $\{1,2,4,7\}$: sum of cubes $= 1+8+64+343 = 416 \neq 392$.

No subset works, so **$k=2$ fails**.

## $k=4$ works (Thue-Morse construction)

For $4k = 16$: $\{0,1,\ldots,15\}$. Partition by the **Thue-Morse sequence** $t(
i) = $ (parity of binary digit sum of $i$):

- **Even parity** ($S$): $\{0,3,5,6,9,10,12,15\}$
- **Odd parity** ($T$): $\{1,2,4,7,8,11,13,14\}$

By the **Prouhet–Thue-Morse theorem**, partitioning $\{0,\ldots,2^n-1\}$ by Thue
-Morse gives equal sums of powers up to degree $n-1$. With $n=4$, equal sums hol
d up to degree $3$.

**Verification:**
- Sizes: $|S| = |T| = 8$ ✓
- Sums: $0+3+5+6+9+10+12+15 = 60$; $1+2+4+7+8+11+13+14 = 60$ ✓
- Sum of squares: $0+9+25+36+81+100+144+225 = 620$; $1+4+16+49+64+121+169+196 =
620$ ✓
- Sum of cubes: $0+27+125+216+729+1000+1728+3375 = 7200$; $1+8+64+343+512+1331+2
197+2744 = 7200$ ✓

Since the conditions are shift-invariant, the same partition pattern applied to
$\{a, a+1, \ldots, a+15\}$ for any $a \geq 1$ also works.

## Conclusion

- $k=1$: fails (odd, ruled out by mod 2; also direct check).
- $k=2$: fails (exhaustive enumeration).
- $k=3$: fails (odd, ruled out by mod 2).
- $k=4$: works (Thue-Morse construction, verified).

The smallest $k$ is $\boxed{4}$.
