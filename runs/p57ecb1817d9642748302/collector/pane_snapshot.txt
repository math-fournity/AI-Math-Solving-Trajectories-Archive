## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. For every bounded linear operator $T: X \to Y$ between Banach sp
aces, there exists a sequence $\{x_i\}\subset X$ with $\|T\|=\lim_{i\to\infty}\|
Tx_i\|_Y$.

**Proof.** Recall the definition of the operator norm:
$$\|T\|=\sup_{\|x\|_X\le 1}\|Tx\|_Y.$$
This is a supremum of a subset of $\mathbb{R}$ (namely $S:=\{\|Tx\|_Y:\|x\|_X\le
 1\}\subset[0,\infty)$), and $S$ is nonempty since $0\in S$.

A basic property of the supremum of a nonempty set $S\subset\mathbb{R}$ is that
it is the limit of a sequence drawn from $S$: there exist $s_n\in S$ with $s_n\t
o\sup S$. Concretely, for each $n\ge 1$, by the definition of supremum there exi
sts $s_n\in S$ with
$$\sup S - \frac{1}{n} \;<\; s_n \;\le\; \sup S.$$
(If $\sup S=\infty$ this would fail, but here $S$ is bounded above by $\|T\|<\in
fty$ since $T$ is bounded.) Applying this with $\sup S=\|T\|$: for each $n\ge 1$
 there exists $x_n\in X$ with $\|x_n\|_X\le 1$ such that
$$\|T\|-\frac{1}{n}<\|Tx_n\|_Y\le\|T\|.$$
By the squeeze theorem,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\|Tx_n\|_Y=\|T\|.$$

This covers all cases, including $T=0$ (then $\|T\|=0$ and any sequence, e.g. $x
_n=0$, gives $\|Tx_n\|=0\to 0$). The argument uses only that $T$ is bounded (so
$\|T\|<\infty$) and the elementary fact that a finite supremum in $\mathbb{R}$ i
s approached by a sequence from the set. The completeness of $X$ and $Y$ (the Ba
nach space hypothesis) is not even required; the result holds for bounded linear
 operators between any normed spaces.
