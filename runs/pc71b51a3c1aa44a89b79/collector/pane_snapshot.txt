**Answer: Yes, $H_n(M) = 0$ (it is trivial).**

## Proof

Let $M$ be an oriented, compact, connected $n$-dimensional smooth manifold with
(nonempty) boundary $\partial M$.

**Key fact.** For any compact, connected, oriented $n$-manifold $M$ with nonempt
y boundary $\partial M \neq \emptyset$, the top singular homology group satisfie
s
$$H_n(M) \cong 0.$$

We establish this via the long exact sequence of the pair $(M, \partial M)$ and
the orientation class.

---

### Step 1. The relative homology $H_n(M, \partial M)$

Since $M$ is oriented, compact, and connected, the orientation determines a fund
amental (relative) class
$$[M] \in H_n(M, \partial M),$$
and in fact
$$H_n(M, \partial M) \cong \mathbb{Z},$$
generated by $[M]$. This is the standard theorem: an oriented compact connected
$n$-manifold with boundary has its top relative homology isomorphic to $\mathbb{
Z}$, generated by the fundamental class.

### Step 2. The long exact sequence of the pair

The long exact sequence in singular homology for the pair $(M, \partial M)$ cont
ains the segment
$$H_n(\partial M) \xrightarrow{\;i_*\;} H_n(M) \xrightarrow{\;j_*\;} H_n(M, \par
tial M) \xrightarrow{\;\partial\;} H_{n-1}(\partial M) \xrightarrow{\;i_*\;} H_{
n-1}(M).$$

### Step 3. $H_n(\partial M) = 0$

The boundary $\partial M$ is an $(n-1)$-dimensional manifold (without boundary).
 Since $\partial M$ has dimension $n-1$, it has no $n$-simplices in any triangul
ation, and more generally its singular chain group in degree $n$ is generated by
 singular $n$-simplices mapping into an $(n-1)$-manifold. By dimension considera
tions (or the standard fact that $H_k = 0$ for $k > \dim$ for manifolds),
$$H_n(\partial M) = 0.$$

### Step 4. Exactness gives an injection

From the exact sequence and Step 3, the map $i_*: H_n(\partial M) \to H_n(M)$ ha
s zero domain, so
$$j_*: H_n(M) \hookrightarrow H_n(M, \partial M) \cong \mathbb{Z}$$
is **injective**. Thus $H_n(M)$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}$, i.e
. $H_n(M) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ or $H_n(M) \cong d\mathbb{Z}$ for some $d \geq 0$ (h
ence $0$ or $\mathbb{Z}$, up to isomorphism, since subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}$ are
 $d\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}$ for $d \geq 1$, or $0$).

### Step 5. The connecting map $\partial: H_n(M,\partial M) \to H_{n-1}(\partial
 M)$ is injective

We show $\ker \partial = 0$. The connecting homomorphism sends the fundamental c
lass $[M]$ to $[\partial M]$, the fundamental class of the boundary:
$$\partial([M]) = [\partial M] \in H_{n-1}(\partial M).$$

The boundary $\partial M$ of an oriented manifold is itself oriented (with the i
nduced/boundary orientation). When $\partial M \neq \emptyset$, the boundary is
a closed (compact, no boundary) oriented $(n-1)$-manifold. Its fundamental class
 $[\partial M] \in H_{n-1}(\partial M)$ is **nonzero** — this is the fundamental
 theorem that for any closed oriented connected $(n-1)$-manifold, $H_{n-1} \cong
 \mathbb{Z}$ generated by the fundamental class; and if $\partial M$ has multipl
e components, $[\partial M]$ is the sum of the fundamental classes of the compon
ents, which is nonzero.

Since $\partial$ is a homomorphism from $\mathbb{Z}$ (generated by $[M]$) and $\
partial([M]) = [\partial M] \neq 0$, the map $\partial$ is **injective**:
$$\ker \partial = 0.$$

### Step 6. Conclusion by exactness

By exactness at $H_n(M, \partial M)$:
$$\operatorname{im} j_* = \ker \partial = 0.$$

But $j_*$ is injective (Step 4), so $\operatorname{im} j_* \cong H_n(M)$. Theref
ore
$$H_n(M) \cong \operatorname{im} j_* = \ker \partial = 0.$$

---

### Intuition / sanity check

A manifold with nonempty boundary is homotopy equivalent to its interior, which
deformation retracts onto an $(n-1)$-dimensional spine (e.g., a closed disk $D^n
$ retracts to a point, a collar retracts onto $\partial M$). The top-dimensional
 cycle would have to be a closed $n$-cycle, but any $n$-chain in $M$ can "leak"
through the boundary, so no nontrivial $n$-cycle can be closed. Concretely, $H_n
(D^n) = 0$ for the disk, consistent with the theorem.

---

$$\boxed{H_n(M) = 0 \text{ for an oriented, compact, connected } n\text{-manifol
d with nonempty boundary.}}$$
