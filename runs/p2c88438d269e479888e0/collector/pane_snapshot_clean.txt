## Proof

**Step 1: Linear change of coordinates.**

Consider the invertible $\mathbb{C}$-linear map $\Phi : \mathbb{C}^2 \to \mathbb
{C}^2$ defined by
$$
\Phi(z_1, z_2) = (u, v) = (z_1 - z_2,\; z_2).
$$
Its inverse is $\Phi^{-1}(u, v) = (u + v,\; v)$, so $\Phi$ is a homeomorphism (i
n fact a linear isomorphism) of $\mathbb{C}^2$.

**Step 2: Image of the diagonal.**

A point $(z_1, z_2)$ lies on the diagonal $\Delta$ iff $z_1 = z_2$. Under $\Phi$
,
$$
z_1 = z_2 \iff z_1 - z_2 = 0 \iff u = 0.
$$
Hence $\Phi(\Delta) = \{(0, v) : v \in \mathbb{C}\}$, the $v$-axis, i.e., the co
mplex line $\{u = 0\}$.

**Step 3: Identify the complement.**

Since $\Phi$ is a homeomorphism carrying $\Delta$ to $\{u = 0\}$,
$$
\mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \Delta \;\cong\; \mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \{u = 0\} = \{(u,
 v) \in \mathbb{C}^2 : u \neq 0\}.
$$
This set splits as a product:
$$
\{(u, v) : u \neq 0\} = \mathbb{C}^* \times \mathbb{C}, \qquad \mathbb{C}^* = \m
athbb{C} \setminus \{0\}.
$$

**Step 4: Compute the fundamental group.**

The fundamental group of a product is the product of the fundamental groups:
$$
\pi_1(\mathbb{C}^* \times \mathbb{C}) \cong \pi_1(\mathbb{C}^*) \times \pi_1(\ma
thbb{C}).
$$

- $\mathbb{C}$ is contractible, so $\pi_1(\mathbb{C}) = 0$.
- $\mathbb{C}^*$ deformation retracts onto the unit circle $S^1$ via $u \mapsto
u/|u|$, so $\pi_1(\mathbb{C}^*) \cong \pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by
 a single loop winding once around the origin.

Therefore
$$
\pi_1(\mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \Delta) \cong \mathbb{Z} \times 0 \cong \mathbb{Z}.
$$

**Step 5: Geometric interpretation of the generator.**

The generator of $\pi_1(\mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \Delta) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is the
loop
$$
\gamma : [0,1] \to \mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \Delta, \qquad \gamma(t) = (e^{2\pi i
t}, 0),
$$
which winds once around the diagonal in the $u = z_1 - z_2$ coordinate while kee
ping $v = z_2 = 0$ fixed. Its winding number around the removed complex hyperpla
ne $\{u = 0\}$ is $1$, and every loop is classified up to homotopy by this integ
er winding number.

## Conclusion

$$
\boxed{\pi_1\!\left(\mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \Delta\right) \cong \mathbb{Z}.}
$$

The group is infinite cyclic, generated by a single loop encircling the diagonal
 $\Delta$ once.
