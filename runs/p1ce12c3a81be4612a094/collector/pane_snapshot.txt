## Proof

**Answer: Yes.**

**Key fact.** Every open connected subset $O \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is path-con
nected. Indeed, fix $x \in O$ and let $U$ be the set of points in $O$ that can b
e joined to $x$ by a continuous path in $O$. Since $O$ is open, every point of $
O$ has a ball around it contained in $O$, and any two points in a ball are joina
ble by a straight segment. Hence both $U$ and $O \setminus U$ are open in $O$. T
hey are disjoint, their union is $O$, and $U \neq \emptyset$ (it contains $x$).
By connectedness of $O$, we must have $O \setminus U = \emptyset$, so $U = O$. T
hus $O$ is path-connected.

**Construction of $K$.** Let $x, y \in O$ be distinct. By path-connectedness, th
ere exists a continuous map $\gamma : [0,1] \to O$ with $\gamma(0) = x$ and $\ga
mma(1) = y$. Set

$$K := \gamma([0,1]).$$

We verify the required properties:

1. **$K \subseteq O$**: Immediate, since $\gamma$ maps into $O$.
2. **$K$ is compact**: $[0,1]$ is compact and $\gamma$ is continuous, so the ima
ge $K$ is compact (in $\mathbb{R}^n$, hence also as a subset of $O$ with the sub
space topology).
3. **$x, y \in K$**: $\gamma(0) = x \in K$ and $\gamma(1) = y \in K$.
4. **$K$ is infinite**: $K$ is the continuous image of the connected set $[0,1]$
, so $K$ is connected. A connected subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$ (a Hausdorff, indeed
 metrizable, space) that contains two distinct points $x \neq y$ must be uncount
able. Concretely, $K$ contains a connected set with at least two points; any con
nected subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$ with two or more points is an interval-like cont
inuum and is uncountable. In particular $K$ is infinite.

   (A quick justification: if $K$ were finite, it would be totally disconnected
as a finite subset of a Hausdorff space, contradicting that $K$ is connected and
 contains two distinct points. Even more, $K$ cannot be countably infinite eithe
r: a countable connected subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$ with more than one point does
not exist, since $\mathbb{R}^n$ is a continuum in which every connected subset w
ith two points is uncountable. So $K$ is uncountable, hence infinite.)

Therefore $K$ is an infinite compact subset of $O$ containing both $x$ and $y$.
Since $x, y$ were an arbitrary distinct pair in $O$, the statement holds for eve
ry such pair.
