The problem statement does not specify the actual functional $S[y_1, y_2]$ or th
e explicit form of the coupled Euler–Lagrange equations. Without the concrete co
upling terms, no *specific* numerical linear transformation can be determined. H
owever, the decoupling problem has a complete general solution, which I give bel

## General setup

Consider a functional of two fields whose Lagrangian density has the standard qu
adratic-coupled form

$$
S[y_1,y_2]=\int L\,dx,\qquad
L=\tfrac12\big(\dot y_1^{\,2}+\dot y_2^{\,2}\big)-\tfrac12\big(a\,y_1^{\,2}+2b\,
y_1y_2+c\,y_2^{\,2}\big),
$$

with $a,b,c$ constants and $b\neq 0$ (otherwise the system is already decoupled)
. The Euler–Lagrange equations

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot y_i}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial y_i}
=0
$$

give the **coupled** system

$$
\boxed{\;\ddot y_1 + a\,y_1 + b\,y_2 = 0,\qquad \ddot y_2 + b\,y_1 + c\,y_2 = 0.
\;}
$$

In matrix form, with $\mathbf y=(y_1,y_2)^T$,

$$
\ddot{\mathbf y} + M\,\mathbf y = \mathbf 0,\qquad
M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\ b&c\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The coupling is entirely encoded in the symmetric matrix $M$; the kinetic term $
\dot y_1^2+\dot y_2^2$ is already diagonal (and proportional to the identity), s
o it is invariant under any orthogonal change of variables. Therefore decoupling
 reduces to **diagonalizing $M$ by an orthogonal transformation**.

## The decoupling transformation

Since $M$ is real symmetric, it admits an orthonormal eigenbasis. Let $\lambda_1
,\lambda_2$ be the eigenvalues and $\mathbf v_1,\mathbf v_2$ the corresponding o
rthonormal eigenvectors. Define new variables

$$
\boxed{\;
\mathbf z = Q^T\mathbf y,\qquad
Q=\big(\mathbf v_1\;\big|\;\mathbf v_2\big),\qquad Q^T Q=I,
\;}
$$

i.e.

$$
z_1 = \mathbf v_1\cdot\mathbf y = v_{11}y_1+v_{21}y_2,\qquad
z_2 = \mathbf v_2\cdot\mathbf y = v_{12}y_1+v_{22}y_2.
$$

Because $Q$ is orthogonal, $\dot y_1^2+\dot y_2^2=\dot z_1^2+\dot z_2^2$, so the
 kinetic term is preserved. Substituting $\mathbf y=Q\mathbf z$ into $\ddot{\mat
hbf y}+M\mathbf y=\mathbf 0$ and left-multiplying by $Q^T$:

$$
\ddot{\mathbf z} + \underbrace{Q^T M Q}_{\Lambda}\,\mathbf z = \mathbf 0,\qquad
\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\lambda_2).
$$

This is **decoupled**:

$$
\boxed{\;\ddot z_1+\lambda_1 z_1=0,\qquad \ddot z_2+\lambda_2 z_2=0.\;}
$$

## Explicit form of the rotation

For the $2\times2$ symmetric matrix $M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&c\end{pmatrix}$, th
e diagonalizing orthogonal matrix is a rotation by the angle $\theta$ satisfying

$$
\tan 2\theta=\frac{2b}{a-c}.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\;
Q=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\ \sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix},\qq
uad
\begin{aligned}
z_1&=y_1\cos\theta+y_2\sin\theta,\\
z_2&=-y_1\sin\theta+y_2\cos\theta,
\end{aligned}
\;}
$$

with eigenvalues

$$
\lambda_{1,2}=\frac{a+c}{2}\pm\sqrt{\Big(\frac{a-c}{2}\Big)^2+b^2}.
$$

One verifies directly: $Q^T M Q=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)$, so th
e cross term $b\,y_1y_2$ is eliminated and the two Euler–Lagrange equations beco
me independent oscillators.

## Why this is the unique (up to signs/permutation) decoupling linear transforma
tion

Any linear transformation $\mathbf z=P\mathbf y$ that decouples the system must
(i) keep the kinetic term diagonal and (ii) diagonalize $M$. Condition (i) force
s $P^T P$ to be a scalar multiple of the identity; rescaling absorbs the scalar,
 so $P$ is orthogonal. Condition (ii) then forces the columns of $P$ to be eigen
vectors of $M$. For a $2\times2$ symmetric matrix with distinct eigenvalues, the
 orthonormal eigenbasis is unique up to signs and ordering — i.e. up to $z_i\map
sto\pm z_i$ and swapping $z_1\leftrightarrow z_2$. Hence the rotation above is t
he decoupling linear transformation, determined uniquely modulo these trivial re
dundancies.

## Conclusion

The linear transformation that decouples the coupled Euler–Lagrange equations is
 the **orthogonal transformation that diagonalizes the coupling matrix** $M$. Co
ncretely, it is the rotation

$$
\mathbf z=Q^T\mathbf y,\qquad Q=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\ \sin\the
ta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix},\qquad \tan 2\theta=\frac{2b}{\,a-c\,},
$$

which sends the coupled system $\ddot{\mathbf y}+M\mathbf y=\mathbf 0$ to the de
coupled system $\ddot z_i+\lambda_i z_i=0$ ($i=1,2$), with $\lambda_{1,2}=\tfrac
{a+c}{2}\pm\sqrt{\big(\tfrac{a-c}{2}\big)^2+b^2}$.
