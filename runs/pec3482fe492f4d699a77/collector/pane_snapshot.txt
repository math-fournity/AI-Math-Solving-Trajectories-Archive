## Proof

**Claim.** The maximum number of points in $\mathbb{R}^n$ with all pairwise dist
ances equal to $1$ is $n+1$.

**Upper bound: at most $n+1$ points.**

Let $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_m \in \mathbb{R}^n$ satisfy $\|x_i - x_j\| = 1$ for all
$i \neq j$. Set $v_i = x_i - x_1$ for $i = 2, \dots, m$. We show $v_2, \dots, v_
m$ are linearly independent, forcing $m - 1 \le n$.

For each $i \ge 2$: $\|v_i\|^2 = \|x_i - x_1\|^2 = 1$.

For $i, j \ge 2$, $i \neq j$: $\|v_i - v_j\|^2 = \|x_i - x_j\|^2 = 1$. Expanding
,
$$1 = \|v_i\|^2 - 2\, v_i \cdot v_j + \|v_j\|^2 = 1 - 2(v_i \cdot v_j) + 1,$$
so $v_i \cdot v_j = \tfrac{1}{2}$ for $i \neq j$, while $v_i \cdot v_i = 1$.

Suppose $\sum_{i=2}^{m} c_i v_i = 0$. Taking the inner product with $v_j$:
$$c_j + \sum_{i \neq j} c_i \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} = 0 \implies \tfrac{1}{2}c_j + \t
frac{1}{2}S = 0,$$
where $S = \sum_{i=2}^{m} c_i$. Thus $c_j = -S$ for every $j$. Summing over $j$:
$$S = \sum_{j=2}^{m} c_j = -(m-1)S \implies mS = 0 \implies S = 0 \implies c_j =
 0 \;\forall\, j.$$

So $\{v_2, \dots, v_m\}$ is linearly independent, hence $m - 1 \le n$, i.e. $m \
le n+1$.

**Lower bound: $n+1$ points are achievable.**

The vertices of a regular simplex in $\mathbb{R}^n$ realize $n+1$ points with al
l pairwise distances equal. Explicitly, take the $n+1$ standard basis vectors $e
_1, \dots, e_{n+1}$ in $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$, which lie in the hyperplane $\sum x_i
 = 1$ (an $n$-dimensional affine subspace), and rescale so that pairwise distanc
es become $1$. Since $\|e_i - e_j\| = \sqrt{2}$, scaling by $1/\sqrt{2}$ gives $
n+1$ points in an $n$-dimensional affine subspace of $\mathbb{R}^{n+1} \cong \ma
thbb{R}^n$ with all pairwise distances equal to $1$.

**Conclusion.** The maximum is $\boxed{n+1}$.
