**Claim.** The weak $\sigma$-$(V, V')$ topology on a normed space $V$ is Hausdor
ff.

**Proof.**

Recall that the weak topology $\sigma(V, V')$ on $V$ is the coarsest topology ma
king every functional $f \in V'$ (the continuous dual of $V$) continuous. A neig
hborhood subbase at a point $x_0 \in V$ is given by the sets
$$
\{x \in V : |f(x) - f(x_0)| < \varepsilon\}, \qquad f \in V',\ \varepsilon > 0.
$$

To show the topology is Hausdorff, let $x, y \in V$ with $x \neq y$. We must pro
duce disjoint weakly open sets $U, W$ with $x \in U$ and $y \in W$.

Set $z := x - y \neq 0$. By the Hahn–Banach theorem (the norming corollary), for
 every nonzero $z \in V$ there exists $f \in V'$ such that $f(z) = \|z\| \neq 0$
. Applying this to $z = x - y$, we obtain $f \in V'$ with
$$
f(x) - f(y) = f(x - y) \neq 0,
$$
i.e. $f(x) \neq f(y)$.

Since the scalar field ($\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$) is Hausdorff, there exist
disjoint open neighborhoods $I_x \ni f(x)$ and $I_y \ni f(y)$. Define
$$
U := f^{-1}(I_x), \qquad W := f^{-1}(I_y).
$$
Because $f$ is continuous in the weak topology (by definition of $\sigma(V, V')$
), both $U$ and $W$ are weakly open. They are disjoint since $I_x \cap I_y = \va
rnothing$ implies $f^{-1}(I_x) \cap f^{-1}(I_y) = \varnothing$. Moreover $x \in
U$ (as $f(x) \in I_x$) and $y \in W$ (as $f(y) \in I_y$).

Thus every pair of distinct points can be separated by disjoint weakly open sets
, so $\sigma(V, V')$ is Hausdorff. $\blacksquare$

**Key ingredient.** The only fact needed beyond the definition of the weak topol
ogy is that $V'$ separates the points of $V$, which is a direct corollary of the
 Hahn–Banach theorem: the continuous dual of a normed space is total (separating
).
