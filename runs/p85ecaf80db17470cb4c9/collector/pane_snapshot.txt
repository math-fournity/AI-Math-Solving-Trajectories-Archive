# Solution

## Setup

The space $Y$ is the **mapping torus** of the antipodal map $a: S^2 \to S^2$, $a
(p) = -p$. That is,

$$Y = (S^2 \times [0,1]) \big/ (p, 1) \sim (-p, 0).$$

## Step 1: $Y$ is the total space of a fiber bundle over $S^1$ with fiber $S^2$.

Define the projection $\pi: Y \to S^1 = [0,1]/(0 \sim 1)$ by $\pi([p, t]) = [t]$
. This is well-defined: the identification $(p,1) \sim (-p,0)$ sends $t=1$ and $
t=0$ to the same point $[0]=[1] \in S^1$.

**Local triviality:** Over any open arc $U \subset S^1$ not containing the basep
oint $[0]$, the preimage is $S^2 \times U$, which is trivially fibered. Over an
open arc $V$ containing $[0]$, the preimage is $(S^2 \times (1-\varepsilon, 1])
\cup (S^2 \times [0, \varepsilon))$ with the gluing $(p,1) \sim (-p,0)$. Since t
he antipodal map is a **homeomorphism**, we construct a local trivialization $S^
2 \times V \to \pi^{-1}(V)$ by:
- For $t \in [0, \varepsilon)$: $(p, t) \mapsto [p, t]$,
- For $t \in (-\varepsilon, 0)$: $(p, t) \mapsto [a^{-1}(p),\; 1+t] = [-p,\; 1+t
]$.

This is continuous and a homeomorphism on each fiber. Hence $S^2 \hookrightarrow
 Y \xrightarrow{\pi} S^1$ is a **fiber bundle**, and therefore a **Serre fibrati
on**.

## Step 2: Apply the long exact sequence of homotopy groups.

The fibration $S^2 \to Y \to S^1$ yields the long exact sequence:

$$\cdots \to \pi_2(S^1) \to \pi_1(S^2) \to \pi_1(Y) \to \pi_1(S^1) \to \pi_0(S^2
) \to \cdots$$

Computing each term:

| Term | Value | Reason |
|------|-------|--------|
| $\pi_2(S^1)$ | $0$ | $S^1$ is 1-dimensional |
| $\pi_1(S^2)$ | $0$ | $S^2$ is simply connected |
| $\pi_1(S^1)$ | $\mathbb{Z}$ | Standard |
| $\pi_0(S^2)$ | $0$ | $S^2$ is connected |

Substituting, the exact sequence becomes:

$$0 \longrightarrow 0 \longrightarrow \pi_1(Y) \xrightarrow{\;\pi_*\;} \mathbb{Z
} \longrightarrow 0$$

## Step 3: Conclude by exactness.

- **Injectivity of $\pi_*$:** $\ker(\pi_*) = \operatorname{im}(0 \to \pi_1(Y)) =
 0$.
- **Surjectivity of $\pi_*$:** $\operatorname{im}(\pi_*) = \ker(\mathbb{Z} \to 0
) = \mathbb{Z}$.

Therefore $\pi_*: \pi_1(Y) \to \mathbb{Z}$ is an isomorphism.

## Result

$$\boxed{\pi_1(Y) \cong \mathbb{Z}.}$$

**Remark.** The result is independent of the specific choice of gluing map $f: S
^2 \to S^2$, because $\pi_1(S^2) = 0$ makes the monodromy action on $\pi_1$ vacu
ous. The antipodal map (degree $-1$) only affects $\pi_2$ via its action on $\pi
_2(S^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, not $\pi_1$. Consistency check: the universal cover o
f $Y$ is $S^2 \times \mathbb{R}$ (the pullback of the fibration along $\mathbb{R
} \to S^1$), which is simply connected, confirming $\pi_1(Y) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ w
ith deck group $\mathbb{Z}$ acting by $(p, t) \mapsto (-p, t+1)$.
