## Proof

Define $g:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}$ by
$$
g(x)=\begin{cases}-1,& x\in[-1,0),\\[2pt]\phantom{-}1,& x\in(0,1].\end{cases}
$$
Then for every $u\in C[-1,1]$,
$$
F(u)=\int_{0}^{1}u(x)\,dx-\int_{-1}^{0}u(x)\,dx=\int_{-1}^{1}u(x)\,g(x)\,dx.
$$

**Upper bound.** For any $u\in C[-1,1]$ with $\|u\|_{\infty}=1$,
$$
|F(u)|=\left|\int_{-1}^{1}u(x)\,g(x)\,dx\right|\le\int_{-1}^{1}|u(x)|\,|g(x)|\,d
x\le\|u\|_{\infty}\int_{-1}^{1}|g(x)|\,dx.
$$
Since $|g(x)|=1$ a.e. on $[-1,1]$,
$$
\int_{-1}^{1}|g(x)|\,dx=2,
$$
so $|F(u)|\le 2$. Hence $\|F\|\le 2$.

**Lower bound (attainment in the limit).** For $n\ge 1$, define $u_n\in C[-1,1]$
 by
$$
u_n(x)=\begin{cases}
-1,& x\in[-1,-\tfrac1n],\\[2pt]
\text{linear},& x\in[-\tfrac1n,\tfrac1n],\\[2pt]
\phantom{-}1,& x\in[\tfrac1n,1],
\end{cases}
$$
where the linear part connects $(-\tfrac1n,-1)$ to $(\tfrac1n,1)$. Then $\|u_n\|
_{\infty}=1$, and $u_n(x)=g(x)$ for all $x\in[-1,-\tfrac1n]\cup[\tfrac1n,1]$. Th
erefore
$$
F(u_n)=\int_{-1}^{1}u_n(x)\,g(x)\,dx=\int_{-1}^{-1/n}1\,dx+\int_{-1/n}^{1/n}u_n(
x)\,g(x)\,dx+\int_{1/n}^{1}1\,dx.
$$
The two outer integrals contribute $\bigl(1-\tfrac1n\bigr)+\bigl(1-\tfrac1n\bigr
)=2-\tfrac2n$. On $[-\tfrac1n,\tfrac1n]$ we have $|u_n|\le 1$ and $|g|\le 1$, so
 the middle integral is bounded in absolute value by $\tfrac2n$. Hence
$$
F(u_n)\ge 2-\frac2n-\frac2n=2-\frac4n\;\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\;2.
$$
Thus $\|F\|\ge\lim_{n\to\infty}|F(u_n)|=2$.

Combining both bounds,
$$
\boxed{\|F\|=2}.
$$
