**Problem.** If $A$ and $B$ are two normal operators such that $A+B=AB$, does it
 follow that $AB=BA$?

**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

Work on a Hilbert space $H$; "normal operator" means a bounded normal operator.
Rewrite the hypothesis:
$$A+B=AB \;\Longleftrightarrow\; AB-A-B+I=I \;\Longleftrightarrow\; (A-I)(B-I)=I
.$$
Set $C:=A-I$ and $D:=B-I$, so
$$CD=I. \tag{1}$$

**Step 1. $C$ and $D$ are normal.** Since $A$ is normal,
$$C^*C=(A^*-I)(A-I)=A^*A-A^*-A+I=AA^*-A-A^*+I=(A-I)(A^*-I)=CC^*,$$
using $A^*A=AA^*$. Hence $C$ is normal; likewise $D$ is normal.

**Step 2. A surjective bounded normal operator is injective.** Let $T$ be bounde
d normal and surjective. Then $\operatorname{ran}(T)=H$ is closed, so
$$\operatorname{ran}(T)=\overline{\operatorname{ran}(T)}=(\ker T^*)^\perp=H,$$
which forces $\ker T^*=\{0\}$. For normal $T$ we have $\|Tx\|=\|T^*x\|$ for all
$x$ (since $\|Tx\|^2=\langle T^*Tx,x\rangle=\langle TT^*x,x\rangle=\|T^*x\|^2$),
 hence $\ker T=\ker T^*=\{0\}$. So $T$ is injective.

**Step 3. $C$ is invertible.** From (1), $C$ is surjective: for every $y\in H$,
$y=C(Dy)$. By Step 2 (applied to the normal operator $C$), $C$ is also injective
. Thus $C:H\to H$ is a bounded bijection between Banach spaces; by the Bounded I
nverse Theorem, $C^{-1}$ is bounded.

**Step 4. Conclude $DC=I$.** From $CD=I$ and the existence of $C^{-1}$, left-mul
tiply by $C^{-1}$:
$$D=C^{-1}.$$
Therefore $DC=C^{-1}C=I$, i.e.
$$(B-I)(A-I)=I.$$
Expanding: $BA-B-A+I=I$, so $BA=A+B$. Since $A+B=AB$, we obtain
$$BA=AB.$$

Hence $AB=BA$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Remark.** In finite dimensions the conclusion is immediate: $(A-I)(B-I)=I$ for
 square matrices forces $(B-I)(A-I)=I$ (a one-sided inverse of a square matrix i
s a two-sided inverse), so normality is not even needed. The content of the resu
lt is in the infinite-dimensional setting, where normality of $A$ is the hypothe
sis that upgrades "surjective" to "bijective" and thus makes the one-sided inver
se two-sided.
