Claim: Yes. The Krull dimension of any (non-zero) commutative semilocal Hilbert
ring is equal to zero.

Proof.

Recall that a commutative ring $R$ is a Hilbert ring (Jacobson ring) if every
prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ is the intersection of all maximal ideals containing
it:

$$\mathfrak{p} = \bigcap_{\mathfrak{m} \supseteq \mathfrak{p},; \mathfrak{m}
\text{ maximal}} \mathfrak{m}.$$

A ring is semilocal if it has only finitely many maximal ideals, say $
\mathfrak{m}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{m}_n$.

Let $\mathfrak{p}$ be any prime ideal of $R$. Since $\mathfrak{p}$ is a proper
ideal, by Zorn's lemma it is contained in some maximal ideal, so the set

$$S = {i \in {1,\dots,n} : \mathfrak{p} \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_i}$$

is non-empty. By the Hilbert ring property,

$$\mathfrak{p} = \bigcap_{i \in S} \mathfrak{m}_i.$$

Key step. We show $|S| = 1$.

Suppose for contradiction that $|S| \geq 2$. Pick distinct $i, j \in S$. Since $
\mathfrak{m}_i$ and $\mathfrak{m}_j$ are distinct maximal ideals, they are
comaximal: $\mathfrak{m}_i + \mathfrak{m}_j = R$. Hence there exist $a \in
\mathfrak{m}_i$ and $b \in \mathfrak{m}_j$ with $a + b = 1$.

  • $a \notin \mathfrak{p}$: If $a \in \mathfrak{p} \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_j$,
  then $1 = a + b \in \mathfrak{m}_j$, contradicting $\mathfrak{m}_j \neq R$.
  • $b \notin \mathfrak{p}$: If $b \in \mathfrak{p} \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_i$,
  then $1 = a + b \in \mathfrak{m}_i$, contradicting $\mathfrak{m}_i \neq R$.
  • $ab \in \mathfrak{p}$: Since $a \in \mathfrak{m}_i$ and $b \in \mathfrak{m}
  _j$, we have $ab \in \mathfrak{m}_i \cap \mathfrak{m}j \subseteq \bigcap{k \in
  S} \mathfrak{m}_k = \mathfrak{p}$.

Thus $ab \in \mathfrak{p}$ with $a \notin \mathfrak{p}$ and $b \notin
\mathfrak{p}$, contradicting the primality of $\mathfrak{p}$.

Therefore $|S| = 1$, which means $\mathfrak{p} = \mathfrak{m}_i$ for some $i$.
Every prime ideal is maximal, so the Krull dimension of $R$ is $0$. $
\blacksquare$
