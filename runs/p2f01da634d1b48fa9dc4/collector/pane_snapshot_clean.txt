## Generating Polynomial for Stirling Cycle Numbers

**Claim.** The generating polynomial for the Stirling cycle numbers (of the firs
t kind) is

$$P_n(u) \;=\; \sum_{r=0}^{n}\left[{n \atop r}\right]u^r \;=\; u(u+1)(u+2)\cdots
(u+n-1) \;=\; \prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(u+k) \;=\; u^{\overline{n}},$$

the **rising factorial** (Pochhammer symbol).

---

### Proof

We use the fundamental recurrence for Stirling cycle numbers. Inserting a new el
ement $n$ into a permutation of $\{1,\dots,n-1\}$, either $n$ forms a cycle by i
tself (contributing $+1$ cycle) or $n$ is inserted into one of the existing cycl
es by placing it immediately after any of the $n-1$ existing elements (keeping t
he cycle count unchanged). This gives

$$\left[{n \atop r}\right] \;=\; \left[{n-1 \atop r-1}\right] \;+\; (n-1)\left[{
n-1 \atop r}\right], \qquad n\ge 1,\; r\ge 1, \tag{1}$$

with boundary conditions $\left[{0 \atop 0}\right]=1$ and $\left[{n \atop 0}\rig
ht]=0$ for $n\ge 1$.

**Base case ($n=1$).** We have $\left[{1 \atop 1}\right]=1$, so

$$P_1(u) = u = \prod_{k=0}^{0}(u+k). \checkmark$$

**Inductive step.** Assume $P_{n-1}(u) = \prod_{k=0}^{n-2}(u+k)$ for some $n\ge
2$. Multiplying recurrence (1) by $u^r$ and summing over $r$:

$$P_n(u) = \sum_{r\ge 1}\left[{n-1 \atop r-1}\right]u^r + (n-1)\sum_{r\ge 1}\lef
t[{n-1 \atop r}\right]u^r.$$

The first sum is $u\sum_{r\ge 0}\left[{n-1 \atop r}\right]u^r = u\,P_{n-1}(u)$ (
re-indexing $r\mapsto r-1$; the $r=0$ term vanishes since $\left[{n-1\atop 0}\ri
ght]=0$ for $n-1\ge 1$, and the $n=1$ case is the base). The second sum is $(n-1
)P_{n-1}(u)$ (the $r=0$ term is $0$). Therefore

$$P_n(u) = u\,P_{n-1}(u) + (n-1)\,P_{n-1}(u) = (u+n-1)\,P_{n-1}(u).$$

Applying the induction hypothesis,

$$P_n(u) = (u+n-1)\prod_{k=0}^{n-2}(u+k) = \prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(u+k). \qquad\blacks
quare$$

---

### Verification (small cases)

- $n=2$: $u(u+1)=u^2+u$, and indeed $\left[{2\atop 2}\right]=1,\;\left[{2\atop 1
}\right]=1$. ✓
- $n=3$: $u(u+1)(u+2)=u^3+3u^2+2u$, and $\left[{3\atop 3}\right]=1,\;\left[{3\at
op 2}\right]=3,\;\left[{3\atop 1}\right]=2$. ✓
- $n=4$: $u(u+1)(u+2)(u+3)=u^4+6u^3+11u^2+6u$, matching $\left[{4\atop 4}\right]
=1,\;\left[{4\atop 3}\right]=6,\;\left[{4\atop 2}\right]=11,\;\left[{4\atop 1}\r
ight]=6$. ✓

### Remark (combinatorial interpretation)

The factor $u^{\overline{n}}=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(u+k)$ admits a direct bijective p
roof: build a permutation of $\{1,\dots,n\}$ by inserting elements $1,2,\dots,n$
 in order. When element $k$ is inserted, it either starts a new cycle (factor $u
$, weighted by the indeterminate marking cycle count) or joins one of the $k-1$
existing positions right after an already-placed element (factor $k-1$, contribu
ting $k-1$ to the numeric coefficient). The product of these choices is exactly
$u\cdot(u+1)\cdot(u+2)\cdots(u+n-1)$, and the coefficient of $u^r$ counts permut
ations with exactly $r$ cycles, i.e. $\left[{n\atop r}\right]$.
