Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether, given the field chain and

**Proof.**

We are given $F \leq E \leq K$ with $\operatorname{char}(F) \neq 2, 3$, $[E:F] =
 2$ (so $E = F(x)$ for some $x \notin F$), and $z \in K$ whose minimal polynomia

**Key observation.** For any $c \in E^*$, set $y = cz$. Then:
- $y^3 = c^3 z^3 = c^3 a =: b \in E$,
- $E(y) = E(cz) = E(z) = K$ (since $c \in E^*$ gives $z = y/c \in E(y)$ and $y \
in E(z)$),
- $X^3 - b$ is irreducible over $E$: if $b = d^3$ for $d \in E$, then $a = (d/c)
^3$ is a cube in $E$, contradicting irreducibility of $X^3 - a$.

So it suffices to find $c \in E^*$ with $b = ac^3 \in E \setminus F$.

**Case 1: $a \in E \setminus F$.** Take $c = 1$, $y = z$, $b = a \in E \setminus
 F$. Done.

**Case 2: $a \in F^*$.** We need $c \in E^*$ with $c^3 \in E \setminus F$ (then
$b = ac^3 \in E \setminus F$ since $a \in F^*$).

**Lemma.** If $c \in E \setminus F$ and $c^3 \in F$, then $E$ contains a primiti
ve cube root of unity $\omega$, and $c = d\omega$ or $c = d\omega^2$ for some $d
 \in F^*$.

*Proof.* Since $c \in E \setminus F$ and $[E:F]=2$, $c$ has degree 2 over $F$. T
he polynomial $X^3 - c^3 \in F[X]$ has $c$ as a root, so it has an irreducible f
actor of degree 2 over $F$. Being cubic, it also has a root $d \in F$, giving $c
^3 = d^3$, $d \in F^*$. Then $(c/d)^3 = 1$ with $c/d \neq 1$ (as $c \notin F$),
so $\omega := c/d$ is a primitive cube root of unity in $E$, and $c = d\omega$.
$\square$

We now find $c$ by subcases based on primitive cube roots of unity:

**Subcase 2a: $E$ contains no primitive cube root of unity.** Take $c = x$. By t

setminus F$. ✓

**Subcase 2b: $\omega \in E \setminus F$ (primitive cube root of unity).** Then
$E = F(\omega)$, since $\omega$ satisfies $X^2+X+1=0$ which is irreducible over
$F$ (as $\omega \notin F$), giving $[F(\omega):F]=2=[E:F]$. Set $c = 1 + 2\omega
$. Using $\omega^2 + \omega + 1 = 0$:
$$c^2 = 1 + 4\omega + 4\omega^2 = 1 + 4\omega + 4(-\omega - 1) = -3.$$
Since $\operatorname{char}(F) \neq 3$, we have $-3 \in F^*$, so:
$$c^3 = c \cdot c^2 = -3(1 + 2\omega).$$
Now $c = 1+2\omega \neq 0$ (else $\omega = -1/2 \in F$, contradiction) and $c \n
otin F$ (else $\omega \in F$ since $2$ is invertible, using $\operatorname{char}
(F)\neq 2$). Thus $c^3 = -3c \in E \setminus F$ (as $-3 \in F^*$, $c \in E \setm
inus F$). ✓

**Subcase 2c: $\omega \in F$.** Take $c = x \in E \setminus F$. By the Lemma, if
 $c^3 \in F$ then $c = d\omega$ or $d\omega^2$ for $d \in F^*$. But $d\omega, d\
omega^2 \in F$ (since $d, \omega \in F$), contradicting $c \notin F$. So $c^3 \i
n E \setminus F$. ✓

In every subcase we have found $c \in E^*$ with $c^3 \in E \setminus F$. Setting
 $y = cz$ and $b = ac^3$:
- $b = ac^3 \in E \setminus F$ (since $a \in F^*$ and $c^3 \in E \setminus F$),
- $y^3 = b$ and $X^3 - b$ is the minimal polynomial of $y$ over $E$ (irreducible

- $E(y) = E(z) = K$.

This covers all cases, completing the proof. $\blacksquare$
