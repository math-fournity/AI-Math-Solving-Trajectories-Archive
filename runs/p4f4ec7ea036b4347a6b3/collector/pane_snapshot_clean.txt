## Proof

**Claim.** The smallest such $n$ is $\boxed{m^d + 1}$.

Let the columns of $X$ be $x_1,\dots,x_n \in \{1,2,\dots,m\}^d$, and let $K \in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ be defined by $K_{ij}=k(x_i,x_j)$.

---

### Upper bound: $n = m^d+1$ guarantees singularity

The set $\{1,2,\dots,m\}^d$ contains exactly $m^d$ distinct points. If $n = m^d+
1$, then by the pigeonhole principle, among the $n$ columns $x_1,\dots,x_n$ at l
east two are identical, say $x_p = x_q$ with $p \neq q$. Consequently, for *any*
 function $k$ (and in particular for any kernel function),

$$K_{pj} = k(x_p,x_j) = k(x_q,x_j) = K_{qj} \quad \text{for all } j,$$

so rows $p$ and $q$ of $K$ coincide. Hence $\operatorname{rank}(K) < n$ and $K$
is singular. This holds for every $X \in \{1,\dots,m\}^{d\times n}$ and every ke
rnel $k$, so $n=m^d+1$ is sufficient.

---

### Lower bound: $n = m^d$ does not guarantee singularity

We exhibit a specific $X$ and a specific (positive semi-definite) kernel $k$ for
 which $K$ is nonsingular.

Take $X$ whose $n = m^d$ columns are *all distinct* points of $\{1,\dots,m\}^d$
(in any order). Define the kernel

$$k(x,y) = \mathbf{1}\{x = y\}, \qquad x,y \in \mathbb{R}^d.$$

This is a valid PSD kernel: it is the inner product induced by the feature map $
\phi(x) = e_x$ (the standard basis vector indexed by $x$) in $\mathbb{R}^{m^d}$.
 For this $X$ and $k$,

$$K_{ij} = \mathbf{1}\{x_i = x_j\} = \delta_{ij},$$

so $K = I_{m^d}$, the identity matrix, which is nonsingular.

Therefore $n = m^d$ does **not** guarantee singularity, and consequently no $n \
le m^d$ does (the same construction works for any $n \le m^d$ by taking $n$ dist
inct points).

---

### Conclusion

The smallest $n$ such that $K$ is guaranteed singular for every $X \in \{1,\dots
,m\}^{d\times n}$ and every kernel $k$ is

$$n = m^d + 1.$$
