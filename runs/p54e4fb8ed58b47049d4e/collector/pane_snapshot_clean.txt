compact) open domain $U \subset \mathbb{C}$, every sequence in $U$ converging to
 a boundary point forces a holomorphic $f: U \to \mathbb{C}$ to diverge to infin
ity.

**Answer: No.** In fact, we prove something much stronger: **no** holomorphic fu
nction $f: U \to \mathbb{C}$ on a bounded open set $U$ can have the property tha
t $f(z_n) \to \infty$ for every sequence $\{z_n\} \subset U$ with $z_n \to p \in
 \partial U$.

## Proof

Since $U$ is relatively compact in $\mathbb{C}$, $U$ is bounded. Suppose for con
tradiction that there exists a holomorphic function $f: U \to \mathbb{C}$ such t
hat $f(z_n) \to \infty$ for every sequence $\{z_n\} \subset U$ converging to a p
oint of $\partial U$.

**Step 1: $f$ is a proper map.** The condition means: for every $M > 0$, the set
 $K_M := \{z \in U : |f(z)| \leq M\}$ is compact in $U$. Indeed, if $K_M$ were n
ot compact, there would exist a sequence $\{z_n\} \subset K_M$ with no subsequen
ce converging in $U$; since $\overline{U}$ is compact, a subsequence converges t
o some $p \in \overline{U}$, and $p \notin U$ (else it would converge in $U$), s
o $p \in \partial U$. But then $f(z_n) \to \infty$ by hypothesis, contradicting
$|f(z_n)| \leq M$. Thus $f: U \to \mathbb{C}$ is proper.

**Step 2: $f$ has finitely many zeros.** The set $f^{-1}(0) = K_0$ is compact in
 $U$ (by properness) and discrete (since $f$ is holomorphic and not identically
zero — $f$ is surjective as a proper map into the connected set $\mathbb{C}$). A
 compact discrete set is finite. Let the zeros be $a_1, \dots, a_k$ with multipl
icities $m_1, \dots, m_k$.

**Step 3: Construct a bounded holomorphic function vanishing at the boundary.**
Define
$$\varphi(z) = \frac{1}{f(z)} \prod_{i=1}^{k} (z - a_i)^{m_i}.$$
Since the zeros of $f$ at $a_i$ (of order $m_i$) are canceled by the factors $(z
-a_i)^{m_i}$, the function $\varphi$ is holomorphic on all of $U$.

Now we examine the behavior of $\varphi$ near $\partial U$. As $z \to \partial U
$:
- $|f(z)| \to \infty$ by hypothesis, so $|1/f(z)| \to 0$.
- $\prod_{i=1}^k |z - a_i|^{m_i}$ is **bounded** on $U$, because $U$ is bounded
(say $U \subset D(0,R)$, so each $|z - a_i| \leq |z| + |a_i| \leq R + \max_i |a_
i|$, which is a fixed constant).

Therefore $|\varphi(z)| \to 0$ as $z \to \partial U$.

**Step 4: Apply the maximum principle.** For any $z_0 \in U$ and any $\varepsilo
n > 0$, since $|\varphi(z)| \to 0$ near $\partial U$, there exists $\delta > 0$
such that $|\varphi(z)| < \varepsilon$ for all $z \in U$ with $\operatorname{dis
t}(z, \partial U) < \delta$. Let $U_\delta = \{z \in U : \operatorname{dist}(z,
\partial U) > \delta\}$; for $\delta$ small enough, $z_0 \in U_\delta$ and $U_\d
elta$ is a bounded domain. On $\partial U_\delta \subset U$, we have $|\varphi|
< \varepsilon$. By the maximum principle applied to $\varphi$ on $U_\delta$:
$$|\varphi(z_0)| \leq \max_{z \in \partial U_\delta} |\varphi(z)| < \varepsilon.
$$
Since $\varepsilon > 0$ was arbitrary, $\varphi(z_0) = 0$. Since $z_0 \in U$ was
 arbitrary, $\varphi \equiv 0$ on $U$.

**Step 5: Contradiction.** But $\varphi = \frac{1}{f}\prod(z-a_i)^{m_i}$, and $f
$ is not identically zero (it is surjective onto $\mathbb{C}$, being a proper ma
p into a connected, locally compact target), and $\prod(z-a_i)^{m_i}$ is a nonze
ro polynomial. So $\varphi$ is not identically zero — contradiction.

## Conclusion

No such holomorphic function exists. The answer to the question is **no**: it is
 not the case that every sequence converging to a boundary point causes a holomo
rphic function to diverge to infinity. In fact, on any bounded domain $U \subset
 \mathbb{C}$, **no** holomorphic function $f: U \to \mathbb{C}$ can diverge to i
nfinity along every sequence approaching the boundary. The obstruction is that $
1/f$, after clearing its finitely many poles, yields a holomorphic function tend
ing to $0$ at the boundary, which must vanish identically by the maximum princip
le — contradicting the nonvanishing of $f$.

The essential ingredients are: (1) properness forces finitely many zeros, (2) bo
undedness of $U$ makes the polynomial factor $\prod(z-a_i)^{m_i}$ bounded, and (
3) the maximum principle forces the resulting boundary-vanishing holomorphic fun
ction to be identically zero.
