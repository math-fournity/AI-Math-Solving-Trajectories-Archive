## Proof

Let $P(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y,z]$ satisfy $P(a,b,c)=0 \iff a=b=c$ over $\mathbb
{R}$. Define
$$R(n,m)=P(n,\,n+m,\,n+2m).$$
We seek the largest $r$ such that $m^r\mid R(n,m)$ for all integers $n,m$ and al
l admissible $P$.

**Step 1: The gradient of $P$ vanishes on the diagonal.**

The zero set $Z(P)=\{(t,t,t):t\in\mathbb{R}\}$ is a one-dimensional curve. Suppo
se at some diagonal point $p=(t,t,t)$ we had $\nabla P(p)\neq 0$. By the implici
t function theorem, $Z(P)$ would locally be a smooth two-dimensional hypersurfac
e near $p$, which cannot be contained in a one-dimensional curve — it would cont
ain points off the diagonal, contradicting $Z(P)$ being exactly the diagonal. He
nce
$$P_x=P_y=P_z=0\quad\text{on }\{(t,t,t)\}.$$

**Step 2: $m^2\mid R(n,m)$, so $r\ge 2$.**

Since $P(t,t,t)\equiv 0$, we have $R(n,0)=P(n,n,n)=0$ for all $n$, so $m\mid R(n
,m)$ in $\mathbb{Z}[n,m]$; write $R(n,m)=m\,S(n,m)$ with $S\in\mathbb{Z}[n,m]$.

Now compute $S(n,0)$. Since $R=mS$,
$$S(n,0)=\left.\frac{\partial R}{\partial m}\right|_{m=0}=\left.\bigl(P_y\cdot 1
+P_z\cdot 2\bigr)\right|_{(n,n,n)}=\bigl(P_y+2P_z\bigr)(n,n,n).$$
By Step 1, $P_y=P_z=0$ on the diagonal, so $S(n,0)\equiv 0$. Thus $m\mid S(n,m)$
 in $\mathbb{Z}[n,m]$; write $S(n,m)=m\,T(n,m)$ with $T\in\mathbb{Z}[n,m]$. Ther
efore
$$R(n,m)=m^2\,T(n,m),$$
so $m^2\mid R(n,m)$ for all integers $n,m$ (the case $m=0$ is trivial since $R(n
,0)=0$). This holds for every admissible $P$, giving $r\ge 2$.

**Step 3: $r\le 2$ via an example.**

Take $P(x,y,z)=(x-y)^2+(y-z)^2$, which has integer coefficients and vanishes exa
ctly on $a=b=c$. Then
$$R(n,m)=(-m)^2+(-m)^2=2m^2.$$
For $m^r\mid 2m^2$ to hold for **all** integers $m$, we need $m^{r-2}\mid 2$ for
 all $m\neq 0$. Taking $m=3$: $3^{r-2}\mid 2$ forces $r-2\le 0$, i.e. $r\le 2$.

**Conclusion.** Steps 2 and 3 together give $r=2$.
