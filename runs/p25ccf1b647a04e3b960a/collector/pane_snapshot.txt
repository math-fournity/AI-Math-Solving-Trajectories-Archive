## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. The condition implies that $M$ is locally flat (its Riemann curv
ature tensor, and hence Gaussian curvature, vanishes identically).

**Interpretation of the hypothesis.** The hypothesis states that every point $p
\in M$ admits a coordinate chart $\varphi : U \to \mathbb{R}^n$ (with $U \ni p$
open) that is an **isometry**, i.e., it preserves inner products at *every* poin
t of its domain:
$$g_p(v, w) = \langle d\varphi_p(v),\, d\varphi_p(w)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^n}, \qqu
ad \forall\, p \in U,\ \forall\, v, w \in T_p M.$$

**Step 1: The metric is Euclidean in these coordinates.** Let $x^1, \dots, x^n$
be the coordinate functions of $\varphi$. The coordinate vector fields $\partial
_i = \tfrac{\partial}{\partial x^i}$ push forward under $d\varphi$ to the standa
rd basis $e_i$ of $\mathbb{R}^n$. The isometry condition gives
$$g_{ij}(q) = g_q(\partial_i, \partial_j) = \langle e_i, e_j \rangle = \delta_{i
j}, \qquad \forall\, q \in U.$$
So the metric components are $g_{ij} \equiv \delta_{ij}$ (constant, equal to the
 identity) on the entire neighborhood $U$.

**Step 2: Christoffel symbols vanish.** The Christoffel symbols are
$$\Gamma^k_{ij} = \tfrac{1}{2} g^{k\ell}\!\left(\partial_i g_{j\ell} + \partial_
j g_{i\ell} - \partial_\ell g_{ij}\right).$$
Since every $g_{ij} = \delta_{ij}$ is constant on $U$, all partial derivatives $
\partial_i g_{j\ell}$ vanish, hence
$$\Gamma^k_{ij} \equiv 0 \quad \text{on } U.$$

**Step 3: The Riemann curvature tensor vanishes on $U$.** In coordinates, the cu
rvature tensor components are
$$R^\ell{}_{ijk} = \partial_i \Gamma^\ell_{jk} - \partial_j \Gamma^\ell_{ik} + \
Gamma^\ell_{im}\Gamma^m_{jk} - \Gamma^\ell_{jm}\Gamma^m_{ik}.$$
Every term is zero because all $\Gamma^k_{ij} \equiv 0$ on $U$. Therefore $R \eq
uiv 0$ on $U$.

**Step 4: Curvature vanishes globally.** The Riemann curvature tensor is a *tens
or*: if it vanishes in one coordinate system on an open set, it vanishes as a te
nsor on that open set. Since every point $p \in M$ possesses such a neighborhood
 $U$ on which $R \equiv 0$, we conclude $R \equiv 0$ on all of $M$.

**Step 5: Gaussian curvature vanishes.** The Gaussian curvature $K$ (in dimensio
n $n = 2$) and all sectional curvatures (in general dimension) are built from th
e Riemann tensor. With $R \equiv 0$, every sectional curvature is zero; in parti
cular $K \equiv 0$.

**Remark on why the hypothesis is not vacuous/trivial.** One must distinguish th
is hypothesis from the weaker condition that at each point *alone* one can find
coordinates with $g_{ij}(p) = \delta_{ij}$ (e.g., normal coordinates), which hol
ds on *every* Riemannian manifold and says nothing about curvature. The hypothes
is here is strictly stronger: the chart is an isometry on an entire *neighborhoo
d*, forcing $g_{ij} = \delta_{ij}$ on an open set, which forces the connection t
o be flat there.

**Conclusion.** The existence, around every point, of a coordinate chart that is
 an isometry onto its image forces the metric to be locally Euclidean, the conne
ction to be flat, and the Riemann curvature tensor to vanish. Hence $M$ is local
ly flat, and its Gaussian curvature vanishes identically. $\blacksquare$
