Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a function $G: X \to \mathb
b{R}$ can be constructed that is continuous on $X \setminus A$, discontinuous on
 $A$, and of bounded variation, given the hypotheses.

**Answer: Yes, such a function always exists.**

## Construction

Define $G: X \to \mathbb{R}$ by
$$G(x) = \sum_{\substack{a \in A \\ a < x}} g(a) \;+\; \tfrac{1}{2}\, g(x)\,\mat
hbf{1}_A(x).$$

Since $\sum_{a \in A}|g(a)| < \infty$, the series converges absolutely and $G$ i
s well-defined. Write $G = F + H$ where $F(x) = \sum_{a \in A,\, a < x} g(a)$ an
d $H(x) = \frac{1}{2}g(x)\mathbf{1}_A(x)$.

## Claim 1: $G$ has bounded variation

For any partition $x_0 < x_1 < \cdots < x_n$ in $X$, the intervals $[x_{i-1}, x_
i)$ are disjoint, so each $a \in A$ contributes to at most one difference $F(x_i
) - F(x_{i-1})$. Thus
$$\sum_{i=1}^n |F(x_i)-F(x_{i-1})| \le \sum_{a \in A}|g(a)|.$$

For $H$: each $x_i \in A$ appears in at most two consecutive differences, each c
ontributing at most $\frac{1}{2}|g(x_i)|$, so
$$\sum_{i=1}^n |H(x_i)-H(x_{i-1})| \le \sum_{a \in A}|g(a)|.$$

Therefore $V(G) \le 2\sum_{a \in A}|g(a)| < \infty$. $\checkmark$

## Claim 2: $G$ is continuous at every $x_0 \in X \setminus A$

Here $H(x_0)=0$ and $G(x_0) = F(x_0)$. Given $\varepsilon > 0$, since $\sum|g(a)
|<\infty$, there exists a **finite** set $A_\varepsilon \subseteq A$ with $\sum_
{a \in A \setminus A_\varepsilon}|g(a)| < \varepsilon$.

Since $x_0 \notin A$ and $A_\varepsilon$ is finite, $x_0 \notin A_\varepsilon$,
so $\delta := \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{dist}(x_0, A_\varepsilon) > 0$.

For any $x \in X \cap (x_0-\delta,\, x_0+\delta)$:
- **$F$ part:** All $a$ in the sum $F(x)-F(x_0) = \sum_{a \in A,\, \min(x_0,x)\l
e a < \max(x_0,x)} g(a)$ lie in $(x_0-\delta, x_0+\delta)$, hence in $A \setminu
s A_\varepsilon$. So $|F(x)-F(x_0)| \le \sum_{a \in A\setminus A_\varepsilon}|g(
a)| < \varepsilon$.
- **$H$ part:** If $x \in A$ then $x \in A \setminus A_\varepsilon$, so $|H(x)|
= \frac{1}{2}|g(x)| < \varepsilon/2$; if $x \notin A$ then $H(x)=0$. Either way
$|H(x)-H(x_0)| < \varepsilon$.

Thus $|G(x)-G(x_0)| < 2\varepsilon$, proving continuity at $x_0$. $\checkmark$

## Claim 3: $G$ is discontinuous at every $a_0 \in A$

Since $X \setminus A$ is dense in $X$ and $a_0 \in X$, the point $a_0$ is a limi
t point of $X \setminus A$. So there exists a sequence $(x_n) \subset X \setminu
s A$ with $x_n \to a_0$, and infinitely many $x_n$ lie on at least one side of $
a_0$.

**Key lemma:** For any $a_0 \in A$, $\displaystyle\sum_{\substack{a \in A \\ a_0
 < a < x}} g(a) \to 0$ as $x \to a_0^+$, and $\displaystyle\sum_{\substack{a \in
 A \\ x \le a < a_0}} g(a) \to 0$ as $x \to a_0^-$.

*Proof of lemma:* Given $\varepsilon > 0$, pick finite $A_\varepsilon \subseteq
A \setminus \{a_0\}$ with $\sum_{a \in A \setminus (A_\varepsilon \cup \{a_0\})}
|g(a)| < \varepsilon$. Since $a_0 \notin A_\varepsilon$ and $A_\varepsilon$ is f
inite, $\operatorname{dist}(a_0, A_\varepsilon) > 0$. For $x$ close enough to $a
_0$, the interval between $a_0$ and $x$ avoids $A_\varepsilon$, so all terms in
the sum lie in $A \setminus (A_\varepsilon \cup \{a_0\})$, giving a bound of $\v
arepsilon$. $\square$

**Case 1: $x_n < a_0$ infinitely often.** Pass to a subsequence with $x_n < a_0$
. Then
$$G(a_0) - G(x_n) = \sum_{\substack{a \in A \\ x_n \le a < a_0}} g(a) + \tfrac{1
}{2}g(a_0).$$
By the lemma, the sum $\to 0$, so $\lim G(x_n) = G(a_0) - \frac{1}{2}g(a_0) \neq
 G(a_0)$ since $g(a_0) \neq 0$.

**Case 2: $x_n > a_0$ infinitely often.** Pass to a subsequence with $x_n > a_0$
. Then
$$G(x_n) - G(a_0) = \sum_{\substack{a \in A \\ a_0 \le a < x_n}} g(a) - \tfrac{1
}{2}g(a_0) = \tfrac{1}{2}g(a_0) + \sum_{\substack{a \in A \\ a_0 < a < x_n}} g(a
).$$
By the lemma, the sum $\to 0$, so $\lim G(x_n) = G(a_0) + \frac{1}{2}g(a_0) \neq
 G(a_0)$.

In either case, $G(x_n) \not\to G(a_0)$, so $G$ is discontinuous at $a_0$. $\che
ckmark$

The $\frac{1}{2}$-splitting of the jump is essential: it ensures discontinuity f
rom **both** sides, handling the case where $a_0$ is an endpoint of $X$ (approac
hable from only one side).

## Conclusion

The function $G(x) = \sum_{a \in A,\, a < x} g(a) + \frac{1}{2}g(x)\mathbf{1}_A(
x)$ is continuous on $X \setminus A$, discontinuous at every point of $A$, and h
as bounded variation $V(G) \le 2\sum_{a \in A}|g(a)| < \infty$. Therefore, **yes
**, such a construction is always possible under the given hypotheses.
