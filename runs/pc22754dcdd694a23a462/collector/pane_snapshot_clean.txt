## Solution

We interpret $f^{n}(0)$ as the $n$-th derivative $f^{(n)}(0)$ (the denominator $
2^n n!$ is the natural normalization for Taylor coefficients).

**Decomposition of $f$.** Since $f$ is holomorphic on $\Omega = D(0,2)\setminus\
{1/2\}$ with a simple pole at $1/2$ of residue $1$, we may write
$$f(z) = \frac{1}{z - \tfrac12} + h(z),$$
where $h$ is holomorphic on the full disk $D(0,2)$.

**Taylor coefficients of the principal part.** Expanding $\frac{1}{z-1/2}$ at $z
=0$:
$$\frac{1}{z-\tfrac12} = -\frac{1}{\tfrac12 - z} = -2\cdot\frac{1}{1-2z} = -\sum
_{n=0}^{\infty} 2^{n+1} z^n,$$
valid for $|z| < 1/2$. Hence the $n$-th Taylor coefficient of the principal part
 at $0$ is $-2^{n+1}$.

**Taylor coefficients of $h$.** Let $h(z) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty b_n z^n$ be the Ta
ylor expansion of $h$ at $0$. Since $h$ is holomorphic on $D(0,2)$, its radius o
f convergence satisfies $R_h \ge 2$, so by Cauchy–Hadamard
$$\limsup_{n\to\infty} |b_n|^{1/n} \le \tfrac12.$$
Consequently, for every $\varepsilon > 0$, $|b_n| \le \bigl(\tfrac12 + \varepsil
on\bigr)^n$ for all sufficiently large $n$, and therefore
$$\left|\frac{b_n}{2^n}\right| \le \left(\frac{\tfrac12+\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{
\!n} = \left(\tfrac14 + \tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{\!n} \xrightarrow[n\to\in
fty]{} 0.$$
Thus $\displaystyle\frac{b_n}{2^n}\to 0$.

**Combining.** The $n$-th Taylor coefficient of $f$ at $0$ is
$$a_n = \frac{f^{(n)}(0)}{n!} = -2^{n+1} + b_n.$$
Therefore
$$\frac{f^{(n)}(0)}{2^n\, n!} = \frac{a_n}{2^n} = \frac{-2^{n+1} + b_n}{2^n} = -
2 + \frac{b_n}{2^n} \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{} -2 + 0 = -2.$$

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{f^{(n)}(0)}{2^n\, n!} = -2.}$$
