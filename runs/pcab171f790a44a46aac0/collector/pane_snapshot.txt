## Solution

**Interpretation.** $M$ is a binary ($\{0,1\}$-valued) matrix with $\operatornam
e{rank}(M)=r$, and $M'$ is another binary matrix with $\operatorname{rank}(M')\l
e r$ (the natural setting, e.g. any rank-$r$ binary approximation or peer of $M$

able.

**Answer: No.** Such an example does not exist.

### Proof

We use the standard **subadditivity of rank**: for any two matrices $A,B$ of the
 same dimensions,
$$
\operatorname{rank}(A+B)\le \operatorname{rank}(A)+\operatorname{rank}(B),
$$
because the column space of $A+B$ is contained in $\operatorname{col}(A)+\operat
orname{col}(B)$, whose dimension is at most $\operatorname{rank}(A)+\operatornam
e{rank}(B)$.

Since $\operatorname{rank}(-B)=\operatorname{rank}(B)$, applying this with $A=M$
 and $B=-M'$ gives
$$
\operatorname{rank}(M-M')=\operatorname{rank}(M+(-M'))\le \operatorname{rank}(M)
+\operatorname{rank}(M').
$$
With $\operatorname{rank}(M)=r$ and $\operatorname{rank}(M')\le r$,
$$
\operatorname{rank}(M-M')\le r+r=2r.
$$
Therefore
$$
\frac{\operatorname{rank}(M-M')}{\operatorname{rank}(M)}\le \frac{2r}{r}=2.
$$
Hence the ratio can **never strictly exceed** $2$.

### Tightness (the bound $2$ is sharp)

The value $2$ is attainable, so the supremum is exactly $2$ and the strict inequ
ality $>2$ is genuinely impossible (not an artifact of a loose bound).

Take $r=1$. Let
$$
M=e_1 e_1^{\!T}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
M'=e_2 e_2^{\!T}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Both are binary with rank $1$. Then
$$
M-M'=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},
$$
which has rank $2$. Thus
$$
\frac{\operatorname{rank}(M-M')}{\operatorname{rank}(M)}=\frac{2}{1}=2.
$$
So equality is reached, but it can never be surpassed.

### Conclusion

For any binary matrix $M$ of rank $r$ and any binary matrix $M'$ with $\operator
name{rank}(M')\le r$, the subadditivity of rank forces $\operatorname{rank}(M-M'
)\le 2r$, whence $\operatorname{rank}(M-M')/\operatorname{rank}(M)\le 2$. The bo
und is tight (achievable at exactly $2$) but cannot be exceeded. Therefore **no
example exists** with $\operatorname{rank}(M-M')/\operatorname{rank}(M)>2$.
