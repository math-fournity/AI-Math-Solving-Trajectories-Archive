## Setup via symmetric functions

Let $s = x + y$ and $p = xy$. The third equation gives $p = z^2$.

From the first equation: $s + z = a$, so $s = a - z$.

From the second equation, using $x^2 + y^2 = s^2 - 2p = s^2 - 2z^2$:
$$s^2 - 2z^2 + z^2 = b^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; s^2 - z^2 = b^2.$$

Substituting $s = a - z$:
$$(a-z)^2 - z^2 = b^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; a^2 - 2az = b^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; z
= \frac{a^2 - b^2}{2a}.$$

This requires $a \neq 0$. (If $a = 0$, then $x+y+z=0$ and $xy = z^2 = (x+y)^2$ g
ive $x^2+xy+y^2=0$, forcing $x=y=z=0$ and $b=0$ — not distinct positive. So $a=0
$ is excluded.)

Then:
$$s = a - z = \frac{a^2 + b^2}{2a}, \qquad p = z^2 = \frac{(a^2-b^2)^2}{4a^2}.$$

## Solving for $x$ and $y$

$x$ and $y$ are the roots of
$$t^2 - st + p = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; t^2 - \frac{a^2+b^2}{2a}\,t + \frac{(a^2-
b^2)^2}{4a^2} = 0.$$

The discriminant is
$$\Delta = s^2 - 4p = \frac{(a^2+b^2)^2 - 4(a^2-b^2)^2}{4a^2} = \frac{-3a^4 + 10
a^2b^2 - 3b^4}{4a^2}.$$

Factoring (with $u = a^2, v = b^2$):
$$-3u^2 + 10uv - 3v^2 = (3u - v)(3v - u) = (3a^2 - b^2)(3b^2 - a^2).$$

Hence
$$\Delta = \frac{(3a^2 - b^2)(3b^2 - a^2)}{4a^2},$$

and the solutions are
$$\boxed{\;z = \frac{a^2 - b^2}{2a},\qquad x,\,y = \frac{a^2 + b^2 \;\pm\; \sqrt
{(3a^2 - b^2)(3b^2 - a^2)}}{4a}\;}$$

(with $x$ and $y$ interchanged giving the same unordered solution). The only rea
l solutions are this pair (swap of $x,y$), plus the degenerate $a=0$ case above.

## Conditions for distinct positive $x, y, z$

Assume $a > 0$ (justified below) and $b > 0$ (only $b^2$ appears, so WLOG).

**(i) $z > 0$:** $\;\dfrac{a^2 - b^2}{2a} > 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; a^2 > b^2 \;\L
ongrightarrow\; b < a.$

**(ii) $x, y > 0$:** Their sum $s = \dfrac{a^2+b^2}{2a} > 0$ (automatic for $a>0
$) and product $p = z^2 > 0$ (automatic once $z \neq 0$, i.e. $b \neq a$). So bo
th roots positive.

**(iii) $x, y$ real and distinct:** $\;\Delta > 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; (3a^2 - b^
2)(3b^2 - a^2) > 0.$

For $a, b > 0$: $3a^2 - b^2 > 0 \iff b < a\sqrt{3}$, and $3b^2 - a^2 > 0 \iff b
> a/\sqrt{3}$. Both factors having the same sign requires (both positive) $a/\sq
rt{3} < b < a\sqrt{3}$; both negative is impossible. So:
$$\frac{a}{\sqrt{3}} < b < a\sqrt{3}.$$

**(iv) $z$ distinct from $x$ and $y$:** If $z = x$ (with $x \neq 0$), then $xy =
 z^2 = x^2$ gives $y = x$, contradicting $x \neq y$. Similarly $z = y$ forces $x
 = y$. So once $x \neq y$ (from $\Delta > 0$), all three are automatically disti
nct.

**(v) Why $a > 0$:** If $a < 0$, then $s = (a^2+b^2)/(2a) < 0$, but $x, y > 0$ r
equires $s > 0$ — contradiction. So $a > 0$ is necessary.

## Combining the conditions

From (i): $b < a$. From (iii): $b > a/\sqrt{3}$ and $b < a\sqrt{3}$. Since $b <
a$ is stricter than $b < a\sqrt{3}$, the combined condition is:

$$\boxed{\;a > 0 \quad\text{and}\quad \frac{a}{\sqrt{3}} < b < a\;}$$

(equivalently, since only $b^2$ enters the equations: $a > 0$ and $\dfrac{a^2}{3
} < b^2 < a^2$).
