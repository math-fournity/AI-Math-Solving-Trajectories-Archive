## Proof

**Setup.** The lens has radius 1. Its center $(x,y)$ must keep the lens within t
he wafer, so $(x,y) \in [1, 2009]^2$, a region of area $2008^2$. Every position
is equally likely.

**Key observation — only the containing microchip can be covered.** The wafer is
 tiled by unit squares $[i,i+1]\times[j,j+1]$ for $0 \le i,j \le 2009$. If the l
ens center lies in cell $[i,i+1]\times[j,j+1]$, consider any *neighboring* cell,
 say $[i+1,i+2]\times[j,j+1]$. Its farthest corner from $(x,y)$ is at distance $
\ge \sqrt{(i+2-x)^2 + 0^2} \ge 1$ (since $x \le i+1$), with equality only on a s
et of measure zero. Since complete coverage requires *all four corners* within d
istance 1, no neighboring cell can be covered (except on a null set). By the sam
e argument in all directions, **only the cell containing the lens center can be
completely covered**, with positive probability.

**Reduction to a single cell.** Write $x = i + f_x$, $y = j + f_y$ with $(f_x, f
_y) \in [0,1)^2$. The coverage condition depends only on $(f_x, f_y)$, which is
uniform on $[0,1]^2$. Thus the probability equals the area of the set of $(f_x,f
_y) \in [0,1]^2$ such that the lens centered at $(f_x, f_y)$ completely covers t
he unit square $[0,1]^2$.

**Coverage condition.** The unit square $[0,1]^2$ is completely covered by a uni
t disk centered at $(x,y)$ iff every point of the square is within distance 1, w
hich (since the maximum distance to a convex set is attained at a vertex) requir
es all four corners $(0,0), (1,0), (0,1), (1,1)$ to be within distance 1. So the
 favorable region is:

$$\mathcal{R} = \bigcap_{k \in \{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\}} \overline{D}(k, 1) \
cap [0,1]^2$$

the intersection of four unit disks centered at the corners of the unit square.
(This intersection is automatically contained in $[0,1]^2$, since any point with
 $x > 1$ is at distance $> 1$ from $(0,0)$.)

**Computing the area via four-fold symmetry.** The region is symmetric under $x
\mapsto 1-x$ and $y \mapsto 1-y$. Compute the area in the quadrant $[0,\tfrac{1}
{2}]\times[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]$ and multiply by 4.

In this quadrant, the constraint from corner $(0,0)$ is always satisfied (max di
stance $\sqrt{1/2} < 1$). The three binding constraints are:
- From $(1,0)$: $(1-x)^2 + y^2 \le 1$ — circle centered at $(1,0)$
- From $(0,1)$: $x^2 + (1-y)^2 \le 1$ — circle centered at $(0,1)$
- From $(1,1)$: $(1-x)^2 + (1-y)^2 \le 1$ — circle centered at $(1,1)$

**Finding the vertices.** Pairwise intersections of the boundary circles within
the quadrant:

- Circles $(1,0)$ and $(1,1)$ meet at $(a, \tfrac{1}{2})$ where $a = 1 - \tfrac{
\sqrt{3}}{2}$ (solving $(1-x)^2 + \tfrac{1}{4} = 1$ gives $x = 1 - \tfrac{\sqrt{
3}}{2}$).
- Circles $(0,1)$ and $(1,1)$ meet at $(\tfrac{1}{2}, a)$ by the $x \leftrightar
row y$ symmetry.
- Circles $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ meet at $(0,0)$ (the other intersection $(1,1)$ is
 outside the quadrant).

The curvilinear triangle has vertices $A = (0,0)$, $B = (a, \tfrac{1}{2})$, $C =
 (\tfrac{1}{2}, a)$, with arcs:
- Arc $AB$ on the circle centered at $(1,0)$
- Arc $BC$ on the circle centered at $(1,1)$
- Arc $CA$ on the circle centered at $(0,1)$

**Area = triangle + three circular segments.** Each arc bulges outward from the
triangle (the triangle lies inside all three disks, and the arcs are the disk bo
undaries), so:

$$\text{Area}_{\text{quad}} = \text{Area}(\triangle ABC) + 3 \times \text{Segmen
t}$$

**Triangle area:**
$$\text{Area}(\triangle ABC) = \tfrac{1}{2}\left|\det\begin{pmatrix} a & \tfrac{
1}{2} \\ \tfrac{1}{2} & a \end{pmatrix}\right| = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(a^2 - \tfrac{
1}{4}\right) \cdot (-1) = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(\tfrac{1}{4} - a^2\right)$$

Since $a^2 = (1 - \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2})^2 = \tfrac{7}{4} - \sqrt{3}$, we get $\tf
rac{1}{4} - a^2 = \sqrt{3} - \tfrac{3}{2}$, so:

$$\text{Area}(\triangle ABC) = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - \tfrac{3}{4}$$

**Segment areas.** Each segment has radius $r=1$ and central angle $\theta$. Com
puting the angle for arc $AB$ (center $O_1 = (1,0)$):

$$\cos\theta = \frac{\vec{O_1A}\cdot\vec{O_1B}}{|\vec{O_1A}||\vec{O_1B}|} = \fra
c{(-1,0)\cdot(-\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\tfrac{1}{2})}{1\cdot 1} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
 \implies \theta = \frac{\pi}{6}$$

By the $x \leftrightarrow y$ symmetry, arc $CA$ (center $(0,1)$) also has $\thet
a = \pi/6$. For arc $BC$ (center $(1,1)$):

$$\cos\theta = \frac{(-\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2},-\tfrac{1}{2})\cdot(-\tfrac{1}{2},-\t
frac{\sqrt{3}}{2})}{1} = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}+\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} = \tfrac{\sqrt{
3}}{2} \implies \theta = \frac{\pi}{6}$$

All three central angles equal $\pi/6$. Each segment area:

$$\text{Segment} = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 1^2\left(\tfrac{\pi}{6} - \sin\tfrac{\pi}{6
}\right) = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{6} - \tfrac{1}{2}\right) = \tfrac{\pi}{
12} - \tfrac{1}{4}$$

**Assembling:**
$$\text{Area}_{\text{quad}} = \left(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - \tfrac{3}{4}\right) +
3\left(\tfrac{\pi}{12} - \tfrac{1}{4}\right) = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \tfrac{\pi}
{4} - \tfrac{3}{2}$$

$$\text{Area}(\mathcal{R}) = 4 \cdot \text{Area}_{\text{quad}} = 4\left(\tfrac{\
sqrt{3}}{2} + \tfrac{\pi}{4} - \tfrac{3}{2}\right) = 2\sqrt{3} + \pi - 6$$

**Numerical check:** $2\sqrt{3} + \pi - 6 \approx 3.4641 + 3.1416 - 6 = 0.6057$,
 which lies in $(0,1)$ and is consistent with the geometry (the intersection of
four unit disks is a substantial fraction of the unit square).

**Conclusion.** The probability that the lens completely obscures at least one m
icrochip is:

$$\boxed{2\sqrt{3} + \pi - 6}$$
