## Proof

We are given the simple function

$$s_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n\,2^n} \frac{i-1}{2^n}\,\mathbb{1}_{E_{n,i}} \;+\; n\,\math
bb{1}_{A_n},$$

with

$$E_{n,i} = \Bigl\{x : \tfrac{i-1}{2^n} \le f(x) < \tfrac{i}{2^n}\Bigr\}, \qquad
 A_n = \{x : f(x) \ge n\},$$

and we must evaluate $\int s_4\,d\mu$ for Lebesgue measure $\mu$.

### Step 1 — Substitute $n=4$

$$2^4 = 16, \qquad 4\cdot 2^4 = 64.$$

Hence

$$s_4 = \sum_{i=1}^{64} \frac{i-1}{16}\,\mathbb{1}_{E_{4,i}} \;+\; 4\,\mathbb{1}
_{A_4},$$

where

$$E_{4,i} = \Bigl\{x : \tfrac{i-1}{16} \le f(x) < \tfrac{i}{16}\Bigr\},\quad i=1
,\dots,64, \qquad A_4 = \{x : f(x) \ge 4\}.$$

### Step 2 — Disjointness of the level sets

The sets $E_{4,1}, E_{4,2}, \dots, E_{4,64}$ correspond to the half-open interva
ls

$$\bigl[\tfrac{0}{16},\tfrac{1}{16}\bigr),\;\bigl[\tfrac{1}{16},\tfrac{2}{16}\bi
gr),\;\dots,\;\bigl[\tfrac{63}{16},\tfrac{64}{16}\bigr) = \bigl[\tfrac{63}{16},4
\bigr),$$

which are pairwise disjoint, and $A_4 = [4,\infty)$ is disjoint from all of them
. Therefore

$$E_{4,1},\,E_{4,2},\,\dots,\,E_{4,64},\,A_4$$

are pairwise disjoint measurable sets (assuming $f$ is measurable, which is the
standing hypothesis of this construction).

### Step 3 — Integrate the simple function

A simple function written as $\sum_j c_j\,\mathbb{1}_{B_j}$ with the $B_j$ pairw
ise disjoint has Lebesgue integral $\sum_j c_j\,\mu(B_j)$. Applying this to $s_4
$:

$$\boxed{\int s_4\,d\mu \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{64} \frac{i-1}{16}\;\mu\!\Bigl(\Bigl\{
x : \tfrac{i-1}{16} \le f(x) < \tfrac{i}{16}\Bigr\}\Bigr) \;+\; 4\;\mu\bigl(\{x
: f(x) \ge 4\}\bigr).}$$

### Step 4 — Interpretation

This is the standard lower approximation to $\int f\,d\mu$ in the construction o
f the Lebesgue integral for a non-negative measurable function $f$:

- The range $[0,4)$ is partitioned into $64$ subintervals of width $1/16$, and o
n each subinterval $f$ is approximated from below by its left endpoint $\frac{i-
1}{16}$.
- The tail $\{f \ge 4\}$ is approximated by the constant $4$.

As $n\to\infty$, $s_n \nearrow f$ pointwise and $\int s_n\,d\mu \nearrow \int f\
,d\mu$ by the Monotone Convergence Theorem. For $n=4$ we obtain precisely the bo
xed expression above — a lower Riemann-type sum with mesh $1/16$ and truncation
level $4$.

No specific $f$ is given in the problem, so the integral cannot be reduced to a
single number; its value is the explicit sum of measures of the level sets of $f
$ shown above.
