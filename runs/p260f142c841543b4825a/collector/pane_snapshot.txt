## Proof

Place the triangle in a coordinate system. Since $AB = AC$, the triangle is isos
celes with apex $A$. Set
$$B = (-b, 0),\quad C = (b, 0),\quad A = (0, a)$$
for $a, b > 0$.

**Midpoint $E$ of $AC$:**
$$E = \left(\frac{b}{2},\, \frac{a}{2}\right).$$

**Point $D$ on $BC$ with $3\,CD = BC$:** Since $BC = 2b$, we have $CD = \tfrac{2
b}{3}$. Moving from $C = (b,0)$ toward $B = (-b,0)$ by $\tfrac{2b}{3}$ gives
$$D = \left(b - \frac{2b}{3},\, 0\right) = \left(\frac{b}{3},\, 0\right).$$

**Orthocenter $H$:** The altitude from $A$ to $BC$ is the $y$-axis ($x = 0$). Th
e side $AC$ has slope $-\tfrac{a}{b}$, so the altitude from $B$ has slope $\tfra
c{b}{a}$; its equation is $y = \tfrac{b}{a}(x + b)$. Setting $x = 0$:
$$H = \left(0,\, \frac{b^{2}}{a}\right).$$

**Direction vectors:**
$$\overrightarrow{BE} = E - B = \left(\frac{3b}{2},\, \frac{a}{2}\right) \sim (3
b,\, a),$$
$$\overrightarrow{HD} = D - H = \left(\frac{b}{3},\, -\frac{b^{2}}{a}\right).$$

**Dot product:**
$$\overrightarrow{BE}\cdot\overrightarrow{HD} = (3b)\!\left(\frac{b}{3}\right) +
 (a)\!\left(-\frac{b^{2}}{a}\right) = b^{2} - b^{2} = 0.$$

Since the dot product of the direction vectors of $BE$ and $HD$ is zero, the two
 lines are perpendicular. $\blacksquare$
