**Setting up the residue computation.**

The integrand is $f(z) = (z-3)\sin\!\left(\frac{1}{z+2}\right)$. The only singul
arity is at $z_0 = -2$, where $\frac{1}{z+2}$ has a pole and $\sin$ of it has an
 **essential singularity**. Since $R \geq 4 > 2$, the point $z_0 = -2$ lies stri
ctly inside the circle $|z|=R$. No other singularities exist, so by the residue
theorem

$$I = \operatorname{Res}_{z=-2} (z-3)\sin\!\left(\frac{1}{z+2}\right).$$

**Change of variable.** Set $w = z+2$, so $z = w-2$ and $z - 3 = w - 5$. The res
idue at $z=-2$ becomes the residue at $w=0$:

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=-2} f(z) = \operatorname{Res}_{w=0}\, (w-5)\sin\!\left(\
frac{1}{w}\right).$$

**Laurent expansion of $\sin(1/w)$ at $w=0$.** Using the Taylor series of $\sin$
,

$$\sin\!\left(\frac{1}{w}\right) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{(2n+1)!}\,
w^{-(2n+1)}.$$

**Extracting the $w^{-1}$ coefficient of $(w-5)\sin(1/w)$.** Split the product:

$$w\sin\!\left(\frac{1}{w}\right) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{(2n+1)!}\,
 w^{-2n}.$$

The powers here are $w^{0}, w^{-2}, w^{-4}, \dots$ — all even. There is **no** $
w^{-1}$ term, so this part contributes $0$ to the residue.

$$-5\sin\!\left(\frac{1}{w}\right) = -5\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{(2n+1)!
}\, w^{-(2n+1)}.$$

The $w^{-1}$ term occurs when $2n+1 = 1$, i.e. $n = 0$, giving coefficient

$$-5 \cdot \frac{(-1)^0}{1!} = -5.$$

**Combining.** The residue is

$$\operatorname{Res}_{w=0}\,(w-5)\sin\!\left(\frac{1}{w}\right) = 0 + (-5) = -5.
$$

**Conclusion.**

$$I = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=R} (z-3)\sin\!\left(\frac{1}{z+2}\right)dz = \bo
xed{-5}.$$
