## Proof

Label the vertices of the regular 3600-gon as $0, 1, \dots, 3599$ around the cir
cle.

**Regular $k$-gons inside a regular $n$-gon.** A regular $k$-gon inscribed in th
e circle has vertices at angles $\frac{360j}{k}$, $j=0,\dots,k-1$. For these to
be vertices of the $n$-gon (at angles $\frac{360i}{n}$), we need $j\cdot\frac{n}
{k}\in\mathbb Z$ for all $j$, hence $k\mid n$. Both $40\mid 3600$ (step $90$) an
d $72\mid 3600$ (step $50$) hold.

**Parametrizing the 40-gons.** A regular 40-gon is a coset of
$$H=\langle 90\rangle=\{0,90,180,\dots,3510\},\quad |H|=40.$$
The distinct 40-gons are $s+H$ for $s\in\{0,1,\dots,89\}$, giving $3600/40=90$ t
otal.

**The red 72-gon.** The red vertices form a coset of
$$K=\langle 50\rangle=\{0,50,100,\dots,3550\},\quad |K|=72,$$
namely $r+K$ for some fixed $r$.

**When does a 40-gon meet the red 72-gon?** The cosets $s+H$ and $r+K$ intersect
 iff $s-r\in H+K$. Since
$$H+K=\langle \gcd(90,50)\rangle=\langle 10\rangle,$$
intersection occurs iff $10\mid(s-r)$, i.e. $s\equiv r\pmod{10}$.

**Counting.** Among $s=0,1,\dots,89$, those with $s\equiv r\pmod{10}$ are $a, a+
10, \dots, a+80$ where $a=r\bmod 10$ — exactly $9$ values (since $a+80\le 89 < a
+90$). So the number of 40-gons **disjoint** from the red 72-gon is
$$90 - 9 = 81.$$

**Consistency check.** $H\cap K=\langle\operatorname{lcm}(90,50)\rangle=\langle
450\rangle$ has order $3600/450=8$. Each intersecting 40-gon shares $|H\cap K|=8
$ red vertices. The $9$ intersecting 40-gons account for $9\cdot 8=72$ red verti
ces, and each red vertex lies in exactly one 40-gon (the unique coset $s+H$ with
 $s\equiv v\pmod{90}$), confirming $72/8=9$. ✓

The count is independent of which 72-gon is painted red.
