## Setting Up the Structure of $f$

Write any positive integer as $2^b \cdot m$ with $m$ odd.

**Odd input** ($b=0$): $m = 2n-1$, so $f(m) = 2^n = 2^{(m+1)/2}$.

**Even input** ($b \geq 1$): $2n = 2^b m$, so $n = 2^{b-1}m$ and $d(n) = m$. The
n:
$$f(2^b m) = 2^{b-1}m + \frac{2^b m}{m} = 2^{b-1}m + 2^b = 2^{b-1}(m+2).$$

Since $m$ is odd, $m+2$ is odd, so $f$ maps $2^b m \mapsto 2^{b-1}(m+2)$.

## Injectivity of $f$

- **Two odd inputs** $m_1 \neq m_2$: outputs $2^{(m_1+1)/2} \neq 2^{(m_2+1)/2}$.
 ✓
- **Two even inputs** $2^{b_1}m_1 \neq 2^{b_2}m_2$: outputs $2^{b_1-1}(m_1+2)$ a
nd $2^{b_2-1}(m_2+2)$. Since $m_i+2$ are odd, equality forces $b_1=b_2$ and $m_1
=m_2$. ✓
- **Odd vs. even**: $f(\text{odd}) = 2^{\text{power}}$ (pure power of 2), while
$f(\text{even}) = 2^{b-1}\cdot(m+2)$ with $m+2 \geq 3$ odd — never a pure power
of 2. So these never collide. ✓

**$f$ is injective.** Since the sequence visits $2^n$ for all $n$ (shown below),

997 appears at most once.
