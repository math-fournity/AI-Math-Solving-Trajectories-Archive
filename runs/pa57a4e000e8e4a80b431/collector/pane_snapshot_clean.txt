## Proof

The statement is **true** for any $n > 1$ (indeed, for any $n \geq 1$).

Let $x_0 \in X$ be the basepoint, so $\pi_n(X) = \pi_n(X, x_0)$. We regard $f$ a
s a based map $f: (S^n, s_0) \to (X, x_0)$ and identify $S^n = \partial D^{n+1}$
.

---

### $(\Leftarrow)$: $f$ extends to $D^{n+1}$ $\Rightarrow$ $[f] = 1$.

Suppose $F: D^{n+1} \to X$ is a continuous map with $F|_{\partial D^{n+1}} = f$.
 Define a homotopy

$$G: S^n \times I \to X, \qquad G(x, t) = F\big((1-t)\,x + t\,s_0\big).$$

Since $|(1-t)x + t\,s_0| \leq (1-t)|x| + t|s_0| = 1$, the argument lies in $D^{n
+1}$, so $G$ is well-defined and continuous. We check:

- **At $t = 0$:** $G(x, 0) = F(x) = f(x)$.
- **At $t = 1$:** $G(x, 1) = F(s_0) = f(s_0) = x_0$, the constant map at the bas
epoint.
- **Based throughout:** $G(s_0, t) = F(s_0) = x_0$ for all $t$.

Thus $G$ is a **based** null-homotopy from $f$ to the constant map, so $[f] = 1
\in \pi_n(X, x_0)$.

---

### $(\Rightarrow)$: $[f] = 1$ $\Rightarrow$ $f$ extends to $D^{n+1}$.

Since $[f] = 1$, there exists a based homotopy $H: S^n \times I \to X$ with

$$H(x, 0) = f(x), \qquad H(x, 1) = x_0, \qquad H(s_0, t) = x_0 \text{ for all }
t.$$

Identify $D^{n+1}$ with the cone on $S^n$ via polar coordinates $(r, \theta) \in
 [0,1] \times S^n$, where $r = 1$ corresponds to $\partial D^{n+1} = S^n$ and $r
 = 0$ corresponds to the center. Define

$$F: D^{n+1} \to X, \qquad F(0) = x_0, \qquad F(r, \theta) = H(\theta,\; 1 - r)
\text{ for } r > 0.$$

**On the boundary** ($r = 1$): $F(1, \theta) = H(\theta, 0) = f(\theta)$, so $F|
_{S^n} = f$. ✓

**Continuity at the center** ($r = 0$): As $r \to 0^+$, we have $F(r, \theta) =
H(\theta, 1 - r) \to H(\theta, 1) = x_0$. Since $S^n \times I$ is compact, $H$ i
s uniformly continuous, so this convergence is uniform in $\theta$. Hence $F$ is
 continuous at the center, and therefore on all of $D^{n+1}$.

Thus $f$ extends to $D^{n+1}$.

---

### Conclusion

Both directions hold for any $n \geq 1$. The key points are:

- **$(\Leftarrow)$**: The linear contraction of $S^n$ to the basepoint $s_0$ *in
side* $D^{n+1}$, composed with the extension $F$, yields a **based** null-homoto
py—no path-connectedness assumption on $X$ is needed because the contraction kee
ps $s_0$ fixed throughout.
- **$(\Rightarrow)$**: A based null-homotopy $H$ assembles into a continuous ext
ension over the cone $D^{n+1} \cong S^n \times I / (S^n \times \{1\})$, since th
e based condition $H(s_0, t) = x_0$ guarantees the constant value at the cone po
int is well-defined and the homotopy collapses the top of the cylinder to a sing
le point.

Therefore, for any $n > 1$ (and indeed for all $n \geq 1$):

$$[f] = 1 \in \pi_n(X) \iff f \text{ extends to } D^{n+1}.$$
