## Solution

**Setting up the parametrization.** The second equation $x^2 + y^2 = 2x$ rewrite
s as $(x-1)^2 + y^2 = 1$, a cylinder of radius $1$ with axis along the $z$-axis.
 Parametrize the circular cross-section:
$$x = 1 + \cos t,\quad y = \sin t,\quad t\in[0,2\pi).$$

The first equation $e^z = x$ gives $z = \ln x = \ln(1+\cos t)$, which requires $
x > 0$ (satisfied on our region).

**Determining the range.** The condition $x \geq \tfrac{3}{2}$ becomes $1+\cos t
 \geq \tfrac{3}{2}$, i.e. $\cos t \geq \tfrac{1}{2}$, so $t \in [-\pi/3,\, \pi/3
]$.

**Computing the speed.** The curve is $\mathbf{r}(t) = \bigl(1+\cos t,\;\sin t,\
;\ln(1+\cos t)\bigr)$, with
$$\mathbf{r}'(t) = \left(-\sin t,\;\cos t,\;\frac{-\sin t}{1+\cos t}\right).$$

Then
$$|\mathbf{r}'(t)|^2 = \sin^2 t + \cos^2 t + \frac{\sin^2 t}{(1+\cos t)^2} = 1 +
 \frac{\sin^2 t}{(1+\cos t)^2}.$$

Using $\sin^2 t = (1-\cos t)(1+\cos t)$:
$$\frac{\sin^2 t}{(1+\cos t)^2} = \frac{1-\cos t}{1+\cos t},$$
so
$$|\mathbf{r}'(t)|^2 = 1 + \frac{1-\cos t}{1+\cos t} = \frac{2}{1+\cos t}.$$

**Evaluating the arc length.**
$$L = \int_{-\pi/3}^{\pi/3}\sqrt{\frac{2}{1+\cos t}}\,dt.$$

By the half-angle identity $1+\cos t = 2\cos^2(t/2)$:
$$\sqrt{\frac{2}{1+\cos t}} = \frac{1}{\cos(t/2)} = \sec(t/2),$$
where $\cos(t/2)>0$ on $[-\pi/3,\pi/3]$.

By symmetry and the substitution $u = t/2$:
$$L = 2\int_0^{\pi/3}\sec(t/2)\,dt = 4\int_0^{\pi/6}\sec u\,du = 4\Bigl[\ln|\sec
 u + \tan u|\Bigr]_0^{\pi/6}.$$

Since $\sec(\pi/6) = \tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}$ and $\tan(\pi/6) = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}
$:
$$\sec(\pi/6)+\tan(\pi/6) = \frac{3}{\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{3},$$

$$L = 4\ln\sqrt{3} = 4\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\ln 3 = 2\ln 3.$$

**The length of the portion of $C$ with $x \geq \tfrac{3}{2}$ is $\boxed{2\ln 3}
$.**
