## Proof

**Claim.** The subspace topology induced on $\mathbb{R}^1$ is the discrete topol
ogy.

**Setup.** Recall the topology on $\mathcal{N}$. A set $V\subseteq\mathcal{N}$ i
s open iff for every point $p\in V$:
- If $p=(x,y)\in\mathcal{H}$ (so $y>0$), there is a Euclidean open disk around $
p$ contained in $V\cap\mathcal{H}$.
- If $p=x\in\mathbb{R}^1$, there exists an open disk $D\subseteq\mathcal{H}$ tan
gent to $\mathbb{R}^1$ at $x$ such that $\{x\}\cup D\subseteq V$.

Equivalently, the topology is generated by the Euclidean open subsets of $\mathc
al{H}$ together with the basic neighborhoods $B(x,D):=\{x\}\cup D$ for $x\in\mat
hbb{R}^1$ and $D$ an open disk in $\mathcal{H}$ tangent to $\mathbb{R}^1$ at $x$
.

**Step 1 — Every singleton $\{x\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^1$ is open in the subspace
topology.**

Fix $x\in\mathbb{R}^1$. Pick any open disk $D\subseteq\mathcal{H}$ tangent to $\
mathbb{R}^1$ at $x$ (e.g. the disk of radius $1$ centered at $(x,1)$). Then $B(x
,D)=\{x\}\cup D$ is a basic open set in $\mathcal{N}$. Intersecting with $\mathb
b{R}^1$:
$$B(x,D)\cap\mathbb{R}^1=\{x\}.$$
Hence $\{x\}$ is open in the subspace topology on $\mathbb{R}^1$.

**Step 2 — Every subset of $\mathbb{R}^1$ is open in the subspace topology.**

Let $A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^1$ be arbitrary. By Step 1, each $\{x\}$ for $x\in A$
is open in the subspace topology. Therefore
$$A=\bigcup_{x\in A}\{x\}$$
is a union of open sets, hence open in the subspace topology.

**Step 3 — The subspace topology is exactly the discrete topology.**

We have shown every subset of $\mathbb{R}^1$ is open in the subspace topology. S
ince the subspace topology is by definition a topology on $\mathbb{R}^1$, and it
 contains every subset, it equals the power set $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^1)$, i.e
. the discrete topology.

**Direct verification (optional, for completeness).** Conversely, every open set
 $V$ of $\mathcal{N}$ can be written as a union of basic open sets: Euclidean op
en sets in $\mathcal{H}$ and sets of the form $B(x,D)=\{x\}\cup D$. The Euclidea
n open sets in $\mathcal{H}$ are disjoint from $\mathbb{R}^1$, so they contribut
e nothing to $V\cap\mathbb{R}^1$. Each $B(x,D)$ contributes exactly $\{x\}$. Hen
ce
$$V\cap\mathbb{R}^1=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^1 : \text{some }B(x,D)\subseteq V\},$$
which is an arbitrary subset of $\mathbb{R}^1$. This confirms that the family $\
{V\cap\mathbb{R}^1:V\text{ open in }\mathcal{N}\}$ is precisely $\mathcal{P}(\ma
thbb{R}^1)$.

**Conclusion.** The topological structure induced on $\mathbb{R}^1$ as a subspac
e of the Nemytskii space $\mathcal{N}$ is the **discrete topology**: every subse
t of $\mathbb{R}^1$ is open (and hence also closed). In particular, every single
ton $\{x\}$ for $x\in\mathbb{R}^1$ is an isolated point of the subspace.
