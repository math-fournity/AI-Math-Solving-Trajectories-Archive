## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. For every finite abelian group $H$, there exists a non-abelian g
roup $G$ with $Z(G) \cong H$.

We handle two cases.

### Case 1: $H$ is trivial

Take $G = S_3$. This is non-abelian, and $Z(S_3) = \{e\} \cong H$. ✓

### Case 2: $H$ is non-trivial

**Key construction.** For any commutative ring $R$ with $1 \neq 0$, define $UT(3
, R)$ as the group of $3 \times 3$ upper unitriangular matrices over $R$:
$$UT(3,R) = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} 1 & a & c \\ 0 & 1 & b \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pma
trix} : a, b, c \in R \right\}.$$

**$UT(3,R)$ is non-abelian.** Take $X = \begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{p
matrix}$ and $Y = \begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&1\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}$. The $(1,3)$-
entry of $XY$ is $1$ while that of $YX$ is $0$, so $XY \neq YX$.

**Center computation.** Let $X = \begin{pmatrix}1&a&c\\0&1&b\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}
$ be fixed and $Y = \begin{pmatrix}1&x&z\\0&1&y\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}$ vary. Direc
t multiplication gives:
- $(1,3)$-entry of $XY$: $z + ay + c$
- $(1,3)$-entry of $YX$: $c + xb + z$

The other entries always agree, so $X$ is central iff $ay = xb$ for all $x, y \i
n R$. Setting $y=0, x=1$ gives $b=0$; setting $x=0, y=1$ gives $a=0$. Thus:
$$Z(UT(3,R)) = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & c \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{
pmatrix} : c \in R \right\} \cong (R, +).$$

For $R = \mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$ (with $p$ prime, $k \geq 1$), the additive gr
oup $(R,+) \cong \mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$, and $UT(3, \mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z})
$ is non-abelian. So:

$$Z\!\left(UT(3,\, \mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z})\right) \cong \mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{
Z}.$$

**Assembling $H$.** By the structure theorem for finite abelian groups, since $H
$ is non-trivial:
$$H \cong \mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z} \times A$$
for some prime $p$, integer $k \geq 1$, and finite abelian group $A$.

Set $G_0 = UT(3, \mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z})$ and $G = G_0 \times A$. Then:

- **$G$ is non-abelian:** $G_0$ is non-abelian, so the direct product $G_0 \time
s A$ is non-abelian.
- **$Z(G) \cong H$:** Using the standard fact $Z(G_1 \times G_2) = Z(G_1) \times
 Z(G_2)$ and $Z(A) = A$ (since $A$ is abelian):
$$Z(G) = Z(G_0) \times Z(A) \cong \mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z} \times A \cong H.$$

### Conclusion

In both cases, we have constructed a non-abelian group $G$ with $Z(G) \cong H$.
Therefore, **for every finite abelian group $H$, there exists a non-abelian grou
p $G$ such that $Z(G) \cong H$**. $\blacksquare$
