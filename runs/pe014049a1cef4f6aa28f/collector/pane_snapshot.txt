## Solution

**Setting up the hexagon.** A regular hexagon centered at the origin with opposi
te sides 1 unit apart has apothem $1/2$. Since apothem $= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}s$ f
or side length $s$, we get $s = 1/\sqrt{3}$, and circumradius $r = s = 1/\sqrt{3
}$.

With one pair of sides parallel to the imaginary axis (vertical), the vertical s
ides lie at $x = \pm 1/2$. The vertices are at angles $30°, 90°, 150°, 210°, 270
°, 330°$ with radius $1/\sqrt{3}$:
$$(\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}),\ (0, \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}),\ (-\tfrac{1}{
2}, \tfrac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}),\ \ldots$$

**The inversion $w = 1/z$.** Since the hexagon contains the origin, the exterior
 region $R$ (unbounded, containing $\infty$) maps under $w=1/z$ to a bounded reg
ion $S$ containing $0$ (as $\infty \mapsto 0$). The boundary of $S$ is the image
 of the hexagon's boundary.

**Image of each side.** A line not through the origin maps to a circle through t
he origin. The side at $x = 1/2$: setting $\operatorname{Re}(1/w) = 1/2$ gives $
u/(u^2+v^2) = 1/2$, i.e. $(u-1)^2 + v^2 = 1$ — a circle of radius $1$ centered a
t $(1,0)$, passing through the origin.

The side's endpoints $z = \frac{1}{2} \pm \frac{i}{2\sqrt{3}}$ map to $w = \frac
{3}{2} \mp \frac{i\sqrt{3}}{2}$, and the midpoint $z=1/2$ maps to $w=2$. So the
image is the **outer arc** of this circle (through $(2,0)$), from $\left(\frac{3
}{2}, -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$ to $\left(\frac{3}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\righ
t)$.

**Six-fold symmetry.** The map $w=1/z$ commutes with rotations ($1/(e^{i\theta}z
) = e^{-i\theta}/z$), so $S$ has 6-fold symmetry. The six sides map to six arcs,
 each on a circle of radius $1$ centered at a 6th root of unity $e^{ik\pi/3}$ ($
k=0,\dots,5$). The six vertices of $S$ lie at distance $\sqrt{3}$ from the origi
n (since $|1/z| = \sqrt{3}$ for $|z|=1/\sqrt{3}$), at angles $30°, 90°, \ldots,
330°$.

**Computing the area.** By symmetry, $\text{Area}(S) = 6 \times (\text{area of o
ne sector})$. Consider the sector bounded by the two radii from the origin to th
e vertices at angles $330°$ and $30°$, and the arc of $(u-1)^2+v^2=1$.

Parameterize the arc: $u = 1+\cos\theta$, $v = \sin\theta$, with $\theta \in [-\
pi/3, \pi/3]$ (since $\cos\theta = 1/2$ at the endpoints). Using Green's theorem
, the area is
$$A_{\text{sector}} = \frac{1}{2}\oint (x\,dy - y\,dx).$$

The two radial line segments contribute $0$ (since $x\,dy - y\,dx = 0$ on any li
ne through the origin). On the arc:
$$x\,dy - y\,dx = (1+\cos\theta)\cos\theta\,d\theta + \sin^2\theta\,d\theta = 1
+ \cos\theta.$$

Therefore:
$$A_{\text{sector}} = \frac{1}{2}\int_{-\pi/3}^{\pi/3}(1+\cos\theta)\,d\theta =
\frac{1}{2}\Big[\theta + \sin\theta\Big]_{-\pi/3}^{\pi/3} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\fr
ac{2\pi}{3} + \sqrt{3}\right) = \frac{\pi}{3} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}.$$

**Total area:**
$$\text{Area}(S) = 6\left(\frac{\pi}{3} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) = 2\pi + 3\s
qrt{3} = 2\pi + \sqrt{27}.$$

This matches the form $a\pi + \sqrt{b}$ with $a = 2$ and $b = 27$, both positive
 integers.

**Sanity check:** The inscribed hexagon (vertices at distance $\sqrt{3}$) has ar
ea $\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}\cdot 3 = \frac{9\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 7.79$; the circumsc
ribing circle (radius $2$) has area $4\pi \approx 12.57$. Our area $\approx 11.4
8$ lies between them, consistent with outward-bulging arcs reaching distance $2$
. ✓

$$a + b = 2 + 27 = \boxed{29}$$
