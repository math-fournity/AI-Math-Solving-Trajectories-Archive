## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** A subsequence of $(g_n)$ can be extracted that converges (with
respect to $\delta$) to a constant speed geodesic. The justification rests on th
e compactness of the space of geodesics and the stability of the constant-speed
geodesic property under uniform limits.

---

**Step 1: All geodesics share common endpoints.**

The hypothesis $g_n(0) = g_{n+1}(0)$ and $g_n(1) = g_{n+1}(1)$ for every $n \in
\mathbb{N}$ implies, by transitivity, that there exist points $p, q \in X$ such
that
$$g_n(0) = p \quad \text{and} \quad g_n(1) = q \qquad \text{for all } n.$$

**Step 2: Extract a convergent subsequence via compactness.**

Let $\mathcal{G}(X)$ denote the space of geodesics on $X$ equipped with the metr
ic $\delta(f,g) = \sup_{t \in [0,1]} d(f(t), g(t))$. By hypothesis, $\mathcal{G}
(X)$ is compact. Since $\mathcal{G}(X)$ is a metric space, compactness is equiva
lent to sequential compactness. Therefore, every sequence in $\mathcal{G}(X)$ ad
mits a convergent subsequence.

Applying this to the sequence $(g_n) \subset \mathcal{G}(X)$, there exists a sub
sequence $(g_{n_k})$ and a limit $g \in \mathcal{G}(X)$ such that
$$\delta(g_{n_k}, g) = \sup_{t \in [0,1]} d(g_{n_k}(t), g(t)) \xrightarrow{k \to
 \infty} 0.$$

In particular, $g_{n_k} \to g$ uniformly on $[0,1]$.

**Step 3: The limit $g$ has the same endpoints.**

Uniform convergence implies pointwise convergence. Hence
$$d(g(0), p) = d\!\left(g(0),\, g_{n_k}(0)\right) \le \delta(g, g_{n_k}) \to 0,$
$
so $g(0) = p$. Likewise $g(1) = q$. Thus $g$ shares the common endpoints of the
sequence.

**Step 4: The limit $g$ is a constant speed geodesic.**

Each $g_{n_k}$ is a constant speed geodesic, meaning
$$d\!\left(g_{n_k}(s),\, g_{n_k}(t)\right) = |s - t| \cdot d\!\left(g_{n_k}(0),\
, g_{n_k}(1)\right) = |s - t| \cdot d(p, q)$$
for all $s, t \in [0,1]$.

Fix $s, t \in [0,1]$. By the triangle inequality in $X$,
$$\big|d(g_{n_k}(s), g_{n_k}(t)) - d(g(s), g(t))\big| \le d(g_{n_k}(s), g(s)) +
d(g_{n_k}(t), g(t)) \le 2\,\delta(g_{n_k}, g) \to 0.$$

Therefore,
$$d(g(s), g(t)) = \lim_{k \to \infty} d(g_{n_k}(s), g_{n_k}(t)) = \lim_{k \to \i
nfty} |s - t| \cdot d(p, q) = |s - t| \cdot d(p, q).$$

This holds for all $s, t \in [0,1]$, which is precisely the defining property of
 a constant speed geodesic (a curve parameterized proportionally to arc length).
 Hence $g$ is a constant speed geodesic.

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

The subsequence $(g_{n_k})$ converges to $g$ in $(\mathcal{G}(X), \delta)$, and
$g$ is a constant speed geodesic with the same endpoints $p, q$ as every member
of the original sequence. The extraction is justified entirely by the sequential
 compactness of the (compact) metric space $(\mathcal{G}(X), \delta)$, and the c
onstant-speed property is preserved because it is expressed by an equality of co
ntinuous functions that is stable under uniform limits.

**Remark.** The hypotheses that $\mathcal{G}(X)$ is complete and separable are c
onsistent with compactness (in a metric space, compactness implies both complete
ness and total boundedness, hence separability), but the essential property used
 is compactness, which directly yields sequential compactness.
