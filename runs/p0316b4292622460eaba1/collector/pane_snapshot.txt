## Proof

**Setup.** Let $R: X \to C(E)$ be the restriction map $R(f) = f|_E$.

**$X$ is a Banach space.** By hypothesis $X$ is a Banach subspace of $C(K)$, i.e
., $X$ is complete under the inherited sup-norm. Since $C(K)$ is a Banach space,
 completeness of the subspace $X$ is equivalent to $X$ being closed in $C(K)$.

**$C(E)$ is a Banach space.** Since $K$ is compact and $E \subseteq K$ is closed
, $E$ is compact. Hence $C(E)$ equipped with the supremum norm $\|g\| = \sup_{t
\in E}|g(t)|$ is a Banach space.

**$R$ is a bounded linear operator.** Linearity is immediate. For the norm:
$$\|R(f)\|_{C(E)} = \sup_{t \in E}|f(t)| \le \sup_{t \in K}|f(t)| = \|f\|_{C(K)}
,$$
so $\|R\| \le 1$, and $R$ is bounded.

**$R$ is surjective.** This is exactly the hypothesis: for every $g \in C(E)$, t
here exists $f \in X$ with $f|_E = g$, i.e., $R(f) = g$.

**Applying the Open Mapping Theorem.** We have a bounded, surjective linear map
$R: X \to C(E)$ between Banach spaces. By the Open Mapping Theorem, $R$ is an op
en map. Consequently, there exists a constant $c > 0$ such that for every $g \in
 C(E)$, there exists $f \in X$ with
$$R(f) = g \quad \text{and} \quad \|f\| \le c\,\|g\|.$$

*Explicit derivation of the constant.* Since $R$ is open, $R\big(B_X(0,1)\big)$
is an open neighborhood of $0$ in $C(E)$, so there exists $\delta > 0$ with $B_{
C(E)}(0,\delta) \subseteq R\big(B_X(0,1)\big)$. For any $g \in C(E)$ with $g \ne
 0$, set $g_0 = \frac{\delta}{2}\frac{g}{\|g\|}$, so $\|g_0\| = \delta/2 < \delt
a$. By the inclusion above, there exists $f_0 \in X$ with $\|f_0\| < 1$ and $R(f
_0) = g_0$. Define $f = \frac{2\|g\|}{\delta}\,f_0$. Then
$$R(f) = \frac{2\|g\|}{\delta}\,R(f_0) = \frac{2\|g\|}{\delta}\cdot\frac{\delta}
{2}\cdot\frac{g}{\|g\|} = g,$$
and
$$\|f\| = \frac{2\|g\|}{\delta}\,\|f_0\| < \frac{2}{\delta}\,\|g\|.$$
Taking $c = 2/\delta$ (or any value $\ge 1/\delta$), we obtain $\|f\| \le c\,\|g
\|$. For $g = 0$, take $f = 0$.

**Conclusion.** There exists a constant $c > 0$ such that every $g \in C(E)$ adm
its an extension $f \in X$ with $f|_E = g$ and $\|f\| \le c\,\|g\|$. $\blacksqua
