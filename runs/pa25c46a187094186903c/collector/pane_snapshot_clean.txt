**Answer: No, not in general.**

**Proof.**

We exhibit a counterexample.

Let $X = S^1$ (the unit circle), and let $f \colon S^1 \to S^1$ be the constant
map $f(x) = p$ for some fixed point $p \in S^1$ and all $x \in S^1$.

*Continuity.* A constant map between topological spaces is always continuous.

*Idempotence.* For every $x \in S^1$,
$$(f \circ f)(x) = f(f(x)) = f(p) = p = f(x),$$
so $f \circ f = f$.

*The image.* $f(S^1) = \{p\}$, a single point.

*Non-equivalence.* The image $f(S^1) = \{p\}$ is a point, hence contractible, wi
th $\pi_1(\{p\}) = 0$. On the other hand, $S^1$ has fundamental group $\pi_1(S^1
) \cong \mathbb{Z} \neq 0$. A homotopy equivalence between spaces induces an iso
morphism on all homotopy groups (in particular on $\pi_1$). Since $\pi_1(S^1) \n
ot\cong \pi_1(\{p\})$, the spaces $S^1$ and $\{p\}$ are **not** homotopy equival
ent.

Thus we have a continuous idempotent self-map $f$ of a topological space $X$ suc
h that $X$ is not homotopy equivalent to $f(X)$.

**Remark.** What *is* true is the following. Since $f \circ f = f$, the restrict
ion $f|_{f(X)} \colon f(X) \to f(X)$ is the identity (if $y = f(x) \in f(X)$, th
en $f(y) = f(f(x)) = f(x) = y$). Hence $f(X)$ is a **retract** of $X$: letting $
i \colon f(X) \hookrightarrow X$ be the inclusion, we have $f \circ i = \mathrm{
id}_{f(X)}$ (where $f$ is corestricted to its image). A retract yields a homotop
y equivalence precisely when it is a *deformation* retract, i.e. when $i \circ f
 \simeq \mathrm{id}_X$. The idempotence $f \circ f = f$ alone guarantees only a
retract, not a deformation retract, so homotopy equivalence need not follow. The
 constant-map example above is exactly a retraction that is not a deformation re
traction.
