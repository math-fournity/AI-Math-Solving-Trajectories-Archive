No. $M$ need not be projective over $R$. Here is a counterexample.

**Counterexample.** Let $R = \mathbb{Z}$ and let $A = \mathbb{Z}$ with the stand
ard $\mathbb{Z}$-algebra structure (the identity map $\mathbb{Z} \to A$). Then $
A \cong R$ is free of rank $1$ as an $R$-module, hence projective over $R$.

A right $A$-module is the same thing as a right $\mathbb{Z}$-module, i.e., an ab
elian group. Take $M = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, which is a (right) $A$-module in
 the obvious way.

We claim $M$ is **not** projective as a $\mathbb{Z}$-module. Indeed, over a PID
such as $\mathbb{Z}$, every projective module is free (this is the standard stru
cture theorem: a projective module over a PID is a direct summand of a free modu
le, and over a PID direct summands of free modules are free). A free $\mathbb{Z}
$-module is torsion-free, but $M = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ has torsion (it is ki
lled by $2$). Hence $M$ is not free, and therefore not projective, over $\mathbb
{Z} = R$.

Thus we have exhibited an $R$-projective algebra $A$ and a right $A$-module $M$
that is not projective over $R$, so the answer to the question is **no**: $M$ is
 not automatically projective as a right $R$-module. $\blacksquare$
