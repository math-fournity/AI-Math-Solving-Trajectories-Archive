Looking at this problem, I need to find the area on the circumscribed sphere whe
re the sum of spherical distances to P, Q, R, S is ≥ 6π.

## Step 1: Identify the Circumscribed Sphere

In triangle QRS: ∠QRS = 50°, ∠QSR = 40°, so ∠RQS = 90° (right angle at Q).
In triangle PRS: ∠PSR = 50°, ∠PRS = 40°, so ∠SPR = 90° (right angle at P).

By **Thales' theorem**, since ∠SPR = 90° and ∠SQR = 90°, both P and Q lie on the
 sphere with diameter SR. Together with R and S (endpoints of the diameter), all
 four vertices lie on the sphere centered at the midpoint M of SR with radius R
= SR/2 = 3.

Since the four points are non-coplanar (P and Q are at different azimuthal posit
ions, as PQ = 4 ≠ 0), this is the unique circumscribed sphere: **radius 3, cente
r at midpoint of SR**.

## Step 2: Exploit Antipodality of R and S

R and S are endpoints of a diameter, so they are **antipodal** on the circumscri
bed sphere. For any point X on the sphere, the spherical distance to R plus the
spherical distance to S equals half the circumference:

$$d(X,R) + d(X,S) = 3\pi$$

This holds because the central angles satisfy α(X,R) + α(X,S) = π (always true f
or antipodal points), and spherical distance = 3 × central angle.

## Step 3: Reduce the Condition

The sum of spherical distances is:
$$d(X,P) + d(X,Q) + d(X,R) + d(X,S) = 3\bigl(\alpha(X,P) + \alpha(X,Q) + \pi\big
r)$$

The condition "≥ 6π" becomes:
$$\alpha(X,P) + \alpha(X,Q) + \pi \geq 2\pi \iff \alpha(X,P) + \alpha(X,Q) \geq
\pi$$

## Step 4: Antipodal Symmetry Gives Exactly Half the Sphere

Consider the antipodal map X → X' (the point diametrically opposite X). This is
an isometry of the sphere. For any point X:

$$\alpha(X', P) = \pi - \alpha(X, P), \qquad \alpha(X', Q) = \pi - \alpha(X, Q)$
$

Therefore:
$$\alpha(X',P) + \alpha(X',Q) = 2\pi - \bigl(\alpha(X,P) + \alpha(X,Q)\bigr)$$

This means:
- If α(X,P) + α(X,Q) > π, then α(X',P) + α(X',Q) < π.
- If α(X,P) + α(X,Q) < π, then α(X',P) + α(X',Q) > π.

The antipodal map is a bijection that swaps the region {sum > π} with the region
 {sum < π}. Since it is an isometry, **both regions have equal area**.

The boundary {α(X,P) + α(X,Q) = π} is a continuous curve (a spherical ellipse wi
th foci P, Q), which has measure zero on the 2-sphere.

## Step 5: Compute the Area

The total surface area of the sphere is:
$$4\pi R^2 = 4\pi \cdot 9 = 36\pi$$

By the symmetry above, the region where α(X,P) + α(X,Q) ≥ π has exactly half thi
s area:

$$\boxed{18\pi}$$
