## Solution

**Contour analysis.** The contour is $\gamma(t) = t + i\sqrt{\pi^2 - t^2}$ for $
-\pi \le t \le \pi$. Writing $x = t$ and $y = \sqrt{\pi^2 - t^2}$, we have
$$x^2 + y^2 = t^2 + (\pi^2 - t^2) = \pi^2,$$
with $y \ge 0$. Thus $C$ is the **upper semicircle** of radius $\pi$ centered at
 the origin, traversed from $-\pi$ (at $t=-\pi$) to $\pi$ (at $t=\pi$), i.e. cou
nterclockwise through the upper half-plane.

**Antiderivative.** The integrand $f(z) = \cos(z/2)$ is entire (analytic on all
of $\mathbb{C}$). An antiderivative is
$$F(z) = 2\sin(z/2), \qquad F'(z) = \cos(z/2).$$

**Applying the fundamental theorem.** Since $f$ is entire, the integral depends
only on the endpoints of the contour:
$$\int_C \cos(z/2)\,dz = F(\pi) - F(-\pi) = 2\sin(\pi/2) - 2\sin(-\pi/2).$$

Using $\sin(\pi/2) = 1$ and $\sin(-\pi/2) = -1$:
$$\int_C \cos(z/2)\,dz = 2(1) - 2(-1) = 2 + 2 = 4.$$

**Result.**
$$\boxed{\int_C \cos(z/2)\,dz = 4}$$
