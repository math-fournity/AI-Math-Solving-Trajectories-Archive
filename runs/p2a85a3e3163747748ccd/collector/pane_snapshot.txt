## Proof

**Step 1: Identify the base ring.**

Here $\pi$ and $\sqrt{11}$ are real numbers adjoined to $\mathbb{Q}$, while $x,
y$ are indeterminates. So
$$\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{11},x,y] = \mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{11}][x,y].$$

**Step 2: Simplify $\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{11}]$.**

- $\sqrt{11}$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$ with minimal polynomial $t^2 - 11$
(irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ by Eisenstein at $p=11$). Thus $\mathbb{Q}[\sqrt{
11}] = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{11})$, a field.
- $\pi$ is transcendental over $\mathbb{Q}$. Since $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{11})/\mathb
b{Q}$ is algebraic, $\pi$ remains transcendental over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{11})$.
- Therefore $\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{11}] \cong \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{11})[\pi]$, a poly
nomial ring in one variable over the field $k := \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{11})$.

**Step 3: Identify the full polynomial ring.**

$$\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{11},x,y] \cong k[\pi, x, y],$$
a polynomial ring in **3 variables** over the field $k$. Hence $\dim(k[\pi,x,y])
 = 3$.

**Step 4: Show $f = x^2 - y^3 + xy + 3$ is irreducible in $k[\pi,x,y]$.**

Since $f$ does not involve $\pi$, irreducibility in $k[\pi,x,y]$ is equivalent t
o irreducibility in $k[x,y]$.

View $f$ as a polynomial in $x$ over $k[y]$:
$$f = x^2 + xy + (3 - y^3), \quad \deg_x f = 2.$$

If $f$ factors in $k[x,y]$, either:
- *(a)* $f = g(y)\cdot h(x,y)$ with $\deg_x g = 0$: The leading coefficient of $
x^2$ in $f$ is $1$, so the $x^2$-coefficient of $h$ is $1/g(y)$, forcing $g(y)$
to be a unit. No nontrivial such factor exists.
- *(b)* $f$ splits into two factors each linear in $x$: This requires the discri
minant
$$\Delta = y^2 - 4(3-y^3) = 4y^3 + y^2 - 12$$
to be a perfect square in $k[y]$. But $\deg \Delta = 3$ is odd, and a perfect sq
uare polynomial has even degree. Contradiction.

So $f$ is irreducible in $k[x,y]$, hence in $k[\pi,x,y]$.

**Step 5: Compute the dimension.**

Since $k[\pi,x,y]$ is a UFD, the irreducible element $f$ generates a prime ideal
 $\langle f \rangle$ of height $1$ (by Krull's principal ideal theorem, and heig
ht is exactly $1$ since $f$ is not a unit).

For a polynomial ring over a field, the dimension formula holds:
$$\operatorname{ht}(P) + \dim(R/P) = n,$$
where $R = k[\pi,x,y]$, $n=3$, and $P = \langle f \rangle$ with $\operatorname{h
t}(P)=1$.

Therefore:
$$\dim(A) = \dim\!\left(k[\pi,x,y]/\langle f\rangle\right) = 3 - 1 = \boxed{2}.$
$
