# Commutator of Covariant Derivatives on a Vector Field

## Setup

Let $\nabla_\mu$ be the covariant derivative associated with a connection $\Gamm
a^\rho_{\mu\nu}$ (torsion-free or not — the result below holds generally). Actin
g on a vector field $V^\rho$,

$$\nabla_\mu V^\rho = \partial_\mu V^\rho + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} V^\sigma.$$

We compute the commutator $[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu] V^\rho = \nabla_\mu \nabla_\
nu V^\rho - \nabla_\nu \nabla_\mu V^\rho$.

## First covariant derivative

$\nabla_\nu V^\rho$ is a $(1,1)$-tensor. Its covariant derivative is

$$\nabla_\mu(\nabla_\nu V^\rho) = \partial_\mu(\nabla_\nu V^\rho) + \Gamma^\rho_
{\mu\lambda}(\nabla_\nu V^\lambda) - \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}(\nabla_\lambda V^\r
ho).$$

The three terms correspond to: the upper index $\rho$ (contravariant, $+$), the
lower index $\nu$ (covariant, $-$), and the derivative index $\mu$ itself (no te
rm).

## Expand

$$\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu V^\rho = \partial_\mu\partial_\nu V^\rho + (\partial_\mu\
Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma})V^\sigma + \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma}\partial_\mu V^\sigma +
 \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\partial_\nu V^\lambda + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma
^\lambda_{\nu\sigma}V^\sigma - \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}\partial_\lambda V^\rho -
\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}\Gamma^\rho_{\lambda\sigma}V^\sigma.$$

## Antisymmetrize $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$

Subtract the same expression with $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$. Track which terms c
ancel:

- $\partial_\mu\partial_\nu V^\rho - \partial_\nu\partial_\mu V^\rho = 0$ (parti
al derivatives commute).
- $\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma}\partial_\mu V^\sigma - \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma}\partia
l_\nu V^\sigma$ and the symmetric partner $\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\partial_\nu
V^\lambda - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\partial_\mu V^\lambda$ **cancel pairwise**
(rename dummy $\sigma \leftrightarrow \lambda$).
- $-\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}\partial_\lambda V^\rho + \Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\mu}\par
tial_\lambda V^\rho = -(\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\mu})\parti
al_\lambda V^\rho = -T^\lambda_{\mu\nu}\,\nabla_\lambda V^\rho$ (using $\partial
_\lambda V^\rho = \nabla_\lambda V^\rho$ at leading order; more precisely this i
s the **torsion** contribution).

The surviving $V^\sigma$ terms:

$$\big[(\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma
}) + (\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lamb
da}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma})\big] V^\sigma - (\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma
^\lambda_{\nu\mu})\,\nabla_\lambda V^\rho.$$

## Identify the curvature tensor

The bracket is precisely the **Riemann curvature tensor** (in the convention wit
h all upper-lower index structure):

$$R^\rho_{\ \sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\G
amma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} - \Ga
mma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma}.$$

The remaining piece is the torsion $T^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}
 - \Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\mu}$.

## Result

$$\boxed{[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu] V^\rho = R^\rho_{\ \sigma\mu\nu}\,V^\sigma - T
^\lambda_{\mu\nu}\,\nabla_\lambda V^\rho.}$$

### Specialization to the Levi-Civita connection

For the torsion-free Christoffel connection ($\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = \Gamma^\
lambda_{\nu\mu}$, so $T^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = 0$), this reduces to the standard ide
ntity

$$\boxed{[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu] V^\rho = R^\rho_{\ \sigma\mu\nu}\,V^\sigma.}$$

## Verification of index structure

- $R^\rho_{\ \sigma\mu\nu}$ carries indices $(\rho\text{ up}, \sigma\text{ down}
, \mu,\nu\text{ down})$, antisymmetric in $\mu\leftrightarrow\nu$ by constructio
n. Contracted with $V^\sigma$ (down $\sigma$) gives an object with free indices
$(\rho\text{ up}, \mu,\nu\text{ down})$ — matching $[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]V^\rh
o$. ✓
- Antisymmetry $[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu] = -[\nabla_\nu,\nabla_\mu]$ matches $R^\
rho_{\ \sigma\mu\nu} = -R^\rho_{\ \sigma\nu\mu}$. ✓
- For a scalar $\phi$ (no upper index), the same computation gives $[\nabla_\mu,
\nabla_\nu]\phi = -T^\lambda_{\mu\nu}\nabla_\lambda\phi$, consistent with $R^\rh
o_{\ \sigma\mu\nu}$ acting only on tensor indices — a sanity check that the curv
ature term genuinely targets the vector index. ✓
