## Solution

We determine the distributional limit of $f_n = n^2\chi_{[-1/n,0)} - n^2\chi_{(0
,1/n]}$ by testing against an arbitrary $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$.

**Pairing with a test function.** For $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$,

$$\langle f_n, \varphi\rangle = n^2\int_{-1/n}^{0}\varphi(x)\,dx \;-\; n^2\int_{
0}^{1/n}\varphi(x)\,dx.$$

**Taylor expansion about the origin.** Since $\varphi$ is smooth, for $|x|\le 1/
n$:

$$\varphi(x) = \varphi(0) + \varphi'(0)\,x + \tfrac{1}{2}\varphi''(0)\,x^2 + O(x
^3).$$

Integrating term by term:

$$\int_{-1/n}^{0}\varphi(x)\,dx = \frac{\varphi(0)}{n} - \frac{\varphi'(0)}{2n^2
} + \frac{\varphi''(0)}{6n^3} + O(n^{-4}),$$

$$\int_{0}^{1/n}\varphi(x)\,dx = \frac{\varphi(0)}{n} + \frac{\varphi'(0)}{2n^2}
 + \frac{\varphi''(0)}{6n^3} + O(n^{-4}).$$

**Substituting.** The $\varphi(0)/n$ and $\varphi''(0)/(6n^3)$ terms cancel, whi
le the $\varphi'(0)$ terms add:

$$\langle f_n, \varphi\rangle = n^2\!\left(-\frac{\varphi'(0)}{n^2} + O(n^{-4})\
right) = -\varphi'(0) + O(n^{-2}).$$

**Taking the limit.** Therefore

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle f_n,\varphi\rangle = -\varphi'(0).$$

**Identifying the distribution.** The derivative of the Dirac delta, $\delta'$,
acts on test functions by

$$\langle \delta', \varphi\rangle = -\langle \delta, \varphi'\rangle = -\varphi'
(0).$$

This matches the limit above for every test function $\varphi$.

**Conclusion.** Since $\langle f_n,\varphi\rangle \to \langle \delta',\varphi\ra
ngle$ for all $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$,

$$f_n \xrightarrow{\;\mathcal{D}'\;} \delta'.$$

The sequence $f_n$ converges in the sense of distributions to the **derivative o
f the Dirac delta distribution**, $\delta'$.
