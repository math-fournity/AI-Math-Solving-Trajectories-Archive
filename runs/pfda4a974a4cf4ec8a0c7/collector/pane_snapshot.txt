## Proof

We compute

$$S = \sum_{x=0}^{p-1}\left(\frac{x(x+k)}{p}\right),$$

where $\left(\frac{\cdot}{p}\right)$ is the Legendre symbol and $\gcd(p,k)=1$ (s
o $k\not\equiv 0 \pmod p$).

**Step 1: Multiplicativity and the zero terms.**

The Legendre symbol is multiplicative, so for every $x$,

$$\left(\frac{x(x+k)}{p}\right) = \left(\frac{x}{p}\right)\left(\frac{x+k}{p}\ri
ght).$$

Let $\chi$ denote the quadratic character modulo $p$, extended by $\chi(0)=0$. T
hen

$$S = \sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_p} \chi(x)\,\chi(x+k).$$

The term at $x=0$ equals $\chi(0)\chi(k)=0$, and the term at $x=-k$ equals $\chi
(-k)\chi(0)=0$. Hence

$$S = \sum_{\substack{x\in\mathbb{F}_p\\ x\neq 0,\; x\neq -k}} \chi(x)\,\chi(x+k
).$$

**Step 2: Change of variable.**

For $x\neq 0$ and $x\neq -k$, both $x$ and $x+k$ are nonzero. Set

$$w = \frac{x+k}{x} = 1 + \frac{k}{x}.$$

Since $x\mapsto k/x$ is a bijection of $\mathbb{F}_p^{\ast}$ onto itself, the qu
antity $1+k/x$ ranges over $\{1+v : v\in\mathbb{F}_p^{\ast}\} = \mathbb{F}_p\set
minus\{1\}$. The exclusion $x\neq -k$ is equivalent to $w\neq 0$. Therefore, as
$x$ runs over $\mathbb{F}_p\setminus\{0,-k\}$, the parameter $w$ runs over $\mat
hbb{F}_p\setminus\{0,1\}$, and the map is a bijection.

Using multiplicativity and $\chi(x)^2 = 1$ for $x\neq 0$,

$$\chi(x)\,\chi(x+k) = \chi(x)\,\chi(xw) = \chi(x)\,\chi(x)\,\chi(w) = \chi(x)^2
\,\chi(w) = \chi(w).$$

Thus

$$S = \sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_p\setminus\{0,1\}} \chi(w).$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the character sum.**

It is a basic property of the quadratic character that

$$\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_p}\chi(w) = 0,$$

because there are equally many quadratic residues and non-residues modulo $p$ (n
amely $(p-1)/2$ each), and $\chi(0)=0$. Therefore

$$\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_p\setminus\{0\}}\chi(w) = 0 - \chi(0) = 0,$$

and subtracting the $w=1$ term (where $\chi(1)=1$) gives

$$S = \sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_p\setminus\{0,1\}}\chi(w) = 0 - \chi(1) = -1.$$

**Step 4: Verification.**

As a check, take $p=5$, $k=1$:

| $x$ | $x(x+1)\bmod 5$ | $\left(\frac{\cdot}{5}\right)$ |
|-----|------------------|--------------------------------|
| 0   | 0                | 0                              |
| 1   | 2                | $-1$                           |
| 2   | 1                | $+1$                           |
| 3   | 2                | $-1$                           |
| 4   | 0                | 0                              |

The sum is $0-1+1-1+0 = -1$, confirming the result.

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\sum_{x=0}^{p-1}\left(\frac{x(x+k)}{p}\right) = -1}$$

for every prime $p>3$ and every $k\in\mathbb{N}$ with $\gcd(p,k)=1$.
