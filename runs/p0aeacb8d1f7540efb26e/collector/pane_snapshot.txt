# Solution

## Setup

For $P=(a,a^2)$, the line $\ell(P)$ has slope $2a$ and passes through $(a,a^2)$,
 giving equation $y = 2ax - a^2$. This is precisely the **tangent line** to the
parabola $y=x^2$ at $P$.

The three tangent lines are:
$$L_i:\ y = 2a_i x - a_i^2,\quad i=1,2,3.$$

**Pairwise intersections** (vertices of $\Delta$): solving $L_i \cap L_j$ gives
$$V_{ij} = \left(\frac{a_i+a_j}{2},\ a_i a_j\right).$$

Note $V_{12}$ and $V_{23}$ both lie on $L_2$, so the side $V_{12}V_{23}$ lies on
 $L_2$, with direction $(1,2a_2)$. Similarly the other sides lie on $L_1, L_3$.

## Equilateral condition via angles

Let $\theta_i = \arctan(2a_i)$ be the angle of line $L_i$. The interior angle of
 $\Delta$ at vertex $V_{ij}$ is the angle between $L_i$ and $L_j$.

For an equilateral triangle, all interior angles are $60°$. Ordering the three l
ine-angles as $\theta_1 < \theta_2 < \theta_3$ (mod $180°$), the three interior
angles are:
$$\theta_2-\theta_1,\quad \theta_3-\theta_2,\quad 180°-(\theta_3-\theta_1).$$
Setting each to $60°$ yields $\theta_2-\theta_1 = 60°$ and $\theta_3-\theta_2 =
60°$, so the three angles are **equally spaced by $60°$**.

## Parametrization

Set $\varphi = \theta_1$ and define $b_i = 2a_i = \tan\!\big(\varphi + (i{-}1)\t
frac{\pi}{3}\big)$ for $i=1,2,3$. Then $a_i = b_i/2$.

**Centroid of $\Delta$:**
$$x_c = \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{(a_1{+}a_2)+(a_2{+}a_3)+(a_1{+}a_3)}{2} = \frac{a_
1+a_2+a_3}{3} = \frac{b_1+b_2+b_3}{6},$$
$$y_c = \frac{a_1a_2+a_2a_3+a_1a_3}{3} = \frac{b_1b_2+b_2b_3+b_1b_3}{12}.$$

## Vieta's formulas

The values $b_1,b_2,b_3$ are the three roots of $\tan(3x)=\tan(3\varphi)$, i.e.,
 they satisfy
$$\frac{3b-b^3}{1-3b^2} = T,\quad T:=\tan(3\varphi),$$
which rearranges to
$$b^3 - 3T\,b^2 - 3b + T = 0.$$
By Vieta's formulas:
$$b_1+b_2+b_3 = 3T,\qquad b_1b_2+b_2b_3+b_1b_3 = -3,\qquad b_1b_2b_3 = -T.$$

## The locus

Substituting:
$$x_c = \frac{3T}{6} = \frac{T}{2} = \frac{\tan(3\varphi)}{2},\qquad y_c = \frac
{-3}{12} = -\frac{1}{4}.$$

The $y$-coordinate is **constant** $-\tfrac{1}{4}$, while $x_c = \tfrac{1}{2}\ta
n(3\varphi)$ ranges over all of $\mathbb{R}$ as $\varphi$ varies (excluding the
finitely many values where some $a_i\to\infty$, which correspond to $\tan(3\varp
hi)$ passing through $\pm\infty$).

## Verification

Take $\varphi=0$: $a_1=0,\ a_2=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2},\ a_3=-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$. Vert
ices: $(\tfrac{\sqrt3}{4},0)$, $(0,-\tfrac34)$, $(-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{4},0)$. All si
de lengths $= \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ ✓, centroid $= (0,-\tfrac14)$ ✓.

Take $\varphi=\tfrac{\pi}{12}$: $T=\tan 45°=1$, giving $x_c=\tfrac12$, $y_c=-\tf
rac14$ ✓ (verified by direct computation).

## Conclusion

The locus of the center of the equilateral triangle $\Delta$ is the horizontal l
ine

$$\boxed{y = -\dfrac{1}{4}}$$
