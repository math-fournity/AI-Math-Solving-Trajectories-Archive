## Proof

We wish to compute the functional derivative

$$\frac{\delta F[p]}{\delta p(x)}, \qquad F[p] = \int_X p(x)\ln(p(x))\,dx.$$

**Definition.** The functional derivative $\delta F/\delta p(x)$ is defined (in
the sense of Volterra/Fréchet) by the relation

$$\delta F = F[p+\varepsilon\,\eta] - F[p] = \varepsilon \int_X \frac{\delta F[p
]}{\delta p(x)}\,\eta(x)\,dx + o(\varepsilon)$$

for every admissible variation $\eta(x)$ (with $\int_X \eta\,dx = 0$ so that $p+
\varepsilon\eta$ remains a probability density to first order), where $\varepsil
on\to 0$.

**Perturbation.** Introduce $p_\varepsilon(x) = p(x) + \varepsilon\,\eta(x)$. Th
en

$$F[p_\varepsilon] = \int_X p_\varepsilon(x)\ln p_\varepsilon(x)\,dx.$$

Expand the integrand to first order in $\varepsilon$. With $g(u) = u\ln u$, we h
ave $g'(u) = \ln u + 1$, so

$$g\bigl(p+\varepsilon\eta\bigr) = g(p) + \varepsilon\,\eta\,g'(p) + o(\varepsil
on) = p\ln p + \varepsilon\,\eta\,(\ln p + 1) + o(\varepsilon).$$

Therefore

$$F[p_\varepsilon] = \int_X p\ln p\,dx + \varepsilon\int_X \eta(x)\bigl(\ln p(x)
 + 1\bigr)\,dx + o(\varepsilon).$$

Subtracting $F[p]$:

$$\delta F = \varepsilon\int_X \eta(x)\bigl(\ln p(x) + 1\bigr)\,dx + o(\varepsil
on).$$

**Identification.** Comparing with the defining relation

$$\delta F = \varepsilon\int_X \frac{\delta F[p]}{\delta p(x)}\,\eta(x)\,dx + o(
\varepsilon),$$

and using the arbitrariness of the variation $\eta(x)$, the fundamental lemma of

$$\boxed{\dfrac{\delta F[p]}{\delta p(x)} = \ln p(x) + 1.}$$

**Verification (boundary terms).** Since the integrand $f(p)=p\ln p$ depends on
$p$ but not on derivatives of $p$, the general formula for a functional $F[p]=\i
nt f(p)\,dx$ yields

$$\frac{\delta F}{\delta p(x)} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial p} - \frac{d}{dx}\fr
ac{\partial f}{\partial p'} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial p},$$

because $f$ has no $p'$ dependence, so no integration-by-parts/boundary term ari
ses. With $f(p)=p\ln p$,

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial p} = \ln p + 1,$$

confirming the result.

the constraint $\int p\,dx=1$ is preserved by admissible variations ($\int\eta\,
dx=0$). The functional derivative computed above is the *unconstrained* (intrins
