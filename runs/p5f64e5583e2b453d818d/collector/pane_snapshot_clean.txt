**Claim.** The kernel of $\varphi\colon R[T]\to R[w]$, $T\mapsto w=u/v$, is the
$v$-saturation of the principal ideal $(vT-u)$:

$$\ker\varphi \;=\; (vT-u)R[T]:v^{\infty} \;=\; \{\,f\in R[T]\;:\;\exists\,n\ge
0,\; v^{n}f\in (vT-u)R[T]\,\}.$$

---

**Proof.**

Let $S=R[1/v]=R_{v}$ be the localization of $R$ at the multiplicative set $\{1,v
,v^{2},\dots\}$ (this is legitimate since $v\neq 0$ in the domain $R$, as $w=u/v
$ lives in $\operatorname{Frac}(R)$). The map $\varphi$ factors as

$$R[T]\;\longrightarrow\;S[T]\;\xrightarrow{\;\operatorname{ev}\;}\;S,\qquad T\m
apsto u/v,$$

and the image of $R[T]$ in $S$ is exactly $R[u/v]=R[w]$.

**Step 1 — the kernel over $S$.** In $S[T]$ the element $v$ is a unit, so $vT-u$
 is a degree-one polynomial whose leading coefficient is invertible. Hence divis
ion by $vT-u$ is exact in $S[T]$, and the evaluation map $\operatorname{ev}\colo
n S[T]\to S$, $T\mapsto u/v$, has kernel precisely

$$\ker(\operatorname{ev})=(vT-u)\,S[T].$$

**Step 2 — contraction to $R[T]$.** Since $\varphi$ is the restriction of $\oper
atorname{ev}$ to $R[T]$,

$$\ker\varphi \;=\; R[T]\cap\ker(\operatorname{ev}) \;=\; R[T]\cap (vT-u)\,S[T].
 \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Step 3 — identifying the contraction with the saturation.** We prove

$$R[T]\cap (vT-u)\,S[T] \;=\; (vT-u)R[T]:v^{\infty}.$$

*$\subseteq$:* Let $f\in R[T]\cap(vT-u)S[T]$, so $f=(vT-u)\,g$ for some $g\in S[
T]=R[1/v][T]$. Write $g=\sum_{i}g_{i}T^{i}$ with $g_{i}\in R[1/v]$. Choose $n\ge
 0$ large enough that $v^{n}g_{i}\in R$ for every $i$ (clear the finitely many d
enominators). Then $v^{n}g\in R[T]$, and

$$v^{n}f=(vT-u)\,(v^{n}g)\in (vT-u)R[T].$$

Hence $f\in (vT-u)R[T]:v^{\infty}$.

*$\supseteq$:* Let $f\in (vT-u)R[T]:v^{\infty}$, so $v^{n}f=(vT-u)\,h$ for some
$h\in R[T]$ and $n\ge 0$. Passing to $S[T]$ (where $v$ is invertible),

$$f=(vT-u)\,\frac{h}{v^{n}}\in (vT-u)\,S[T].$$

Since also $f\in R[T]$, we get $f\in R[T]\cap(vT-u)S[T]$.

Combining with $(\ast)$:

$$\boxed{\;\ker\varphi \;=\; (vT-u)R[T]:v^{\infty}\;}$$

i.e. $\ker\varphi=\{\,f\in R[T]:\exists\,n\ge 0,\ v^{n}f\in(vT-u)R[T]\,\}$.

**Remarks.**

1. **The integrally-closed hypothesis is not needed for the equality.** The argu
ment uses only that $R$ is a domain (so that $v\neq 0$ and $u/v\in\operatorname{
Frac}(R)$ make sense, and $S=R[1/v]$ is a localization). The same formula $\ker\
varphi=(vT-u)R[T]:v^{\infty}$ holds for any commutative ring in which $v$ is a n
on-zerodivisor. The integrally-closed assumption is natural context (it governs
further properties of $R[w]$, e.g. whether $w$ is integral over $R$) but does no
t change the kernel.

2. **When the kernel collapses to $(vT-u)$.** One has $\ker\varphi=(vT-u)R[T]$ e
xactly when $v$ is a non-zerodivisor on $R[T]/(vT-u)$, i.e. when $(vT-u):v^{\inf
ty}=(vT-u)$. A sufficient condition is that $vT-u$ be *primitive* in the sense t
hat the ideal of $R$ generated by its coefficients — here $(u,v)$ — equals $R$ (
then $v$ acts invertibly modulo $(vT-u)$). Without such a condition the kernel c
an be strictly larger, even when $R$ is integrally closed:

   *Example.* Take $R=k[x,y]$ (a UFD, hence integrally closed), $u=x^{2}$, $v=xy
$, so $w=x/y$. Then $vT-u=x(yT-x)$, and $yT-x\in\ker\varphi$ since $y\cdot(x/y)-
x=0$, yet $yT-x\notin (x(yT-x))$ because $x$ is not a unit. Thus $\ker\varphi=(y
T-x)\supsetneq(vT-u)$, confirming that the saturation (not merely the principal
ideal) is the correct answer.
