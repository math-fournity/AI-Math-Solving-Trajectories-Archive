## Proof

We seek an upper bound for
$$\sup_{a,b \in B(0,r)} \left| \frac{e^a - e^b}{a-b} \right|,$$
where $B(0,r) = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| \le r\}$ is the closed ball of radius $
r$ in $\mathbb{C}$.

**Key identity (integral representation of the difference quotient).** For any $
a, b \in \mathbb{C}$ with $a \neq b$, writing $e^a - e^b = \int_0^1 \frac{d}{dt}
 e^{ta+(1-t)b}\,dt$ gives
$$\frac{e^a - e^b}{a - b} = \int_0^1 e^{ta + (1-t)b}\,dt.$$
This also extends continuously to $a = b$ (where the value is $e^a$), so the ide
ntity holds for all $a, b$.

**Bounding the modulus.** Taking absolute values and using the triangle inequali
ty for integrals:
$$\left| \frac{e^a - e^b}{a-b} \right| = \left| \int_0^1 e^{ta+(1-t)b}\,dt \righ
t| \le \int_0^1 \left| e^{ta+(1-t)b} \right| dt = \int_0^1 e^{\operatorname{Re}(
ta+(1-t)b)}\,dt.$$

**Using convexity of the ball.** Since $B(0,r)$ is convex and $a, b \in B(0,r)$,
 every convex combination $ta + (1-t)b$ (with $t \in [0,1]$) also lies in $B(0,r
)$. Hence
$$|ta + (1-t)b| \le r \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \operatorname{Re}(ta + (1-t)b)
 \le |ta + (1-t)b| \le r.$$

Therefore
$$\left| \frac{e^a - e^b}{a-b} \right| \le \int_0^1 e^r\,dt = e^r.$$

Since this holds for every $a, b \in B(0,r)$,
$$\sup_{a,b \in B(0,r)} \left| \frac{e^a - e^b}{a-b} \right| \le e^r.$$

**The bound is attained.** Taking $a = b = r$ (both real, on the boundary of $B(
0,r)$), the difference quotient equals $e^r$ exactly. Thus the supremum is in fa
ct equal to $e^r$.

$$\boxed{\sup_{a,b \in B(0,r)} \left| \frac{e^a - e^b}{a-b} \right| = e^r}$$
