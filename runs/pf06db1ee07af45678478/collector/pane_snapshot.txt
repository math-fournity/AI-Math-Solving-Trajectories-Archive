Let $P(x) = 5x^{2015}+x^2+x+1 = 5\prod_{i=1}^{2015}(x-x_i)$.

### Part 1: $\prod_{i=1}^{2015}(1+x_i)$

Evaluate $P(-1)$:
$$P(-1) = 5(-1)^{2015}+(-1)^2+(-1)+1 = -5+1-1+1 = -4.$$

On the other hand,
$$P(-1)=5\prod_{i=1}^{2015}(-1-x_i)=5\cdot(-1)^{2015}\prod_{i=1}^{2015}(1+x_i)=-
5\prod_{i=1}^{2015}(1+x_i).$$

So $-5\prod(1+x_i)=-4$, giving
$$\prod_{i=1}^{2015}(1+x_i)=\frac{4}{5}.$$

### Part 2: $\sum_{i=1}^{2015}\frac{1}{1-x_i}$

Using logarithmic differentiation,
$$\frac{P'(x)}{P(x)}=\sum_{i=1}^{2015}\frac{1}{x-x_i}.$$

Setting $x=1$:
$$\sum_{i=1}^{2015}\frac{1}{1-x_i}=\frac{P'(1)}{P(1)}.$$

Compute:
$$P(1)=5+1+1+1=8,$$
$$P'(x)=5\cdot 2015\,x^{2014}+2x+1,\qquad P'(1)=10075+2+1=10078.$$

Hence
$$\sum_{i=1}^{2015}\frac{1}{1-x_i}=\frac{10078}{8}=\frac{5039}{4}.$$

### Combining

$$\left(\prod_{i=1}^{2015}(1+x_i)\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2015}\frac{1}{1-x_i}\r
ight)=\frac{4}{5}\cdot\frac{5039}{4}=\frac{5039}{5}.$$

$$\boxed{\dfrac{5039}{5}}$$
