# Solution

**Answer: No.** Not every subset of $\mathbb{N}$ is semi-linear.

## Key Lemma: Every semi-linear subset of $\mathbb{N}$ is eventually periodic.

A set $A \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ is *eventually periodic* if there exist $N \in \m
athbb{N}$ and $p \geq 1$ such that for all $n \geq N$:
$$n \in A \iff n + p \in A.$$

**Step 1: Every linear subset of $\mathbb{N}$ is eventually periodic.**

Let $L = u_0 + \langle u_1, \dots, u_m \rangle \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ be linear.

- If all $u_i = 0$ for $i \geq 1$, then $L = \{u_0\}$ is finite, hence eventuall
y periodic (trivially).

- Otherwise, let $g = \gcd\{u_i : 1 \leq i \leq m,\ u_i > 0\}$. Consider the num
erical semigroup
$$S = \langle u_1, \dots, u_m \rangle = \{t_1 u_1 + \cdots + t_m u_m : t_i \in \
mathbb{N}\}.$$
By the Frobenius/numerical semigroup theorem, there exists $N_0 \in \mathbb{N}$
such that for every $k \geq N_0$ with $g \mid k$, we have $k \in S$.

Now $n \in L \iff n - u_0 \in S$ (and $n \geq u_0$). For all $n \geq u_0 + N_0$:
$$n \in L \iff n - u_0 \in S \iff g \mid (n - u_0) \iff n \equiv u_0 \pmod{g}.$$
So $L$ is eventually periodic with period $g$ (and threshold $u_0 + N_0$).

**Step 2: A finite union of eventually periodic sets is eventually periodic.**

If $A_1, \dots, A_k$ are eventually periodic with periods $p_1, \dots, p_k$ and
thresholds $N_1, \dots, N_k$, then $\bigcup_i A_i$ is eventually periodic with p
eriod $p = \operatorname{lcm}(p_1, \dots, p_k)$ and threshold $N = \max_i N_i$.

Combining Steps 1 and 2: **every semi-linear subset of $\mathbb{N}$ is eventuall
y periodic.** $\square$

## Counterexample: The set of perfect squares is not semi-linear.

Let $Q = \{n^2 : n \in \mathbb{N}\} = \{0, 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, \dots\}$.

**Claim:** $Q$ is not eventually periodic.

Suppose for contradiction that $Q$ is eventually periodic with period $p \geq 1$
 and threshold $N$. Choose $k$ large enough that $k^2 \geq N$ and $2k+1 > p$ (i.
e., $(k+1)^2 - k^2 > p$). Then:
- $k^2 \in Q$ and $k^2 \geq N$, so by eventual periodicity, $k^2 + p \in Q$.
- But $k^2 < k^2 + p < k^2 + (2k+1) = (k+1)^2$, so $k^2 + p$ lies strictly betwe
en two consecutive perfect squares. Hence $k^2 + p \notin Q$.

This is a contradiction. Therefore $Q$ is not eventually periodic.

By the Key Lemma, since $Q$ is not eventually periodic, **$Q$ is not semi-linear
**.

## Conclusion

Since $Q \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ is a subset of the natural numbers that is not se
mi-linear, **not every subset of $\mathbb{N}$ is semi-linear**. In fact, the sem
i-linear subsets of $\mathbb{N}$ are exactly the eventually periodic sets (the c
onverse — that every eventually periodic set is semi-linear — also holds, since
a single arithmetic progression $\{a + kp : k \geq 0\}$ is the linear set $a + \
langle p \rangle$, and a finite set is a finite union of singletons $u_0 + \lang
le \rangle$).
