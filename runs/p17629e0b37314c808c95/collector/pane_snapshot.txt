## Proof

Let $F$ be a free group with basis $X$, and let $F' = [F,F]$ be its commutator s
ubgroup. Define
$$X' = \{xF' : x \in X\} \subseteq F/F'.$$

We claim the natural map $\varphi: X \to X'$, $\varphi(x) = xF'$, is a bijection
, so $|X| = |X'|$.

**Surjectivity** is immediate from the definition: every element of $X'$ is of t
he form $xF'$ for some $x \in X$.

**Injectivity.** Suppose $x, y \in X$ satisfy $xF' = yF'$. Then $xy^{-1} \in F'$
.

Consider the abelianization homomorphism
$$\pi: F \to F/F' \cong \bigoplus_{x \in X} \mathbb{Z},$$
where $F/F'$ is the free abelian group on the image of $X$. Under $\pi$, each $x
 \in X$ maps to the corresponding standard basis element $e_x$ of the direct sum
, and $\pi$ sends $xy^{-1}$ to $e_x - e_y$.

Since $xy^{-1} \in F' = \ker\pi$, we have $\pi(xy^{-1}) = 0$, i.e.,
$$e_x - e_y = 0 \quad\text{in } \bigoplus_{x \in X} \mathbb{Z}.$$

The elements $\{e_x\}_{x \in X}$ are linearly independent in the free abelian gr
oup (they form a basis), so $e_x = e_y$ forces $x = y$.

Therefore $\varphi$ is injective.

Having shown $\varphi$ is both injective and surjective, it is a bijection, and
consequently
$$|X| = |X'|.$$
