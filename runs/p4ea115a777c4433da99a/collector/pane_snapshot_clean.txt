# Chern Number of $\mathbb{C}P^1 \times \mathbb{C}P^1$

## The Error in the Given Calculation

The expression $(1+2a^2)(1+2a^2) = 1+4a^2+4a^4$ is **notationally wrong**: it us
es the same symbol for the generators of the two distinct factors. The two proje
ction maps $\pi_1, \pi_2: \mathbb{C}P^1\times\mathbb{C}P^1\to\mathbb{C}P^1$ pull
 back the generator $a\in H^2(\mathbb{C}P^1;\mathbb{Z})$ to **two different clas
ses**:
$$a_1=\pi_1^*(a),\qquad a_2=\pi_2^*(a).$$

These satisfy $a_1^2=0$, $a_2^2=0$ (each lives in a 2-real-dimensional factor),
but $a_1a_2\neq 0$ and generates $H^4(\mathbb{C}P^1\times\mathbb{C}P^1;\mathbb{Z
})\cong\mathbb{Z}$.

## Correct Total Chern Class

For $\mathbb{C}P^n$, $c(T\mathbb{C}P^n)=(1+a)^{n+1}$. For $\mathbb{C}P^1$, this
gives $(1+a)^2=1+2a$ (since $a^2=0$ in $H^*(\mathbb{C}P^1)$). By the product for
mula (Künneth + Whitney sum for tangent bundles of products):

$$c\bigl(T(\mathbb{C}P^1\times\mathbb{C}P^1)\bigr)=\pi_1^*c(T\mathbb{C}P^1)\cdot
\pi_2^*c(T\mathbb{C}P^1)=(1+2a_1)(1+2a_2).$$

Expanding:
$$c = 1 + 2(a_1+a_2) + 4a_1a_2.$$

So $c_1 = 2(a_1+a_2)$ and $c_2 = 4a_1a_2$.

## Computing $c_1^2$

$$c_1^2 = 4(a_1+a_2)^2 = 4\bigl(a_1^2 + 2a_1a_2 + a_2^2\bigr) = 4\cdot 2a_1a_2 =
 8\,a_1a_2,$$

using $a_1^2=a_2^2=0$. Evaluating on the fundamental class:

$$c_1^2\bigl[\mathbb{C}P^1\times\mathbb{C}P^1\bigr] = 8\,\langle a_1a_2,\,[\math
bb{C}P^1\times\mathbb{C}P^1]\rangle = 8\cdot 1 = \boxed{8}.$$

## Why 16 Is Wrong

The value 16 arises from the erroneous identification of the two generators. If
one (incorrectly) writes both factors with the same class and reads off $c_1=4a^
2$ from $1+4a^2+4a^4$, then computes $c_1^2=(4a^2)^2=16a^4$, one gets 16. This d
ouble-counts: it treats $c_1$ as $2a+2a=4a$ (same generator) instead of $2a_1+2a
_2$ (distinct generators), and the squaring then produces $16a^4$ instead of the
 correct $8a_1a_2$. The cross-term $2a_1a_2$ — which is the only surviving term
— is counted once (coefficient 8) in the correct computation, but the erroneous
computation conflates the generators and inflates the coefficient to 16.

## Independent Verification

$\mathbb{C}P^1\times\mathbb{C}P^1$ is the Hirzebruch surface $F_0$, a del Pezzo
surface of degree 8. Its anticanonical class is $-K=2H_1+2H_2$ where $H_1=\mathb
b{C}P^1\times\{\mathrm{pt}\}$, $H_2=\{\mathrm{pt}\}\times\mathbb{C}P^1$, with in
tersection form $H_1^2=H_2^2=0$, $H_1\cdot H_2=1$. Then
$$(-K)^2=(2H_1+2H_2)^2=4\cdot0+8\cdot1+4\cdot0=8,$$
confirming $c_1^2=8$.

## Conclusion

The Chern number is **8**, not 16. The error producing 16 stems from failing to
distinguish the two independent generators $a_1\neq a_2$ of $H^2(\mathbb{C}P^1\t
imes\mathbb{C}P^1)$.
