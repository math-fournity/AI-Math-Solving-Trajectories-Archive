**Problem.** Find all $f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$ with $f(f(x))=x^4$ and $f(x)\l
e Cx^2$ for some constant $C$.

**Claim.** The unique solution is $f(x)=x^2$.

---

### Step 1. Basic consequences

Setting $x=0$: $f(f(0))=0$. The bound gives $f(0)\le C\cdot 0=0$, and $f\ge 0$,
so $f(0)=0$.

Apply $f$ to both sides of $f(f(x))=x^4$:
$$f(f(f(x)))=f(x^4).$$
On the other hand, substituting $y=f(x)$ into $f(f(y))=y^4$ gives $f(f(f(x)))=f(
x)^4$. Hence
$$\boxed{f(x^4)=f(x)^4}\qquad\text{for all }x\ge 0. \tag{1}$$

### Step 2. Two-sided quadratic bounds

The hypothesis $f(t)\le Ct^2$ with $t=f(x)\ge 0$ yields
$$x^4=f(f(x))\le C\,f(x)^2 \implies f(x)\ge \frac{x^2}{\sqrt C}.$$
Combined with the upper bound, for all $x\ge 0$:
$$\frac{x^2}{\sqrt C}\le f(x)\le Cx^2. \tag{2}$$
(If $C=0$ then $f\equiv 0$, contradicting $f(f(x))=x^4$ for $x>0$; so $C>0$. Als
o (2) forces $1/\sqrt C\le C$, i.e. $C\ge 1$.)

### Step 3. Reduction to a bounded functional equation

For $x>0$ define $h(x)=f(x)/x^2$. By (2), $h(x)\in[1/\sqrt C,\,C]$, so $h$ is bo
unded and strictly positive. Equation (1) becomes
$$h(x^4)\,x^8 = h(x)^4\,x^8 \implies h(x^4)=h(x)^4. \tag{3}$$

Set $x=e^u$ ($u\in\mathbb R$) and $H(u)=\log h(e^u)$. Then $H$ is bounded: from
(2),
$$-\tfrac12\log C \le H(u)\le \log C, \qquad\text{so } |H(u)|\le M:=\log C. \tag
{4}$$
Equation (3) reads $H(4u)=4H(u)$, equivalently
$$H(u)=\tfrac14 H(4u). \tag{5}$$

### Step 4. $H\equiv 0$

Iterating (5): $H(u)=\frac{1}{4^n}H(4^n u)$ for every $n\ge 1$. By boundedness (
4),
$$|H(u)|=\frac{|H(4^n u)|}{4^n}\le \frac{M}{4^n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.$$
Hence $H(u)=0$ for every $u$, i.e. $h\equiv 1$, i.e.
$$f(x)=x^2\qquad\text{for all }x>0.$$
Together with $f(0)=0$ this gives $f(x)=x^2$ for all $x\ge 0$.

### Step 5. Verification

$f(f(x))=f(x^2)=x^4$ ✓, and $f(x)=x^2\le 1\cdot x^2$ ✓.

---

Therefore the unique function satisfying both conditions is $\boxed{f(x)=x^2}$.
