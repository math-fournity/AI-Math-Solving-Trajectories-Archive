# Solution

**Yes**, the extension is integral over $\mathbb{C}[x]$.

## Proof

Let $R = \mathbb{C}[x,y,z]/(z^2 - xy,\; y^2 - xz)$, and let $\bar{x}, \bar{y}, \
bar{z}$ denote the images of $x, y, z$ in $R$. We view $R$ as a $\mathbb{C}[\bar
{x}]$-algebra; note $R$ is generated over $\mathbb{C}[\bar{x}]$ by $\bar{y}$ and
 $\bar{z}$.

**Step 1: $\bar{z}$ is integral over $\mathbb{C}[\bar{x}]$.**

In $R$ we have the relation $\bar{z}^2 - \bar{x}\,\bar{y} = 0$, i.e. $\bar{z}$ s
atisfies the monic polynomial
$$T^2 - \bar{x}\,\bar{y} \in \mathbb{C}[\bar{x}][T].$$
Hence $\bar{z}$ is integral over $\mathbb{C}[\bar{x}]$.

**Step 2: $\bar{y}$ is integral over $\mathbb{C}[\bar{x}]$.**

Using the two relations in $R$:
$$\bar{y}^2 = \bar{x}\,\bar{z} \implies \bar{y}^4 = \bar{x}^2\,\bar{z}^2 = \bar{
x}^2 \cdot \bar{x}\,\bar{y} = \bar{x}^3\,\bar{y}.$$
Therefore $\bar{y}$ satisfies
$$T^4 - \bar{x}^3\, T \in \mathbb{C}[\bar{x}][T],$$
which is monic in $T$. Hence $\bar{y}$ is integral over $\mathbb{C}[\bar{x}]$.

**Step 3: $R$ is integral over $\mathbb{C}[\bar{x}]$.**

The ring $R$ is generated as a $\mathbb{C}[\bar{x}]$-algebra by $\bar{y}$ and $\
bar{z}$, both of which are integral over $\mathbb{C}[\bar{x}]$. By the standard
theorem on integrality (Atiyah–Macdonald, Prop. 5.1 and Cor. 5.3): *if $B$ is an
 $A$-algebra generated by finitely many elements each integral over $A$, then $B
$ is a finitely generated $A$-module, and consequently $B$ is integral over $A$.
*

Applying this with $A = \mathbb{C}[\bar{x}]$, $B = R$, and generators $\bar{y},
\bar{z}$, we conclude that $R$ is integral (in fact finite) over $\mathbb{C}[\ba
r{x}]$.

**Remark (geometric sanity check).** The variety $V(z^2-xy,\, y^2-xz) \subset \m
athbb{A}^3$ projects to the $x$-axis with finite fibers: for $x \neq 0$ the fibe
r consists of $4$ points $(x,0,0)$ and $(x,\omega x, \omega^2 x)$ for $\omega^3=
1$, and for $x=0$ the single point $(0,0,0)$. A surjective morphism with finite
fibers from an affine variety is finite, confirming that $R$ is a finite $\mathb
b{C}[x]$-module.

### PROOF COMPLETE
