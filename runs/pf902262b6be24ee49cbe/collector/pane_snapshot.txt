## Counterexample: The Particular Point Topology on $\mathbb{N}$

Let $X = \mathbb{N} = \{0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots\}$ equipped with the **particular poi
nt topology** at $0$:

$$\tau = \{U \subseteq \mathbb{N} : 0 \in U\} \cup \{\emptyset\}.$$

That is, a set is open if and only if it is empty or contains the point $0$.

### Claim 1: $(X, \tau)$ is second-countable.

Consider the collection

$$\mathcal{B} = \{\{0\}\} \cup \{\{0, n\} : n \in \mathbb{N},\; n \geq 1\}.$$

This is countable. We verify it is a base. Let $U$ be open and $x \in U$.

- If $x = 0$: then $\{0\} \in \mathcal{B}$ and $\{0\} \subseteq U$ (since $0 \in
 U$).
- If $x \geq 1$: then $0 \in U$ (as $U$ is open and non-empty) and $x \in U$, so
 $\{0, x\} \subseteq U$, and $\{0, x\} \in \mathcal{B}$.

Thus $\mathcal{B}$ is a countable base, and $X$ is second-countable. $\checkmark
$

### Claim 2: $(X, \tau)$ is not paracompact — some open cover has no locally fin
ite open refinement.

Consider the open cover

$$\mathcal{U} = \big\{\{0, n\} : n \geq 1\big\} \cup \big\{\{0\}\big\}.$$

Each member is open (each contains $0$), and $\mathcal{U}$ covers $X$: every $n
\geq 1$ lies in $\{0,n\}$, and $0$ lies in $\{0\}$ (and in every other member).

Suppose for contradiction that $\mathcal{V}$ is a locally finite open refinement
 of $\mathcal{U}$.

**Step 1 — Structure of $\mathcal{V}$.** Every $V \in \mathcal{V}$ is open and n
on-empty, so $0 \in V$. Since $\mathcal{V}$ refines $\mathcal{U}$, each $V$ is c
ontained in some $U \in \mathcal{U}$:

- If $V \subseteq \{0\}$, then $V = \{0\}$ (since $0 \in V$).
- If $V \subseteq \{0, n\}$ for some $n \geq 1$, then $V \in \big\{\{0\},\; \{0,
 n\}\big\}$.

So every member of $\mathcal{V}$ is either $\{0\}$ or $\{0, n\}$ for some $n \ge
q 1$.

**Step 2 — $\mathcal{V}$ must contain $\{0, n\}$ for every $n \geq 1$.** Fix $n
\geq 1$. The point $n$ must be covered by some $V \in \mathcal{V}$. The only pos
sible members of $\mathcal{V}$ containing $n$ are $\{0, n\}$ (since $\{0\}$ does
 not contain $n$, and $\{0, m\}$ for $m \neq n$ does not contain $n$). Therefore
 $\{0, n\} \in \mathcal{V}$ for every $n \geq 1$.

**Step 3 — $\mathcal{V}$ is not locally finite at $0$.** Let $W$ be any open nei
ghborhood of $0$. Then $0 \in W$, so for every $n \geq 1$:

$$W \cap \{0, n\} \ni 0 \neq \emptyset.$$

Since $\{0, n\} \in \mathcal{V}$ for all $n \geq 1$, the neighborhood $W$ meets
**infinitely many** members of $\mathcal{V}$. This holds for *every* neighborhoo
d $W$ of $0$, contradicting local finiteness.

Therefore no locally finite open refinement of $\mathcal{U}$ exists. $\checkmark
$

## Conclusion

The space $(\mathbb{N}, \tau)$ with the particular point topology is **second-co
untable** (Claim 1) yet **not paracompact** (Claim 2): the open cover $\mathcal{
U}$ admits no locally finite open refinement.

The essential obstruction is that the distinguished point $0$ lies in *every* no
n-empty open set, so any open refinement—whose members must all contain $0$—cann
ot be locally finite at $0$ unless it is finite, but finiteness is incompatible
with covering the infinitely many isolated points $n \geq 1$, each of which can
only be covered by the specific set $\{0, n\}$.

Hence the answer to the question is **no**: not every second-countable topologic
