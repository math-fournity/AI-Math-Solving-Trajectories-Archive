We evaluate
$$I=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x\cos\!\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}x\right)}{x^{2}-2x+5
}\,dx.$$

Since $\cos(-\theta)=\cos\theta$, the integrand is $\dfrac{x\cos\!\left(\frac{\p
i}{2}x\right)}{x^{2}-2x+5}$.

### Setup

Consider the complex integral
$$J=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}x}}{x^{2}-2x+5}\,dx,\qquad
I=\operatorname{Re}J.$$

Let
$$f(z)=\frac{z\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}z}}{z^{2}-2z+5}.$$

The denominator factors as
$$z^{2}-2z+5=(z-1-2i)(z-1+2i),$$
with poles at $z=1\pm 2i$.

### Contour choice

For $z=x+iy$,
$$e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}z}=e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}x-\frac{\pi}{2}y},$$
which decays exponentially in the **upper** half-plane ($y>0$). We close the con
tour with a large semicircle in the upper half-plane.

The rational prefactor $\dfrac{z}{z^{2}-2z+5}\sim\dfrac{1}{z}$ satisfies $\deg Q
=\deg P+1$, so by **Jordan's lemma** the arc contribution vanishes as the radius
 $R\to\infty$. Hence
$$J=2\pi i\sum_{\operatorname{Im}z_{k}>0}\operatorname{Res}(f,z_{k}).$$

### Residue at the enclosed pole

The only pole in the upper half-plane is $z_{0}=1+2i$. It is simple, so
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,z_{0})=\frac{z_{0}\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}z_{0}}}{2z_{0}-2}=\f
rac{(1+2i)\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}(1+2i)}}{4i}.$$

Compute the exponential:
$$e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}(1+2i)}=e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}-\pi}=e^{-\pi}\cdot e^{i\pi/2}=e^{-\
pi}\,i.$$

Therefore
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,z_{0})=\frac{(1+2i)\,e^{-\pi}\,i}{4i}=\frac{(1+2i)e^{-\pi
}}{4}.$$

### Apply the residue theorem

$$J=2\pi i\cdot\frac{(1+2i)e^{-\pi}}{4}=\frac{\pi i\,e^{-\pi}(1+2i)}{2}=\frac{\p
i e^{-\pi}}{2}\bigl(i+2i^{2}\bigr)=\frac{\pi e^{-\pi}}{2}(-2+i).$$

### Extract the real part

$$I=\operatorname{Re}J=\frac{\pi e^{-\pi}}{2}\cdot(-2)=-\pi e^{-\pi}.$$

### Verification of convergence

For large $|x|$, the integrand behaves like $\dfrac{\cos(\pi x/2)}{x}$, which is
 conditionally convergent by the Dirichlet test. The Jordan's-lemma contour argu
ment rigorously handles this conditional convergence, so the residue computation
 is valid.

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x\cos\!\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}x\
right)}{x^{2}-2x+5}\,dx=-\pi e^{-\pi}}$$
