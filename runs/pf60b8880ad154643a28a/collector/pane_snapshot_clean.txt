## Solution

We evaluate $I = \int_0^{\infty} \frac{\log x}{1+x^2}\,dx$ by contour integratio
n.

**Setup.** Consider the function
$$f(z) = \frac{(\log z)^2}{1+z^2}$$
where $\log z = \ln|z| + i\arg z$ with branch $0 < \arg z < 2\pi$ (branch cut al
ong the positive real axis). We integrate $f$ over a keyhole contour $\Gamma$ co
nsisting of:
- $\gamma_1$: the upper edge of the positive real axis from $\epsilon$ to $R$ (w
here $\arg z \to 0^+$),
- $C_R$: the large circle $|z|=R$ traversed counterclockwise,
- $\gamma_2$: the lower edge of the positive real axis from $R$ to $\epsilon$ (w
here $\arg z \to 2\pi^-$),
- $C_\epsilon$: the small circle $|z|=\epsilon$ traversed clockwise.

**Boundary values.** On $\gamma_1$: $\log z = \ln x$, so $f = \frac{(\ln x)^2}{1
+x^2}$. On $\gamma_2$: $\log z = \ln x + 2\pi i$, so $f = \frac{(\ln x + 2\pi i)
^2}{1+x^2} = \frac{(\ln x)^2 + 4\pi i\ln x - 4\pi^2}{1+x^2}$.

**Vanishing of the arcs.**
- On $C_R$: $|f(z)| \le \frac{(\ln R + 2\pi)^2}{R^2-1}$, so $\left|\int_{C_R} f\
right| \le 2\pi R\cdot\frac{(\ln R+2\pi)^2}{R^2-1}\to 0$ as $R\to\infty$.
- On $C_\epsilon$: $|f(z)| \le \frac{(|\ln\epsilon|+2\pi)^2}{1-\epsilon^2}$, so
$\left|\int_{C_\epsilon} f\right| \le 2\pi\epsilon\cdot\frac{(|\ln\epsilon|+2\pi
)^2}{1-\epsilon^2}\to 0$ as $\epsilon\to 0$.

Both vanish since $\epsilon(\ln\epsilon)^2 \to 0$ and $(\ln R)^2/R \to 0$.

**Contour integral in the limit.** Taking $\epsilon\to 0$, $R\to\infty$:
$$\oint_\Gamma f\,dz = \int_0^\infty \frac{(\ln x)^2}{1+x^2}\,dx - \int_0^\infty
 \frac{(\ln x)^2 + 4\pi i\ln x - 4\pi^2}{1+x^2}\,dx = -4\pi i\int_0^\infty \frac
{\ln x}{1+x^2}\,dx + 4\pi^2\int_0^\infty \frac{1}{1+x^2}\,dx.$$

**Residues.** The poles inside $\Gamma$ are at $z=i$ and $z=-i$ (both simple pol
es of $\frac{1}{1+z^2}$).

- At $z = i = e^{i\pi/2}$: $\log(i) = i\pi/2$, so
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=i} f = \frac{(\log i)^2}{2i} = \frac{(i\pi/2)^2}{2i} = \
frac{-\pi^2/4}{2i} = \frac{\pi^2 i}{8}.$$

- At $z = -i = e^{i3\pi/2}$: $\log(-i) = i3\pi/2$, so
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=-i} f = \frac{(\log(-i))^2}{-2i} = \frac{(i3\pi/2)^2}{-2
i} = \frac{-9\pi^2/4}{-2i} = -\frac{9\pi^2 i}{8}.$$

Sum of residues:
$$\frac{\pi^2 i}{8} - \frac{9\pi^2 i}{8} = -\pi^2 i.$$

By the residue theorem:
$$\oint_\Gamma f\,dz = 2\pi i\cdot(-\pi^2 i) = 2\pi^3.$$

**Equating real and imaginary parts.** We have
$$-4\pi i\int_0^\infty \frac{\ln x}{1+x^2}\,dx + 4\pi^2\int_0^\infty \frac{1}{1+
x^2}\,dx = 2\pi^3.$$

The known value $\int_0^\infty \frac{1}{1+x^2}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{2}$ gives $4\pi^
2\cdot\frac{\pi}{2} = 2\pi^3$ on the left, matching the real part of the right-h
and side. The imaginary part yields:
$$-4\pi\int_0^\infty \frac{\ln x}{1+x^2}\,dx = 0.$$

Therefore
$$\boxed{\int_0^{\infty} \frac{\log x}{1+x^2}\,dx = 0.}$$
