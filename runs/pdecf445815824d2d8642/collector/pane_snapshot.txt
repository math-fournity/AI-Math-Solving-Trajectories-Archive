## Proof

**Answer: No.** The closed unit ball $B_{L^2((0,1))}$ is **not** relatively comp
act in $L^1((0,1))$.

**Counterexample.** Define $f_n(x) = \sqrt{2}\sin(2\pi n x)$ for $x \in (0,1)$,
$n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$

**Step 1: $\{f_n\} \subset B_{L^2}$.** We compute
$$\|f_n\|_{L^2}^2 = \int_0^1 2\sin^2(2\pi n x)\,dx = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 1,$$
so $f_n \in B_{L^2}$ for all $n$.

**Step 2: $\|f_n\|_{L^1}$ is constant and positive.** Since $|\sin(2\pi n x)|$ h
as $2n$ half-periods on $(0,1)$, each contributing $\frac{1}{\pi n}$ to the inte
gral:
$$\|f_n\|_{L^1} = \sqrt{2}\int_0^1 |\sin(2\pi n x)|\,dx = \sqrt{2}\cdot 2n \cdot
 \frac{1}{\pi n} = \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\pi} > 0 \quad \text{for all } n.$$

**Step 3: $f_n \rightharpoonup 0$ weakly in $L^2$.** For any $g \in L^2((0,1)) \
subset L^1((0,1))$ (since $(0,1)$ has finite measure), the integral
$$\int_0^1 \sqrt{2}\sin(2\pi n x)\,g(x)\,dx \to 0 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty$
$
by the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma (these are Fourier sine coefficients of $g \in L^1
$). Hence $f_n \rightharpoonup 0$ in $L^2$.

**Step 4: No subsequence converges in $L^1$.** Suppose for contradiction that so
me subsequence $\{f_{n_k}\}$ converges in $L^1$ to a limit $g \in L^1((0,1))$. T
hen:

- *$L^1$ convergence implies distributional convergence:* For every $\varphi \in
 C_c^\infty((0,1))$, $\int f_{n_k}\varphi \to \int g\varphi$.
- *Weak $L^2$ convergence implies distributional convergence to $0$:* Since $\va
rphi \in C_c^\infty \subset L^2$, Step 3 gives $\int f_{n_k}\varphi \to 0$.

By uniqueness of distributional limits, $g = 0$ a.e. But then $\|f_{n_k}\|_{L^1}
 \to \|g\|_{L^1} = 0$, which contradicts $\|f_{n_k}\|_{L^1} = \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\
pi} > 0$ for all $k$.

**Conclusion.** The sequence $\{f_n\} \subset B_{L^2}$ has no $L^1$-convergent s
ubsequence. Therefore $B_{L^2((0,1))}$ is not relatively compact in $L^1((0,1))$
.

**Remark.** This is consistent with the Kolmogorov–Riesz compactness criterion:
while $B_{L^2}$ is bounded in $L^1$ (by Cauchy–Schwarz, $\|f\|_1 \leq \|f\|_2$),
 the $L^1$-translation modulus of continuity is not uniformly small over $B_{L^2
}$—the highly oscillatory functions $f_n$ demonstrate this failure.
