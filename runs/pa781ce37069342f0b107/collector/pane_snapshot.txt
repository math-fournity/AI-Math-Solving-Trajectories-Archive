## Proof

**Claim.** The maximum modulus principle applies to antiholomorphic functions. S
pecifically, if $f$ is antiholomorphic on a bounded domain $\Omega \subset \math
bb{C}$ and continuous on $\overline{\Omega}$, then $|f|$ attains its maximum on
$\partial\Omega$.

**Definition.** A function $f : \Omega \to \mathbb{C}$ is *antiholomorphic* if $
\frac{\partial f}{\partial z} = 0$ on $\Omega$, equivalently $f$ is locally expr
essible as a convergent power series in $\bar{z}$. A basic equivalent characteri
zation is:

> $f$ is antiholomorphic on $\Omega$ if and only if $\overline{f}$ is holomorphi
c on $\Omega$.

*Proof of the characterization.* Writing $f = u + iv$ and using the Wirtinger de
rivatives, $\frac{\partial f}{\partial z} = 0$ is equivalent to $\frac{\partial
\overline{f}}{\partial \bar{z}} = 0$, which is precisely the holomorphicity of $
\overline{f}$. (One may also verify directly from the Cauchy–Riemann equations:
$f$ antiholomorphic means $u_x = -v_y$ and $u_y = v_x$, which is exactly the Cau
chy–Riemann system for $\overline{f} = u - iv$.) $\square$

**Main proof.** Let $f$ be antiholomorphic on a bounded domain $\Omega$ and cont
inuous on $\overline{\Omega}$. By the characterization above, $g := \overline{f}
$ is holomorphic on $\Omega$ and continuous on $\overline{\Omega}$. Since for ev
ery $z$,
$$|g(z)| = |\overline{f(z)}| = |f(z)|,$$
the two functions $|f|$ and $|g|$ are identical on $\overline{\Omega}$.

The classical maximum modulus principle (for holomorphic functions) applied to $
g$ gives
$$\max_{z \in \overline{\Omega}} |g(z)| = \max_{z \in \partial\Omega} |g(z)|.$$
Because $|g| \equiv |f|$, this becomes
$$\max_{z \in \overline{\Omega}} |f(z)| = \max_{z \in \partial\Omega} |f(z)|,$$
which is exactly the maximum modulus principle for $f$. $\square$

**Remark (on the strong form).** The strong form also carries over: if $|f|$ att
ains a local maximum at an interior point $z_0 \in \Omega$, then $|g| = |f|$ att
ains a local maximum at $z_0$; by the strong maximum modulus principle for the h
olomorphic function $g$, this forces $g$ to be constant on the connected compone
nt, hence $f = \overline{g}$ is constant as well. So a nonconstant antiholomorph
ic function cannot attain a local maximum of $|f|$ in the interior.

**Conclusion.** The maximum modulus principle applies verbatim to antiholomorphi
c functions, with the identical statement and (via the conjugation trick above)
an essentially identical proof. The key observation is that complex conjugation
preserves moduli ($|\bar{w}| = |w|$) while swapping holomorphic and antiholomorp
hic, so every modulus-theoretic result for holomorphic functions transfers immed
iately to antiholomorphic functions.
