$$K - K = \{x - y : x, y \in K\}.$$
Since $K$ is convex, $K-K = K + (-K)$ is convex (the Minkowski sum of convex set

**Step 2: The unit circle avoids $K-K$.** If there were $x, y \in K$ with $|x -
y| = 1$, then $x - y \in K - K$ and $|x-y| = 1$, placing $x - y$ on the unit cir
cle $S^1 = \{p : |p| = 1\}$. Our assumption rules this out, so
$$K - K \;\cap\; S^1 = \emptyset.$$

**Step 3: $K - K$ lies inside the open unit disk.** Since $0 \in K-K$ and $K-K$
is convex, suppose for contradiction that some $p \in K-K$ satisfies $|p| \geq 1
$. Then the entire segment $\{tp : t \in [0,1]\}$ lies in $K-K$. The continuous
function $g(t) = |tp| = t|p|$ satisfies $g(0) = 0$ and $g(1) = |p| \geq 1$, so b
y the Intermediate Value Theorem there exists $t^* \in [0,1]$ with $g(t^*) = 1$.
 The point $t^*p \in K-K$ then lies on $S^1$, contradicting Step 2. Hence every
point of $K-K$ has norm strictly less than $1$:
$$K - K \subseteq \{p : |p| < 1\}.$$
Therefore $\operatorname{area}(K - K) \leq \pi$ (the area of the open unit disk
is $\pi$).

$$\pi < \operatorname{area}(K - K) \leq \pi,$$
which is impossible. The contradiction shows that our assumption was false: $K$
must contain a pair of points exactly $1$ unit apart. $\blacksquare$
