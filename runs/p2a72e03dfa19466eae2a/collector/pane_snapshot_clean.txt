## Solution

**The surface.** The equation $x^2+y^2+z^2=2az+3a^2$ rewrites as
$$x^2+y^2+(z-a)^2=4a^2,$$
a sphere of radius $2a$ centered at $(0,0,a)$. It meets the plane $z=0$ where $x
^2+y^2=3a^2$, a circle of radius $\sqrt{3}\,a$. The required surface $S$ is the
part of the sphere with $z\ge 0$; "outward" means away from the sphere's center.

**Divergence.** For $\mathbf F=(x^2+y^2+2+z^2)\,\mathbf i+(e^{x^2}+y^2)\,\mathbf
 j+(3+x)\,\mathbf k$,
$$\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf F=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x^2+y^2+2+z^2)+\frac{\par
tial}{\partial y}(e^{x^2}+y^2)+\frac{\partial}{\partial z}(3+x)=2x+2y+0=2(x+y).$
$

**Closing the surface.** Let $D=\{(x,y,0):x^2+y^2\le 3a^2\}$ be the disk in the
plane $z=0$. Then $S\cup D$ bounds the solid region
$$V=\{(x,y,z):z\ge 0,\ x^2+y^2+(z-a)^2\le 4a^2\}.$$
On $D$ the outward normal to $V$ points downward, $\mathbf n=-\mathbf k$.

**Volume integral.** The region $V$ is rotationally symmetric about the $z$-axis
, hence symmetric under $x\mapsto -x$ and $y\mapsto -y$. The integrand $2(x+y)$
is odd in both $x$ and $y$, so
$$\iiint_V 2(x+y)\,dV=0.$$

**Flux across the disk.** On $D$, $z=0$ and $\mathbf F\cdot(-\mathbf k)=-(3+x)$.
 Thus
$$\iint_D \mathbf F\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=-\iint_D(3+x)\,dA=-3\cdot\operatorname{Are
a}(D)-\iint_D x\,dA.$$
By symmetry $\iint_D x\,dA=0$, and $\operatorname{Area}(D)=3\pi a^2$, giving
$$\iint_D \mathbf F\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=-9\pi a^2.$$

**Applying the divergence theorem.**
$$\iint_{S\cup D}\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=\iiint_V\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf F\,d
V=0,$$
so
$$\iint_S \mathbf F\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=-\iint_D \mathbf F\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=9\pi
a^2.$$

The flux of $\mathbf F$ outward across the given spherical surface is

$$\boxed{9\pi a^2}.$$
