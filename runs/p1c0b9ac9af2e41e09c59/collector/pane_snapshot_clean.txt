## Proof

Let $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5}, \sqrt{7})$ with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$.
We show that **no** prime $p$ makes $(p)$ a prime ideal of $\mathcal{O}_K$.

**Step 1: $K/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois with Galois group $(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^2
$.**

Since $\sqrt{5}$ and $\sqrt{7}$ are algebraically independent over $\mathbb{Q}$
(their squarefree parts $5$ and $7$ are distinct primes), $:\mathbb{Q}] = 4$.
The field $K$ is the splitting field of $(x^2-5)(x^2-7)$, hence $K/\mathbb{Q}$ i
s Galois. The automorphisms are determined by $\sqrt{5}\mapsto\pm\sqrt{5}$, $\sq
rt{7}\mapsto\pm\sqrt{7}$, giving
$$\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^2,$$
an abelian group of **exponent $2$** (every non-identity element has order exact
ly $2$).

**Step 2: Splitting of unramified primes.**

For a prime $p$ unramified in $K$, let $(p) = \mathfrak{P}_1\cdots\mathfrak{P}_g
$ with residue degrees $f_i = f$ (equal since the extension is Galois). Then $fg
 = :\mathbb{Q}] = 4$. The residue degree $f$ equals the order of the Frobenius
 element $\operatorname{Frob}_p \in \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$.

Since $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^2$ has ex
ponent $2$, the order of $\operatorname{Frob}_p$ is either $1$ or $2$. Therefore
 $f \in \{1, 2\}$, which forces $g = 4/f \in \{4, 2\}$.

- If $f=1$: $(p) = \mathfrak{P}_1\mathfrak{P}_2\mathfrak{P}_3\mathfrak{P}_4$ (sp
lits completely).
- If $f=2$: $(p) = \mathfrak{P}_1\mathfrak{P}_2$ (splits into two primes of degr
ee $2$).

In neither case is $(p)$ itself a prime ideal, since $g \geq 2$.

**Step 3: Ramified primes.**

The discriminant of $K$ is the product of the discriminants of its three quadrat
ic subfields $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{7})$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3
5})$:
$$\operatorname{disc}(K) = 5 \cdot 28 \cdot 140 = 19600 = 2^4 \cdot 5^2 \cdot 7^
2.$$
The ramified primes are exactly $2, 5, 7$. For a ramified prime, $(p)$ is divisi
ble by $\mathfrak{P}^e$ with $e \geq 2$ for some prime ideal $\mathfrak{P}$, so
$(p)$ is not even squarefree, hence certainly not a prime ideal.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Every rational prime $p$ is either ramified (in which case $(p)$ has a repeated
prime factor) or unramified (in which case $g \geq 2$ because the Frobenius has
order at most $2 < 4$). In both cases $(p)$ is **not** a prime ideal of $\mathca
l{O}_K$.

The essential obstruction is that $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\math
bb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^2$ has exponent $2$, so no Frobenius element can have order $
4 = :\mathbb{Q}]$, making it impossible for any prime to be inert.

Therefore, **there does not exist** a prime number $p$ such that $(p)$ is a prim
e ideal in the ring of algebraic integers of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5},\sqrt{7})$.
