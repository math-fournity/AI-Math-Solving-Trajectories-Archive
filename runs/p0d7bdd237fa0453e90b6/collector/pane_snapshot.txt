## Solution

We seek the smallest positive prime $p$ with $p + p^{-1} \equiv 25 \pmod{143}$.

**Reformulation.** Since $p^{-1}$ exists modulo 143, we need $\gcd(p, 143) = 1$,
 so $p \neq 11, 13$. Multiplying through by $p$:

$$p^2 - 25p + 1 \equiv 0 \pmod{143}.$$

Since $143 = 11 \times 13$, this splits into:

$$p^2 - 25p + 1 \equiv 0 \pmod{11}, \qquad p^2 - 25p + 1 \equiv 0 \pmod{13}.$$

**Mod 11:** $25 \equiv 3$, so $p^2 - 3p + 1 \equiv 0$. Discriminant $\Delta = 9
- 4 = 5$. Since $4^2 = 16 \equiv 5 \pmod{11}$, we get $\sqrt{5} \equiv \pm 4$. W
ith $2^{-1} \equiv 6 \pmod{11}$:

$$p \equiv \frac{3 \pm 4}{2} \equiv 7 \cdot 6 \equiv 9 \pmod{11} \quad\text{or}\
quad p \equiv (-1)\cdot 6 \equiv 5 \pmod{11}.$$

**Mod 13:** $25 \equiv 12$, so $p^2 - 12p + 1 \equiv 0$. Discriminant $\Delta =
144 - 4 = 140 \equiv 10 \pmod{13}$. Since $6^2 = 36 \equiv 10 \pmod{13}$, we get
 $\sqrt{10} \equiv \pm 6$. With $2^{-1} \equiv 7 \pmod{13}$:

$$p \equiv \frac{12 \pm 6}{2} \equiv 18 \cdot 7 \equiv 9 \pmod{13} \quad\text{or
}\quad p \equiv 6 \cdot 7 \equiv 3 \pmod{13}.$$

**CRT combinations** (four residue classes mod 143):

| $p \bmod 11$ | $p \bmod 13$ | $p \bmod 143$ |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| 9 | 9 | 9 |
| 9 | 3 | 42 |
| 5 | 9 | 126 |
| 5 | 3 | 16 |

(Details: e.g., $p \equiv 5 \pmod{11}, p \equiv 9 \pmod{13}$: set $p = 11a+5$, t
hen $11a \equiv 4 \pmod{13}$, $a \equiv 4\cdot 6 = 24 \equiv 11 \pmod{13}$, givi
ng $p = 143b + 126$.)

**Finding the smallest prime.** We check candidates in each class below 269:

- **Class 9:** $9$ (composite), $152 = 8 \times 19$, next is $295 > 269$.
- **Class 16:** $16$ (composite), $159 = 3 \times 53$, next is $302 > 269$.
- **Class 42:** $42$ (composite), $185 = 5 \times 37$, next is $328 > 269$.
- **Class 126:** $126$ (composite), $269$ — check primality.

**Primality of 269:** $\sqrt{269} \approx 16.4$, so we test primes $2, 3, 5, 7,
11, 13$: 269 is odd, $2+6+9 = 17$ (not divisible by 3), doesn't end in 0 or 5, $
269/7 \approx 38.4$, $269/11 \approx 24.5$, $269/13 \approx 20.7$. **269 is prim
e.**

**Verification of the congruence.** Since $269 \equiv 126 \pmod{143}$, we need $
126^{-1} \pmod{143}$. Extended Euclidean:

$$143 = 1\cdot126 + 17,\quad 126 = 7\cdot17 + 7,\quad 17 = 2\cdot7 + 3,\quad 7 =
 2\cdot3 + 1.$$

Back-substituting: $1 = 7 - 2\cdot3 = 7 - 2(17-2\cdot7) = 5\cdot7 - 2\cdot17 = 5
(126-7\cdot17) - 2\cdot17 = 5\cdot126 - 37\cdot17 = 5\cdot126 - 37(143-126) = 42
\cdot126 - 37\cdot143$.

So $126^{-1} \equiv 42 \pmod{143}$, and $126 + 42 = 168 = 143 + 25 \equiv 25 \pm
od{143}$. ✓

**Conclusion.** The smallest positive prime satisfying the congruence is $\boxed
{269}$.
