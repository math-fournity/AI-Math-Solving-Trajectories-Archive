## Proof

**Claim.** If $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^N$ has compact boundary and satisfies t
he uniform cusp property (Definition 6.4(ii)) associated with $h(\theta)=\theta^
\alpha$, $0<\alpha<1$, then
$$\dim_H(\partial\Omega) \;\le\; N-\alpha.$$

---

### Setup: local cusp geometry

By the uniform cusp property, there exist $\rho>0$ and $c>0$ (uniform over bound
ary points) such that for every $x\in\partial\Omega$ one can find an orthonormal
 coordinate system $(z',z_N)$, $z'\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}$, centered at $x$, with th
e cusp region
$$C_x \;=\; \bigl\{(z',z_N)\;:\; |z'|<\rho,\; 0<z_N< c\,h(|z'|)\bigr\} \;=\; \bi
gl\{(z',z_N)\;:\; |z'|<\rho,\; 0<z_N<c\,|z'|^\alpha\bigr\}$$
contained in $\mathbb{R}^N\setminus\overline{\Omega}$ (the exterior cusp pointin
g into $\Omega$), with its tip at $x$. Consequently $\partial\Omega\cap B(x,\rho
)$ is trapped inside the closed cusp $\overline{C_x}$, and in particular lies un
der the Hölder graph
$$z_N \;=\; c\,|z'|^\alpha, \qquad |z'|<\rho. \tag{1}$$

Since $\partial\Omega$ is compact, finitely many such cusp neighborhoods $\{B(x_
i,\rho)\}_{i=1}^{M}$ cover $\partial\Omega$. It suffices to bound $\dim_H(\parti
al\Omega\cap B(x_i,\rho))$ for each $i$.

---

### Covering estimate

Fix one cusp neighborhood and write $f(z')=c\,|z'|^\alpha$. The function $f:\mat
hbb{R}^{N-1}\to\mathbb{R}$ is Hölder continuous with exponent $\alpha$:
$$|f(z')-f(w')| \;\le\; c\,\bigl||z'|^\alpha-|w'|^\alpha\bigr| \;\le\; c\,|z'-w'
|^\alpha. \tag{2}$$

Partition the tangential domain $\{|z'|<\rho\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}$ into cube
s $Q$ of side $\varepsilon$. There are $\asymp \varepsilon^{-(N-1)}$ such cubes.
 On each cube $Q$, by (2) the oscillation of $f$ satisfies
$$\operatorname{osc}_Q f \;\le\; c\,\varepsilon^\alpha.$$

The part of the graph (1) — and hence the part of $\partial\Omega$ trapped under
 it — lying over a single cube $Q$ is contained in a rectangular box of dimensio
ns $\varepsilon\times\cdots\times\varepsilon\times c\,\varepsilon^\alpha$. Since
 $\alpha<1$ we have $\varepsilon^\alpha\gg\varepsilon$, so this box is covered b
y
$$\asymp\;\frac{\varepsilon^\alpha}{\varepsilon} \;=\; \varepsilon^{\alpha-1}$$
balls of radius $\varepsilon$ (stacked in the $z_N$-direction).

Therefore the total number of $\varepsilon$-balls needed to cover $\partial\Omeg
a\cap B(x,\rho)$ satisfies
$$N(\varepsilon) \;\lesssim\; \varepsilon^{-(N-1)}\cdot\varepsilon^{\alpha-1} \;
=\; \varepsilon^{-(N-\alpha)}. \tag{3}$$

---

### From box dimension to Hausdorff dimension

Estimate (3) gives, for the upper Minkowski (box) dimension,
$$\overline{\dim}_B(\partial\Omega\cap B(x,\rho)) \;\le\; N-\alpha.$$

Summing over the finitely many cusp neighborhoods covering the compact set $\par
tial\Omega$ preserves the bound:
$$\overline{\dim}_B(\partial\Omega)\;\le\; N-\alpha.$$

Since the Hausdorff dimension is always dominated by the upper box dimension,
$$\dim_H(\partial\Omega)\;\le\;\overline{\dim}_B(\partial\Omega)\;\le\;N-\alpha.
$$

---

### Consistency check

- When $\alpha\to 1^-$, the cusp degenerates to a Lipschitz (cone) graph and the
 bound recovers $\dim_H(\partial\Omega)\le N-1$, the classical result for Lipsch
itz boundaries. ✓
- When $\alpha\to 0^+$, the cusp becomes maximally fat and the bound approaches
$N$, allowing space-filling boundaries. ✓
- The Hölder exponent $\alpha$ of the cusp profile $|z'|^\alpha$ directly contro
ls the excess $(N-\alpha)-(N-1)=1-\alpha$ of dimension over the smooth case. ✓

---

**Conclusion.** The Hausdorff dimension of $\partial\Omega$ is bounded above by

$$\boxed{\dim_H(\partial\Omega)\;\le\; N-\alpha.}$$
