## Proof

**Setup.** Place the vertex $O$ at the origin, with the sector symmetric about t
he $x$-axis. The two boundary radii $OA$ and $OB$ lie at angles $+\alpha$ and $-
\alpha$ from the $x$-axis, and the outer arc is the circle of radius $R$ centere
d at $O$. The boundary corners are
$$A = (R\cos\alpha,\; R\sin\alpha), \qquad B = (R\cos\alpha,\; -R\sin\alpha).$$

**Locating $O_1$.** By symmetry, the center $O_1$ of the inscribed circle $\omeg
a$ lies on the $x$-axis (the angle bisector), say $O_1 = (d, 0)$.

- **Tangency to the two radii:** the distance from $O_1$ to either boundary line
 (through $O$ at angle $\alpha$) is $d\sin\alpha$, so the radius of $\omega$ is
$r = d\sin\alpha$.
- **Tangency to the outer arc (internal tangency):** $d + r = R$, i.e. $d(1+\sin
\alpha) = R$, giving
$$d = \frac{R}{1+\sin\alpha}.$$

**Computing $\angle AO_1B$.** The vectors from $O_1$ to the corners are
$$\overrightarrow{O_1A} = (R\cos\alpha - d,\; R\sin\alpha), \qquad \overrightarr
ow{O_1B} = (R\cos\alpha - d,\; -R\sin\alpha).$$
By symmetry about the $x$-axis,
$$\tan\!\left(\tfrac{\angle AO_1B}{2}\right) = \frac{R\sin\alpha}{R\cos\alpha -
d} = \frac{\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha - \frac{1}{1+\sin\alpha}} = \frac{\sin\alpha(1
+\sin\alpha)}{\cos\alpha(1+\sin\alpha) - 1}.$$

**Using the structural constant.** The given equation is
$$\cos^3\alpha + \cos^2\alpha + \cos\alpha = 1.$$
Let $c = \cos\alpha$, $s = \sin\alpha = \sqrt{1-c^2}$ (with $0<c<1$). I claim th
e equation is equivalent to
$$c(1+s) = 1.$$

*Proof of equivalence.* Starting from $c(1+s)=1$, i.e. $cs = 1-c$. Squaring (bot
h sides positive since $0<c<1$):
$$c^2 s^2 = (1-c)^2 \implies c^2(1-c^2) = (1-c)^2 \implies c^2(1-c)(1+c) = (1-c)
^2.$$
Since $c\neq 1$, divide by $(1-c)$:
$$c^2(1+c) = 1-c \implies c^2 + c^3 = 1 - c \implies c^3 + c^2 + c = 1. \quad\ch
eckmark$$
Each step is reversible, so the two equations are equivalent.

**Conclusion.** Since $c(1+s)=1$, the denominator in the tangent formula is
$$\cos\alpha(1+\sin\alpha) - 1 = 0.$$
Therefore $\tan\!\left(\frac{\angle AO_1B}{2}\right) \to \infty$, which gives
$$\frac{\angle AO_1B}{2} = 90^\circ \implies \angle AO_1B = 180^\circ.$$

Geometrically, the condition $c(1+s)=1$ is exactly $d = R\cos\alpha$, so $O_1 =
(R\cos\alpha, 0)$ — the midpoint of segment $AB$ (which is the vertical segment
$x = R\cos\alpha$, $-R\sin\alpha \le y \le R\sin\alpha$). Thus $O_1$ lies on seg
ment $AB$, and the surveyor sees the two corners in exactly opposite directions.

$$\boxed{\angle AO_1B = 180^\circ}$$
