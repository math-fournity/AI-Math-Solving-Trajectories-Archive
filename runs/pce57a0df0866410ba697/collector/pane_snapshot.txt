## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. The frame inequality holds with $A = \inf\sigma(S) > 0$ and $B =
 \|S\|$.

**Step 1: $S$ is a positive operator.**

For every $f \in H$,
$$
\langle Sf, f\rangle = \left\langle \sum_{j\in J}\langle f,e_j\rangle e_j,\; f\r
ight\rangle = \sum_{j\in J}\langle f,e_j\rangle\langle e_j, f\rangle = \sum_{j\i
n J}|\langle f,e_j\rangle|^2 \ge 0.
$$
Hence $S$ is positive (self-adjoint and $\langle Sf,f\rangle\ge 0$). Note that s
elf-adjointness also follows: $\langle Sf,g\rangle = \sum\langle f,e_j\rangle\ov
erline{\langle g,e_j\rangle} = \overline{\langle Sg,f\rangle}$.

**Step 2: Upper bound.**

Since $S$ is bounded,
$$
\langle Sf,f\rangle \le \|Sf\|\,\|f\| \le \|S\|\,\|f\|^2.
$$
So the upper inequality holds with $B = \|S\| < \infty$.

**Step 3: Lower bound — the key point.**

$S$ is a positive bounded operator that is also invertible (with bounded inverse
, by the bounded inverse theorem, since $S$ is bounded and bijective).

For a positive self-adjoint operator $S$, the spectrum satisfies $\sigma(S) \sub
seteq [0, \|S\|]$. Since $S$ is invertible, $0 \notin \sigma(S)$. Therefore
$$
\sigma(S) \subseteq [m, \|S\|], \qquad m := \inf\sigma(S) > 0.
$$

By the spectral theorem for bounded self-adjoint operators,
$$
\langle Sf, f\rangle \ge m\|f\|^2 \quad \text{for all } f\in H.
$$

(Equivalently: $S \ge mI$ in the operator order, since $S - mI$ has non-negative
 spectrum and is therefore positive.) Setting $A = m > 0$ gives the lower bound.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 2 and 3,
$$
A\|f\|^2 \le \langle Sf, f\rangle \le B\|f\|^2, \qquad A = \inf\sigma(S) > 0,\qu
ad B = \|S\| < \infty,
$$
with $A \le B$ since $A = \inf\sigma(S) \le \sup\sigma(S) = \|S\| = B$.

Thus the frame inequality holds. $\blacksquare$
