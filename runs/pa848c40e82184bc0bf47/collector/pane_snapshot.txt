## Solution

Let
$$S_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{3n^2+2nk}{(n+k)(2n+k)}-\ln 3\right).$$

**Step 1: Rewrite as a Riemann sum.** Set $x=k/n$. Then
$$\frac{3n^2+2nk}{(n+k)(2n+k)}=\frac{3+2x}{(1+x)(2+x)}.$$
Partial fractions give $\frac{3+2x}{(1+x)(2+x)}=\frac{1}{1+x}+\frac{1}{2+x}$. De
fine
$$g(x)=\frac{1}{1+x}+\frac{1}{2+x}-\ln 3,\qquad S_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n}g\!\left(\tfra
c{k}{n}\right).$$

**Step 2: The leading (order $n$) term vanishes.**
$$\int_0^1 g(x)\,dx=\Big[\ln(1+x)+\ln(2+x)\Big]_0^1-\ln 3=(\ln 2+\ln 3)-(\ln 1+\
ln 2)-\ln 3=0.$$
Hence $\frac{S_n}{n}\to 0$, so $S_n=o(n)$ and we must extract the constant term.

**Step 3: Euler–Maclaurin expansion.** Apply the Euler–Maclaurin formula to $h(t
)=g(t/n)$:
$$\sum_{k=1}^{n}h(k)=\int_0^n h(t)\,dt+\frac{h(n)-h(0)}{2}+\frac{h'(n)-h'(0)}{12
}+O(n^{-2}).$$
Since $\int_0^n h(t)\,dt=n\int_0^1 g(u)\,du=0$, $h(n)=g(1)$, $h(0)=g(0)$, and $h
'(t)=g'(t/n)/n$,
$$S_n=\frac{g(1)-g(0)}{2}+\frac{g'(1)-g'(0)}{12\,n}+O(n^{-2}).$$

**Step 4: Evaluate the constants.**
$$g(0)=1+\tfrac12-\ln 3=\tfrac32-\ln 3,\qquad g(1)=\tfrac12+\tfrac13-\ln 3=\tfra
c56-\ln 3,$$
$$\frac{g(1)-g(0)}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\!\left(\tfrac56-\tfrac32\right)=\frac{1}{2}\!\
left(-\tfrac23\right)=-\frac13.$$
$$g'(x)=-\frac{1}{(1+x)^2}-\frac{1}{(2+x)^2},\quad g'(0)=-1-\tfrac14=-\tfrac54,\
quad g'(1)=-\tfrac14-\tfrac19=-\tfrac{13}{36},$$
$$g'(1)-g'(0)=-\tfrac{13}{36}+\tfrac54=\tfrac{32}{36}=\tfrac89,\qquad \frac{g'(1
)-g'(0)}{12\,n}=\frac{8}{108\,n}=\frac{2}{27\,n}.$$

**Conclusion.**
$$\boxed{\displaystyle S_n=-\frac13+\frac{2}{27\,n}+O\!\left(n^{-2}\right)\quad(
n\to+\infty).}$$
