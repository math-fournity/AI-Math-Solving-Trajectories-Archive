## Construction

Define $f(x) = x^{p-1} \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$.

## Verification

**Claim**: $f(0) = 0$, $f(a) = 1$ for all $a \in \mathbb{F}_p \setminus \{0\}$,
and $\deg(f) < p^3(p-1)$.

**Proof**:

**(1) Value at the origin.** Since $p \geq 2$, we have $p - 1 \geq 1$, so:
$$f(0) = 0^{p-1} = 0. \checkmark$$

**(2) Value at all other points.** Let $a \in \mathbb{F}_p \setminus \{0\}$. The
n $a$ is an element of the multiplicative group $\mathbb{F}_p^*$, which has orde
r $p - 1$. By Lagrange's theorem (equivalently, Fermat's little theorem), every
element of a finite group of order $n$ satisfies $g^n = e$. Applying this:
$$a^{p-1} = 1 \quad \text{for all } a \in \mathbb{F}_p^*.$$
Therefore $f(a) = a^{p-1} = 1$ for all $a \in \mathbb{F}_p \setminus \{0\}$. $\c
heckmark$

**(3) Degree bound.** The degree of $f(x) = x^{p-1}$ is $p - 1$. We verify:
$$p - 1 < p^3(p - 1).$$
Since $p \geq 2$, we have $p^3 \geq 8 > 1$, so $p^3(p-1) > 1 \cdot (p-1) = p - 1
$. More explicitly:
$$p^3(p-1) - (p-1) = (p-1)(p^3 - 1) > 0$$
since $p - 1 > 0$ (as $p \geq 2$) and $p^3 - 1 > 0$. Thus $\deg(f) = p - 1 < p^3
(p-1)$. $\checkmark$

## Conclusion

The polynomial $f(x) = x^{p-1} \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ has degree $p - 1 < p^3(p-1)
$, evaluates to $0$ at the origin, and evaluates to $1$ at every other point of
$\mathbb{F}_p$, by Fermat's little theorem. $\blacksquare$
