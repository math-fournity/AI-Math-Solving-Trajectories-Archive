## Proof

Let $\nu_{n,d}\colon \mathbb{P}^n \to \mathbb{P}^N$ (with $N=\binom{d+n}{n}-1$)
be the Veronese map, sending $[x_0:\cdots:x_n]$ to the tuple of all degree-$d$ m
onomials $[M_0:\cdots:M_N]$.

**Definition of degree.** For an irreducible curve $\Gamma \subset \mathbb{P}^N$
, the degree $\deg(\Gamma)$ is the number of intersection points (counted with m
ultiplicity) of $\Gamma$ with a general hyperplane $H \subset \mathbb{P}^N$. Equ
ivalently, $\deg(\Gamma) = \int_\Gamma c_1(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^N}(1))|_\Gamm
a$, i.e. the degree of the hyperplane class $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^N}(1)$ rest
ricted to $\Gamma$.

**Pullback of the hyperplane class.** The Veronese map is an embedding, and by c
onstruction
$$\nu_{n,d}^*\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^N}(1) \;\cong\; \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n
}(d).$$
Indeed, a hyperplane $H = \{L=0\} \subset \mathbb{P}^N$ is defined by a linear f
orm $L$ in the coordinates $[M_0:\cdots:M_N]$; pulling $L$ back along $\nu_{n,d}
$ gives a homogeneous polynomial of degree $d$ in $x_0,\dots,x_n$, i.e. a sectio
n of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}(d)$. This isomorphism of line bundles is the st
andard characterization of the Veronese embedding.

**Restricting to the curve.** Let $\Gamma = \nu_{n,d}(C)$. Since $\nu_{n,d}$ is
an embedding, it is an isomorphism onto its image, so
$$\deg(\Gamma) \;=\; \int_\Gamma c_1\!\big(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^N}(1)\big)\bi
g|_\Gamma \;=\; \int_C \nu_{n,d}^*\,c_1\!\big(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^N}(1)\big)
 \;=\; \int_C c_1\!\big(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}(d)\big).$$

**Computing the integral.** By the Chern class relation $c_1(\mathcal{O}_{\mathb
b{P}^n}(d)) = d\cdot c_1(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}(1))$, and by the definition
of the degree of $C$,
$$\int_C c_1\!\big(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}(1)\big) = \deg(C) = a.$$
Therefore
$$\deg\!\big(\nu_{n,d}(C)\big) \;=\; \int_C c_1\!\big(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}
(d)\big) \;=\; d\cdot\int_C c_1\!\big(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}(1)\big) \;=\; d
\cdot a \;=\; ad.$$

**Geometric check.** A general hyperplane $H\subset\mathbb{P}^N$ pulls back to a
 general degree-$d$ hypersurface $V\subset\mathbb{P}^n$. By Bertini (or directly
, since $C$ is general relative to $V$), $V$ intersects $C$ transversely in $\de
g(C)\cdot\deg(V) = a\cdot d$ points. These intersection points correspond biject
ively to $H\cap\Gamma$, confirming $\deg(\Gamma)=ad$.

### Conclusion

The degree of the image of $C$ under the Veronese map $\nu_{n,d}$ is

$$\boxed{ad}.$$
