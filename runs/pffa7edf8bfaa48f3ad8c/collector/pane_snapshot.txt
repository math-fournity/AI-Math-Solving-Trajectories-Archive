Let $\zeta = e^{2\pi i/5}$, a primitive 5th root of unity, and set $K = \mathbb{
Q}(\zeta)$. Let $\alpha = \sqrt[5]{2}$. We determine the minimal polynomial of $
\alpha$ over $K$.

**Step 1: Degrees of the two extensions over $\mathbb{Q}$.**

The minimal polynomial of $\zeta$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is the 5th cyclotomic polyno
mial
$$\Phi_5(x) = x^4 + x^3 + x^2 + x + 1,$$
which is irreducible of degree 4, so $[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta):\mathbb{Q}] = 4$.

The minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is $x^5 - 2$, which is irre
ducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ by Eisenstein's criterion at $p = 2$, so $[\mathbb{Q}(
\alpha):\mathbb{Q}] = 5$.

**Step 2: The intersection $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta) \cap \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ is $\math
bb{Q}$.**

Since 5 is prime, $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)/\mathbb{Q}$ has no proper intermediate fie
lds: any subfield has degree dividing 5, so it is either $\mathbb{Q}$ or $\mathb
b{Q}(\alpha)$ itself. The intersection $L = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta) \cap \mathbb{Q}(\a
lpha)$ is a subfield of $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$, so $[L:\mathbb{Q}] \in \{1, 5\}$.
It is also a subfield of $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$, so $[L:\mathbb{Q}]$ divides $[\mat
hbb{Q}(\zeta):\mathbb{Q}] = 4$. Since $\gcd(1,4)=1$ and $\gcd(5,4)=1$ but $[L:\m
athbb{Q}]$ must divide 4, the only possibility is $[L:\mathbb{Q}] = 1$, i.e. $L
= \mathbb{Q}$.

**Step 3: Degree of $\alpha$ over $K$.**

Since the two extensions have trivial intersection,
$$[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta, \alpha) : \mathbb{Q}] = [\mathbb{Q}(\zeta):\mathbb{Q}] \cdo
t [\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}] = 4 \cdot 5 = 20.$$
Therefore
$$[K(\alpha) : K] = \frac{[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta,\alpha):\mathbb{Q}]}{[\mathbb{Q}(\ze
ta):\mathbb{Q}]} = \frac{20}{4} = 5.$$
So the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $K$ has degree 5.

**Step 4: Identifying the minimal polynomial.**

The element $\alpha$ is a root of $x^5 - 2 \in \mathbb{Q}[x] \subseteq K[x]$. Th
e minimal polynomial $m_{\alpha, K}(x) \in K[x]$ is monic, divides $x^5 - 2$ in
$K[x]$, and has degree $[K(\alpha):K] = 5$. Since $x^5 - 2$ is monic of degree 5
 and is divisible by the degree-5 monic polynomial $m_{\alpha,K}$, we must have
$$m_{\alpha, K}(x) = x^5 - 2.$$

This polynomial has rational (indeed integer) coefficients, as required.
