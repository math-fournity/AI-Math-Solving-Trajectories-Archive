# Proof

**Answer: Yes.** Every root of $m_\alpha$ is a Galois conjugate of $\alpha$ unde
r $\text{Gal}(K/F)$.

## Setup and key observations

Since $K/F$ is Galois, it is **normal** and **separable**, and $[K:F]<\infty$.

**Claim 1:** $m_\alpha$ splits completely in $K$.

Since $\alpha \in K$ and $K/F$ is finite, $\alpha$ is algebraic over $F$, so $m_
\alpha$ exists. Since $m_\alpha$ is irreducible over $F$ and has the root $\alph
a \in K$, normality of $K/F$ forces $m_\alpha$ to split completely over $K$. Sep
arability ensures $m_\alpha$ has no repeated roots.

**Claim 2:** For every $\phi \in \text{Gal}(K/F)$, $\phi(\alpha)$ is a root of $
m_\alpha$.

The coefficients of $m_\alpha$ lie in $F$, and $\phi$ fixes $F$ pointwise. Apply
ing $\phi$ to $m_\alpha(\alpha)=0$ gives $m_\alpha(\phi(\alpha))=0$. Hence the o
rbit $\{\phi(\alpha):\phi\in\text{Gal}(K/F)\}$ is contained in the root set of $
m_\alpha$.

## The converse (the substantive direction)

Let $\beta$ be any root of $m_\alpha$. By Claim 1, $\beta \in K$. We must find $
\phi\in\text{Gal}(K/F)$ with $\phi(\alpha)=\beta$.

**Step 1: Isomorphism of simple extensions.** Since $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are bot
h roots of the irreducible polynomial $m_\alpha\in F[x]$, the evaluation maps gi
ve an $F$-isomorphism
$$\sigma: F(\alpha) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} F(\beta), \qquad \sigma(\alpha)=\beta
,\quad \sigma|_F=\mathrm{id}.$$

**Step 2: Extension to an automorphism of $K$.** Since $K/F$ is normal, $K$ is t
he splitting field of some polynomial $f\in F[x]$. Then $K$ is also the splittin
g field of $f$ over $F(\alpha)$, and $\sigma(K)$ is the splitting field of $f$ (
with coefficients transported by $\sigma$, but $\sigma$ fixes $F$ so $f$ is unch
anged) over $F(\beta)$. But $F(\beta)\subseteq K$ and $K$ is normal over $F$, so
 $K$ itself is a splitting field of $f$ over $F(\beta)$. By the **isomorphism ex
tension theorem** (splitting fields over isomorphic base fields are isomorphic,
and the isomorphism extends), the $F$-isomorphism $\sigma:F(\alpha)\to F(\beta)$
 extends to an $F$-automorphism
$$\phi: K \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} K, \qquad \phi|_{F(\alpha)}=\sigma.$$

Since $\phi$ is an $F$-automorphism of $K$, we have $\phi\in\text{Gal}(K/F)$, an
d
$$\phi(\alpha)=\sigma(\alpha)=\beta.$$

## Conclusion

We have shown:
- Every $\phi(\alpha)$ for $\phi\in\text{Gal}(K/F)$ is a root of $m_\alpha$ (Cla
im 2),
- Every root $\beta$ of $m_\alpha$ equals $\phi(\alpha)$ for some $\phi\in\text{
Gal}(K/F)$ (Steps 1–2).

Therefore the set of roots of $m_\alpha$ is **exactly** the orbit of $\alpha$ un
der $\text{Gal}(K/F)$. In particular, **yes**, every root of $m_\alpha$ is a Gal
ois conjugate of $\alpha$.
