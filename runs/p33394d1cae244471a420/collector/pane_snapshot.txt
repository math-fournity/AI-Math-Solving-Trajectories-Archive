**Theorem.** *Let $X$ be a compact connected manifold (without boundary) and $U
\subset X$ a non-empty open set. Then there exists a smooth vector field on $X$
with only finitely many zeros, all contained in $U$.*

**Proof.**

Let $n = \dim X$.

**Case $n = 0$:** $X$ is a single point, so $U = X$. The zero vector field has o
ne zero, which lies in $U$. ✓

**Case $n = 1$:** $X \cong S^1$ and $\chi(S^1) = 0$. The standard non-vanishing
vector field $\partial/\partial\theta$ has no zeros at all. ✓

set U$ with $\overline{B} \subset U$. It suffices to construct a smooth vector f
