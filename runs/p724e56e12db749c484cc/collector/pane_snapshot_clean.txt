## Proof of Morley's Congruence

Let $p \ge 5$ be prime and set $n = \frac{p-1}{2}$, so $p = 2n+1$.

### Step 1: Two product representations

**LHS product.** Since $\binom{p-1}{n} = \prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{p-k}{k} = (-1)^n\p
rod_{k=1}^{n}\left(1-\frac{p}{k}\right)$, we get:
$$(-1)^n\binom{p-1}{n} = \prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(1-\frac{p}{k}\right). \tag{1}$$

**RHS product.** We claim the exact identity (in $\mathbb{Q}$):
$$4^n = \prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(1+\frac{p}{2k-1}\right). \tag{2}$$

*Proof of (2):* Since $(p-1)! = n!\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{n}(n+k)$, we compute:
$$\prod_{k=1}^{n}(n+k) = \prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{p-1+2k}{2} = \frac{1}{2^n}\prod_{k
=1}^{n}(p+2k-1) = \frac{1}{2^n}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(2k-1)\left(1+\frac{p}{2k-1}\right
).$$
Using $\prod_{k=1}^{n}(2k-1) = \frac{(2n)!}{2^n n!} = \frac{(p-1)!}{2^n n!}$, we
 get:
$$\prod_{k=1}^{n}(n+k) = \frac{(p-1)!}{4^n n!}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(1+\frac{p}{2k
-1}\right).$$
Substituting back: $(p-1)! = n!\cdot\frac{(p-1)!}{4^n n!}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(1+
\frac{p}{2k-1}\right)$, which gives (2). $\square$

From (2): $4^{p-1} = 4^{2n} = (4^n)^2 = \left[\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(1+\frac{p}{2k
-1}\right)\right]^2. \tag{3}$

### Step 2: $p$-adic logarithms

Both sides of (1) and (3) are $\equiv 1 \pmod{p}$ (each factor is $\equiv 1 \pmo
d{p}$, and $4^{p-1}\equiv 1\pmod{p}$ by Fermat). For $p$ odd, the $p$-adic logar
ithm $\ln(1+x) \equiv x - \frac{x^2}{2} \pmod{p^3}$ when $v_p(x)\ge 1$, and $\ln
$ is a $p$-adic isometry on $1+p\mathbb{Z}_p$ (i.e., $v_p(\ln A - \ln B) = v_p(A
-B)$ when $A\equiv B\equiv 1\pmod{p}$). So it suffices to show the logarithms ag
ree mod $p^3$.

Define $S_1 = \sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}$, $\;S_2 = \sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k^2}$,
 $\;O_1 = \sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2k-1}$, $\;O_2 = \sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(2k-1)^
2}$.

**LHS log:** $\displaystyle\ln\left((-1)^n\binom{p-1}{n}\right) = \sum_{k=1}^{n}
\ln\left(1-\frac{p}{k}\right) \equiv -p\,S_1 - \frac{p^2}{2}\,S_2 \pmod{p^3}. \t
ag{4}$

**RHS log:** $\displaystyle\ln\left(4^{p-1}\right) = 2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\ln\left(1+\
frac{p}{2k-1}\right) \equiv 2p\,O_1 - p^2\,O_2 \pmod{p^3}. \tag{5}$

### Step 3: Harmonic sum congruences

The full harmonic sums decompose as $H_{p-1} = O_1 + \frac{1}{2}S_1$ (odd terms
give $O_1$, even terms $2,4,\ldots,2n$ give $\frac{1}{2}S_1$) and $H_{p-1}^{(2)}
 = O_2 + \frac{1}{4}S_2$.

**(a) Wolstenholme's theorem** ($p\ge 5$): $H_{p-1} \equiv 0 \pmod{p^2}$, so:
$$O_1 \equiv -\tfrac{1}{2}\,S_1 \pmod{p^2}. \tag{6}$$

**(b)** $H_{p-1}^{(2)} \equiv 0 \pmod{p}$: since $\frac{1}{k^2}\equiv k^{p-3}\pm
od{p}$ and $\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}k^m\equiv 0\pmod{p}$ when $(p-1)\nmid m$ (here $m=p-
3$ with $0<p-3<p-1$ for $p\ge 5$). This gives:
$$O_2 \equiv -\tfrac{1}{4}\,S_2 \pmod{p}. \tag{7}$$

**(c)** $S_2 \equiv 0 \pmod{p}$: by pairing $k$ with $p-k$ in $H_{p-1}^{(2)}$, w
e get $\sum_{k=n+1}^{2n}\frac{1}{k^2} = \sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(p-j)^2}\equiv \s
um_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j^2}=S_2\pmod{p}$, so $H_{p-1}^{(2)}\equiv 2S_2\pmod{p}$. S
ince $H_{p-1}^{(2)}\equiv 0$ and $p\ge 5$ (so $2$ is invertible):
$$S_2 \equiv 0 \pmod{p}. \tag{8}$$

### Step 4: Conclusion

Substituting (6), (7), (8) into (5):
$$\text{RHS log} \equiv 2p\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}S_1\right) - p^2\left(-\tfrac{1}{4}
S_2\right) = -p\,S_1 + \tfrac{p^2}{4}\,S_2 \equiv -p\,S_1 \pmod{p^3},$$
since $\frac{p^2}{4}S_2 \equiv 0 \pmod{p^3}$ by (8).

Substituting (8) into (4):
$$\text{LHS log} \equiv -p\,S_1 - \tfrac{p^2}{2}\,S_2 \equiv -p\,S_1 \pmod{p^3}.
$$

Therefore $\ln\left((-1)^n\binom{p-1}{n}\right) \equiv \ln\left(4^{p-1}\right) \
pmod{p^3}$. Since the $p$-adic logarithm is an isometry on $1+p\mathbb{Z}_p$ (fo
r $p$ odd), this implies:
$$(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\binom{p-1}{\frac{p-1}{2}} \equiv 4^{p-1} \pmod{p^3}.$$

Hence:
$$(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\binom{p-1}{\frac{p-1}{2}} - 4^{p-1} \equiv \boxed{0} \pmo
d{p^3}.$$
