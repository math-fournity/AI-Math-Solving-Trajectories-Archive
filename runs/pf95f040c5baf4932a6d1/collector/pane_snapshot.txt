Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum $m$ (as a function of $n$) s
uch that some simplex with $n$ vertices in $\mathbb{R}^{n-1}$ projects to a regu
lar $m$-gon in 2D.

**Answer: $m = n$.**

---

**Upper bound: $m \leq n$.**

The projection of $n$ points into $\mathbb{R}^2$ produces at most $n$ distinct p
oints. Their convex hull is a polygon with at most $n$ vertices. For this to be
a regular $m$-gon, we need $m \leq n$.

---

**Construction achieving $m = n$.**

Let $w_0, w_1, \ldots, w_{n-1}$ be the vertices of a regular $n$-gon in $\mathbb
{R}^2$, e.g., $w_k = (\cos(2\pi k/n), \sin(2\pi k/n))$.

Work in $\mathbb{R}^{n-1} = \mathbb{R}^2 \times \mathbb{R}^{n-3}$ (for $n \geq 3
$; cases $n=1,2$ are trivial). Let $P:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}^2$ be the pr
ojection onto the first two coordinates. We seek points $p_k = (w_k, q_k)$ with
$q_k \in \mathbb{R}^{n-3}$ such that $p_0,\ldots,p_{n-1}$ are affinely independe
nt. Then $P(p_k) = w_k$ gives the regular $n$-gon.

**Affine independence condition.** The points $p_0,\ldots,p_{n-1}$ are affinely
independent iff there is no nonzero $c = (c_0,\ldots,c_{n-1}) \in \mathbb{R}^n$
with:
$$\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} c_k = 0, \qquad \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} c_k\, w_k = 0, \qquad \sum_{
k=0}^{n-1} c_k\, q_k = 0.$$

The first two conditions define the **affine dependence space** of the regular $
n$-gon vertices. Since the $w_k$ lie in $\mathbb{R}^2$ and have affine rank $3$
(any three vertices of a regular $n$-gon, $n\geq 3$, are non-collinear), this de
pendence space has dimension $n - 3$.

Let $d_1, \ldots, d_{n-3} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ be a basis for this dependence space
, and form the matrix $D = [d_1^T; \cdots; d_{n-3}^T]$, which is $(n{-}3)\times
n$ with full row rank $n{-}3$.

Any $c$ satisfying the first two conditions can be written $c = D^T \alpha$ for
some $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}^{n-3}$. The third condition becomes:
$$\sum_k c_k\, q_k = 0 \iff Q^T c = 0 \iff Q^T D^T \alpha = 0 \iff (DQ)^T \alpha
 = 0,$$
where $Q$ is the $n \times (n{-}3)$ matrix with rows $q_0, \ldots, q_{n-1}$.

**Choosing $Q$.** Since $D$ has full row rank $n{-}3$, the linear map $Q \mapsto
 DQ$ from $\mathbb{R}^{n \times (n-3)}$ to $\mathbb{R}^{(n-3)\times(n-3)}$ is su
rjective. Choose $Q$ such that $DQ = I_{n-3}$ (the identity matrix). Then $(DQ)^
T \alpha = \alpha = 0$ forces $\alpha = 0$, hence $c = 0$.

Therefore $p_0, \ldots, p_{n-1}$ are affinely independent, forming a valid simpl
ex in $\mathbb{R}^{n-1}$, and their projection is the regular $n$-gon.

**Small cases.** For $n=1$: a single point projects to a point ($m=1$). For $n=2
$: a segment projects to a segment ($m=2$). For $n=3$: $n-3=0$, so $q_k \in \mat
hbb{R}^0$ and $p_k = w_k$ are the vertices of an equilateral triangle, already a
ffinely independent in $\mathbb{R}^2$ ($m=3$).

---

**Conclusion.** The upper bound $m \leq n$ and the construction achieving $m = n
$ together give:

$$\boxed{m = n}.$$
