## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** Every continuous function $f$ on $\overline{\Omega}$ that vanis
hes on $\partial\Omega$ can be approximated uniformly on $\overline{\Omega}$ by
smooth functions with compact support contained in $\Omega$.

---

**Setup and key observation.** Let
$$\Omega_k := \{x \in \Omega : \operatorname{dist}(x, \partial\Omega) > 1/k\}, \
qquad k = 1,2,\dots$$
Since $\Omega$ is bounded, $\overline{\Omega}$ is compact, and $f$ is uniformly
continuous on $\overline{\Omega}$. Since $f = 0$ on $\partial\Omega$, for every
$\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $\delta > 0$ such that
$$\operatorname{dist}(x, \partial\Omega) < \delta \;\Longrightarrow\; |f(x)| < \
varepsilon. \tag{1}$$

**Step 1 — Truncation by a smooth cutoff.** Choose $k$ large enough that $1/k <
\delta/2$, and let $\psi \in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ be a smooth cutoff satisfying
$$\psi \equiv 1 \text{ on } \overline{\Omega}_{2k}, \qquad \operatorname{supp}\p
si \subset \Omega_k, \qquad 0 \le \psi \le 1.$$
Such a $\psi$ exists by the standard smooth-Urysohn / mollified-distance constru
ction (e.g. $\psi = \rho_{1/(4k)} * \mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{3k/2}}$ with a suitable
cutoff, supported in $\Omega_k$ and equal to $1$ on $\Omega_{2k}$).

Define $g := \psi \cdot f$, extended by $0$ outside $\operatorname{supp}\psi$. T
hen:
- $g$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}^n$ with $\operatorname{supp} g \subset \Omega
_k \Subset \Omega$ (compact subset of $\Omega$);
- On $\overline{\Omega}_{2k}$, $g = f$; on $\Omega \setminus \Omega_{2k}$, $|g -
 f| = |(\psi - 1)f| \le |f| < \varepsilon$ by (1), since $\operatorname{dist}(x,
\partial\Omega) < 2/k < \delta$ there; on $\partial\Omega$, both $g$ and $f$ are
 $0$.

Hence
$$\|g - f\|_{L^\infty(\overline{\Omega})} \le \varepsilon. \tag{2}$$

**Step 2 — Mollification preserving compact support in $\Omega$.** Since $\opera
torname{supp} g \subset \Omega_k$, i.e. $\operatorname{dist}(\operatorname{supp}
g, \partial\Omega) \ge 1/k$, choose a mollifier radius $r < 1/(2k)$ and set
$$h := g * \rho_r,$$
where $\rho_r(x) = r^{-n}\rho(x/r)$ is a standard smooth mollifier. Then:
- $h \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$;
- $\operatorname{supp} h \subset \operatorname{supp}g + \overline{B(0,r)} \subse
t \{x : \operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega) \ge 1/k - r\} \subset \Omega_{k/2}
 \Subset \Omega$,

so $h$ has **compact support in $\Omega$** and is smooth.

Since $g$ is uniformly continuous (it is continuous with compact support), for $
r$ sufficiently small,
$$\|h - g\|_{L^\infty} = \|g * \rho_r - g\|_{L^\infty} \le \varepsilon. \tag{3}$
$

**Step 3 — Conclusion.** Combining (2) and (3),
$$\|h - f\|_{L^\infty(\overline{\Omega})} \le \|h - g\|_\infty + \|g - f\|_{L^\i
nfty(\overline{\Omega})} \le 2\varepsilon.$$

Since $\varepsilon > 0$ was arbitrary, we obtain a sequence $h_m \in C_c^\infty(
\Omega)$ with $h_m \to f$ uniformly on $\overline{\Omega}$.

**Remark.** The essential reason this works is that $f$ vanishing on $\partial\O
mega$ makes $f$ small near the boundary, which is exactly the regime where compa
ctly-supported-in-$\Omega$ approximants are forced to be zero. The hypotheses th
at $\Omega$ is bounded (giving compactness of $\overline{\Omega}$ and uniform co
ntinuity) and that $f = 0$ on $\partial\Omega$ are both used; connectedness is n
ot needed for the result.
