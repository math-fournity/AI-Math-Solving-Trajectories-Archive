## Proof

We show that $f$ is a closed immersion. Since $Y$ is affine, every closed subsch
eme of $Y$ is affine, and the conclusion follows.

**Step 1 (Monomorphism $\Rightarrow$ radicial and injective on points).** Monomo
rphisms are stable under base change. For each $y \in Y$, the fiber $X_y = X \ti
mes_Y \operatorname{Spec} k(y) \to \operatorname{Spec} k(y)$ is a monomorphism.
A monomorphism $Z \to \operatorname{Spec} k$ to the spectrum of a field is injec
tive on underlying points, so $Z$ has at most one point; and if $z \in Z$ is tha
t point, then $k \to k(z)$ is a ring epimorphism (because $\operatorname{Spec} k
(z) \to \operatorname{Spec} k$ is a monomorphism). A field extension that is a r
ing epimorphism must be an isomorphism: if $L \supsetneq k$, then (for $L/k$ alg
ebraic) two distinct $k$-embeddings of $L$ into an algebraic closure, or (for $L
/k$ transcendental) a nontrivial $k$-automorphism of $L$, contradict the epimorp
hism property. Hence $k(z) = k(y)$. Therefore each fiber is either empty or $\op
eratorname{Spec} k(y)$; in particular $f$ is injective on points and radicial.

**Step 2 (Proper $+$ radicial $\Rightarrow$ finite).** Since $f$ is radicial, ev
ery fiber has at most one point, so $f$ is quasi-finite. A morphism that is both
 proper and quasi-finite is finite (Grothendieck's form of Zariski's Main Theore
m, EGA IV, 8.11.1). Thus $f$ is finite.

**Step 3 (Finite monomorphism $\Rightarrow$ closed immersion).** Since $f$ is fi
nite, $X \cong \operatorname{Spec}_Y \mathcal{A}$ where $\mathcal{A} = f_*\mathc
al{O}_X$ is a coherent $\mathcal{O}_Y$-algebra. A morphism is a monomorphism iff
 its diagonal is an isomorphism; under $X = \operatorname{Spec}_Y \mathcal{A}$,
the diagonal corresponds to the multiplication map
$$\mu : \mathcal{A} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_Y} \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{A}, \quad a
 \otimes b \mapsto ab.$$
So $f$ is a monomorphism iff $\mu$ is an isomorphism.

The map $\mu$ is always surjective (since $\mu(a \otimes 1) = a$), hence $\mu$ i
s an isomorphism iff $\ker \mu = 0$. The kernel of $\mu$ is the ideal generated
by $\{a \otimes 1 - 1 \otimes a : a \in \mathcal{A}\}$, so $\ker \mu = 0$ iff $a
 \otimes 1 = 1 \otimes a$ for all $a \in \mathcal{A}$. The equalizer of the two
maps $\mathcal{A} \rightrightarrows \mathcal{A} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_Y} \mathcal
{A}$ (given by $a \mapsto a \otimes 1$ and $a \mapsto 1 \otimes a$) is exactly t
he image of $\mathcal{O}_Y \to \mathcal{A}$. Therefore $\mu$ is an isomorphism i
ff $\mathcal{O}_Y \to \mathcal{A}$ is surjective.

Setting $\mathcal{I} = \ker(\mathcal{O}_Y \to \mathcal{A})$, we get $\mathcal{A}
 \cong \mathcal{O}_Y / \mathcal{I}$, and thus
$$X \cong \operatorname{Spec}_Y(\mathcal{O}_Y/\mathcal{I}) = V(\mathcal{I}) \hoo
krightarrow Y,$$
i.e., $f$ is a closed immersion.

**Conclusion.** Since $f$ is a closed immersion and $Y$ is affine, $X$ is isomor
phic to a closed subscheme of an affine scheme, hence $X$ is affine. (The hypoth
eses that $X$ is connected and that $Y$ is Noetherian of finite Krull dimension
are not needed for the conclusion.) $\square$
