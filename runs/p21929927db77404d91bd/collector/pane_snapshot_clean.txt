**Answer: Yes, every Hilbert space has an orthonormal basis** (assuming the Axio
m of Choice, equivalently Zorn's Lemma).

---

**Proof.**

Let $H$ be a Hilbert space (over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$). We must show tha
t $H$ admits a maximal orthonormal set, and that such a maximal set is an orthon
ormal basis (i.e., its linear span is dense in $H$).

**Step 1: Existence of a maximal orthonormal set via Zorn's Lemma.**

Let $\mathcal{O}$ denote the collection of all orthonormal subsets of $H$, parti
ally ordered by inclusion. $\mathcal{O}$ is nonempty when $H \neq \{0\}$ (any un
it vector forms an orthonormal set $\{e\}$); when $H = \{0\}$ the empty set is a
n orthonormal basis and we are done.

Let $\{S_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in A}$ be a totally ordered (by inclusion) chain in $
\mathcal{O}$. We claim $S = \bigcup_{\alpha \in A} S_\alpha$ is an upper bound i
n $\mathcal{O}$.

- $S$ is orthonormal: take any two distinct $x, y \in S$. Then $x \in S_\alpha$
and $y \in S_\beta$ for some $\alpha, \beta$. Since the chain is totally ordered
, say $S_\alpha \subseteq S_\beta$, so both $x, y \in S_\beta$, which is orthono
rmal; hence $\langle x, y \rangle = 0$ and $\|x\| = \|y\| = 1$.
- Clearly $S_\alpha \subseteq S$ for every $\alpha$, so $S$ is an upper bound.

Thus every chain has an upper bound. By **Zorn's Lemma**, $\mathcal{O}$ has a ma
ximal element $B$.

**Step 2: $B$ is an orthonormal basis.**

It remains to show $\overline{\operatorname{span}(B)} = H$. Suppose for contradi
ction that $\overline{\operatorname{span}(B)} \subsetneq H$. Then there exists $
v \in H \setminus \overline{\operatorname{span}(B)}$. Let $P$ be the orthogonal
projection onto the closed subspace $\overline{\operatorname{span}(B)}$, and set
$$u = v - Pv.$$
Since $v \notin \overline{\operatorname{span}(B)}$, we have $u \neq 0$, and $u \
perp \overline{\operatorname{span}(B)}$, in particular $u \perp B$. Setting $e =
 u/\|u\|$, the set $B \cup \{e\}$ is orthonormal and strictly contains $B$, cont
radicting the maximality of $B$.

Therefore $\overline{\operatorname{span}(B)} = H$, and $B$ is an orthonormal bas
is of $H$.

**Step 3: The Axiom of Choice is essential.**

The use of Zorn's Lemma (equivalent to the Axiom of Choice) is unavoidable in ge
neral. In models of ZF without Choice, there exist Hilbert spaces — even closed
subspaces of $\ell^2$ in certain models — that admit no orthonormal basis. Concr
etely, in ZF it is consistent that there exists a Hilbert space with no orthonor
mal basis (e.g., a Hilbert space whose unit ball lacks a well-ordering, or where
 one cannot extract a maximal orthonormal set). Thus the statement "every Hilber
t space has an orthonormal basis" is equivalent (over ZF) to a suitable choice p
rinciple and is **not** provable in ZF alone.

**Conclusion.** In ZFC (standard foundations of functional analysis), every Hilb
ert space $H$ possesses an orthonormal basis: a maximal orthonormal set exists b
y Zorn's Lemma, and maximality forces its closed linear span to be all of $H$. T
he result relies essentially on the Axiom of Choice and fails in ZF without it.
