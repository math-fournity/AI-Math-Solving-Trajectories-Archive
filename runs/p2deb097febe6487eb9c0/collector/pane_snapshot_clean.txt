# Proof

**Answer: Yes.** $\mathcal{V}(S,T)$ is the closure of $\mathcal{W}$ with respect
 to the norm $\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}} = \bigl(E\!\bigl[\int_S^T f^2\,dt\bigr]\bigr)^
{1/2}$.

We prove this by showing $\mathcal{W}$ is dense in $\mathcal{V}$ under this norm
, via a standard three-step approximation.

---

## Setup and notation

Recall $\mathcal{W}$ consists of **elementary** (simple) adapted functions of th
e form
$$\phi(t,\omega)=\sum_{j} e_j(\omega)\,\mathbf{1}_{(t_j,t_{j+1}]}(t), \qquad e_j
 \in \mathcal{F}_{t_j},\quad S\le t_j < t_{j+1}\le T,$$
with $E\!\bigl[\int_S^T \phi^2\,dt\bigr]<\infty$. Clearly $\mathcal{W}\subset\ma
thcal{V}$, since elementary functions are $\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{F}$-measura
ble, adapted, and square-integrable by assumption.

We must show: for every $f\in\mathcal{V}$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, there exist
s $\phi\in\mathcal{W}$ with $\|f-\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}}<\varepsilon$.

---

## Step 1 — Bounded functions continuous in $t$

Let $f\in\mathcal{V}$ be **bounded** and **continuous in $t$** for a.e. $\omega$
. Partition $[S,T]$ by $S=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_N=T$ with mesh $\|\pi\|<\delta$. Defi
ne
$$\phi_n(t,\omega)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1} f(t_j,\omega)\,\mathbf{1}_{(t_j,t_{j+1}]}(t)
.$$
Since $f(t_j,\cdot)$ is $\mathcal{F}_{t_j}$-measurable (adaptedness), $\phi_n$ i
s elementary, hence $\phi_n\in\mathcal{W}$. By continuity in $t$ and boundedness
,
$$\sup_{t\in[S,T]}|f(t,\omega)-\phi_n(t,\omega)|\xrightarrow{\|\pi\|\to 0}0 \qua
d\text{for a.e. }\omega,$$
and the convergence is dominated by $2\|f\|_\infty$. By dominated convergence,
$$E\!\Bigl[\int_S^T (f-\phi_n)^2\,dt\Bigr]\to 0. \qquad\square_{\text{Step 1}}$$

---

## Step 2 — General bounded adapted functions

Let $f\in\mathcal{V}$ be **bounded** (not necessarily continuous in $t$). We reg
ularize in $t$ by time-averaging. For $n\ge 1$ define (extending $f$ by $0$ for
$t<S$)
$$f_n(t,\omega)=n\int_{t-1/n}^{t} f(s,\omega)\,ds, \qquad t\in[S,T].$$

**Adaptedness:** For each $t$, the integral involves only $f(s,\cdot)$ with $s\l
e t$, and $f(s,\cdot)$ is $\mathcal{F}_s\subseteq\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable. Henc
e $f_n(t,\cdot)$ is $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable.

**Measurability and boundedness:** $f_n$ is $\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{F}$-measu
rable, bounded by $\|f\|_\infty$, and continuous in $t$ (as a moving average). T
hus $f_n\in\mathcal{V}$ and satisfies the hypotheses of Step 1.

**Convergence:** By Fubini and the Lebesgue differentiation theorem, for a.e. $\
omega$, $f_n(\cdot,\omega)\to f(\cdot,\omega)$ in $L^2([S,T])$ (in fact a.e. in
$t$). Since $|f_n|\le\|f\|_\infty$, dominated convergence gives
$$E\!\Bigl[\int_S^T (f_n-f)^2\,dt\Bigr]\to 0.$$

By Step 1, each $f_n$ is approximable by elementary functions. By the triangle i
nequality for $\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{W}}$, $f$ is approximable by elementary funct
ions. $\square_{\text{Step 2}}$

---

## Step 3 — General (unbounded) functions in $\mathcal{V}$

Let $f\in\mathcal{V}$ be arbitrary. Truncate:
$$f_n(t,\omega)=f(t,\omega)\,\mathbf{1}_{\{|f(t,\omega)|\le n\}}.$$
Then $f_n$ is bounded, $\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{F}$-measurable, and $\mathcal{
F}_t$-adapted (multiplying an $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable function by a determini
stic-in-$t$ cutoff preserves adaptedness). So $f_n\in\mathcal{V}$ and is bounded
, falling under Step 2.

Since $f\in\mathcal{V}$ means $E\!\bigl[\int_S^T f^2\,dt\bigr]<\infty$, we have
$f\in L^2([S,T]\times\Omega)$, and $f_n\to f$ pointwise with $|f_n|\le|f|$. By d
ominated convergence (dominated by $f^2$),
$$E\!\Bigl[\int_S^T (f_n-f)^2\,dt\Bigr]\to 0.$$

Combining with Step 2 and the triangle inequality, $f$ is approximable by elemen
tary functions in $\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{W}}$. $\square_{\text{Step 3}}$

---

## Conclusion

Every $f\in\mathcal{V}$ is the $\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{W}}$-limit of a sequence in
$\mathcal{W}$, and $\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{V}$. Therefore
$$\mathcal{V}=\overline{\mathcal{W}}^{\,\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{W}}}.$$

This is precisely the density result that licenses the definition of the Itô int
egral via $L^2(P)$-limits of elementary-function integrals, as stated in the pro
blem.
