**Construction.** Let $V$ be the vector space over a field $k$ with countable ba

$$A(e_1) = 0, \qquad A(e_n) = e_{n-1} \quad \text{for } n \ge 2.$$
This is the "backward shift" (or "left shift") on the space of finitely supporte
d sequences. $V$ is infinite-dimensional since its basis is infinite.

**Every vector is annihilated by some power of $A$.** Let $v \in V$ be arbitrary
. Since $v$ is a finite linear combination, $v = \sum_{i=1}^{m} a_i e_i$ for som
e $m$ and scalars $a_i$. A direct computation shows $A^n(e_i) = e_{i-n}$ for $i
> n$ and $A^n(e_i) = 0$ for $i \le n$. Hence
$$A^m(v) = \sum_{i=1}^{m} a_i\, A^m(e_i) = \sum_{i=1}^{m} a_i \cdot 0 = 0.$$
So for every $v$ there exists $n$ (namely $n = m$, the highest index appearing i
n $v$) with $A^n(v) = 0$.

**$A$ is not nilpotent.** For any positive integer $N$, consider the basis vecto
r $e_{N+1}$. Then
$$A^N(e_{N+1}) = e_1 \ne 0.$$
Therefore $A^N \ne 0$ for every $N \ge 1$, so $A$ is not nilpotent.

**Conclusion.** The operator $A$ on the infinite-dimensional space $V$ satisfies
: every vector is killed by some power of $A$, yet no single power of $A$ is the
 zero map. Hence such a linear transformation exists.
