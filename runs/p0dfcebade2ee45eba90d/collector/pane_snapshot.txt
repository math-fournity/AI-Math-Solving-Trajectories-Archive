## Proof

**Claim:** In an algebraically closed field $k$, every prime ideal corresponding
 to an irreducible curve of dimension 1 in the affine plane $\mathbb{A}^2_k$ is
principal.

**Setup.** The affine plane $\mathbb{A}^2_k$ has coordinate ring $R = k[x,y]$. S
ince $k$ is a field, $R$ is a polynomial ring over a field, hence a UFD (in fact
, a regular ring of dimension 2).

An irreducible curve of dimension 1 in $\mathbb{A}^2_k$ is an irreducible closed
 subset of dimension 1. By the standard correspondence between irreducible close
d subsets of an affine variety and prime ideals in its coordinate ring, such a c
urve corresponds to a prime ideal $\mathfrak{p} \subset R$ with $\dim(R/\mathfra
k{p}) = 1$.

**Height computation.** The ring $R = k[x,y]$ has Krull dimension 2. Since $R$ i
s a finitely generated algebra over a field, it is catenary, and for every prime
 $\mathfrak{p}$ we have
$$\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) + \dim(R/\mathfrak{p}) = \dim(R) = 2.$$
Since $\dim(R/\mathfrak{p}) = 1$, we obtain $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) = 1
$.

**Key theorem: In a UFD, every height-1 prime ideal is principal.**

*Proof of theorem.* Let $R$ be a UFD and let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a prime ideal of
height 1. Since $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) = 1$, we have $(0) \subsetneq \
mathfrak{p}$, so $\mathfrak{p}$ contains a non-zero element. Pick $0 \neq f \in
\mathfrak{p}$ and factor it into irreducibles:
$$f = u \cdot p_1 p_2 \cdots p_r,$$
where $u$ is a unit and each $p_i$ is irreducible. Since $\mathfrak{p}$ is a pri
me ideal and $p_1 \cdots p_r \in \mathfrak{p}$, primality gives $p_i \in \mathfr
ak{p}$ for some $i$.

In a UFD, every irreducible element is prime (this is equivalent to the uniquene
ss of factorization). Therefore $(p_i)$ is a prime ideal. We now have a chain
$$(0) \subsetneq (p_i) \subseteq \mathfrak{p}.$$
Since $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) = 1$, there is no prime strictly between
$(0)$ and $\mathfrak{p}$. The prime $(p_i)$ satisfies $(0) \subsetneq (p_i) \sub
seteq \mathfrak{p}$, so we must have $(p_i) = \mathfrak{p}$. Hence $\mathfrak{p}
$ is principal, generated by the irreducible element $p_i$. $\square$

**Conclusion.** The prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ corresponding to an irreducible c
urve of dimension 1 in $\mathbb{A}^2_k$ has height 1 in the UFD $k[x,y]$. By the
 theorem above, $\mathfrak{p}$ is principal — generated by a single irreducible
polynomial.

**Answer: Yes.** Every prime ideal corresponding to an irreducible curve of dime
nsion 1 in the affine plane over an algebraically closed field is principal. Con
cretely, such a prime ideal is of the form $(f)$ where $f \in k[x,y]$ is an irre
ducible polynomial.
