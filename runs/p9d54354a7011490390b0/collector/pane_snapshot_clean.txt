## Solution

**Key observation.** The numerator and denominator are the imaginary and real pa
rts of a binomial sum with complex exponentials.

**Step 1: Complex representation.**

Let $\theta = 1/n^2$. Define
$$S_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\sin\frac{k}{n^2},\qquad C_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n}\
binom{n}{k}\cos\frac{k}{n^2}.$$

By the binomial theorem applied to complex exponentials:
$$C_n + i\,S_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}e^{ik/n^2} = \left(1+e^{i/n^2}\right)
^n.$$

**Step 2: Factor $1+e^{i\theta}$.**

Using the half-angle identity:
$$1+e^{i\theta} = 1+\cos\theta+i\sin\theta = 2\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\left(\cos\fra
c{\theta}{2}+i\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\right)=2\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cdot e^{i\theta/
2}.$$

With $\theta = 1/n^2$:
$$\left(1+e^{i/n^2}\right)^n = \left(2\cos\frac{1}{2n^2}\right)^n \cdot e^{i/(2n
)}.$$

Therefore, separating real and imaginary parts:
$$C_n = \left(2\cos\frac{1}{2n^2}\right)^n\cos\frac{1}{2n},\qquad S_n = \left(2\
cos\frac{1}{2n^2}\right)^n\sin\frac{1}{2n}.$$

**Step 3: The ratio is exact.**

The common factor $\left(2\cos\frac{1}{2n^2}\right)^n$ cancels:
$$\frac{S_n}{C_n} = \frac{\sin\frac{1}{2n}}{\cos\frac{1}{2n}} = \tan\frac{1}{2n}
.$$

So the original expression becomes:
$$\left(\tan\frac{1}{2n}+1\right)^n.$$

**Step 4: Evaluate the limit.**

Take the logarithm:
$$n\ln\left(1+\tan\frac{1}{2n}\right).$$

As $n\to\infty$, $\tan\frac{1}{2n} = \frac{1}{2n}+O(n^{-3})$, so:
$$\ln\left(1+\tan\frac{1}{2n}\right) = \tan\frac{1}{2n}-\frac{1}{2}\tan^2\frac{1
}{2n}+\cdots = \frac{1}{2n}+O(n^{-2}).$$

Hence:
$$n\ln\left(1+\tan\frac{1}{2n}\right) = \frac{1}{2}+O(n^{-1})\;\longrightarrow\;
\frac{1}{2}.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\sin\frac{k}{n^2}}{\sum
_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\cos\frac{k}{n^2}}+1\right)^n = e^{1/2}=\boxed{\sqrt{e}}.$
$
