## Proof

Let $x_n = 1 + \frac{1}{n}$ and define the iterates $a_1 = x_n$, $a_{k+1} = x_n^
{a_k}$. Then $f_n(x_n) = a_n$.

**Step 1: Existence of an attracting fixed point.** For $n \geq 3$, we have $x_n
 \leq \frac{4}{3} < e^{1/e} \approx 1.445$. The fixed-point equation $L = x_n^L$
 is equivalent to $\frac{\ln L}{L} = \ln x_n$. The function $h(t) = \frac{\ln t}
{t}$ satisfies $h(1)=0$, increases to $h(e)=1/e$, then decreases. Since $0 < \ln
 x_n < 1/e$, there is a unique solution $L_n \in (1, e)$. At this fixed point, $
g'(L_n) = L_n \ln x_n = \ln L_n \in (0,1)$, so $L_n$ is attracting.

**Step 2: The initial value is below the fixed point.** We have $a_1 = x_n > 1$
and $a_2 = x_n^{x_n} > x_n^1 = a_1$ (since $x_n > 1$ and the exponent $x_n > 1$)
. The relation $g(a_1) > a_1$ means $a_1$ lies below the smaller fixed point, so
 $a_1 < L_n$.

**Step 3: Monotone increase, bounded above by $L_n$.** Since $g(t) = x_n^t$ is i
ncreasing and $a_1 < L_n$:
- $a_2 = g(a_1) < g(L_n) = L_n$, and by induction $a_k < L_n$ for all $k$.
- $a_2 > a_1$, and since $g$ is increasing, $a_{k+1} = g(a_k) > g(a_{k-1}) = a_k
$ for all $k$.

Thus $(a_k)$ is increasing and bounded above by $L_n$, giving:
$$1 + \frac{1}{n} = a_1 \leq a_n \leq L_n.$$

**Step 4: The fixed point tends to 1.** The map $x \mapsto L(x)$ (the smaller fi
xed point of $t \mapsto x^t$) is increasing in $x$ on $(1, e^{1/e})$. Since $x_n
 = 1+1/n$ is decreasing in $n$, $L_n$ is decreasing, so $L_n \leq L_3$ for all $
n \geq 3$. From $\ln L_n = L_n \ln(1+1/n) \leq L_3 \cdot \frac{1}{n}$, we get $\
ln L_n \to 0$, hence $L_n \to 1$.

**Step 5: Squeeze.** Combining Steps 3 and 4:
$$1 + \frac{1}{n} \;\leq\; f_n\!\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right) = a_n \;\leq\; L_n \;
\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\; 1.$$
Both the lower bound $1 + \frac{1}{n} \to 1$ and the upper bound $L_n \to 1$, so
 by the squeeze theorem:
$$\lim_{n \to \infty} f_n\!\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right) = 1.$$

The statement is **true**. $\blacksquare$
