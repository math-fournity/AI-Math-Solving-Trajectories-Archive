**Proof.** Define the linear map
$$T : L^{2}(X) \to L^{1}(X), \qquad T(f) = f\overline{g}.$$
By hypothesis $T$ is well-defined on all of $L^{2}(X)$, and it is clearly linear
. Both $L^{2}(X)$ and $L^{1}(X)$ are Banach spaces, so we may apply the Closed G
raph Theorem to conclude that $T$ is bounded, provided its graph is closed.

*Closedness of the graph.* Suppose $f_{n} \to f$ in $L^{2}$ and $Tf_{n} = f_{n}\
overline{g} \to h$ in $L^{1}$. Passing to a subsequence (still denoted $f_{n}$),
 we may assume $f_{n} \to f$ $\mu$-a.e. Since $g$ is finite a.e. (it is bounded)
, $f_{n}\overline{g} \to f\overline{g}$ $\mu$-a.e. On the other hand, $f_{n}\ove
rline{g} \to h$ in $L^{1}$ implies a further subsequence converging to $h$ $\mu$
-a.e. Hence $h = f\overline{g} = Tf$ $\mu$-a.e., so the graph is closed.

By the Closed Graph Theorem, $T$ is bounded: there exists $C>0$ with
$$\|f\overline{g}\|_{1} \le C\|f\|_{2} \qquad \forall\, f\in L^{2}(X).$$

Now consider the conjugate-linear functional
$$\Phi : L^{2}(X) \to \mathbb{C}, \qquad \Phi(f) = \int_{X} f\,\overline{g}\,d\m
u.$$
By Hölder (or directly from the bound above),
$$|\Phi(f)| \le \|f\overline{g}\|_{1} \le C\|f\|_{2},$$
so $\Phi$ is a bounded conjugate-linear functional on $L^{2}(X)$. By the Riesz R
epresentation Theorem for $L^{2}$, every bounded conjugate-linear functional on
$L^{2}(X)$ is of the form $f \mapsto \int f\,\overline{h}\,d\mu$ for a unique $h

$$\int_{X} f\,\overline{g}\,d\mu = \int_{X} f\,\overline{h}\,d\mu \qquad \forall

ximating general sets) yields $\overline{g} = \overline{h}$ $\mu$-a.e., i.e. $g
= h$ $\mu$-a.e. Therefore $g \in L^{2}(X)$, with $\|g\|_{2} = \|\Phi\| \le C$.
