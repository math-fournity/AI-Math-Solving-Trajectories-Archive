## Interpretation

The question asks: for a **single arbitrary cyclic representation** $\pi: A \to
B(H)$, does $\pi(a) \geq 0$ imply $a \geq 0$ in $A$? (The phrase "for all $a \in
 A$" quantifies over the element being tested.) The answer is **No**.

## Proof

**Claim.** There exist a C\*-algebra $A$, a cyclic representation $\pi: A \to B(
H)$, and an element $a \in A$ such that $\pi(a) \geq 0$ but $a \not\geq 0$ in $A
$.

**Counterexample.** Let $A = C([0,1])$, the commutative C\*-algebra of continuou
s functions on $[0,1]$, with the sup-norm and pointwise operations. Recall that
$f \geq 0$ in $A$ iff $f(t) \geq 0$ for every $t \in [0,1]$.

Define a representation $\pi: A \to B(\mathbb{C})$ by evaluation at $t_0 = 0$:
$$\pi(f) = f(0) \cdot \mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{C}}.$$

This is a \*-homomorphism (it preserves addition, multiplication, scalar multipl
ication, and involution $f^* = \overline{f}$). It is **cyclic**: $H = \mathbb{C}
$ is one-dimensional, and any nonzero vector $\xi \in \mathbb{C}$ is cyclic sinc
e $\pi(A)\xi = \{f(0)\xi : f \in C([0,1])\} = \mathbb{C}\cdot\xi = H$ (using tha
t $f(0)$ ranges over all of $\mathbb{C}$).

Now take $a = f$ where
$$f(t) = \tfrac{1}{2} - t.$$

Then:
- $\pi(f) = f(0) \cdot \mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{C}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{
\mathbb{C}} \geq 0$ in $B(\mathbb{C})$.
- But $f(1) = \tfrac{1}{2} - 1 = -\tfrac{1}{2} < 0$, so $f \not\geq 0$ in $C([0,
1])$.

Thus $\pi(a) \geq 0$ does **not** imply $a \geq 0$ for a single cyclic represent
ation. $\square$

**Remark (the correct statement).** What *is* true is that $a \geq 0$ in $A$ iff
 $\pi_u(a) \geq 0$, where $\pi_u = \bigoplus_{\varphi \in S(A)} \pi_\varphi$ is
the **universal representation** — the direct sum over all states $\varphi$ of t
he GNS representations $\pi_\varphi$ (each of which is cyclic). The universal re
presentation is faithful (isometric), so it detects positivity. The counterexamp
le above shows that no **single** cyclic representation suffices in general; one
 needs the entire family.
