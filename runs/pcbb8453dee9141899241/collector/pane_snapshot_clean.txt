**Answer: Yes, it is possible.**

## Proof

**Construction (n = 1).** Consider the Smith–Volterra–Cantor (fat Cantor) set $C
 \subset [0,1]$. This set is constructed by iteratively removing open middle int
ervals of decreasing length from $[0,1]$, with total removed length $\frac{1}{2}
$. The set $C$ has three key properties:

1. **Closed**: $C$ is the complement of a countable union of open intervals, hen
ce closed.
2. **Empty interior**: every open interval in $[0,1]$ contains a removed subinte
rval, so $\operatorname{int}(C) = \emptyset$.
3. **Positive measure**: $m(C) = 1 - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2}$.

Define $U = \mathbb{R} \setminus C$. Since $C$ is closed, $U$ is open. Since $C$
 has empty interior, every point of $C$ is a limit point of $U$, so $\overline{U
} = \mathbb{R}$. Therefore:

$$\partial U = \overline{U} \setminus U = \mathbb{R} \setminus (\mathbb{R} \setm
inus C) = C.$$

The Lebesgue measure of the boundary is $m(\partial U) = m(C) = \frac{1}{2} > 0$
.

Now take the collection $\{U\}$ (a single open set). The subcollection $\{U\}$ h
as union $U$, and $\partial U$ has measure $\frac{1}{2} \neq 0$.

**Generalization to $\mathbb{R}^n$ ($n \geq 2$).** Let $C$ be the fat Cantor set
 as above and define

$$V = (\mathbb{R} \setminus C) \times (0,1)^{n-1}.$$

Then $V$ is open in $\mathbb{R}^n$. Its closure is $\overline{V} = \mathbb{R} \t
imes [0,1]^{n-1}$, so

$$\partial V = \overline{V} \setminus V \supseteq C \times [0,1]^{n-1}.$$

The Lebesgue measure of $C \times [0,1]^{n-1}$ is $\frac{1}{2} \cdot 1^{n-1} = \
frac{1}{2} > 0$, hence $m(\partial V) \geq \frac{1}{2} > 0$.

The collection $\{V\}$ is a collection of open sets in $\mathbb{R}^n$, and the s
ubcollection $\{V\}$ has union $V$ with boundary of positive measure.

**Conclusion.** In every dimension $n$, there exists a collection of open sets i
n $\mathbb{R}^n$ and a subcollection whose union has a boundary with non-zero vo
lume. The key ingredient is the existence of closed sets with empty interior and
 positive Lebesgue measure (fat Cantor sets), whose complements are open sets wi
th boundaries of positive measure.
