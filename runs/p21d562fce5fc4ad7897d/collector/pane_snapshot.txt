### Setting up the sum

The signal $S$ has 44 terms (angles $2°, 4°, \ldots, 88°$) with the 4-operation
cycle $+\cos,-\sin,-\cos,+\sin$. Term $j$ ($j=0,\dots,43$) uses angle $2j+2$:

$$S = \sum_{j=0}^{43} c_j,\quad c_j = \begin{cases}+\cos(2j+2) & j\bmod 4=0\\ -\
sin(2j+2) & j\bmod 4=1\\ -\cos(2j+2) & j\bmod 4=2\\ +\sin(2j+2) & j\bmod 4=3\end
{cases}$$

### Converting all terms to cosines

Using $\sin x = \cos(90°-x)$, the $\sin$-terms become $\cos$-terms:
- $-\sin(2j+2) = -\cos(88-2j)$
- $+\sin(2j+2) = +\cos(88-2j)$

### Grouping into 22 consecutive pairs

Pair $m$ ($m=0,\dots,21$) contains $j=2m,\,2m+1$. A direct check of the four cas
es ($m$ even/odd × the two residues) gives:

$$\text{pair}_m = 2\sin 44°\cdot (-1)^m \sin(42°-4m°)$$

since $\cos A - \cos B = -2\sin\!\tfrac{A+B}{2}\sin\!\tfrac{A-B}{2}$ and here $\
tfrac{A+B}{2}=44°$ for every pair.

Therefore
$$S = 2\sin 44° \sum_{m=0}^{21}(-1)^m\sin(42°-4m°).$$

### Evaluating the alternating sine sum

Let $T=\sum_{m=0}^{21}(-1)^m\sin(42°-4m°)$. Using $\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}(-1)^m e^{i(a
+md)}=\dfrac{e^{ia}\bigl(1-(-e^{id})^n\bigr)}{1+e^{id}}$ with $a=42°,\ d=-4°,\ n
=22$:

$$(-e^{-i4°})^{22}=e^{-i88°},\qquad 1-e^{-i88°}=2i\sin 44°\,e^{-i44°},\qquad 1+e
^{-i4°}=2\cos 2°\,e^{-i2°}.$$

Hence
$$\sum_{m=0}^{21}(-1)^m e^{i(42-4m)°}=e^{i42°}\cdot\frac{2i\sin44°\,e^{-i44°}}{2
\cos2°\,e^{-i2°}}=\frac{\sin44°}{\cos2°}\,i\,e^{i0°}.$$

Taking the imaginary part: $T=\dfrac{\sin44°}{\cos2°}$.

### Closing the computation

$$S = 2\sin 44°\cdot\frac{\sin44°}{\cos2°}=\frac{2\sin^2 44°}{\cos2°}=\frac{1-\c
os88°}{\cos2°}=\frac{1-\sin2°}{\cos2°}.$$

### Identifying $\theta$

The calibration model gives $S=\sec\theta-\tan\theta=\dfrac{1-\sin\theta}{\cos\t
heta}$.

Comparing $\dfrac{1-\sin2°}{\cos2°}=\dfrac{1-\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$ yields $\t
heta=2°$.

### Verification of uniqueness

$g(\theta)=\dfrac{1-\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}=\tan(45°-\theta/2)$ is strictly decr
easing on $(-90°,90°)$, so the solution is unique.

$$\boxed{\theta = 2°}$$
