## Solution

The operation: given $n$, choose natural numbers $a,b$ with $ab=n$, and replace
$n$ by $a+b$. We may apply this any finite number of times (including zero), so
$f(n)\le n$ always. We seek the minimum reachable value.

**Key observation:** For a prime $p$, the only factorization is $1\times p \to 1
+p$, which strictly increases the value, so we cannot reduce a prime. Thus $f(p)
=p$ (stop immediately).

For composite $n$, splitting into balanced factors $a,b$ with $ab=n$ tends to re
duce the value (since $a+b \le n$ when $a,b\ge 2$ and $ab=n$, with equality only
 for $n=4=2\cdot2$).

**Computing each value:**

- $f(1)$: Only $1\times1\to2$, which increases. Stop: $f(1)=1$.
- $f(2)$: Prime. $1\times2\to3$ increases. $f(2)=2$.
- $f(3)$: Prime. $1\times3\to4$ increases. $f(3)=3$.
- $f(4)$: $1\times4\to5$ or $2\times2\to4$. Best is $4$. $f(4)=4$.
- $f(5)$: Prime. $f(5)=5$.
- $f(6)$: $2\times3\to5$. From $5$ (prime) we stop. $f(6)=5$.
- $f(7)$: Prime. $f(7)=7$.
- $f(8)$: $2\times4\to6$, then $6\to5$. $f(8)=5$.
- $f(9)$: $3\times3\to6$, then $6\to5$. $f(9)=5$.
- $f(10)$: $2\times5\to7$. From $7$ (prime) we stop. $f(10)=7$.

**Verification of minimality:** At each composite, the non-trivial factorization
 gives the smallest $a+b$; once we reach a prime, no further reduction is possib
le. The chains are:
- $8\to6\to5$ (prime, stop)
- $9\to6\to5$ (prime, stop)
- $6\to5$ (prime, stop)
- $10\to7$ (prime, stop)

No alternative factorization yields a smaller endpoint (e.g., $8=1\times8\to9$ i
s worse; $10=1\times10\to11$ is worse).

**Sum:**

$$f(1)+f(2)+f(3)+f(4)+f(5)+f(6)+f(7)+f(8)+f(9)+f(10)$$
$$=1+2+3+4+5+5+7+5+5+7=44$$
