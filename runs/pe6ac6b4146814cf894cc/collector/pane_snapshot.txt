## Quadratic Variation of $Y(t)$ over $[-1, 2]$

**Setup and continuity check.** The process is
$$Y(t) = \begin{cases} 0 & -1 \le t \le 0 \\ Z(t) - tZ(1) & 0 < t < 1 \\ 1 - t &
 1 \le t \le 2. \end{cases}$$

Check continuity at the seam points:
- At $t = 0$: $Y(0) = 0$, and $\lim_{t \to 0^+} Y(t) = Z(0) - 0 = 0$. ✓
- At $t = 1$: $\lim_{t \to 1^-} Y(t) = Z(1) - Z(1) = 0$, and $Y(1) = 1 - 1 = 0$.
 ✓

So $Y$ is continuous on $[-1, 2]$.

**Decomposition on each subinterval.**

*On $[-1, 0]$:* $Y(t) = 0$, constant. A constant path has zero quadratic variati
on:
$$[Y]_{[-1,0]} = 0.$$

*On $[1, 2]$:* $Y(t) = 1 - t$, a deterministic $C^1$ function. Any continuously
differentiable (indeed, any finite-variation) path has zero quadratic variation:
$$[Y]_{[1,2]} = 0.$$

*On $[0, 1]$:* Write
$$Y(t) = Z(t) - t\,Z(1).$$
Here $Z(1)$ is a fixed random variable (a random constant), so $t \mapsto t\,Z(1
)$ is a process of finite variation on $[0,1]$. A fundamental property of quadra
tic variation is that it is **insensitive to addition of finite-variation proces
ses**: if $X = M + A$ where $M$ is a continuous local martingale and $A$ is a co
ntinuous finite-variation process, then $[X] = [M]$.

Concretely, for any partition $\pi = \{t_i\}$ of $[0,1]$,
$$\sum_i \bigl(\Delta Y_{t_i}\bigr)^2 = \sum_i \bigl(\Delta Z_{t_i} - Z(1)\,\Del
ta t_i\bigr)^2.$$
Expanding:
$$= \sum_i (\Delta Z_{t_i})^2 - 2Z(1)\sum_i \Delta Z_{t_i}\,\Delta t_i + Z(1)^2
\sum_i (\Delta t_i)^2.$$
As $|\pi| \to 0$:
- $\sum_i (\Delta Z_{t_i})^2 \to [Z]_1 = 1$ (quadratic variation of Brownian mot
ion).
- $\sum_i \Delta Z_{t_i}\,\Delta t_i \to 0$: this is the cross-variation between
 the martingale $Z$ and the finite-variation process $t$; by the Kunita–Watanabe
 / bilinear-variation identity, $[Z, t] \equiv 0$ since $t$ has zero quadratic v
ariation. (Equivalently, $|\sum \Delta Z_{t_i}\,\Delta t_i| \le \max_i |\Delta Z
_{t_i}| \cdot \sum \Delta t_i \to 0$ a.s. since $Z$ is continuous, hence uniform
ly continuous on $[0,1]$.)
- $\sum_i (\Delta t_i)^2 \le |\pi| \cdot \sum_i \Delta t_i = |\pi| \to 0$.

Therefore
$$[Y]_{[0,1]} = \lim_{|\pi|\to 0} \sum_i (\Delta Y_{t_i})^2 = 1.$$

**Combining the intervals.** Since $Y$ is continuous across the seam points, the
 quadratic variation is additive over the subintervals:
$$[Y]_{[-1,2]} = [Y]_{[-1,0]} + [Y]_{[0,1]} + [Y]_{[1,2]} = 0 + 1 + 0 = 1.$$

**Result.** The quadratic variation of $Y(t)$ over $[-1, 2]$ is

$$\boxed{[Y]_{[-1,2]} = 1}.$$
