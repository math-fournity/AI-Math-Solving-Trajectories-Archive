**Theorem.** The partial order $(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}), \subseteq)$ can be ext
ended to a total order.

**Proof.**

We prove the general Szpilrajn extension theorem: every partial order can be ext
ended to a total order (using the Axiom of Choice in the form of Zorn's Lemma).
The result then applies to $(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}), \subseteq)$.

Let $(P, \leq)$ be any partial order. Define

$$\mathcal{F} = \{\preceq \;\subseteq\; P \times P : \preceq \text{ is a partial
 order on } P \text{ and } \leq \;\subseteq\; \preceq\},$$

i.e., the collection of all partial orders on $P$ that extend $\leq$. Note $\mat
hcal{F} \neq \emptyset$ since $\leq \in \mathcal{F}$.

**Partial order on $\mathcal{F}$.** Order $\mathcal{F}$ by set-theoretic inclusi
on of relations: $\preceq_1 \sqsubseteq \preceq_2$ iff $\preceq_1 \subseteq \pre
ceq_2$ (as subsets of $P \times P$). This is itself a partial order on $\mathcal
{F}$.

**Every chain in $\mathcal{F}$ has an upper bound.** Let $\{\preceq_\alpha\}_{\a
lpha \in I}$ be a chain in $(\mathcal{F}, \sqsubseteq)$. Set $\preceq^* = \bigcu
p_{\alpha \in I} \preceq_\alpha$. We verify $\preceq^* \in \mathcal{F}$:

- *Reflexivity*: Each $\preceq_\alpha$ contains $(x,x)$ for all $x \in P$, so $\
preceq^*$ does.
- *Transitivity*: If $x \preceq^* y$ and $y \preceq^* z$, then $x \preceq_\alpha
 y$ and $y \preceq_\beta z$ for some $\alpha, \beta$. Since the family is a chai
n, either $\preceq_\alpha \subseteq \preceq_\beta$ or $\preceq_\beta \subseteq \
preceq_\alpha$; say $\preceq_\alpha \subseteq \preceq_\beta$. Then $x \preceq_\b
eta y \preceq_\beta z$, so $x \preceq_\beta z$, giving $x \preceq^* z$.
- *Antisymmetry*: If $x \preceq^* y$ and $y \preceq^* x$, then $x \preceq_\alpha
 y$ and $y \preceq_\beta x$ for some $\alpha, \beta$. WLOG $\preceq_\alpha \subs
eteq \preceq_\beta$, so $x \preceq_\beta y$ and $y \preceq_\beta x$, giving $x =
 y$ by antisymmetry of $\preceq_\beta$.
- *Extension*: $\leq \subseteq \preceq_\alpha \subseteq \preceq^*$ for any $\alp
ha$.

So $\preceq^* \in \mathcal{F}$ is an upper bound for the chain.

**Applying Zorn's Lemma.** By Zorn's Lemma, $\mathcal{F}$ has a maximal element
$\preceq_{\max}$.

**$\preceq_{\max}$ is total.** Suppose for contradiction that $\preceq_{\max}$ i
s not total. Then there exist $a, b \in P$ with $a \neq b$ such that neither $a
\preceq_{\max} b$ nor $b \preceq_{\max} a$. Define a new relation $\preceq'$ on
$P$ by:

$$x \preceq' y \iff x \preceq_{\max} y \;\;\text{or}\;\; \bigl(x \preceq_{\max}
a \;\text{and}\; b \preceq_{\max} y\bigr).$$

We claim $\preceq'$ is a partial order strictly extending $\preceq_{\max}$ (with
 $b \preceq' a$ but not $b \preceq_{\max} a$), contradicting maximality.

- *Contains $\preceq_{\max}$*: If $x \preceq_{\max} y$ then $x \preceq' y$. ✓
- *Strictly larger*: We have $b \preceq' a$ (take $x = b, y = a$: $b \preceq_{\m
ax} b$ and $a \preceq_{\max} a$), but $b \not\preceq_{\max} a$ by assumption. ✓
- *Reflexivity*: $x \preceq_{\max} x$ gives $x \preceq' x$. ✓
- *Transitivity*: Suppose $x \preceq' y$ and $y \preceq' z$.
  - If $x \preceq_{\max} y$: either $y \preceq_{\max} z$ (so $x \preceq_{\max} z
 \preceq' z$), or $y \preceq_{\max} a$ and $b \preceq_{\max} z$; then $x \preceq
_{\max} a$ and $b \preceq_{\max} z$, giving $x \preceq' z$.
  - If $x \preceq_{\max} a$ and $b \preceq_{\max} y$: then $y \preceq' z$ means
either $y \preceq_{\max} z$ (so $b \preceq_{\max} y \preceq_{\max} z$, giving $b
 \preceq_{\max} z$ and $x \preceq_{\max} a$, hence $x \preceq' z$), or $y \prece
q_{\max} a$ and $b \preceq_{\max} z$ (so $b \preceq_{\max} z$ and $x \preceq_{\m
ax} a$, hence $x \preceq' z$). ✓
- *Antisymmetry*: Suppose $x \preceq' y$ and $y \preceq' x$. We show $x = y$.
  - If both $x \preceq_{\max} y$ and $y \preceq_{\max} x$: $x = y$ by antisymmet
ry of $\preceq_{\max}$.
  - If $x \preceq_{\max} y$ and ($y \preceq_{\max} a$ and $b \preceq_{\max} x$):
 then $b \preceq_{\max} x \preceq_{\max} y \preceq_{\max} a$, so $b \preceq_{\ma
x} a$—contradicting our choice of $a, b$.
  - If ($x \preceq_{\max} a$ and $b \preceq_{\max} y$) and $y \preceq_{\max} x$:
 then $b \preceq_{\max} y \preceq_{\max} x \preceq_{\max} a$, so $b \preceq_{\ma
x} a$—contradiction.
  - If both ($x \preceq_{\max} a$ and $b \preceq_{\max} y$) and ($y \preceq_{\ma
x} a$ and $b \preceq_{\max} x$): then $b \preceq_{\max} y \preceq_{\max} a$, so
$b \preceq_{\max} a$—contradiction.

  In all cases either $x = y$ or we reach a contradiction, so $\preceq'$ is anti
symmetric. ✓

Thus $\preceq' \in \mathcal{F}$ with $\preceq_{\max} \subsetneq \preceq'$, contr
adicting the maximality of $\preceq_{\max}$. Therefore $\preceq_{\max}$ must be
total.

**Conclusion.** $\preceq_{\max}$ is a total order on $P$ extending $\leq$. Takin
g $P = \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})$ and $\leq = \subseteq$, we obtain a total order
extending the inclusion order on the power set of $\mathbb{R}$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** This result (the Order Extension Principle) is equivalent over ZF to
 the Boolean Prime Ideal Theorem, a weak form of the Axiom of Choice, so the ext
ension cannot be constructed in ZF alone.
