## Solution

**Claim.** No such digraph exists.

The key observation is that taking the line digraph preserves the odd girth exac
tly. We prove this as a lemma and then derive the claim.

---

**Lemma (Invariance of odd girth under line-digraph iteration).** *For every dig
raph $G$ and every $i\ge 0$, the odd girth of $L^{(i)}(G)$ equals the odd girth
of $G$ (both being finite and equal, or both infinite).*

*Proof of Lemma.* It suffices to prove the case $i=1$; the general case follows
by induction. We establish, for every $k\ge 1$, a bijection between the closed d
irected walks of length $k$ in $G$ and the closed directed walks of length $k$ i
n $L(G)$.

Recall that the vertices of $L(G)$ are the edges of $G$, and there is an arc $e\
to f$ in $L(G)$ precisely when $\operatorname{head}_G(e)=\operatorname{tail}_G(f
)$.

A closed directed walk of length $k$ in $L(G)$ is a sequence $e_1,e_2,\dots,e_k$
 of vertices of $L(G)$ (hence edges of $G$) with $e_j\to e_{j+1}$ in $L(G)$ for
$j=1,\dots,k$ (indices taken modulo $k$, so $e_{k+1}=e_1$). The adjacency condit
ion $e_j\to e_{j+1}\in E(L(G))$ is exactly $\operatorname{head}_G(e_j)=\operator
name{tail}_G(e_{j+1})$. Thus $e_1,e_2,\dots,e_k$ is precisely a sequence of edge
s of $G$ forming a closed directed walk of length $k$ in $G$. Conversely, every
closed directed walk of length $k$ in $G$ (viewed as its edge sequence) yields s
uch a closed walk in $L(G)$. Since a directed walk is determined by its edge seq
uence, this correspondence is bijective.

Hence for every $k$, $G$ admits a closed directed walk of length $k$ if and only
 if $L(G)$ does; in particular this holds for every odd $k$.

Now recall that the *odd girth* of a digraph is the length of a shortest directe
d cycle of odd length, equivalently the minimum odd $k$ for which a closed direc
ted walk of length $k$ exists. (These two characterizations coincide: every odd
directed cycle of length $k$ is an odd closed walk of length $k$; and every odd
closed walk decomposes into directed cycles whose lengths sum to an odd number,
so at least one summand is an odd cycle of length $\le k$.) By the bijection abo
ve, this minimum odd $k$ is the same for $G$ and for $L(G)$. If no odd closed wa
lk exists for either, both odd girths are infinite. Iterating gives the lemma fo
r all $i\ge 0$. $\square$

---

**Main proof.** Let $G$ be any digraph; we show the required property fails.

*Case 1: $G$ contains an odd directed cycle.* Let $d=\operatorname{oddgirth}(G)<
\infty$. By the Lemma,
$$\operatorname{oddgirth}\!\bigl(L^{(i)}(G)\bigr)=d\qquad\text{for every }i\ge 0
.$$
Choose $g=d+1$. Then for every $i$, $\operatorname{oddgirth}(L^{(i)}(G))=d<g$, s
o no $i$ makes the odd girth at least $g$. The condition fails.

*Case 2: $G$ contains no odd directed cycle.* By the Lemma, $L^{(i)}(G)$ contain
s no odd directed cycle for every $i\ge 0$. Consequently no iterated line digrap
h $L^{(i)}(G)$ possesses a (finite) odd girth at all, so in particular there is
no $i$ for which $L^{(i)}(G)$ has an odd girth of at least $g$, for any positive
 $g$. The condition fails.

Both cases lead to failure. Therefore no digraph $G$ satisfies the stated proper
ty. $\blacksquare$
