## Proof

**Setup.** Number the cards $0, 1, \dots, M-1$. Let $c_k \in \{0,1\}$ denote the
 label on card $k$. Define the potential function
$$V \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{M-1} c_k \cdot 2^k.$$
Initially every $c_k = 1$, so $V = 2^M - 1$. Clearly $V \geq 0$ at all times sin
ce every $c_k \in \{0,1\}$.

**Effect of a move.** A move consists of choosing an index $i$ with $i + n < M$
and $c_{i+n} = 1$, then flipping (toggling) every card $j$ with $i \leq j \leq i
+n$.

Let us compute the change $\Delta V$.

- **Card $i+n$:** By the legality condition $c_{i+n}=1$, and it gets flipped, so
 $c_{i+n}: 1 \to 0$. This contributes $-2^{i+n}$ to $\Delta V$.

- **Cards $j$ with $i \leq j \leq i+n-1$:** Each is toggled. If it was $0$ it be
comes $1$ (contribution $+2^j$); if it was $1$ it becomes $0$ (contribution $-2^
j$). In either case the contribution is $\varepsilon_j \cdot 2^j$ with $\varepsi
lon_j \in \{+1,-1\}$.

Thus
$$\Delta V \;=\; -\,2^{i+n} \;+\; \sum_{j=i}^{i+n-1} \varepsilon_j\, 2^j.$$

**Bounding the change.** The largest possible value of the sum over the lower-in
dexed cards is achieved when every $\varepsilon_j = +1$:
$$\sum_{j=i}^{i+n-1} \varepsilon_j\, 2^j \;\leq\; \sum_{j=i}^{i+n-1} 2^j \;=\; 2
^{i+n} - 2^i.$$

Therefore
$$\Delta V \;\leq\; -\,2^{i+n} + \bigl(2^{i+n} - 2^i\bigr) \;=\; -\,2^i \;<\; 0.
$$

So **every legal move strictly decreases $V$**, and in fact decreases it by at l
east $2^i \geq 1$.

**Termination.** The quantity $V$ is a non-negative integer (it lies in $\{0, 1,
 \dots, 2^M - 1\}$) and it strictly decreases with every move. A strictly decrea
sing sequence of non-negative integers cannot be infinite; after at most $2^M -
1$ moves no further legal move is possible. Hence the game always ends. $\blacks
quare$
