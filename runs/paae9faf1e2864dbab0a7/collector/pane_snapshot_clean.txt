## Proof

We evaluate the Cauchy Principal Value

$$\text{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\cos kx}{x-a}\,dx,\qquad a\in\mathbb{
R},\; k>0.$$

**Step 1: Shift out the pole.** Substitute $u = x - a$ ($dx = du$):

$$\text{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\cos kx}{x-a}\,dx = \text{PV}\!\int_{
-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\cos\bigl(k(u+a)\bigr)}{u}\,du.$$

Expand $\cos(ku+ka) = \cos ku\cos ka - \sin ku\sin ka$:

$$= \cos(ka)\,\text{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos ku}{u}\,du \;-\; \sin
(ka)\,\text{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin ku}{u}\,du.$$

**Step 2: The cosine term vanishes.** The integrand $\frac{\cos ku}{u}$ is odd (
even × odd = odd). The Cauchy principal value of an odd function over symmetric
limits $(-R,R)$ is exactly $0$ for every $R$, hence

$$\text{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos ku}{u}\,du = 0.$$

**Step 3: The sine term — the Dirichlet integral.** The integrand $\frac{\sin ku
}{u}$ is even (odd × odd = even), so the principal value equals the ordinary (co
nvergent) improper integral:

$$\text{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin ku}{u}\,du = 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\f
rac{\sin ku}{u}\,du.$$

With $t = ku$ ($dt = k\,du$, $u = t/k$):

$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin ku}{u}\,du = \int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin t}{t}\,dt
 = \frac{\pi}{2},$$

the classical Dirichlet integral (valid for $k>0$). Therefore

$$\text{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin ku}{u}\,du = 2\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}
= \pi.$$

**Step 4: Combine.**

$$\text{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\cos kx}{x-a}\,dx = \cos(ka)\cdot 0 \
;-\; \sin(ka)\cdot\pi = -\pi\sin(ka).$$

**Result.**

$$\boxed{\,\text{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\cos kx}{x-a}\,dx = -\pi\sin
(ka)\,}$$
