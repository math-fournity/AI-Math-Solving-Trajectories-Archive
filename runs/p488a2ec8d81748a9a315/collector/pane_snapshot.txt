# IMO 1992 Problem 2 — Solution

**Problem.** Find all functions $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ such that for all $x
,y\in\mathbb{R}$,
$$f\bigl(x^2+f(y)\bigr)=y+\bigl(f(x)\bigr)^2. \tag{P(x,y)}$$

**Answer.** The unique solution is $f(x)=x$.

---

## Step 1. Injectivity

Suppose $f(a)=f(b)$. Comparing $P(x,a)$ and $P(x,b)$ gives $a+(f(x))^2=b+(f(x))^
2$, hence $a=b$. So $f$ is **injective**.

## Step 2. An involution-type relation; surjectivity

Set $x=0$ in $(P)$: $f(f(y))=y+(f(0))^2$. Let $b:=f(0)$ and $c:=b^2\ge 0$; then
$$f(f(y))=y+c. \tag{1}$$
Since $y+c$ ranges over all of $\mathbb{R}$, $f$ is **surjective**, hence biject
ive.

Let $a$ be the unique real with $f(a)=0$. Setting $y=a$ in $(P)$:
$$f(x^2)=a+(f(x))^2. \tag{2}$$
With $x=0$ in (2): $b=a+b^2$, so $a=b-b^2$.

## Step 3. Determining $b$

Replacing $x$ by $-x$ in (2) and comparing: $(f(x))^2=(f(-x))^2$, so $f(-x)=\pm
f(x)$. By injectivity, $f(-x)=f(x)\Rightarrow x=0$; thus for $x\neq 0$, $f(-x)=-
f(x)$.

Apply this at $x=a$ (where $f(a)=0$): if $a\neq 0$, then $f(-a)=-f(a)=0$, contra
dicting injectivity (both $a$ and $-a$ would map to $0$). Hence $a=0$, i.e. $b-b
^2=0$, giving $b\in\{0,1\}$.

**Rule out $b=1$.** Then $a=0$ but $f(a)=f(0)=b=1\neq 0$, contradicting $f(a)=0$
. So $b=0$.

## Step 4. Consequences of $b=0$

Now $f(0)=0$, $c=0$, and
$$f(f(y))=y \quad\text{($f$ is an involution)},\qquad f(x^2)=(f(x))^2. \tag{3}$$
From $f(x^2)=(f(x))^2\ge 0$, every $t\ge 0$ (being a square) satisfies $f(t)\ge
0$. Combined with $f(-x)=-f(x)$ (now valid also at $0$), $f$ is **odd** and **no
n-negativity preserving**.

## Step 5. Additivity

Using $(f(x))^2=f(x^2)$ in $(P)$:
$$f\bigl(x^2+f(y)\bigr)=y+f(x^2).$$
Set $t=x^2\ge 0$; since $f$ is surjective, $u:=f(y)$ ranges over $\mathbb{R}$ wi
th $y=f(u)$ (by (3)):
$$f(t+u)=f(t)+f(u)\qquad\text{for all }t\ge 0,\;u\in\mathbb{R}. \tag{$\ast$}$$

Extend to all $a,b\in\mathbb{R}$. If $a\ge 0$, use $(\ast)$. If $a<0$, write $a=
-s$, $s>0$; by $(\ast)$ with $w=b,\;t=s$: $f(b-s)=f(b)-f(s)$, and $f(a)+f(b)=-f(
s)+f(b)=f(b)-f(s)$. Hence
$$f(a+b)=f(a)+f(b)\qquad\text{for all }a,b\in\mathbb{R}. \tag{4}$$

## Step 6. $f$ is the identity

From (4), $f(q)=q\,f(1)$ for all $q\in\mathbb{Q}$. Using (3) with rational $q\ne
q 0$: $q^2 f(1)=f(q^2)=(f(q))^2=q^2(f(1))^2$, so $f(1)=(f(1))^2$, giving $f(1)\i
n\{0,1\}$. Injectivity and $f(0)=0$ force $f(1)\neq 0$, hence $f(1)=1$ and $f(q)
=q$ for all $q\in\mathbb{Q}$.

Since $f$ is additive and non-negativity preserving, it is **monotone**: $a\le b
\Rightarrow f(b)-f(a)=f(b-a)\ge 0$. A monotone additive function is linear: $f(x
)=cx$; with $f(1)=1$ we get $c=1$.

## Step 7. Verification

$f(x)=x$: $f(x^2+f(y))=x^2+y=y+(f(x))^2$. ✓

---

**Conclusion.** The only function satisfying the equation is $\boxed{f(x)=x}$.
