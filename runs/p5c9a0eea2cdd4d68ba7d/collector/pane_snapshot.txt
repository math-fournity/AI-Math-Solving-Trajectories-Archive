## Solution

The answer depends on whether $M$ has a boundary.

### Case 1: $M$ is compact **without** boundary (closed manifold) — Yes, $df$ al
ways has zeros.

Since $M$ is compact and $f$ is continuous, $f$ attains its maximum and minimum
on $M$. Let $p \in M$ be a point where $f$ achieves, say, its maximum. Since $M$
 has no boundary, $p$ is an interior point. In any local coordinate chart $(U, x
^1, \dots, x^n)$ around $p$, the function $f$ has a local maximum at $p$, so

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^i}\bigg|_p = 0 \quad \text{for all } i.$$

Hence $df_p = \sum_i \frac{\partial f}{\partial x^i}\big|_p\, dx^i|_p = 0$. The
same argument applies at a minimum. Therefore $df$ vanishes at (at least) the po
ints where $f$ attains its extreme values.

### Case 2: $M$ is compact **with** boundary — No, $df$ need not have zeros.

**Counterexample.** Let $M = [0,1]$, a compact 1-manifold with boundary, and let
 $f(x) = x$. Then

$$df = dx,$$

which is nowhere vanishing on $[0,1]$. The maximum ($f(1)=1$) and minimum ($f(0)
=0$) both occur on the boundary $\partial M = \{0, 1\}$, where there is no requi
rement that $df$ vanish.

### Conclusion

- If $M$ is a **closed** manifold (compact, no boundary), then $df$ **always** h
as zeros, because $f$ attains its extrema at interior critical points.
- If $M$ is allowed to have a **boundary**, then $df$ **need not** have zeros, a
s shown by the counterexample $M=[0,1]$, $f(x)=x$.

Under the standard convention that "compact manifold" means closed (no boundary)
, the answer is **yes**: $df$ always has zeros on $M$.
