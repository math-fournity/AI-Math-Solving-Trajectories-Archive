## Proof

f the sequence and the question of its convergence/limit. No solution, hint, or
answer value is embedded. Proceeding.

Let $a_k := \dfrac{\mu(k)}{k}$. Then the given sequence is the **Cesàro mean** o
f $(a_k)$:

$$b_n = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k.$$

**Step 1. The sequence $(a_k)$ converges to $0$.**

For every $k \ge 1$, the Möbius function satisfies $|\mu(k)| \le 1$, hence

$$|a_k| = \frac{|\mu(k)|}{k} \le \frac{1}{k}.$$

Since $\frac{1}{k} \to 0$, by the squeeze theorem $a_k \to 0$.

**Step 2. Apply the Cesàro mean theorem.**

A classical result (Cesàro's theorem) states:

> If a sequence $(a_k)$ converges to a limit $L$, then its Cesàro means $\displa
ystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k$ also converge to $L$.

*Proof of the theorem (for completeness).* Given $\varepsilon > 0$, choose $N$ s
uch that $|a_k - L| < \varepsilon/2$ for all $k > N$. For $n > N$,

$$\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k - L\right| \le \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{N}|a
_k - L| + \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=N+1}^{n}|a_k - L|.$$

The first term equals $\frac{C_N}{n}$ (where $C_N = \sum_{k=1}^{N}|a_k-L|$ is a
fixed constant), which is $< \varepsilon/2$ for all $n$ large enough. The second
 term is $< \frac{1}{n}\cdot n\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \frac{\varepsilon}{2}
$. Hence the total is $< \varepsilon$ for all sufficiently large $n$. $\square$

**Step 3. Conclusion.**

Since $a_k \to 0$, by Step 2 the Cesàro mean satisfies

$$b_n = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\mu(k)}{k} \;\longrightarrow\; 0.$$

The sequence $(b_n)$ **converges**, and its limit is

$$\boxed{0}.$$
