## Proof

**Claim:** $A_1 = A_2$.

Let $U := A_1 \cup B_1 = A_2 \cup B_2$. Note $A_1, B_1, A_2, B_2 \subseteq U$ (s
ince e.g. $A_1 \subseteq A_1 \cup B_1 = U$).

**Step 1: Each $A_i$ is a union of connected components of $U$.**

Since $A_i \cap B_i = \emptyset$ and $A_i \cup B_i = U$, the sets $A_i, B_i$ for
m a separation of $U$ into two disjoint open sets (open in the Hilbert space, he
nce open in the subspace topology of $U$).

Let $C$ be any connected component of $U$. Then
$$C = (C \cap A_i) \cup (C \cap B_i),$$
where $C \cap A_i$ and $C \cap B_i$ are disjoint and relatively open in $C$. Sin
ce $C$ is connected, one of them must be empty. Hence $C \subseteq A_i$ or $C \s
ubseteq B_i$.

Therefore each connected component of $U$ lies entirely in $A_i$ or entirely in
$B_i$, so $A_i$ is a union of connected components of $U$.

**Step 2: Connected components of $U$ are open.**

A Hilbert space is a normed vector space, hence locally convex (open balls are c
onvex, hence path-connected). Thus it is locally path-connected, hence locally c
onnected. In a locally connected space, the connected components of every open s
et are open. Since $U$ is open, its connected components are open.

**Step 3: $A_i$ is a single connected component of $U$.**

By Step 1, $A_i$ is a union of connected components of $U$. By Step 2, these com
ponents are pairwise disjoint, non-empty, open sets. If $A_i$ were the union of
two or more such components, it would be disconnected (the components themselves
 would form a separation). But $A_i$ is given to be connected. Hence $A_i$ consi
sts of exactly one connected component of $U$.

**Step 4: $A_1 = A_2$.**

Since $0 \in A_i$, the unique connected component of $U$ comprising $A_i$ must b
e the one containing $0$. Both $A_1$ and $A_2$ equal the connected component of
$U$ containing the origin. Therefore
$$A_1 = A_2. \qquad \blacksquare$$
