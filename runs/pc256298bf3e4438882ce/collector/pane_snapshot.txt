## Solution

Let $Y_n = \prod_{i=1}^n X_i$ and $S = \sum_{n=1}^\infty Y_n$. Since each $Y_n \
geq 0$, the partial sums $S_N = \sum_{n=1}^N Y_n$ are non-negative and non-decre
asing in $N$, so $S = \lim_{N\to\infty} S_N$ is a well-defined (possibly $+\inft
y$) non-negative random variable.

**Computing $\mathbb{E}[Y_n]$:** Since the $X_i$ are independent and $X_i \sim \
mathrm{Uniform}[0,1]$ with $\mathbb{E}[X_i] = \tfrac{1}{2}$, by independence:

$$\mathbb{E}[Y_n] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\prod_{i=1}^n X_i\right] = \prod_{i=1}^n \
mathbb{E}[X_i] = \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^n.$$

**Applying the Monotone Convergence Theorem:** Since $S_N \geq 0$ and $S_N \near
row S$, the MCT gives:

$$\mathbb{E}[S] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\lim_{N\to\infty} S_N\right] = \lim_{N\to\in
fty} \mathbb{E}[S_N] = \lim_{N\to\infty} \sum_{n=1}^N \mathbb{E}[Y_n] = \sum_{n=
1}^\infty \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^n = 1.$$

**Conclusion:** We have shown $\mathbb{E}[S] = 1 < \infty$ and $S \geq 0$. If it
 were the case that $\mathbb{P}(S = \infty) > 0$, then we would have

$$\mathbb{E}[S] \geq \infty \cdot \mathbb{P}(S = \infty) = \infty,$$

contradicting $\mathbb{E}[S] = 1$. Therefore $\mathbb{P}(S = \infty) = 0$, i.e.,
 $\mathbb{P}(S < \infty) = 1$.

$$\boxed{\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sum_{n=1}^\infty \prod_{i=1}^n X_i < \infty\right) =
 1.}$$
