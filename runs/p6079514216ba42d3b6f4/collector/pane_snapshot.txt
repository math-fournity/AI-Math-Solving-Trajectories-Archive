We evaluate $\oint_C \frac{dz}{2-\sin z}$, where $C$ is the circle $|z-i|=0.5$.

**Step 1: Locate the singularities of $f(z)=\frac{1}{2-\sin z}$.**

Singularities occur where $\sin z = 2$. Writing $z=x+iy$,
$$\sin z = \sin x\cosh y + i\cos x\sinh y = 2.$$
Since $2\in\mathbb{R}$, we need
$$\cos x\sinh y = 0,\qquad \sin x\cosh y = 2.$$

- If $\sinh y=0$, then $y=0$ and $\sin x=2$, which has no real solution.
- Hence $\cos x=0$, so $x=\frac{\pi}{2}+k\pi$. Then $\sin x=\pm1$, and we need $
\sin x\cosh y=2$, which forces $\sin x=1$ (so $x=\frac{\pi}{2}+2k\pi$) and $\cos
h y=2$, giving $y=\pm\operatorname{arcosh}2=\pm\ln(2+\sqrt{3})$.

Thus all poles lie at
$$z_k^{\pm}=\frac{\pi}{2}+2k\pi \pm i\ln(2+\sqrt{3}),\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}.$$

**Step 2: Determine which poles lie inside $C:|z-i|<0.5$.**

The contour is centered at $z=i=(0,1)$ with radius $0.5$, so every point inside
satisfies $|x|<0.5$ (since $|x-0|\le|z-i|<0.5$). But every pole has real part
$$\operatorname{Re} z_k^{\pm}=\frac{\pi}{2}+2k\pi,$$
whose smallest absolute value is $\frac{\pi}{2}\approx 1.571 > 0.5$. Therefore *
*no pole of $f$ lies inside $C$** (nor on $C$).

**Step 3: Apply Cauchy's theorem.**

The integrand $f(z)=\frac{1}{2-\sin z}$ is holomorphic on and inside the closed
contour $C$, since $2-\sin z\neq 0$ throughout the disk $|z-i|\le 0.5$. By Cauch
y's integral theorem,
$$\oint_C \frac{dz}{2-\sin z}=0.$$
