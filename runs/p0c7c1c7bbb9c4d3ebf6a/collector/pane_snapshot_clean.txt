## Proof

**Answer:** The image of the closed unit disk $|z|\le 1$ is the closed unit disk
 $|w|\le 1$.

---

**Step 1: Each factor is a Blaschke factor (up to phase).**

For each $k$, define
$$\phi_k(z)=\frac{z_k-z}{1-\bar z_k z},\qquad 0<|z_k|<1.$$
Writing $b_k(z)=\frac{z-z_k}{1-\bar z_k z}$ (the standard Blaschke factor with z
ero at $z_k$), we have $\phi_k(z)=-b_k(z)$, i.e. a Blaschke factor multiplied by
 the unimodular constant $e^{i\pi}$. Hence $w(z)=\prod_{k=1}^n\phi_k(z)$ is a fi
nite Blaschke product of degree $n$.

**Step 2: The boundary $|z|=1$ maps onto $|w|=1$.**

For $|z|=1$ (so $\bar z = 1/z$),
$$|z_k-z|^2=(z_k-z)(\bar z_k-\bar z)=|z_k|^2-z_k\bar z-\bar z_k z+1,$$
$$|1-\bar z_k z|^2=(1-\bar z_k z)(1-z_k\bar z)=1-z_k\bar z-\bar z_k z+|z_k|^2.$$
These are equal, so $|\phi_k(z)|=1$ for every $k$, giving $|w(z)|=\prod|\phi_k(z
)|=1$ on $|z|=1$.

**Step 3: The interior $|z|<1$ maps into $|w|<1$.**

For $|z|<1$, each $b_k$ (hence $\phi_k$) is an automorphism-class map sending th
e disk into itself with $|\phi_k(z)|<1$. (Indeed $b_k$ is a Möbius map of the di
sk onto itself, and $|\phi_k(z)|=|b_k(z)|<1$.) Therefore $|w(z)|=\prod_{k=1}^n|\
phi_k(z)|<1$ for $|z|<1$.

**Step 4: The map is onto the open disk (surjectivity).**

$w$ is holomorphic and non-constant on $|z|<1$, continuous on $|z|\le 1$, with $
|w|=1$ on $|z|=1$ and $|w|<1$ on $|z|<1$. By the argument principle, for any $w_
0$ with $|w_0|<1$, the number of solutions of $w(z)=w_0$ in $|z|<1$ (counted wit
h multiplicity) equals
$$\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{w'(z)}{w(z)-w_0}\,dz.$$
Since $|w(z)|=1>|w_0|$ on $|z|=1$, the function $w(z)-w_0$ has no zeros on the b
oundary, and the integral equals the total number of zeros of $w(z)-w_0$ inside.
 As a degree-$n$ Blaschke product, $w$ has exactly $n$ zeros (at $z=z_1,\dots,z_
n$) and winds $n$ times around the origin; more precisely, $w$ maps $|z|=1$ onto
 $|w|=1$ with winding number $n$, so for every $w_0\in\mathbb D$ the equation $w
(z)=w_0$ has exactly $n$ solutions in $\mathbb D$ (counting multiplicity). In pa
rticular, at least one solution exists, proving surjectivity onto the open disk.

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

- $|z|<1 \Rightarrow |w|<1$ (Step 3),
- $|z|=1 \Rightarrow |w|=1$ (Step 2),
- every $|w|<1$ is attained (Step 4),
- every $|w|=1$ is attained since $|z|=1$ maps onto $|w|=1$ (the boundary map ha
s winding number $n\ge 1$, hence is surjective on the circle).

Therefore the image of the closed unit disk $|z|\le 1$ is exactly the closed uni
t disk
$$\boxed{\,|w|\le 1\,}.$$
