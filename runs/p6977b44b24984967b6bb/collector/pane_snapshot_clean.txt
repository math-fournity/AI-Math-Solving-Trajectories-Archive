## Proof

Place coordinates with $F$ at the origin and $AB$ along the $x$-axis. Since $ABC
$ is acute, $F$ lies between $A$ and $B$. Set
$$A = (-a, 0),\quad B = (b, 0),\quad C = (0, c), \qquad a, b, c > 0.$$
The condition $AC > BC$ gives $a^2 + c^2 > b^2 + c^2$, hence $a > b$. Since $AF
= a$ and $PF = a$ with $P \neq A$ on line $AB$, we get $P = (a, 0)$.

**Key observation.** The circumcenter $O$ satisfies $OA = OC$, so $O$ lies on th
e perpendicular bisector of $AC$. Since $M$ is the midpoint of $AC$, line $OM$ *
is* the perpendicular bisector of $AC$.

**Concyclicity criterion.** By definition $Z \in OF$ and $Y \in OM$, so $O$ is t
he intersection of lines $FZ$ and $MY$. By the converse of the intersecting chor
ds theorem, $F, M, Y, Z$ are concyclic if and only if the signed products satisf
y
$$OF \cdot OZ = OM \cdot OY.$$
Parametrize $Z = O + \mu(F-O)$ and $Y = O + \lambda(M-O)$; then the condition be
comes $\mu\,|OF|^2 = \lambda\,|OM|^2$.

**Computing the key points.** The orthocenter, circumcenter, and midpoint are:
$$H = \Bigl(0,\,\tfrac{ab}{c}\Bigr),\quad O = \Bigl(\tfrac{b-a}{2},\,\tfrac{c^2-
ab}{2c}\Bigr),\quad M = \Bigl(-\tfrac{a}{2},\,\tfrac{c}{2}\Bigr).$$

*Finding $Z$.* Since $F$ is the origin, points on line $OF$ have the form $t\cdo
t O$, so $Z = (1-\mu)\,O$. Line $AC$ has equation $cx - ay + ac = 0$. Substituti
ng gives
$$(1-\mu)\cdot\frac{b(a^2+c^2)-2ac^2}{2c} + ac = 0.$$
Set $D = b(a^2+c^2) - 2ac^2$. Then $\mu = \dfrac{b(a^2+c^2)}{D}$.

*Finding $Y$.* First compute $X = BC \cap HP$. Line $HP$ has direction $(c, -b)$
; line $BC$ has direction $(-b, c)$. Solving,
$$X = \Bigl(\tfrac{b(c^2-ab)}{c^2-b^2},\;\tfrac{bc(a-b)}{c^2-b^2}\Bigr).$$
Line $FX$ (through the origin) has direction $(c^2 - ab,\; c(a-b))$.

Now $Y = OM \cap FX$. Writing $Y = O + \lambda(M - O)$ and imposing $Y \in FX$,
substitute into the parametric equations of line $FX$. Setting $s = 1 - \lambda$
, the system reduces to
$$s\cdot(-bD) = c^2(a^2 + c^2 - 2ab),$$
so $s = -\dfrac{c^2(a^2+c^2-2ab)}{bD}$ and
$$\lambda = 1 - s = \frac{bD + c^2(a^2+c^2-2ab)}{bD} = \frac{(a^2+c^2)(b^2+c^2)
- 4abc^2}{bD}.$$
Set $K = (a^2+c^2)(b^2+c^2) - 4abc^2$, so $\lambda = \dfrac{K}{bD}$.

**Computing squared distances.**
$$|OF|^2 = \Bigl(\tfrac{a-b}{2}\Bigr)^2 + \Bigl(\tfrac{ab-c^2}{2c}\Bigr)^2 = \fr
ac{(a-b)^2c^2 + (ab-c^2)^2}{4c^2} = \frac{K}{4c^2},$$
where the last equality uses $(a-b)^2c^2 + (ab-c^2)^2 = a^2b^2 + a^2c^2 + b^2c^2
 + c^4 - 4abc^2 = (a^2+c^2)(b^2+c^2) - 4abc^2 = K$.

$$|OM|^2 = \Bigl(\tfrac{b}{2}\Bigr)^2 + \Bigl(\tfrac{ab}{2c}\Bigr)^2 = \frac{b^2
(a^2+c^2)}{4c^2}.$$

**Verification.**
$$\mu\,|OF|^2 = \frac{b(a^2+c^2)}{D}\cdot\frac{K}{4c^2} = \frac{b(a^2+c^2)\,K}{4
c^2\,D},$$
$$\lambda\,|OM|^2 = \frac{K}{bD}\cdot\frac{b^2(a^2+c^2)}{4c^2} = \frac{b(a^2+c^2
)\,K}{4c^2\,D}.$$

These are identically equal, so $OF \cdot OZ = OM \cdot OY$ (signed). Since $O$
is the intersection of lines $FZ$ and $MY$, the converse of the intersecting cho
rds theorem guarantees that $F, M, Y, Z$ are concyclic. $\blacksquare$
