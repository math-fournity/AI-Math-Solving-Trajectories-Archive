## Solution

The statement is **false**. The "only if" direction fails: invariance under all
permutations of $X$ does not force uniformity on $Y^X$.

### Key Observation

The symmetric group $S_X$ acts on $Y^X$ (functions $g: X \to Y$) by precompositi
on: $\sigma$ sends $g \mapsto g \circ \sigma$. Two functions lie in the same orb
it iff they have the same **fiber-size profile** $\bigl(|g^{-1}(y)|\bigr)_{y \in
 Y}$. A distribution invariant under all of $S_X$ need only be **constant on eac
h orbit**; it may assign different total masses to different orbits. Uniformity
is the special case where every *element* gets equal mass — a much stronger cond
ition. Whenever $|X|\ge 2$ and $|Y|\ge 2$, there is more than one orbit, so non-
uniform invariant distributions exist.

### Counterexample

Let $X = \{1,2\}$ and $Y = \{0,1\}$. Identify each $g \in Y^X$ with the pair $(g
(1), g(2))$:
$$Y^X = \{(0,0),\;(0,1),\;(1,0),\;(1,1)\}.$$

The group $S_X = \{e, \tau\}$, where $\tau$ swaps $1 \leftrightarrow 2$. The act
ion of $\tau$ sends $(g(1),g(2)) \mapsto (g(2),g(1))$, so:
- $(0,0) \mapsto (0,0)$ — fixed,
- $(1,1) \mapsto (1,1)$ — fixed,
- $(0,1) \leftrightarrow (1,0)$ — swapped.

Define $\mu$ on $Y^X$ by
$$\mu(0,0) = \tfrac{1}{2},\qquad \mu(1,1) = \tfrac{1}{2},\qquad \mu(0,1) = \mu(1
,0) = 0.$$

**$\mu$ is a valid probability distribution:** the masses sum to $1$.

**$\mu$ is $S_X$-invariant:** Under $\tau$, the swapped pair $(0,1),(1,0)$ both
carry mass $0$, so swapping preserves them; the fixed points $(0,0),(1,1)$ keep
their masses. Trivially $\mu$ is invariant under $e$. Hence $\mu \circ f_\sigma^
{-1} = \mu$ for every $\sigma \in S_X$.

**$\mu$ is not uniform:** The uniform distribution on $Y^X$ assigns $\frac{1}{4}
$ to each of the four elements, but $\mu(0,0) = \frac{1}{2} \neq \frac{1}{4}$.

### Conclusion

We have exhibited a probability distribution on $Y^X$ that is invariant under ev
ery permutation of $X$ yet is not the uniform distribution. Therefore the "only
if" direction of the claimed equivalence is false, and the statement as a whole
is **false**.

(The "if" direction is trivially true — the uniform distribution is invariant un
der any bijection of $Y^X$, hence under each $f_\sigma$ — but this does not resc
ue the biconditional.)
