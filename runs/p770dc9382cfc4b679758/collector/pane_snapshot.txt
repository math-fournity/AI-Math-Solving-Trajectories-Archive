Let
$$I(a)=\int_{0}^{\pi}\bigl[\log(1-2a\cos x+a^{2})\bigr]^{2}\,dx,\qquad a>1.$$

### Step 1. Factor and expand the logarithm

Since $1-2a\cos x+a^{2}=(a-e^{ix})(a-e^{-ix})$ and $a>1$, set $r:=1/a\in(0,1)$.
Then
$$
1-2a\cos x+a^{2}=a^{2}\Bigl(1-\frac{e^{ix}}{a}\Bigr)\Bigl(1-\frac{e^{-ix}}{a}\Bi
gr)=a^{2}(1-re^{ix})(1-re^{-ix}).
$$

Taking logarithms and using $\log(1-z)=-\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{z^{n}}{n}$ for $|z|<1
$:
$$
\log(1-2a\cos x+a^{2})=2\log a+\log(1-re^{ix})+\log(1-re^{-ix})
=2\log a-2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}\cos(nx)}{n}.
$$

Denote $L:=2\log a$ and $S(x):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}\cos(nx)}{n}$, so t
he integrand is $(L-2S)^{2}=L^{2}-4LS+4S^{2}$.

### Step 2. Integrate term by term

**Constant term.** $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}L^{2}\,dx=\pi L^{2}=4\pi(\log a)^
{2}$.

**Cross term.** $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}S(x)\,dx=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^
{n}}{n}\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos(nx)\,dx=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n}\cdot\frac{\
sin(n\pi)}{n}=0.$

**Quadratic term.** Using the orthogonality $\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos(mx)\cos(nx)\,dx=
\frac{\pi}{2}\delta_{mn}$ for $m,n\ge1$:
$$
\int_{0}^{\pi}S(x)^{2}\,dx=\sum_{m,n\ge1}\frac{r^{m+n}}{mn}\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos(mx
)\cos(nx)\,dx
=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2n}}{n^{2}}
=\frac{\pi}{2}\operatorname{Li}_{2}(r^{2}).
$$

(Justification for termwise integration: the series for $S$ converges absolutely
 and uniformly on $[0,\pi]$ since $r<1$, and the same holds for the Cauchy produ
ct governing $S^{2}$.)

### Step 3. Combine

$$
I(a)=4\pi(\log a)^{2}+4\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}\operatorname{Li}_{2}(r^{2})
=4\pi(\log a)^{2}+2\pi\,\operatorname{Li}_{2}\!\Bigl(\frac{1}{a^{2}}\Bigr).
$$

### Step 4. Verification at $a=1$ (boundary)

At $a=1$: $1-2\cos x+1=4\sin^{2}(x/2)$, so
$$
I(1)=\int_{0}^{\pi}\bigl[\log(4\sin^{2}(x/2))\bigr]^{2}dx
=8\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\bigl[\log(2\sin u)\bigr]^{2}du.
$$
Using the classical values $\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\log(2\sin u)\,du=0$ and $\int_{0}^{
\pi/2}(\log\sin u)^{2}du=\frac{\pi}{2}(\log2)^{2}+\frac{\pi^{3}}{24}$, one obtai
ns $\int_{0}^{\pi/2}[\log(2\sin u)]^{2}du=\frac{\pi^{3}}{24}$, hence $I(1)=\frac
{\pi^{3}}{3}$.

The formula gives $4\pi\cdot0+2\pi\operatorname{Li}_{2}(1)=2\pi\cdot\frac{\pi^{2
}}{6}=\frac{\pi^{3}}{3}$. ✓

### Result

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}\bigl[\log(1-2a\cos x+a^{2})\bigr]^{2}dx
=4\pi(\log a)^{2}+2\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}\,a^{2n}}
=4\pi(\log a)^{2}+2\pi\,\operatorname{Li}_{2}\!\Bigl(\frac{1}{a^{2}}\Bigr),\qqua
d a>1.}
$$
