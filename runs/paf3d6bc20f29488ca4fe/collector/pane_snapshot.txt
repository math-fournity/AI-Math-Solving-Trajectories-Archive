## Solution

Let the sides of the cyclic quadrilateral be $a=AB=6$, $b=BC$, $c=CD=6$, $d=AD$,
 with diagonals $AC=BD=8$.

**Step 1: Find $AD$ and $BC$ via Ptolemy's theorem.**

Since $ABCD$ is cyclic, Ptolemy gives
$$AC\cdot BD = AB\cdot CD + AD\cdot BC.$$
$$8\cdot 8 = 6\cdot 6 + AD\cdot BC \;\Longrightarrow\; AD\cdot BC = 28.$$
Given $BC = AD+3$, let $AD=x$:
$$x(x+3)=28 \;\Longrightarrow\; x^2+3x-28=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; x=\frac{-3+11}{2
}=4.$$
So $\boxed{AD=4,\; BC=7}$.

**Step 2: Verify the diagonals are consistent (sanity check).**

Using the cyclic-quadrilateral diagonal relation for $AC$ (splitting into $\tria
ngle ABC$ and $\triangle CDA$, with $B+D=180^\circ$):
$$\cos B = \frac{a^2+b^2-c^2-d^2}{2(ab+cd)}=\frac{36+49-36-16}{2(42+24)}=\frac{3
3}{132}=\frac14.$$
$$AC^2 = a^2+b^2-2ab\cos B = 36+49-2\cdot6\cdot7\cdot\tfrac14 = 85-21=64.\;\chec
kmark$$
Similarly $\cos A=\frac{a^2+d^2-b^2-c^2}{2(ad+bc)}=\frac{36+16-49-36}{2(24+42)}=
-\frac14$, giving $BD^2=36+16+12=64$. $\checkmark$

**Step 3: Compute the area via Brahmagupta's formula.**

The semiperimeter is
$$s=\frac{6+7+6+4}{2}=\frac{23}{2}.$$
$$s-a=\frac{11}{2},\quad s-b=\frac{9}{2},\quad s-c=\frac{11}{2},\quad s-d=\frac{
15}{2}.$$
$$\text{Area}=\sqrt{(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)(s-d)}=\sqrt{\frac{11\cdot9\cdot11\cdot15}{16
}}=\frac{11\sqrt{9\cdot15}}{4}=\frac{33\sqrt{15}}{4}.$$

Here $p=33$, $q=15$, $r=4$, with $\gcd(33,4)=1$ and $15$ square-free.

$$p+q+r = 33+15+4 = \boxed{52}.$$
