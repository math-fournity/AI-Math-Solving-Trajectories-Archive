## Proof

Let $s_n = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{1}{k}$. We use the **Arithmetic–Geometric Mean i
nequality** in its strict form: for positive reals $x_1, \dots, x_m$ that are no
t all equal,

$$\frac{x_1 + \cdots + x_m}{m} > (x_1 \cdots x_m)^{1/m}.$$

---

### Part 1: $n(n+1)^{1/n} < n + s_n$ for $n > 1$

Consider the $n$ positive numbers

$$a_k = 1 + \frac{1}{k} = \frac{k+1}{k}, \qquad k = 1, 2, \dots, n.$$

**Their sum telescopes:**

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k = \sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(1 + \frac{1}{k}\right) = n + s_n.$$

**Their product telescopes:**

$$\prod_{k=1}^{n} a_k = \prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{k+1}{k} = \frac{2}{1}\cdot\frac{3}{
2}\cdot\frac{4}{3}\cdots\frac{n+1}{n} = n+1.$$

Since $n > 1$, the numbers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ are **not all equal** (indeed
$a_1 = 2 \neq 3/2 = a_2$). Applying the strict AM–GM inequality:

$$\frac{n + s_n}{n} > \left(\prod_{k=1}^{n} a_k\right)^{1/n} = (n+1)^{1/n}.$$

Multiplying both sides by $n$:

$$\boxed{n(n+1)^{1/n} < n + s_n.} \qquad \blacksquare$$

---

### Part 2: $(n-1)\,n^{-1/(n-1)} < n - s_n$ for $n > 2$

Consider the $n-1$ positive numbers

$$b_k = 1 - \frac{1}{k} = \frac{k-1}{k}, \qquad k = 2, 3, \dots, n.$$

**Their sum telescopes:**

$$\sum_{k=2}^{n} b_k = \sum_{k=2}^{n}\left(1 - \frac{1}{k}\right) = (n-1) - \sum
_{k=2}^{n}\frac{1}{k} = (n-1) - (s_n - 1) = n - s_n.$$

**Their product telescopes:**

$$\prod_{k=2}^{n} b_k = \prod_{k=2}^{n}\frac{k-1}{k} = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{
3}\cdot\frac{3}{4}\cdots\frac{n-1}{n} = \frac{1}{n}.$$

Since $n > 2$, the numbers $b_2, b_3, \dots, b_n$ are **not all equal** (indeed
$b_2 = 1/2 \neq 2/3 = b_3$). Applying the strict AM–GM inequality to these $n-1$
 numbers:

$$\frac{n - s_n}{n-1} > \left(\prod_{k=2}^{n} b_k\right)^{1/(n-1)} = \left(\frac
{1}{n}\right)^{1/(n-1)} = n^{-1/(n-1)}.$$

Multiplying both sides by $n-1$:

$$\boxed{(n-1)\,n^{-1/(n-1)} < n - s_n.} \qquad \blacksquare$$

---

### Summary

Both inequalities are direct consequences of the **strict AM–GM inequality** app
lied to carefully chosen sets of positive numbers whose products and sums both t
elescope:

| Part | Numbers | Count | Product (telescopes) | Sum (telescopes) |
|------|---------|-------|----------------------|-------------------|
| 1 | $\frac{k+1}{k},\; k=1,\dots,n$ | $n$ | $n+1$ | $n+s_n$ |
| 2 | $\frac{k-1}{k},\; k=2,\dots,n$ | $n-1$ | $1/n$ | $n-s_n$ |

The strictness of AM–GM is guaranteed because the chosen numbers are pairwise di
stinct whenever $n>1$ (Part 1) or $n>2$ (Part 2), which is exactly the stated ra
nge of validity.

### PROOF COMPLETE
