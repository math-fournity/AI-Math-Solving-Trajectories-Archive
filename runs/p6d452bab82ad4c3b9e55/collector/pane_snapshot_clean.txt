**Moments of Laplace(0,1):**
$$E[X_i]=0, \quad \text{Var}(X_i)=E[X_i^2]=\int_0^\infty x^2 e^{-x}\,dx = \Gamma

**Applying CLT:** Since $\text{Var}(\sum_{i=1}^n X_i)=2n$ and similarly for $Y$:
$$\frac{\sum X_i}{\sqrt{2n}} \xrightarrow{d} N(0,1), \qquad \frac{\sum Y_i}{\sqr

independently (the pairs $(X_n,Y_n)$ are independent and $X_n, Y_n$ are independ

**Rewriting the event:**
$$P\!\left(\sqrt{\left(\sum X_i\right)^2+\left(\sum Y_i\right)^2}>c\sqrt{n}\righ
t) = P\!\left(\frac{(\sum X_i)^2+(\sum Y_i)^2}{n}>c^2\right).$$

**Limiting distribution:** Let $Z_1, Z_2 \sim N(0,1)$ independent. Then
$$\frac{(\sum X_i)^2+(\sum Y_i)^2}{n} = 2\cdot\frac{(\sum X_i)^2+(\sum Y_i)^2}{2
n} \xrightarrow{d} 2(Z_1^2+Z_2^2)=2\chi^2_2.$$

Since $\chi^2_2 \sim \text{Exp}(1/2)$ with survival function $P(W>x)=e^{-x/2}$,
the variable $U=2W$ has survival:
$$P(U>u)=P(W>u/2)=e^{-u/4}.$$

**Solving for $c$:** Setting the limit equal to 0.05:
$$P(U>c^2)=e^{-c^2/4}=0.05.$$

Taking logarithms:
$$-\frac{c^2}{4}=\ln(0.05)=-\ln(20),$$
$$c^2=4\ln 20, \qquad c=2\sqrt{\ln 20}.$$
