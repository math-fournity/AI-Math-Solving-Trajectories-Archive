## Proof

Let $s = x_1 + x_2$. Observe that the map $\Phi$ preserves the sum of coordinate
s:
$$
y_1 + y_2 = \bigl(x_1 + h(s)\bigr) + \bigl(x_2 - h(s)\bigr) = x_1 + x_2 = s.
$$

**Change of variables.** Introduce coordinates $(s, t)$ with $s = x_1+x_2$ and $
t = x_1$ (so $x_2 = s-t$). The linear map $(x_1,x_2)\mapsto(s,t)$ has Jacobian m
atrix $\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ with determinant $-1$, hence $|{\de
t}|=1$ and $dx_1\,dx_2 = ds\,dt$.

In these coordinates the domain becomes
$$
\Omega = \{(s,t): s\in[0,2],\; t\in[\max(0,s-1),\,\min(1,s)]\},
$$
and $\Phi$ acts by
$$
(s,t)\longmapsto (s,\; t+h(s)),
$$
since $y_1 = t + h(s)$ and $y_1+y_2=s$. Thus $\Phi$ is a **translation in $t$ by
 the amount $h(s)$** on each fiber $\{s=\text{const}\}$.

**Image set.** Therefore
$$
\Phi(\Omega) = \bigl\{(s,u): s\in[0,2],\; u\in[\max(0,s-1)+h(s),\,\min(1,s)+h(s)
]\bigr\}.
$$
Note this description is valid for arbitrary continuous $h$; no differentiabilit
y is needed.

**Measure computation.** Using the inverse change of variables $(y_1,y_2)\mapsto
(s,y_1)$ with $s=y_1+y_2$, whose Jacobian also has absolute value $1$, the Lebes
gue measure of $\Phi(\Omega)$ equals
$$
\int_0^{2}\Bigl(\min(1,s)-\max(0,s-1)\Bigr)\,ds.
$$
For $s\in[0,1]$: $\min(1,s)-\max(0,s-1)=s-0=s$. For $s\in[1,2]$: $\min(1,s)-\max
(0,s-1)=1-(s-1)=2-s$. Hence
$$
\int_0^{2}\bigl(\min(1,s)-\max(0,s-1)\bigr)\,ds = \int_0^{1}s\,ds + \int_1^{2}(2
-s)\,ds = \frac12 + \frac12 = 1.
$$

The translation $t\mapsto t+h(s)$ preserves the length of each fiber-interval, s
o the measure is independent of the choice of continuous $h$.

$$
\boxed{m\bigl(\Phi(\Omega)\bigr)=1.}
$$
