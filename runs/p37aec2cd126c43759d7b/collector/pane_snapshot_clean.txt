e{\varphi}(g) = \text{pr}_1(\sigma(g))$, where $\text{pr}_1: L \to \text{Homeo}(

**Key equivalence.** The extension $1 \to K \to L \to G \to 1$ (with $K = \pi_1(
X,x_0)$) is a **direct product** (trivial extension) if and only if:

1. The extension **splits** (there exists a homomorphic section $\sigma: G \to L

2. The image $\sigma(G)$ **centralizes** the kernel $K$, i.e., $\sigma(g)\,\gamm

*Proof of the equivalence.* If $L \cong K \times G$ as groups, with the isomorph

**Main argument.** The hypothesis states that the extension is trivial, i.e., $L

$$\sigma(g)\,\gamma\,\sigma(g)^{-1} = \gamma \quad \text{for all } g \in G,\; \g
amma \in \pi_1(X, x_0).$$

Now, the deck transformation corresponding to $\gamma \in \pi_1(X, x_0)$ is the

The conjugation relation $\sigma(g)\,\gamma\,\sigma(g)^{-1} = \gamma$ in $L$ tra

$$\widetilde{\varphi}(g) \circ D_\gamma \circ \widetilde{\varphi}(g)^{-1} = D_\g

$$\widetilde{\varphi}(g) \circ D_\gamma = D_\gamma \circ \widetilde{\varphi}(g)
\quad \text{for all } g \in G,\; \gamma \in \pi_1(X, x_0).$$

This is precisely the statement that **the lifted group action of $G$ on $\widet

**Conclusion.** The triviality of the extension (being a direct product) is *equ

Therefore, **yes**, the lifted group action commutes with the action of the fund
