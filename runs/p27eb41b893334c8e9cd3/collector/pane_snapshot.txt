Looking at this problem, I need to find all $n$ with $1 \le n \le 100$ such that
 there exists a permutation of the $n$ bays where the $n-1$ clockwise displaceme
nts are all distinct and in $\{1, \ldots, n-1\}$.

Since there are $n-1$ displacements, all distinct, each in $\{1, \ldots, n-1\}$,
 they must form exactly the set $\{1, 2, \ldots, n-1\}$.

## Necessary Condition

Label bays $0, 1, \ldots, n-1$ on the circle $\mathbb{Z}_n$. Let the visit seque
nce be $a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_n$ (a permutation of $\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$). The displa
cements are $d_i = (a_{i+1} - a_i) \bmod n$.

**Sum constraint:** The displacements sum to $\sum_{i=1}^{n-1} d_i = 1+2+\cdots+
(n-1) = \frac{n(n-1)}{2}$.

Telescoping: $\sum d_i \equiv a_n - a_1 \pmod{n}$.

So $\frac{n(n-1)}{2} \equiv a_n - a_1 \pmod{n}$.

- **If $n$ is odd** ($n > 1$): $\frac{n(n-1)}{2} \equiv 0 \pmod{n}$, forcing $a_
n \equiv a_1 \pmod{n}$, hence $a_n = a_1$. This contradicts all positions being
distinct. **No odd $n > 1$ works.**

- **If $n$ is even:** $\frac{n(n-1)}{2} \equiv \frac{n}{2} \pmod{n}$, so $a_n -
a_1 \equiv \frac{n}{2} \pmod{n}$ (diametrically opposite). This is necessary but
 not obviously sufficient.

- **$n = 1$:** Zero displacements, condition vacuously satisfied. **Works.**

## Sufficiency for Even $n$

**Explicit construction for $n = 2m$:** Define the sequence
$$0,\; 1,\; 2m{-}1,\; 2,\; 2m{-}2,\; 3,\; 2m{-}3,\; \ldots,\; m{+}1,\; m$$
i.e., $a_1 = 0$, $a_{2k} = k$ for $k=1,\ldots,m$, and $a_{2k+1} = 2m - k$ for $k
=1,\ldots,m-1$.

**All elements distinct:** The even-indexed terms give $\{0, 1, 2, \ldots, m\}$
and the odd-indexed terms (after $a_1$) give $\{2m-1, 2m-2, \ldots, m+1\}$. Toge
ther: $\{0, 1, \ldots, 2m-1\}$. ✓

**Displacements are all distinct:** The differences alternate:
- Odd-indexed differences: $1, 3, 5, \ldots, 2m-1$ (all $m$ odd numbers from $1$
 to $n-1$)
- Even-indexed differences: $2m-2, 2m-4, \ldots, 2$ (all $m-1$ even numbers from
 $2$ to $n-2$)

Together these are exactly $\{1, 2, \ldots, 2m-1\} = \{1, 2, \ldots, n-1\}$. ✓

**Verification for small cases:**
- $n=2$: sequence $0, 1$; displacement $\{1\}$. ✓
- $n=4$: sequence $0, 1, 3, 2$; displacements $1, 2, 3$. ✓
- $n=6$: sequence $0, 1, 5, 2, 4, 3$; displacements $1, 4, 3, 2, 5 = \{1,2,3,4,5
\}$. ✓
- $n=8$: sequence $0, 1, 7, 2, 6, 3, 5, 4$; displacements $1, 6, 3, 4, 5, 2, 7 =
 \{1,...,7\}$. ✓

So **every even $n$ is a hub number**, and no odd $n > 1$ is.

## Computing the Sum

$$S = \{1\} \cup \{2, 4, 6, \ldots, 100\}$$

$$\sum_{n \in S} n = 1 + (2 + 4 + 6 + \cdots + 100) = 1 + 2\cdot\frac{50 \cdot 5
1}{2} = 1 + 2550 = \boxed{2551}$$
