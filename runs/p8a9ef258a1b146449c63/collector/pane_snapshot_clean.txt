## Proof

**Setup.** We have the probability density on $(S^2)^N$
$$
p(\Gamma)=\frac{1}{Z}\exp\!\Big(\delta\sum_{j=1}^N \cos\gamma_j\Big),\qquad
Z=\int_{(S^2)^N}\exp\!\Big(\delta\sum_{j=1}^N \cos\gamma_j\Big)\,dS,
$$
and we must show
$$
\langle \sin\gamma_i\rangle=\int_{(S^2)^N}\sin\gamma_i\,p(\Gamma)\,dS=0.
$$

**Key observation — the density is even in every $\gamma_j$.**
Consider the change of variables $\Phi_i:\gamma_i\mapsto -\gamma_i$ (equivalentl
y $\gamma_i\mapsto 2\pi-\gamma_i$), leaving all other angles $\gamma_j$ ($j\neq
i$) and every azimuthal variable fixed. This is a reflection of the $i$-th spher
e, hence an orthogonal transformation. The surface measure $dS$ on $(S^2)^N$ is
invariant under any orthogonal transformation of any factor, so
$$
\Phi_i^{*}(dS)=dS.
$$
Moreover
$$
\cos(-\gamma_i)=\cos\gamma_i,
$$
so the Boltzmann weight is unchanged:
$$
\exp\!\Big(\delta\sum_{j=1}^N\cos\gamma_j\Big)\;\xrightarrow{\;\Phi_i\;}\;\exp\!
\Big(\delta\sum_{j=1}^N\cos\gamma_j\Big).
$$
Thus $Z$ and $p(\Gamma)$ are both invariant under $\Phi_i$.

**The observable is odd.** Under the same map,
$$
\sin\gamma_i\;\xrightarrow{\;\Phi_i\;}\;\sin(-\gamma_i)=-\sin\gamma_i.
$$

**Parity argument.** Using the invariance of $p(\Gamma)\,dS$ under $\Phi_i$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle\sin\gamma_i\rangle
&=\int_{(S^2)^N}\sin\gamma_i\;p(\Gamma)\,dS \\
&=\int_{(S^2)^N}\sin(\Phi_i(\gamma_i))\;p(\Phi_i(\Gamma))\,\Phi_i^{*}(dS) \qquad
(\text{change of variables }\Gamma\mapsto\Phi_i(\Gamma))\\
&=\int_{(S^2)^N}(-\sin\gamma_i)\;p(\Gamma)\,dS \qquad(\text{oddness of }\sin\tex
t{, invariance of }p\text{ and }dS)\\
&=-\langle\sin\gamma_i\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence $2\langle\sin\gamma_i\rangle=0$, and therefore
$$
\boxed{\;\langle\sin\gamma_i\rangle=0\;}.
$$

**Remark.** No assumption on the sign or magnitude of $\delta$ was used; the res
ult holds for every real $\delta$ (and trivially also for $\delta=0$, where $p$
is uniform). The only ingredients are (i) the reflection invariance of the unifo
rm surface measure on $S^2$, and (ii) the fact that the weight depends on $\gamm
a_i$ only through $\cos\gamma_i$, which is even, while the observable $\sin\gamm
a_i$ is odd. The same argument applies simultaneously to every index $i$, so $\l
angle\sin\gamma_i\rangle=0$ for all $i=1,\dots,N$.
