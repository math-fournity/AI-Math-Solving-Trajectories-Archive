## Solution

**Setup.** Place the circumcenter of equilateral triangle $ABC$ (side $6$) at th
e origin. The circumradius is $R = \tfrac{6}{\sqrt{3}} = 2\sqrt{3}$. Set
$$A=(2\sqrt{3},0),\quad B=(-\sqrt{3},3),\quad C=(-\sqrt{3},-3),$$
at polar angles $0°, 120°, 240°$ respectively.

**Parametrize $P$.** Since $P$ lies on the arc from $A$ to $C$ not containing $B
$, write $P$ at angle $240°+\alpha$ with $\alpha\in(0°,120°)$, and set $\beta=\a
lpha/2\in(0°,60°)$.

Chord lengths on a circle of radius $R=2\sqrt{3}$:
$$AP = 4\sqrt{3}\sin(60°-\beta),\qquad PC = 4\sqrt{3}\sin\beta.$$

**Use the constraint $AP\cdot PC=10$.**
$$48\,\sin(60°-\beta)\sin\beta = 10.$$
Product-to-sum: $\sin(60°-\beta)\sin\beta = \tfrac{1}{2}[\cos(60°-2\beta)-\cos 6
0°] = \tfrac{1}{2}[\cos(60°-2\beta)-\tfrac{1}{2}]$. Hence
$$24\bigl[\cos(60°-2\beta)-\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr]=10 \;\Longrightarrow\; \cos(60°-2\
beta)=\frac{11}{12}.$$

**Compute $BP$.** The angle between $B$ ($120°$) and $P$ ($240°+2\beta$) gives,
using the minor arc,
$$BP = 4\sqrt{3}\sin(60°+\beta)$$
(since $\sin(120°-\beta)=\sin(60°+\beta)$ covers both sub-cases $\beta\lessgtr 3
0°$).

Now
$$\sin^2(60°+\beta)=\frac{1-\cos(120°+2\beta)}{2}.$$
Since $120°+2\beta = 180°-(60°-2\beta)$,
$$\cos(120°+2\beta)=-\cos(60°-2\beta)=-\frac{11}{12}.$$
Therefore
$$\sin^2(60°+\beta)=\frac{1+\frac{11}{12}}{2}=\frac{23}{24},$$
and
$$BP^2 = 48\cdot\frac{23}{24}=46.$$

**Verification via Ptolemy.** For cyclic quadrilateral $A,P,C,B$ (in circular or
der), Ptolemy gives $AC\cdot PB = AP\cdot CB + PC\cdot AB$, i.e. $6\,BP = 6(AP+P
C)$, so $BP=AP+PC$. Indeed $(AP+PC)^2 = 48\cos^2(30°-\beta) = 48\sin^2(60°+\beta
)=46$, consistent. ✓

$$\boxed{BP=\sqrt{46}}$$
