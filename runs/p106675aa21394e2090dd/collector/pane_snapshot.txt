The problem is the **Gap Conjecture** of Grigorchuk, formalized as a Lean theore

 group theory.

**Analysis of the problem:**

The conjecture states: if a finitely generated group has superpolynomial growth
(with respect to some finite generating set), then its growth function is at lea
st $e^{\sqrt{n}}$ in Grigorchuk's preorder on growth functions, where comparison
 is witnessed by linearly rescaling the radius.

This result was proven mathematically by Grigorchuk (2012, arXiv:1202.6044), but
 the proof is extremely involved. It requires:

1. **Structure theory of branch groups and self-similar groups** — the proof pro
ceeds by analyzing the possible growth types of finitely generated groups, using
 the fact that groups of intermediate growth must be "close to" branch/self-simi
lar groups.

2. **Grigorchuk's gap theorem** — showing that the growth function of any group
with superpolynomial growth is $\succeq e^{\sqrt{n}}$, where $\succeq$ denotes t
he preorder comparing functions up to linear rescaling of the argument.

3. **Detailed case analysis** — the proof classifies groups by their growth and
shows that no group can have growth strictly between polynomial and $e^{\sqrt{n}
}$ (in the preorder sense). This uses:
   - The Tits alternative and growth dichotomies
   - Properties of the first group of intermediate growth (Grigorchuk group) and
 its generalizations
   - Lower bound techniques for growth functions of self-similar groups
   - The connection between growth and the spectral radius / return probability
of random walks

4. **Lower bound $e^{\sqrt{n}}$** — the key insight is that for any group with s
uperpolynomial growth, the return probability of a simple random walk decays at
most like $e^{-c\sqrt{n}}$, which translates (via the Varopoulos-Carne bounds an
d their converses) to a growth lower bound of $e^{\sqrt{n}}$.
