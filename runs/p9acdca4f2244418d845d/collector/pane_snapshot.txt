## Proof

**Claim:** Every finite-dimensional Banach space is super-reflexive. The answer
is **yes**.

We use the standard characterization: a Banach space $X$ is *super-reflexive* if
 and only if every Banach space finitely representable in $X$ is reflexive.

Recall that a Banach space $Y$ is *finitely representable* in $X$ if for every f
inite-dimensional subspace $F \subseteq Y$ and every $\varepsilon > 0$, there ex
ists a linear embedding $T: F \to X$ such that
$$(1+\varepsilon)^{-1}\|x\| \leq \|Tx\| \leq (1+\varepsilon)\|x\| \quad \text{fo
r all } x \in F.$$

**Step 1: Finite representability preserves dimension bounds.**

Let $X$ be a finite-dimensional Banach space with $\dim X = n$. Let $Y$ be any B
anach space finitely representable in $X$. We show that $\dim Y \leq n$.

Suppose for contradiction that $Y$ contains a finite-dimensional subspace $F$ wi
th $\dim F = m > n$. By finite representability, for any $\varepsilon > 0$ (say
$\varepsilon = 1$), there exists a linear embedding $T: F \to X$ with
$$\tfrac{1}{2}\|x\| \leq \|Tx\| \leq 2\|x\| \quad \text{for all } x \in F.$$
Since $T$ is injective (it is bounded below), $T(F)$ is an $m$-dimensional subsp
ace of $X$. But $X$ has dimension $n < m$, so it cannot contain an $m$-dimension
al subspace. Contradiction.

Therefore every finite-dimensional subspace of $Y$ has dimension $\leq n$. Takin
g the union of an increasing chain of finite-dimensional subspaces (or simply no
ting that if $\dim Y > n$, then $Y$ contains an $(n+1)$-dimensional subspace), w
e conclude $\dim Y \leq n$.

**Step 2: Finite-dimensional spaces are reflexive.**

Every finite-dimensional Banach space is reflexive. Indeed, if $\dim Y = k < \in
fty$, then $Y$ is linearly homeomorphic (in fact isometrically isomorphic after
choosing bases) to $(\mathbb{R}^k, \|\cdot\|)$ for some norm. The canonical embe
dding $J: Y \to Y^{**}$ is a linear map between spaces of the same finite dimens
ion $k$. Since $J$ is always injective (a standard consequence of the Hahn–Banac
h theorem: if $y \neq 0$, there exists $f \in Y^*$ with $f(y) \neq 0$, so $J(y)
\neq 0$), and $\dim Y = \dim Y^{**} = k$, the injective linear map $J$ is an iso
morphism. Hence $Y$ is reflexive.

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

Let $X$ be a finite-dimensional Banach space. By Step 1, every Banach space $Y$
finitely representable in $X$ satisfies $\dim Y \leq \dim X < \infty$. By Step 2
, every such $Y$ is reflexive. By the definition of super-reflexivity, $X$ is su
per-reflexive.

**Alternative argument (via uniformly convex renorming):** Every finite-dimensio
nal Banach space $X$ of dimension $n$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^n$ with some
norm. Since all norms on $\mathbb{R}^n$ are equivalent, the Euclidean norm $\|\c
dot\|_2$ is equivalent to the given norm on $X$. The Euclidean norm is uniformly
 convex (by the parallelogram identity). Hence $X$ admits an equivalent uniforml
y convex norm, which by Enflo's theorem is equivalent to super-reflexivity. $\sq
uare$

Since $X$ was an arbitrary finite-dimensional Banach space, **every finite-dimen
sional Banach space is super-reflexive**.
