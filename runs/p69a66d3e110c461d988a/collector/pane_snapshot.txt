the sequence $({n\alpha})_{n \geq 1}$ is equidistributed in $[0,1)$. This means
that for any interval $I \subseteq [0,1)$ of Lebesgue measure $\ell$,

$$\lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{|{1 \leq n \leq N : {n\alpha} \in I}|}{N} = \ell.$$

Now suppose for contradiction that there exist $d \in (0,1]$ and $S \subseteq
\mathbb{N}$ with natural density $d$ such that the subsequence $({i\alpha} : i
\in S)$ converges to some limit $L$.

Since ${n\alpha} \in [0,1)$ for all $n$, we have $L \in [0,1]$.

Claim: $d = 0$, contradicting $d > 0$.

Fix any $\epsilon \in (0, 1/2)$. Since ${i\alpha} \to L$ for $i \in S$, there
exists $N_0$ such that for all $i \in S$ with $i \geq N_0$,

$${i\alpha} \in (L - \epsilon,; L + \epsilon) \pmod{1}.$$

Let $T_\epsilon = {n \in \mathbb{N} : {n\alpha} \in (L - \epsilon, L + \epsilon)
\pmod{1}}$. The set $(L-\epsilon, L+\epsilon) \pmod{1}$ is an interval (or union
of two intervals) in $[0,1)$ of total Lebesgue measure $2\epsilon$. By
equidistribution, $T_\epsilon$ has natural density $2\epsilon$.

Since $S \setminus T_\epsilon$ is finite (all elements of $S$ beyond $N_0$ lie
in $T_\epsilon$), we have for all sufficiently large $N$:

$$|S \cap {1, \ldots, N}| \leq |T_\epsilon \cap {1, \ldots, N}| + N_0.$$

Dividing by $N$ and letting $N \to \infty$:

$$d \leq 2\epsilon.$$

yields $d = 0$, contradicting $d > 0$.
