## Solution

**The answer is yes.** The inequality holds for all probability measures $\mu, \
nu$ on $\mathbb{R}$. In fact, we prove a stronger statement: convolution with *a
ny* fixed probability measure is non-expansive in the Prokhorov metric.

---

### Theorem

For all probability measures $\mu, \nu, \gamma$ on $\mathbb{R}$,
$$d_P(\gamma * \mu,\; \gamma * \nu) \;\le\; d_P(\mu, \nu).$$

Setting $\gamma = \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ gives the claimed result.

---

### Proof

Let $\eta := d_P(\mu,\nu)$. Fix an arbitrary $\varepsilon > \eta$. By definition
 of $d_P$ as an infimum, the Prokhorov condition holds at level $\varepsilon$: f
or every Borel set $B \subseteq \mathbb{R}$,
$$\mu(B) \le \nu(B^\varepsilon) + \varepsilon \qquad\text{and}\qquad \nu(B) \le
\mu(B^\varepsilon) + \varepsilon. \tag{$\ast$}$$

We will show that for every Borel set $B$,
$$(\gamma * \mu)(B) \le (\gamma * \nu)(B^\varepsilon) + \varepsilon. \tag{$\ast\
ast$}$$

The symmetric inequality (with $\mu, \nu$ interchanged) follows by the identical
 argument, so together they yield $d_P(\gamma*\mu,\,\gamma*\nu)\le\varepsilon$.
Letting $\varepsilon \downarrow \eta$ then gives the theorem.

---

**Setup.** Fix a Borel set $B$. Let $Z \sim \gamma$ and define two Borel-measura
ble functions $f, g : \mathbb{R} \to [0,1]$ by
$$f(x) := P(x + Z \in B) = \gamma(B - x), \qquad g(y) := P(y + Z \in B^\varepsil
on) = \gamma(B^\varepsilon - y).$$

Measurability of $f$ and $g$ follows from Fubini's theorem, since $(x,z)\mapsto
\mathbf{1}_B(x+z)$ is Borel. Note the identities
$$(\gamma * \mu)(B) = \int_{\mathbb{R}} f(x)\,d\mu(x), \qquad (\gamma * \nu)(B^\
varepsilon) = \int_{\mathbb{R}} g(y)\,d\nu(y).$$

---

**Key observation.** *If $|x - y| \le \varepsilon$, then $f(x) \le g(y)$.*

*Proof of observation.* On the event $\{x + Z \in B\}$, we have
$$|(y+Z) - (x+Z)| = |y - x| \le \varepsilon,$$
so by definition of $B^\varepsilon = \{w : \inf_{b\in B}|w - b| \le \varepsilon\
}$, it follows that $y + Z \in B^\varepsilon$. Hence $\{x+Z \in B\} \subseteq \{
y+Z \in B^\varepsilon\}$, and taking probabilities gives $f(x) \le g(y)$. $\squa
re$

---

**Relating the level sets.** For each $t \in [0,1]$, define Borel sets
$$A_t := \{x : f(x) \ge t\}, \qquad C_t := \{y : g(y) \ge t\}.$$

We claim $A_t^\varepsilon \subseteq C_t$. Indeed, if $x \in A_t$ and $y \in A_t^
\varepsilon$, then $\exists\, x' \in A_t$ with $|y - x'| \le \varepsilon$. By th
e key observation, $g(y) \ge f(x') \ge t$, so $y \in C_t$.

Applying the Prokhorov condition $(\ast)$ to the Borel set $A_t$ and using $A_t^
\varepsilon \subseteq C_t$:
$$\mu(A_t) \;\le\; \nu(A_t^\varepsilon) + \varepsilon \;\le\; \nu(C_t) + \vareps
ilon. \tag{1}$$

---

**Layer-cake integration.** Since $f$ is $[0,1]$-valued and measurable, the Cava
lieri (layer-cake) formula gives
$$\int f\,d\mu = \int_0^1 \mu(A_t)\,dt, \qquad \int g\,d\nu = \int_0^1 \nu(C_t)\
,dt.$$

Integrating inequality (1) over $t \in [0,1]$:
$$(\gamma * \mu)(B) = \int f\,d\mu = \int_0^1 \mu(A_t)\,dt \;\le\; \int_0^1\!\bi
gl(\nu(C_t) + \varepsilon\bigr)\,dt = \int g\,d\nu + \varepsilon = (\gamma * \nu
)(B^\varepsilon) + \varepsilon.$$

This establishes $(\ast\ast)$.

---

**Symmetric direction.** Swapping the roles of $\mu$ and $\nu$ throughout (and u
sing the second inequality in $(\ast)$) gives
$$(\gamma * \nu)(B) \le (\gamma * \mu)(B^\varepsilon) + \varepsilon \qquad \text
{for all Borel } B.$$

Combining both directions, $d_P(\gamma * \mu,\, \gamma * \nu) \le \varepsilon$.

---

**Conclusion.** Since $\varepsilon > d_P(\mu,\nu)$ was arbitrary,
$$d_P(\gamma * \mu,\, \gamma * \nu) \le d_P(\mu, \nu).$$

Taking $\gamma = \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ yields
$$d_P\bigl(\mathcal{N}(0,1) * \mu,\; \mathcal{N}(0,1) * \nu\bigr) \le d_P(\mu, \
nu) \qquad \text{for all probability measures } \mu, \nu \text{ on } \mathbb{R}.
 \qquad \blacksquare$$

---

**Remark.** The Gaussian structure of $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ plays no role; the argu
ment uses only that $\gamma$ is a probability measure. The essential point is th
at adding a *common* independent noise $Z$ to both sides of a coupling preserves
 the distance $|X - Y|$, and the layer-cake argument transfers this to the setwi
se Prokhorov condition.
