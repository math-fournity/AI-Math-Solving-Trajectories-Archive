Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest $n$ such that every monomia
l $x^i y^j z^k$ with $i+j+k \geq n$ lies in the ideal $\mathcal{B} = (e_1, e_2,
e_3) \subset \mathbb{Z}[x,y,z]$, where $e_1 = x+y+z$, $e_2 = xy+yz+zx$, $e_3 = x
yz$.

**Answer: $n = 4$.**

---

## Proof that $n \geq 4$: $x^2 y \notin \mathcal{B}$

Suppose $x^2 y = e_1 P + e_2 Q + e_3 R$ with $P, Q, R \in \mathbb{Z}[x,y,z]$. By
 degree considerations (homogeneous degree 3), we need $\deg P = 2$, $\deg Q = 1
$, $\deg R = 0$. Write:
$$P = ax^2+bxy+cxz+dy^2+eyz+fz^2,\quad Q=gx+hy+kz,\quad R=m.$$

Expanding $e_1 P + e_2 Q + e_3 R$ and matching coefficients with $x^2 y$:

| Monomial | Equation |
|----------|----------|
| $x^3$ | $a = 0$ |
| $y^3$ | $d = 0$ |
| $z^3$ | $f = 0$ |
| $x^2y$ | $b + a + g = 1$, so $b+g=1$ |
| $x^2z$ | $c + a + g = 0$, so $c+g=0$ |
| $xy^2$ | $d + b + h = 0$, so $b+h=0$ |
| $y^2z$ | $e + d + h = 0$, so $e+h=0$ |
| $xz^2$ | $f + c + k = 0$, so $c+k=0$ |
| $yz^2$ | $f + e + k = 0$, so $e+k=0$ |

From $c+g=0$: $c=-g$. From $c+k=0$: $k=g$. From $b+h=0$: $h=-b$. From $e+h=0$: $
e=b$. From $e+k=0$: $b+g=0$.

But $b+g=1$ (from $x^2y$) and $b+g=0$ (from $yz^2$). **Contradiction.** So $x^2y
 \notin \mathcal{B}$, hence $n \geq 4$.

## Proof that $n \leq 4$: Every monomial of degree $\geq 4$ lies in $\mathcal{B}
$

**Step 1: Key elements in $\mathcal{B}$.**

Since $e_1, e_2, e_3 \in \mathcal{B}$ and $\mathcal{B}$ is an ideal:
- $e_1 \cdot x - e_2 = x^2 - yz \in \mathcal{B}$
- $e_1 \cdot y - e_2 = y^2 - xz \in \mathcal{B}$
- $e_1 \cdot z - e_2 = z^2 - xy \in \mathcal{B}$

**Step 2: $x^3, y^3, z^3 \in \mathcal{B}$.**

$$x^3 = x(x^2 - yz) + xyz = x(e_1 x - e_2) + e_3 \in \mathcal{B}.$$

By symmetry, $y^3, z^3 \in \mathcal{B}$.

**Step 3: All degree-4 monomials in $\mathcal{B}$.**

The 15 monomials of degree 4 fall into groups:

*Group A* (products of degree-3 elements with variables — 12 monomials): $x^4, y
^4, z^4, x^3y, x^3z, xy^3, y^3z, xz^3, yz^3, x^2yz, xy^2z, xyz^2$ — each is a va
riable times an element of $\mathcal{B}$ from Step 2 (or $xyz \in \mathcal{B}$),
 hence in $\mathcal{B}$.

*Group B* (the three remaining monomials):

$$x^2y^2 = y^2(x^2 - yz) + y^3 z \in \mathcal{B},$$
$$x^2z^2 = z^2(x^2 - yz) + yz^3 \in \mathcal{B},$$
$$y^2z^2 = z^2(y^2 - xz) + xz^3 \in \mathcal{B}.$$

Each uses $x^2-yz$ or $y^2-xz$ from Step 1 (in $\mathcal{B}$) plus a Group A mon
omial (in $\mathcal{B}$). All coefficients are integers.

**Step 4: Degree $\geq 5$.**

Any monomial of degree $d \geq 5$ factors as (degree-4 monomial)$\times$(degree
$d{-}4$ monomial). Since the degree-4 factor is in $\mathcal{B}$ and $\mathcal{B
}$ is an ideal, the product is in $\mathcal{B}$.

---

## Conclusion

- $x^2y \notin \mathcal{B}$ (degree 3), so $n \geq 4$.
- Every monomial of degree $\geq 4$ is in $\mathcal{B}$, so $n \leq 4$.

$$\boxed{n = 4}$$
