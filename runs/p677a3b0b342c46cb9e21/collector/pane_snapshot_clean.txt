Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every finite cyclic group $
Q$ can be realized as the outer automorphism group of some finite group $G_Q$.

**Answer: Yes, it is always possible.**

## Proof

Let $Q \cong \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$ for $m \geq 1$. We construct $G_Q$ explicit
ly.

### Construction

By **Dirichlet's theorem** on primes in arithmetic progressions, there exists a
prime $p$ with $p \equiv 1 \pmod{m}$, i.e., $m \mid (p-1)$. Set $n = (p-1)/m$.

Since $(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^*$ is cyclic of order $p-1$ and $n \mid (p-1)$,
there exists $r \in (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^*$ of order $n$.

Define the semidirect product:
$$G_Q = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} \rtimes_r \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} = \langle a, b \
mid a^p = 1,\; b^n = 1,\; bab^{-1} = a^r \rangle.$$

Note $n < p$ (since $n = (p-1)/m < p$), so $\langle a \rangle$ is the unique Syl
ow $p$-subgroup, hence characteristic in $G_Q$.

### Computing $\operatorname{Aut}(G_Q)$

An automorphism $\phi$ must send $a \mapsto a^i$ for $i \in (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb
{Z})^*$ (since $\langle a \rangle$ is characteristic). Write $\phi(b) = a^j b^k$
 with $j \in \mathbb{F}_p$, $k \in (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*$ (coprimality need
ed for surjectivity).

Preserving the relation $bab^{-1} = a^r$:
$$\phi(b)\phi(a)\phi(b)^{-1} = \phi(a)^r \implies a^{ir^k} = a^{ir} \implies r^k
 \equiv r \pmod{p}.$$

Since $r$ has order $n$: $n \mid (k-1)$, so $k \equiv 1 \pmod{n}$, forcing $k =
1$.

One checks $(a^j b)^n = 1$ for all $j$ (the exponent sums to $j \cdot \frac{r^n
- 1}{r - 1} = 0$ when $n > 1$; trivial when $n=1$). So:
$$\operatorname{Aut}(G_Q) = \{\phi_{i,j} : i \in (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^*,\; j
 \in \mathbb{F}_p\}, \quad |\operatorname{Aut}(G_Q)| = p(p-1),$$
where $\phi_{i,j}(a) = a^i,\; \phi_{i,j}(b) = a^j b$. The composition law is $\p
hi_{i,j} \circ \phi_{i',j'} = \phi_{ii',\, ij'+j}$.

### Computing $\operatorname{Inn}(G_Q)$

For $n > 1$, $r \neq 1$, so $Z(G_Q) = 1$ (no non-identity element commutes with
both $a$ and $b$). Thus $|\operatorname{Inn}(G_Q)| = |G_Q| = pn$.

Conjugation by $a^s$: $a \mapsto a$, $b \mapsto a^{s(1-r)}b$, giving $\phi_{1,\,
 s(1-r)}$.
Conjugation by $b$: $a \mapsto a^r$, $b \mapsto b$, giving $\phi_{r, 0}$.

The $i$-values in $\operatorname{Inn}(G_Q)$ are $\langle r \rangle = \{1, r, \ld
ots, r^{n-1}\}$, and for each such $i$, the $j$-values cover all of $\mathbb{F}_
p$ (since $1 - r \neq 0$). Therefore:
$$\operatorname{Inn}(G_Q) = \{\phi_{i,j} : i \in \langle r \rangle,\; j \in \mat
hbb{F}_p\}, \quad |\operatorname{Inn}(G_Q)| = pn.$$

### Computing $\operatorname{Out}(G_Q)$

The projection $\pi: \operatorname{Aut}(G_Q) \to (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^*$, $\
phi_{i,j} \mapsto i$, is a homomorphism (by the composition law) with $\ker \pi
= \{\phi_{1,j}\} \subseteq \operatorname{Inn}(G_Q)$ and $\pi(\operatorname{Inn}(
G_Q)) = \langle r \rangle$. Hence:
$$\operatorname{Out}(G_Q) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^* / \langle r \rangle.$
$

Since $(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/(p-1)\mathbb{Z}$ is cyclic an
d $\langle r \rangle$ is its unique subgroup of order $n$:
$$\operatorname{Out}(G_Q) \cong \mathbb{Z}/(p-1)\mathbb{Z}\,\big/\,\mathbb{Z}/n\
mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/((p-1)/n)\mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \cong Q.
$$

### Edge cases

- **$n = 1$** (i.e., $p = m+1$ is prime): $G_Q = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ is abel
ian, so $\operatorname{Inn}(G_Q) = 1$ and $\operatorname{Out}(G_Q) = (\mathbb{Z}
/p\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/(p-1)\mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \cong
Q$. ✓

- **$m = 1$**: Take any prime $p$, $n = p-1$. Then $G_Q = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
 \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/(p-1)\mathbb{Z}$ (the holomorph), and $|\operatorname{Aut}(G
_Q)| = p(p-1) = |G_Q| = |\operatorname{Inn}(G_Q)|$, giving $\operatorname{Out}(G
_Q) = 1 \cong Q$. ✓

### Conclusion

For every finite cyclic group $Q \cong \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$, the group $G_Q =
 \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} \rtimes_r \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ — with $p \equiv 1 \pm
od{m}$ prime (guaranteed by Dirichlet's theorem), $n = (p-1)/m$, and $r$ of orde
r $n$ in $(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^*$ — satisfies $\operatorname{Out}(G_Q) \cong
 Q$. $\blacksquare$
