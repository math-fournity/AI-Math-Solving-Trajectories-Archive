**Answer: Yes.** We exhibit the **pseudo-arc** as a witness.

---

**The pseudo-arc.** Moise (1948) and Bing (1951) constructed the pseudo-arc $P$:
 a non-degenerate continuum (compact, connected, metrizable space) that is **her
editarily indecomposable** — every subcontinuum of $P$ is indecomposable (i.e.,
cannot be expressed as the union of two proper subcontinua). We verify the three
 required properties.

**(1) $P$ is connected.** Immediate, since $P$ is a continuum.

**(2) $|P| = 2^{\aleph_0}$.** Since $P$ is a compact metric space, $|P| \le 2^{\
aleph_0}$. For the reverse inequality: $P$ is non-degenerate, so choose distinct
 $a, b \in P$. The space $P$ is metric, hence normal; by Urysohn's Lemma there i
s a continuous $g : P \to [0,1]$ with $g(a)=0,\; g(b)=1$, so $g$ surjects onto $
[0,1]$. Thus $|P| \ge |[0,1]| = 2^{\aleph_0}$. Hence $|P| = 2^{\aleph_0}$.

**(3) Every continuous $f:[0,1]\to P$ is constant.** Let $f:[0,1]\to P$ be conti
nuous and set $K := f([0,1])$.

- **$K$ is a subcontinuum of $P$.** The interval $[0,1]$ is compact and connecte
d; $P$ is Hausdorff; so $K$ is compact (hence closed in $P$) and connected.
- **$K$ is indecomposable.** Since $P$ is hereditarily indecomposable, every sub
continuum — including $K$ — is indecomposable.
- **$K$ is locally connected.** The interval $[0,1]$ is a compact, connected, lo
cally connected metric space. A continuous surjection from a compact space onto
a Hausdorff space is a quotient map, and a quotient image of a locally connected
 compactum is locally connected (Hahn–Mazurkiewicz). Hence $K$ is a locally conn
ected continuum (a Peano continuum).

We now use a standard characterization from continuum theory:

> **Theorem.** A continuum is indecomposable if and only if every proper subcont
inuum has empty interior.

Suppose for contradiction that $K$ is non-degenerate. Pick distinct $p, q \in K$
. Since $K$ is a locally connected metric (hence regular) continuum, $p$ admits
a connected open neighborhood $U$ with $\overline{U} \subseteq K \setminus \{q\}
$; then $\overline{U}$ is a **proper** subcontinuum of $K$ (it misses $q$) with
**nonempty interior** (it contains $U$). By the theorem above, $K$ is **not** in
decomposable — contradicting the indecomposability established in the second bul
let.

Therefore $K$ is degenerate, i.e., $K = \{\text{point}\}$, and $f$ is constant.

---

**Conclusion.** The pseudo-arc $P$ is a connected topological space with $|P| =
2^{\aleph_0}$ such that every continuous $f:[0,1] \to P$ is constant. Hence such
 a space exists. $\blacksquare$
