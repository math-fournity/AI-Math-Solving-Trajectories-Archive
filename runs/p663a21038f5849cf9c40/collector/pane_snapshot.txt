## Proof

Let $A = (a_{pq})$ be a real $n \times n$ matrix, and let $E_{ij}$ denote the ma
trix with $1$ in position $(i,j)$ and $0$ elsewhere. For every pair $i \neq j$,
the matrix
$$B_{ij} := E_{ij} - E_{ji}$$
is real skew-symmetric, so by hypothesis $AB_{ij} = B_{ij}A$.

**Step 1: $A$ is diagonal (for $n \ge 3$).**

Compute the $(k,l)$-entry of both sides. Since $(B_{ij})_{ml} = \delta_{mi}\delt
a_{jl} - \delta_{mj}\delta_{il}$,

$$(AB_{ij})_{kl} = \sum_m a_{km}(B_{ij})_{ml} = a_{ki}\delta_{jl} - a_{kj}\delta
_{il},$$

$$(B_{ij}A)_{kl} = \sum_m (B_{ij})_{km}a_{ml} = \delta_{ki}a_{jl} - \delta_{kj}a
_{il}.$$

Equating and setting $l = j$ with $k \notin \{i,j\}$ (so that $\delta_{jl}=1$, $
\delta_{il}=0$, $\delta_{ki}=0$, $\delta_{kj}=0$) gives
$$a_{ki} = 0.$$

Now fix any $i$ and any $k \neq i$. When $n \ge 3$ we may choose an index $j$ di
stinct from both $i$ and $k$, and the above yields $a_{ki} = 0$. Hence every off
-diagonal entry of $A$ is zero, so $A = \operatorname{diag}(d_1, \dots, d_n)$.

**Step 2: All diagonal entries are equal.**

With $A$ diagonal, $a_{km} = d_k\delta_{km}$, the commutation relation $AB_{ij}
= B_{ij}A$ becomes
$$d_k\bigl(\delta_{ki}\delta_{jl} - \delta_{kj}\delta_{il}\bigr) = d_l\bigl(\del
ta_{ki}\delta_{jl} - \delta_{kj}\delta_{il}\bigr).$$

Set $k = i$ and $l = j$ (with $i \neq j$): the left side is $d_i\cdot 1$ and the
 right side is $d_j \cdot 1$, so
$$d_i = d_j \qquad \text{for all } i \neq j.$$

Thus $d_1 = d_2 = \cdots = d_n =: k$, and therefore
$$A = k\,I_n, \qquad k \in \mathbb{R}.$$

**Edge cases.** For $n = 1$ the result is trivial. For $n = 2$ the space of skew
-symmetric matrices is one-dimensional (spanned by $J = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0
\end{pmatrix}$), and every matrix of the form $\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\-b&a\end{pmat
rix}$ commutes with $J$; hence the statement as written requires $n \ge 3$ (or $
n=1$), which is the standard setting of the theorem. Under that assumption the p
roof above is complete. $\blacksquare$
