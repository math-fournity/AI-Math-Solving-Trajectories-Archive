**Theorem.** For positive terms $a_n$, the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\inf
ty}\frac{a_n}{(1+a_1)(1+a_2)\cdots(1+a_n)}=1$ if and only if $\displaystyle\sum_
{n=1}^{\infty}a_n$ diverges.

**Proof.**

Set $P_0=1$ and $P_n=(1+a_1)(1+a_2)\cdots(1+a_n)$ for $n\ge 1$.

**Step 1 (Telescoping).** For each $n\ge 1$,
$$
\frac{1}{P_{n-1}}-\frac{1}{P_n}
=\frac{P_n-P_{n-1}}{P_{n-1}P_n}
=\frac{P_{n-1}(1+a_n)-P_{n-1}}{P_{n-1}P_n}
=\frac{a_n}{P_n}.
$$
Hence the $N$-th partial sum telescopes:
$$
S_N:=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{a_n}{P_n}
=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Bigl(\frac{1}{P_{n-1}}-\frac{1}{P_n}\Bigr)
=1-\frac{1}{P_N}.
\tag{1}
$$
Therefore
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_n}{P_n}=1
\iff \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{P_N}=0
\iff \lim_{N\to\infty}P_N=+\infty.
\tag{2}
$$

**Step 2 (Reduction to a product–sum equivalence).** Since every $a_n>0$, every
$P_n>1$ and $P_n$ is increasing. Taking logarithms,
$$
\log P_N=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\log(1+a_n).
\tag{3}
$$
Thus $P_N\to+\infty$ iff $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\log(1+a_n)=+\infty$. Combining wit
h (2), it suffices to prove
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\log(1+a_n)=+\infty
\iff
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n=+\infty.
\tag{4}

\log(1+x)\ge \frac{x}{2}.
