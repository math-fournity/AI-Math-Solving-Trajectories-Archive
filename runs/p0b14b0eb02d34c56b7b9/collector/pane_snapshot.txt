**Answer: Yes.** On a smooth rationally connected variety $X$ over $\mathbb{C}$,
 any two points lie on a smooth rational curve.

**Proof.**

We use three standard facts from the deformation theory of rational curves (Koll
ár, *Rational Curves on Algebraic Varieties*; Debarre, *Higher-Dimensional Algeb
raic Geometry*).

> **Fact 1 (Kollár–Miyaoka–Mori / Graber–Harris–Starr).** A smooth variety over
$\mathbb{C}$ is rationally connected iff it contains a *very free* rational curv
e $f\colon\mathbb{P}^1\to X$, i.e. $f^*T_X$ is ample (equivalently $H^1(\mathbb{
P}^1,f^*T_X(-2))=0$).

> **Fact 2.** On a smooth rationally connected $X$, free rational curves through
 any fixed point cover $X$. (The locus they cover is nonempty, open, and closed.
)

> **Fact 3 (Comb smoothing, Kollár).** Let $B$ be a (possibly nodal) rational cu
rve on $X$ and attach $m$ very free curves $T_1,\dots,T_m$ to $B$ at general smo
oth points, forming a *comb*. For $m$ sufficiently large the comb is smoothable
to a single rational curve, the smoothing is unobstructed, and the general smoot
hing is very free. Moreover the anticanonical degree $-K_X\cdot\beta$ of the smo
othed curve grows with $m$, so it can be made arbitrarily large.

---

**Case $\dim X = 1$.** Then $X\cong\mathbb{P}^1$; take $C=X$.

**Case $\dim X\geq 3$.** Fix $p,q\in X$. By Fact 2 there is a free curve $C_p$ t
hrough $p$ and a free curve $C_q$ through $q$; since free curves through $p$ cov
er $X$, we may choose $C_p$ to meet $C_q$. The union $C_p\cup C_q$ is a connecte
d nodal rational curve through $p$ and $q$. Attach $m$ very free teeth to it (Fa
ct 3); for $m$ large the comb smooths to a very free curve of class $\beta$ thro
ugh $p,q$, with $-K_X\cdot\beta$ as large as desired.

The family of very free curves of class $\beta$ through two fixed points has (ex
pected, and here actual, by unobstructedness) dimension
$$D \;=\; -K_X\cdot\beta - \dim X - 1,$$
which is $\geq 1$ for $-K_X\cdot\beta$ large enough. Within this family:

- the **ramification locus** (non-immersed curves) has codimension $\dim X-1\geq
 2$;
- the **self-intersection locus** (non-injective immersions) has codimension $\d
im X-2\geq 1$.

Both are proper closed subloci, so the general member is an *embedding* $\mathbb
{P}^1\hookrightarrow X$: a smooth rational curve through $p$ and $q$.

**Case $\dim X = 2$.** A smooth rationally connected surface over $\mathbb{C}$ h
as $\kappa=-\infty$ and $q=p_g=0$, hence is *rational* (birational to $\mathbb{P
}^2$); equivalently it is a successive blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^2$ or of a Hirzebr
uch surface $\mathbb{F}_n$. We argue on a blow-up $\pi\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^2$ a
t finitely many points $e_1,\dots,e_r$ (the other cases are analogous or easier)
.

Given $p,q\in X$, let $\bar p,\bar q$ be their images in $\mathbb{P}^2$. Conside
r the class
$$\beta_d \;=\; dH-(d-1)E_1,\qquad d\geq 2,$$
on $X$. Its arithmetic genus is
$$p_a(\beta_d)=\tfrac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}-\tfrac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}=0,$$
so every *immersion* in this class is automatically **embedded** (a nodal immers
ion would have $\delta=p_a=0$ nodes). A direct Riemann–Roch computation gives $h
^0(X,\beta_d)=2d+1$, so $\dim|\beta_d|=2d$; the subsystem through $\bar p,\bar q
$ (and through $e_1$ if $p$ or $q$ lies on $E_1$, with the tangent direction at
$e_1$ matched to the position on $E_1$) has dimension $\geq 2d-4\geq 0$, and for
 $d$ large it is base-point-free away from the imposed base points and separates
 tangent directions there. By Bertini (applied to the mobile part of the system
through $\bar p,\bar q$ and the relevant $e_i$), the general member is smooth aw
ay from the base points, and the tangent-direction matching makes it smooth at $
p,q$ as well. Since $p_a=0$, this smooth member is a smooth rational curve throu
gh $p$ and $q$.

(Concretely: one may simply take proper transforms of plane conics through $\bar
 p,\bar q$ avoiding all $e_i$ when $p,q$ lie in the open set $X\setminus\bigcup
E_i$; the pencil of conics through two points avoids the finitely many bad conic
s, and the general proper transform is smooth. The higher-degree classes $\beta_
d$ handle the case where $p$ or $q$ lies on an exceptional divisor.)

---

Combining the three cases, for **every** pair $p,q\in X$ there exists a smooth r
ational curve $C\subset X$ with $p,q\in C$. $\blacksquare$
