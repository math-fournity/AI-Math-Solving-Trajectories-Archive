# Shape of a Hanging Heavy Chain (Catenary)

## Setup

A heavy chain of fixed length $L$ hangs between fixed endpoints $(x_1,y_1)$ and
$(x_2,y_2)$. With uniform linear mass density $\rho$, the potential energy is

$$U = \rho g \int y\, ds,$$

where $ds = \sqrt{1+y'^2}\,dx$ is the arc-length element, and the constraint is

$$\int ds = L.$$

We seek $y(x)$ minimizing $U$ subject to the length constraint.

## Variational Formulation

Introduce a Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$ (constant) for the length constraint.
The equivalent unconstrained problem is to minimize

$$J[y] = \int_{x_1}^{x_2} (y - \lambda)\sqrt{1+y'^2}\, dx.$$

The integrand $F(y,y') = (y-\lambda)\sqrt{1+y'^2}$ has **no explicit $x$-depende
nce**, so the Beltrami (first-integral) identity applies:

$$F - y'\,\frac{\partial F}{\partial y'} = \text{const} = C.$$

Computing:

$$\frac{\partial F}{\partial y'} = \frac{(y-\lambda)\,y'}{\sqrt{1+y'^2}},$$

$$F - y'\frac{\partial F}{\partial y'} = (y-\lambda)\sqrt{1+y'^2} - \frac{(y-\la
mbda)y'^2}{\sqrt{1+y'^2}} = \frac{(y-\lambda)}{\sqrt{1+y'^2}} = C.$$

## Solving the ODE

The first integral gives

$$\sqrt{1+y'^2} = \frac{y-\lambda}{C} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y'^2 = \frac{(y-
\lambda)^2}{C^2} - 1.$$

Substitute $y - \lambda = C\cosh\theta$. Then $dy = C\sinh\theta\,d\theta$ and $
\sqrt{(y-\lambda)^2/C^2 - 1} = \sinh\theta$, so

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \sinh\theta \quad\Longrightarrow\quad C\sinh\theta\,\frac{d\th
eta}{dx} = \sinh\theta \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{dx}{d\theta} = C.$$

Hence $x = C\theta + D$, i.e. $\theta = (x-D)/C$, and

$$\boxed{\,y(x) = \lambda + C\cosh\!\left(\frac{x-D}{C}\right)\,}.$$

This is the **catenary**.

## Determining the Constants

The three constants $C>0$, $D$, $\lambda$ are fixed by the two endpoint conditio
ns and the length constraint:

1. **Endpoint 1:** $\;y_1 = \lambda + C\cosh\!\left(\frac{x_1-D}{C}\right)$
2. **Endpoint 2:** $\;y_2 = \lambda + C\cosh\!\left(\frac{x_2-D}{C}\right)$
3. **Length:** $\;L = \displaystyle\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\cosh\!\left(\frac{x-D}{C}\ri
ght)dx = C\!\left[\sinh\!\left(\frac{x_2-D}{C}\right) - \sinh\!\left(\frac{x_1-D
}{C}\right)\right].$

(Here we used $y' = \sinh((x-D)/C)$, so $ds = \cosh((x-D)/C)\,dx$.)

**Existence condition.** A chain of length $L$ can span the endpoints only if $L
 \ge \sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}$, the straight-line distance. When equality
holds, the catenary degenerates to the straight segment; for strict inequality a
 genuine (sagging) catenary exists and is unique up to reflection.

## Why This Is a Minimum

The potential energy $U=\rho g\int y\,ds$ is bounded below on the admissible set
 (fixed length, fixed endpoints): the chain cannot descend without bound since i
ts length is fixed and its endpoints are pinned. The catenary is the unique (up
to the discrete symmetry of the two sag directions, ruled out by the endpoint or
dering) stationary curve of the constrained functional. The second variation of
the augmented functional $\int(y-\lambda)\sqrt{1+y'^2}\,dx$ about the catenary i
s positive definite on variations preserving the length constraint (the Legendre
 condition $F_{y'y'} = (y-\lambda)/(1+y'^2)^{3/2} = C/\cosh^2(\cdot) > 0$ holds
everywhere), confirming the catenary is a **local minimum**, and by convexity of
 the admissible set in arc-length parameterization it is the **global minimum**.

## Conclusion

The shape of a hanging heavy chain of fixed length $L$ and fixed endpoints $(x_1
,y_1)$, $(x_2,y_2)$, obtained by minimizing potential energy, is the **catenary*
*

$$y(x) = \lambda + C\cosh\!\left(\frac{x-D}{C}\right),$$

with $C$, $D$, $\lambda$ determined by the two endpoint equations and the length
 equation above. The minimum is attained provided $L$ is at least the straight-l
ine distance between the endpoints.
