## Proof

**Setup.** Place the incenter $I$ at the origin, with the incircle of radius $r$
. Each side of $\triangle ABC$ is a tangent line to the incircle. Parameterize t
he tangent points by angles: the tangent to the circle $x^2+y^2=r^2$ at angle $\
theta$ (point $(r\cos\theta, r\sin\theta)$) is the line $x\cos\theta + y\sin\the
ta = r$.

The intersection of tangent lines at angles $\theta_1, \theta_2$ is:
$$\frac{r}{\cos\frac{\theta_2-\theta_1}{2}}\left(\cos\frac{\theta_1+\theta_2}{2}
,\;\sin\frac{\theta_1+\theta_2}{2}\right).$$

The interior angle at that vertex (the angle on the side containing the circle)
equals $\pi - |\theta_2-\theta_1|$ (taken in $[0,\pi]$).

**Choosing angles.** Let $\alpha = A/2,\;\beta = B/2,\;\gamma = C/2$, so $\alpha
+\beta+\gamma = \pi/2$. Set the tangent point of $BC$ at $\theta_K = 0$, the tan
gent point of $AB$ at $\theta_M = \pi - B = \pi - 2\beta$, and the tangent point
 of $AC$ at $\theta_L = C - \pi = 2\gamma - \pi$. One verifies $|\theta_M - \the
ta_K| = \pi - 2\beta$ gives interior angle $2\beta = B$; similarly for the other
s, and $|\theta_L - \theta_M|$ yields interior angle $A$.

**Coordinates.** Then:
- $K = (r, 0)$.
- $B = \text{intersect}(\theta_M, \theta_K) = \frac{r}{\cos\frac{\pi-2\beta}{2}}
\left(\cos\frac{\pi-2\beta}{2},\sin\frac{\pi-2\beta}{2}\right) = (r,\; r\cot\bet
a)$.
- $C = \text{intersect}(\theta_L, \theta_K) = \frac{r}{\cos\frac{2\gamma-\pi}{2}
}\left(\cos\frac{2\gamma-\pi}{2},\sin\frac{2\gamma-\pi}{2}\right) = (r,\; -r\cot
\gamma)$.
- $A = \text{intersect}(\theta_L, \theta_M)$: direction $\frac{\theta_L+\theta_M
}{2} = \gamma - \beta$, half-difference $\frac{\theta_M-\theta_L}{2} = \frac{\pi
+\alpha}{2}$ (since $2\pi - 2\beta - 2\gamma = \pi + 2\alpha$... checking: $\the
ta_M - \theta_L = (\pi-2\beta)-(2\gamma-\pi) = 2\pi - 2\beta - 2\gamma = 2\pi -
2(\beta+\gamma) = 2\pi - (\pi - 2\alpha) = \pi + 2\alpha$). Then $\cos\frac{\pi+
2\alpha}{2} = -\sin\alpha$, so the distance is $\frac{r}{-\sin\alpha}$, giving:
$$A = \frac{-r}{\sin\alpha}\bigl(\cos(\gamma-\beta),\;\sin(\gamma-\beta)\bigr) =
 \frac{-r}{\cos(\beta+\gamma)}\bigl(\cos(\gamma-\beta),\;\sin(\gamma-\beta)\bigr
),$$
using $\sin\alpha = \cos(\beta+\gamma)$.

**Orthocenter formula.** For a triangle with vertices $O=(0,0)$, $P$, $Q$, the o
rthocenter $H$ satisfies $H\cdot Q = P\cdot Q$ and $H\cdot P = P\cdot Q$ (the al
titude from $P$ is $\{X : (X-P)\cdot Q = 0\}$, similarly from $Q$).

**Computing $H_B$ (orthocenter of $\triangle ACI$, with $I$ at origin).** Here $
P = A$, $Q = C$. We need $H_B\cdot C = A\cdot C$ and $H_B \cdot A = A\cdot C$.

First compute dot products:
$$A\cdot C = \frac{-r^2}{\cos(\beta+\gamma)}\Bigl[\cos(\gamma-\beta) - \sin(\gam
ma-\beta)\cot\gamma\Bigr] = \frac{-r^2\sin\beta}{\cos(\beta+\gamma)\sin\gamma},$
$
since $\cos(\gamma-\beta)\sin\gamma - \sin(\gamma-\beta)\cos\gamma = \sin\beta$.

Setting $H_B = (x_B, y_B)$, the system $H_B\cdot C = A\cdot C$ and $H_B \cdot A
= A\cdot C$ yields (after substituting and solving, using $\cos(\beta+\gamma)+\c
os(\gamma-\beta) = 2\cos\beta\cos\gamma$):

$$\boxed{H_B = \left(\frac{r\sin\beta\cos 2\gamma}{\cos(\beta+\gamma)\sin\gamma}
,\;\;\frac{2r\sin\beta\cos\gamma}{\cos(\beta+\gamma)}\right).}$$

**Computing $H_C$ (orthocenter of $\triangle ABI$).** Here $P = A$, $Q = B$. Sim
ilarly $A\cdot B = \frac{-r^2\sin\gamma}{\cos(\beta+\gamma)\sin\beta}$, and solv
ing gives:

$$\boxed{H_C = \left(\frac{r\sin\gamma\cos 2\beta}{\cos(\beta+\gamma)\sin\beta},
\;\;\frac{-2r\sin\gamma\cos\beta}{\cos(\beta+\gamma)}\right).}$$

**Collinearity check.** Points $H_B, H_C, K=(r,0)$ are collinear iff $(x_B - r)\
,y_C - (x_C - r)\,y_B = 0$, i.e., $x_B y_C - x_C y_B = r(y_C - y_B)$.

*Left side:*
$$x_B y_C - x_C y_B = \frac{-2r^2}{\cos^2(\beta+\gamma)}\Bigl[\sin\beta\cos\beta
\cos 2\gamma + \sin\gamma\cos\gamma\cos 2\beta\Bigr]$$
$$= \frac{-r^2}{\cos^2(\beta+\gamma)}\Bigl[\sin 2\beta\cos 2\gamma + \sin 2\gamm
a\cos 2\beta\Bigr] = \frac{-r^2\sin 2(\beta+\gamma)}{\cos^2(\beta+\gamma)} = -2r
^2\tan(\beta+\gamma).$$

*Right side:*
$$r(y_C - y_B) = r\cdot\frac{-2r(\sin\gamma\cos\beta + \sin\beta\cos\gamma)}{\co
s(\beta+\gamma)} = \frac{-2r^2\sin(\beta+\gamma)}{\cos(\beta+\gamma)} = -2r^2\ta
n(\beta+\gamma).$$

Both sides are equal, so $H_B$, $H_C$, and $K$ are collinear. $\blacksquare$
