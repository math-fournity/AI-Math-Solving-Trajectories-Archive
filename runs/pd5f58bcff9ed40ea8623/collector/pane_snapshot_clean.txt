**Setup.** We have the Bachelier model $S_t = 1 + \mu t + \sigma W_t$ under the
physical measure $\mathbb{P}$, with $S_0 = 1$, and a digital option paying $1\!\
!(S_1 > 1)$ at time $T = 1$. Let $r$ denote the risk-free rate (the problem does
 not specify it; the standard convention when omitted is $r = 0$, but we keep $r
$ explicit first).

**Risk-neutral measure.** In the Bachelier model the discounted price $e^{-rt}S_
t$ must be a martingale under the risk-neutral measure $\mathbb{Q}$. Since $dS_t
 = \mu\,dt + \sigma\,dW_t$, Girsanov's theorem gives a measure $\mathbb{Q}$ unde
r which $\widetilde W_t = W_t + \tfrac{\mu - r}{\sigma}t$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-Brow
nian motion. Hence

$$dS_t = r\,dt + \sigma\,d\widetilde W_t, \qquad S_t = 1 + rt + \sigma \widetild
e W_t.$$

Crucially, **the physical drift $\mu$ has disappeared** — the option value will
not depend on $\mu$.

**Distribution of $S_1$ under $\mathbb{Q}$.** Since $\widetilde W_1 \sim \mathca
l{N}(0,1)$ under $\mathbb{Q}$,

$$S_1 = 1 + r + \sigma \widetilde W_1 \sim \mathcal{N}(1 + r,\; \sigma^2).$$

**Risk-neutral valuation.** The option value is the discounted $\mathbb{Q}$-expe
ctation of the payoff:

$$V_0 = e^{-r}\,\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\!\big[1(S_1 > 1)\big] = e^{-r}\,\mathbb{
Q}(S_1 > 1).$$

Now

$$\mathbb{Q}(S_1 > 1) = \mathbb{Q}\!\left(\widetilde W_1 > -\frac{r}{\sigma}\rig
ht) = 1 - \Phi\!\left(-\frac{r}{\sigma}\right) = \Phi\!\left(\frac{r}{\sigma}\ri
ght),$$

where $\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF. Therefore

$$\boxed{V_0 = e^{-r}\,\Phi\!\left(\frac{r}{\sigma}\right).}$$

**Specialization $r = 0$.** When the risk-free rate is zero (the natural reading
 of a problem that introduces no rate),

$$V_0 = e^{0}\,\Phi(0) = 1 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} = \boxed{\tfrac{1}{2}}.$$

**Verification of the $r=0$ case by symmetry.** With $r=0$, under $\mathbb{Q}$ w
e have $S_1 = 1 + \sigma\widetilde W_1$, which is symmetric about $1$. Thus $\ma
thbb{Q}(S_1 > 1) = \mathbb{Q}(\widetilde W_1 > 0) = \tfrac12$ by symmetry of the
 standard normal, confirming $V_0 = \tfrac12$.

**Key point.** The risk-neutral valuation argument shows the option value is **i
ndependent of the physical drift $\mu$**; only $r$ and $\sigma$ matter. With $r
= 0$ the answer is exactly $\tfrac12$, regardless of $\sigma$.
