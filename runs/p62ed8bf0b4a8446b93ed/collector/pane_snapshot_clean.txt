**Setting up the polynomial.** The roots of $P(x)$ are $\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi k
}{2023}\right)$ for $k=1,\dots,2022$. Since $2023$ is odd, $\cos\!\left(\frac{2\
pi k}{2023}\right)=\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi(2023-k)}{2023}\right)$, so the $2022$
roots pair up into $1011$ distinct values, each with multiplicity $2$:

$$P(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{1011}\left(x-\cos\tfrac{2\pi k}{2023}\right)^{2}.$$

**Evaluating at $x=1$.** Using $1-\cos\theta = 2\sin^{2}(\theta/2)$:

$$P(1)=\prod_{k=1}^{1011}\left(1-\cos\tfrac{2\pi k}{2023}\right)^{2}=\prod_{k=1}
^{1011}\left(2\sin^{2}\tfrac{\pi k}{2023}\right)^{2}=4^{1011}\prod_{k=1}^{1011}\
sin^{4}\!\left(\tfrac{\pi k}{2023}\right).$$

**Applying the sine-product identity.** The classical identity gives

$$\prod_{k=1}^{2022}\sin\!\left(\tfrac{\pi k}{2023}\right)=\frac{2023}{2^{2022}}
.$$

By the symmetry $\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi k}{2023}\right)=\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi(202
3-k)}{2023}\right)$, the left side equals $\left(\prod_{k=1}^{1011}\sin\!\left(\
frac{\pi k}{2023}\right)\right)^{2}$. Hence

$$\prod_{k=1}^{1011}\sin^{4}\!\left(\tfrac{\pi k}{2023}\right)=\left(\frac{2023}
{2^{2022}}\right)^{2}=\frac{2023^{2}}{2^{4044}}.$$

**Combining.**

$$P(1)=4^{1011}\cdot\frac{2023^{2}}{2^{4044}}=2^{2022}\cdot\frac{2023^{2}}{2^{40
44}}=\frac{2023^{2}}{2^{2022}}.$$

**Reducing to lowest terms.** Since $2023=7\cdot 17^{2}$ is odd, $\gcd(2023^{2},
\,2^{2022})=1$. Thus

$$m=2023^{2}=4{,}092{,}529,\qquad n=2^{2022}.$$

**Computing $(m+n)\bmod 100$.**

- For $n$: the sequence $2^{k}\bmod 100$ has period $20$ for $k\ge 2$ (since $2^
{20}\equiv 76\pmod{100}$ and $2^{20}\cdot 2^{20}\equiv 76^{2}=5776\equiv 76$, co

2}=4\pmod{100}$.

Therefore

$$(m+n)\bmod 100 = (29+4)\bmod 100 = \boxed{33}.$$
