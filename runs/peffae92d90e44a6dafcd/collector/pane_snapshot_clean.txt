Looking at this problem, I need to find the intersection volume of two hexagonal
 pyramids inside a regular hexagonal prism.

## Setup

Let the floor hexagon have side length $a$ and circumradius $a$, centered at $O=
(0,0,0)$, with the ceiling at height $h$ centered at $O_1=(0,0,h)$. The prism vo
lume is $V = \tfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}a^2 h$.

Place floor vertices at angles $0°, 60°, \ldots, 300°$:
$$A=(a,0,0),\ B=(\tfrac{a}{2},\tfrac{a\sqrt3}{2},0),\ \ldots$$

The ceiling anchors $M,N,P,Q,R,S$ are midpoints of the ceiling hexagon edges. Th
ese form a regular hexagon at $z=h$ with circumradius $a\cos 30° = \tfrac{a\sqrt
3}{2}$, rotated $30°$ from the floor.

## Cross-Sectional Analysis

**Pyramid 1** (apex $O_1$, base = floor): At height $z$, cross-section is a hexa
gon $H_1$ with circumradius $r_1 = a(1-t)$, orientation $0°$, where $t = z/h$.

**Pyramid 2** (apex $O$, base = midpoint hexagon): At height $z$, cross-section
is a hexagon $H_2$ with circumradius $r_2 = \tfrac{a\sqrt3}{2}t$, orientation $3
0°$.

Both hexagons are centered on the $z$-axis. Their apothems are:
$$d_1 = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}r_1 = \tfrac{a\sqrt3}{2}(1-t), \qquad d_2 = \tfrac{\sqr
t3}{2}r_2 = \tfrac{3a}{4}t$$

The ratio is $\frac{d_1}{d_2} = \frac{2\sqrt3(1-t)}{3t}$.

## Intersection of Two Rotated Hexagons

$H_1$ has side-normals at $30°{+}k60°$; $H_2$ has side-normals at $0°{+}k60°$. T
ogether, 12 half-planes with normals every $30°$.

- **$H_2 \subset H_1$** when $d_1/d_2 \geq 2/\sqrt3$ (i.e., $t \leq 1/2$): inter
section $= H_2$, area $= 2\sqrt3\, d_2^2$.
- **$H_1 \subset H_2$** when $d_1/d_2 \leq \sqrt3/2$ (i.e., $t \geq 4/7$): inter
section $= H_1$, area $= 2\sqrt3\, d_1^2$.
- **Dodecagon** when $\sqrt3/2 < d_1/d_2 < 2/\sqrt3$ (i.e., $1/2 < t < 4/7$).

**Dodecagon area:** Using the $30°$-symmetry, I integrate the radial function $\
min(r_1(\theta), r_2(\theta))$ over a $30°$ wedge. The switch point $\theta^*$ s
atisfies $\tan\theta^* = 2d_1/d_2 - \sqrt3$, and by symmetry $\tan(30°{-}\theta^
*) = 2d_2/d_1 - \sqrt3$. This gives:

$$A_{\text{dodec}} = 6\bigl[d_2^2\tan\theta^* + d_1^2\tan(30°{-}\theta^*)\bigr]
= 24\,d_1 d_2 - 6\sqrt3\,(d_1^2+d_2^2)$$

(Verified: at $d_1=d_2=d$ this yields $12d^2(2-\sqrt3)$, the known regular dodec
agon area with apothem $d$.)

## Integrating Over Height

Substituting $d_1 = \tfrac{a\sqrt3}{2}(1-t)$, $d_2 = \tfrac{3a}{4}t$:

**Region 1** ($0 \leq t \leq \tfrac{1}{2}$): $A = \tfrac{9\sqrt3\,a^2}{8}t^2$

**Region 2** ($\tfrac{1}{2} \leq t \leq \tfrac{4}{7}$): Simplifying $24d_1d_2 -
6\sqrt3(d_1^2+d_2^2)$:
$$A = -\tfrac{9\sqrt3\,a^2}{8}(15t^2 - 16t + 4)$$

**Region 3** ($\tfrac{4}{7} \leq t \leq 1$): $A = \tfrac{3\sqrt3\,a^2}{2}(1-t)^2
$

(Continuity verified at $t=\tfrac{1}{2}$: both give $\tfrac{9\sqrt3\,a^2}{32}$;
at $t=\tfrac{4}{7}$: both give $\tfrac{27\sqrt3\,a^2}{98}$.)

## Computing the Integrals

$$I_1 = \int_0^{1/2} \tfrac{9\sqrt3\,a^2}{8}t^2\,dt = \tfrac{3\sqrt3\,a^2}{64}$$

$$I_2 = -\tfrac{9\sqrt3\,a^2}{8}\Big[5t^3 - 8t^2 + 4t\Big]_{1/2}^{4/7} = -\tfrac
{9\sqrt3\,a^2}{8}\!\left(\tfrac{208}{343} - \tfrac{5}{8}\right) = \tfrac{459\sqr
t3\,a^2}{21952}$$

$$I_3 = \int_{4/7}^{1} \tfrac{3\sqrt3\,a^2}{2}(1-t)^2\,dt = \tfrac{27\sqrt3\,a^2
}{686}$$

With common denominator $21952 = 2^6 \cdot 7^3$:

$$I_1 + I_2 + I_3 = \frac{1029 + 459 + 864}{21952}\sqrt3\,a^2 = \frac{2352}{2195
2}\sqrt3\,a^2 = \frac{3}{28}\sqrt3\,a^2$$

## Final Volume

$$V_{\text{intersect}} = h \cdot \frac{3\sqrt3\,a^2}{28} = \frac{3\sqrt3\,a^2 h}
{28}$$

Since $V = \tfrac{3\sqrt3}{2}a^2 h$:

$$\boxed{\frac{V_{\text{intersect}}}{V} = \frac{3\sqrt3\,a^2 h/28}{3\sqrt3\,a^2
h/2} = \frac{2}{28} = \frac{1}{14}}$$

The volume of the common intersection region is $\dfrac{V}{14}$.
