## Proof

We prove that for every multiplicative function $f:\mathbb{N}\to\{-1,1\}$ with $
f(1)=1$, the limit $\displaystyle L=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(
n)$ exists. The proof uses **Halász's theorem** (1968), a refinement of Wirsing'
s theorem (1967).

---

### Halász's Theorem (1968)

Let $g:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}$ be multiplicative with $|g(n)|\le 1$ for all $n$
. Define
$$D(t)=\sum_p \frac{1-\operatorname{Re}(g(p)\,p^{-it})}{p}, \qquad t\in\mathbb{R
}.$$

**(H1)** If there exists $t_0\in\mathbb{R}$ with $D(t_0)<\infty$, then
$$\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\le x}g(n)\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{1}{1+it_0}\prod_p\left(1-
\frac{1}{p}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{g(p^k)}{p^{k(1+it_0)}}.$$

**(H2)** If $D(t)=\infty$ for every $t\in\mathbb{R}$, then
$$\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\le x}g(n)\;\longrightarrow\;0.$$

In both cases the limit exists. $\square$ (Halász)

---

### Application to $f:\mathbb{N}\to\{-1,1\}$

Since $f$ is real-valued with $f(p)\in\{-1,+1\}$, we have
$$\operatorname{Re}(f(p)\,p^{-it})=f(p)\cos(t\log p),$$
so
$$D(t)=\sum_p\frac{1-f(p)\cos(t\log p)}{p}.$$

At $t=0$: $\;D(0)=\displaystyle\sum_p\frac{1-f(p)}{p}=\sum_{\substack{p\\f(p)=-1
}}\frac{2}{p}.$

We distinguish two cases.

---

### Case 1: $D(0)<\infty$ (i.e., $\sum_{p:\,f(p)=-1}\frac{1}{p}<\infty$)

**Claim.** $D(t)=\infty$ for every $t\ne 0$, so $t_0=0$ is the unique minimizer.

*Proof of Claim.* For $t\ne 0$, since $1-\operatorname{Re}(z)\ge 1-|z|$ for any
$z\in\mathbb{C}$,
$$D(t)\ge\sum_p\frac{1-|f(p)\cos(t\log p)|}{p}=\sum_p\frac{1-|\cos(t\log p)|}{p}
,$$
since $|f(p)|=1$. It suffices to show this last sum diverges.

By Weyl's criterion, the sequence $\bigl\{\frac{t\log p}{2\pi}\bigr\}_{p\text{ p
rime}}$ is equidistributed modulo $1$ for $t\ne 0$: this follows because for eve
ry nonzero integer $h$,
$$\sum_{p\le x}e^{2\pi i h\cdot t\log p/(2\pi)}=\sum_{p\le x}p^{iht}=o(\pi(x)),$
$
which is a standard consequence of the Prime Number Theorem (the partial sums $\
sum_{p\le x}p^{i\alpha}$, $\alpha\ne 0$, are $o(x/\log x)$).

Therefore $|\cos(t\log p)|$ is distributed according to the pushforward of the u
niform measure on $[0,2\pi)$ under $\theta\mapsto|\cos\theta|$, and
$$\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\sum_{p\le x}\bigl(1-|\cos(t\log p)|\bigr)\;\longrightarrow\;\
frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}(1-|\cos\theta|)\,d\theta=1-\frac{2}{\pi}>0.$$

By partial summation (Abel summation with $\sum_{p\le x}1/p\sim\log\log x$),
$$\sum_{p\le x}\frac{1-|\cos(t\log p)|}{p}\;\sim\;\left(1-\frac{2}{\pi}\right)\l
og\log x\;\longrightarrow\;\infty.$$

Hence $D(t)=\infty$ for all $t\ne 0$. $\square$

By Halász (H1) with $t_0=0$, the limit exists and equals
$$L=\prod_p\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\left(1+\frac{f(p)}{p}+\frac{f(p^2)}{p^2}+\
cdots\right).$$

**Convergence of the product.** Each factor equals
$$\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{f(p^k)}{p^k}=1+\frac{f(p)-1
}{p}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{p^2}\right).$$
The Euler product converges absolutely because $\sum_p\frac{|f(p)-1|}{p}=D(0)<\i
nfty$ and $\sum_p O(1/p^2)<\infty$.

---

### Case 2: $D(0)=\infty$ (i.e., $\sum_{p:\,f(p)=-1}\frac{1}{p}=\infty$)

We show $D(t)=\infty$ for **every** $t\in\mathbb{R}$, so that Halász (H2) applie
s.

- **$t=0$:** $D(0)=\infty$ by assumption.

- **$t\ne 0$:** As shown in Case 1, $D(t)\ge\sum_p\frac{1-|\cos(t\log p)|}{p}=\i
nfty$ by the equidistribution argument.

Therefore $D(t)=\infty$ for all $t$, and Halász (H2) gives
$$\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n\le N}f(n)\;\longrightarrow\;0.$$

---

### Conclusion

In **Case 1** the limit exists and equals the convergent Euler product $M(f)$; i
n **Case 2** the limit exists and equals $0$. Since every multiplicative $f:\mat
hbb{N}\to\{-1,1\}$ with $f(1)=1$ falls into exactly one of these two cases, the
limit

$$\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(n)$$

always exists. $\square$
