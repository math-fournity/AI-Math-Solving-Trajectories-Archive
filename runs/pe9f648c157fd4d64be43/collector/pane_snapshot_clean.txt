# Smallest Dimension of a Non-Commutative C*-Algebra

**Claim.** The smallest dimension of a non-commutative C*-algebra is **4**.

## Proof

**Step 1. Structure of finite-dimensional C*-algebras.**

Every finite-dimensional C*-algebra $A$ is *-isomorphic to a finite direct sum o
f full matrix algebras:
$$A \cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^{k} M_{n_i}(\mathbb{C}),$$
where each $n_i \geq 1$. This is the standard Wedderburn–Artin-type structure th
eorem for finite-dimensional C*-algebras (see e.g. Davidson, *C*-Algebras by Exa
mple*, or Murphy). Its vector-space dimension is
$$\dim_{\mathbb{C}} A = \sum_{i=1}^{k} n_i^2.$$

**Step 2. Commutativity criterion.**

A direct sum $\bigoplus_i M_{n_i}(\mathbb{C})$ is commutative if and only if eve
ry summand is commutative, which happens if and only if $n_i = 1$ for all $i$. T
hus $A$ is commutative exactly when $A \cong \mathbb{C}^k$ for some $k$.

Consequently, $A$ is **non-commutative** if and only if at least one summand has
 $n_i \geq 2$.

**Step 3. Ruling out dimensions 1, 2, 3.**

We enumerate all finite-dimensional C*-algebras of dimension $\leq 3$ by finding
 all tuples $(n_1,\dots,n_k)$ with $n_i \geq 1$ and $\sum n_i^2 \leq 3$:

| $\sum n_i^2$ | Possible tuples | Algebra | Commutative? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $(1)$ | $\mathbb{C}$ | Yes |
| 2 | $(1,1)$ | $\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}$ | Yes |
| 3 | $(1,1,1)$ | $\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}$ | Yes |

The tuple $(2)$ would give $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ with $\sum n_i^2 = 4 > 3$, so it is
 excluded. Every C*-algebra of dimension $\leq 3$ is therefore a direct sum of c
opies of $\mathbb{C}$ only, hence commutative.

**Step 4. Achieving dimension 4 with non-commutativity.**

The algebra $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ is a C*-algebra (with the usual adjoint and operat
or norm) of dimension $2^2 = 4$. It is non-commutative: for instance,
$$\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 \en
d{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \neq \begin{pmatrix} 0
 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin
{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

**Step 5. Infinite-dimensional algebras are larger.**

Any infinite-dimensional C*-algebra has (vector-space) dimension $\geq \aleph_0
> 4$, so it cannot beat the finite candidate. Hence the global minimum is attain
ed in the finite-dimensional case.

**Conclusion.** No non-commutative C*-algebra exists in dimensions 1, 2, or 3, a
nd $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ provides a non-commutative C*-algebra of dimension 4. There
fore the smallest dimension a non-commutative C*-algebra can have is

$$\boxed{4}.$$
