**Yes, such a model exists.**

## Proof

Let $L = \{+,\cdot,<,0,1\}$ be the language of ordered fields, which is countabl
e. Let $T = \operatorname{Th}(\mathbb{R})$ be the complete first-order theory of
 the real ordered field in this language.

**Step 1: $T$ has a countable model.**

By the downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem, every infinite structure in a countabl
e language has a countable elementary substructure. Since $\mathbb{R}$ is an inf
inite $L$-structure, there exists a countable $L$-structure $M \prec \mathbb{R}$
 (an elementary substructure). In particular, $M \models T$.

**Step 2: $M$ is an ordered field.**

The axioms of an ordered field are all first-order sentences in $L$ (field axiom
s, order axioms, and compatibility axioms such as $\forall x\,\forall y\,\forall
 z\,(x < y \to x + z < y + z)$ and $\forall x\,\forall y\,((0 < x \wedge 0 < y)
\to 0 < x \cdot y)$). Since $\mathbb{R}$ is an ordered field, all these axioms b
elong to $T = \operatorname{Th}(\mathbb{R})$. Since $M \models T$, the structure
 $M$ satisfies every ordered-field axiom, so $M$ is an ordered field.

**Step 3: $M$ is not a complete ordered field.**

A classical theorem states that every Dedekind-complete ordered field is uniquel
y determined up to (unique) isomorphism, and is isomorphic to $\mathbb{R}$. In p
articular, every complete ordered field has cardinality $|\mathbb{R}| = 2^{\alep
h_0}$, hence is uncountable.

Since $M$ is countable, $M$ cannot be a complete ordered field.

**Conclusion.**

$M$ is a model of $\operatorname{Th}(\mathbb{R})$ that is an ordered field but n
ot a complete ordered field. Therefore, there exists a model of $\operatorname{T
h}(\mathbb{R})$ that is not a complete ordered field. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The essential point is that Dedekind completeness is a *second-order
* property (it quantifies over all subsets), so it is not expressible in first-o
rder logic and is not pinned down by $\operatorname{Th}(\mathbb{R})$. The first-
order theory cannot control the cardinality of its models beyond what the Löwenh
eim–Skolem theorems dictate, so models of $\operatorname{Th}(\mathbb{R})$ exist
in cardinalities other than $2^{\aleph_0}$ (both countable, by downward LS, and
arbitrarily large, by upward LS), and none of these can be complete ordered fiel
ds.
