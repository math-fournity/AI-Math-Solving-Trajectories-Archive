## Setup and Key Definitions

**Circles of antisimilitude:** For two non-concentric circles α (center A, radiu
s a) and β (center B, radius b), the circles of antisimilitude γ are the two cir
cles in which inversion maps α to β. Their centers are the centers of similitude
 of α and β, and their radii are:
- External: $\rho_{\text{ext}} = \frac{d\sqrt{ab}}{|a-b|}$
- Internal: $\rho_{\text{int}} = \frac{d\sqrt{ab}}{a+b}$

where $d = AB$.

## Step 1: Antisimilitude is Preserved Under Inversion

**Claim:** If γ is a circle of antisimilitude of α and β (meaning $I_\gamma(\alp
ha) = \beta$), then after inverting all three by θ, γ' is a circle of antisimili
tude of α' and β'.

**Proof:** Let $I_\theta$ denote inversion by θ. A fundamental property of inver
sions is that conjugation of an inversion by another inversion yields the invers
ion in the image circle:

$$I_\theta \circ I_\gamma \circ I_\theta = I_{I_\theta(\gamma)} = I_{\gamma'}$$

Therefore:
$$I_{\gamma'}(\alpha') = I_\theta\bigl(I_\gamma\bigl(I_\theta(\alpha')\bigr)\big
r) = I_\theta\bigl(I_\gamma(\alpha)\bigr) = I_\theta(\beta) = \beta'$$

So **γ' is always the circle of antisimilitude of α' and β'**, regardless of the
 choice of θ. The condition in the problem is automatically satisfied. ✓

## Step 2: Radius Transformation Under Inversion

Under inversion by θ (center O, power $k = \rho^2$), a circle with center C and
radius r maps to a circle with radius:

$$r' = \frac{k \cdot r}{|\text{Pow}(O, \text{circle})|}$$

where $\text{Pow}(O, \text{circle}) = OC^2 - r^2$ is the power of O with respect
 to the circle.

Applying this to γ:

$$r(\gamma') = \frac{k \cdot r(\gamma)}{|\text{Pow}(O, \gamma)|}$$

## Step 3: Imposing the Condition $r(\gamma') = 2\,r(\gamma)$

Setting $r(\gamma') = 2\,r(\gamma)$:

$$\frac{k}{|\text{Pow}(O, \gamma)|} = 2$$

$$\boxed{k = 2\,|\text{Pow}(O, \gamma)| = 2\,\left|OO_\gamma^2 - r_\gamma^2\righ

where $O_\gamma$ is the center of γ and $r_\gamma$ is its radius.

## Conclusion

The power of inversion $k$ of θ must equal **twice the absolute power of the cen
ter of θ with respect to γ**. Since the antisimilitude relationship is preserved

$$k = 2\,|\text{Pow}(O, \gamma)|$$

The value of $k$ is $\boxed{2}$, expressed in units of $|\text{Pow}(O,\gamma)|$
— that is, the power of inversion equals **2 times** the power of the inversion
