**Answer: Yes, such an operator exists.**

## Proof

**Step 1: A continuous idempotent cannot have the stated property.**

If $P$ is bounded (continuous) and $P^2=P$, then for any sequence $\{x_n\}$ with
 $\|x_n\|\to 0$,
$$\|Px_n\|\le \|P\|\,\|x_n\|\to 0,$$
contradicting $\|Px_n\|\to\infty$. Hence $P$ must be **discontinuous**, and the
problem reduces to constructing a discontinuous projection on an infinite-dimens
ional Banach space.

**Step 2: Construct a discontinuous linear functional.**

Let $X$ be an infinite-dimensional Banach space and let $B$ be a Hamel basis of
$X$ (whose existence uses the Axiom of Choice). Since $X$ is infinite-dimensiona
l, $B$ is infinite; select a countably infinite subset $\{b_n\}_{n=1}^\infty\sub
seteq B$. Define a linear functional $f:X\to\mathbb{K}$ by specifying its values
 on the basis:
$$f(b_n)=n^2\,\|b_n\|,\qquad f(b)=0\;\text{for }b\in B\setminus\{b_n\},$$
and extend linearly. Set
$$x_n=\frac{b_n}{n\,\|b_n\|}.$$
Then $\|x_n\|=\tfrac{1}{n}\to 0$, while
$$f(x_n)=\frac{f(b_n)}{n\,\|b_n\|}=\frac{n^2\|b_n\|}{n\|b_n\|}=n\to\infty.$$
So $f$ is a **discontinuous** linear functional (a continuous one would be bound
ed on the unit ball).

**Step 3: Build a projection from $f$.**

Since $f\neq 0$, pick $u\in X$ with $f(u)=1$ (for instance $u=b_1/\|b_1\|$ scale
d appropriately, or any vector with $f(u)=1$). Define
$$P:X\to X,\qquad P(x)=f(x)\,u.$$

*Idempotence:* For every $x\in X$,
$$P^2(x)=P(f(x)u)=f(f(x)u)\,u=f(x)\,f(u)\,u=f(x)\cdot 1\cdot u=P(x).$$
Hence $P^2=P$.

*Discontinuity:* $P$ is discontinuous because $f$ is discontinuous (if $P$ were
bounded, then $f(x)=\frac{f(x)u}{\ldots}$... more directly, $f(x)\,u=Px$, and co
mposing with the continuous map $y\mapsto$ the scalar coefficient of $u$ would m
ake $f$ continuous; equivalently $\|Px_n\|=|f(x_n)|\,\|u\|\to\infty$ while $\|x_
n\|\to 0$ shows $P$ is unbounded).

*The required sequence:* Using the sequence $\{x_n\}$ from Step 2,
$$\|Px_n\|=\|f(x_n)\,u\|=|f(x_n)|\,\|u\|=n\,\|u\|\to\infty,$$
since $\|u\|>0$, while $\|x_n\|=\tfrac1n\to 0$.

**Step 4: $P$ is a genuine projection (algebraic direct sum decomposition).**

Since $f(u)=1\neq 0$, we have $u\notin\ker f$. Every $x\in X$ decomposes as
$$x=\bigl(x-f(x)u\bigr)+f(x)u,\qquad x-f(x)u\in\ker f,\quad f(x)u\in\operatornam
e{span}\{u\},$$
and $\ker f\cap\operatorname{span}\{u\}=\{0\}$. Thus $X=\ker f\oplus\operatornam
e{span}\{u\}$ algebraically, confirming $P$ is the projection onto $\operatornam
e{span}\{u\}$ along $\ker f$. The kernel $\ker f$ is **not closed** (a discontin
uous functional has non-closed kernel), which is exactly why $P$ is discontinuou
s — consistent with the standard fact that a projection is continuous iff both i
ts range and kernel are closed.

## Conclusion

On any infinite-dimensional Banach space $X$, using a Hamel basis one constructs
 a discontinuous linear functional $f$ with a sequence $x_n\to 0$ and $f(x_n)\to
\infty$. From $f$ and any $u$ with $f(u)=1$, the operator $P(x)=f(x)u$ is a disc
ontinuous idempotent ($P^2=P$) satisfying $\|x_n\|\to 0$ yet $\|Px_n\|\to\infty$
.

**Such a non-continuous idempotent linear operator exists.**
