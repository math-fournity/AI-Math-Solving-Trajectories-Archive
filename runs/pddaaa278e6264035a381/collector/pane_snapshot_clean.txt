## Claim

The conjecture is **false**: the series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{\pi^{1/n}\
, p_n}$ **diverges** (its partial sums tend to $+\infty$, not $2$).

## Proof

Let
$$a_n = \frac{1}{\pi^{1/n}\, p_n}, \qquad b_n = \frac{1}{p_n}.$$

**Step 1: Limit comparison.** Compute the ratio
$$\frac{a_n}{b_n} = \frac{1/\pi^{1/n}\, p_n}{1/p_n} = \pi^{-1/n} = e^{-\ln\pi\,/
\,n}.$$
As $n \to \infty$, $\ln\pi / n \to 0$, so
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{a_n}{b_n} = e^{0} = 1 > 0.$$

**Step 2: Divergence of $\sum 1/p_n$.** It is a classical result (Euler, 1737; s
harpened by Mertens, 1874) that the sum of reciprocals of the primes diverges:
$$\sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{1}{p_n} \sim \ln\ln N \to +\infty \quad (N \to \infty).$$

**Step 3: Apply the limit comparison test.** Since $\lim_{n\to\infty} a_n/b_n =
1 \in (0,\infty)$ and $\sum b_n = \sum 1/p_n$ diverges, the limit comparison tes
t gives that $\sum a_n = \sum \frac{1}{\pi^{1/n}\, p_n}$ also **diverges**.

**Step 4: Conclusion.** The partial sums
$$S_k = \sum_{n=1}^{k} \frac{1}{\pi^{1/n}\, p_n} \xrightarrow{k\to\infty} +\inft
y,$$
which is not equal to $2$. Hence the conjecture is false.

**Remark (numerical sanity check).** The first few terms are
$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\approx 0.159,\quad \frac{1}{3\sqrt\pi}\approx 0.188,\quad \frac
{1}{5\,\pi^{1/3}}\approx 0.137,\quad \frac{1}{7\,\pi^{1/4}}\approx 0.107,\ \ldot
s$$
Already $S_4 \approx 0.59$, and since $\pi^{1/n}\to 1$ the tail behaves like $\s
um 1/p_n$, which grows without bound (logarithmically slowly, but unboundedly).
The series cannot converge to any finite value, let alone $2$.
