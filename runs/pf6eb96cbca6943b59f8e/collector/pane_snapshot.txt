**Theorem.** Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a set equipped with a subatlas $\mathcal{A}=\{
(U_\alpha,\phi_\alpha)\}_{\alpha}$ (a collection of pairwise-compatible charts w

**Proof.**

Define
$$\mathcal{A}^* \;=\; \bigl\{(U,\varphi)\;:\;(U,\varphi)\text{ is a chart on }\m
athcal{M}\text{ compatible with every }(U_\alpha,\phi_\alpha)\in\mathcal{A}\bigr

We show that $\mathcal{A}^*$ is *the* unique maximal atlas containing $\mathcal{

---

**Step 1. $\mathcal{A}^*$ is an atlas containing $\mathcal{A}$.**

*Containment.* Every chart of $\mathcal{A}$ is compatible with every other chart
 of $\mathcal{A}$ (by definition of subatlas), so $\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{
A}^*$. In particular the domains of $\mathcal{A}^*$ cover $\mathcal{M}$.

*Pairwise compatibility.* This is the key lemma:

> **Lemma.** If two charts $(U,\varphi)$ and $(V,\psi)$ are each compatible with
 every chart of an atlas $\mathcal{A}$ that covers $\mathcal{M}$, then $(U,\varp
hi)$ and $(V,\psi)$ are compatible with each other.

*Proof of Lemma.* Let $p\in U\cap V$. Since $\mathcal{A}$ covers $\mathcal{M}$,
pick $(U_\alpha,\phi_\alpha)\in\mathcal{A}$ with $p\in U_\alpha$, and set $W=U\c
ap V\cap U_\alpha$, an open neighborhood of $p$. On $\varphi(W)$ we have the fac
torization
$$\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}\;=\;\underbrace{(\psi\circ\phi_\alpha^{-1})}_{\text{smoo
th}}\circ\underbrace{(\phi_\alpha\circ\varphi^{-1})}_{\text{smooth}}.$$
The first factor is smooth because $(V,\psi)$ is compatible with $(U_\alpha,\phi
_\alpha)$; the second is smooth because $(U,\varphi)$ is compatible with $(U_\al
pha,\phi_\alpha)$. Hence $\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}$ is smooth on the open neighborh

\varphi^{-1}$ is smooth on all of $\varphi(U\cap V)$. By symmetry $\varphi\circ\
psi^{-1}$ is smooth on $\psi(U\cap V)$. $\quad\blacksquare$

Applying the lemma to any two charts of $\mathcal{A}^*$, we conclude that $\math
cal{A}^*$ is pairwise compatible. Hence $\mathcal{A}^*$ is an atlas.

**Step 2. $\mathcal{A}^*$ contains every atlas that contains $\mathcal{A}$.**

Let $\mathcal{B}$ be any atlas with $\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{B}$. Every cha
rt of $\mathcal{B}$ is compatible with every chart of $\mathcal{A}$ (since all c
harts of an atlas are pairwise compatible and $\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{B}$)

$\mathcal{A}^*$. Thus $\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{A}^*$.

---

**Step 3. Uniqueness.**

Let $\mathcal{M}_{\max}$ be any maximal atlas extending $\mathcal{A}$. By Step 2
, $\mathcal{M}_{\max}\subseteq\mathcal{A}^*$. But $\mathcal{A}^*$ is itself an a
tlas containing $\mathcal{A}$ (Step 1). Maximality of $\mathcal{M}_{\max}$ means
 it is not properly contained in any larger atlas; since $\mathcal{A}^*$ is an a
tlas containing $\mathcal{M}_{\max}$, we must have $\mathcal{M}_{\max}=\mathcal{
A}^*$.

Therefore every maximal atlas extending $\mathcal{A}$ coincides with $\mathcal{A
}^*$, so the maximal extension is **unique**. $\quad\blacksquare$
