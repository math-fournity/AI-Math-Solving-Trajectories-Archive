## Proof

**Setup.** Let $M = \#_{k}(S^{2}\times S^{1})$, the connected sum of $k$ copies
of $S^{2}\times S^{1}$.

**Step 1: Spin structures are classified by $H^{1}(M;\mathbb{Z}/2)$.**

For an orientable Riemannian manifold, the set of spin structures (up to equival
ence) is an affine space over $H^{1}(M;\mathbb{Z}/2)$. Hence the number of inequ
ivalent spin structures equals $\bigl|H^{1}(M;\mathbb{Z}/2)\bigr|$, provided $M$
 admits a spin structure at all.

**Step 2: $M$ is orientable and spin.**

Each $S^{2}\times S^{1}$ is orientable, and the connected sum of orientable mani
folds is orientable, so $M$ is orientable. Every orientable 3-manifold is parall
elizable (Stiefel's theorem), so all Stiefel–Whitney classes vanish; in particul
ar $w_{2}(M)=0$, which is precisely the condition for $M$ to admit a spin struct
ure. Thus spin structures exist.

**Step 3: Compute $H_{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})$.**

For a connected sum of connected 3-manifolds, $H_{1}\!\left(\#_{k} M_{i}\right)
\cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^{k} H_{1}(M_{i})$. Since $H_{1}(S^{2}\times S^{1})\cong \m
athbb{Z}$ (generated by the $S^{1}$ factor), we obtain
$$H_{1}(M;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{k}.$$

**Step 4: Compute $H^{1}(M;\mathbb{Z}/2)$ via the Universal Coefficient Theorem.
**

$$H^{1}(M;\mathbb{Z}/2) \cong \operatorname{Hom}\!\bigl(H_{1}(M),\,\mathbb{Z}/2\
bigr) \oplus \operatorname{Ext}\!\bigl(H_{0}(M),\,\mathbb{Z}/2\bigr).$$

Since $H_{0}(M)\cong\mathbb{Z}$, we have $\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{
Z}/2)=0$. Also
$$\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{k},\,\mathbb{Z}/2) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2)^{k}.$$

Therefore
$$H^{1}(M;\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong (\mathbb{Z}/2)^{k},$$
which has exactly $2^{k}$ elements.

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

The number of inequivalent spin structures on $M$ equals $\bigl|H^{1}(M;\mathbb{
Z}/2)\bigr| = 2^{k}$.

$$\boxed{2^{k}}$$
