## Proof

We evaluate $I = \displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\ln x}{x^2+x+1}\,dx$.

**Substitution.** Let $x = e^u$, so $dx = e^u\,du$ and $\ln x = u$. As $x$ range
s over $(0,\infty)$, $u$ ranges over $(-\infty, \infty)$. The integral becomes

$$I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{u\, e^u}{e^{2u}+e^u+1}\,du.$$

**Symmetry.** Define $g(u) = \dfrac{e^u}{e^{2u}+e^u+1}$. We check that $g$ is ev
en:

$$g(-u) = \frac{e^{-u}}{e^{-2u}+e^{-u}+1} = \frac{e^{-u}\cdot e^{2u}}{1+e^{u}+e^
{2u}} = \frac{e^{u}}{e^{2u}+e^{u}+1} = g(u).$$

**Convergence.** The integrand $u\,g(u)$ is integrable on $(-\infty,\infty)$:
- As $u\to+\infty$: $g(u)\sim e^u/e^{2u}=e^{-u}$, so $u\,g(u)\sim u\,e^{-u}$, wh
ich is integrable.
- As $u\to-\infty$: $g(u)\sim e^u/1 = e^u$, so $u\,g(u)\sim u\,e^u$, which is in
tegrable.

**Conclusion.** Since $g(u)$ is even and $u$ is odd, the product $u\,g(u)$ is od
d. The integral of an odd, integrable function over the symmetric interval $(-\i
nfty,\infty)$ is zero:

$$I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} u\,g(u)\,du = 0.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\ln x}{x^2+x+1}\,dx = 0.}$$
