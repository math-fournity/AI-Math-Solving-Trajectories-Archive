## Solution

We evaluate
$$L = \lim_{x\to 0^+}\int_x^{1/x}\frac{\cos(4r)-\cos(2r)}{r}\,dr.$$

**Step 1. Reduce to the improper integral over $(0,\infty)$.**

Near $r=0$, $\cos(4r)-\cos(2r)=\bigl(1-8r^2+O(r^4)\bigr)-\bigl(1-2r^2+O(r^4)\big
r)=-6r^2+O(r^4)$, so $\frac{\cos(4r)-\cos(2r)}{r}=O(r)$, which is integrable at
$0$. Hence $\int_0^x \cdots \to 0$.

At infinity, $\frac{\cos(4r)-\cos(2r)}{r}=\frac{\cos 4r}{r}-\frac{\cos 2r}{r}$,
and each term $\int_R^\infty \frac{\cos(ar)}{r}\,dr\to 0$ as $R\to\infty$ by Dir
ichlet's test (since $1/r\downarrow 0$ and $\int_0^R\cos(ar)\,dr$ is bounded). T
hus $\int_{1/x}^{\infty}\cdots\to 0$ as $x\to 0^+$.

Therefore
$$L=\int_0^\infty \frac{\cos(4r)-\cos(2r)}{r}\,dr.$$

**Step 2. Laplace-transform representation of $1/r$.**

For $r>0$,
$$\frac{1}{r}=\int_0^\infty e^{-rt}\,dt.$$

Substituting and formally swapping the order of integration (justified by absolu
te convergence of the double integral after inserting a cutoff, or by Fubini on
the regularized version $\int_\varepsilon^R$ and then taking limits):
$$L=\int_0^\infty\!\left[\int_0^\infty\bigl(\cos(4r)-\cos(2r)\bigr)e^{-tr}\,dr\r
ight]dt.$$

**Step 3. Evaluate the inner integral via the Laplace transform of $\cos(ar)$.**

The standard Laplace transform gives, for $t>0$,
$$\int_0^\infty \cos(ar)\,e^{-tr}\,dr=\frac{t}{t^2+a^2}.$$

Hence the inner integral equals
$$\frac{t}{t^2+16}-\frac{t}{t^2+4}.$$

**Step 4. Evaluate the outer integral.**

$$L=\int_0^\infty\!\left(\frac{t}{t^2+16}-\frac{t}{t^2+4}\right)dt
=\frac12\Bigl[\ln(t^2+16)-\ln(t^2+4)\Bigr]_0^\infty
=\frac12\left[\ln\frac{t^2+16}{t^2+4}\right]_0^\infty.$$

As $t\to\infty$, $\frac{t^2+16}{t^2+4}\to 1$, so the logarithm tends to $0$. At
$t=0$, $\frac{16}{4}=4$, so the logarithm equals $\ln 4$. Therefore
$$L=\frac12\bigl(0-\ln 4\bigr)=-\frac12\ln 4=-\ln 2.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\lim_{x\to 0^+}\int_x^{1/x}\frac{\cos(4r)-\cos(2r)}{r}\,dr=-\ln 2.}$$
