## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, such integer functions $f(n)$ and $g(n)$ exist.

**Step 1: $\zeta(2) = \pi^2/6$ is irrational.**

The number $\pi$ is transcendental (Lindemann–Weierstrass theorem, 1882). If $\p
i^2$ were rational, say $\pi^2 = r \in \mathbb{Q}$, then $\pi$ would be a root o
f the polynomial $x^2 - r \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$, making $\pi$ algebraic — a contrad
iction. Hence $\pi^2$ is irrational, and so $\zeta(2) = \pi^2/6$ is irrational.

**Step 2: Continued fraction convergents give the desired approximation.**

Let $\alpha = \zeta(2)$, an irrational number. Consider the (simple) continued f
raction expansion of $\alpha$:
$$\alpha = [a_0; a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots],$$
and let $p_n/q_n$ denote the $n$-th convergent. A standard result in the theory
of continued fractions states that for every $n \geq 0$:
$$\left|\alpha - \frac{p_n}{q_n}\right| < \frac{1}{q_n^2},$$
which gives
$$|q_n \alpha - p_n| < \frac{1}{q_n}.$$

Since the denominators $q_n$ form a strictly increasing sequence of positive int
egers (for $n \geq 1$), we have $q_n \to \infty$, and therefore:
$$|q_n \alpha - p_n| < \frac{1}{q_n} \to 0 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty.$$

Moreover, $q_n \alpha - p_n \neq 0$ for every $n$, because if $q_n \alpha - p_n
= 0$, then $\alpha = p_n/q_n \in \mathbb{Q}$, contradicting the irrationality of
 $\alpha$.

**Step 3: Define $f$ and $g$.**

Set $f(n) = q_n$ and $g(n) = -p_n$ for each positive integer $n$. Then:

- $f(n)$ and $g(n)$ are integers (convergent numerators and denominators are int
egers).
- $f(n)\,\zeta(2) + g(n) = q_n\,\alpha - p_n \neq 0$ for all $n$, by the argumen
t above.
- $\lim_{n \to \infty} f(n)\,\zeta(2) + g(n) = \lim_{n \to \infty} (q_n\,\alpha
- p_n) = 0$.

**Conclusion.** Such integer functions exist. Explicitly, taking $f(n) = q_n$ an
d $g(n) = -p_n$, where $p_n/q_n$ are the convergents of the continued fraction o
f $\zeta(2) = \pi^2/6$, satisfies both required conditions. $\blacksquare$
