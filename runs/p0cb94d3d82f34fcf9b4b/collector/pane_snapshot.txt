## Solution

**Given:** $L: V \to V$ satisfies $L^2 + I_v = 0$, i.e., $L^2 = -I_v$.

**Define:** $T = L^3 + 2L^2 + L + 3I_v$.

**Reduction using $L^2 = -I_v$.** Since $L^2 = -I_v$, we compute:
$$L^3 = L \cdot L^2 = L(-I_v) = -L.$$

Substituting into the expression for $T$:
$$T = L^3 + 2L^2 + L + 3I_v = (-L) + 2(-I_v) + L + 3I_v = -L - 2I_v + L + 3I_v =
 I_v.$$

The terms $-L$ and $+L$ cancel, and $-2I_v + 3I_v = I_v$. Therefore:
$$T = I_v.$$

**Minimal polynomial of $T$.** The operator $T = I_v$ is the identity operator.
The identity operator satisfies $T - I_v = 0$, so its minimal polynomial divides
 $x - 1$. Since $x - 1$ is irreducible (degree 1) and $T - I_v = 0$ is a nontriv
ial relation (for $V \neq \{0\}$), the minimal polynomial is exactly:
$$\boxed{x - 1}.$$

**Uniqueness.** This result holds over any field and for any $L$ satisfying $L^2
 + I_v = 0$ (regardless of the dimension of $V$ or the specific structure of $L$
), because the reduction $T = I_v$ is a purely algebraic consequence of $L^2 = -
I_v$. Hence there is exactly **one** possibility for the minimal polynomial.

**Remark on the trivial case.** If $V = \{0\}$ is the zero vector space, the min
imal polynomial is conventionally $1$, but this is a degenerate case typically e
xcluded. For all nontrivial $V$, the unique possibility is $x - 1$.
