Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the sheafification of the c
onstant presheaf (the constant sheaf) always satisfies $\mathcal{O}_X(U) \cong \
mathbb{Z}^i$ where $i$ is the number of connected components of $U$.

## Analysis

**The sheaf $\mathcal{O}_X$.** The sheafification of the constant presheaf $\mat
hbb{Z}$ is the **constant sheaf**, whose sections on an open set $U$ are the loc
ally constant functions $f: U \to \mathbb{Z}$.

**Key fact.** A locally constant function is always constant on each connected c
omponent (since on a connected space, a locally constant function is constant: t
he fibers $f^{-1}(n)$ are open and partition the space, so by connectedness only
 one is nonempty). Thus:
$$\mathcal{O}_X(U) \subseteq \prod_{C \in \pi_0(U)} \mathbb{Z}$$
with **equality if and only if** every connected component of $U$ is open (so th
at "constant on each component" implies "locally constant").

**Does the hypothesis propagate to open subsets?** The hypothesis "each connecte
d component of $X$ is open" means $X = \coprod_j C_j$ (topological sum). For ope
n $U \subseteq X$, we get $U = \coprod_j (U \cap C_j)$ with each $U \cap C_j$ op
en in $U$. However, the connected components of $U \cap C_j$ (as a subspace of t
he connected space $C_j$) **need not be open** in $U$. The hypothesis does **not
** guarantee that connected components of every open subset are open.

**Finite case.** If $U$ has **finitely** many connected components, each is clos
ed (components are always closed) and the complement of finitely many closed set
s, hence open. So for finite $i$, the statement **holds**.

**Infinite case — counterexample.** The statement fails for infinitely many comp
onents.

## Counterexample

Define $X' \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ as:
$$X' = \underbrace{[0,1]\times\{0\}}_{A} \;\cup\; \underbrace{\{0\}\times[0,1]}_
{V_0} \;\cup\; \bigcup_{n\geq 1} \underbrace{\{1/n\}\times[0,1]}_{V_n}$$

**$X'$ is connected:** $A$ is connected, and each $V_n$ (and $V_0$) is connected
 and meets $A$. So $X'$ has a single connected component ($X'$ itself), which is
 open. ✓ The hypothesis is satisfied.

**Open subset $U' = X' \setminus A$:** Since $A$ is compact in the Hausdorff spa
ce $X'$, it is closed, so $U'$ is open. We have:
$$U' = \{0\}\times(0,1] \;\cup\; \bigcup_{n\geq 1} \{1/n\}\times(0,1]$$

**Connected components of $U'$:** Each fiber $\{1/n\}\times(0,1]$ and $\{0\}\tim
es(0,1]$ is connected (homeomorphic to $(0,1]$). They are pairwise disjoint and
separated, giving countably many ($i = \aleph_0$) connected components:
$$C_0 = \{0\}\times(0,1], \quad C_n = \{1/n\}\times(0,1] \;(n \geq 1).$$

**$C_0$ is not open in $U'$:** Any open ball around $(0, y) \in C_0$ contains po
ints $(1/n, y)$ for all sufficiently large $n$ (since $1/n \to 0$). So $C_0$ is
closed but **not open** in $U'$.

**Computing $\mathcal{O}_X(U')$:** A locally constant function $f: U' \to \mathb
b{Z}$ is constant on each component, say $f|_{C_n} = c_n$. For $f$ to be locally
 constant at $(0,y) \in C_0$, some open neighborhood of $(0,y)$ must see $f$ con
stant — but every such neighborhood meets $C_n$ for all large $n$. Therefore:
$$c_n = c_0 \quad \text{for all sufficiently large } n.$$

Conversely, any sequence $(c_0, c_1, c_2, \ldots)$ with $c_n = c_0$ for $n \geq
N$ defines a locally constant function (on $C_n$ for $n \geq 1$, use $C_n$ itsel
f as the open neighborhood; on $C_0$, use $C_0 \cup \bigcup_{n \geq N} C_n$, whi
ch is open). Thus:
$$\mathcal{O}_X(U') \cong \{(c_n)_{n \geq 0} \in \mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}_0} : c_n
 = c_0 \text{ for all sufficiently large } n\}.$$

**Cardinality mismatch:** $\mathcal{O}_X(U')$ is a countable union of countable
sets $\bigcup_{N=0}^{\infty} \mathbb{Z}^{N+1}$, hence **countable** ($\aleph_0$)
. But $\mathbb{Z}^i = \mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}_0}$ (the full product) has cardinal
ity $2^{\aleph_0}$ (**uncountable**). Therefore:
$$\mathcal{O}_X(U') \not\cong \mathbb{Z}^i.$$

## Conclusion

The statement is **false**. While it holds when $U$ has finitely many connected
components (finitely many closed components are automatically open), it fails in
 general for open subsets with infinitely many connected components. The hypothe
sis that connected components of $X$ are open does not propagate to connected co
mponents of arbitrary open subsets, and the resulting sheaf of locally constant
functions can be a proper subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}^i$.
