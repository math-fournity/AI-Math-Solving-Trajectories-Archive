**Proof.**

Let $M$ be a smooth connected $n$-manifold with boundary ($n \geq 1$), second-co
untable and Hausdorff. Denote by $\operatorname{Int}(M)$ the interior and $\part

**Key local claim.** For every $p \in \partial M$, there exists a neighborhood $
U$ of $p$ in $M$ such that $U \cap \operatorname{Int}(M)$ is connected.

*Proof of claim.* Take a boundary chart $\varphi: U \to V \subset \mathbb{H}^n$
with $\varphi(p) \in \{x_n = 0\}$. By restricting, choose $V$ to be a convex ope
n set in $\mathbb{H}^n$ (e.g., a half-ball $\{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : |x - \varphi(
p)| < \varepsilon,\; x_n \geq 0\}$). Then $V \cap \{x_n > 0\}$ is convex (inters
ection of convex sets), hence connected. Since $U \cap \operatorname{Int}(M) = \
varphi^{-1}(V \cap \{x_n > 0\})$, it is connected. $\square$

Note this holds for $n=1$ as well: a half-ball in $\mathbb{H}^1 = [0,\infty)$ ar
ound $0$ is $[0,\varepsilon)$, and $(0,\varepsilon)$ is connected.

**Main argument.** Suppose for contradiction that $\operatorname{Int}(M)$ is dis
connected:
$$\operatorname{Int}(M) = A \sqcup B, \quad A, B \neq \emptyset,$$
where $A, B$ are open in $\operatorname{Int}(M)$, hence open in $M$ (since $\ope
ratorname{Int}(M)$ is open in $M$).

For each $p \in \partial M$, by the claim there is a neighborhood $U_p$ with $U_
p \cap \operatorname{Int}(M)$ connected. Since $U_p \cap \operatorname{Int}(M) \
subset A \cup B$ and is connected, it lies entirely in $A$ or entirely in $B$. D
efine:
$$\partial M_A = \{p \in \partial M : U_p \cap \operatorname{Int}(M) \subset A \
text{ for some } U_p\},$$
$$\partial M_B = \{p \in \partial M : U_p \cap \operatorname{Int}(M) \subset B \
text{ for some } U_p\}.$$

This is well-defined (a connected set can't lie in both $A$ and $B$ since $A \ca
p B = \emptyset$), and $\partial M = \partial M_A \sqcup \partial M_B$.

**$\partial M_A$ and $\partial M_B$ are open in $\partial M$.** If $p \in \parti
al M_A$ with witness $U_p$, then for any $q \in U_p \cap \partial M$, choose a c
onvex-chart neighborhood $W_q \subset U_p$ of $q$ with $W_q \cap \operatorname{I
nt}(M)$ connected. Then $W_q \cap \operatorname{Int}(M) \subset U_p \cap \operat
orname{Int}(M) \subset A$, so $q \in \partial M_A$. Hence $U_p \cap \partial M \
subset \partial M_A$, proving openness. Similarly for $\partial M_B$.

**Define $A' = A \cup \partial M_A$ and $B' = B \cup \partial M_B$.** Then:
- $A' \cap B' = \emptyset$ (since $A \cap B = \emptyset$, $A \cap \partial M = \
emptyset$, $\partial M \cap B = \emptyset$, and $\partial M_A \cap \partial M_B
= \emptyset$).
- $A' \cup B' = \operatorname{Int}(M) \cup \partial M = M$.
- $A' \neq \emptyset$ (contains $A$) and $B' \neq \emptyset$ (contains $B$).

**$A'$ and $B'$ are open in $M$.** For $p \in A'$: if $p \in A$, use that $A$ is
 open. If $p \in \partial M_A$ with witness $U_p$, the argument above shows $U_p
 \subset A \cup \partial M_A = A'$. So $A'$ is open; similarly $B'$ is open.

Thus $A', B'$ form a separation of $M$, contradicting the connectedness of $M$.
$\square$

well-behaved manifold (paracompact, metrizable). The essential ingredients are:
connectedness of $M$, and the local connectedness of the interior near boundary
points, which follows from the half-space structure of charts and holds in all d
imensions $n \geq 1$.
