## Proof

**Setup and well-formedness.** Consider objects $A, B, C, D$ together with morph
isms
$$f: A \to B, \qquad g: B \to C, \qquad h: D \to C.$$
These are exactly the data required for all three fibred products appearing in t
he question:

- $B \times_C D$ is the pullback of $g: B \to C$ and $h: D \to C$, with projecti
ons $\pi_B: B \times_C D \to B$ and $\pi_D: B \times_C D \to D$ satisfying $g \c
irc \pi_B = h \circ \pi_D$.
- $A \times_B (B \times_C D)$ is the pullback of $f: A \to B$ and $\pi_B: B \tim
es_C D \to B$.
- $A \times_C D$ is the pullback of $g \circ f: A \to C$ and $h: D \to C$.

Note that $A \times_C (A \times_B D)$ is **not even well-defined** in general un
der the given data: forming $A \times_B D$ requires a morphism $D \to B$, but we
 are only given $D \to C$. No canonical lift $D \to B$ exists in general, so thi
s candidate is ruled out on type-theoretic grounds alone.

**Claim.** $A \times_B (B \times_C D) \cong A \times_C D$.

**Proof via the pullback pasting lemma.** Consider the diagram

$$
A \xleftarrow{\;\rho_A\;} A \times_B (B \times_C D) \xrightarrow{\;\rho_P\;} B \
times_C D \xrightarrow{\;\pi_B\;} B \xrightarrow{\;g\;} C,
$$

and also the projection $\rho_P$ followed by $\pi_D: B \times_C D \to D$:

$$
A \times_B (B \times_C D) \xrightarrow{\;\pi_D \circ \rho_P\;} D \xrightarrow{\;
h\;} C.
$$

We verify the two squares in the composite

$$
\begin{array}{ccccc}
A \times_B (B \times_C D) & \xrightarrow{\rho_P} & B \times_C D & \xrightarrow{\
pi_B} & B \\
\downarrow \rho_A & & \downarrow \pi_D & & \downarrow g \\
A & \xrightarrow{f} & D \xrightarrow{h} & C
\end{array}
$$

More precisely, the **right square** is the pullback square defining $B \times_C
 D$:
$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
B \times_C D & \xrightarrow{\pi_B} & B \\
\downarrow \pi_D & & \downarrow g \\
D & \xrightarrow{h} & C
\end{array}
$$
This is a pullback by construction.

The **left square** is the pullback square defining $A \times_B (B \times_C D)$:
$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
A \times_B (B \times_C D) & \xrightarrow{\rho_P} & B \times_C D \\
\downarrow \rho_A & & \downarrow \pi_B \\
A & \xrightarrow{f} & B
\end{array}
$$
This is a pullback by construction.

**Pullback pasting lemma.** *If the right square is a pullback, then the left sq
uare is a pullback if and only if the outer (composite) square is a pullback.*

Since both the right square and the left square are pullbacks, the **outer squar
e** is a pullback:
$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
A \times_B (B \times_C D) & \xrightarrow{\pi_D \circ \rho_P} & D \\
\downarrow \rho_A & & \downarrow h \\
A & \xrightarrow{g \circ f} & C
\end{array}
$$

Indeed, the outer horizontal composite is $\pi_B \circ \rho_P$, and the commutat
ivity $g \circ (\pi_B \circ \rho_P) = h \circ (\pi_D \circ \rho_P)$ follows from
 chaining:
$$g \circ \pi_B \circ \rho_P = h \circ \pi_D \circ \rho_P,$$
which holds because $g \circ \pi_B = h \circ \pi_D$ in the right pullback.

**Uniqueness up to unique isomorphism.** The outer square being a pullback of $g
 \circ f: A \to C$ along $h: D \to C$ means that $A \times_B (B \times_C D)$, eq
uipped with the projections $\rho_A$ and $\pi_D \circ \rho_P$, satisfies the uni
versal property of $A \times_C D$. Since pullbacks are unique up to unique isomo
rphism, there exists a unique isomorphism
$$
\varphi: A \times_B (B \times_C D) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} A \times_C D
$$
commuting with the projections to $A$ and to $D$:
$$
p_A \circ \varphi = \rho_A, \qquad p_D \circ \varphi = \pi_D \circ \rho_P,
$$
where $p_A: A \times_C D \to A$ and $p_D: A \times_C D \to D$ are the canonical
projections.

**Explicit description of $\varphi$ (concrete construction).** To make the isomo
rphism tangible, we construct $\varphi$ and its inverse directly from the univer
sal properties.

*Construction of $\varphi$.* An element of $A \times_B (B \times_C D)$ is a pair
 $(a, (b, d))$ with $f(a) = b$, $g(b) = h(d)$. Then $g(f(a)) = g(b) = h(d)$, so
$(a, d)$ satisfies the pullback condition for $A \times_C D$. Define
$$\varphi(a, (b,d)) = (a, d).$$

*Construction of $\varphi^{-1}$.* An element of $A \times_C D$ is a pair $(a, d)
$ with $g(f(a)) = h(d)$. Set $b = f(a)$; then $g(b) = g(f(a)) = h(d)$, so $(b, d
) \in B \times_C D$, and $f(a) = b$, so $(a, (b, d)) \in A \times_B (B \times_C
D)$. Define
$$\varphi^{-1}(a, d) = (a, (f(a), d)).$$

*Verification.* These are mutually inverse:
$$\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(a,d)) = \varphi(a, (f(a), d)) = (a, d),$$
$$\varphi^{-1}(\varphi(a, (b,d))) = \varphi^{-1}(a, d) = (a, (f(a), d)) = (a, (b
, d)),$$
where the last equality uses $f(a) = b$. Both maps are morphisms by the universa
l property (they are the unique maps induced by the respective pullback universa
l properties), and they are mutual inverses, hence $\varphi$ is an isomorphism.

**Conclusion.** The correct isomorphism is
$$
\boxed{\, A \times_B (B \times_C D) \;\cong\; A \times_C D \,}
$$
given by $(a, (b, d)) \mapsto (a, d)$, with inverse $(a, d) \mapsto (a, (f(a), d
))$. The alternative $A \times_C (A \times_B D)$ is not even well-formed in gene
ral, since it requires a morphism $D \to B$ that is not part of the given data.
