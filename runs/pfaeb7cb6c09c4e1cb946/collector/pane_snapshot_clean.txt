## Proof

**Claim.** The perimeter (i.e. the one‑dimensional length / 1‑dimensional Hausdo
rff measure of the boundary) of the Mandelbrot set $\mathcal M$ is **infinite**.

We use one standard ingredient from complex dynamics and one standard fact from
geometric measure theory.

### 1. Setup and definitions

Let $\mathcal M\subset\mathbb C$ be the Mandelbrot set,
$$
\mathcal M=\Bigl\{c\in\mathbb C:\text{ the orbit }0,\;f_c(0),\;f_c^{\circ 2}(0),
\dots\text{ is bounded}\Bigr\},\qquad f_c(z)=z^2+c.
$$
Its boundary is denoted $\partial\mathcal M$. The *perimeter* of $\mathcal M$ is
 the length of its boundary, which for a planar compact set is precisely its **1
‑dimensional Hausdorff measure** $\mathcal H^1(\partial\mathcal M)$ (with the co
nvention that a non‑rectifiable boundary has infinite length).

### 2. Geometric‑measure‑theory fact

For any set $E\subset\mathbb R^n$ with Hausdorff dimension $\dim_H E=d$, the Hau
sdorff measures satisfy
$$
\mathcal H^s(E)=\infty\ \text{for every }s<d,\qquad \mathcal H^s(E)=0\ \text{for
 every }s>d.
$$
This is the defining threshold behaviour of Hausdorff measure at the critical ex
ponent $s=\dim_H E$.

### 3. Shishikura's theorem

M. Shishikura (1998) proved:

> **Theorem (Shishikura).** The Hausdorff dimension of the boundary of the Mande
lbrot set equals $2$:
> $$\dim_H(\partial\mathcal M)=2.$$

(The same paper shows the boundary of a generic quadratic Julia set has dimensio
n $2$; the Mandelbrot‑boundary statement is the parameter‑space analogue. The lo
wer bound $\dim_H\ge 2$ comes from exhibiting, near every parabolic parameter, a
 family of baby Mandelbrot copies whose boundaries accumulate and force dimensio
n $2$; the upper bound $\dim_H\le 2$ is automatic since $\partial\mathcal M\subs
et\mathbb C\cong\mathbb R^2$.)

### 4. Conclusion

Apply the threshold behaviour of §2 with $E=\partial\mathcal M$ and $d=2$. Takin
g $s=1<2=\dim_H(\partial\mathcal M)$,
$$
\mathcal H^1(\partial\mathcal M)=\infty.
$$
Since the perimeter of $\mathcal M$ is exactly $\mathcal H^1(\partial\mathcal M)
$, the perimeter is **infinite**.

### 5. Remark on why this is the right notion of "perimeter"

One might worry that "perimeter" only makes sense for rectifiable boundaries. Th
at worry cuts in the same direction: a rectifiable boundary has finite $\mathcal
 H^1$, so Shishikura's result ($\mathcal H^1=\infty$) shows the boundary is **no
n‑rectifiable**, and a non‑rectifiable boundary is, by the standard convention,
declared to have infinite length. Under either interpretation — literal $\mathca
l H^1$ measure, or the rectifiable/non‑rectifiable dichotomy — the answer is the
 same.

Therefore the perimeter of the Mandelbrot set is **infinite**.
