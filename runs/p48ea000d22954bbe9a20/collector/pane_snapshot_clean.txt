## Setup

Place $B = (0,0)$, $C = (1,0)$, $A = (x, y)$ with $y > 0$. The triangle $ABC$ is
 counterclockwise. The acute conditions are:
- Angle at $B$ acute: $x > 0$
- Angle at $C$ acute: $x < 1$
- Angle at $A$ acute: $y^2 > x(1-x)$

## Centers of external squares

For a counterclockwise triangle, the exterior of each edge is to the right (rota
tion by $-90°$). The center of an external square on a segment is the midpoint p
lus a perpendicular offset of half the side length.

**Square on $BC$** (direction $C-B = (1,0)$, rotate $-90°$: $(0,-1)$):
$$M = \left(\tfrac{1}{2}, -\tfrac{1}{2}\right)$$

**Square on $CA$** (direction $A-C = (x\!-\!1, y)$, rotate $-90°$: $(y, 1\!-\!x)
$):
$$N = \left(\tfrac{1+x+y}{2},\; \tfrac{y+1-x}{2}\right)$$

**Square on $AB$** (direction $B-A = (-x,-y)$, rotate $-90°$: $(-y, x)$):
$$P = \left(\tfrac{x-y}{2},\; \tfrac{y+x}{2}\right)$$

## Area computation

$$\vec{NM} = N - M = \left(\tfrac{x+y}{2},\; \tfrac{y+2-x}{2}\right), \quad \vec
{PM} = P - M = \left(\tfrac{x-y-1}{2},\; \tfrac{y+x+1}{2}\right)$$

$$\det(\vec{NM}, \vec{PM}) = \tfrac{(x+y)(y+x+1) - (y+2-x)(x-y-1)}{4}$$

Expanding both products:
- $(x+y)(x+y+1) = x^2 + 2xy + y^2 + x + y$
- $(y+2-x)(x-y-1) = -x^2 + 2xy + 3x - y^2 - 3y - 2$

Their difference: $2x^2 + 2y^2 - 2x + 4y + 2 = 2(x^2 + y^2 - x + 2y + 1)$.

Since $x^2 + y^2 - x + 2y + 1 = (x-\tfrac12)^2 + (y+1)^2 - \tfrac14 > 0$ (as $(y
+1)^2 \geq 1$), the area is:

$$[MNP] = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\tfrac{2(x^2+y^2-x+2y+1)}{4} = \tfrac{x^2+y^2-x+2y+1}
{4}$$

The area of $ABC$ is $[ABC] = \tfrac{y}{2}$, so the ratio is:

$$f(x,y) = \frac{x^2 + y^2 - x + 2y + 1}{2y}$$

## Minimization

**Step 1: Minimize over $x$ for fixed $y$.** The numerator is $(x - \tfrac12)^2
+ (y^2 + 2y + \tfrac34)$, minimized at $x = \tfrac12$. This point is feasible (s
atisfies $y^2 > x(1-x) = \tfrac14$) precisely when $y > \tfrac12$.

- **Case $y > \tfrac12$:** Set $x = \tfrac12$, giving $g(y) = \tfrac{y^2 + 2y +
3/4}{2y} = \tfrac{y}{2} + 1 + \tfrac{3}{8y}$.

  $g'(y) = \tfrac12 - \tfrac{3}{8y^2} = 0 \implies y^2 = \tfrac34 \implies y = \
tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} \approx 0.866 > \tfrac12$. ✓

  $$g\!\left(\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\right) = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{4} + 1 + \tfrac{\sqrt3}{4
} = 1 + \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} = \tfrac{2+\sqrt3}{2}$$

- **Case $y \le \tfrac12$:** The constraint $y^2 > x(1-x)$ is binding, forcing $
x$ away from $\tfrac12$. On the boundary $y^2 = x(1-x)$ (right angle at $A$), th
e ratio becomes $\frac{2y+1}{2y} = 1 + \frac{1}{2y} \ge 1 + 1 = 2$, since $y \le
 \tfrac12$. This exceeds $\tfrac{2+\sqrt3}{2} \approx 1.866$.

**Step 2: Check other boundaries.**
- $x \to 0^+$ (angle $B \to 90°$): $f = \frac{(y+1)^2}{2y} = \frac{y}{2} + 1 + \
frac{1}{2y}$, minimized at $y=1$ with value $2$.
- $x \to 1^-$ (angle $C \to 90°$): by symmetry, minimum value $2$.
- $y \to 0^+$ (degenerate): $f \to \infty$ since $x^2 - x + 1 > 0$ always.

All boundary values are $\geq 2 > \frac{2+\sqrt3}{2}$.

## Conclusion

The global minimum of the ratio $\frac{[MNP]}{[ABC]}$ over all acute triangles i
s $\frac{2+\sqrt3}{2}$, achieved uniquely at the equilateral triangle ($x = \tfr
ac12$, $y = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$).

$$\boxed{k = \frac{2+\sqrt{3}}{2}}$$
