## Setup and the min–max values

Let $H\ge 0$ be self-adjoint on $\mathcal H$ and let $Q(H)=D(H^{1/2})$ be its fo
rm domain. The numbers $\lambda_n$ in (12.12) are the **min–max (Courant–Fischer
) values**:

$$
\lambda_n \;=\; \inf_{\substack{M\subset Q(H)\\ \dim M=n}}\;\sup_{\substack{\psi
\in M\\ \|\psi\|=1}} \langle \psi,H\psi\rangle ,\qquad n=1,2,\dots
$$

(equivalently $\lambda_n=\sup_{\phi_1,\dots,\phi_{n-1}}\inf_{\psi\perp\phi_i,\,\
|\psi\|=1}\langle\psi,H\psi\rangle$). The sequence is non-decreasing, $\lambda_1
\le\lambda_2\le\cdots$, and $0\le\lambda_n\le\infty$. Since $Q(H)$ is dense in $
\mathcal H$, for every $n$ there exist $n$-dimensional subspaces of $Q(H)$, and
on each such subspace the quadratic form is a bounded finite-dimensional form; h
ence $\lambda_n<\infty$ for all $n$ whenever $\mathcal H$ is infinite-dimensiona
l. Thus the only quantity to be classified is the limit

$$
\lambda_\infty \;:=\; \lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_n\;\in\;[0,\infty].
$$

The min–max principle identifies $\lambda_n$ with the $n$-th eigenvalue of $H$ c
ounted from below **as long as it lies below the essential spectrum**, and ident
ifies $\lambda_\infty$ with the bottom of the essential spectrum:

$$
\lambda_\infty \;=\; \inf\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H),
$$

with the convention $\inf\varnothing=+\infty$.

## The three cases

### Case 1 — $\lambda_n\to\infty$ (purely discrete spectrum)

Here $\lambda_\infty=\infty$, so $\inf\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H)=\infty$, i.e.

$$
\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H)=\varnothing .
$$

The spectrum of $H$ consists entirely of isolated eigenvalues of finite multipli
city. Concretely, $\lambda_n$ is exactly the $n$-th eigenvalue of $H$ (counted w
ith multiplicity, in non-decreasing order):

$$
\sigma(H)=\sigma_{\mathrm{disc}}(H)=\{\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\cdots\},\qquad \l
ambda_n\to\infty .
$$

Equivalently, the resolvent $(H+1)^{-1}$ is compact (or $H$ has compact resolven
t). This is the situation of Schrödinger operators with confining potentials, th
e harmonic oscillator, the Dirichlet Laplacian on a bounded domain, etc.

### Case 2 — $\lambda_n\to\lambda_\infty<\infty$ with $\lambda_n<\lambda_\infty$
 for every $n$

Now $\lambda_\infty=\inf\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H)<\infty$ and the sequence approa
ches the threshold strictly from below. The min–max principle gives:

- Each $\lambda_n$ is an **eigenvalue of $H$ of finite multiplicity**, and these
 are *all* the eigenvalues strictly below $\lambda_\infty$ (counted with multipl
icity).
- These discrete eigenvalues can accumulate **only** at $\lambda_\infty$; there
is no accumulation point below $\lambda_\infty$.
- $\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H)\subset[\lambda_\infty,\infty)$ and $\inf\sigma_{\mat
hrm{ess}}(H)=\lambda_\infty$; in particular $\lambda_\infty\in\sigma_{\mathrm{es
s}}(H)$ (the essential spectrum is closed).
- Since every $\lambda_n<\lambda_\infty$, the threshold $\lambda_\infty$ is **no
t** an eigenvalue of infinite multiplicity; it belongs to the essential spectrum
 as a limit point (continuous or accumulation spectrum), not as an infinite-mult
iplicity eigenvalue.

This is the generic situation for one-particle Schrödinger operators with a pote
ntial decaying at infinity: finitely or infinitely many bound states below the i
onization threshold, with essential spectrum $[\lambda_\infty,\infty)$.

### Case 3 — $\lambda_n=\lambda_\infty<\infty$ for some (hence all sufficiently
large) $n$

The non-decreasing sequence becomes eventually constant at the finite value $\la
mbda_\infty$. By the min–max principle this happens precisely when the spectral
subspace below (and at) the threshold is infinite-dimensional in a way that forc
es the $n$-th min–max value to equal the threshold. Concretely:

- $\lambda_\infty=\inf\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H)$ as before.
- $\lambda_\infty$ is an **eigenvalue of infinite multiplicity** (this is exactl
y what makes the min–max values saturate at $\lambda_\infty$ rather than approac
hing it strictly).
- There are only **finitely many** eigenvalues (counted with multiplicity) stric
tly below $\lambda_\infty$; once $n$ exceeds their number, the $n$-th eigenvalue
 from below is $\lambda_\infty$ itself, repeated infinitely often.
- $\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H)\subset[\lambda_\infty,\infty)$ with $\inf\sigma_{\ma
thrm{ess}}(H)=\lambda_\infty$, and $\lambda_\infty$ is an embedded (threshold) e
igenvalue of infinite multiplicity.

This occurs, for example, for multi-particle Schrödinger operators where the bot
tom of the essential spectrum is reached by a cluster-decomposition channel with
 a zero-energy bound state, producing infinite multiplicity at the threshold.
