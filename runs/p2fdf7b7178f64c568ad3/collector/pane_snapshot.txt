## Proof

**Claim.** The space $F(\omega)=\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}$ of all real sequences,
equipped with
$$d(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\frac{|x_i-y_i|}{1+|x_i-y_i|},$$
is a topological vector space.

The strategy is: (1) show the metric topology of $d$ coincides with the product
topology on $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}$; (2) using the product topology, verify se
parately that vector addition and scalar multiplication are continuous.

---

### Step 1. The metric $d$ is well defined and induces the product topology

Each summand satisfies $0\le \frac{|x_i-y_i|}{1+|x_i-y_i|}<1$, so the series is
dominated by $\sum 2^{-i}=1$; hence $d$ is a well-defined metric (positivity, sy
mmetry, and the triangle inequality follow from the fact that $t\mapsto \frac{t}
{1+t}$ is increasing and subadditive on $[0,\infty)$, since $\frac{a+b}{1+a+b}\l
e \frac{a}{1+a}+\frac{b}{1+b}$).

Let $\tau_d$ be the metric topology and $\tau_{\pi}$ the product topology on $\m
athbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}$.

**$\tau_{\pi}\subseteq \tau_d$.** A basic $\tau_{\pi}$-neighborhood of $x$ has t
he form $U=\prod_i U_i$ with $U_i=\mathbb{R}$ for $i>N$ and $U_i=(x_i-r_i,\,x_i+
r_i)$ for $i\le N$. We exhibit $\varepsilon>0$ with $B_d(x,\varepsilon)\subseteq
 U$. For $y\in B_d(x,\varepsilon)$ and $i\le N$,
$$\frac{|y_i-x_i|}{1+|y_i-x_i|}\le 2^{i}\,d(x,y)<2^{i}\varepsilon.$$
Provided $2^{i}\varepsilon<1$, this gives $|y_i-x_i|<\frac{2^{i}\varepsilon}{1-2
^{i}\varepsilon}$. Choose
$$\varepsilon<\min\Bigl\{\,2^{-N},\ \min_{1\le i\le N}\frac{r_i}{2^{i}(r_i+1)}\B
igr\}.$$
Then $2^{i}\varepsilon<1$ and $\frac{2^{i}\varepsilon}{1-2^{i}\varepsilon}<r_i$
for every $i\le N$, so $y\in U$.

**$\tau_d\subseteq \tau_{\pi}$.** Given $B_d(x,\varepsilon)$, pick $N$ with $\su
m_{i>N}2^{-i}<\varepsilon/2$. For $i\le N$ choose $\delta_i>0$ with $\frac{1}{2^
{i}}\frac{\delta_i}{1+\delta_i}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2N}$. The product-open set $V
=\prod_i V_i$ with $V_i=(x_i-\delta_i,x_i+\delta_i)$ for $i\le N$ and $V_i=\math
bb{R}$ for $i>N$ satisfies, for every $y\in V$,
$$d(x,y)\le \sum_{i\le N}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\frac{\delta_i}{1+\delta_i}+\sum_{i>N}2^
{-i}< N\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{2N}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=\varepsilon.$$
Hence $V\subseteq B_d(x,\varepsilon)$.

Therefore $\tau_d=\tau_{\pi}$: **the metric topology is exactly the product topo
logy on $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}$.**

---

### Step 2. Vector addition is continuous

Equip $F(\omega)\times F(\omega)$ with the product topology (which, under the id
entification $F(\omega)\times F(\omega)\cong \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}\sqcup\mathbb
{N}}$, is again a product topology). A map into a product space is continuous if
f every coordinate projection is continuous. The $i$-th coordinate of the additi
on map $A:(x,y)\mapsto x+y$ is
$$A_i(x,y)=x_i+y_i,$$
which is the usual addition on $\mathbb{R}$, hence continuous. Since all coordin
ates are continuous, $A$ is continuous.

(Equivalently, one may note directly that $d(x+y,x'+y')\le d(x,x')+d(y,y')$ by t
he subadditivity noted above, so addition is even Lipschitz.)

---

### Step 3. Scalar multiplication is continuous

Equip $\mathbb{R}\times F(\omega)$ with the product topology. The $i$-th coordin
ate of the scalar multiplication map $M:(\lambda,x)\mapsto \lambda x$ is
$$M_i(\lambda,x)=\lambda\, x_i,$$
which is the usual scalar multiplication on $\mathbb{R}$, hence continuous. Sinc
e every coordinate is continuous and the codomain carries the product topology,
$M$ is continuous.

---

### Conclusion

$(F(\omega),d)$ is an abelian group under addition with a continuous addition op
eration and a continuous scalar multiplication $\mathbb{R}\times F(\omega)\to F(
\omega)$. Hence **$(F(\omega),d)$ is a topological vector space.**

(Moreover, since the topology is the product topology of the locally convex spac
es $\mathbb{R}$, it is locally convex; and it is complete — a $d$-Cauchy sequenc
e is coordinatewise Cauchy, hence converges coordinatewise, and the coordinatewi
se limit is the $d$-limit. Thus it is in fact a Fréchet space in the full sense,
 consistent with its name.)
