## Solution

We claim the critical value is

\[
\boxed{r=\sqrt{2}}.
\]

More precisely, the property "a.s. for all sufficiently large \(n\), \(M_n \le r
\sqrt{\log n}\)" holds for every \(r>\sqrt{2}\) and fails for every \(r\le \sqrt
{2}\). The threshold value requested is therefore \(r=\sqrt{2}\).

---

### Step 1. Reduction to an i.i.d. sequence

By the independent increments and stationarity of Brownian motion, the random va
riables

\[
M_n=\max_{n-1\le t\le n}|B_t-B_{n-1}|,\qquad n=1,2,\dots
\]

are i.i.d., each distributed as

\[
M:=\sup_{0\le t\le 1}|B_t|.
\]

Hence the question becomes a Borel–Cantelli question about the i.i.d. sequence \
((M_n)\) with threshold \(r\sqrt{\log n}\).

---

### Step 2. Tail asymptotics of \(M=\sup_{0\le t\le 1}|B_t|\)

By the reflection principle, for \(x\ge 0\),

\[
\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sup_{0\le t\le 1}B_t\ge x\right)=2\bigl(1-\Phi(x)\bigr),
\]

where \(\Phi\) is the standard normal cdf. By symmetry,

\[
\mathbb{P}\!\left(\inf_{0\le t\le 1}B_t\le -x\right)=2\bigl(1-\Phi(x)\bigr).
\]

Using a union bound and the trivial lower bound,

\[
2\bigl(1-\Phi(x)\bigr)\;\le\;\mathbb{P}(M\ge x)\;\le\;4\bigl(1-\Phi(x)\bigr).
\]

The standard Mills-ratio asymptotic gives, as \(x\to\infty\),

\[
1-\Phi(x)\sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\frac{e^{-x^2/2}}{x}.
\]

Consequently there exist constants \(0<c_1<c_2<\infty\) such that for all large
\(x\),

\[
c_1\,\frac{e^{-x^2/2}}{x}\;\le\;\mathbb{P}(M\ge x)\;\le\;c_2\,\frac{e^{-x^2/2}}{
x}.
\tag{1}
\]

---

### Step 3. The case \(r>\sqrt{2}\): the inequality eventually holds

Set \(x_n=r\sqrt{\log n}\). Then \(x_n^2/2=(r^2/2)\log n\), so by (1),

\[
\mathbb{P}(M_n\ge r\sqrt{\log n})
\;\le\; c_2\,\frac{n^{-r^2/2}}{\sqrt{\log n}}.
\]

Since \(r^2/2>1\),

\[
\sum_{n\ge 2}\mathbb{P}(M_n\ge r\sqrt{\log n})
\;\le\; c_2\sum_{n\ge 2}\frac{1}{n^{r^2/2}\sqrt{\log n}}
\;<\;\infty.
\]

By the **first Borel–Cantelli lemma** (no independence needed), with probability
 one only finitely many events \(\{M_n\ge r\sqrt{\log n}\}\) occur. Equivalently
, a.s. for all sufficiently large \(n\),

\[
M_n\le r\sqrt{\log n}.
\]

---

### Step 4. The case \(r<\sqrt{2}\): the inequality fails infinitely often

Now \(r^2/2<1\), and by the lower bound in (1),

\[
\mathbb{P}(M_n\ge r\sqrt{\log n})
\;\ge\; c_1\,\frac{n^{-r^2/2}}{\sqrt{\log n}}.
\]

Since \(r^2/2<1\),

\[
\sum_{n\ge 2}\mathbb{P}(M_n\ge r\sqrt{\log n})
\;\ge\; c_1\sum_{n\ge 2}\frac{1}{n^{r^2/2}\sqrt{\log n}}
\;=\;\infty.
\]

Because the \(M_n\) are **independent**, the **second Borel–Cantelli lemma** app
lies: a.s. infinitely many events \(\{M_n\ge r\sqrt{\log n}\}\) occur. Hence the
 inequality \(M_n\le r\sqrt{\log n}\) fails infinitely often, so the desired pro
perty does **not** hold.

---

### Step 5. The borderline case \(r=\sqrt{2}\): the inequality also fails

Here \(r^2/2=1\), and by (1),

\[
\mathbb{P}(M_n\ge \sqrt{2\log n})
\;\asymp\;\frac{1}{n\sqrt{\log n}}.
\]

The series

\[
\sum_{n\ge 2}\frac{1}{n\sqrt{\log n}}
\]

diverges, since \(\displaystyle\int_2^\infty \frac{dx}{x\sqrt{\log x}}=\Bigl[2\s
qrt{\log x}\Bigr]_2^\infty=\infty.\)

By independence and the second Borel–Cantelli lemma, a.s. infinitely many events
 \(\{M_n\ge\sqrt{2\log n}\}\) occur. Thus the property fails at \(r=\sqrt{2}\) a
s well.

---

### Step 6. Conclusion

Combining Steps 3–5:

\[
\mathbb{P}\!\left(\exists N\;\forall n\ge N:\; M_n\le r\sqrt{\log n}\right)=
\begin{cases}
1, & r>\sqrt{2},\\[4pt]
0, & r\le\sqrt{2}.
\end{cases}
\]

The threshold (critical) value of \(r\) is therefore

\[
\boxed{\,r=\sqrt{2}\,}.
\]
