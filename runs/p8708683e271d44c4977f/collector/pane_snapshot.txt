## Solution

Let $\Phi$ be a homogeneous PPP of intensity $\lambda$ on $\mathbb{R}$, and let
$0 < x_0 < x_1 < \dots < x_k$ be the first $k+1$ points in $\mathbb{R}^+$.

**Joint density of $(x_0, x_k)$.** For a PPP on $\mathbb{R}^+$, the joint densit
y of the first $k+1$ ordered points is
$$f(a_0,\dots,a_k) = \lambda^{k+1} e^{-\lambda a_k}, \quad 0 < a_0 < a_1 < \dots
 < a_k.$$
Integrating out the intermediate points $a_1,\dots,a_{k-1}$ over the simplex $\{
a_0 < a_1 < \dots < a_{k-1} < a_k\}$, whose volume is $(a_k - a_0)^{k-1}/(k-1)!$
, gives the joint density of the extremes:
$$f_{x_0,x_k}(a,b) = \frac{\lambda^{k+1}}{(k-1)!}\, e^{-\lambda b}\,(b-a)^{k-1},
 \quad 0 < a < b.$$

**Computing the CCDF.** For $t \in (0,1)$,
$$\mathbb{P}\!\left(\frac{x_0}{x_k} > t\right) = \mathbb{P}(x_0 > t\,x_k) = \int
_0^\infty \int_{tb}^{b} \frac{\lambda^{k+1}}{(k-1)!}\, e^{-\lambda b}\,(b-a)^{k-
1}\, da\, db.$$
Substituting $u = b - a$, the inner integral becomes
$$\int_0^{b(1-t)} \frac{u^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\, du = \frac{[b(1-t)]^k}{k!}.$$
Therefore
$$\mathbb{P}\!\left(\frac{x_0}{x_k} > t\right) = (1-t)^k \int_0^\infty \frac{\la
mbda^{k+1}\, b^k\, e^{-\lambda b}}{k!}\, db.$$
The remaining integral is exactly the total mass of the $\text{Erlang}(k+1,\lamb
da)$ density $f_{x_k}(b) = \frac{\lambda^{k+1} b^k}{k!}e^{-\lambda b}$, which eq
uals $1$. Hence
$$\boxed{\mathbb{P}\!\left(\frac{x_0}{x_k} > t\right) = (1-t)^k, \quad 0 \le t \
le 1.}$$

**Remarks.**
- For $t < 0$ the CCDF is $1$ (since the ratio is positive), and for $t > 1$ it
is $0$ (since $x_0 < x_k$).
- The intensity $\lambda$ **cancels out**: the ratio $x_0/x_k$ is scale-invarian
t, so its distribution depends only on $k$. This is also seen directly from the
Palm/conditioning characterization — given $x_k$, the points $x_0,\dots,x_{k-1}$
 are distributed as the order statistics of $k$ i.i.d. $\mathrm{Uniform}(0,x_k)$
 variables, so $x_0/x_k \mid x_k$ is the minimum of $k$ i.i.d. $\mathrm{Uniform}
(0,1)$ variables, whose CCDF is $(1-t)^k$, independent of $x_k$ and hence of $\l
ambda$.
