## Proof

We work in a coordinate basis. Let $\tilde{A} = A_\nu \tilde{e}^\nu$ be a one-fo
rm field. The covariant derivative of a one-form is defined by requiring compati
bility with the contraction: for any vector field $V = V^\lambda \tilde{e}_\lamb
da$,

$$\nabla_\mu (A_\nu V^\nu) = (\nabla_\mu A_\nu) V^\nu + A_\nu (\nabla_\mu V^\nu)
.$$

Since $A_\nu V^\nu$ is a scalar field, its covariant derivative equals its parti
al derivative:

$$\nabla_\mu (A_\nu V^\nu) = \partial_\mu (A_\nu V^\nu) = (\partial_\mu A_\nu) V
^\nu + A_\nu (\partial_\mu V^\nu).$$

For the vector field, the covariant derivative of its components is

$$\nabla_\mu V^\nu = \partial_\mu V^\nu + \Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda} V^\lambda.$$

Substituting both into the Leibniz rule:

$$(\partial_\mu A_\nu) V^\nu + A_\nu (\partial_\mu V^\nu) = (\nabla_\mu A_\nu) V
^\nu + A_\nu \bigl(\partial_\mu V^\nu + \Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda} V^\lambda\bigr).
$$

Cancel the common term $A_\nu \partial_\mu V^\nu$ from both sides:

$$(\partial_\mu A_\nu) V^\nu = (\nabla_\mu A_\nu) V^\nu + A_\nu \Gamma^\nu_{\mu\
lambda} V^\lambda.$$

Relabel the dummy index in the last term ($\nu \leftrightarrow \lambda$):

$$(\partial_\mu A_\nu) V^\nu = (\nabla_\mu A_\nu) V^\nu + A_\lambda \Gamma^\lamb
da_{\mu\nu} V^\nu.$$

Since this holds for **arbitrary** $V^\nu$, the coefficients of $V^\nu$ must mat
ch:

$$\partial_\mu A_\nu = \nabla_\mu A_\nu + \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} A_\lambda.$$

Solving for the covariant derivative of the one-form components:

$$\boxed{\nabla_\mu A_\nu = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}\, A_\la
mbda.}$$

### Interpretation

- For a **vector** (contravariant), the Christoffel term enters with a **plus**
sign: $\nabla_\mu V^\nu = \partial_\mu V^\nu + \Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda} V^\lambda
$.
- For a **one-form** (covariant), the Christoffel term enters with a **minus** s
ign, and the contracted index is the **upper** index of $\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}
$, with the lower indices of $\Gamma$ being the derivative index $\mu$ and the o
ne-form index $\nu$.

This sign reversal is required so that the covariant derivative commutes with co
ntraction (i.e., so the Leibniz rule holds with $\nabla_\mu$ reducing to $\parti
al_\mu$ on scalars), which is precisely the compatibility condition we imposed a
t the outset.
