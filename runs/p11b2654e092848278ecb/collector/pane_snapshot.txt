## Proof

**Claim.** The derivative $f'(x_0) : X \to X$ is a compact linear operator. Cons
equently, for every bounded sequence $\{y_n\}\subset X$, the sequence $\{f'(x_0)
y_n\}$ possesses a convergent subsequence. The full sequence $\{f'(x_0)y_n\}$ ne
ed not converge; the limit "as $n\to\infty$" exists only along a convergent subs
equence, and its value is determined by compactness of $f'(x_0)$.

---

**Step 1. $f'(x_0)$ is a bounded (continuous) linear operator.**

By definition of the Fréchet derivative, $f'(x_0)\in \mathcal{L}(X,X)$: there ex
ists a bounded linear operator $A := f'(x_0)$ and a mapping $r$ with $r(x_0,h)/\
|h\|\to 0$ as $h\to 0$ such that
$$f(x_0 + h) = f(x_0) + Ah + r(x_0,h), \qquad \lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\|r(x_0,h)\|}{\
|h\|}=0.$$
So $A=f'(x_0)$ is bounded linear.

**Step 2. $f'(x_0)$ is compact.**

We show $A = f'(x_0)$ maps the unit ball $B = \{h : \|h\|\le 1\}$ into a relativ
ely compact set. Let $\{h_n\}\subset B$ be any sequence; we must extract a conve
rgent subsequence of $\{Ah_n\}$.

Since $f$ is compact and $B$ is bounded, $\overline{f(x_0 + tB)}$ is compact for
 every $t>0$ with $x_0 + tB \subset G$ (true for small $t$ since $G$ is open). F
ix such $t\in (0,1]$. Then $\{f(x_0 + t h_n)\}$ has a convergent subsequence; re
label it $\{f(x_0 + t h_n)\}$ converging to some $z\in X$.

Using the differentiability expansion with $h = t h_n$:
$$f(x_0 + t h_n) = f(x_0) + A(t h_n) + r(x_0, t h_n) = f(x_0) + t\,A h_n + r(x_0
, t h_n).$$

Hence
$$A h_n = \frac{f(x_0 + t h_n) - f(x_0)}{t} - \frac{r(x_0, t h_n)}{t}.$$

The first term converges (to $(z - f(x_0))/t$) along the convergent subsequence.
 For the remainder, since $\|t h_n\| = t\|h_n\|\le t$ and $r(x_0,h)/\|h\|\to 0$:
$$\left\|\frac{r(x_0, t h_n)}{t}\right\| = \frac{\|r(x_0, t h_n)\|}{\|t h_n\|}\c
dot\|h_n\| \le \frac{\|r(x_0, t h_n)\|}{\|t h_n\|}\cdot 1 \;\xrightarrow{n\to\in
fty}\; 0.$$

Therefore $\{A h_n\}$ converges along this subsequence. Since $\{h_n\}\subset B$
 was arbitrary, $A(B)$ is relatively compact, i.e. **$A = f'(x_0)$ is a compact
linear operator.**

**Step 3. Consequence for $\{f'(x_0)y_n\}$.**

Let $\{y_n\}$ be bounded, say $\|y_n\|\le M$. Then $\{y_n/M\}\subset B$, and by
Step 2, $\{A(y_n/M)\}$ has a convergent subsequence. Scaling by $M$, $\{A y_n\}
= \{f'(x_0)y_n\}$ has a convergent subsequence:
$$\exists\, \{n_k\},\ \exists\, L\in X:\quad f'(x_0)\,y_{n_k}\xrightarrow{k\to\i
nfty} L.$$

The limit $L$ depends on the chosen subsequence; without additional hypotheses o
n $\{y_n\}$ (e.g. convergence of $y_n$ itself) the full sequence $\{f'(x_0)y_n\}
$ need not converge.

---

**Conclusion.** Because $f$ is compact and differentiable at $x_0$, its derivati
ve $f'(x_0)$ is a **compact linear operator**. Therefore, for every bounded sequ
ence $\{y_n\}\subset X$,

$$\boxed{\;\{f'(x_0)\,y_n\}\ \text{has a convergent subsequence.}\;}$$

Equivalently, $f'(x_0)$ maps bounded sets to relatively compact sets, so $\overl
ine{f'(x_0)(\{y_n\})}$ is compact. The limit of $\{f'(x_0)y_n\}$ as $n\to\infty$
 exists precisely along a convergent subsequence, and this sequential-compactnes
s property is exactly the compactness of $f'(x_0)$.
