## Proof

**Claim:** The boundary of an open ball in \(\mathbb{R}^n\) is **not** homeomorp
hic to \(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\) (for every \(n \geq 1\)).

---

### Setup

Let \(B = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : \|x\| < r\}\) be an open ball of radius \(r > 0
\). Its boundary (in \(\mathbb{R}^n\)) is

\[
\partial B = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : \|x\| = r\} \cong S^{n-1},
\]

the \((n-1)\)-sphere of radius \(r\), which is homeomorphic to the unit sphere \
(S^{n-1}\) via scaling.

We treat two cases.

---

### Case 1: \(n \geq 2\)

**Compactness is a topological invariant.** A homeomorphism is in particular a c
ontinuous bijection with continuous inverse, and the continuous image of a compa
ct set is compact; hence compactness is preserved by homeomorphism.

- \(S^{n-1}\) is **compact**: it is closed (preimage of \(\{r\}\) under the cont
inuous map \(x \mapsto \|x\|\)) and bounded in \(\mathbb{R}^n\), hence compact b
y the Heine–Borel theorem.
- \(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\) is **not compact**: it is not bounded (e.g., the sequence
 \(k \cdot e_1\) has no convergent subsequence), so by Heine–Borel it is not com
pact. Equivalently, the open cover \(\{B(0,k)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\) has no finite s
ubcover.

Since \(S^{n-1}\) is compact and \(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\) is not, they cannot be hom
eomorphic.

---

### Case 2: \(n = 1\)

An open ball in \(\mathbb{R}\) is an open interval \((a, b)\). Its boundary is t
he two-point set \(\{a, b\}\). Meanwhile \(\mathbb{R}^{0}\) is a single point. A
 homeomorphism is in particular a bijection, but \(|\{a,b\}| = 2 \neq 1 = |\math
bb{R}^0|\), so no bijection (hence no homeomorphism) exists.

---

### Conclusion

In every dimension \(n \geq 1\), the boundary of an open ball in \(\mathbb{R}^n\
) fails to be homeomorphic to \(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\):

- For \(n \geq 2\), by the compactness invariant (\(S^{n-1}\) compact, \(\mathbb
{R}^{n-1}\) non-compact).
- For \(n = 1\), by cardinality (2 points vs. 1 point).

**The answer is no.**
