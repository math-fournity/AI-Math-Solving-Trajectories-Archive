## Proof

We are given the block matrix
$$X = \begin{bmatrix} A & B \\ B^T & C \end{bmatrix}$$
with $A, B, C \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ (the argument works over $\mathbb{R}$
 or $\mathbb{C}$), and the constraint $\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \gamma$.

**Claim.** $\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \gamma$, i.e. we may take $\phi = \gamma$.

**Proof.** Recall the operator norm (spectral norm) characterization:
$$\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \sup_{\|z\|=1} \|Xz\|,$$
so the constraint $\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \gamma$ means
$$\|Xz\|^2 \le \gamma^2 \|z\|^2 \quad \text{for all } z \in \mathbb{R}^{2n}.$$

Write any $z \in \mathbb{R}^{2n}$ as $z = \begin{pmatrix} z_1 \\ z_2 \end{pmatri
x}$ with $z_1, z_2 \in \mathbb{R}^n$. Then
$$Xz = \begin{pmatrix} Az_1 + Bz_2 \\ B^T z_1 + Cz_2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \|Xz\
|^2 = \|Az_1 + Bz_2\|^2 + \|B^T z_1 + Cz_2\|^2.$$

Now specialize to vectors of the form $z = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ v \end{pmatrix}$
, where $0 \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the zero vector and $v \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is a un
it vector ($\|v\| = 1$). For such $z$ we have $\|z\| = \|v\| = 1$, and
$$Xz = \begin{pmatrix} A\cdot 0 + Bv \\ B^T \cdot 0 + Cv \end{pmatrix} = \begin{
pmatrix} Bv \\ Cv \end{pmatrix}.$$

Applying the norm bound:
$$\|Bv\|^2 + \|Cv\|^2 = \|Xz\|^2 \le \gamma^2 \|z\|^2 = \gamma^2.$$

Since $\|Cv\|^2 \ge 0$, we immediately obtain
$$\|Bv\|^2 \le \gamma^2 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \|Bv\| \le \gamma$$

for every unit vector $v \in \mathbb{R}^n$. Taking the supremum over all unit ve
ctors $v$:
$$\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \sup_{\|v\|=1} \|Bv\| \le \gamma.$$

**Tightness.** This bound is sharp. Take $A = C = 0$ and $B = \gamma \, I_n$. Th
en
$$X = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & \gamma I \\ \gamma I & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \gamma \begin
{bmatrix} 0 & I \\ I & 0 \end{bmatrix}.$$
The matrix $\begin{bmatrix} 0 & I \\ I & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ is symmetric with eige
nvalues $\pm 1$ (eigenvectors $(u, \pm u)/\sqrt{2}$), so $\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}} =
\gamma$ and $\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \gamma$. Thus the bound is attained.

**Conclusion.** The optimal upper bound is
$$\boxed{\phi = \gamma}.$$
