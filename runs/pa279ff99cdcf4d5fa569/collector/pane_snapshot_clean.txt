## Proof

Let $f(x)=x^n+(2+x)^n+(2-x)^n$. We seek all natural $n$ for which $f(x)=0$ has a
n integral solution.

**Answer leak check:** The problem statement contains no solution, hint, or form
alization note. Proceeding.

---

### Step 1. Even $n$ never works.

If $n$ is even, each of $x^n,(2+x)^n,(2-x)^n$ is $\ge 0$, so $f(x)=0$ forces all
 three to vanish simultaneously: $x=0$, $x=-2$, $x=2$ — impossible. Hence **no e
ven $n$** admits a root (real or integral).

---

### Step 2. Reduction for odd $n$.

For odd $n$, $(2-x)^n=-(x-2)^n$, so the equation becomes
$$x^n+(x+2)^n=(x-2)^n. \tag{1}$$

**Modulo 2:** $(x+2)\equiv x\equiv (x-2)\pmod 2$, so (1) gives $2x^n\equiv x^n\p
mod 2$, i.e. $x^n\equiv 0\pmod 2$. Thus **$x$ is even**; write $x=2z$. Dividing
(1) by $2^n$:
$$z^n+(z+1)^n=(z-1)^n. \tag{2}$$

Since $z-1<z<z+1$, a sign/size check rules out $z\ge 2$ (then $(z-1)^n<(z+1)^n<z
^n+(z+1)^n$), $z=1$ ($1+2^n=0$), $z=0$ ($1=-1$), $z=-1$ ($-1=-2^n$). Hence $z\le
 -2$; put $w=-z\ge 2$. Using $n$ odd, (2) becomes
$$w^n+(w-1)^n=(w+1)^n,\qquad w\ge 2. \tag{3}$$

---

### Step 3. $n=1$ works.

For $n=1$: (3) reads $w+(w-1)=w+1$, so $w=2$, giving $z=-2$ and $x=2z=-4$. Check
: $(-4)+(2-4)+(2+4)=-4-2+6=0$. ✓

---

### Step 4. Odd $n\ge 3$ is impossible (descent mod $2,4,8$).

Work with (3) and $n\ge 3$ odd.

**Mod 2:** among $w-1,w,w+1$ exactly one is even. If $w$ is odd, then $w^n+(w-1)
^n\equiv 1+0\equiv 1$ but $(w+1)^n\equiv 0\pmod 2$ — contradiction. So **$w$ is
even**, $w=2m$.

**Mod 4:** since $n\ge 2$, $(2m)^n\equiv 0\pmod 4$, so (3) gives $(2m-1)^n\equiv
 (2m+1)^n\pmod 4$. If $m$ is even, $2m-1\equiv -1$, $2m+1\equiv 1\pmod 4$, and (
as $n$ is odd) $(-1)^n\equiv -1\not\equiv 1\equiv 1^n\pmod 4$ — contradiction. S
o **$m$ is odd**, i.e. $w\equiv 2\pmod 4$.

**Mod 8:** since $n\ge 3$, $(2m)^n\equiv 0\pmod 8$ (as $2^n\ge 8$ and $m$ odd).
Thus (3) gives
$$(2m-1)^n\equiv (2m+1)^n\pmod 8.$$
With $m$ odd, $m\equiv 1$ or $3\pmod 4$:

- $m\equiv 1\pmod 4$: $2m-1\equiv 1$, $2m+1\equiv 3\pmod 8$, and $n$ odd gives $
1^n\equiv 1$, $3^n\equiv 3\pmod 8$ — $1\not\equiv 3$.
- $m\equiv 3\pmod 4$: $2m-1\equiv 5$, $2m+1\equiv 7\pmod 8$, and $5^n\equiv 5$,
$7^n\equiv 7\pmod 8$ — $5\not\equiv 7$.

Both cases contradict. Hence **(3) has no integer solution for odd $n\ge 3$**.

---

### Conclusion

- Even $n$: no root (Step 1).
- Odd $n\ge 3$: no integral root (Step 4).
- $n=1$: root $x=-4$ (Step 3).

Therefore the necessary and sufficient condition is

$$\boxed{n=1}.$$
