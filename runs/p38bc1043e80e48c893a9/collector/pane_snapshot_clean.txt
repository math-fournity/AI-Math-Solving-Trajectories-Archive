## Proof

Let $S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$ and $T_n = \sum_{i=1}^n Y_i$. We want $\lim_{n\to\i
nfty} P(S_n \ge T_n) = \lim_{n\to\infty} P(S_n - T_n \ge 0)$.

**Distributions and moments.** $X_i \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)$, so $\mathbb{E}[
X_i] = 1/2$. $Y_i$ has density $e^{-x}\mathbb{I}\{x\ge 0\}$, i.e. $Y_i \sim \mat
hrm{Exp}(1)$, so $\mathbb{E}[Y_i] = 1$.

**Applying the Strong Law of Large Numbers.** The $X_i$ are i.i.d. with finite m
ean $1/2$, and the $Y_i$ are i.i.d. with finite mean $1$. By the Strong Law of L
arge Numbers,

$$\frac{S_n}{n} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \mathbb{
E}[X_1] = \frac{1}{2},$$
$$\frac{T_n}{n} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \mathbb{
E}[Y_1] = 1.$$

Therefore, almost surely,

$$\frac{S_n - T_n}{n} = \frac{S_n}{n} - \frac{T_n}{n} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}}
\frac{1}{2} - 1 = -\frac{1}{2}.$$

**Consequence.** Since $\frac{S_n - T_n}{n} \to -\frac{1}{2} < 0$ almost surely,
 it follows that $S_n - T_n \to -\infty$ almost surely. In particular, the event
 $\{S_n - T_n \ge 0\}$ occurs only finitely many times almost surely (by the ele
mentary fact: if a sequence $a_n \to -\infty$, then $a_n \ge 0$ for at most fini
tely many $n$).

Hence $\mathbb{I}\{S_n \ge T_n\} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} 0$. Since the indicat
or is bounded, by the Dominated (Bounded) Convergence Theorem,

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} P(S_n \ge T_n) = \lim_{n\to\infty} \mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{I}
\{S_n \ge T_n\}\big] = \mathbb{E}\big[\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{I}\{S_n \ge T_n\}
\big] = \mathbb{E}[0] = 0.$$

**Intuition check (optional).** $\mathbb{E}[S_n - T_n] = -n/2 \to -\infty$ while
 $\mathrm{Var}(S_n - T_n) = n(1/12 + 1) = 13n/12 = O(n)$. The mean drifts to $-\
infty$ linearly in $n$ while the standard deviation grows only as $\sqrt{n}$, so
 the probability of the sum being non-negative vanishes — consistent with the SL
LN conclusion above.

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^n X_i \ge \sum_{i=1}^n Y_i\right)
= 0.}$$
