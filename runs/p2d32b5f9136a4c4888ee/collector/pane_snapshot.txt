# Galois Group of the Splitting Field of $x^4 - x - 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$

## Step 1: Irreducibility over $\mathbb{Q}$

Let $f(x) = x^4 - x - 1$. By Gauss's Lemma, it suffices to check irreducibility
over $\mathbb{Z}$.

**No linear factors:** By the Rational Root Theorem, the only candidates are $\p
m 1$. We have $f(1) = -1 \neq 0$ and $f(-1) = 1 \neq 0$, so no rational roots.

**No quadratic factors:** Suppose $f(x) = (x^2 + ax + b)(x^2 + cx + d)$ with $a,
b,c,d \in \mathbb{Z}$. Expanding and comparing coefficients:

$$\begin{cases} a + c = 0 \\ b + d + ac = 0 \\ ad + bc = -1 \\ bd = -1 \end{case
s}$$

From $bd = -1$, we have $(b,d) \in \{(1,-1), (-1,1)\}$. In both cases $b + d = 0
$, so $ac = 0$, and since $c = -a$, we get $a^2 = 0$, hence $a = 0$. But then $a
d + bc = a(d - b) = 0 \neq -1$. Contradiction.

Therefore $f(x)$ is **irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$**, and its Galois group is a
 transitive subgroup of $S_4$.

## Step 2: Compute the discriminant

The polynomial is in depressed form $x^4 + px^2 + qx + r$ with $p = 0$, $q = -1$
, $r = -1$.

The discriminant of a depressed quartic $x^4 + px^2 + qx + r$ is:

$$\Delta = 256r^3 - 128p^2r^2 + 144pq^2r - 27q^4 + 16p^4r - 4p^3q^2$$

Substituting $p = 0$, $q = -1$, $r = -1$:

$$\Delta = 256(-1)^3 - 27(-1)^4 = -256 - 27 = -283$$

Now, $283$ is prime (checking divisibility by $2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13$ — all fail si
nce $\sqrt{283} \approx 16.8$). Hence $-283$ is **not a perfect square** in $\ma
thbb{Q}$.

Since the discriminant is not a square, the Galois group is **not contained in $
A_4$**. This rules out $A_4$ and $V_4$ (the Klein four-group, which is a subgrou
p of $A_4$).

## Step 3: Compute the resolvent cubic

For the depressed quartic $x^4 + px^2 + qx + r$, the resolvent cubic is:

$$g(y) = y^3 - py^2 - 4ry + (4pr - q^2)$$

Substituting $p = 0$, $q = -1$, $r = -1$:

$$g(y) = y^3 + 4y - 1$$

## Step 4: Irreducibility of the resolvent cubic

Check $g(y) = y^3 + 4y - 1$ for rational roots. By the Rational Root Theorem, th
e only candidates are $\pm 1$:

- $g(1) = 1 + 4 - 1 = 4 \neq 0$
- $g(-1) = -1 - 4 - 1 = -6 \neq 0$

Since $g$ is a cubic with no rational roots, it is **irreducible over $\mathbb{Q
}$**.

## Step 5: Determine the Galois group

We apply the standard classification of Galois groups of irreducible quartics ov
er $\mathbb{Q}$:

| Resolvent cubic $g$ | $\Delta$ square? | Galois group |
|---|---|---|
| irreducible | no | $S_4$ |
| irreducible | yes | $A_4$ |
| linear × irreducible quadratic | no | $D_4$ |
| linear × irreducible quadratic | yes | $C_4$ |
| splits completely | — | $V_4$ |

In our case:
- The resolvent cubic $g(y) = y^3 + 4y - 1$ is **irreducible** over $\mathbb{Q}$
.
- The discriminant $\Delta = -283$ is **not a square** in $\mathbb{Q}$.

By the classification table, the Galois group is $\boxed{S_4}$.

## Verification of the logic

The key facts underlying the classification are:
1. The resolvent cubic's splitting field is the fixed field of the Galois group'
s intersection with $V_4 = \{e, (12)(34), (13)(24), (14)(23)\}$. If the resolven
t cubic is irreducible, the Galois group acts transitively on the three pairs of
 roots, meaning $[G : G \cap V_4] = 3$, so $|G \cap V_4| = |G|/3$.
2. The discriminant being a square iff $G \subseteq A_4$.

With $g$ irreducible: $|G \cap V_4| = |G|/3$. The transitive subgroups with this
 property are $S_4$ (where $|G \cap V_4| = 8$) and $A_4$ (where $|G \cap V_4| =
4$). Since $\Delta$ is not a square, $G \not\subseteq A_4$, so $G = S_4$.

## Conclusion

The Galois group of the splitting field of $x^4 - x - 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is **
$S_4$** (the symmetric group on 4 elements, of order 24).
