## Eigenvalue Density of States for $H = -d^2/dx^2$ on $[-L/2, L/2]$ with Dirich
let BC

### 1. The eigenvalue problem

We solve
$$-y''(x) = \lambda\, y(x), \qquad y(-L/2)=0,\; y(L/2)=0.$$

For $\lambda>0$, write $\lambda = k^2$ with $k>0$. The general solution is
$$y(x) = A\cos(kx) + B\sin(kx).$$

Shift to the variable $u = x + L/2 \in [0,L]$. The boundary conditions become $y
(0)=y(L)=0$, giving the standard particle-in-a-box spectrum:
$$k_n = \frac{n\pi}{L}, \qquad n = 1,2,3,\dots$$
with normalized eigenfunctions $y_n(x)=\sqrt{2/L}\,\sin\!\big(\tfrac{n\pi}{L}(x+
L/2)\big)$ and eigenvalues
$$\boxed{\lambda_n = \left(\frac{n\pi}{L}\right)^2, \qquad n=1,2,3,\dots}$$

For $\lambda \le 0$ there are no nontrivial solutions satisfying both Dirichlet
conditions, so the spectrum is purely discrete and positive.

### 2. Exact spectral measure (density of states as a distribution)

The **exact density of states** is the spectral measure
$$\rho(\lambda) \;=\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \delta\!\left(\lambda - \left(\frac{n\
pi}{L}\right)^{\!2}\right), \qquad \lambda>0.$$

Equivalently, the **counting function** (integrated density of states) is
$$N(\lambda) \;=\; \#\{n\ge 1 : \lambda_n \le \lambda\} \;=\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty
}\Theta\!\left(\lambda-\left(\frac{n\pi}{L}\right)^{\!2}\right) \;=\; \Big\lfloo
r \frac{L\sqrt{\lambda}}{\pi}\Big\rfloor, \qquad \lambda\ge 0,$$
and $N(\lambda)=0$ for $\lambda<0$. Here $\Theta$ is the Heaviside step function
. By construction $\rho(\lambda)=dN/d\lambda$ in the distributional sense.

### 3. Smooth (Weyl) density of states

The physically meaningful **smooth density of states** is obtained by averaging
out the oscillatory staircase structure of $N(\lambda)$. Writing
$$N(\lambda) = \frac{L\sqrt{\lambda}}{\pi} - \frac{1}{2} + \text{oscillatory rem
ainder},$$
the leading (smooth) part is
$$N_{\text{smooth}}(\lambda) = \frac{L\sqrt{\lambda}}{\pi}, \qquad \lambda>0.$$
(The constant $-1/2$ is the boundary correction from the floor function; it is $
\lambda$-independent and therefore contributes nothing to the density.) Differen
tiating,
$$\boxed{\rho(\lambda) \;=\; \frac{dN_{\text{smooth}}}{d\lambda} \;=\; \frac{L}{
2\pi\sqrt{\lambda}}, \qquad \lambda>0,}$$
and $\rho(\lambda)=0$ for $\lambda\le 0$.

### 4. Consistency check via the Weyl law

The general Weyl asymptotics in $d$ dimensions for a domain $\Omega$ with Dirich
let boundary conditions is
$$N(\lambda) \sim \frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega)}{(2\pi)^d}\,\operatorname{Vol
}(B_d)\,\lambda^{d/2}, \qquad \lambda\to\infty.$$
Here $d=1$, $\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega)=L$, $\operatorname{Vol}(B_1)=2$, giving
$$N(\lambda) \sim \frac{L}{2\pi}\cdot 2\cdot \lambda^{1/2} = \frac{L\sqrt{\lambd
a}}{\pi},$$
in agreement with §3. Differentiating recovers $\rho(\lambda)=L/(2\pi\sqrt{\lamb
da})$.

### 5. Verification by direct summation

As a sanity check, integrate the smooth density over $[\lambda_{\min},\lambda]$:
$$\int_{0}^{\lambda}\frac{L}{2\pi\sqrt{\mu}}\,d\mu = \frac{L}{2\pi}\cdot 2\sqrt{
\lambda} = \frac{L\sqrt{\lambda}}{\pi} = N_{\text{smooth}}(\lambda),$$
which equals the number of levels up to $\lambda$ up to the bounded oscillatory
remainder, confirming self-consistency. Also, the total count up to $\lambda_n$
is exactly $n = L\sqrt{\lambda_n}/\pi = L(n\pi/L)/\pi = n$. ✓
