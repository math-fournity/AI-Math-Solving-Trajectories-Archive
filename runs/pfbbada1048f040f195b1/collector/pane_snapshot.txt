## Proof

**Definition.** A *Moore graph* of diameter $d$ and valency $k$ is a $k$-regular
 graph with diameter $d$ that attains the *Moore bound* — the maximum possible n
umber of vertices for a $k$-regular graph of diameter $d$.

### Part 1: The number of vertices is $k^2 + 1$

**Deriving the Moore bound for $d = 2$.** Let $G$ be a $k$-regular graph with di
ameter 2. Fix a vertex $v$ and let $N_i(v) = \{u \in V(G) : \text{dist}(v, u) =
i\}$.

- $|N_0(v)| = 1$ (just $v$ itself).
- $|N_1(v)| = k$ (the $k$ neighbors of $v$, by $k$-regularity).
- For $N_2(v)$: each neighbor $u \in N_1(v)$ has $k$ neighbors. At least one of
these is $v$ itself (in $N_0$), so at most $k - 1$ neighbors of $u$ lie in $N_2(
v)$. Thus $|N_2(v)| \leq k(k-1)$.

Since the diameter is 2, every vertex lies in $N_0(v) \cup N_1(v) \cup N_2(v)$,
giving:

$$n = |N_0| + |N_1| + |N_2| \leq 1 + k + k(k-1) = k^2 + 1.$$

This is the **Moore bound** $M(k, 2) = k^2 + 1$.

Since $G$ is a Moore graph, it attains this bound, so:

$$\boxed{n = k^2 + 1.}$$

### Part 2: The graph has girth 5

**Girth $\geq 5$ (no 3-cycles or 4-cycles).** Since $G$ attains the Moore bound,
 equality holds in the counting above for *every* choice of $v$. This forces two
 conditions:

**(a) No triangles (3-cycles).** Equality $|N_2(v)| = k(k-1)$ requires that each
 neighbor $u$ of $v$ has exactly $k - 1$ neighbors in $N_2(v)$. If two neighbors
 $u_1, u_2$ of $v$ were adjacent (forming a triangle $v, u_1, u_2$), then $u_2 \
in N_1(v)$ would be a neighbor of $u_1$ in $N_1(v)$, reducing the count of $u_1$
's neighbors in $N_2(v)$ below $k - 1$. Since $v$ is arbitrary, **no triangles e
xist**.

**(b) No 4-cycles.** Equality also requires that the $N_2$-neighborhoods of dist
inct neighbors of $v$ are disjoint. If some $w \in N_2(v)$ were adjacent to two
distinct neighbors $u_1, u_2$ of $v$, then $v, u_1, w, u_2$ would form a 4-cycle
, and $w$ would be counted in both $u_1$'s and $u_2$'s contributions, making $|N
_2(v)| < k(k-1)$. Since $v$ is arbitrary, **no 4-cycles exist**.

Together, (a) and (b) give girth $\geq 5$.

**Girth $\leq 5$ (a 5-cycle exists).** We construct a 5-cycle. Since a $k$-regul
ar graph of diameter 2 requires $k \geq 2$:

- **Case $k = 2$:** A 2-regular graph is a disjoint union of cycles. Diameter 2
forces a single cycle $C_5$ (since $C_3$ has diameter 1, $C_4$ has diameter 2 bu
t $n = 4 \neq 5 = k^2+1$, and $C_n$ for $n \geq 6$ has diameter $\geq 3$). So $G
 = C_5$, which has girth 5. ✓

- **Case $k \geq 3$:** Pick any vertex $v$, a neighbor $u_1 \in N_1(v)$, and a v
ertex $w \in N_2(v)$ adjacent to $u_1$ (such $w$ exists since $|N_2(v)| = k(k-1)
 > 0$). Since $k \geq 3$, $w$ has a neighbor $w' \neq u_1$.

  - $w'$ is **not adjacent to $u_1$** (else $u_1, w, w'$ form a triangle, contra
dicting (a)).
  - $w'$ is **not adjacent to $v$**: if $w' \in N_1(v)$, then $w'$ and $u_1$ are
 both neighbors of $v$, non-adjacent (no triangles), yet share common neighbors
$v$ and $w$ — giving two common neighbors for a non-adjacent pair, i.e., a 4-cyc
le $v\text{-}u_1\text{-}w\text{-}w'\text{-}v$, contradicting (b).

  So $w' \in N_2(v)$. Since $v$ and $w'$ are non-adjacent at distance 2, they ha
ve a unique common neighbor $x \in N_1(v)$ (uniqueness follows from no 4-cycles)
. Note $x \neq u_1$ since $u_1$ is not adjacent to $w'$.

  Now consider the closed walk $v \to x \to w' \to w \to u_1 \to v$. All five ve
rtices are distinct:
  - $v, u_1, w$ are at distances $0, 1, 2$ from $v$ — distinct.
  - $w' \neq w$ (different neighbors of $w$), $w' \neq u_1$ (shown), $w' \neq v$
 (shown).
  - $x \neq v$ (neighbor of $v$), $x \neq u_1$ (shown), $x \neq w$ ($x \in N_1(v
)$ but $w \in N_2(v)$), $x \neq w'$ ($x$ is adjacent to $w'$, not equal to it).

  This is a **5-cycle**: $v - x - w' - w - u_1 - v$.

Since girth $\geq 5$ and a 5-cycle exists:

$$\boxed{\text{girth}(G) = 5.}$$

### Conclusion

For a Moore graph of diameter 2 and valency $k$:
- Attaining the Moore bound gives $n = 1 + k + k(k-1) = k^2 + 1$.
- The equality conditions force the absence of 3-cycles and 4-cycles (girth $\ge

irth is exactly 5.

Both required properties are verified. $\blacksquare$
