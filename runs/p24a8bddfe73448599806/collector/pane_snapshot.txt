# Solution

## Part (a): Deriving the Bessel series expansion

**Goal.** Show that for $0 < x < 1$,

$$x^4 = 2\sum_{j=1}^{\infty} \frac{J_4(\alpha_j\, x)}{\alpha_j\, J_5(\alpha_j)},
$$

where $\{\alpha_j\}_{j\ge 1}$ are the positive zeros of $J_4$.

### Step 1. The Fourier–Bessel expansion framework

The family $\{J_n(\alpha_j x)\}_{j\ge 1}$, where $\alpha_j$ runs over the positi
ve zeros of $J_n$, forms an orthogonal system on $(0,1)$ with weight $x$:

$$\int_0^1 x\, J_n(\alpha_j x)\, J_n(\alpha_k x)\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\bigl[J_{n+1}(
\alpha_j)\bigr]^2\,\delta_{jk}.$$

This orthogonality follows from the standard identity (Sturm–Liouville form of B
essel's equation): multiplying $x^2 J_n'' + x J_n' + (x^2 - n^2)J_n = 0$ by $J_n
/x$ and integrating, or equivalently using

$$\int_0^1 x\, J_n(\alpha_j x)^2\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[J_{n+1}(\alpha_j)\bigr]
^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[J_n'(\alpha_j)\bigr]^2,$$

where the last equality uses $J_n'(\alpha_j) = -J_{n+1}(\alpha_j)$ at a zero of
$J_n$.

A function $f$ satisfying suitable regularity conditions admits the expansion

$$f(x) = \sum_{j=1}^{\infty} c_j\, J_n(\alpha_j x), \qquad 0 < x < 1,$$

with coefficients

$$c_j = \frac{2}{\bigl[J_{n+1}(\alpha_j)\bigr]^2}\int_0^1 x\, f(x)\, J_n(\alpha_
j x)\,dx.$$

### Step 2. Specialize to $n = 4$, $f(x) = x^4$

Here $n = 4$ and $f(x) = x^4$, so

$$c_j = \frac{2}{\bigl[J_5(\alpha_j)\bigr]^2}\int_0^1 x^5\, J_4(\alpha_j x)\,dx.
$$

### Step 3. Evaluate the integral $\int_0^1 x^5 J_4(\alpha_j x)\,dx$

Substitute $t = \alpha_j x$, $dx = dt/\alpha_j$:

$$\int_0^1 x^5 J_4(\alpha_j x)\,dx = \frac{1}{\alpha_j^6}\int_0^{\alpha_j} t^5\,
 J_4(t)\,dt.$$

We use the standard Bessel integral identity

$$\int t^{\nu+1}\, J_\nu(t)\,dt = t^{\nu+1}\, J_{\nu+1}(t) + C,$$

which follows directly from $\frac{d}{dt}\bigl[t^{\nu+1}J_{\nu+1}(t)\bigr] = t^{
\nu+1}J_\nu(t)$ (a consequence of the recurrence $[t^{\nu+1}J_{\nu+1}]' = t^{\nu
+1}J_\nu$).

Setting $\nu = 4$:

$$\int_0^{\alpha_j} t^5\, J_4(t)\,dt = \Bigl[t^5\, J_5(t)\Bigr]_0^{\alpha_j} = \
alpha_j^5\, J_5(\alpha_j) - \lim_{t\to 0^+} t^5 J_5(t).$$

Since $J_5(t) \sim t^5/2^5\cdot 5! = t^5/3840$ as $t\to 0$, we have $t^5 J_5(t)
\to 0$. Therefore

$$\int_0^{\alpha_j} t^5\, J_4(t)\,dt = \alpha_j^5\, J_5(\alpha_j),$$

and

$$\int_0^1 x^5\, J_4(\alpha_j x)\,dx = \frac{\alpha_j^5\, J_5(\alpha_j)}{\alpha_
j^6} = \frac{J_5(\alpha_j)}{\alpha_j}.$$

### Step 4. Assemble the coefficients

$$c_j = \frac{2}{\bigl[J_5(\alpha_j)\bigr]^2}\cdot\frac{J_5(\alpha_j)}{\alpha_j}
 = \frac{2}{\alpha_j\, J_5(\alpha_j)}.$$

### Step 5. Write the expansion

$$\boxed{x^4 = 2\sum_{j=1}^{\infty} \frac{J_4(\alpha_j\, x)}{\alpha_j\, J_5(\alp
ha_j)}, \qquad 0 < x < 1.}$$

### Remarks on validity

- **At $x = 0$:** Every term $J_4(\alpha_j\cdot 0) = J_4(0) = 0$, so the series
sums to $0 = 0^4$. Consistent.
- **At $x = 1$:** Every term $J_4(\alpha_j\cdot 1) = J_4(\alpha_j) = 0$, so the
series sums to $0$, whereas $1^4 = 1$. The series does **not** represent $f$ at
the endpoint $x=1$; this is the standard endpoint behavior of a Fourier–Bessel e
xpansion of type I (expansion in zeros of $J_n$): the series vanishes at $x = 1$
 regardless of $f(1)$.
- **Convergence on $(0,1)$:** Since $f(x) = x^4$ is piecewise smooth (in fact $C
^\infty$) on $[0,1]$, the Fourier–Bessel series converges pointwise to $f(x)$ fo
r every $x \in (0,1)$, and converges to $\tfrac{1}{2}[f(1^-) + 0] = \tfrac{1}{2}
$ at $x = 1$ in the sense of the symmetric limit of the Sturm–Liouville expansio
n — though each individual term is $0$ at $x=1$, so the pointwise sum is $0$; th
e value $\tfrac{1}{2}$ is the limit of partial sums $S_N(x)$ as $x\to 1^-$, refl
ecting a Gibbs-type overshoot.

## Part (b): Behavior of partial sums

Define the $N$-th partial sum

$$S_N(x) = 2\sum_{j=1}^{N} \frac{J_4(\alpha_j\, x)}{\alpha_j\, J_5(\alpha_j)}, \
qquad 0 \le x \le 1.$$

### Qualitative behavior

1. **Interior convergence ($0 < x < 1$, away from $1$).** For any fixed $x_0 \in
 (0,1)$, $S_N(x_0) \to x_0^4$ as $N\to\infty$. The convergence is uniform on eve
ry compact subinterval $[0, 1-\varepsilon]$. The coefficients decay as $|c_j| =
2/|\alpha_j J_5(\alpha_j)|$. Since $\alpha_j \sim (j + \tfrac{n}{2} - \tfrac{1}{
4})\pi \sim j\pi$ for large $j$ and $|J_5(\alpha_j)| \sim \sqrt{2/(\pi\alpha_j)}
$ (the standard large-argument asymptotics of $J_5$ evaluated at a zero of $J_4$
, where $J_5$ is of order $\alpha_j^{-1/2}$), we get $|c_j| \sim \text{const}/\a
lpha_j^{3/2} \sim \text{const}/j^{3/2}$. Combined with $|J_4(\alpha_j x)| \le 1$
, the terms are $O(j^{-3/2})$, so the series converges absolutely and uniformly
on $[0,1]$.

2. **Near $x = 1$: Gibbs-type phenomenon.** Because the series is forced to $0$
at $x = 1$ (every basis function $J_4(\alpha_j x)$ vanishes there) while $f(1^-)
 = 1$, the partial sums $S_N$ develop an overshoot near $x = 1$. Specifically, $
S_N(1) = 0$ for all $N$, but $S_N(x)$ for $x$ slightly less than $1$ rises towar
d $1$ and overshoots, with the overshoot region shrinking and moving toward $x =
 1$ as $N$ increases. The maximum overshoot is approximately $9\%$ of the jump $
1 - 0 = 1$ (the classical Gibbs constant $\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \frac{\sin t}{
t}\,dt - 1 \approx 0.0895$), localized in a boundary layer of width $O(1/\alpha_
N) \sim O(1/N)$.

3. **Near $x = 0$.** Here $f(0) = 0$ and $S_N(0) = 0$, so there is no mismatch.
The partial sums approximate $x^4$ well even for modest $N$, since $x^4$ is smal
l and flat near the origin.

### Sketch of partial sums (description)

- **$N = 1$:** A single lobe $2\,J_4(\alpha_1 x)/[\alpha_1 J_5(\alpha_1)]$; rise
s from $0$, peaks, and returns to $0$ at $x = 1$. Crude approximation to $x^4$.
- **$N = 3, 5$:** The sum tracks $x^4$ increasingly well on $[0, 0.8]$; a visibl
e overshoot appears near $x \approx 0.9$–$1.0$.
- **$N = 10, 20$:** Excellent agreement with $x^4$ on $[0, 1-\varepsilon]$; the
Gibbs overshoot is confined to a narrow spike just inside $x = 1$, with $S_N(1)
= 0$ exactly.

### Summary

The partial sums converge uniformly to $x^4$ on every $[0, 1-\varepsilon]$, exhi
bit the exact value $0$ at $x = 0$ (matching $f$), and display a Gibbs-type over
shoot near $x = 1$ where the expansion is constrained to vanish while $f(1^-) =
1$.
