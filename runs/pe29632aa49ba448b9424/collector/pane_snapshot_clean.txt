## Proof

We are given the completed zeta function identity:

$$\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s) = \frac{1}{s-1} - \frac{1}{s} + \frac{1}{2}\int_
1^\infty \bigl(u^{-s/2-1/2} + u^{s/2-1}\bigr)\bigl[\theta(u)-1\bigr]\,du.$$

**Step 1: The integral is an entire function of $s$.**

For each fixed $u \ge 1$, the integrand $\bigl(u^{-s/2-1/2}+u^{s/2-1}\bigr)[\the
ta(u)-1]$ is entire in $s$. As $u\to\infty$, the Jacobi theta function satisfies
 $\theta(u)-1 = O(e^{-\pi u})$ (exponential decay), which dominates any polynomi
al growth $u^{|s|}$ on compact sets in $s$. Near $u=1$, the integrand is bounded
. Hence the integral converges absolutely and uniformly on every compact subset
of $\mathbb{C}$, and by Morera's theorem it defines an entire function of $s$.

**Step 2: Locate the pole on the right-hand side at $s=1$.**

The right-hand side is
$$\underbrace{\frac{1}{s-1}}_{\text{simple pole, res. }1} \;-\; \underbrace{\fra
c{1}{s}}_{\text{analytic at }s=1} \;+\; \underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\int_1^\infty(\cd
ots)\,du}_{\text{entire}}.$$

The only singularity at $s=1$ is the simple pole $\frac{1}{s-1}$, which has resi
due $1$. Therefore the right-hand side has a simple pole at $s=1$ with residue

$$\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\left[\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\right] = 1.$$

**Step 3: Extract the residue of $\zeta(s)$.**

On the left-hand side, examine each factor at $s=1$:

- $\pi^{-s/2}$ is entire and nonzero at $s=1$, with value $\pi^{-1/2}$.
- $\Gamma(s/2)$ is meromorphic with poles at non-positive even integers; it is a
nalytic and nonzero at $s=1$, with value $\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt{\pi}$.
- $\zeta(s)$ is meromorphic with a single simple pole at $s=1$ (the only singula
rity we need).

Thus the only contribution to the pole of the product at $s=1$ comes from $\zeta
(s)$. Computing the residue of the product:

$$\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\bigl[\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\bigr] = \pi^{-1/2}
\cdot\Gamma(1/2)\cdot\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\zeta(s).$$

Using $\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt{\pi}$:

$$\pi^{-1/2}\cdot\sqrt{\pi}\cdot\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\zeta(s) = 1\cdot\operat
orname{Res}_{s=1}\zeta(s).$$

**Step 4: Equate and conclude.**

Setting the residues of both sides equal:

$$\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\zeta(s) = 1.$$

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\zeta(s) = 1}$$
