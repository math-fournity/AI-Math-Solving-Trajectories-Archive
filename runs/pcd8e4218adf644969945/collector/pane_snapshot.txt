## Solution

**Key Lemma.** *The reflections of the orthocenter of a triangle over its sides
lie on the circumcircle.*

*Proof.* Let triangle $ABC$ have circumcircle $\omega$, orthocenter $H$, and let
 $H_1$ be the reflection of $H$ over side $BC$. Since $AH \perp BC$ and $H_1$ is
 the mirror image of $H$ across $BC$, the point $H_1$ lies on the altitude from
$A$, on the opposite side of $BC$ from $H$. The standard orthocenter identity gi
ves $\angle BHC = 180^\circ - \angle A$. As $H$ and $H_1$ are on opposite sides
of $BC$, the angles $\angle BHC$ and $\angle BH_1C$ are supplementary, so $\angl
e BH_1C = 180^\circ - (180^\circ - \angle A) = \angle A = \angle BAC$. Since $H_
1$ is on the opposite side of $BC$ from $A$ and subtends the same angle over cho
rd $BC$, it lies on the arc $BC$ not containing $A$; hence $H_1 \in \omega$. The
 same holds for $H_2, H_3$. $\square$

### Construction

1. **Circumcircle.** By the Lemma, $H_1, H_2, H_3$ all lie on the circumcircle $
\omega$ of the desired triangle. Construct $\omega$ as the circumcircle of $\tri
angle H_1H_2H_3$: erect the perpendicular bisectors of $H_1H_2$ and $H_2H_3$; th
eir intersection is the circumcenter $O$; draw the circle through the three poin
ts.

2. **Vertex $A$.** $A$ is a midpoint of an arc $\widehat{H_2H_3}$ of $\omega$. C
onstruct the perpendicular bisector of chord $H_2H_3$; it meets $\omega$ in two
points (the two arc midpoints). Choose either as $A$.

3. **Vertex $B$.** Likewise $B$ is a midpoint of an arc $\widehat{H_1H_3}$. Cons
truct the perpendicular bisector of $H_1H_3$; it meets $\omega$ in two points. C
hoose either as $B$.

4. **Vertex $C$.** Let $H_1'$ be the antipode of $H_1$ on $\omega$ (extend the d
iameter $OH_1$ to meet $\omega$ again). Construct $C$ as the point of $\omega$ o
btained by rotating $H_1'$ about $O$ through the directed angle $\angle BOA$: at
 $O$, lay off the directed angle from ray $OH_1'$ equal to the directed angle fr
om $OB$ to $OA$; the resulting ray meets $\omega$ at $C$.

5. **Output.** $\triangle ABC$ is the desired triangle.

### Proof of correctness

Place $\omega$ as the unit circle centered at the origin $O$ in the complex plan
e, and write the coordinates of $A,B,C,H_1,H_2,H_3$ as $a,b,c,h_1,h_2,h_3$ with
$|a|=\cdots=|h_3|=1$.

**Orthocenter.** For a triangle inscribed in the unit circle centered at the ori
gin, the orthocenter is $h = a+b+c$.

**Reflection over a chord.** The reflection of a point $z$ over the line through
 two points $p,q$ on the unit circle is $z' = p+q - pq\,\bar z$. Reflecting the
orthocenter over side $BC$:
$$h_1 \;=\; b+c - bc\,\bar h \;=\; b+c - bc\!\left(\tfrac{1}{a}+\tfrac{1}{b}+\tf
rac{1}{c}\right) \;=\; b+c - \tfrac{bc}{a}-c-b \;=\; -\tfrac{bc}{a}.$$
Cyclically, $h_2 = -\tfrac{ca}{b}$ and $h_3 = -\tfrac{ab}{c}$.

**Arc midpoints.** If $h_2 = e^{i\theta_2}$, $h_3 = e^{i\theta_3}$, the midpoint
 of an arc $\widehat{H_2H_3}$ is at $e^{i(\theta_2+\theta_3)/2}$, whose square i
s $e^{i(\theta_2+\theta_3)} = h_2 h_3$. Thus the construction in steps 2–3 gives
 $a^2 = h_2 h_3$ and $b^2 = h_1 h_3$ (each up to the choice of which arc midpoin
t, i.e. up to a sign $\varepsilon \in \{+1,-1\}$).

**Step 4 enforces consistency.** Write $a = \varepsilon_a e^{i(\theta_2+\theta_3
)/2}$, $b = \varepsilon_b e^{i(\theta_1+\theta_3)/2}$, $c = \varepsilon_c e^{i(\
theta_1+\theta_2)/2}$. Then $\tfrac{bc}{a} = \tfrac{\varepsilon_b\varepsilon_c}{
\varepsilon_a}\,h_1$, and the reflection formula $h_1 = -\tfrac{bc}{a}$ requires
 $\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b\varepsilon_c = -1$. Step 4 defines $C$ by $\arg c =
 \arg(-h_1)+\arg a - \arg b$, i.e. $c = -h_1\cdot a/b$, which gives $\tfrac{bc}{
a} = -h_1$ *directly*. The other two relations then follow:
$$\tfrac{ca}{b} = \frac{(-h_1 a/b)\cdot a}{b} = -\frac{h_1 a^2}{b^2} = -\frac{h_
1\cdot h_2 h_3}{h_1 h_3} = -h_2, \qquad \tfrac{ab}{c} = \frac{ab}{-h_1 a/b} = -\
frac{b^2}{h_1} = -\frac{h_1 h_3}{h_1} = -h_3.$$
Also $c^2 = h_1^2 a^2/b^2 = h_1^2\cdot h_2h_3/(h_1h_3) = h_1 h_2$, so $C$ is ind
eed an arc midpoint of $\widehat{H_1H_2}$, consistent with the construction.

Therefore $h_1 = -bc/a$, $h_2 = -ca/b$, $h_3 = -ab/c$, which by the reflection f
ormula means $H_1, H_2, H_3$ are exactly the reflections of the orthocenter $h =
 a+b+c$ over sides $BC, CA, AB$ respectively. $\square$

### Number of solutions

There are **4 solutions** in general, corresponding to the $2\times 2$ independe
nt choices for $A$ (step 2) and $B$ (step 3), with $C$ then uniquely determined
by step 4. Equivalently, the four solutions correspond to choosing an **odd** nu
mber of the three vertices to be the "other" arc midpoint (the condition $\varep
silon_a\varepsilon_b\varepsilon_c = -1$).

### Degenerate cases

- If $H_1, H_2, H_3$ are **collinear**, no circumcircle exists; the problem has
no non-degenerate solution.
- If two of $H_1, H_2, H_3$ **coincide**, the original triangle is right-angled
(the orthocenter lies on a side, so its reflection over that side is itself); th
e construction degenerates accordingly.
