## Solution

Define
$$f(x) = e^{2x}\cdot\frac{1-x}{1+x}.$$

The integral becomes
$$I = \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{2x}}{f(x)^2+1}\cdot\frac{x^2}{(1+x)^2}\,dx.$$

**Key step: compute $f'(x)$.**

$$f(x)=e^{2x}\cdot\frac{1-x}{1+x},\qquad \frac{d}{dx}\!\left(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\rig
ht)=\frac{-(1+x)-(1-x)}{(1+x)^2}=\frac{-2}{(1+x)^2}.$$

So
$$f'(x)=2e^{2x}\frac{1-x}{1+x}+e^{2x}\frac{-2}{(1+x)^2}=\frac{2e^{2x}\bigl((1-x)
(1+x)-1\bigr)}{(1+x)^2}=\frac{2e^{2x}(1-x^2-1)}{(1+x)^2}=\frac{-2x^2 e^{2x}}{(1+
x)^2}.$$

Therefore
$$\frac{e^{2x}\,x^2}{(1+x)^2} = -\frac{f'(x)}{2}.$$

**Rewrite the integral.**

$$I = \int_0^\infty \frac{1}{f(x)^2+1}\cdot\frac{e^{2x}x^2}{(1+x)^2}\,dx = -\fra
c{1}{2}\int_0^\infty \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)^2+1}\,dx = -\frac{1}{2}\Bigl[\arctan f(x)
\Bigr]_0^\infty.$$

**Evaluate $f$ at the boundaries.**

- At $x=0$: $f(0)=e^0\cdot\frac{1}{1}=1$, so $\arctan f(0)=\arctan 1=\dfrac{\pi}
{4}$.
- As $x\to\infty$: $\dfrac{1-x}{1+x}\to -1$ and $e^{2x}\to\infty$, so $f(x)\to -
\infty$, hence $\arctan f(x)\to -\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

**Continuity check.** $f$ is continuous on $[0,\infty)$ (the only potential issu

$$I = -\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right) = -\frac{1}{2}\left(
