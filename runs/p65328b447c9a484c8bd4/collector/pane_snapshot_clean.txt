## Proof

**Yes.** Epi-convergence of $f_k$ to $f$ implies that $f$ is lower semicontinuou
s.

**Definition.** $f_k \xrightarrow{e} f$ if:

- **(LI)** For every $x$ and every sequence $x_k \to x$: $\liminf_{k\to\infty} f
_k(x_k) \geq f(x)$.
- **(LS)** For every $x$, there exists a sequence $x_k \to x$ with $\limsup_{k\t
o\infty} f_k(x_k) \leq f(x)$.

We show $f$ is lsc: for every $x_0$ and every $x_n \to x_0$, $f(x_0) \leq \limin
f_{n} f(x_n)$.

**Proof by contradiction.** Suppose $f$ is not lsc at some $x_0$. Then there exi
sts $x_n \to x_0$ with $\liminf_n f(x_n) < f(x_0)$. Passing to a subsequence (st
ill denoted $x_n$), assume $f(x_n) \to L$ with $L < f(x_0)$ (allowing $L = -\inf
ty$).

By condition **(LS)**, for each $n$ there is a sequence $y_k^{(n)} \to x_n$ (as
$k\to\infty$) with $\limsup_k f_k(y_k^{(n)}) \leq f(x_n)$.

**Diagonal construction.** Inductively choose $k_1 < k_2 < \cdots$ with $k_n$ la
rge enough that
$$d\!\left(y_{k_n}^{(n)},\, x_n\right) < \tfrac{1}{n} \quad\text{and}\quad f_{k_
n}\!\left(y_{k_n}^{(n)}\right) \leq f(x_n) + \tfrac{1}{n}.$$
This is possible since $y_k^{(n)} \to x_n$ and $\limsup_k f_k(y_k^{(n)}) \leq f(
x_n)$.

Define $z_k$ by $z_{k_n} = y_{k_n}^{(n)}$ and $z_k = x_0$ for $k \notin \{k_n\}$
.

**$z_k \to x_0$:** For $k = k_n$,
$$d(z_{k_n}, x_0) \leq d\!\left(y_{k_n}^{(n)}, x_n\right) + d(x_n, x_0) < \tfrac
{1}{n} + d(x_n, x_0) \to 0.$$
For $k \notin \{k_n\}$, $z_k = x_0$.

**Contradiction with (LI):**
$$\liminf_{k\to\infty} f_k(z_k) \;\leq\; \liminf_{n\to\infty} f_{k_n}(z_{k_n}) \
;\leq\; \liminf_{n\to\infty}\!\left(f(x_n) + \tfrac{1}{n}\right) = L < f(x_0).$$
But condition **(LI)** requires $\liminf_k f_k(z_k) \geq f(x_0)$ for every $z_k
\to x_0$. Contradiction. $\quad\blacksquare$

**Remark (epigraph viewpoint).** Epi-convergence is equivalent to $\operatorname
{epi}(f) = \operatorname{PK\text{-}lim}_k \operatorname{epi}(f_k)$ (Painlevé–Kur
atowski convergence). The outer PK limit $\operatorname{Ls}_k C_k$ is always clo
sed: if $x_j \in \operatorname{Ls}_k C_k$ and $x_j \to x$, then for each $j$ the
re exist $k_j$ (subsequence) and $y_j \in C_{k_j}$ with $y_j \to x_j$; choosing
$k_j$ strictly increasing gives $y_j \to x$, so $x \in \operatorname{Ls}_k C_k$.
 When the PK limit exists it equals this closed set, so $\operatorname{epi}(f)$
is closed, i.e., $f$ is lsc.
