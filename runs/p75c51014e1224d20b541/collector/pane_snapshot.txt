In a tournament on 251 (odd) vertices, a regular tournament gives every vertex o

This is achievable: the score sequence $(124, \underbrace{125,\ldots,125}_{249},

**Step 2: Upper bound on $A$'s total out-degree.**

If $A$ has strict minimum out-degree $d = d^+(A)$ in an $n$-vertex tournament, a

2}$:

$$d + (n-1)(d+1) \leq \frac{n(n-1)}{2} \implies nd \leq \frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2} \im
plies d \leq \frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2n}$$

**Step 3: Combining.**

Since $d^+(A) = a + d^+_D(A) \geq a \geq 126$ (where $d^+_D(A) \geq 0$ is out-de
gree to non-California hubs):

$$126 \leq \frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2n} \implies n^2 - 255n + 2 \geq 0 \implies n \geq
255$$

For $n = 254$: $\frac{253 \times 252}{508} \approx 125.5 < 126$. ✗
For $n = 255$: $\frac{254 \times 253}{510} \approx 126.004$, so $d \leq 126$. ✓
(tight)

Since $n = 5N$ and $n \geq 255$, we get $N \geq 51$.
