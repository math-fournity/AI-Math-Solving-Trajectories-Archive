## Setup and Separation of Variables

We seek $u(x,t)=X(x)T(t)$. Substituting into (1) and dividing by $(1-x/H)^2 XT$:

$$\frac{T''}{a^2 T}=\frac{1}{(1-x/H)^2 X}\frac{d}{dx}\!\left[(1-x/H)^2 X'\right]
=-\lambda.$$

This yields the two ODEs

$$T''+\lambda a^2 T=0,\qquad \frac{d}{dx}\!\left[(1-x/H)^2 X'\right]+\lambda(1-x
/H)^2 X=0,\qquad X(0)=X(l)=0. \tag{2}$$

## Solving the Spatial Equation

Introduce $s=1-x/H$ (so $dx=-H\,ds$, and $X'=-\dot X/H$ where $\dot{}=d/ds$). Th
en

$$\frac{d}{dx}\!\left[s^2 X'\right]=\frac{1}{H^2}\frac{d}{ds}\!\left[s^2\dot X\r
ight]=\frac{1}{H^2}\!\left(s^2\ddot X+2s\dot X\right),$$

and (2) becomes

$$s^2\ddot X+2s\dot X+\lambda H^2 s^2 X=0. \tag{3}$$

Set $X(s)=Z(s)/s$. A direct computation gives $s^2\ddot X+2s\dot X=s\ddot Z$, wh
ile $\lambda H^2 s^2 X=\lambda H^2 sZ$. Dividing (3) by $s$:

$$\boxed{\;\ddot Z+\lambda H^2 Z=0.\;}$$

Hence $Z=A\cos(\mu s)+B\sin(\mu s)$ with $\mu=\sqrt{\lambda}\,H$, and

$$X(x)=\frac{A\cos\!\big(\mu(1-x/H)\big)+B\sin\!\big(\mu(1-x/H)\big)}{1-x/H}. \t
ag{4}$$

## Eigenvalues from the Boundary Conditions

Note $H=lR/(R-r)$, so $1-l/H=r/R$. The conditions $X(0)=X(l)=0$ (the numerator m
ust vanish since the denominator is finite on $[0,l]$) give, at $s=1$ and $s=r/R
$:

$$A\cos\mu+B\sin\mu=0,\qquad A\cos(\mu r/R)+B\sin(\mu r/R)=0.$$

A nontrivial solution requires the determinant to vanish:

$$\cos\mu\sin(\mu r/R)-\sin\mu\cos(\mu r/R)=\sin\!\big(\mu(r/R-1)\big)=0.$$

Since $1-r/R=l/H$, this is $\sin(\mu\, l/H)=0$ (up to sign), giving

$$\mu_n\,\frac{l}{H}=n\pi\;\Longrightarrow\;\mu_n=\frac{n\pi H}{l},\qquad n=1,2,
3,\dots$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{\;\lambda_n=\left(\frac{\mu_n}{H}\right)^2=\left(\frac{n\pi}{l}\right)^
2,\qquad \omega_n=a\sqrt{\lambda_n}=\frac{an\pi}{l}.\;}$$

## Eigenfunctions

From $A\cos\mu_n+B\sin\mu_n=0$, taking $A=-\sin\mu_n$, $B=\cos\mu_n$ (up to scal
e), the numerator of (4) is

$$-\sin\mu_n\cos(\mu_n s)+\cos\mu_n\sin(\mu_n s)=\sin\!\big(\mu_n(s-1)\big)=\sin
\!\big(-\mu_n x/H\big)=-\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{l}\right).$$

Absorbing the sign and the constant, the normalized eigenfunctions are

$$\boxed{\;X_n(x)=\frac{\sin(n\pi x/l)}{1-x/H},\qquad n=1,2,3,\dots\;}$$

One verifies directly: $X_n(0)=\sin 0/1=0$ and $X_n(l)=\sin(n\pi)/(r/R)=0$. ✓

## Orthogonality

Equation (2) is a Sturm–Liouville problem with weight $w(x)=(1-x/H)^2$. Thus

$$\int_0^l (1-x/H)^2 X_n X_m\,dx=\int_0^l \sin\!\frac{n\pi x}{l}\,\sin\!\frac{m\
pi x}{l}\,dx=\begin{cases}0,&n\neq m,\\ l/2,&n=m.\end{cases}$$

So the eigenfunctions are orthogonal with the **ordinary sine inner product** —
a key simplification.

## General Solution and Coefficients

The time equation gives $T_n(t)=a_n\cos(\omega_n t)+b_n\sin(\omega_n t)$ with $\
omega_n=an\pi/l$. Superposing:

$$\boxed{\;u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(n\pi x/l)}{1-x/H}\left[a_n\cos\f
rac{an\pi t}{l}+b_n\sin\frac{an\pi t}{l}\right].\;} \tag{5}$$

**From $u(x,0)=f(x)$:** multiplying by $(1-x/H)$ gives $(1-x/H)f(x)=\sum a_n\sin
(n\pi x/l)$, a standard sine series, so

$$\boxed{\;a_n=\frac{2}{l}\int_0^l (1-x/H)\,f(x)\,\sin\!\frac{n\pi x}{l}\,dx.\;}
 \tag{6}$$

**From $u_t(x,0)=F(x)$:** differentiating (5) in $t$ at $t=0$ and multiplying by
 $(1-x/H)$ gives $(1-x/H)F(x)=\sum b_n\,(an\pi/l)\,\sin(n\pi x/l)$, so

$$b_n\,\frac{an\pi}{l}=\frac{2}{l}\int_0^l (1-x/H)\,F(x)\,\sin\!\frac{n\pi x}{l}
\,dx,$$

$$\boxed{\;b_n=\frac{2}{an\pi}\int_0^l (1-x/H)\,F(x)\,\sin\!\frac{n\pi x}{l}\,dx
.\;} \tag{7}$$

## Verification

- **PDE:** Each term in (5) has $X_n$ satisfying (2) with $\lambda_n=(n\pi/l)^2$
 and $T_n''=-\lambda_n a^2 T_n$, so (1) holds termwise.
- **BCs:** $X_n(0)=X_n(l)=0$ for all $n$, hence $u(0,t)=u(l,t)=0$. ✓
- **ICs:** (6)–(7) are exactly the Fourier–sine coefficients of $(1-x/H)f$ and $
(1-x/H)F$, so $u(x,0)=f(x)$ and $u_t(x,0)=F(x)$ by completeness of $\{\sin(n\pi
x/l)\}$ on $[0,l]$. ✓

## Final Answer

The longitudinal vibrations of the conical rod are

$$u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(n\pi x/l)}{1-x/H}\left[a_n\cos\frac{an\pi
 t}{l}+b_n\sin\frac{an\pi t}{l}\right],$$

with eigenvalues $\lambda_n=(n\pi/l)^2$, eigenfrequencies $\omega_n=an\pi/l$, ei
genfunctions $X_n(x)=\dfrac{\sin(n\pi x/l)}{1-x/H}$, and coefficients

$$a_n=\frac{2}{l}\int_0^l (1-x/H)\,f(x)\,\sin\!\frac{n\pi x}{l}\,dx,\qquad b_n=\
frac{2}{an\pi}\int_0^l (1-x/H)\,F(x)\,\sin\!\frac{n\pi x}{l}\,dx.$$

The spectrum coincides with that of a uniform rod of length $l$; the conicity en
ters only through the factor $(1-x/H)^{-1}$ in the spatial eigenfunctions and th
e weight $(1-x/H)$ in the coefficient integrals.
