## Solution

**Define** $Q(x) = x \cdot P(x) - 1$.

Since $P$ has degree 1996, $x \cdot P(x)$ has degree 1997, so $Q$ is a polynomia
l of degree 1997.

**Zeros of $Q$.** For each $n = 1, 2, \ldots, 1997$:
$$Q(n) = n \cdot P(n) - 1 = n \cdot \frac{1}{n} - 1 = 0.$$

So $Q$ has 1997 distinct roots $1, 2, \ldots, 1997$, and since $\deg Q = 1997$:
$$Q(x) = c\,(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-1997)$$
for some constant $c$.

**Find $c$ using $x = 0$.**
$$Q(0) = 0 \cdot P(0) - 1 = -1.$$
On the other hand:
$$Q(0) = c\,(0-1)(0-2)\cdots(0-1997) = c\,(-1)^{1997}\cdot 1997! = -c\cdot 1997!
.$$

So $-1 = -c\cdot 1997!$, giving $c = \dfrac{1}{1997!}$.

**Evaluate at $x = 1998$.**
$$Q(1998) = 1998\cdot P(1998) - 1 = c\,(1998-1)(1998-2)\cdots(1998-1997) = c\cdo
t 1997\cdot 1996\cdots 1 = c\cdot 1997!.$$

Substituting $c = \frac{1}{1997!}$:
$$1998\cdot P(1998) - 1 = \frac{1}{1997!}\cdot 1997! = 1,$$
$$1998\cdot P(1998) = 2,$$
$$P(1998) = \frac{2}{1998} = \boxed{\dfrac{1}{999}}.$$
