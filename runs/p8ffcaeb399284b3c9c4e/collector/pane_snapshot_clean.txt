Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every ideal of $R/J^n$ is p
rincipal, given $R$ finite commutative, $J$ maximal, $n \geq 2$.

**Answer: No.** Not every ideal of $R/J^n$ need be principal. I provide an expli
cit counterexample.

## Counterexample

Let $p$ be any prime. Set
$$R = \mathbb{F}_p[x,y]\,/\,(x,y)^3, \qquad J = (x,y)/(x,y)^3, \qquad n = 2.$$

**$R$ is a finite commutative ring.** The elements of $R$ are $\mathbb{F}_p$-lin
ear combinations of $\{1, x, y, x^2, xy, y^2\}$, so $|R| = p^6 < \infty$.

**$J$ is a maximal ideal of $R$.** The maximal ideals of $R$ correspond to maxim
al ideals of $\mathbb{F}_p[x,y]$ containing $(x,y)^3$. Any prime ideal containin
g $(x,y)^3$ must contain $(x,y)$, and $(x,y)$ is maximal in $\mathbb{F}_p[x,y]$.
 Hence $(x,y)/(x,y)^3$ is the unique maximal ideal of $R$, i.e., $R$ is local wi
th maximal ideal $J$.

**Compute $R/J^2$.** We have $J^2 = (x,y)^2/(x,y)^3$, so
$$R/J^2 \;=\; \big(\mathbb{F}_p[x,y]/(x,y)^3\big)\big/\big((x,y)^2/(x,y)^3\big)
\;\cong\; \mathbb{F}_p[x,y]/(x,y)^2 \;=\; \mathbb{F}_p[x,y]/(x^2,\, xy,\, y^2).$
$

Denote the images of $x, y$ in $S := R/J^2$ by $\bar{x}, \bar{y}$. Then $S = \ma
thbb{F}_p \oplus \mathbb{F}_p\bar{x} \oplus \mathbb{F}_p\bar{y}$ with $\bar{x}^2
 = \bar{x}\bar{y} = \bar{y}^2 = 0$.

**The ideal $I = (\bar{x}, \bar{y})$ is not principal.**

$I$ is the maximal ideal of $S$, consisting of all $\mathbb{F}_p$-linear combina
tions $a\bar{x} + b\bar{y}$. As an $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector space, $\dim_{\mathbb{F
}_p} I = 2$.

Suppose for contradiction that $I = (f)$ for some $f \in I$, so $f = a\bar{x} +
b\bar{y}$ with $(a,b) \neq (0,0)$. For any $g = c_0 + c_1\bar{x} + c_2\bar{y} \i
n S$ (with $c_i \in \mathbb{F}_p$), we compute:
$$g \cdot f = (c_0 + c_1\bar{x} + c_2\bar{y})(a\bar{x} + b\bar{y}) = c_0(a\bar{x
} + b\bar{y}) + \underbrace{c_1 a\bar{x}^2 + c_1 b\,\bar{x}\bar{y} + c_2 a\,\bar
{y}\bar{x} + c_2 b\,\bar{y}^2}_{=\,0} = c_0(a\bar{x} + b\bar{y}).$$

Therefore $(f) = \{c_0(a\bar{x} + b\bar{y}) : c_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p\}$, which is
a $1$-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_p$-subspace of $I$. But $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} I = 2
$, so $(f) \subsetneq I$, contradicting $I = (f)$.

Hence $I = (\bar{x}, \bar{y})$ is a non-principal ideal of $R/J^2$.

---

## Conclusion

There exist a finite commutative ring $R$, a maximal ideal $J \triangleleft R$,
and an integer $n \geq 2$ such that $R/J^n$ contains a non-principal ideal. Ther
efore, **it is not true in general that every ideal of $R/J^n$ is principal**.
