**Claim:** Yes. The point $x$ has a local base of cardinality at most $|\mathscr
{U}|$.

**Proof.**

Write $\mathscr{U}=\{U_\alpha\}_{\alpha\in A}$ and set $F_\alpha:=X\setminus U_\
alpha$, a closed set with $x\notin F_\alpha$. The hypothesis $\bigcap\mathscr{U}
=\{x\}$ gives
$$X\setminus\{x\}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in A}F_\alpha.$$

Let $\mathscr{B}$ denote the family of all *finite* intersections of members of
$\mathscr{U}$:
$$\mathscr{B}:=\Bigl\{\,\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}U_{\alpha_i}:n<\omega,\ \alpha_i\in A\B
igr\}.$$
Every member of $\mathscr{B}$ is an open neighborhood of $x$. We show $\mathscr{
B}$ is a local base at $x$.

Let $V$ be any open neighborhood of $x$. Then $K:=X\setminus V$ is closed in $X$
, hence compact, and $x\notin K$, so $K\subseteq X\setminus\{x\}=\bigcup_{\alpha
}F_\alpha$. By compactness of $K$, finitely many $F_{\alpha_1},\dots,F_{\alpha_n
}$ cover $K$:
$$K\subseteq F_{\alpha_1}\cup\cdots\cup F_{\alpha_n}.$$
Taking complements,
$$U_{\alpha_1}\cap\cdots\cap U_{\alpha_n}\subseteq V.$$
The left-hand side is a member of $\mathscr{B}$ contained in $V$. Hence $\mathsc
r{B}$ is a local base at $x$.

It remains to bound $|\mathscr{B}|$. Since $\mathscr{U}$ is uncountable, $|A|=|\
mathscr{U}|$ is infinite. The number of finite subsets of $A$ is
$$|[A]^{<\omega}|=|A|,$$
because for each $n$, $|A|^n=|A|$ (infinite cardinal arithmetic), and a countabl
e union of sets of size $|A|$ still has size $|A|$. Each finite subset contribut
es at most one element to $\mathscr{B}$, so
$$|\mathscr{B}|\le|A|=|\mathscr{U}|.$$

Thus $x$ admits a local base of cardinality at most $|\mathscr{U}|$. $\blacksqua
re$
