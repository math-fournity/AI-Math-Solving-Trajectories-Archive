## Proof

**Setup and identification of key points.**

Since $T$ is inside $\triangle ABC$ with $\angle ATB = \angle BTC = 120°$, we ge
t $\angle ATC = 360° - 120° - 120° = 120°$. Thus $T$ is the **Fermat point** (fi
rst isogonic center, $X_{13}$) of the triangle, which exists inside the triangle
 since all angles are less than $120°$ (guaranteed by the triangle being acute).

The three midpoints of the sides of a triangle lie on the **nine-point circle**,
 whose center $E$ is the **nine-point center** ($X_5$). Since three non-collinea
r points determine a unique circle, $E$ is the nine-point center.

**Trilinear coordinates.**

Using standard trilinear coordinates:
- $B = (0 : 1 : 0)$
- Fermat point $T = X_{13} = \csc(A+60°) : \csc(B+60°) : \csc(C+60°)$
- Nine-point center $E = X_5 = \cos(B-C) : \cos(C-A) : \cos(A-B)$

**Collinearity condition.**

The points $B, T, E$ are collinear iff the determinant of their trilinear coordi
nates vanishes:

$$\begin{vmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ \csc(A+60°) & \csc(B+60°) & \csc(C+60°) \\ \cos(B
-C) & \cos(C-A) & \cos(A-B) \end{vmatrix} = 0$$

Expanding along the first row:

$$\csc(A+60°)\cos(A-B) - \csc(C+60°)\cos(B-C) = 0$$

$$\frac{\cos(A-B)}{\sin(A+60°)} = \frac{\cos(B-C)}{\sin(C+60°)}$$

**Simplification.**

Cross-multiplying and using $C = 180° - A - B$:

- **Left side:** $\cos(A-B)\sin(C+60°) = \cos(A-B)\bigl[\sin(A+B) - \sqrt{3}\cos
(A+B)\bigr] = 2\cos(A-B)\sin(A+B-60°)$

- **Right side:** $\cos(B-C)\sin(A+60°)$. Since $B - C = A + 2B - 180°$, we have
 $\cos(B-C) = -\cos(A+2B)$, giving $-2\cos(A+2B)\sin(A+60°)$.

The equation becomes:
$$\cos(A-B)\sin(A+B-60°) + \cos(A+2B)\sin(A+60°) = 0$$

**Product-to-sum conversion.**

Using $\cos X \sin Y = \tfrac{1}{2}[\sin(Y+X) + \sin(Y-X)]$:

- First term: $\tfrac{1}{2}[\sin(2A-60°) + \sin(2B-60°)]$
- Second term: $\tfrac{1}{2}[\sin(2A+2B+60°) + \sin(60°-2B)] = \tfrac{1}{2}[\sin
(2A+2B+60°) - \sin(2B-60°)]$

Adding:
$$\sin(2A-60°) + \sin(2A+2B+60°) = 0$$

Since $2A+2B = 360°-2C$, we have $\sin(2A+2B+60°) = \sin(420°-2C) = \sin(60°-2C)
$, so:

$$\sin(2A-60°) + \sin(60°-2C) = 0 \implies \sin(2A-60°) = \sin(2C-60°)$$

**Solving.**

This yields two cases:
1. $2A-60° = 2C-60° + 360°k \implies A = C$. **Rejected** — the triangle is scal
ene.
2. $2A-60° = 180°-(2C-60°)+360°k \implies 2A+2C = 300°+360°k \implies A+C = 150°
+180°k$.

Since $A+C = 180°-B$, we get $180°-B = 150°$, hence:

$$\boxed{\angle ABC = 30°}$$

**Verification.** With $B=30°$, $A=80°$, $C=70°$: the collinearity determinant e
quals $\csc(140°)\cos(50°) - \csc(130°)\cos(40°) = \frac{\sin 40°}{\sin 40°} - \
frac{\sin 50°}{\sin 50°} = 1 - 1 = 0$. ✓ The triangle is acute, scalene, and all
 angles are below $120°$, so the Fermat point lies inside.
