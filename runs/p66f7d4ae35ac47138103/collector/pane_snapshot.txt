## Proof

**Claim:** Let $P$ and $Q$ be Sylow $p$-subgroups of a group $G$. Then $N_P(Q) :
= N_G(Q) \cap P = P \cap Q$.

**Proof.** We establish two inclusions.

**($\subseteq$)** Since $Q \trianglelefteq N_G(Q)$ and $Q$ is a Sylow $p$-subgro
up of $G$, the index $[G:Q]$ is not divisible by $p$, hence $[N_G(Q):Q]$, dividi
ng $[G:Q]$, is also not divisible by $p$. As $Q \trianglelefteq N_G(Q)$, the sub
group $Q$ is therefore the **unique** Sylow $p$-subgroup of $N_G(Q)$. Consequent
ly every $p$-subgroup of $N_G(Q)$ is contained in $Q$.

Now $N_P(Q) = N_G(Q) \cap P$ is a subgroup of $P$, hence a $p$-group, and it lie
s in $N_G(Q)$. By the observation above,
$$N_P(Q) \subseteq Q.$$
Since also $N_P(Q) \subseteq P$, we obtain $N_P(Q) \subseteq P \cap Q$.

**($\supseteq$)** Since $Q \leq N_G(Q)$, any subgroup of $Q$ lies in $N_G(Q)$. I
n particular $P \cap Q \leq N_G(Q)$, and of course $P \cap Q \leq P$. Hence
$$P \cap Q \leq N_G(Q) \cap P = N_P(Q).$$

Combining both inclusions, $N_P(Q) = P \cap Q$. $\blacksquare$
