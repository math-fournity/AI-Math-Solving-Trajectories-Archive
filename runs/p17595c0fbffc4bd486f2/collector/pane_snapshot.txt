**Proof.**

A conic Γ defined by a symmetric matrix $A$ (representing the quadratic form $\m
athbf{x}^T A\,\mathbf{x}=0$) transforms under a change of basis $\mathbf{x}=P\ma
thbf{y}$ according to

$$B = P^{T} A\, P.$$

(This is the correct transformation law for a quadratic form; the $P^{-1}AP$ for
m stated in the problem is the law for the associated polarity as a linear map.
For the conic itself we use $P^T A P$.)

Taking determinants:

$$\det(B)=\det(P^T A P)=\det(P)^2\cdot\det(A).$$

**Computing $\det(A)$**, where $A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3\\2&4&5\\3&5&6\end{pmatrix
}$:

$$\det(A)=1(4\cdot6-5\cdot5)-2(2\cdot6-5\cdot3)+3(2\cdot5-4\cdot3)$$
$$=1(-1)-2(-3)+3(-2)=-1+6-6=\boxed{-1}.$$

**Computing $\det(B)$**, where $B=\begin{pmatrix}6&5&3\\5&4&2\\3&2&1\end{pmatrix
}$:

$$\det(B)=6(4\cdot1-2\cdot2)-5(5\cdot1-2\cdot3)+3(5\cdot2-4\cdot3)$$
$$=6(0)-5(-1)+3(-2)=0+5-6=\boxed{-1}.$$

**Solving for $\det(P)$:**

$$-1=\det(P)^2\cdot(-1)\implies \det(P)^2=1\implies \det(P)=\pm1.$$

**Verification that both signs are attainable.** Observe that $B = JAJ$ where $J
=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\0&1&0\\1&0&0\end{pmatrix}$ is the exchange (anti-diagonal
) matrix, since $J$ reverses both rows and columns of $A$:

$$JAJ = \begin{pmatrix}6&5&3\\5&4&2\\3&2&1\end{pmatrix}=B.\;\checkmark$$

Since $J$ is symmetric ($J^T=J$) and involutory ($J^2=I$, so $J^{-1}=J$), $P=J$
satisfies both $B=P^TAP$ and $B=P^{-1}AP$, with $\det(J)=-1$. Likewise $P=-J$ sa
tisfies $B=P^TAP$ with $\det(-J)=(-1)^3(-1)=+1$. Hence both signs are realized.

$$\boxed{\det(P)=\pm 1}$$
