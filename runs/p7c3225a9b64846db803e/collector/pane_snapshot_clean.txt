## Proof

**Answer: False.**

**Key observation.** The hypothesis—"every one-dimensional subspace of $X$ is on
e-complemented"—is satisfied by *every* Banach space, so it imposes no restricti
on at all.

Indeed, let $Y = \operatorname{span}\{x\}$ be a one-dimensional subspace with $\
|x\|=1$. By the Hahn–Banach theorem, there exists a norm-one functional $f \in X
^*$ with $f(x) = 1$. Define
$$P : X \to X, \qquad P(y) = f(y)\, x.$$
Then $P$ is a projection onto $Y$ (since $P(x)=f(x)\,x = x$), and
$$\|P\| = \sup_{\|y\|=1} \|f(y)\,x\| = \sup_{\|y\|=1} |f(y)| \cdot \|x\| = 1 \cd
ot 1 = 1.$$
So $Y$ is one-complemented. This holds for an arbitrary Banach space $X$ and an
arbitrary one-dimensional subspace.

**Counterexample.** Take $X = \ell^2_1(\mathbb{R})$, i.e., $\mathbb{R}^2$ with t
he $\ell^1$-norm $\|(a,b)\|_1 = |a|+|b|$. By the observation above, every one-di
mensional subspace of $X$ is one-complemented.

Now $X$ itself is a two-dimensional subspace of $X$. We claim $X$ is **not** iso
metrically isomorphic to a Hilbert space.

A two-dimensional Hilbert space is isometric to $(\mathbb{R}^2, \|\cdot\|_2)$ wi
th the Euclidean norm, whose closed unit ball is a (Euclidean) disk—an ellipse.
The closed unit ball of $(\mathbb{R}^2, \|\cdot\|_1)$ is the diamond $\{(a,b): |
a|+|b|\le 1\}$, a square with vertices $(\pm 1, 0), (0, \pm 1)$.

A linear isometry between two normed spaces maps the closed unit ball of one ont
o the closed unit ball of the other (as a linear image). A non-degenerate linear
 map sends ellipses to ellipses; in particular it cannot send a disk to a polygo
n. Since the diamond is a polygon (not an ellipse), no linear isometry from $(\m
athbb{R}^2, \|\cdot\|_2)$ onto $(\mathbb{R}^2, \|\cdot\|_1)$ exists.

Therefore $X = \ell^2_1$ is a Banach space in which every one-dimensional subspa
ce is one-complemented, yet $X$ itself is a two-dimensional subspace that is not
 isometrically isomorphic to any Hilbert space.

Hence the statement is **false**.
