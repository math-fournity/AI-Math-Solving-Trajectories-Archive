## Proof

**Definitions.** Given an NTU game $V$ and weights $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}_{++}^
N$, the $\lambda$-transfer game is the TU game
$$v^\lambda(S) = \max\Bigl\{\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_i\, y_i : y \in V(S)\Bigr\}.$$

An allocation $x$ is **strongly inhibitive** if for every $\lambda>0$ and every
proper coalition $\emptyset\subsetneq S\subsetneq N$:
$$\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_i x_i > v^\lambda(S). \tag{$*$}$$

An allocation $x$ is **inhibitive** if the same condition holds with weak inequa
lity:
$$\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_i x_i \ge v^\lambda(S). \tag{$**$}$$

The inhibitive condition says no proper coalition can improve upon $x$ in *any*
$\lambda$-transfer game. The strongly inhibitive condition requires a *strict* m
argin in every such game.

---

**Construction of the approximating sequence.** Let $x$ be inhibitive, so $(**)$
 holds for all $\lambda>0$ and $S\subsetneq N$. For each $n\ge 1$, set
$$x^n := x + \tfrac{1}{n}\,\mathbf{1}_N, \qquad \mathbf{1}_N=(1,\dots,1)\in\math
bb{R}^N.$$

**Convergence.** $\|x^n - x\| = \frac{1}{n}\|\mathbf{1}_N\|\to 0$, so $x^n\to x$
.

**Each $x^n$ is strongly inhibitive.** Fix any $\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{++}^N$ and
 $\emptyset\subsetneq S\subsetneq N$. Then
$$\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_i\, x^n_i \;=\; \sum_{i\in S}\lambda_i\, x_i \;+\; \frac{
1}{n}\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_i.$$

Since $x$ is inhibitive, $\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_i x_i \ge v^\lambda(S)$. Since $\
lambda_i>0$ for all $i$ and $S\neq\emptyset$, we have $\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_i >
0$. Therefore
$$\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_i\, x^n_i \;\ge\; v^\lambda(S) + \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in S}
\lambda_i \;>\; v^\lambda(S),$$
which is precisely the strongly inhibitive condition $(*)$. Since $\lambda>0$ an
d $S$ were arbitrary, $x^n$ is strongly inhibitive. $\square$

---

**Why the definition guarantees this.** The essence is that the definition of an
 inhibitive allocation—weak inequalities $(**)$ against *every* $\lambda$-transf
er game—differs from the strongly inhibitive definition only by replacing $>$ wi
th $\ge$. The gap between weak and strict is exactly the positive quantity $\fra
c{1}{n}\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_i > 0$ that the uniform upward shift $\frac{1}{n}\ma
thbf{1}_N$ injects into the left-hand side of every coalitional constraint simul
taneously. Because $\lambda$ is strictly positive and every proper coalition $S$
 is nonempty, this shift is *uniformly* effective across all $(\lambda, S)$ pair
s: it converts every weak inequality into a strict one in a single step, and the
 shift vanishes as $n\to\infty$, yielding convergence to $x$.

This is why the inhibitive condition, by its very definition as the weak-inequal
ity version of the strongly inhibitive condition, must be approximable: any boun
dary case (where equality holds for some $(\lambda, S)$) is resolved by the pert
urbation, and the perturbation can be made arbitrarily small.
