## Solution

We evaluate
$$L = \lim_{n \to \infty} \int_0^n \left(1 - \frac{x}{n}\right)^n \cos\!\left(\f
rac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right) e^{x/2}\, dx.$$

**Step 1: Pointwise limit of the integrand.**

Extend the integrand to all of $[0,\infty)$ by defining
$$f_n(x) = \left(1 - \frac{x}{n}\right)^n \cos\!\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right)
e^{x/2} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{[0,n]}(x).$$

For each fixed $x \ge 0$, once $n > x$ we have $x \in [0,n]$, and:
- $\left(1 - \frac{x}{n}\right)^n \to e^{-x}$ (standard limit),
- $\cos\!\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right) \to \cos(0) = 1$,
- $e^{x/2}$ is constant in $n$.

Hence $f_n(x) \to e^{-x} \cdot 1 \cdot e^{x/2} = e^{-x/2}$ pointwise on $[0,\inf
ty)$.

**Step 2: Dominating function.**

Using $\ln(1 - t) \le -t$ for $t \in [0,1)$, so for $x \in [0,n]$:
$$\left(1 - \frac{x}{n}\right)^n = \exp\!\left(n \ln\!\left(1 - \frac{x}{n}\righ
t)\right) \le \exp\!\left(n \cdot \left(-\frac{x}{n}\right)\right) = e^{-x}.$$

Since $|\cos(\cdot)| \le 1$ and $e^{x/2} > 0$:
$$|f_n(x)| \le e^{-x} \cdot 1 \cdot e^{x/2} = e^{-x/2}, \qquad x \in [0,n],$$
and $|f_n(x)| = 0$ for $x > n$. Thus $|f_n(x)| \le e^{-x/2}$ for all $x \ge 0$.

The function $g(x) = e^{-x/2}$ is integrable on $[0,\infty)$:
$$\int_0^\infty e^{-x/2}\, dx = \left[-2e^{-x/2}\right]_0^\infty = 2.$$

**Step 3: Apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem.**

Since $|f_n| \le g \in L^1([0,\infty))$ and $f_n \to e^{-x/2}$ pointwise, the DC
T gives
$$L = \int_0^\infty e^{-x/2}\, dx = 2.$$

$$\boxed{L = 2}$$
