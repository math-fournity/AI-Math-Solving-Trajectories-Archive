## Proof

Let $S_n = X_1 + \cdots + X_n$ and $Y(z) = \min\{n \geq 1 : S_n > z\}$. Define t
he **overshoot** $R(z) = S_{Y(z)} - z \geq 0$.

### Decomposition

By definition $S_{Y(z)} = z + R(z)$, i.e.,
$$\sum_{i=1}^{Y(z)} X_i = z + R(z).$$

Subtracting $M\cdot Y(z)$ from both sides and rearranging:
$$Y(z) = \frac{z}{M} + \frac{R(z)}{M} - \frac{1}{M}\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{Y(z)}
(X_i - M)}_{=:\, W(z)}.$$

### Step 1 — The overshoot is bounded in probability

Since $M > 0$ and $E[X_i^2] < \infty$ (as $\sigma^2 < \infty$), standard renewal
 theory gives that the overshoot $R(z)$ converges in distribution to a proper li
miting random variable (the equilibrium residual life). In particular $R(z) = O_
p(1)$, so
$$\frac{R(z)}{\sqrt{z}} \xrightarrow{p} 0, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(R(z)) = O(1
).$$

### Step 2 — Wald's identities applied to $W(z)$

$Y(z)$ is a stopping time w.r.t. $\mathcal{F}_n = \sigma(X_1,\dots,X_n)$, and $E
[Y(z)] < \infty$ (since $M>0$, the elementary renewal theorem gives $E[Y(z)] \si
m z/M$).

- **Wald's first identity:** $E[W(z)] = E[Y(z)]\cdot E[X_i - M] = 0$.
- **Wald's second identity:** $E[W(z)^2] = \sigma^2\, E[Y(z)]$.

Hence $\operatorname{Var}(W(z)) = E[W(z)^2] = \sigma^2 E[Y(z)] \sim \sigma^2 z /
 M$, so $W(z) = O_p(\sqrt{z})$.

### Step 3 — Variance of $Y(z)$

From the decomposition,
$$\operatorname{Var}(Y(z)) = \frac{1}{M^2}\operatorname{Var}(W(z)) + \frac{1}{M^
2}\operatorname{Var}(R(z)) - \frac{2}{M^2}\operatorname{Cov}(W(z), R(z)).$$

- $\operatorname{Var}(W(z)) \sim \sigma^2 z / M$ (Step 2).
- $\operatorname{Var}(R(z)) = O(1)$ (Step 1).
- By Cauchy–Schwarz, $|\operatorname{Cov}(W(z), R(z))| \leq \sqrt{\operatorname{
Var}(W(z))\operatorname{Var}(R(z))} = O(\sqrt{z}) = o(z)$.

Therefore
$$\operatorname{Var}(Y(z)) = \frac{1}{M^2}\cdot\frac{\sigma^2 z}{M} + O(\sqrt{z}
) = \frac{\sigma^2 z}{M^3} + o(z).$$

### Step 4 — The limit

$$\lim_{z\to\infty} \frac{V(z)\,M^3}{\sigma^2 z} = \lim_{z\to\infty}\frac{M^3}{\
sigma^2 z}\left(\frac{\sigma^2 z}{M^3} + o(z)\right) = \lim_{z\to\infty}\left(1
+ o(1)\right) = \boxed{1}.$$
