- tx + \delta = 0$, hence are algebraic numbers. By the Cayley–Hamilton theorem,
$$A^2 = tA - \delta I.$$
By induction, every power $A^n$ ($n \geq 0$) is a $\mathbb{C}$-linear combinatio

$$\sin A = \alpha A + \beta I \tag{1}$$
for some $\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{C}$ determined by the eigenvalues. By the sp

$$\operatorname{tr}(\sin A) = \sin\lambda_1 + \sin\lambda_2, \qquad \det(\sin A)

radiction. Similarly for $\cos\gamma = \frac{e^{i\gamma}+e^{-i\gamma}}{2}$.

We now show that $\sin A \in M(2,\mathbb{Z})$ forces $t = 0$ and $\delta = 0$.
