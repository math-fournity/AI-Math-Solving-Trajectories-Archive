# Problem

In a specialized digital security protocol, a "Master Key" is defined as a sequence of single-digit passcodes. To ensure maximum entropy, a Master Key must satisfy two strict security criteria:

1.  **Consecutive Pair Rule:** Every pair of adjacent digits in the sequence (read from left to right) must form a two-digit prime number. (Recall that a prime number is an integer greater than 1 whose only divisors are 1 and itself).
2.  **Unique Link Rule:** Every two-digit prime number formed by these adjacent pairs must be unique within the sequence. No two-digit prime can be repeated.

For instance, the sequence **43797** is a valid five-digit key because the pairs 43, 37, 79, and 97 are all prime numbers, and no two are the same. 

While many such sequences exist with more than five digits, the system's security depends on the longest possible chain. What is the largest positive integer that can serve as a Master Key under these conditions?
