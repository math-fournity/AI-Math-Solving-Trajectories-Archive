## Proof

We evaluate $I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{x\sin x}{x^2+4}\,dx$ by consideri
ng the complex integral
$$J = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{x\,e^{ix}}{x^2+4}\,dx,$$
whose imaginary part is $I$ (the real part involves $x\cos x/(x^2+4)$, an odd in
tegrand, hence zero).

**Contour.** Let $f(z)=\dfrac{z\,e^{iz}}{z^2+4}$ and integrate over the closed c
ontour $C_R$ consisting of $[-R,R]$ on the real axis plus the upper semicircle $
\Gamma_R: z=Re^{i\theta},\,0\le\theta\le\pi$.

**Poles.** The denominator factors as $(z-2i)(z+2i)$. Only $z=2i$ lies in the up
per half-plane. It is a simple pole with residue
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=2i} f(z) = \lim_{z\to 2i}(z-2i)\frac{z\,e^{iz}}{(z-2i)(z
+2i)} = \frac{2i\cdot e^{i(2i)}}{2i+2i} = \frac{2i\,e^{-2}}{4i} = \frac{e^{-2}}{
2}.$$

By the residue theorem,
$$\oint_{C_R} f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i\cdot \frac{e^{-2}}{2} = \pi i\,e^{-2}.$$

**Jordan's lemma / arc estimate.** On $\Gamma_R$, write $f(z)=g(z)e^{iz}$ with $
g(z)=z/(z^2+4)$. Since $\deg(z^2+4)-\deg(z)=1$, Jordan's lemma applies: $g(z)\to
 0$ uniformly as $R\to\infty$, and for $z=Re^{i\theta}$ in the upper half-plane,
 $|e^{iz}|=e^{-R\sin\theta}\le 1$ with exponential decay away from the endpoints
. Concretely,
$$\left|\int_{\Gamma_R} f(z)\,dz\right| \le \int_0^\pi \frac{R}{R^2-4}\,e^{-R\si
n\theta}\,R\,d\theta \le \frac{R^2}{R^2-4}\int_0^\pi e^{-R\sin\theta}\,d\theta \
to 0$$
as $R\to\infty$, since $\int_0^\pi e^{-R\sin\theta}\,d\theta = O(1/R)$ by Jordan
's inequality ($\sin\theta\ge 2\theta/\pi$ on $[0,\pi/2]$, symmetric on $[\pi/2,
\pi]$). Thus
$$\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\Gamma_R} f(z)\,dz = 0.$$

**Taking the limit.** Therefore
$$J = \lim_{R\to\infty}\oint_{C_R} f(z)\,dz = \pi i\,e^{-2}.$$

**Extracting the answer.** Writing $e^{ix}=\cos x+i\sin x$,
$$J = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x\cos x}{x^2+4}\,dx + i\int_{-\infty}^{\infty
}\frac{x\sin x}{x^2+4}\,dx = 0 + i\,I.$$
(The cosine integral vanishes because the integrand is odd.) Equating imaginary
parts with $J=\pi i\,e^{-2}$:
$$I = \pi\,e^{-2} = \frac{\pi}{e^2}.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x\sin x}{x^2+4}\,dx = \frac{\
pi}{e^2}}$$
