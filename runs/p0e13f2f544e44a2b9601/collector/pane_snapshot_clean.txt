## Solution

Let $n = -p$ and $m = -q$ where $p, q$ are positive integers. The integral becom
es

$$I = \oint_{|z|=2} \frac{dz}{z^p(1-z)^q}.$$

The integrand $f(z) = \frac{1}{z^p(1-z)^q}$ has poles at $z = 0$ (order $p$) and
 $z = 1$ (order $q$), both lying inside the circle $|z| = 2$.

**Key observation: behavior at infinity.** As $z \to \infty$,

$$f(z) = \frac{1}{z^p(1-z)^q} = \frac{1}{z^p \cdot (-z)^q(1 - 1/z)^q} = \frac{(-
1)^{-q}}{z^{p+q}}\left(1 - \frac{1}{z}\right)^{-q} = \frac{(-1)^q}{z^{p+q}}\left
(1 + \frac{q}{z} + \cdots\right).$$

Since $p, q \geq 1$, we have $p + q \geq 2$, so $f(z) = O(1/z^2)$ as $z \to \inf
ty$.

**Residue at infinity.** The residue at infinity is

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=\infty} f(z) = -\operatorname{Res}_{w=0}\frac{f(1/w)}{w^
2}, \qquad w = 1/z.$$

Computing:

$$\frac{f(1/w)}{w^2} = \frac{1}{w^2}\cdot\frac{w^p\cdot w^q}{(w-1)^q} = \frac{w^
{p+q-2}}{(w-1)^q}.$$

Since $p+q-2 \geq 0$, this expression is analytic at $w = 0$ (the only singulari
ty is at $w = 1$). Therefore

$$\operatorname{Res}_{w=0}\frac{w^{p+q-2}}{(w-1)^q} = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\qua
d \operatorname{Res}_{z=\infty} f(z) = 0.$$

**Applying the residue theorem.** The sum of all residues (including the residue
 at infinity) of a rational function is zero:

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f + \operatorname{Res}_{z=1} f + \operatorname{Res}_{
z=\infty} f = 0.$$

Since $\operatorname{Res}_{z=\infty} f = 0$, we get $\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f
+ \operatorname{Res}_{z=1} f = 0$. Both finite poles lie inside $|z|=2$, so

$$I = 2\pi i\left(\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f + \operatorname{Res}_{z=1} f\right)
 = 2\pi i \cdot 0 = 0.$$

**Verification.** For the case $p = q = 1$: $\frac{1}{z(1-z)} = \frac{1}{z} + \f
rac{1}{1-z}$, giving residues $+1$ at $z=0$ and $-1$ at $z=1$, summing to $0$. ✓
 For $p=2, q=1$: $\frac{1}{z^2(1-z)} = \frac{1}{z}+\frac{1}{z^2}+\frac{1}{1-z}$,
 residues $+1$ at $z=0$ and $-1$ at $z=1$, summing to $0$. ✓

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \oint_{|z|=2} z^n(1-z)^m\,dz = 0}$$
