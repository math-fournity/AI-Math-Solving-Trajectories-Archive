**Theorem.** Let $V$ be a non-zero finite-dimensional vector space and $L \subse
teq \mathfrak{gl}(V)$ a set of pairwise commuting nilpotent linear transformatio
ns. Then there exists a non-zero vector $v \in V$ such that $x(v) = 0$ for all $
x \in L$.

**Proof.** We proceed by induction on $n = \dim V$.

*Base case ($n = 1$).* Every nilpotent endomorphism of a one-dimensional space i
s the zero map. Hence any non-zero $v \in V$ lies in the kernel of every $x \in
L$.

*Inductive step.* Assume the result holds for all spaces of dimension strictly l
ess than $n$, and let $\dim V = n \geq 2$.

If $L$ is empty or every element of $L$ is the zero map, then any non-zero $v \i
n V$ satisfies $x(v)=0$ for all $x \in L$, and we are done. Otherwise, pick $x_0
 \in L$ with $x_0 \neq 0$.

Since $x_0$ is nilpotent, $0$ is its only eigenvalue, so $\ker(x_0) \neq \{0\}$.
 Since $x_0 \neq 0$, we have $\ker(x_0) \neq V$. Thus
$$0 < \dim \ker(x_0) < n.$$

**Claim:** $\ker(x_0)$ is invariant under every $y \in L$.

*Proof of claim.* Let $w \in \ker(x_0)$, so $x_0(w) = 0$. For any $y \in L$, sin
ce $y$ and $x_0$ commute,
$$x_0\bigl(y(w)\bigr) = y\bigl(x_0(w)\bigr) = y(0) = 0,$$
so $y(w) \in \ker(x_0)$. ∎

Therefore the restrictions $\{y|_{\ker(x_0)} : y \in L\}$ form a set of pairwise
 commuting nilpotent endomorphisms of the space $\ker(x_0)$, which has dimension
 strictly between $0$ and $n$. By the induction hypothesis, there exists a non-z
ero vector $v \in \ker(x_0)$ such that $y(v) = 0$ for all $y \in L$.

In particular $x_0(v) = 0$ (since $v \in \ker(x_0)$) and $y(v) = 0$ for all $y \
in L$. This $v$ is the desired common kernel vector.

By induction, the theorem holds for all finite-dimensional non-zero $V$. $\black
square$

**Remark.** This is the key step in Engel's theorem: a Lie subalgebra consisting
 of nilpotent endomorphisms is simultaneously strictly upper-triangularizable. T
he commutativity hypothesis lets us avoid the full Lie-algebra machinery—a singl
e common eigenvector followed by restriction to the invariant kernel suffices, a
nd iteration produces a full flag annihilating all of $L$.
