## Solution

**Yes**, such a finite constant $c$ exists.

**Setup.** Let $Y_i = \sqrt{12}\,(X_i - \tfrac{1}{2})$. Then $Y_i \sim U[-\sqrt{
3},\,\sqrt{3}]$, i.i.d. with $E[Y_i]=0$, $\operatorname{Var}(Y_i)=1$, and

$$\sqrt{12n}\Big(\bar X - \tfrac12\Big) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i =:
S_n.$$

Let $F_n(x) = \mathbb P(S_n \le x)$.

**Key observations.**

1. **Symmetry $\Rightarrow$ vanishing third cumulant.** Since $Y_i$ has a symmet
ric distribution about $0$, all odd cumulants vanish; in particular $\kappa_3 =
E[Y_i^3] = 0$.

2. **Cramér's condition holds.** The characteristic function of $Y_i$ is
$$\varphi_Y(t) = \frac{\sin(\sqrt{3}\,t)}{\sqrt{3}\,t} \to 0 \quad\text{as } |t|
\to\infty,$$
so $\limsup_{|t|\to\infty}|\varphi_Y(t)| = 0 < 1$.

3. **All moments finite.** $Y_i$ is bounded, so $E|Y_i|^k < \infty$ for every $k
$.

**Edgeworth expansion (uniform version).** By the classical Edgeworth expansion
theorem (see e.g. Petrov, *Sums of Independent Random Variables*, Ch. VI, Theore
m 3; or Feller, Vol. II, Ch. XVI.5): if $E|Y_1|^{k}<\infty$ for $k\ge 3$ and Cra
mér's condition holds, then

$$\sup_{x}\left|F_n(x) - \Phi(x) - \sum_{j=1}^{k-2} n^{-j/2}\,P_j(x)\,\varphi(x)
\right| = o\!\left(n^{-(k-2)/2}\right),$$

where $\varphi$ is the standard normal density and each $P_j$ is a polynomial de
termined by the cumulants of $Y_1$ up to order $j+2$.

Taking $k=4$ (finite fourth moment, which certainly holds here):

$$\sup_x\left|F_n(x) - \Phi(x) - n^{-1/2}P_1(x)\,\varphi(x) - n^{-1}P_2(x)\,\var
phi(x)\right| = o(n^{-1}). \tag{$\ast$}$$

The leading polynomial is $P_1(x) = -\frac{\kappa_3}{6}(x^2-1)$. Since $\kappa_3
=0$, **$P_1 \equiv 0$**, and the $n^{-1/2}$ term vanishes entirely.

**Bounding the remainder.** From $(\ast)$ with $P_1=0$:

$$\sup_x\big|F_n(x) - \Phi(x)\big| \;\le\; \frac{1}{n}\sup_x\big|P_2(x)\,\varphi
(x)\big| \;+\; o(n^{-1}).$$

The function $P_2(x)\,\varphi(x)$ is a polynomial times the Gaussian density, he
nce continuous and tending to $0$ as $|x|\to\infty$; therefore $M := \sup_x |P_2
(x)\varphi(x)| < \infty$. Consequently

$$\sup_x\big|F_n(x)-\Phi(x)\big| \le \frac{M}{n} + o(n^{-1}) \le \frac{c_0}{n}$$

for all $n \ge N_0$ (some fixed $N_0$) and a finite constant $c_0$.

**Handling small $n$.** For each of the finitely many $n < N_0$, the quantity $n
\cdot\sup_x|F_n(x)-\Phi(x)|$ is a finite number (both $F_n$ and $\Phi$ are bound
ed CDFs, so the supremum is finite). Set

$$c := \max\!\Big(c_0,\;\max_{1\le n < N_0}\; n\!\sup_x|F_n(x)-\Phi(x)|\Big) < \
infty.$$

Then for **every** $n\ge 1$ and **every** $x\in\mathbb R$,

$$\left|\mathbb P\!\left(\sqrt{12n}\big(\bar X - \tfrac12\big)\le x\right) - \Ph
i(x)\right| \le \frac{c}{n}.$$

**Conclusion.** The existence of such a finite constant $c$ rests on two facts s
pecific to the uniform distribution:

- **Symmetry** of $U[-\sqrt3,\sqrt3]$ kills the $n^{-1/2}$ Edgeworth term ($\kap
pa_3=0$), promoting the uniform error rate from $O(n^{-1/2})$ (generic Berry–Ess
een) to $O(n^{-1})$.
- **Cramér's condition** (characteristic function $\to 0$) and **finite moments
of all orders** guarantee the Edgeworth expansion with a *uniform* $o(n^{-1})$ r
emainder.

Hence a finite constant $c$ satisfying the stated inequality uniformly in $x$ **
does exist**. $\blacksquare$
