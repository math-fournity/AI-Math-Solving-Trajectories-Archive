## Proof

We use the **classification theorem for compact surfaces**: every closed (compac
t, boundaryless) surface is homeomorphic to exactly one of

- $M_0 = S^2$ (the sphere),
- $M_g = \underbrace{T^2\#\cdots\#T^2}_{g}$ for $g\ge 1$ (orientable, genus $g$)
,
- $N_k = \underbrace{\mathbb{RP}^2\#\cdots\#\mathbb{RP}^2}_{k}$ for $k\ge 1$ (no
n-orientable, genus $k$).

(If "compact surface" is taken to allow boundary, every such surface is obtained
 from a closed one by removing open disks; the connected-sum argument below appl
ies to the closed double and descends to the bounded case. We treat the closed c
ase, which is the essential one.)

**Connected-sum arithmetic.** Under connected sum the genera add, with the stand
ard cross-type rules:

$$M_g \# M_h \;\cong\; M_{g+h}, \qquad N_k \# N_m \;\cong\; N_{k+m}, \qquad M_g
\# N_k \;\cong\; N_{2g+k}.$$

These follow from cutting a disk out of each summand and gluing along the bounda
ry, together with $T^2\#\mathbb{RP}^2 \cong \mathbb{RP}^2\#\mathbb{RP}^2$ (Dyck'
s theorem: $M_1\#N_1\cong N_3$).

---

### Step 1. $S^2$ works.

For any compact surface $X$, removing an open disk from $S^2$ leaves a closed di
sk $D^2$. Forming $X\# S^2$ means removing a disk from $X$ and capping the resul
ting boundary circle with $D^2$. Capping a boundary circle of $X\setminus \mathr
ing{D}^2$ with a disk exactly restores $X$. Hence

$$X \# S^2 \;\cong\; X \qquad\text{for every compact surface } X.$$

So $Y\cong S^2$ is a valid solution.

---

### Step 2. No orientable surface $M_g$ with $g\ge 1$ works.

Suppose $Y \cong M_g$ with $g\ge 1$. Take $X = M_1 = T^2$. Then

$$X \# Y \;\cong\; M_1 \# M_g \;\cong\; M_{1+g} \;\cong\; M_{g+1}.$$

By classification $M_{g+1}\not\cong M_1$ since $g+1\ge 2\neq 1$. Thus $X\#Y\not\
cong X$, contradicting the defining property of $Y$. So $g=0$, i.e. $Y\cong S^2$
.

---

### Step 3. No non-orientable surface $N_k$ works.

Suppose $Y\cong N_k$ with $k\ge 1$. Take $X = M_0 = S^2$. Then

$$X \# Y \;\cong\; S^2 \# N_k \;\cong\; N_k.$$

By classification $N_k\not\cong S^2$ (different orientability / Euler characteri
stic: $\chi(N_k)=2-k\neq 2=\chi(S^2)$ for $k\ge 1$). Hence $X\#Y\not\cong X$, ag
ain a contradiction.

(Equally, taking $X=N_1$ gives $X\#Y\cong N_{1+k}\not\cong N_1$.)

---

### Conclusion

The only compact surface $Y$ satisfying $X\#Y\cong X$ for **every** compact surf
ace $X$ is the sphere:

$$\boxed{Y \;\cong\; S^2.}$$

Indeed $S^2$ works (Step 1), and every other compact surface—orientable of posit
ive genus (Step 2) or non-orientable of any genus (Step 3)—fails already on a su
itably chosen test surface $X$, by the additivity of genus under connected sum t
ogether with the classification theorem.
