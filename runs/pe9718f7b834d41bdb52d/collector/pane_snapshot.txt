## Solution

**Setup.** A regular pentagonal pyramid has 6 vertices: an apex $A$ and five bas
e vertices $v_0, v_1, v_2, v_3, v_4$ forming a regular pentagon. The edges are $
Av_i$ (5 lateral edges) and $v_i v_{i+1 \pmod 5}$ (5 base edges). We color with
at most 6 colors so that every edge has two distinct endpoint colors, counting c
olorings up to rotation.

**Rotation group.** The rotational symmetry group of a regular pentagonal pyrami
d is the cyclic group $C_5$ of order 5, generated by the rotation through $72°$
about the axis through the apex and the center of the base. Its elements are rot
ations by $0°, 72°, 144°, 216°, 288°$, each acting as a 5-cycle (or identity) on
 the base vertices while fixing the apex.

**Burnside's lemma.** The number of distinct colorings is
$$\frac{1}{5}\sum_{g\in C_5} |\operatorname{Fix}(g)|.$$

**Non-identity rotations fix nothing.** A rotation by $72°, 144°, 216°$, or $288
°$ acts as a single 5-cycle on the base. A coloring fixed by such a rotation mus
t assign the same color to all five base vertices. But consecutive base vertices
 are joined by an edge, so they must receive *different* colors — a contradictio
n. Hence $|\operatorname{Fix}(g)|=0$ for each non-identity $g$.

**Counting proper colorings (identity).** Fix the apex color $a$; there are 6 ch
oices. Each base vertex $v_i$ is adjacent to $A$, so $v_i$ must avoid color $a$.
 Thus the base vertices are properly colored using colors from the remaining $k=
5$ colors, forming a proper coloring of the cycle $C_5$.

The chromatic polynomial of the cycle $C_n$ is
$$P(C_n, k) = (k-1)^n + (-1)^n(k-1).$$
For $n=5$, $k=5$:
$$P(C_5, 5) = 4^5 + (-1)^5\cdot 4 = 1024 - 4 = 1020.$$

So the total number of proper colorings (labeled) is
$$6 \times 1020 = 6120.$$

**Apply Burnside.**
$$\frac{1}{5}\bigl(6120 + 0 + 0 + 0 + 0\bigr) = \frac{6120}{5} = 1224.$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{1224}$.
