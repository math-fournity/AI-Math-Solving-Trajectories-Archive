## Setup and Key Observation

Label cup holders $C_1, \dots, C_n$ where $C_j$ sits between seats $j$ and $j+1$
 (for $j < n$), and $C_n$ is to the right of seat $n$. Seat 1 is adjacent only t
o $C_1$; seat $k$ ($2 \le k \le n$) is adjacent to $C_{k-1}$ (left) and $C_k$ (r
ight).

**The perfect matching is unique.** Seat 1 can only use $C_1$. Once $C_1$ is tak
en, seat 2 can only use $C_2$, and so on: seat $k \to C_k$ for all $k$. So $p_n$
 equals the probability the random online process produces this specific matchin
g.

## Recurrence

The seating order is a uniformly random permutation. Condition on the first seat
 $s$ chosen (each with probability $1/n$):

**$s = 1$:** Seat 1 is forced to take $C_1$. The remaining seats $\{2,\dots,n\}$
 with cup holders $\{C_2,\dots,C_n\}$ form a subproblem isomorphic to the origin
al with $n-1$ seats (seat 2 now has only $C_2$). Success probability: $p_{n-1}$.

**$s = n$:** Seat $n$ has two empty cup holders; it takes $C_n$ (right) or $C_{n
-1}$ (left) with probability $1/2$ each.
- *Right ($C_n$):* Remaining seats $\{1,\dots,n-1\}$ with cup holders $\{C_1,\do
ts,C_{n-1}\}$ — isomorphic to the original with $n-1$ seats. Probability $p_{n-1
}$.
- *Left ($C_{n-1}$):* The remaining $n-1$ seats have only $n-2$ cup holders ($C_
1,\dots,C_{n-2}$) — no perfect matching exists. Probability $0$.

Contribution: $p_{n-1}/2$.

**Internal $s$ ($2 \le s \le n-1$):** Seat $s$ takes $C_s$ (right) or $C_{s-1}$
(left) with probability $1/2$ each.
- *Right ($C_s$):* The remaining seats split into two **independent** groups: $\
{1,\dots,s-1\}$ with cup holders $\{C_1,\dots,C_{s-1}\}$ (isomorphic to original
, $s-1$ seats) and $\{s+1,\dots,n\}$ with cup holders $\{C_{s+1},\dots,C_n\}$ (s
eat $s+1$ now has only $C_{s+1}$, isomorphic to original, $n-s$ seats). Since th
e groups share no cup holders, success probability is $p_{s-1}\cdot p_{n-s}$.
- *Left ($C_{s-1}$):* The left group $\{1,\dots,s-1\}$ has $s-1$ seats but only
$s-2$ cup holders — impossible. Probability $0$.

Contribution: $p_{s-1}\,p_{n-s}/2$.

Combining (with $p_1 = 1$):

$$p_n = \frac{1}{n}\left[\frac{3}{2}\,p_{n-1} + \frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-2} p_k\
, p_{n-1-k}\right], \qquad n \ge 2.$$

**Verification:** $p_2 = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{3}{2}\cdot 1 = \frac{3}{4}$ ✓. $\
 p_3 = \frac{1}{3}\left[\frac{3}{2}\cdot\frac{3}{4} + \frac{1}{2}\cdot 1\right]
= \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{13}{8} = \frac{13}{24}$ ✓ (confirmed by exhaustive enume
ration).

## Exponential Generating Function

Set $p_n = b_n/(2^{n-1}\,n!)$ with $b_1 = 1$. The recurrence becomes:

$$b_n = 3\,b_{n-1} + 2\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\binom{n-1}{k}\,b_k\,b_{n-1-k}.$$

(Verified: $b_1=1,\ b_2=3,\ b_3=13,\ b_4=75,\ b_5=541$.) Define the EGF $B(x) =
\sum_{n\ge 1} b_n\,\frac{x^n}{n!}$ (with $b_0=0$). The convolution is the coeffi
cient of $x^{n-1}/(n-1)!$ in $B(x)^2$, so for $n\ge 2$:

$$b_n = 3\,b_{n-1} + 2\,[x^{n-1}/(n-1)!]\,B(x)^2.$$

Translating to a differential equation (the $n=1$ term contributes the constant
$b_1=1$):

$$B'(x) = 1 + 3\,B(x) + 2\,B(x)^2 = \bigl(1+B(x)\bigr)\bigl(1+2B(x)\bigr), \qqua
d B(0)=0.$$

## Solving the ODE

Separating variables and using partial fractions $\frac{1}{(1+B)(1+2B)} = \frac{
-1}{1+B}+\frac{2}{1+2B}$:

$$\int \frac{dB}{(1+B)(1+2B)} = \int dx \implies \ln\!\left|\frac{1+2B}{1+B}\rig
ht| = x + \text{const}.$$

From $B(0)=0$: $\ln(1)=0$, so the constant is $0$. Thus $\frac{1+2B}{1+B}=e^x$,
giving:

$$B(x) = \frac{e^x - 1}{2 - e^x}.$$

**Check:** $B(0)=0$ ✓; $B'(x)=\frac{e^x}{(2-e^x)^2}=(1+B)(1+2B)$ ✓.

## Evaluating the Sum

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} p_n = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{b_n}{2^{n-1}\,n!} = 2\sum_
{n=1}^{\infty} b_n\,\frac{(1/2)^n}{n!} = 2\,B\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right).$$

The series converges since $1/2 < \ln 2$ (the singularity of $B$). Therefore:

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} p_n = 2\cdot\frac{e^{1/2}-1}{2-e^{1/2}} = \boxed{\dfrac{2\
bigl(\sqrt{e}-1\bigr)}{2-\sqrt{e}}}.$$

**Numerical check:** $\sqrt{e}\approx 1.6487$, giving $\approx 3.694$. Partial s
ums: $1 + 0.75 + 0.5417 + 0.3906 + 0.2818 + 0.2033 + 0.1466 + 0.1058 + \cdots \a
pprox 3.42$ through $p_8$, consistent with convergence to $\approx 3.694$.
