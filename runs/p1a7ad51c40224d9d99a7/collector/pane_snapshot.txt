Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum number of edges in a graph o
n 10 vertices with no triangles and no 4-cycles.

## Proof

**Model.** Represent the 10 satellites as vertices of a graph, with laser links
as edges. The two constraints translate to:
1. No triangle ($K_3$-free): no three vertices pairwise adjacent.
2. No 4-cycle ($C_4$-free): no four vertices $A,B,C,D$ with edges $AB, BC, CD, D
A$.

Together, these require the graph to have **girth at least 5** (no cycles of len
gth 3 or 4). The "scatter-field" condition (no four coplanar) ensures geometry i
mposes no additional restrictions—this is a pure graph-theoretic extremal proble
m.

**Upper bound: $m \leq 15$.**

Let $G$ be a graph on $n=10$ vertices with girth $\geq 5$, and let $d(v)$ denote
 the degree of vertex $v$.

*Key observation.* For any vertex $v$, consider its neighbors $N(v)$. In a girth
-$\geq 5$ graph:
- **No triangles** ⟹ $N(v)$ is an independent set (no two neighbors of $v$ are a
djacent), so no vertex in $N(v)$ is a "second neighbor" of $v$.
- **No 4-cycles** ⟹ any two neighbors of $v$ share no common neighbor other than
 $v$ itself. Equivalently, the sets $\bigl(N(u)\setminus\{v\}\bigr)$ for $u \in
N(v)$ are pairwise disjoint.

Therefore, the second-neighbors of $v$—counted by $\sum_{u \in N(v)}(d(u)-1)$—ar
e all distinct, and none lies in $\{v\}\cup N(v)$. Since there are only $n-1-d(v
) = 9-d(v)$ vertices outside $\{v\}\cup N(v)$:

$$\sum_{u \in N(v)}(d(u)-1) \leq 9 - d(v) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sum_{u \in
N(v)} d(u) \leq 9.$$

Summing over all vertices $v$:

$$\sum_{v}\sum_{u \in N(v)} d(u) \leq 10 \times 9 = 90.$$

The left-hand side counts, for each vertex $u$, the value $d(u)$ once per neighb
or of $u$—that is, $d(u)$ times total. So:

$$\sum_{u} d(u)^2 \leq 90.$$

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality (or QM–AM):

$$\sum_{u} d(u)^2 \geq \frac{\bigl(\sum_u d(u)\bigr)^2}{n} = \frac{(2m)^2}{10} =
 \frac{4m^2}{10}.$$

Combining:

$$\frac{4m^2}{10} \leq 90 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad m^2 \leq 225 \quad\Longright
arrow\quad m \leq 15.$$

**Lower bound: $m = 15$ is achievable.**

The **Petersen graph** provides a construction with exactly 15 edges and girth 5
.

*Construction (Kneser graph $K(5,2)$).* Take as vertices all $\binom{5}{2}=10$ t
wo-element subsets of $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$. Connect two vertices by an edge if and on
ly if the corresponding subsets are **disjoint**. This gives a 3-regular graph (
each 2-subset is disjoint from exactly $\binom{3}{2}=3$ others) with $10 \times
3 / 2 = 15$ edges.

*Girth verification.* The Petersen graph is a classical **Moore graph** with par
ameters $(10, 3, 5)$:
- **No triangles:** If $A, B, C$ were a triangle, then subsets $A, B, C$ would b
e pairwise disjoint 2-subsets of a 5-element set, requiring $|A|+|B|+|C|=6 > 5$,
 which is impossible.
- **No 4-cycles:** The Petersen graph has girth exactly 5 (it contains 5-cycles
but no shorter cycles), which is a well-established property. Briefly, a 4-cycle
 $A-B-C-D-A$ would require $A \cap B = \emptyset$, $B \cap C = \emptyset$, $C \c
ap D = \emptyset$, $D \cap A = \emptyset$, with $A \cap C \neq \emptyset$ and $B
 \cap D \neq \emptyset$ (otherwise we'd have a shorter cycle or $A=C$). A case a
nalysis on the 5-element ground set shows this is impossible. (Equivalently, the
 Moore bound $n \geq d^2+1 = 10$ is met with equality, which forces girth exactl
y $2\cdot 2+1 = 5$.)

Since the Petersen graph has 15 edges and girth 5, it satisfies both constraints
.

**Conclusion.** The upper bound gives $m \leq 15$, and the Petersen graph achiev
es $m = 15$. The maximum number of laser-link connections is:

$$\boxed{15}$$
