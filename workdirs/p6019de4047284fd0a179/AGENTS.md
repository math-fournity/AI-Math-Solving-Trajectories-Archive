# Problem

In a remote circular sanctuary, a conservationist is planning to set up $n$ discrete observation outposts, where $n \geq 4$. To ensure unbiased data collection, the coordinates for these $n$ outposts—labeled $P_1, P_2, \ldots, P_n$—are selected independently and uniformly at random along the sanctuary’s circular perimeter fence.

Once the outposts are established, a straight security laser is beamed between every pair of adjacent outposts along the perimeter to form a closed, convex $n$-sided polygonal enclosure. The lead engineer needs to assess the structural geometry of this enclosure. Specifically, she is concerned about the interior angles formed at each outpost by the two meeting laser beams.

What is the probability that at least one of these $n$ interior vertex angles is an acute angle (measuring less than 90 degrees)?

The answer should be expressed as a function of $n$.
