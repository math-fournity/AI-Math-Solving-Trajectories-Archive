## Proof

We show that $f$ is smooth on $M$ in the sense of Warner's definition. We must v
erify that for every coordinate map $\phi: V \to \mathbb{R}^n$ (where $(V,\phi)$
 is a chart of $M$), the composition $f \circ \phi^{-1}: \phi(V) \to \mathbb{R}$
 is $C^\infty$.

Let $(V, \phi)$ be any chart on $M$. Since $C^\infty$-ness on an open subset of
$\mathbb{R}^n$ is a local property, it suffices to show that every point $x \in
\phi(V)$ admits an open neighborhood $W_x \subset \phi(V)$ on which $f \circ \ph
i^{-1}$ is $C^\infty$.

Fix $x \in \phi(V)$ and set $p = \phi^{-1}(x) \in V$. By hypothesis, there exist
s an open neighborhood $U_p$ of $p$ in $M$ such that $f|_{U_p}$ is smooth (in Wa
rner's sense). Applying Warner's definition to $f|_{U_p}$ with the coordinate ma
p $\phi$ itself, the function
$$(f|_{U_p}) \circ \phi^{-1}: \phi(U_p \cap V) \to \mathbb{R}$$
is $C^\infty$.

Now $U_p \cap V$ is open (intersection of two open sets) and contains $p$, so $\
phi(U_p \cap V)$ is an open subset of $\phi(V)$ containing $x$. Moreover, for ev
ery $y \in \phi(U_p \cap V)$, we have $\phi^{-1}(y) \in U_p$, hence
$$f \circ \phi^{-1}(y) = (f|_{U_p}) \circ \phi^{-1}(y).$$
Therefore $f \circ \phi^{-1}$ agrees with the $C^\infty$ function $(f|_{U_p}) \c
irc \phi^{-1}$ on the open neighborhood $\phi(U_p \cap V)$ of $x$, so $f \circ \
phi^{-1}$ is $C^\infty$ near $x$.

Since $x \in \phi(V)$ was arbitrary, $f \circ \phi^{-1}$ is $C^\infty$ on all of
 $\phi(V)$. Since the chart $(V, \phi)$ was arbitrary, $f$ satisfies Warner's de
finition of a smooth function on $M$.

**Conclusion:** Yes, $f$ is smooth on $M$. The key point is that smoothness (in
Warner's sense, or any standard sense) is a *local* property: it is checked char
t-by-chart and point-by-point, and the hypothesis provides exactly the local dat
a needed at every point.
