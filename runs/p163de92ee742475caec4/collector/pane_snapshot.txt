Looking at this problem, I need to find the tipping angle of a uniform semicircu
lar disk resting on its diameter.

## Setup

Place the semicircular disk with its diameter along the x-axis from $(-R, 0)$ to
 $(R, 0)$, with the curved arc above. The center of mass of a uniform semicircul
ar lamina lies on the axis of symmetry at distance

$$h = \frac{4R}{3\pi}$$

from the diameter, so the COM is at $(0,\, h)$.

## Tipping Mechanics

When the disk is tipped, it pivots about one endpoint of the diameter — say the
right endpoint $(R, 0)$, which remains on the table. As the tipping angle $\gamm
a$ increases (the diameter tilts up from the table), the COM rises and swings in
 an arc around the pivot.

The disk is **stable** as long as the COM lies on the "inner" side of the pivot
(the side where gravity produces a restoring torque). It **loses balance and tum
bles** when the COM passes directly **above** the pivot point, after which gravi
ty produces an overturning torque.

## Finding the Critical Angle

The vector from the pivot $(R, 0)$ to the COM $(0, h)$ is:

$$\vec{r} = (-R,\; h)$$

This vector makes an angle $\alpha$ with the vertical, where:

$$\tan \alpha = \frac{R}{h} = \frac{R}{4R/(3\pi)} = \frac{3\pi}{4}$$

When the disk is flat ($\gamma = 0$), the COM is tilted to the left of the pivot
 by angle $\alpha$. As the disk rotates clockwise by $\gamma$, the COM swings to
ward the vertical line above the pivot. The critical tipping occurs when $\gamma
 = \alpha$, i.e., when the COM is directly above the pivot:

$$\tan \gamma = \frac{3\pi}{4}$$

$$\gamma = \arctan\!\left(\frac{3\pi}{4}\right)$$

## Numerical Evaluation

$$\frac{3\pi}{4} \approx 2.3562$$

Using $\arctan(x) = 90° - \arctan(1/x)$:

$$\frac{1}{3\pi/4} = \frac{4}{3\pi} \approx 0.42441$$

Since $\tan(23°) \approx 0.42447$, we get $\arctan(4/(3\pi)) \approx 22.997°$, s
o:

$$\gamma = 90° - 22.997° \approx 67.003°$$

## Answer

$$\boxed{\gamma \approx 67°}$$

The minimum tipping angle is **67 degrees** (rounded to the nearest integer).
