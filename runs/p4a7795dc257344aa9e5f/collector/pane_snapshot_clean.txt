## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. For every bounded measurable set $K \subset \mathbb{R}^N$, there
 exists $\eta \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R}^N)$ with $\eta = 1$ on $K$ and $\eta = 0$
outside a neighborhood of $K$.

---

### Construction

Fix $\varepsilon > 0$ (any value; the neighborhood will be $K_\varepsilon$). Def
ine the closed $\varepsilon/2$-neighborhood
$$K_{\varepsilon/2} := \{x \in \mathbb{R}^N : \operatorname{dist}(x, K) \le \var
epsilon/2\}.$$

Since $K$ is bounded, $K_{\varepsilon/2}$ is closed and bounded, hence **compact
**. In particular $\chi_{K_{\varepsilon/2}} \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^N)$.

Let $\rho \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^N)$ be the standard mollifier: $\rho \ge 0$,
 $\operatorname{supp}\rho \subset \overline{B(0,1)}$, $\int \rho = 1$, and set $
\rho_\delta(x) = \delta^{-N}\rho(x/\delta)$. Choose $\delta < \varepsilon/4$ and
 define
$$\eta := \chi_{K_{\varepsilon/2}} * \rho_\delta.$$

### Smoothness

Since $\chi_{K_{\varepsilon/2}} \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ and $\rho_\delta \in C_c
^\infty$, the convolution $\eta$ belongs to $C^\infty(\mathbb{R}^N)$, with $D^\a
lpha \eta = \chi_{K_{\varepsilon/2}} * D^\alpha \rho_\delta$ for every multi-ind
ex $\alpha$.

### $\eta = 1$ on $K$

For $x \in K$, $\operatorname{dist}(x, K_{\varepsilon/2}^c) \ge \varepsilon/2 >
\delta$, so $\overline{B(x,\delta)} \subset K_{\varepsilon/2}$. Therefore
$$\eta(x) = \int_{K_{\varepsilon/2}} \rho_\delta(x-y)\,dy = \int_{B(x,\delta)} \
rho_\delta(x-y)\,dy = \int \rho_\delta = 1.$$

### $\eta = 0$ outside $K_\varepsilon$

For $x \notin K_\varepsilon$, i.e. $\operatorname{dist}(x,K) > \varepsilon$, we
have $\operatorname{dist}(x, K_{\varepsilon/2}) > \varepsilon/2 > \delta$, so $\
overline{B(x,\delta)} \cap K_{\varepsilon/2} = \emptyset$. Hence
$$\eta(x) = \int_{K_{\varepsilon/2}} \rho_\delta(x-y)\,dy = 0.$$

Thus $\eta = 0$ on $\mathbb{R}^N \setminus K_\varepsilon$, and $K_\varepsilon =
\{x : \operatorname{dist}(x,K) \le \varepsilon\}$ is a neighborhood of $K$.

### Range

Since $0 \le \chi_{K_{\varepsilon/2}} \le 1$ and $\rho_\delta \ge 0$ with $\int\
rho_\delta = 1$, we have $0 \le \eta \le 1$ everywhere.

---

### Conclusion

The function $\eta = \chi_{K_{\varepsilon/2}} * \rho_\delta$ (with $\delta < \va
repsilon/4$) is $C^\infty$, equals $1$ on $K$, equals $0$ outside $K_\varepsilon
$, and takes values in $[0,1]$. Measurability of $K$ suffices (it guarantees $\c
hi_{K_{\varepsilon/2}}$ is well-defined and integrable on bounded sets); bounded
ness of $K$ guarantees $K_{\varepsilon/2}$ is compact. $\blacksquare$
