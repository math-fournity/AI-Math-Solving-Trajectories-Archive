## Proof

Let $\mathcal B$ be a Banach space and $F:\mathcal B\to\mathbb R$ be Fréchet dif
ferentiable (the argument is identical for Gâteaux differentiability; Fréchet is
 the "suitable sense" that makes the chain rule clean).

Fix $x\in\mathcal B$ and define $\phi_x:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ by
$$\phi_x(c)=F(cx).$$

Introduce the affine-linear curve
$$\gamma:\mathbb R\to\mathcal B,\qquad \gamma(c)=cx,$$
so that $\phi_x = F\circ\gamma$. The derivative of $\gamma$ is constant:
$$\gamma'(c)=x\in\mathcal B\qquad\text{for all }c\in\mathbb R,$$
since $\gamma(c+h)-\gamma(c)=hx$ and $\|hx\|/|h|=\|x\|\to\|x\|$, with the linear
 map $h\mapsto hx$ being bounded of norm $\|x\|$.

Because $F$ is Fréchet differentiable at the point $cx\in\mathcal B$ and $\gamma
$ is differentiable at $c$, the **chain rule for Fréchet derivatives** applies:
$\phi_x=F\circ\gamma$ is differentiable at $c$ and
$$\phi_x'(c)=DF(cx)\bigl[\gamma'(c)\bigr]=DF(cx)[x].$$

Here $DF(cx)\in\mathcal B^*$ is the Fréchet derivative of $F$ at the point $cx$,
 a bounded linear functional on $\mathcal B$, and $DF(cx)[x]$ denotes its evalua
tion on the vector $x$.

**Equivalence with the directional (Gâteaux) form.** Unpacking the definition of
 the Fréchet derivative,
$$F(cx+h)-F(cx)=DF(cx)[h]+o(\|h\|),\qquad h\to 0.$$
Setting $h=tx$ with $t\to 0$ gives
$$\phi_x(c+t)-\phi_x(c)=F(cx+tx)-F(cx)=DF(cx)[tx]+o(|t|\|x\|)=t\,DF(cx)[x]+o(|t|
),$$
so dividing by $t$ and letting $t\to 0$,
$$\phi_x'(c)=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\phi_x(c+t)-\phi_x(c)}{t}=DF(cx)[x].$$
This recovers the same result directly from the difference quotient, confirming
consistency.

### Result

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \frac{d}{dc}\phi_x(c)=\frac{d}{dc}F(cx)=DF(cx)[x],}$$

i.e. the derivative of $c\mapsto F(cx)$ at $c$ is the Fréchet derivative of $F$
at the point $cx$, evaluated on the direction $x$. In directional-derivative not
ation this is the derivative of $F$ at $cx$ in the direction $x$:
$$\frac{d}{dc}F(cx)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}F(cx+tx).$$
