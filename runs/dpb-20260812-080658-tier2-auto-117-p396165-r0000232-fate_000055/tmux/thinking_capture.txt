Proof.

\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$, the group of integers modulo $p$ under addition. This
is a cyclic group of order $p$, generated by $\bar{1}$ (the class of $1$).

Let $H \leq \mathbb{Z}_p$ be any subgroup. We show that $H$ is either
${\bar{0}}$ (the trivial subgroup) or $\mathbb{Z}_p$ itself.

Since $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is cyclic (generated by $\bar{1}$), every subgroup of a
cyclic group is cyclic. So $H = \langle \bar{k} \rangle$ for some $\bar{k} \in
\mathbb{Z}_p$.

Case 1: $\bar{k} = \bar{0}$. Then $H = {\bar{0}}$, the trivial subgroup.

Case 2: $\bar{k} \neq \bar{0}$. Then $k \not\equiv 0 \pmod{p}$, so $\gcd(k, p) =
1$ (since $p$ is prime and $1 \leq k \leq p-1$). The order of $\bar{k}$ in $
\mathbb{Z}_p$ is $\frac{p}{\gcd(k,p)} = \frac{p}{1} = p$. Thus $\bar{k}$
generates all of $\mathbb{Z}_p$, so $H = \langle \bar{k} \rangle = \mathbb{Z}
_p$.

Alternatively, by Lagrange's theorem: $|H|$ divides $|\mathbb{Z}_p| = p$. Since
$p$ is prime, $|H| \in {1, p}$. If $|H| = 1$, then $H = {\bar{0}}$. If $|H| =
p$, then $H = \mathbb{Z}_p$.

In either case, $H$ is either trivial or the whole group, so $\mathbb{Z}_p$ has
no proper nontrivial subgroups. $\blacksquare$
