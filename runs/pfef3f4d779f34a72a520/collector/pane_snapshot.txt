## Proof

Let $H$ be a separable Hilbert space with orthonormal basis $\{e_n\}_{n=1}^\inft
y$. Define the diagonal operator $D:H\to H$ by
$$Du = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^3}\langle u, e_n\rangle\, e_n,$$
and set $z = \sqrt{6/\pi^2}\sum_{n=1}^\infty e_n/n$, so that $T_k = D + k\langle
\cdot,\, z\rangle z$.

**Step 1: Preliminaries.** First, $z$ is a unit vector:
$$\|z\|^2 = \frac{6}{\pi^2}\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{6}{\pi^2}\cdo
t\frac{\pi^2}{6} = 1.$$

The operator $T_k$ is self-adjoint (since $D$ is self-adjoint and $k\langle\cdot
,z\rangle z$ is self-adjoint for real $k$) and compact (since $D$ is compact and
 a rank-one operator is compact). By the spectral theorem for compact self-adjoi
nt operators, the spectrum of $T_k$ consists of at most countably many real eige
nvalues of finite multiplicity, accumulating only at $0$.

**Step 2: The quadratic form.** For any $u \in H$:
$$\langle T_k u,\, u\rangle = \langle Du,\, u\rangle + k\,|\langle u,\, z\rangle
|^2 = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{|\langle u,\, e_n\rangle|^2}{n^3} + k\,|\langle u,
\, z\rangle|^2.$$

Since $1/n^3 > 0$ for every $n$, the first term satisfies $\langle Du, u\rangle
\geq 0$ for all $u$.

**Step 3: At most one negative eigenvalue.** Suppose for contradiction that $T_k
$ has at least two negative eigenvalues (counting multiplicity) $\lambda_1 \leq
\lambda_2 < 0$, with corresponding orthonormal eigenvectors $u_1, u_2$. Set $M =
 \operatorname{span}\{u_1, u_2\}$, a $2$-dimensional subspace.

For any nonzero $u = \alpha u_1 + \beta u_2 \in M$:
$$\langle T_k u,\, u\rangle = |\alpha|^2\lambda_1 + |\beta|^2\lambda_2 < 0,$$
since both eigenvalues are strictly negative.

Now consider the linear functional $\ell: M \to \mathbb{R}$ (or $\mathbb{C}$) gi
ven by $\ell(u) = \langle u, z\rangle$. Since $M$ is $2$-dimensional and $\ell$
imposes a single linear constraint, the kernel $\ker(\ell|_M)$ has dimension at
least $2 - 1 = 1$. Hence there exists a nonzero $w \in M$ with $\langle w, z\ran
gle = 0$.

For this $w$, the quadratic form evaluates to:
$$\langle T_k w,\, w\rangle = \underbrace{\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{|\langle w, e_
n\rangle|^2}{n^3}}_{\geq\, 0} + k\cdot\underbrace{|\langle w, z\rangle|^2}_{=\,
0} = \langle Dw, w\rangle \geq 0.$$

But since $w \in M\setminus\{0\}$, we also have $\langle T_k w, w\rangle < 0$ fr
om the computation above. This is a contradiction.

Therefore, $T_k$ cannot possess two or more negative eigenvalues. We conclude th
at for $k < 0$, the operator $T_k$ has **at most one** negative eigenvalue. $\bl
acksquare$
