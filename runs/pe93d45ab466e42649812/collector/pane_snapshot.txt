## Solution

We compute the variance of
$$X = \underbrace{\int_0^2 2t\, dW(t)}_{I_1} + \underbrace{\int_4^6 W(t)\, dW(t)
}_{I_2}.$$

### Step 1: Variance of $I_1$

Since $2t$ is deterministic, the Itô isometry gives
$$\operatorname{Var}(I_1) = \int_0^2 (2t)^2\, dt = \int_0^2 4t^2\, dt = 4\cdot\f
rac{8}{3} = \frac{32}{3}.$$

### Step 2: Variance of $I_2$

For the random integrand $W(t)$, the Itô isometry gives
$$\operatorname{Var}(I_2) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_4^6 W(t)^2\, dt\right] = \int
_4^6 \mathbb{E}[W(t)^2]\, dt = \int_4^6 t\, dt = \frac{36-16}{2} = 10.$$

(Cross-check via Itô's formula: $\int_4^6 W\,dW = \tfrac12(W(6)^2-W(4)^2)-1$. Wi
th $A=W(6)\sim N(0,6)$, $B=W(4)\sim N(0,4)$, $\operatorname{Cov}(A,B)=4$, one ob
tains $\operatorname{Var}(\tfrac12(A^2-B^2))=\tfrac14(72+32-64)=10$. ✓)

### Step 3: Covariance $\operatorname{Cov}(I_1, I_2)$

Although the intervals $[0,2]$ and $[4,6]$ are disjoint, $I_2$ depends on $W(4)$
, which depends on increments over $[0,4]\supset[0,2]$, so independence is **not
** automatic. We compute the covariance directly.

$I_1 = \int_0^2 2t\, dW(t)$ is a centered Gaussian random variable (linear funct
ional of $W$ over $[0,2]$). Using Itô's formula,
$$I_2 = \tfrac12\bigl(W(6)^2 - W(4)^2\bigr) - 1,$$
so
$$\operatorname{Cov}(I_1, I_2) = \tfrac12\Bigl(\mathbb{E}[I_1 W(6)^2] - \mathbb{
E}[I_1 W(4)^2]\Bigr).$$

The triple $(I_1, W(6), W(4))$ is **jointly Gaussian with zero mean**. By the Is
serlis (Wick) theorem, every third moment of centered jointly Gaussian variables
 vanishes:
$$\mathbb{E}[I_1\, W(6)^2] = 0, \qquad \mathbb{E}[I_1\, W(4)^2] = 0.$$

Hence
$$\operatorname{Cov}(I_1, I_2) = 0.$$

### Step 4: Combine

$$\operatorname{Var}(X) = \operatorname{Var}(I_1) + \operatorname{Var}(I_2) + 2\
operatorname{Cov}(I_1,I_2) = \frac{32}{3} + 10 + 0 = \boxed{\dfrac{62}{3}}.$$
