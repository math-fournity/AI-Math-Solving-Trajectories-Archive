## Proof

We compute the Laplacian of the rescaled function and examine its $L^{1}$ norm.

**Step 1: Scaling of $\Delta\phi_{t}$.** By definition $\phi_{t}(x)=t^{-n}\phi(x
/t)$. Differentiating twice in the $i$-th coordinate,

$$\partial_{i}\phi_{t}(x)=t^{-n-1}(\partial_{i}\phi)(x/t),\qquad \partial_{i}^{2
}\phi_{t}(x)=t^{-n-2}(\partial_{i}^{2}\phi)(x/t).$$

Summing over $i$,

$$\Delta\phi_{t}(x)=t^{-n-2}(\Delta\phi)(x/t).$$

**Step 2: The $L^{1}$ norm of $\Delta\phi_{t}$.** Using the change of variables
$u=y/t$, $dy=t^{n}\,du$,

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta\phi_{t}(y)|\,dy=t^{-n-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|(\D
elta\phi)(y/t)|\,dy=t^{-n-2}\cdot t^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|(\Delta\phi)(u)|\,d
u=t^{-2}\|\Delta\phi\|_{L^{1}}.$$

**Step 3: $\|\Delta\phi\|_{L^{1}}>0$.** Suppose, for contradiction, that $\Delta
\phi\equiv 0$. Then $\phi$ is harmonic on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$. Since $\phi$ is a te
st function (smooth with compact support), a harmonic function with compact supp
ort must vanish identically: on the open set where $\phi$ is supported, the maxi
mum principle forces $\phi$ to be constant on each connected component of its su
pport's interior, and continuity across the boundary of the support (where $\phi
=0$) forces that constant to be $0$. Hence $\phi\equiv 0$, contradicting $\int\p
hi=1$. Therefore $\Delta\phi\not\equiv 0$, and since $\Delta\phi$ is continuous,
 $\|\Delta\phi\|_{L^{1}}>0$.

**Step 4: The limit.** From Step 2,

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta\phi_{t}(y)|\,dy=t^{-2}\|\Delta\phi\|_{L^{1}},$$

with $\|\Delta\phi\|_{L^{1}}$ a fixed positive constant. As $t\to 0^{+}$, $t^{-2
}\to+\infty$, so

$$\limsup_{t\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta\phi_{t}(y)|\,dy=+\infty.$$

**Conclusion.** The limit superior is **not bounded**; it diverges to $+\infty$
at rate $t^{-2}$. The rescaling $\phi_{t}(x)=t^{-n}\phi(x/t)$ preserves the $L^{
1}$ norm of $\phi_{t}$ itself but amplifies the $L^{1}$ norm of its Laplacian by
 the factor $t^{-2}$, reflecting that each differentiation in the spatial variab
le contributes a factor of $t^{-1}$, and the Laplacian is a second-order operato
r.
