# The Topology on $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$

## Setup

Let $\mathbb{Z}$ carry the discrete topology. The character group (Pontryagin du
al) is
$$\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} = \operatorname{Hom}_{\text{cts}}(\mathbb{Z},\, \mathbb{T
}),$$
where $\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ is the circle group with its usual co
mpact quotient topology, and the group operation on $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is po
intwise addition.

The topology on $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is the **compact-open topology**, which f
or a locally compact abelian group $G$ coincides with the topology of uniform co
nvergence on compact sets.

## Step 1: Every homomorphism $\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{T}$ is continuous

Since $\mathbb{Z}$ is discrete, *every* subset is open. For any homomorphism $\c
hi:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{T}$ and any open $U\subseteq\mathbb{T}$, the preimage $\
chi^{-1}(U)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}$ is automatically open. Hence every group homomor
phism is continuous, and
$$\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} = \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z},\,\mathbb{T}).$$

## Step 2: Group-theoretic identification $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\cong\mathbb{T}$

A homomorphism $\chi:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{T}$ is completely determined by its va
lue on the generator $1\in\mathbb{Z}$, since
$$\chi(n) = n\,\chi(1) \quad\text{(additive notation in }\mathbb{T}\text{)}.$$
Conversely, for any $t\in\mathbb{T}$, the map $\chi_t(n)=nt$ is a well-defined h
omomorphism. This gives a group isomorphism
$$\Phi:\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{T},\qquad \Phi(\chi)=\chi(1).$
$

## Step 3: The compact-open topology on $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$

The compact-open topology has subbasic open sets
$$V(K,U)=\bigl\{\chi\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}:\chi(K)\subseteq U\bigr\},$$
where $K\subseteq\mathbb{Z}$ is compact and $U\subseteq\mathbb{T}$ is open.

**Key fact:** In a discrete space, compact sets are exactly the **finite** sets.
 So $K$ ranges over finite subsets of $\mathbb{Z}$.

A basic neighborhood of $\chi_0\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is therefore of the form
$$\bigl\{\chi : \chi(k_i)\in U_i\text{ for }i=1,\dots,m\bigr\},$$
where $K=\{k_1,\dots,k_m\}$ is finite and each $U_i$ is an open neighborhood of
$\chi_0(k_i)$ in $\mathbb{T}$.

## Step 4: $\Phi$ is a homeomorphism

**Continuity of $\Phi$.** Take the subbasic open set $V(\{1\},U)=\{\chi:\chi(1)\
in U\}$. Under $\Phi$ this maps to $U\subseteq\mathbb{T}$, which is open. Since
the sets $V(\{1\},U)$ (with $K=\{1\}$) already form a subbase, $\Phi$ is continu
ous.

**Openness of $\Phi$.** Let $V(K,U)$ be a subbasic open set in $\widehat{\mathbb
{Z}}$ with $K=\{k_1,\dots,k_m\}$ finite. A character $\chi$ lies in $V(K,U)$ iff
 $\chi(k_i)\in U$ for all $i$, i.e., $k_i\,\chi(1)\in U$. For $k_i\neq 0$ this m
eans $\chi(1)\in \frac{1}{k_i}U$ (the image of $U$ under multiplication by $k_i^
{-1}$, which is an open map on $\mathbb{T}$). For $k_i=0$ the condition $\chi(0)
=0\in U$ is either vacuous (if $0\in U$) or impossible. Thus
$$\Phi\bigl(V(K,U)\bigr)=\bigcap_{i:k_i\neq 0}\tfrac{1}{k_i}\,U,$$
which is a finite intersection of open sets, hence open in $\mathbb{T}$.

Since $\Phi$ is a bijective, continuous, open group homomorphism, it is an isomo
rphism of topological groups.

## Conclusion

The character group $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, equipped with the compact-open topol
ogy (the Pontryagin dual topology), is isomorphic as a topological group to the
circle group $\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ with its standard compact quotie
nt topology:

$$\boxed{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\;\cong\;\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\quad\text
{(compact, with the usual circle topology).}}$$

This is consistent with Pontryagin duality: the dual of a discrete group is comp
act, and the dual of $\mathbb{Z}$ (the free abelian group on one generator) is $
\mathbb{T}$ (the "divisible" circle), reflecting the duality between discretenes
s and compactness.
