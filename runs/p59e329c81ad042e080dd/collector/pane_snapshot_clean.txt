## Proof

**Claim.** The maximum number of algebraically independent homogeneous polynomia
ls of degree $d \geq 2$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ is exactly $n$. In partic
ular, it cannot be as large as $n^2$ or $n^3$.

---

### Upper bound: at most $n$

Algebraic independence of polynomials with integer coefficients over $\mathbb{Z}
$ is equivalent to algebraic independence over $\mathbb{Q}$ (a polynomial relati
on with integer coefficients is the same as one with rational coefficients, afte
r clearing denominators). So we work in the field $K = \mathbb{Q}(x_1,\dots,x_n)
$.

The field $K = \mathbb{Q}(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ is the fraction field of the polynomia
l ring in $n$ indeterminates, hence

$$\operatorname{tr.deg}_{\mathbb{Q}} K = n.$$

A basic theorem of field theory states: *any set of algebraically independent el
ements of $K$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ has cardinality at most $\operatorname{tr.deg}_{
\mathbb{Q}} K$.* Therefore any collection of algebraically independent elements
of $K$ — and in particular any collection of algebraically independent homogeneo
us polynomials of degree $d$ — has size at most $n$.

This immediately rules out $n^2$ and $n^3$ (for $n \geq 2$), since $n^2 > n$ and
 $n^3 > n$.

### Lower bound: $n$ is achievable

Consider the $n$ polynomials

$$f_i = x_i^d, \qquad i = 1, \dots, n.$$

Each $f_i$ is homogeneous of degree $d \geq 2$ with integer coefficients. We cla
im they are algebraically independent over $\mathbb{Q}$.

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a nonzero polynomial $P \in \mathb
b{Q}[y_1,\dots,y_n]$ with

$$P(x_1^d, \dots, x_n^d) = 0 \quad \text{in } \mathbb{Q}[x_1,\dots,x_n].$$

Write $P = \sum_{\alpha} c_\alpha\, y_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots y_n^{\alpha_n}$ with $c
_\alpha \neq 0$ for at least one multi-index $\alpha$. Substituting $y_i = x_i^d
$ gives

$$P(x_1^d,\dots,x_n^d) = \sum_{\alpha} c_\alpha\, x_1^{d\alpha_1}\cdots x_n^{d\a
lpha_n}.$$

Since $d \geq 1$, the map $\alpha \mapsto (d\alpha_1,\dots,d\alpha_n)$ on multi-
indices is injective. Hence distinct monomials $y^\alpha$ in $P$ produce distinc
t monomials $x^{d\alpha}$ in the substitution. Since the monomials $x_1^{d\alpha
_1}\cdots x_n^{d\alpha_n}$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$ (they are
distinct monomials in the indeterminates $x_1,\dots,x_n$), the only way the sum
vanishes is if every $c_\alpha = 0$, contradicting $P \neq 0$.

Therefore no nonzero polynomial relation exists, and $\{x_1^d, \dots, x_n^d\}$ i
s algebraically independent.

### Conclusion

The upper bound gives $\leq n$ and the explicit construction gives $\geq n$, so
the maximum number of algebraically independent homogeneous polynomials of degre
e $d \geq 2$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ is **exactly $n$**. It is limited to
 $n$ and cannot be as large as $n^2$ or $n^3$.
