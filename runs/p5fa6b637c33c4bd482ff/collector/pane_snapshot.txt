## Solution

**The statement is FALSE.**

### Definitions

A basic sequence \((x_n)\) in a Banach space is a Schauder basis of its closed s
pan \([x_n]\). Two basic sequences \((x_n)\), \((y_n)\) are **equivalent** if th
ere is a bounded linear isomorphism \(T:[x_n]\to[y_n]\) with \(T(x_n)=y_n\) for
all \(n\); equivalently, \(\sum a_n x_n\) converges iff \(\sum a_n y_n\) converg
es.

A basic sequence is **unconditional** if every permutation \((x_{\pi(n)})\) is a
 basic sequence equivalent to \((x_n)\).

### Key characterization (standard)

For a basic sequence \((x_n)\) the following are equivalent:
1. \((x_n)\) is unconditional;
2. every permutation of \((x_n)\) is a basic sequence;
3. every permutation of \((x_n)\) is a basic sequence **equivalent** to \((x_n)\
).

Equivalently, the basis is unconditional iff the coordinate projections \(P_A\!\
left(\sum a_n x_n\right)=\sum_{n\in A}a_n x_n\) are uniformly bounded over all f
inite \(A\subset\mathbb N\). (See e.g. Albiac–Kalton, *Topics in Banach Space Th
eory*, Thm. 3.3.1, or Lindenstrauss–Tzafriri, *Classical Banach Spaces I*.)

Hence the statement in the problem is exactly the assertion "every basic sequenc
e is unconditional." It suffices to exhibit a **conditional** basic sequence.

### Counterexample: the summing basis of \(c_0\)

Let \(c_0\) be the Banach space of null sequences with the sup norm, and let \(e
_n\) be the standard unit vectors. Define
\[
s_n := e_1+e_2+\cdots+e_n = (\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{n},0,0,\ldots).
\]
The sequence \((s_n)\) is a Schauder basis of \(c_0\) (the **summing basis**): e
very \(x=(x_1,x_2,\ldots)\in c_0\) admits the unique expansion
\[
x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n s_n,\qquad a_n = x_n-x_{n+1},
\]
since the \(N\)-th partial sum has \(j\)-th coordinate \(x_j-x_{N+1}\to x_j\).

**It is conditional.** For a finite set \(A\subset\mathbb N\) the projection \(P
_A\) acts by
\[
\bigl(P_A x\bigr)_j = \sum_{\substack{n\in A\\ n\ge j}} (x_n-x_{n+1}).
\]
Take \(A=\{2,4,6,\ldots,2N\}\) and \(x=\sum_{n=1}^{2N}(-1)^n s_n\), i.e. \(a_n=(
-1)^n\). Then
\[
x_j=\sum_{n=j}^{2N}(-1)^n,
\]
an alternating sum, so \(\|x\|_\infty\le 1\). But
\[
P_A x = \sum_{\substack{n\le 2N\\ n\text{ even}}}(-1)^n s_n = \sum_{k=1}^{N} s_{
2k},
\]
whose first coordinate equals \(N\), so \(\|P_A x\|_\infty = N\). Thus \(\|P_A\|
\ge N\). Since \(N\) is arbitrary,
\[
\sup_{A\text{ finite}}\|P_A\|=\infty,
\]
so \((s_n)\) is **not** unconditional, i.e. it is conditional.

### Conclusion

Because \((s_n)\) is a conditional basic sequence, by the characterization above
 there exists a permutation \(\pi\) of \(\mathbb N\) such that \((s_{\pi(n)})\)
is **not** equivalent to \((s_n)\) (indeed it fails to even be a basic sequence)
. Therefore it is **not** true that every permutation of a basic sequence is equ
ivalent to the entire sequence.

\[
\boxed{\text{The statement is FALSE.}}
\]
