## Proof

Split the integral into two parts:
$$I = \underbrace{\int_{0}^{\infty} f(x^n + x^{-n})\,\frac{\log x}{x}\,dx}_{I_1}
 \;+\; \underbrace{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{1+x^2}}_{I_2}.$$

### Evaluating $I_2$

This is the standard arctangent integral:
$$I_2 = \Big[\arctan x\Big]_{0}^{\infty} = \frac{\pi}{2} - 0 = \frac{\pi}{2}.$$

### Evaluating $I_1$

Make the substitution $u = \log x$, so $x = e^u$, $dx = e^u\,du$, and
$$\frac{\log x}{x}\,dx = \frac{u}{e^u}\,e^u\,du = u\,du.$$
As $x$ ranges over $(0,\infty)$, $u$ ranges over $(-\infty,\infty)$. Also
$$x^n + x^{-n} = e^{nu} + e^{-nu} = 2\cosh(nu).$$
Hence
$$I_1 = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f\!\big(2\cosh(nu)\big)\,u\,du.$$

Now observe the **parity** of the integrand. The function $2\cosh(nu)$ is **even
** in $u$ (since $\cosh$ is even). Since $f$ is a function of $2\cosh(nu)$ alone
, the factor $f(2\cosh(nu))$ is **even** in $u$. The remaining factor $u$ is **o
dd**. Therefore the full integrand
$$g(u) := f\!\big(2\cosh(nu)\big)\,u$$
is **odd**: $g(-u) = f(2\cosh(nu))\cdot(-u) = -g(u)$.

For the integral $I_1$ to exist (as the problem asks us to evaluate it, converge
nce is implicit), consider the improper integral as independent limits at both e
nds:
$$I_1 = \lim_{\substack{A\to -\infty\\ B\to +\infty}} \int_{A}^{B} g(u)\,du.$$
Decomposing using the oddness of $g$:
$$\int_{A}^{B} g(u)\,du = \int_{0}^{B} g(u)\,du - \int_{0}^{-A} g(u)\,du.$$
Since $g$ is odd, the two one-sided improper integrals $\int_{0}^{\infty} g(u)\,
du$ and $\int_{0}^{\infty} g(u)\,du$ (the second arising from $-A\to\infty$) are
 **identical**. Convergence of $I_1$ forces each to converge, so they cancel:
$$I_1 = \int_{0}^{\infty} g(u)\,du - \int_{0}^{\infty} g(u)\,du = 0.$$

(Equivalently: the symmetric principal value of an odd integrand over $(-\infty,
\infty)$ is $0$, and convergence of the improper integral guarantees it agrees w
ith the principal value.)

### Combining

$$I = I_1 + I_2 = 0 + \frac{\pi}{2} = \frac{\pi}{2}.$$

The result is **independent of the continuous function $f$** and of $n$.

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \int_{0}^{\infty} \left[ f(x^n + x^{-n}) \frac{\log x}{x}
 + \frac{1}{1+x^2} \right] dx = \frac{\pi}{2}}$$
