## Proof

Let $B_t$ be a standard Brownian motion with $B_0 = 0$, and $T_a = \inf\{t \ge 0
 : B_t = a\}$.

**Key observation.** Since $B_0 = 0$ and $2 > 1 > 0$, by continuity of Brownian
paths, to reach level $2$ the process must first pass through level $1$. Hence $
T_1 \le T_2$ a.s., and in fact $T_1 < T_2$ a.s. (the probability of hitting both
 levels at the same instant is $0$). Therefore the condition $T_1 < T_{-1} < T_2
$ is equivalent to requiring $T_1 < T_{-1}$ **and** $T_{-1} < T_2$:

$$P(T_1 < T_{-1} < T_2) = P(T_1 < T_{-1}) \cdot P(T_{-1} < T_2 \mid T_1 < T_{-1}
).$$

**Step 1: $P(T_1 < T_{-1})$.** The levels $-1$ and $1$ are symmetric about the s
tarting point $0$. By symmetry of Brownian motion,

$$P(T_1 < T_{-1}) = \frac{1}{2}.$$

**Step 2: $P(T_{-1} < T_2 \mid T_1 < T_{-1})$.** On the event $\{T_1 < T_{-1}\}$
, at time $T_1$ the Brownian motion is at position $1$. By the **strong Markov p
roperty**, the process $B_{T_1 + t} - B_{T_1}$ is an independent standard Browni
an motion, so the future behavior depends only on the current position $x = 1$.
We need the probability that, starting from $x = 1$, the process hits $-1$ befor
e $2$.

This is the classical gambler's ruin probability for Brownian motion. For a Brow
nian motion starting at $x \in (a, b)$, the probability of hitting $a$ before $b
$ is the harmonic function

$$u(x) = \frac{b - x}{b - a},$$

which solves $u'' = 0$ with boundary conditions $u(a) = 1$, $u(b) = 0$.

Substituting $a = -1$, $b = 2$, $x = 1$:

$$P_1(T_{-1} < T_2) = \frac{2 - 1}{2 - (-1)} = \frac{1}{3}.$$

**Combining.**

$$P(T_1 < T_{-1} < T_2) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{3} = \boxed{\frac{1}{6}}.$$
