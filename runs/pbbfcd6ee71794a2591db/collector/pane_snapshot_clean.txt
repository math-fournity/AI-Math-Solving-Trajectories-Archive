## Analysis

This is **Erdős Problem 683**, a well-known open problem in number theory.

The main theorem `erdos_683` is tagged `@[category research open]` — it is an **
open conjecture**. It asserts the existence of a constant $c > 0$ such that for
all $0 < k < n$,

$$P(n, k) > \min\{n - k + 1,\; k^{1+c}\},$$

where $P(n,k)$ is the largest prime factor of $\binom{n}{k}$.

**What is known (stated in the problem itself):**
- **Sylvester–Schur (1934):** $P(n,k) > k$ for $k \le n/2$ — solved.
- **Erdős (1955):** $P(n,k) \gg k \log k$ for $k \le n/2$ — solved.
- **Heuristic:** $P(n,k) > e^{c\sqrt{k}}$ is expected — open.
- A note indicates Erdős 683 and Erdős 961 are conjectured equivalent.

**Why this cannot be solved:**

The conjecture requires proving a power-type lower bound $k^{1+c}$ (in the regim

nomial coefficients. This is strictly stronger than the Sylvester–Schur theorem
($P > k$, i.e., exponent $1$) and even stronger than Erdős's $k \log k$ improvem
ent. No such constant $c > 0$ has been established in the literature; the proble
