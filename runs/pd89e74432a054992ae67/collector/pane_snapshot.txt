## Proof

Let $C \subseteq [0,1]$ be the Cantor set and $f:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ the Cantor (Devi
l's staircase) function. We determine the Lebesgue measure of $f(C)$.

**Step 1: Description of $C$ via ternary expansions.**

The Cantor set consists exactly of those $x\in[0,1]$ admitting a ternary expansi
on
$$x = 0.a_1 a_2 a_3 \ldots \text{ (base 3)}, \qquad a_i \in \{0,2\}.$$

**Step 2: Action of $f$ on $C$ via binary expansions.**

The Cantor function is characterized on $C$ by
$$f\!\left(0.a_1 a_2 a_3\ldots_{\,(3)},\; a_i\in\{0,2\}\right) = 0.\tfrac{a_1}{2
}\,\tfrac{a_2}{2}\,\tfrac{a_3}{2}\ldots_{\,(2)}.$$
That is, each ternary digit $a_i\in\{0,2\}$ is replaced by the binary digit $a_i
/2\in\{0,1\}$.

**Step 3: $f(C) = [0,1]$.**

Every $y\in[0,1]$ possesses a binary expansion $y = 0.b_1 b_2 b_3\ldots$ (base 2
) with $b_i\in\{0,1\}$. Setting $a_i = 2b_i\in\{0,2\}$ gives a point
$$x = 0.a_1 a_2 a_3\ldots \text{ (base 3)} \in C$$
with $f(x) = y$. Hence $f(C)=[0,1]$.

(Equivalently: $f$ is constant on each removed middle-third interval, so $f([0,1
]\setminus C)$ is at most countable; since $f$ is continuous and surjective onto
 $[0,1]$, removing at most countably many values still leaves $f(C)=[0,1]$.)

**Step 4: The measure.**

The Lebesgue measure of $[0,1]$ is $1$. Therefore
$$\lambda\!\big(f(C)\big) = \lambda([0,1]) = 1.$$

**Answer:** The Lebesgue measure of the image of the Cantor set under the Cantor
 function is $\boxed{1}$.
