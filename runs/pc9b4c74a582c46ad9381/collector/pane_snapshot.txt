**Answer: No.** Pointwise convergence of $g_n$ to $g$ does not imply that $\math
bb{E}\!\left[\int_0^1 |g_n(x)-g(x)|\,dx\right]\to 0$.

**Counterexample.** Take the probability space to be trivial (a single point), s
o that "random functions" are just deterministic functions and expectation is th
e identity. Define

$$g(x) \equiv 0, \qquad g_n(x) = n\,\mathbf{1}_{(0,\,1/n]}(x), \quad x\in[0,1].$
$

**Pointwise convergence.** Fix any $x\in[0,1]$.
- If $x=0$: $g_n(0)=0$ for all $n$, so $g_n(0)\to 0=g(0)$.
- If $x>0$: choose $N$ with $1/N < x$. Then for all $n\ge N$, $x\notin(0,1/n]$,
hence $g_n(x)=0=g(x)$.

Thus $g_n(x)\to g(x)$ for every $x\in[0,1]$.

**The integral does not vanish.** For every $n$,

$$\int_0^1 |g_n(x)-g(x)|\,dx = \int_0^{1/n} n\,dx = n\cdot\frac{1}{n}=1.$$

Since the functions are deterministic, the expectation equals the value itself:

$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^1 |g_n(x)-g(x)|\,dx\right]=1\quad\text{for all }n,$$

which does **not** converge to $0$.

**Justification of why pointwise convergence is insufficient.** The implication
would require interchanging a limit and an integral (and an expectation):

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^1 |g_n-g|\,dx\right]\stackrel{?}{=}\
mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^1 \lim_{n\to\infty}|g_n-g|\,dx\right]=0.$$

Such an interchange is not justified by pointwise convergence alone. It requires
 an additional integrability hypothesis that controls the "mass" of $g_n-g$ unif
ormly in $n$—for instance:

- **Dominated Convergence**: $|g_n(x)-g(x)|\le h(x)$ for some integrable $h$ (pa
thwise, or in expectation via a uniform integrability condition), or
- **Vitali's convergence theorem**: $\{g_n\}$ uniformly integrable plus converge
nce in measure.

The counterexample above violates exactly this: the "spike" $n\mathbf{1}_{(0,1/n
]}$ has constant $L^1$ mass $1$ that slides into a set of vanishing measure, so
no dominating integrable function exists and the family $\{g_n\}$ is not uniform
ly integrable. Pointwise convergence lets the spike escape to a null set, but th
e integral still records the full mass.

Therefore, pointwise convergence $g_n(x)\to g(x)$ for each $x\in[0,1]$ does **no
t** guarantee $\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^1|g_n-g|\,dx\right]\to 0$; an additional
 hypothesis such as dominated convergence or uniform integrability is needed.
