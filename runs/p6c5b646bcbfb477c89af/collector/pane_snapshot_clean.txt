**Proof.**

Let $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ be bounded with $n \ge 2$, and fix $x \in \m
athbb{R}^n$. We must show
$$
I(x) \;=\; \int_\Omega |x-y|^{1-n}\, d\lambda(y) \;<\; \infty.
$$

Since $\Omega$ is bounded, there exists $R>0$ such that $\Omega \subseteq B(x,R)
$, where $B(x,R)$ is the open Euclidean ball of radius $R$ centered at $x$. The
integrand $y \mapsto |x-y|^{1-n}$ is non-negative, so by monotonicity of the int
egral,
$$
I(x) \;=\; \int_\Omega |x-y|^{1-n}\,d\lambda(y) \;\le\; \int_{B(x,R)} |x-y|^{1-n
}\,d\lambda(y).
$$
It suffices to show the right-hand side is finite.

We compute the integral over $B(x,R)$ using polar coordinates centered at $x$. W
riting $y = x + r\omega$ with $r = |x-y| \in (0,R)$ and $\omega \in \mathbb{S}^{
n-1}$, the Lebesgue measure in polar form is $d\lambda(y) = r^{n-1}\,dr\,d\sigma
(\omega)$, where $\sigma$ is the surface measure on $\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$. Since $|
x-y|^{1-n} = r^{1-n}$, the integrand becomes
$$
|x-y|^{1-n}\,d\lambda(y) \;=\; r^{1-n}\cdot r^{n-1}\,dr\,d\sigma(\omega) \;=\; d
r\,d\sigma(\omega).
$$
Therefore, by Tonelli's theorem (the integrand is non-negative),
$$
\int_{B(x,R)} |x-y|^{1-n}\,d\lambda(y)
\;=\; \int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\!\left(\int_0^R 1\,dr\right) d\sigma(\omega)
\;=\; \sigma(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\cdot R \;<\; \infty.
$$

Here we used $n \ge 2$ to ensure $\sigma(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}) < \infty$ (in fact $\
sigma(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}) = 2\pi^{n/2}/\Gamma(n/2)$), and the power $1-n \ge -1 >
-n$ guarantees that the radial singularity $r^{1-n}$ is integrable against $r^{n
-1}\,dr$ near $r=0$, since $r^{1-n}\cdot r^{n-1} = r^0 = 1$.

Combining the estimates,
$$
I(x) \;\le\; \sigma(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\cdot R \;<\; \infty.
$$

Since $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ was arbitrary, the integral $I(x)$ is finite for all
$x \in \mathbb{R}^n$. $\blacksquare$
