## Solution

We compute the distribution $x\delta'_0$, where $\delta'_0(u) = -u'(0)$ for test
 functions $u \in \mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})$.

**Definition of multiplication.** The product of a smooth function $f \in C^\inf
ty(\mathbb{R})$ with a distribution $T$ is defined by
$$(fT)(u) := T(fu), \qquad u \in \mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}).$$

**Computation.** Taking $f(x) = x$ and $T = \delta'_0$:
$$(x\delta'_0)(u) = \delta'_0(xu) = -(xu)'(0).$$

By the product rule,
$$(xu)'(x) = u(x) + x\,u'(x),$$
so evaluating at $x = 0$:
$$(xu)'(0) = u(0) + 0 \cdot u'(0) = u(0).$$

Substituting back:
$$(x\delta'_0)(u) = -u(0) = -\delta_0(u).$$

Since this holds for every test function $u \in \mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})$, we con
clude
$$\boxed{x\,\delta'_0 = -\delta_0}.$$

**Remark.** This is the special case $n=1$ of the general identity $x\,\delta_0^
{(n)} = -n\,\delta_0^{(n-1)}$, which follows from the same product-rule argument
: $(x\delta_0^{(n)})(u) = \delta_0^{(n)}(xu) = (-1)^n(xu)^{(n)}(0)$, and $(xu)^{
(n)} = xu^{(n)} + nu^{(n-1)}$, giving $(-1)^n \cdot n \cdot u^{(n-1)}(0) = -n\,\
delta_0^{(n-1)}(u)$.
