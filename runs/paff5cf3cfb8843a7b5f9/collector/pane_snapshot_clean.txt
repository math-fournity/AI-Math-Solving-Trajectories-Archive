## Answer: Yes

**Claim.** $M^{**}/M$ has finite length.

**Proof.** The key observation is that over a 1-dimensional Noetherian local dom
ain, every finitely generated torsion module has finite length. We show $M^{**}/
M$ is finitely generated and torsion.

**Step 1: $M^{**}/M$ is finitely generated.** Since $R$ is Noetherian and $M$ is
 finitely generated, $M^* = \operatorname{Hom}_R(M, R)$ is finitely generated. A
pplying this again, $M^{**}$ is finitely generated. The quotient $M^{**}/M$ is t
herefore finitely generated.

**Step 2: $M^{**}/M$ is torsion.** Both $M$ and $M^{**}$ have rank $r$ over $K$.
 Indeed, since $M$ is torsion-free of rank $r$, we have $M \otimes_R K \cong K^r
$. Then:
$$M^* \otimes_R K \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}_K(M \otimes_R K,\, K) \;\cong\; K
^r,$$
so $M^*$ has rank $r$, and similarly $M^{**}$ has rank $r$:
$$M^{**} \otimes_R K \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}_K(M^* \otimes_R K,\, K) \;\con
g\; K^r.$$
The canonical map $\varphi: M \to M^{**}$ induces, after tensoring with $K$, the
 natural double-dual isomorphism $K^r \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} K^r$ of $K$-vector
spaces. Hence:
$$(M^{**}/M) \otimes_R K \;\cong\; \operatorname{coker}(\varphi \otimes K) \;=\;
 0,$$
which means $M^{**}/M$ is torsion.

**Step 3: Finite length.** Since $M^{**}/M$ is a finitely generated torsion modu
le over the 1-dimensional Noetherian local domain $R$, its annihilator is non-ze
ro: if $M^{**}/M$ is generated by $\bar{x}_1, \dots, \bar{x}_k$ and $0 \neq r_i
\in R$ kills $\bar{x}_i$ (which exists since each $\bar{x}_i$ is torsion), then
$0 \neq r_1 \cdots r_k$ annihilates the entire module. Therefore $\operatorname{
Supp}(M^{**}/M) \subseteq V(\operatorname{Ann}(M^{**}/M))$, and since $\operator
name{Ann}(M^{**}/M) \neq 0$ and $\dim R = 1$, the only prime in the support is $
\mathfrak{m}$. Thus $M^{**}/M$ is a finitely generated module over the Artinian
local ring $R/\operatorname{Ann}(M^{**}/M)$, hence has finite length. $\blacksqu
are$

**Remark.** The completeness hypothesis on $R$ is not needed for this argument;
the conclusion holds for any 1-dimensional Noetherian local domain.
