## Answer

**Yes.** Every topological space can be structured as a right topological semigr
oup.

## Proof

Let $(X, \tau)$ be an arbitrary topological space. We define a binary operation
$\ast$ on $X$ by

$$x \ast y := y \qquad \text{for all } x, y \in X.$$

This is the **right-zero semigroup** structure on $X$. We verify the two require
d properties.

**Associativity.** For any $x, y, z \in X$,

$$(x \ast y) \ast z = y \ast z = z, \qquad x \ast (y \ast z) = x \ast z = z.$$

Hence $(x \ast y) \ast z = x \ast (y \ast z)$, so $\ast$ is associative.

**Continuity of right translations.** For each fixed $y \in X$, the right-transl
ation map is

$$\ast_y : X \to X, \qquad \ast_y(x) = x \ast y = y.$$

This is the constant map with value $y$. A constant map between topological spac
es is always continuous: for every open set $U \subseteq X$, the preimage $\ast_
y^{-1}(U)$ is either $\varnothing$ (if $y \notin U$) or $X$ (if $y \in U$), both
 of which are open in $\tau$.

**Conclusion.** The triple $(X, \tau, \ast)$ satisfies both the associativity ax
iom and the right-translation continuity axiom, so it is a right topological sem
igroup. Since $(X, \tau)$ was arbitrary, every topological space admits the stru
cture of a right topological semigroup. $\blacksquare$
