We evaluate
$$I = \int_{C(0,1)} \frac{z\,e^{z}}{\tan^{2} z}\,dz$$
by the residue theorem.

### Singularities inside $|z|=1$

The integrand $f(z)=\dfrac{z\,e^{z}}{\tan^{2}z}$ is singular only where $\tan z
= 0$, i.e. at $z=n\pi$, $n\in\mathbb{Z}$. (Where $\tan z$ has poles, $\tan^{2}z\
to\infty$, so $f\to 0$; those points are removable, not singular.)

Since $\pi>1$, the only zero of $\tan z$ inside the unit disk $|z|<1$ is $z=0$.

### Nature of the singularity at $z=0$

Near $z=0$:
$$\tan z = z + \tfrac{z^{3}}{3}+\cdots,\qquad \tan^{2}z = z^{2}\!\left(1+\tfrac{
2z^{2}}{3}+\cdots\right).$$

Hence
$$f(z)=\frac{z\,e^{z}}{z^{2}\bigl(1+\tfrac{2z^{2}}{3}+\cdots\bigr)}=\frac{e^{z}}
{z}\cdot\frac{1}{1+\tfrac{2z^{2}}{3}+\cdots}.$$

Expanding each factor:
$$\frac{e^{z}}{z}=\frac{1}{z}\!\left(1+z+\tfrac{z^{2}}{2}+\cdots\right),\qquad \
frac{1}{1+\tfrac{2z^{2}}{3}+\cdots}=1-\tfrac{2z^{2}}{3}+\cdots.$$

The constant term of the product $\bigl(1+z+\tfrac{z^{2}}{2}+\cdots\bigr)\bigl(1
-\tfrac{2z^{2}}{3}+\cdots\bigr)$ is $1\cdot 1=1$. Therefore
$$f(z)=\frac{1}{z}+1+\cdots,$$
so $z=0$ is a **simple pole** with residue
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,0)=1.$$

### Applying the residue theorem

Since $z=0$ is the only singularity enclosed by $C(0,1)$,
$$I = 2\pi i\;\operatorname{Res}(f,0)=2\pi i\cdot 1 = 2\pi i.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \int_{C(0,1)} \frac{z\,e^{z}}{\tan^{2} z}\,dz = 2\pi i}$$
