## Proof

**Yes, $f(X)$ remains separable in $L[X]$.**

**Key observation.** Since $k$ is a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}$, it has cha
racteristic $0$. Over any field of characteristic $0$, every irreducible polynom
ial is separable (in the sense that it is coprime with its formal derivative). C
oncretely, since $f$ is irreducible in $k[X]$ and $\deg f' < \deg f$ with $f' \n
eq 0$ (characteristic $0$ guarantees $f' \neq 0$), the irreducibility of $f$ for
ces
$$\gcd(f, f') = 1 \quad \text{in } k[X].$$

**Extension of the gcd to $L[X]$.** The greatest common divisor is computed by t
he Euclidean algorithm, which involves only the field operations (addition, subt
raction, multiplication, division). Since $k \hookrightarrow L$ is a field exten
sion, performing the identical sequence of operations in $L[X]$ yields the same
sequence of remainders. Hence the gcd is preserved under extension of scalars:
$$\gcd(f, f') = 1 \quad \text{in } L[X].$$

**No repeated factors.** Suppose, for contradiction, that $f$ has a repeated irr
educible factor in $L[X]$. Then there exists an irreducible $g \in L[X]$ with $g
^2 \mid f$, so we may write $f = g^2 \cdot h$ for some $h \in L[X]$. Differentia
ting:
$$f' = 2g\,g'\,h + g^2\,h',$$
which gives $g \mid f'$. Since also $g \mid f$, we obtain $g \mid \gcd(f, f') =
1$ in $L[X]$, a contradiction (as $g$ is irreducible, hence non-constant).

**Conclusion.** No irreducible factor of $f$ in $L[X]$ appears with multiplicity
 greater than $1$. Therefore $f(X)$ is separable in $L[X]$.

**Remark.** The Archimedean or non-Archimedean nature of the absolute value, and
 the specific structure of the completion $L$, play no role: the argument relies
 only on $k$ (and hence $L$) having characteristic $0$, which is guaranteed by $
k$ being a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}$.
