No. The total-variation norm topology is strictly stronger than the weak* topolo
gy on $M(X)$ for any infinite compact Hausdorff space $X$, so the two topologies
 do not coincide in general.

## Proof

Let $X$ be a compact Hausdorff space and let $M(X)$ denote the Banach space of r
egular countably additive complex Borel measures on $X$, equipped with the total
-variation norm $\|\mu\| = |\mu|(X)$. By the Riesz–Markov–Kakutani representatio
n theorem, $M(X)$ is isometrically isomorphic to the dual $C(X)^*$ of the Banach
 space $C(X)$ of continuous complex-valued functions on $X$ (with the supremum n
orm), the duality being

$$\langle f, \mu \rangle = \int_X f \, d\mu.$$

The **weak* topology** on $M(X) = C(X)^*$ is the coarsest topology making each m
ap $\mu \mapsto \int f\,d\mu$ ($f \in C(X)$) continuous. The **norm topology** i
s the one induced by $\|\cdot\|$. Norm convergence always implies weak* converge
nce (since $|\int f\,d(\mu_n - \mu)| \le \|f\|_\infty \|\mu_n - \mu\|$), so the
norm topology is always at least as strong as the weak* topology.

It remains to show they need not be equal. Take $X = [0,1]$ (compact Hausdorff)
and consider the sequence of Dirac measures

$$\mu_n = \delta_{1/n}, \qquad \mu = \delta_0.$$

**Weak* convergence.** For every $f \in C([0,1])$,

$$\int f\,d\mu_n = f(1/n) \longrightarrow f(0) = \int f\,d\mu$$

by continuity of $f$. Hence $\mu_n \to \mu$ in the weak* topology.

**No norm convergence.** The measures $\delta_{1/n}$ and $\delta_0$ are mutually
 singular (supported on the disjoint singletons $\{1/n\}$ and $\{0\}$), so

$$\|\mu_n - \mu\| = |\delta_{1/n} - \delta_0|([0,1]) = |\delta_{1/n}|([0,1]) + |
\delta_0|([0,1]) = 1 + 1 = 2$$

for every $n$. Thus $\|\mu_n - \mu\| \not\to 0$, and $\mu_n$ does **not** conver
ge to $\mu$ in the norm topology.

Therefore the weak* topology is strictly coarser than the total-variation norm t
opology on $M([0,1])$, and the two topologies do not coincide. $\blacksquare$
