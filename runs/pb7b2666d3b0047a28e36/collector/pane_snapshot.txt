## IMO 2010 A1 — Solution

**Problem.** Find all $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ such that
$$f(\lfloor x\rfloor y)=f(x)\lfloor f(y)\rfloor\qquad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathb
b R.$$

**Answer.** The solutions are exactly
$$f\equiv 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad f\equiv c\ \text{ for a constant }c\in[1,2).$
$

---

### Verification that these work

- $f\equiv 0$: both sides are $0$.
- $f\equiv c$ with $c\in[1,2)$: then $\lfloor f(y)\rfloor=\lfloor c\rfloor=1$, s
o
  $$f(\lfloor x\rfloor y)=c,\qquad f(x)\lfloor f(y)\rfloor=c\cdot 1=c.$$

---

### Proof that these are the only solutions

Throughout, write $E(x,y)$ for the asserted identity.

**Step 1 — value at $0$.** Setting $x=0$ (so $\lfloor x\rfloor=0$):
$$f(0)=f(0)\,\lfloor f(y)\rfloor\qquad\forall y. \tag{1}$$

**Case A: $f(0)\neq 0$.** Then $\lfloor f(y)\rfloor=1$ for all $y$, and $E(x,y)$
 reduces to
$$f(\lfloor x\rfloor y)=f(x)\qquad\forall x,y. \tag{2}$$
For any $t\in\mathbb R$, choose $x\in[1,2)$ (so $\lfloor x\rfloor=1$) and $y=t$;
 then $f(t)=f(x)$. Hence $f$ is constant, say $f\equiv c$. The condition $\lfloo
r f(y)\rfloor=1$ gives $\lfloor c\rfloor=1$, i.e. $c\in[1,2)$. ✓

**Case B: $f(0)=0$.** Setting $y=0$ gives $f(0)=f(x)\lfloor f(0)\rfloor=0$, cons
istent.

**Step 2 — using $\lfloor x\rfloor=1$.** For $x\in[1,2)$ we have $\lfloor x\rflo
or=1$, so
$$f(y)=f(x)\,\lfloor f(y)\rfloor\qquad\forall y. \tag{3}$$

*Subcase B1: $\lfloor f(y)\rfloor=0$ for all $y$.* Then $E(x,y)$ gives $f(\lfloo
r x\rfloor y)=0$ for all $x,y$. Taking $x\in[1,2)$, $y=t$ yields $f(t)=0$ for ev
ery $t$. So $f\equiv 0$. ✓

*Subcase B2: there exists $y_0$ with $\lfloor f(y_0)\rfloor\neq 0$.* Put $k:=\lf
loor f(y_0)\rfloor\neq 0$. From (3), $f(x)\,k=f(y_0)$ for every $x\in[1,2)$. Sin
ce $\mathbb R$ is an integral domain and $k\neq 0$, $f$ is constant on $[1,2)$;
call this constant $a$. Note $a\neq 0$ (otherwise $f(y_0)=0$, contradicting $\lf
loor f(y_0)\rfloor=k\neq 0$). Equation (3) becomes
$$f(y)=a\,\lfloor f(y)\rfloor\qquad\forall y. \tag{4}$$
Plugging $y\in[1,2)$ (where $f(y)=a$) into (4): $a=a\lfloor a\rfloor$, so $\lflo
or a\rfloor=1$, i.e.
$$a\in[1,2). \tag{5}$$

**Step 3 — a step function.** Define $m(y):=\lfloor f(y)\rfloor\in\mathbb Z$. By
 (4),
$$f(y)=a\,m(y),\qquad m(y)=\lfloor a\,m(y)\rfloor. \tag{6}$$
In particular $m(0)=\lfloor 0\rfloor=0$ and $m(1)=\lfloor a\rfloor=1$.

For an integer $n$ (so $\lfloor n\rfloor=n$), $E(n,y)$ reads $f(ny)=f(n)\lfloor
f(y)\rfloor$, i.e.
$$m(ny)=m(n)\,m(y)\qquad\forall y,\ \forall n\in\mathbb Z. \tag{7}$$

Now take $x=n+r$ with $n\in\mathbb Z$ and $r\in[0,1)$, so $\lfloor x\rfloor=n$.
Comparing $E(n+r,y)$ with $E(n,y)$:
$$(f(n+r)-f(n))\,\lfloor f(y)\rfloor=0\qquad\forall y.$$
Choosing $y=1$ (where $\lfloor f(1)\rfloor=m(1)=1\neq 0$) gives $f(n+r)=f(n)$. H
ence **$f$ is constant on every interval $[n,n+1)$**, with value $f(n)=a\,m(n)$.
 Therefore
$$f(y)=a\,m(\lfloor y\rfloor)\qquad\forall y. \tag{8}$$

**Step 4 — the contradiction.** Substitute (8) into the original equation:
$$a\,m\bigl(\lfloor \lfloor x\rfloor y\rfloor\bigr)=a\,m(\lfloor x\rfloor)\,m(\l
floor y\rfloor),$$
so (since $a\neq 0$)
$$m\bigl(\lfloor \lfloor x\rfloor y\rfloor\bigr)=m(\lfloor x\rfloor)\,m(\lfloor
y\rfloor)\qquad\forall x,y. \tag{9}$$

Now choose $x=2$ (so $\lfloor x\rfloor=2$) and any $y\in[\tfrac12,1)$. Then $\lf
loor y\rfloor=0$ but $\lfloor 2y\rfloor=1$. Equation (9) yields
$$m(1)=m(2)\,m(0)=m(2)\cdot 0=0,$$
contradicting $m(1)=1$.

So **Subcase B2 is impossible**; the only solution in Case B is $f\equiv 0$.

---

### Conclusion

Combining Case A and Case B, the solutions are precisely
$$\boxed{f\equiv 0\quad\text{and}\quad f\equiv c\text{ for }c\in[1,2).}$$

*(Remark on the general FloorRing setting: the argument above goes through uncha
nged for any totally ordered ring $R$ that is dense enough to contain an element
 $y\in[0,1)$ with $2y\ge 1$ — in particular for $R=\mathbb R$. The two families
above are solutions in every FloorRing; for non-dense rings such as $R=\mathbb Z
$ additional solutions exist, so the clean classification is the classical $\mat
hbb R\to\mathbb R$ one stated above.)*
