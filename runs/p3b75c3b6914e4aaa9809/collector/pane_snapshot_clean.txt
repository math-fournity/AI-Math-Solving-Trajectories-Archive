## Solution

We evaluate
$$S_{75} = \int_{0}^{\infty} x^{\sigma}\, K_{\sigma}(bx)\,\cos(ax)\,dx, \qquad a
,b>0,\;\; \operatorname{Re}\sigma>-\tfrac{1}{2}.$$

### Step 1. Integral representation of $K_\sigma$

For $\operatorname{Re}\sigma>-1/2$ we use the standard representation
$$K_\sigma(z)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{\sigma}\int_0^\infty t^{-\sig
ma-1}\exp\!\left(-t-\frac{z^2}{4t}\right)dt.$$

Setting $z=bx$ and multiplying by $x^\sigma$:
$$x^\sigma K_\sigma(bx)=\frac{b^\sigma}{2^{\sigma+1}}\,x^{2\sigma}\int_0^\infty
t^{-\sigma-1}\,e^{-t-\,b^2x^2/(4t)}\,dt.$$

### Step 2. Exchange order of integration

For $a,b>0$ and $\operatorname{Re}\sigma>-1/2$ the double integral is absolutely
 convergent, so Fubini applies:
$$S_{75}=\frac{b^\sigma}{2^{\sigma+1}}\int_0^\infty t^{-\sigma-1}e^{-t}\underbra
ce{\left[\int_0^\infty x^{2\sigma}\,e^{-b^2x^2/(4t)}\cos(ax)\,dx\right]}_{I(t)}\
,dt.$$

### Step 3. Evaluate the inner integral $I(t)$

With $\beta=b^2/(4t)>0$ and $\nu=2\sigma+1$ (so $\operatorname{Re}\nu>0$), the s
tandard Gaussian–cosine transform gives
$$\int_0^\infty x^{\nu-1}e^{-\beta x^2}\cos(ax)\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\,\beta^{-\nu/2}\
,\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{\nu}{2}\right)\,{}_1F_1\!\left(\tfrac{\nu}{2};\,\tfrac{1}{
2};\,-\tfrac{a^2}{4\beta}\right).$$

Since $\nu/2=\sigma+1/2$ and $4\beta=b^2/t$,
$$I(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{4t}{b^2}\right)^{\sigma+1/2}\Gamma\!\left(\sigma+\
tfrac{1}{2}\right)\,{}_1F_1\!\left(\sigma+\tfrac{1}{2};\,\tfrac{1}{2};\,-\tfrac{
a^2 t}{b^2}\right).$$

### Step 4. Assemble and simplify

Substituting back:
$$S_{75}=\frac{b^\sigma}{2^{\sigma+1}}\cdot\frac{4^{\sigma+1/2}}{2\,b^{2\sigma+1
}}\,\Gamma\!\left(\sigma+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}\,e^{-t}\,{}_1
F_1\!\left(\sigma+\tfrac{1}{2};\,\tfrac{1}{2};\,-\tfrac{a^2 t}{b^2}\right)dt.$$

The prefactor simplifies as $\dfrac{b^\sigma\cdot 4^{\sigma+1/2}}{2^{\sigma+2}\,
b^{2\sigma+1}}=\dfrac{2^{\sigma-1}}{b^{\sigma+1}}$, giving
$$S_{75}=\frac{2^{\sigma-1}}{b^{\sigma+1}}\,\Gamma\!\left(\sigma+\tfrac{1}{2}\ri
ght)\underbrace{\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}\,e^{-t}\,{}_1F_1\!\left(\sigma+\tfrac{1}{
2};\,\tfrac{1}{2};\,-\tfrac{a^2 t}{b^2}\right)dt}_{J}.$$

### Step 5. Evaluate $J$ via the Laplace transform of ${}_1F_1$

The identity (valid for $\operatorname{Re}c>0$, $\operatorname{Re}s>\operatornam
e{Re}z$)
$$\int_0^\infty e^{-st}\,t^{c-1}\,{}_1F_1(A;\,c;\,zt)\,dt=\frac{\Gamma(c)}{s^c}\
,\bigl(1-\tfrac{z}{s}\bigr)^{-A}$$
is the special case $c=b$ of the general formula $\int_0^\infty e^{-st}t^{c-1}{}
_1F_1(A;B;zt)\,dt=\frac{\Gamma(c)}{s^c}{}_2F_1(A,c;B;z/s)$, using ${}_2F_1(A,c;c
;w)=(1-w)^{-A}$.

Here $c=1/2$, $A=\sigma+1/2$, $z=-a^2/b^2$, $s=1$:
$$J=\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{a^2}{b^2}\right)^{-(\sigma+1/
2)}=\sqrt{\pi}\,\frac{b^{2\sigma+1}}{(a^2+b^2)^{\sigma+1/2}}.$$

### Step 6. Final result

$$S_{75}=\frac{2^{\sigma-1}}{b^{\sigma+1}}\,\Gamma\!\left(\sigma+\tfrac{1}{2}\ri
ght)\cdot\sqrt{\pi}\,\frac{b^{2\sigma+1}}{(a^2+b^2)^{\sigma+1/2}}=\frac{2^{\sigm
a-1}\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma\!\left(\sigma+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\,b^{\sigma}}{(a^2+b^2)^
{\sigma+1/2}}.$$

### Verification ($\sigma=0$)

For $\sigma=0$: $S_{75}=\dfrac{2^{-1}\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(1/2)}{(a^2+b^2)^{1/2}}=\
dfrac{\pi}{2\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}$, which is the classical result $\int_0^\infty K_0(b
x)\cos(ax)\,dx=\dfrac{\pi}{2\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}$. ✓

$$\boxed{\,S_{75}=\dfrac{2^{\sigma-1}\,\sqrt{\pi}\;\Gamma\!\left(\sigma+\frac{1}
{2}\right)\,b^{\sigma}}{\left(a^{2}+b^{2}\right)^{\sigma+1/2}}\,}$$
