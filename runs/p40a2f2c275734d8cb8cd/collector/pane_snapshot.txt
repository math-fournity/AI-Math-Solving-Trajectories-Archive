## Solution

We evaluate
$$L = \lim_{n \to \infty}\frac{(2n)!}{(n^2)!}.$$

**Step 1: Rewrite the ratio for $n \geq 3$.**

For $n \geq 3$ we have $n^2 \geq 3n > 2n$, so $n^2 > 2n$. Hence $(n^2)!$ contain
s $(2n)!$ as an initial segment:
$$(n^2)! = (2n)!\cdot(2n+1)(2n+2)\cdots n^2.$$
Therefore
$$\frac{(2n)!}{(n^2)!} = \frac{1}{\prod_{k=2n+1}^{n^2} k}.$$

**Step 2: Bound the denominator from below.**

The product $\prod_{k=2n+1}^{n^2} k$ has $n^2 - 2n = n(n-2)$ factors, each of wh
ich is at least $2n+1$. For $n \geq 3$, $2n+1 \geq 7 > 1$, so
$$\prod_{k=2n+1}^{n^2} k \;\geq\; (2n+1)^{n(n-2)} \;\geq\; 7^{\,n(n-2)}.$$

**Step 3: Conclude via the squeeze theorem.**

Since the ratio is positive,
$$0 \;<\; \frac{(2n)!}{(n^2)!} \;\leq\; \frac{1}{7^{\,n(n-2)}}.$$

As $n \to \infty$, the exponent $n(n-2) \to \infty$, so $7^{\,n(n-2)} \to \infty
$ and thus $\frac{1}{7^{\,n(n-2)}} \to 0$. By the squeeze theorem,
$$L = \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{(2n)!}{(n^2)!} = 0.$$

**Intuition.** The factorial $(n^2)!$ grows far faster than $(2n)!$ because $n^2
 \gg 2n$; the denominator contains the entire numerator as a prefix multiplied b
y an ever-growing number of factors each exceeding $1$, forcing the ratio to $0$
.

$$\boxed{\lim_{n \to \infty}\frac{(2n)!}{(n^2)!} = 0}$$
