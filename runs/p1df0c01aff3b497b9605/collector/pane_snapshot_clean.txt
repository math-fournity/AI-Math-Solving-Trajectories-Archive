## Proof

Let $P = (p, q)$ be the common point.

**Step 1: Find $p$ and $a$.**

Since both curves pass through $P$:
$$q = p^3 - p, \qquad q = p^2 - a \implies a = p^2 - p^3 + p.$$

Since they share a tangent line at $P$, their derivatives agree at $x = p$:
$$3p^2 - 1 = 2p \implies 3p^2 - 2p - 1 = 0 \implies (3p+1)(p-1) = 0.$$

So $p = 1$ or $p = -\tfrac{1}{3}$.

**Step 2: Identify the bounded region in each case.**

*Case 1:* $p = 1$, giving $a = 1 - 1 + 1 = 1$. The curves are $y = x^3 - x$ and
$y = x^2 - 1$.

Intersections satisfy $x^3 - x = x^2 - 1$, i.e. $x^3 - x^2 - x + 1 = (x-1)^2(x+1
) = 0$. So the curves meet at $x = -1$ and are tangent at $x = 1$. The bounded r
egion lies on $[-1, 1]$.

At $x = 0$: $x^3 - x = 0 > -1 = x^2 - 1$, so the cubic lies above the parabola o
n this interval. The area is
$$A_1 = \int_{-1}^{1}\bigl[(x^3 - x) - (x^2 - 1)\bigr]\,dx = \int_{-1}^{1}(x^3 -
 x^2 - x + 1)\,dx.$$

The terms $x^3$ and $-x$ are odd and vanish over $[-1,1]$, so
$$A_1 = \int_{-1}^{1}(1 - x^2)\,dx = 2\int_0^1(1 - x^2)\,dx = 2\Bigl[x - \tfrac{
x^3}{3}\Bigr]_0^1 = 2\cdot\tfrac{2}{3} = \tfrac{4}{3}.$$

*Case 2:* $p = -\tfrac{1}{3}$, giving $a = \tfrac{1}{9} + \tfrac{1}{27} - \tfrac
{1}{3} = -\tfrac{5}{27}$. The curves are $y = x^3 - x$ and $y = x^2 + \tfrac{5}{
27}$.

Intersections satisfy $x^3 - x^2 - x - \tfrac{5}{27} = 0$. Since $x = -\tfrac{1}
{3}$ is a double root (tangent point), factoring gives
$$\Bigl(x + \tfrac{1}{3}\Bigr)^2\Bigl(x - \tfrac{5}{3}\Bigr) = 0.$$

So the curves meet at $x = \tfrac{5}{3}$ and are tangent at $x = -\tfrac{1}{3}$.
 The bounded region lies on $\bigl[-\tfrac{1}{3}, \tfrac{5}{3}\bigr]$.

At $x = 0$: $x^2 + \tfrac{5}{27} = \tfrac{5}{27} > 0 = x^3 - x$, so the parabola
 lies above the cubic. The area is
$$A_2 = \int_{-1/3}^{5/3}\Bigl[(x^2 + \tfrac{5}{27}) - (x^3 - x)\Bigr]\,dx = \in
t_{-1/3}^{5/3}\Bigl(-x^3 + x^2 + x + \tfrac{5}{27}\Bigr)\,dx.$$

Evaluating the antiderivative $F(x) = -\tfrac{x^4}{4} + \tfrac{x^3}{3} + \tfrac{
x^2}{2} + \tfrac{5x}{27}$:

$$F\Bigl(\tfrac{5}{3}\Bigr) = -\tfrac{625}{324} + \tfrac{125}{81} + \tfrac{25}{1
8} + \tfrac{25}{81} = \tfrac{-625 + 600 + 450}{324} = \tfrac{425}{324},$$

$$F\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{3}\Bigr) = -\tfrac{1}{324} - \tfrac{1}{81} + \tfrac{1}{18} -
 \tfrac{5}{81} = \tfrac{-1 - 4 + 18 - 20}{324} = -\tfrac{7}{324}.$$

$$A_2 = \tfrac{425}{324} - \Bigl(-\tfrac{7}{324}\Bigr) = \tfrac{432}{324} = \tfr
ac{4}{3}.$$

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

Both possible configurations (tangent at $p = 1$ with $a = 1$, or tangent at $p
= -\tfrac{1}{3}$ with $a = -\tfrac{5}{27}$) yield the same bounded area:

$$\boxed{\dfrac{4}{3}}.$$
