## Solution: The Word Problem for Free Groups

### Setup

Let $F$ be a free group of finite rank $n$ with free generators $x_1, \ldots, x_
n$. Let $x_i^{-1}$ denote the formal inverse of $x_i$, and let $S = \{x_1, \ldot
s, x_n, x_1^{-1}, \ldots, x_n^{-1}\}$ be the set of $2n$ symbols. A **word** $w$
 is a finite sequence $w = s_1 s_2 \cdots s_k$ with each $s_j \in S$.

Recall $F$ is presented as
$$F = \langle x_1,\ldots,x_n \mid \;\rangle,$$
i.e. the group with generators $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ and **no relations**. The empty
word $\varepsilon$ is the identity. Two words represent the same element of $F$
iff one can be transformed into the other by a finite sequence of **insertions**
 and **deletions** of adjacent inverse pairs $x_i x_i^{-1}$ or $x_i^{-1} x_i$.

### The Algorithm: Free Reduction

**Input:** a word $w = s_1 \cdots s_k$ over $S$.

**Procedure.** Scan the word left to right. Whenever two adjacent letters $s_j,
s_{j+1}$ satisfy $s_{j+1} = s_j^{-1}$ (i.e. they form an inverse pair), delete b
oth. Repeat until no adjacent inverse pair remains. Concretely, implement this w
ith a stack:

1. Initialize an empty stack.
2. For each letter $s$ of $w$ in order:
   - If the stack is non-empty and the top letter $t$ satisfies $s = t^{-1}$, **
pop** $t$ (cancellation).
   - Otherwise **push** $s$.
3. Output the stack contents as the **reduced word** $\widetilde{w}$.

**Decision rule:** $w$ represents the trivial element of $F$ **if and only if**
$\widetilde{w}$ is the empty word.

### Proof of Correctness

We prove three facts.

**Fact 1 (The reduced word is well-defined / Church–Rosser).** The order in whic
h cancellations are performed does not matter: every maximal sequence of cancell
ations terminates in the same irreducible word.

*Proof.* Define a reduction step as deleting an adjacent inverse pair. Each step
 strictly decreases the word length, so every reduction sequence terminates in f
initely many steps at an **irreducible** word (one with no adjacent inverse pair
). To see the result is unique, it suffices to show the reduction relation is **
locally confluent** (Newman's lemma applies since termination holds). Consider a
n irreducible word reached by two different reduction paths from $w$; the standa
rd critical-pair analysis shows any two single-step reductions of the same word
can be rejoined: if the two cancelled pairs are disjoint they commute; if they o
verlap, the only possible overlap is a length-3 factor $s\, s^{-1}\, s$ or $s^{-
1}\, s\, s^{-1}$, and cancelling either adjacent pair leaves the single letter $
s$ (resp. $s^{-1}$) — the same result. Hence local confluence, hence by Newman's
 lemma global confluence, hence a unique normal form $\widetilde{w}$. $\square$

**Fact 2 (Soundness).** If $\widetilde{w} = \varepsilon$, then $w$ represents th
e identity in $F$.

*Proof.* Each cancellation step deletes an adjacent inverse pair $x_i x_i^{-1}$
or $x_i^{-1} x_i$, which equals the identity in $F$. So the element represented
is invariant under each step. If all letters cancel, the element represented is
the identity. $\square$

**Fact 3 (Completeness).** If $w$ represents the identity in $F$, then $\widetil
de{w} = \varepsilon$.

*Proof.* Suppose $w$ represents the identity. Then $w$ can be obtained from $\va
repsilon$ by a finite sequence of insertions and deletions of adjacent inverse p
airs (this is the definition of equality in the presented group with no relation
s). Reversing this, $\varepsilon$ is reachable from $w$ by deletions and inserti
ons. By Fact 1, the irreducible normal form is a function of the group element,
so it is invariant under both insertions and deletions of inverse pairs (an inse
rtion followed by full reduction returns the same normal form, by confluence). T
he normal form of $\varepsilon$ is $\varepsilon$ itself. Hence the normal form o
f $w$ is $\varepsilon$, i.e. $\widetilde{w} = \varepsilon$. $\square$

Alternatively, completeness follows from the **van der Waerden / normal form the
orem**: every element of a free group has a *unique* reduced word representative
, and the only element with empty reduced form is the identity. The stack algori
thm computes exactly this unique reduced representative.

### Termination and Complexity

The stack algorithm performs exactly $k$ iterations, each doing $O(1)$ work (one
 comparison and one push/pop). Hence it runs in **$O(k)$ time and $O(k)$ space**
, terminating on every input. This gives a total decision procedure:

- **Halting:** guaranteed (length strictly decreases per cancellation, bounded b
y $k$).
- **Decision:** $\widetilde{w} = \varepsilon \iff w =_F 1$.

### Summary

The word problem for a finitely generated free group is decidable by **free redu
ction**: repeatedly cancel adjacent inverse pairs $x_i x_i^{-1}$ or $x_i^{-1} x_
i$ until none remain. The resulting reduced word is the unique normal form of th
e group element (Church–Rosser property of the free-group rewriting system), and
 $w$ is trivial **if and only if** its fully reduced form is the empty word. The
 algorithm runs in linear time in the length of $w$.
