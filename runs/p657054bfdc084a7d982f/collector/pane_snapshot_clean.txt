## Proof

The answer is **No**: the condition does not imply $b = 0$. We provide an explic
it counterexample.

**Setup.** Let $S = \mathbb{C}[x]$, the $*$-algebra of polynomials in one variab
le with complex coefficients, where the involution is coefficient conjugation (s
o $x^* = x$).

Define two norms on $S$:
$$\|p\|_1 = \sup_{t \in [0,1]} |p(t)|, \qquad \|p\|_2 = \sup_{t \in [0,2]} |p(t)
|.$$

**Both are $C^*$-norms.** Each is a norm since a polynomial vanishing on a nontr
ivial interval is identically zero. The $C^*$-identity holds: $p^*p = |p|^2$ as
a function, so
$$\|p^*p\|_i = \sup_{t \in K_i} |p(t)|^2 = \|p\|_i^2, \quad i = 1,2.$$

**Comparison.** Since $[0,1] \subseteq [0,2]$, we have $\|p\|_1 \leq \|p\|_2$ fo
r all $p \in S$.

**Completions.** By the Weierstrass approximation theorem, polynomials are dense
 in $C(K)$ for any compact $K \subseteq \mathbb{R}$. Hence:
- $A = \overline{S}^{\|\cdot\|_1} \cong C([0,1])$,
- $B = \overline{S}^{\|\cdot\|_2} \cong C([0,2])$.

**The counterexample sequence.** Define $b \in C([0,2])$ by
$$b(t) = \max(0,\, t - 1).$$
Then $b \neq 0$ (for instance, $b(2) = 1$), but $b\big|_{[0,1]} \equiv 0$.

By the Weierstrass approximation theorem, there exists a sequence of polynomials
 $(p_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ in $S$ converging uniformly to $b$ on $[0,2]$. Then:

- **Convergence in $\|\cdot\|_2$:** $\|p_n - b\|_2 = \sup_{[0,2]} |p_n(t) - b(t)
| \to 0$, so $p_n \to b$ in $B$.

- **Convergence in $\|\cdot\|_1$:** Since $b|_{[0,1]} = 0$,
$$\|p_n\|_1 = \sup_{[0,1]} |p_n(t)| \to \sup_{[0,1]} |b(t)| = 0,$$
so $p_n \to 0$ in $A$.

**Conclusion.** The sequence $(p_n)$ converges to $0$ in $\|\cdot\|_1$ and to $b
 \neq 0$ in $\|\cdot\|_2$, with $\|\cdot\|_1 \leq \|\cdot\|_2$ and both norms be
ing $C^*$-norms on the $*$-algebra $S$. Therefore, the given hypotheses do **not
** imply $b = 0$.

**Remark.** The surjective $*$-homomorphism $\varphi: B \to A$ induced by the id
entity on $S$ is the restriction map $\varphi(f) = f|_{[0,1]}$, whose kernel $\{
f \in C([0,2]) : f|_{[0,1]} = 0\}$ is nontrivial. The element $b$ lies in this k
ernel, which is precisely why the limit in the stronger norm can be nonzero whil
e the limit in the weaker norm is zero.
