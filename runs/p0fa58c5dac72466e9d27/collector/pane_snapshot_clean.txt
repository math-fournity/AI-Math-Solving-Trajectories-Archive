## Proof

**Claim.** If $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n$ converges (to a finite real number), the
n
$$
\lim_{N\to\infty} \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N} n\,a_n = 0.
$$

**Proof.** This is a direct consequence of Kronecker's lemma; we give the full a
rgument.

Let $s_0 = 0$ and $s_n = \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k$ for $n \ge 1$. By hypothesis $s_n \
to s$ for some finite $s \in \mathbb{R}$. Since $a_n = s_n - s_{n-1}$, we perfor
m summation by parts:

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{N} n\,a_n = \sum_{n=1}^{N} n(s_n - s_{n-1}) = \sum_{n=1}^{N} n\,s_n
- \sum_{n=1}^{N} n\,s_{n-1}.
$$

Re-indexing the second sum with $m = n-1$,

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{N} n\,s_{n-1} = \sum_{m=0}^{N-1}(m+1)\,s_m = s_0 + \sum_{m=1}^{N-1}(
m+1)\,s_m = \sum_{m=1}^{N-1}(m+1)\,s_m,
$$

since $s_0 = 0$. Therefore

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{N} n\,a_n = N\,s_N + \sum_{n=1}^{N-1} n\,s_n - \sum_{n=1}^{N-1}(n+1)
\,s_n = N\,s_N - \sum_{n=1}^{N-1} s_n.
$$

Dividing by $N$,

$$
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N} n\,a_n = s_N - \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1} s_n. \tag{$
\ast$}
$$

Now analyse the two terms on the right as $N \to \infty$:

- Since $s_n \to s$, clearly $s_N \to s$.
- By the Cesàro mean theorem, $\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1} s_n \to s$. Hence
  $$
  \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1} s_n = \frac{N-1}{N}\cdot\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{n=1}^{N
-1} s_n \;\longrightarrow\; 1 \cdot s = s.
  $$

Substituting back into $(\ast)$,

$$
\lim_{N\to\infty} \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N} n\,a_n = s - s = 0.
$$

Thus the convergence of $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n$ **does** imply
$$
\lim_{N\to\infty} \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n \le N} n\,a_n = 0.
$$

$\blacksquare$
