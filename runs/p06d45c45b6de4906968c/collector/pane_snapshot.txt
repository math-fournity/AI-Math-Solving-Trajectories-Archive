We want the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ in the Laurent expansion of $f(z)=\dfrac{e^z
}{\sin^2 z}$ about $z=0$.

**Step 1: Identify the singularity.** Since $\sin z = z - \tfrac{z^3}{6}+\cdots$
, we have $\sin^2 z = z^2\!\left(1-\tfrac{z^2}{3}+\tfrac{2z^4}{45}-\cdots\right)
$, so $\sin^2 z$ has a zero of order $2$ at the origin. With $e^z$ analytic and
$e^0=1\neq 0$, the function $f$ has a pole of order $2$ at $z=0$. The coefficien
t of $z^{-1}$ is the residue of $f$ at $0$.

**Step 2: Reduce to a residue at a double pole.** Write
$$\sin^2 z = z^2\,h(z),\qquad h(z)=\left(\frac{\sin z}{z}\right)^{\!2}=1-\frac{z
^2}{3}+\frac{2z^4}{45}-\cdots,$$
with $h(0)=1$, so $h$ is analytic and nonzero near $0$. Then
$$f(z)=\frac{e^z}{z^2\,h(z)}=\frac{\phi(z)}{z^2},\qquad \phi(z)=\frac{e^z}{h(z)}
=e^z\,g(z),\quad g(z)=\frac{1}{h(z)}.$$
For a pole of order $2$ of the form $\phi(z)/z^2$ with $\phi$ analytic at $0$, t
he residue (coefficient of $z^{-1}$) is $\phi'(0)$.

**Step 3: Compute $\phi'(0)$.** Differentiate $\phi(z)=e^z g(z)$:
$$\phi'(z)=e^z g(z)+e^z g'(z),\qquad \phi'(0)=e^0 g(0)+e^0 g'(0)=g(0)+g'(0).$$
Now $h(z)=(\sin z/z)^2$ is an **even** function of $z$, hence $g(z)=1/h(z)$ is a
lso even. Therefore $g'(0)=0$. Also $g(0)=1/h(0)=1/1=1$.

Thus
$$\phi'(0)=1+0=1.$$

**Step 4: Conclusion.** The coefficient of $z^{-1}$ in the Laurent series of $\d
frac{e^z}{\sin^2 z}$ centered at the origin equals the residue at the double pol
e, which is $\phi'(0)=1$.

**Sanity check via expansion.** With $g(z)=1+\tfrac{z^2}{3}+\tfrac{z^4}{15}+\cdo
ts$ (the standard $\csc^2 z$ expansion stripped of its $z^{-2}$ factor) and $e^z
=1+z+\tfrac{z^2}{2}+\cdots$,
$$\frac{e^z}{\sin^2 z}=z^{-2}\,e^z g(z)=z^{-2}\bigl(\underbrace{1}_{\text{from }
z\cdot 1}\cdot z+\text{(even powers only)}\bigr),$$
so the coefficient of $z^{1}$ in $e^z g(z)$ is $1$ (the $z$ term of $e^z$ times
the constant term $1$ of $g$; $g$ contributes only even powers). Hence the coeff
icient of $z^{-1}$ in $f$ is $1$, confirming the result.
