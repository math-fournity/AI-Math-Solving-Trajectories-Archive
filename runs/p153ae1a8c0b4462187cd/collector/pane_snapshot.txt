## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** The map $\pi$ must send $C_1 \setminus \iota_1(X)$ onto $C_2 \s
etminus \iota_2(X)$, i.e., $\pi(C_1 \setminus \iota_1(X)) = C_2 \setminus \iota_
2(X)$.

We assume the standard convention that a compactification of $X$ is a *compact H
ausdorff* space containing a dense embedded copy of $X$. The proof has two parts
: (A) the image of the remainder *covers* the target remainder, and (B) the imag
e of the remainder *does not spill into* the embedded copy $\iota_2(X)$.

---

### Part A: $\pi(C_1 \setminus \iota_1(X)) \supseteq C_2 \setminus \iota_2(X)$

Since $C_1$ is compact and $C_2$ is Hausdorff, the continuous image $\pi(C_1)$ i
s compact, hence **closed** in $C_2$. Moreover,

$$\pi(C_1) \supseteq \pi(\iota_1(X)) = \iota_2(X),$$

and $\iota_2(X)$ is dense in $C_2$. A closed set containing a dense set is every
thing, so $\pi(C_1) = C_2$; **$\pi$ is surjective**.

Now decompose:

$$C_2 = \pi(C_1) = \pi\bigl(\iota_1(X)\bigr) \cup \pi\bigl(C_1 \setminus \iota_1
(X)\bigr) = \iota_2(X) \cup \pi\bigl(C_1 \setminus \iota_1(X)\bigr).$$

Taking complements of $\iota_2(X)$ on both sides:

$$C_2 \setminus \iota_2(X) \subseteq \pi\bigl(C_1 \setminus \iota_1(X)\bigr). \t
ag{1}$$

---

### Part B: $\pi(C_1 \setminus \iota_1(X)) \subseteq C_2 \setminus \iota_2(X)$

We show that no point of the remainder $C_1 \setminus \iota_1(X)$ maps into $\io
ta_2(X)$.

Suppose for contradiction that $p \in C_1 \setminus \iota_1(X)$ and $\pi(p) = \i
ota_2(x_0)$ for some $x_0 \in X$.

Since $X$ is **locally compact Hausdorff**, $x_0$ admits a compact neighborhood
$K$ in $X$, i.e., there is a compact set $K \subseteq X$ with $x_0 \in \operator
name{int}_X(K)$.

**Key observations:**

1. $\iota_1(K)$ is compact in $C_1$ (continuous image of compact), hence **close
d** in $C_1$ (Hausdorff). So $C_1 \setminus \iota_1(K)$ is open.

2. $\iota_2(K)$ is compact in $C_2$, hence **closed** in $C_2$.

Since $p \in C_1 \setminus \iota_1(X)$ and $\iota_1(K) \subseteq \iota_1(X)$, we
 have $p \notin \iota_1(K)$, i.e., $p \in C_1 \setminus \iota_1(K)$, which is an
 **open** neighborhood of $p$.

Because $\iota_1(X)$ is dense in $C_1$, every open neighborhood of $p$ meets $\i
ota_1(X)$. Intersecting any such neighborhood with the open set $C_1 \setminus \
iota_1(K)$, we get:

$$p \in \overline{\iota_1(X \setminus K)}^{\,C_1}.$$

(Any open neighborhood $U$ of $p$ has $U \cap (C_1 \setminus \iota_1(K))$ open a
nd containing $p$, hence meeting $\iota_1(X)$; the meeting point lies in $\iota_
1(X) \setminus \iota_1(K) = \iota_1(X \setminus K)$.)

By **continuity** of $\pi$ and the identity $\pi \circ \iota_1 = \iota_2$:

$$\iota_2(x_0) = \pi(p) \in \pi\!\left(\overline{\iota_1(X \setminus K)}^{\,C_1}
\right) \subseteq \overline{\pi(\iota_1(X \setminus K))}^{\,C_2} = \overline{\io
ta_2(X \setminus K)}^{\,C_2}. \tag{2}$$

**We now show the opposite:** $\iota_2(x_0) \notin \overline{\iota_2(X \setminus
 K)}^{\,C_2}$, yielding a contradiction.

Since $x_0 \in \operatorname{int}_X(K)$, there exists an open set $W$ in $X$ wit
h $x_0 \in W \subseteq K$. Because $\iota_2$ is a **topological embedding**, $\i
ota_2(W)$ is open in the subspace $\iota_2(X)$, so there exists an open set $O$
in $C_2$ with

$$O \cap \iota_2(X) = \iota_2(W), \qquad \iota_2(x_0) \in O.$$

Now compute:

$$O \cap \iota_2(X \setminus K) = O \cap \iota_2(X) \cap \iota_2(X \setminus K)
= \iota_2(W) \cap \iota_2(X \setminus K) = \iota_2\bigl(W \cap (X \setminus K)\b
igr) = \emptyset,$$

since $W \subseteq K$. Thus $O$ is an **open neighborhood of $\iota_2(x_0)$ disj
oint from $\iota_2(X \setminus K)$**, which means

$$\iota_2(x_0) \notin \overline{\iota_2(X \setminus K)}^{\,C_2}. \tag{3}$$

**Contradiction** between (2) and (3). Therefore no such $p$ exists: every $p \i
n C_1 \setminus \iota_1(X)$ satisfies $\pi(p) \notin \iota_2(X)$, i.e.,

$$\pi\bigl(C_1 \setminus \iota_1(X)\bigr) \subseteq C_2 \setminus \iota_2(X). \t
ag{4}$$

---

### Conclusion

Combining (1) and (4):

$$\boxed{\pi\bigl(C_1 \setminus \iota_1(X)\bigr) = C_2 \setminus \iota_2(X).}$$

The map $\pi$ sends the remainder of $C_1$ **onto** the remainder of $C_2$.

**Remark on the role of hypotheses.** Surjectivity of $\pi$ (Part A) uses only c
ompactness of $C_1$, Hausdorffness of $C_2$, and density of $\iota_2(X)$. The no
n-spillover (Part B) is where **local compactness** of $X$ is essential: it prov
ides the compact neighborhood $K$ of $x_0$, whose image $\iota_1(K)$ is closed i
n $C_1$ (separating $p$ from $\iota_1(K)$) and whose image $\iota_2(K)$ is close
d in $C_2$ (separating $\iota_2(x_0)$ from the closure of $\iota_2(X \setminus K
)$). Without local compactness, a remainder point could in principle collapse on
to an interior point of the other compactification.
