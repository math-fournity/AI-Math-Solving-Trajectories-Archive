Looking at this problem, I need to find the area of pentagon $APBCQ$ given three
 triangular areas.

## Setting Up Coordinates

Place $A$ at the origin. Let $\vec{B} = \mathbf{b}$, $\vec{C} = \mathbf{c}$. Let
 $R$ denote 90° counterclockwise rotation.

**Locating P and Q.** Since $\triangle PAB$ is isosceles right with $\angle P =
90°$, $P$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$ at distance $|AB|/2$ from t
he midpoint. This gives:
$$P = \frac{\mathbf{b} + s\,R\mathbf{b}}{2}, \quad Q = \frac{\mathbf{c} + t\,R\m
athbf{c}}{2}, \quad s,t \in \{+1,-1\}$$

**Convexity determines the signs.** Testing all four sign combinations with $D =
 \det(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}) > 0$ (counterclockwise $\triangle ABC$), only **$s
= -1, t = +1$** yields a convex pentagon $A \to P \to B \to C \to Q$ (verified b
y checking all consecutive cross products have consistent sign). In this configu
ration $P$ is on the opposite side of $AB$ from $C$, and $Q$ is on the opposite
side of $AC$ from $B$ — both outside the field.

## Key Identities

Using $R(x,y) = (-y, x)$, the identities $\det(\mathbf{v}, R\mathbf{w}) = \mathb
f{v} \cdot \mathbf{w}$ and $\det(R\mathbf{v}, R\mathbf{w}) = \det(\mathbf{v}, \m
athbf{w})$ give:

**Area of $\triangle APQ$:**
$$\det(P, Q) = \tfrac{1}{4}\big[D(1+st) + S(t-s)\big] = \tfrac{1}{4}\big[D \cdot
 0 + S \cdot 2\big] = \frac{S}{2}$$
where $S = \mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{c}$. So $[\triangle APQ] = \frac{|S|}{4}$.

**Area of $\triangle BCP$:** With $s = -1$:
$$\det(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{b},\, P - \mathbf{b}) = \tfrac{1}{2}\big[D - S + |\mat
hbf{b}|^2\big] \implies [\triangle BCP] = \frac{|D - S + |\mathbf{b}|^2|}{4}$$

**Area of $\triangle BCQ$:** With $t = +1$:
$$\det(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{b},\, Q - \mathbf{b}) = \tfrac{1}{2}\big[D + |\mathbf{
c}|^2 - S\big] \implies [\triangle BCQ] = \frac{|D + |\mathbf{c}|^2 - S|}{4}$$

## Convexity Forces All Signs Positive

In a convex counterclockwise pentagon $APBCQ$, every triple of consecutive verti
ces has positive orientation:
- $P, B, C$ consecutive $\Rightarrow$ $[\triangle BCP] > 0$, so $X := D - S + |\
mathbf{b}|^2 > 0$
- $B, C, Q$ consecutive $\Rightarrow$ $[\triangle BCQ] > 0$, so $Y := D + |\math
bf{c}|^2 - S > 0$
- $Q, A, P$ consecutive $\Rightarrow$ $[\triangle APQ] > 0$, so $S > 0$

(If the pentagon is clockwise, all three flip sign simultaneously, giving the sa
me absolute areas.)

## Computing the Pentagon Area

The pentagon triangulates from $A$ as:
$$[APBCQ] = [\triangle APB] + [\triangle ABC] + [\triangle ACQ] = \frac{|\mathbf
{b}|^2}{4} + \frac{D}{2} + \frac{|\mathbf{c}|^2}{4}$$

Now observe the algebraic identity. From the definitions:
$$X + Y = (D - S + |\mathbf{b}|^2) + (D + |\mathbf{c}|^2 - S) = 2D + |\mathbf{b}
|^2 + |\mathbf{c}|^2 - 2S$$

Therefore:
$$[APBCQ] = \frac{|\mathbf{b}|^2 + |\mathbf{c}|^2}{4} + \frac{D}{2} = \frac{X +
Y + 2S}{4}$$

Substituting $X = 4[\triangle BCP]$, $Y = 4[\triangle BCQ]$, $S = 4[\triangle AP
Q]$:

$$\boxed{[APBCQ] = [\triangle BCP] + [\triangle BCQ] + 2[\triangle APQ]}$$

## Final Computation

$$[APBCQ] = 16 + 14 + 2(10) = 16 + 14 + 20 = \boxed{50}$$
