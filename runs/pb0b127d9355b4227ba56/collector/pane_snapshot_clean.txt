Let me denote $a = \deg A \geq 1$ and $b = \deg B \geq 1$.

## Proof

**Case 1: $100a \neq 99b$.**

The degree of $A^{100}$ is $100a$ and the degree of $B^{99}$ is $99b$. Since the
se are different, the higher-degree term cannot be cancelled, so:
$$\deg(A^{100} - B^{99}) = \max(100a,\, 99b).$$

I minimize $\max(100a, 99b)$ subject to $a, b \geq 1$ and $\max(a,b) > 1$:

- **$a = 1, b = 2$:** $\max(100, 198) = 198$. ✓ (degree constraint satisfied sin
ce $b > 1$)
- **$a = 2, b = 1$:** $\max(200, 99) = 200$.
- **$a = 1, b = 1$:** Not allowed (both degree 1).
- **$a \geq 2, b \geq 2$:** $\max(100a, 99b) \geq 200$.

So the minimum in this case is **198**, achieved at $a=1, b=2$. The polynomial i
s automatically nonzero since the degree-198 leading term of $B^{99}$ has no cou
nterpart in $A^{100}$.

**Case 2: $100a = 99b$ (leading terms can potentially cancel).**

Since $\gcd(99, 100) = 1$, we need $a = 99k,\, b = 100k$ for some positive integ
er $k$. The smallest solution is $a = 99, b = 100$ (both $> 1$, satisfying the c
onstraint).

I apply the **Mason–Stothers theorem** (polynomial ABC theorem). Set $f = A^{100
}$, $g = -B^{99}$, $h = A^{100} - B^{99}$. Assuming $\gcd(A, B) = 1$ (which only
 helps — common factors factor out and increase the degree), the three are pairw

$$\max(100a,\, 99b,\, \deg h) \leq n_0(fgh) - 1 \leq n_0(A) + n_0(B) + n_0(h) -
1 \leq a + b + \deg h - 1.$$

With $100a = 99b = 9900k$ and $\deg h < 9900k$:

$$9900k \leq 99k + 100k + \deg h - 1 = 199k + \deg h - 1,$$

$$\deg h \geq 9900k - 199k + 1 = 9701k + 1.$$

For $k = 1$: $\deg h \geq 9702$.

**Achievability of 9702:** Take $A(x) = x^{99} + \epsilon$, $B(x) = x^{100} + \f

99j}.$$

The $j=0$ terms cancel ($1-1=0$). The $j=1$ coefficients: $100\epsilon - 99 \cdo

The $j=2$ coefficient: $\binom{100}{2}\epsilon^2 - \binom{99}{2}\frac{100^2\epsi

This gives a term of degree $9900 - 198 = 9702$. All $j \geq 3$ terms have degre
