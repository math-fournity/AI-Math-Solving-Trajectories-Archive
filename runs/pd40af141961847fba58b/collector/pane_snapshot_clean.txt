## Proof

Let $M \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ be the covariance matrix of a multivariate G
aussian distribution. By definition of a covariance matrix, $M$ is **symmetric**
 and **positive semi-definite** ($x^T M x \geq 0$ for all $x$, and $M = M^T$).

We seek a matrix $Q$ such that
$$Q M Q^T = I.$$

### Case 1: $M$ is positive definite (non-degenerate Gaussian)

Assume $M \succ 0$, i.e., all eigenvalues of $M$ are strictly positive. This is
the case for a non-degenerate multivariate Gaussian.

Since $M$ is real symmetric, by the **spectral theorem** there exists an orthogo
nal matrix $U$ ($U^T U = U U^T = I$) and a diagonal matrix $\Lambda = \operatorn
ame{diag}(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n)$ with $\lambda_i > 0$ such that
$$M = U \Lambda U^T.$$

Define
$$\Lambda^{-1/2} = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1^{-1/2}, \dots, \lambda_n^{-1/2}
),$$
and set
$$Q = \Lambda^{-1/2} U^T.$$

Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q M Q^T &= \Lambda^{-1/2} U^T \cdot (U \Lambda U^T) \cdot U \Lambda^{-1/2} \\
&= \Lambda^{-1/2} (U^T U) \Lambda (U^T U) \Lambda^{-1/2} \\
&= \Lambda^{-1/2} \cdot I \cdot \Lambda \cdot I \cdot \Lambda^{-1/2} \\
&= \Lambda^{-1/2} \Lambda \Lambda^{-1/2} \\
&= I.
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence $Q = \Lambda^{-1/2} U^T$ is a valid choice. (Equivalently, one may use the
 Cholesky factorization $M = L L^T$ and take $Q = L^{-T}$.)

### Case 2: $M$ is positive semi-definite but singular (degenerate Gaussian)

Assume $M \succeq 0$ but $M$ is **singular**, i.e., $\operatorname{rank}(M) = r
< n$. This occurs for a degenerate Gaussian whose distribution is supported on a
 lower-dimensional affine subspace.

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists $Q$ with $Q M Q^T = I$. Since $I$
has rank $n$, we would need
$$n = \operatorname{rank}(I) = \operatorname{rank}(Q M Q^T) \leq \operatorname{r
ank}(M) = r < n,$$
a contradiction. (The inequality uses the general fact $\operatorname{rank}(AB)
\leq \min(\operatorname{rank}(A), \operatorname{rank}(B))$.)

Therefore **no such $Q$ exists** when $M$ is singular.

### Conclusion

A matrix $Q$ satisfying $Q M Q^T = I$ exists **if and only if** $M$ is positive
definite (equivalently, the Gaussian is non-degenerate). Such a $Q$ is given exp
licitly by
$$\boxed{Q = \Lambda^{-1/2} U^T}, \quad \text{where } M = U \Lambda U^T \text{ i
s the spectral decomposition of } M.$$

The matrix $Q$ does **not** always exist: it fails to exist precisely for degene
rate (singular-covariance) Gaussians.
