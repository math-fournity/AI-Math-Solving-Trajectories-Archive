## Proof

We need to find the index $n$ of the leading (highest-order) digit of $2^{2006}$
 in decimal.

**Number of digits.** A positive integer $N$ has $\lfloor \log_{10} N \rfloor +
1$ decimal digits. Here
$$\log_{10}(2^{2006}) = 2006 \cdot \log_{10} 2 = 2006 \times 0.301 = 603.806.$$
Since $603 \le 603.806 < 604$, we get $\lfloor \log_{10}(2^{2006}) \rfloor = 603
$, so the number of decimal digits is
$$603 + 1 = 604.$$

**Indexing.** The digits are stored on drives $a_0, a_1, \dots, a_n$, with $a_0$
 = units digit and $a_n$ = leading digit. With 604 digits indexed consecutively
from $0$, the highest index is
$$n = 604 - 1 = 603.$$

**Verification of the leading digit.** Since $\log_{10}(2^{2006}) = 603.806$, we
 have $2^{2006} = 10^{603.806} = 10^{0.806}\cdot 10^{603}$. The mantissa $10^{0.
806}$ lies in $[6, 7)$ (since $\log_{10} 6 \approx 0.778$ and $\log_{10} 7 \appr
ox 0.845$), confirming the leading digit is $6$ and the number indeed occupies e
xactly 604 digits — consistent with $n = 603$.

### Answer

$$\boxed{n = 603}$$
