Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the algebraic independence
of $\{a,b,c,d\}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ forces $\mathbb{Q}(a,b) \cap \mathbb{Q}(c,d)
= \mathbb{Q}$.

**Answer: The statement is TRUE.**

## Proof

Let $\alpha \in \mathbb{Q}(a,b) \cap \mathbb{Q}(c,d)$. We show $\alpha \in \math
bb{Q}$.

Since $\alpha \in \mathbb{Q}(a,b)$, there exist $f, g \in \mathbb{Q}[x,y]$ with
$g \neq 0$ such that
$$\alpha = \frac{f(a,b)}{g(a,b)}.$$
Since $\alpha \in \mathbb{Q}(c,d)$, there exist $h, k \in \mathbb{Q}[u,v]$ with
$k \neq 0$ such that
$$\alpha = \frac{h(c,d)}{k(c,d)}.$$

Note that $g(a,b) \neq 0$ and $k(c,d) \neq 0$: since $\{a,b\}$ and $\{c,d\}$ are
 each algebraically independent over $\mathbb{Q}$ (being subsets of an algebraic
ally independent set), a nonzero polynomial evaluated at them cannot vanish.

From the equality of the two expressions:
$$f(a,b)\, k(c,d) = g(a,b)\, h(c,d). \tag{$\star$}$$

**Key step: Lift to a polynomial identity.** Since $\{a,b,c,d\}$ is algebraicall
y independent over $\mathbb{Q}$, the evaluation map
$$\mathbb{Q}[x,y,u,v] \longrightarrow \mathbb{Q}[a,b,c,d], \quad p(x,y,u,v) \map
sto p(a,b,c,d)$$
is an isomorphism. Therefore ($\star$) holds in $\mathbb{Q}[a,b,c,d]$ if and onl
y if the corresponding identity
$$f(x,y)\, k(u,v) = g(x,y)\, h(u,v) \tag{$\star\star$}$$
holds in $\mathbb{Q}[x,y,u,v]$.

**UFD argument.** The ring $\mathbb{Q}[x,y,u,v]$ is a UFD. Observe that:
- $f(x,y)$ and $g(x,y)$ are elements of the subring $\mathbb{Q}[x,y]$,
- $k(u,v)$ and $h(u,v)$ are elements of the subring $\mathbb{Q}[u,v]$,

and these two subrings involve **disjoint sets of variables**. An irreducible el
ement of $\mathbb{Q}[x,y]$ (viewed in $\mathbb{Q}[x,y,u,v]$) involves only $x,y$
 and therefore cannot be an associate of an irreducible element of $\mathbb{Q}[u
,v]$, which involves only $u,v$ — the only common associates are units in $\math
bb{Q}^*$.

Write the factorizations into irreducibles:
$$f = c_f \prod_i f_i^{e_i}, \quad g = c_g \prod_j g_j^{d_j}, \quad k = c_k \pro
d_p k_p^{a_p}, \quad h = c_h \prod_q h_q^{b_q},$$
where $c_f, c_g, c_k, c_h \in \mathbb{Q}^*$, the $f_i, g_j$ are irreducible in $
\mathbb{Q}[x,y]$, and the $k_p, h_q$ are irreducible in $\mathbb{Q}[u,v]$.

Substituting into ($\star\star$):
$$c_f c_k \prod_i f_i^{e_i} \prod_p k_p^{a_p} = c_g c_h \prod_j g_j^{d_j} \prod_
q h_q^{b_q}.$$

By unique factorization in $\mathbb{Q}[x,y,u,v]$, and using the fact that irredu
cibles from $\mathbb{Q}[x,y]$ and $\mathbb{Q}[u,v]$ cannot be associates of each
 other:

1. The $\mathbb{Q}[x,y]$-factors must match: $\prod_i f_i^{e_i} = \prod_j g_j^{d
_j}$ up to a unit in $\mathbb{Q}^*$, which gives $f = \lambda\, g$ for some $\la
mbda \in \mathbb{Q}^*$.

2. The $\mathbb{Q}[u,v]$-factors must match: $\prod_p k_p^{a_p} = \prod_q h_q^{b
_q}$ up to a unit, which gives $k = \mu\, h$ for some $\mu \in \mathbb{Q}^*$.

3. The constants satisfy $\lambda \mu = 1$.

**Conclusion.** From $f = \lambda g$ with $\lambda \in \mathbb{Q}^*$:
$$\alpha = \frac{f(a,b)}{g(a,b)} = \frac{\lambda\, g(a,b)}{g(a,b)} = \lambda \in
 \mathbb{Q}.$$

Since $\alpha$ was an arbitrary element of $\mathbb{Q}(a,b) \cap \mathbb{Q}(c,d)
$, we conclude
$$\mathbb{Q}(a,b) \cap \mathbb{Q}(c,d) = \mathbb{Q}. \qquad \blacksquare$$

**Remark.** The argument uses two ingredients: (1) algebraic independence makes
the evaluation map $\mathbb{Q}[x,y,u,v] \to \mathbb{Q}[a,b,c,d]$ an isomorphism,
 converting the field equality into a polynomial identity; (2) the UFD structure
 of $\mathbb{Q}[x,y,u,v] = \mathbb{Q}[x,y] \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}[u,v]$
 with disjoint variable sets forces any factorization splitting to be trivial. T
he same proof shows more generally that if $\{x_1,\dots,x_m, y_1,\dots,y_n\}$ is
 algebraically independent over $\mathbb{Q}$, then $\mathbb{Q}(x_1,\dots,x_m) \c
ap \mathbb{Q}(y_1,\dots,y_n) = \mathbb{Q}$.
