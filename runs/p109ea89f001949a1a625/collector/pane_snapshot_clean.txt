## Proof

We have 16 docking pins arranged in a 4×4 grid: 4 rows and 4 columns, with unit
spacing in both directions.

**Key observation.** A rectangular zone whose corners lie on grid pins must be a
xis-aligned with the grid. (The storage-zone framing — "smallest unit," "largest
 possible area," zones in a row/column grid — means a zone is determined by choo
sing a contiguous block of rows and a contiguous block of columns.) Such a recta
ngle is completely and uniquely determined by:

- choosing **2 distinct rows** (the top and bottom edges), and
- choosing **2 distinct columns** (the left and right edges).

The four pins at the intersections of the chosen rows and columns are exactly th
e four corners of the rectangle. Conversely, every axis-aligned rectangle with c
orners on pins arises this way, and different pairs of rows/columns give differe
nt rectangles (different position or size). Squares are included automatically,
since a square is just the special case where the row-separation equals the colu
mn-separation.

**Counting.**

- Number of ways to choose 2 rows out of 4:
$$\binom{4}{2} = \frac{4\cdot 3}{2} = 6.$$
- Number of ways to choose 2 columns out of 4:
$$\binom{4}{2} = 6.$$

Since the row choice and column choice are independent, the total number of rect
angles is

$$\binom{4}{2}\times\binom{4}{2} = 6 \times 6 = 36.$$

**Verification by size.** We can also count by dimensions. A rectangle spanning
$r$ rows and $c$ columns (where $r,c\in\{1,2,3\}$) can be placed in $(4-r)(4-c)$
 positions. The count is:

| $r\backslash c$ | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $3\cdot3=9$ | $3\cdot2=6$ | $3\cdot1=3$ |
| 2 | $2\cdot3=6$ | $2\cdot2=4$ | $2\cdot1=2$ |
| 3 | $1\cdot3=3$ | $1\cdot2=2$ | $1\cdot1=1$ |

Sum: $9+6+3+6+4+2+3+2+1 = 36$. ✓

Both methods agree.

$$\boxed{36}$$
