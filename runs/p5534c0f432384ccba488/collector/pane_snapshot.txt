## Answer

**Yes, if $A$ is Hausdorff (the standard convention in functional analysis); No
in general without the Hausdorff assumption.** Below I prove the positive result
 and then give a counterexample showing the Hausdorff hypothesis is essential.

---

## Proof (Hausdorff case)

**Setup.** Let $(A, \tau)$ be a Hausdorff topological vector space, $B \subseteq
 A$ a linear subspace equipped with a translation-invariant metric $d$ (i.e., $d
(b_1+c,\, b_2+c)=d(b_1,b_2)$ for all $b_1,b_2,c\in B$) such that (i) $(B,d)$ is
complete and (ii) the $d$-topology equals the subspace topology $\tau|_B$.

### Step 1. The $d$-uniformity coincides with the subspace (group) uniformity on
 $B$.

Since $d$ is translation-invariant, write $V_\varepsilon = \{b \in B : d(b,0) <
\varepsilon\}$. Then $d$-balls are translates: $B_\varepsilon(b) = b + V_\vareps
ilon$, and the $d$-uniformity has entourages
$$\mathcal{E}_\varepsilon^d = \{(b_1,b_2)\in B\times B : b_1 - b_2 \in V_\vareps
ilon\}.$$

The subspace uniformity on $B$ (induced by the TVS uniformity of $A$) has entour
ages
$$\mathcal{E}_U = \{(b_1,b_2)\in B\times B : b_1 - b_2 \in U\cap B\},$$
where $U$ ranges over $\tau$-neighborhoods of $0$ in $A$.

Because the $d$-topology equals $\tau|_B$, the family $\{V_\varepsilon\}_{\varep
silon>0}$ is a neighborhood base at $0$ in $B$. Hence:

- **$d$-uniformity $\subseteq$ subspace uniformity:** For any $\varepsilon>0$, $
V_\varepsilon$ is a $\tau|_B$-neighborhood of $0$, so $V_\varepsilon \supseteq U
\cap B$ for some $\tau$-neighborhood $U$ of $0$; thus $\mathcal{E}_U \subseteq \
mathcal{E}_\varepsilon^d$.

- **Subspace uniformity $\subseteq$ $d$-uniformity:** For any $\tau$-neighborhoo
d $U$ of $0$, the set $U\cap B$ is a $\tau|_B$-neighborhood of $0$, so $V_\varep
silon \subseteq U\cap B$ for some $\varepsilon>0$; thus $\mathcal{E}_\varepsilon
^d \subseteq \mathcal{E}_U$.

The two uniformities are therefore identical. $\checkmark$

### Step 2. $d$-completeness $\Rightarrow$ completeness in the subspace uniformi
ty.

This is immediate from Step 1: Cauchy sequences (and Cauchy filters) coincide, a
nd convergence coincides. Since $(B,d)$ is complete, $B$ is complete in the subs
pace uniformity. $\checkmark$

### Step 3. A complete subset of a Hausdorff TVS is closed.

Let $x \in \overline{B}^{\,\tau}$. Since $B$ is metrizable (hence first-countabl
e), the subspace $\overline{B}$ is also first-countable, so there exists a **seq
uence** $(b_n)_{n\geq 1}$ in $B$ with $b_n \to x$ in $\tau$.

**$(b_n)$ is Cauchy in the subspace uniformity:** Given any $\tau$-neighborhood
$W$ of $0$, pick a balanced $\tau$-neighborhood $U$ of $0$ with $U - U \subseteq
 W$. Since $b_n \to x$, for large $n,m$ we have $b_n - x \in U$ and $b_m - x \in
 U$, hence $b_n - b_m \in U - U \subseteq W$.

By Step 2, $(b_n)$ is $d$-Cauchy, so by $d$-completeness there exists $b \in B$
with $b_n \to b$ in $d$, hence $b_n \to b$ in $\tau|_B$, hence $b_n \to b$ in $\
tau$.

But $b_n \to x$ in $\tau$ as well. Since $A$ is **Hausdorff**, limits are unique
, so $x = b \in B$.

Thus $\overline{B}^{\,\tau} \subseteq B$, i.e., $B$ is $\tau$-closed. $\blacksqu
are$

## Counterexample (non-Hausdorff case): $B$ need not be closed

Let $A = \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}$ with the topology
$$\tau = \bigl\{\mathbb{R}\times U : U \subseteq \mathbb{R} \text{ open (usual)}
\bigr\}.$$

This is the product of the indiscrete topology on the first factor and the usual
 topology on the second. One checks:

- **$A$ is a TVS:** Addition and scalar multiplication are continuous (continuit
y reduces to the second coordinate, where the usual topology makes them continuo
us; the first coordinate is unconstrained since its topology is indiscrete).
- **$A$ is not Hausdorff:** Points $(x_1, y)$ and $(x_2, y)$ with $x_1 \neq x_2$
 cannot be separated (every open set containing one contains the other).

Now set $B = \{0\}\times \mathbb{R}$, a linear subspace of $A$.

- **Subspace topology on $B$:** $(\mathbb{R}\times U)\cap(\{0\}\times\mathbb{R})
 = \{0\}\times U$, so $\tau|_B$ is the usual topology on $\mathbb{R}$ (via the i
dentification $(0,y)\leftrightarrow y$).
- **Metric:** $d\bigl((0,y_1),(0,y_2)\bigr) = |y_1 - y_2|$. This is translation-
invariant (translation by $(0,c)$ preserves $|\cdot|$), the $d$-topology is the
usual topology $= \tau|_B$, and $(B,d) \cong (\mathbb{R},|\cdot|)$ is **complete
**.
- **$B$ is not closed:** For any $(x,y)\in A$ and any $\tau$-neighborhood $\math
bb{R}\times U$ of $(x,y)$ (with $y\in U$), we have $(\mathbb{R}\times U)\cap B =
 \{0\}\times U \neq \emptyset$. Hence $\overline{B}^{\,\tau} = A \supsetneq B$.

All hypotheses of the problem are satisfied (metrizable, complete, translation-i
nvariant metric, metric topology $=$ subspace topology), yet $B$ is **not** $\ta
u$-closed. $\blacksquare$

## Conclusion

The answer to the question as stated (without a Hausdorff hypothesis) is **No**:
 $B$ is not necessarily closed. The Hausdorff separation axiom is the crucial ad
ditional ingredient — under it, the translation-invariance of $d$ forces the met
ric uniformity to agree with the TVS subspace uniformity, making $B$ uniformly c
omplete, and uniformly complete subsets of Hausdorff TVS are always closed.
