## Solution

**Problem.** Find the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of
$$\phi(x) = \lambda \int_0^{2\pi} \bigl(2\pi^2 - 6\pi|x-s| + 3(x-s)^2\bigr)\,\ph
i(s)\,ds.$$

---

### Step 1. Rewrite the kernel

Set $f(t) = 2\pi^2 - 6\pi|t| + 3t^2$. Completing the square,
$$f(t) = 3(|t|-\pi)^2 - \pi^2,$$
so $f$ is **even**: $f(-t)=f(t)$. The kernel $K(x,s)=f(x-s)$ is therefore symmet
ric, making the integral operator $T$ self-adjoint on $L^2[0,2\pi]$.

---

### Step 2. Distributional second derivative of $f$

For $t>0$: $f(t)=3t^2-6\pi t+2\pi^2$, so $f'(t)=6t-6\pi$, $f''(t)=6$.
For $t<0$: $f(t)=3t^2+6\pi t+2\pi^2$, so $f'(t)=6t+6\pi$, $f''(t)=6$.

At $t=0$ the derivative jumps:
$$f'(0^+)=-6\pi,\quad f'(0^-)=6\pi,\quad \text{jump} = -12\pi.$$

Hence, in the distributional sense,
$$f''(t) = 6 - 12\pi\,\delta(t).$$

---

### Step 3. Convert the integral equation to an ODE

Let $g(x) = T[\phi](x) = \int_0^{2\pi} f(x-s)\,\phi(s)\,ds$. Differentiating twi
ce (justified distributionally):

$$g''(x) = \int_0^{2\pi} f''(x-s)\,\phi(s)\,ds = \int_0^{2\pi}\bigl[6 - 12\pi\,\
delta(x-s)\bigr]\phi(s)\,ds = 6A - 12\pi\,\phi(x),$$

where $A = \int_0^{2\pi}\phi(s)\,ds$.

---

### Step 4. Boundary conditions (periodic)

**Value:** $g(0) = \int_0^{2\pi} f(s)\,\phi(s)\,ds$ and $g(2\pi)=\int_0^{2\pi} f
(2\pi-s)\,\phi(s)\,ds$. Since $f(2\pi-s)=f(s)$ for $s\in[0,2\pi]$, we get $g(0)=
g(2\pi)$.

**Derivative:** $g'(0)=\int_0^{2\pi} f'(-s)\,\phi(s)\,ds$ and $g'(2\pi)=\int_0^{
2\pi} f'(2\pi-s)\,\phi(s)\,ds$. For $s\in(0,2\pi)$: $f'(-s)=-6s+6\pi$ and $f'(2\
pi-s)=6\pi-6s$, so $f'(-s)=f'(2\pi-s)$, giving $g'(0)=g'(2\pi)$.

If $\phi$ is an eigenfunction with $T\phi = \mu\,\phi$ (where $\mu = 1/\lambda$)
, then $g=\mu\phi$ inherits **periodic boundary conditions**:
$$\phi(0)=\phi(2\pi),\qquad \phi'(0)=\phi'(2\pi).$$

---

### Step 5. Solve the eigenvalue ODE

From $g''=\mu\phi'' = 6A - 12\pi\,\phi$:

$$\phi'' + \omega^2\phi = \frac{6A}{\mu},\qquad \omega^2 = \frac{12\pi}{\mu}.$$

**The constant function is in the null space.** Direct computation gives $T[1](x
)=0$ for all $x$ (the even kernel integrates to zero over the symmetric interval
). Thus $\mu=0$ for $\phi\equiv\text{const}$, so constants are **not** eigenfunc
tions for any finite $\lambda$. For any eigenfunction with $\mu\neq 0$, the cons
tant part of $\mu\phi$ must vanish, forcing $A=0$.

With $A=0$ the ODE becomes $\phi''+\omega^2\phi=0$, with general solution $\phi(
x)=B\cos(\omega x)+C\sin(\omega x)$.

**Periodic BCs** require $\cos(2\pi\omega)=1$, i.e. $\omega = n$ for $n\in\mathb
b{Z}$. The case $n=0$ gives $\phi=$ const, which requires $A=2\pi B=0$, hence is
 trivial. For $n\geq 1$:

$$\boxed{\mu_n = \frac{12\pi}{n^2},\qquad \lambda_n = \frac{n^2}{12\pi},\qquad n
=1,2,3,\dots}$$

with eigenfunctions $\cos(nx)$ and $\sin(nx)$ (each eigenvalue has multiplicity
2).

---

### Step 6. Direct verification via Fourier series

On $[0,2\pi]$, $f(t)=3(t-\pi)^2-\pi^2 = 12\pi^2\,\bar{B}_2\!\bigl(\tfrac{t}{2\pi
}\bigr)$, where $\bar{B}_2$ is the periodic Bernoulli polynomial. Its Fourier se
ries is

$$\bar{B}_2(\theta) = \frac{1}{\pi^2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\cos(2\pi n\theta)
}{n^2},$$

so

$$f(t) = 12\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\cos(nt)}{n^2}.$$

(Verified: $f(0)=2\pi^2 = 12\cdot\frac{\pi^2}{6}$; $f(\pi)=-\pi^2 = 12\cdot(-\fr
ac{\pi^2}{12})$.)

Then for $m\geq 1$:

$$T[\cos(ms)](x) = 12\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^2}\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\bigl(n(x-
s)\bigr)\cos(ms)\,ds = \frac{12}{m^2}\cdot\pi\cos(mx) = \frac{12\pi}{m^2}\cos(mx
),$$

using $\int_0^{2\pi}\cos(ns)\cos(ms)\,ds = \pi\,\delta_{nm}$ and $\int_0^{2\pi}\
sin(ns)\cos(ms)\,ds=0$. Similarly $T[\sin(ms)]=\frac{12\pi}{m^2}\sin(mx)$.

This confirms $\mu_m = 12\pi/m^2$, i.e. $\lambda_m = m^2/(12\pi)$, with eigenfun
ctions $\cos(mx)$ and $\sin(mx)$.

---

### Step 7. Completeness

The operator $T$ is compact and self-adjoint on $L^2[0,2\pi]$. Its null space is
 $\operatorname{span}\{1\}$ (since $T[1]=0$ and the Fourier series shows no othe
r null directions). The remaining eigenfunctions $\{\cos(nx),\sin(nx):n\geq 1\}$
 together with the constant span all of $L^2[0,2\pi]$, so **no eigenvalues are m
issing**.

---

### Answer

The integral equation $\phi = \lambda\,T\phi$ has:

| Eigenvalue $\lambda_n$ | Eigenfunctions | Multiplicity |
|---|---|---|
| $\dfrac{n^2}{12\pi}$, $n=1,2,3,\dots$ | $\cos(nx)$, $\sin(nx)$ | 2 each |

The constant function $\phi\equiv\text{const}$ satisfies $T\phi=0$ and is **not*
* an eigenfunction for any finite $\lambda$.
