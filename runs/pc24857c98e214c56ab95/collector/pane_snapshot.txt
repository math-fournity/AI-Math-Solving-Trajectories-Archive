## Proof

**Setup.** In $\mathbb{R}^n$, consider the $2^n$ closed unit balls $D_v = \{x :
|x - v| \le 1\}$ centered at the vertices $v \in \{\pm 1\}^n$ of the hypercube.
We seek a ball $B_n = \{x : |x| \le R\}$ centered at the origin, of some radius
$R \ge 0$, such that:

1. $B_n$ does not intersect the **interior** of any $D_v$, i.e. $B_n \cap \{x :
|x-v| < 1\} = \emptyset$ for every $v \in \{\pm1\}^n$;
2. $B_n$ contains a point with some coordinate $> 2$.

We want the smallest $n$ for which such an $R$ exists.

**Step 1: Maximal admissible radius.** For a fixed vertex $v$, the closest point
 of $B_n$ to $v$ lies on the ray from the origin through $v$, at distance $R$ fr
om the origin (when $R \le |v|$). Its distance to $v$ is $|v| - R$. The interior
 of $D_v$ is disjoint from $B_n$ iff this minimum distance is $\ge 1$:

$$|v| - R \ge 1 \iff R \le |v| - 1.$$

Since $|v| = \sqrt{1^2 + \cdots + 1^2} = \sqrt{n}$ for every vertex $v \in \{\pm
 1\}^n$, the constraint is the same for all $2^n$ balls:

$$R \le \sqrt{n} - 1.$$

Conversely, $R = \sqrt{n} - 1$ is admissible: the point $\frac{v}{|v|}(\sqrt{n}-
1)$ on the segment from $0$ to $v$ lies at distance exactly $1$ from $v$, i.e. o
n the **boundary** of $D_v$, not in its interior. So $B_n$ touches each $D_v$ on
 the boundary but never enters any interior. Hence the maximal admissible radius
 is

$$R_{\max}(n) = \sqrt{n} - 1.$$

**Step 2: Largest coordinate attainable inside $B_n$.** For any point $x = (x_1,
 \dots, x_n) \in B_n$, we have $|x_1| \le |x| \le R$, with equality achieved at
$x = (R, 0, \dots, 0)$. Therefore the supremum of coordinates of points in $B_n$
 is exactly $R$. A point with a coordinate $> 2$ exists in $B_n$ iff $R > 2$.

**Step 3: Combine.** Such a ball exists iff the maximal admissible radius exceed
s $2$:

$$\sqrt{n} - 1 > 2 \iff \sqrt{n} > 3 \iff n > 9 \iff n \ge 10.$$

**Step 4: Verify the boundary.**
- At $n = 9$: $R_{\max} = \sqrt{9} - 1 = 2$. Every point in $B_n$ has every coor
dinate $\le 2$; the point $(2,0,\dots,0)$ attains coordinate exactly $2$, not st
rictly greater. So no admissible ball contains a point with coordinate $> 2$. ✗
- At $n = 10$: $R_{\max} = \sqrt{10} - 1 \approx 2.1623 > 2$. The ball of radius
 $\sqrt{10}-1$ centered at the origin is admissible (it is tangent to each of th
e $2^{10}$ unit balls on their boundaries, never entering interiors), and it con
tains the point $(\sqrt{10}-1, 0, \dots, 0)$ whose first coordinate is $\sqrt{10
}-1 > 2$. ✓

**Conclusion.** The smallest positive integer $n$ with the required property is

$$\boxed{n = 10}.$$
