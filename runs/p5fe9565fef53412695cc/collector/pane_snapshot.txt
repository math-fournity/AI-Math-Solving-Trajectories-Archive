## Perturbation Expansion of $\ddot{u} + u = \varepsilon u^2$, $u(0)=1$, $\dot{u
}(0)=0$

We seek a regular (Poincaré) expansion
$$u(t) = u_0(t) + \varepsilon\, u_1(t) + \varepsilon^2\, u_2(t) + O(\varepsilon^
3),$$
with each $u_n$ satisfying the homogeneous initial conditions $u_n(0)=0$, $\dot
u_n(0)=0$ for $n\ge 1$, and $u_0(0)=1$, $\dot u_0(0)=0$.

Substituting into the ODE and expanding the RHS:
$$\varepsilon\,u^2 = \varepsilon\bigl(u_0^2 + 2\varepsilon\,u_0 u_1 + O(\varepsi
lon^2)\bigr) = \varepsilon\,u_0^2 + \varepsilon^2\,(2u_0 u_1) + O(\varepsilon^3)
.$$

---

### $O(1)$: $\ddot u_0 + u_0 = 0$, $u_0(0)=1$, $\dot u_0(0)=0$

$$\boxed{u_0 = \cos t.}$$

---

### $O(\varepsilon)$: $\ddot u_1 + u_1 = u_0^2 = \cos^2 t = \tfrac12 + \tfrac12\
cos 2t$

Particular solutions:
- constant $\tfrac12 \Rightarrow$ try $A$: $A=\tfrac12$.
- $\tfrac12\cos 2t \Rightarrow$ try $B\cos 2t$: $(-4+1)B=\tfrac12 \Rightarrow B=
-\tfrac16$.

So $u_1 = C_1\cos t + C_2\sin t + \tfrac12 - \tfrac16\cos 2t$. Initial condition
s give $C_1 = -\tfrac12+\tfrac16 = -\tfrac13$, $C_2=0$:

$$\boxed{u_1 = -\tfrac13\cos t + \tfrac12 - \tfrac16\cos 2t.}$$

---

### $O(\varepsilon^2)$: $\ddot u_2 + u_2 = 2u_0 u_1$

Compute the forcing:
$$2u_0 u_1 = 2\cos t\!\left(-\tfrac13\cos t + \tfrac12 - \tfrac16\cos 2t\right)
= -\tfrac23\cos^2 t + \cos t - \tfrac13\cos t\cos 2t.$$

Using $\cos^2 t = \tfrac12(1+\cos 2t)$ and $\cos t\cos 2t = \tfrac12(\cos 3t + \
cos t)$:
$$2u_0 u_1 = -\tfrac13 - \tfrac13\cos 2t + \cos t - \tfrac16\cos 3t - \tfrac16\c
os t = -\tfrac13 + \tfrac56\cos t - \tfrac13\cos 2t - \tfrac16\cos 3t.$$

Particular solutions (term by term):
- $-\tfrac13 \Rightarrow$ constant $A=-\tfrac13$.
- $-\tfrac13\cos 2t \Rightarrow$ $C\cos 2t$: $(-4+1)C=-\tfrac13 \Rightarrow C=\t
frac19$.
- $-\tfrac16\cos 3t \Rightarrow$ $D\cos 3t$: $(-9+1)D=-\tfrac16 \Rightarrow D=\t
frac1{48}$.
- $\tfrac56\cos t$ is **resonant** $\Rightarrow$ try $B\,t\sin t$: $\ddot{(Bt\si
n t)}+Bt\sin t = 2B\cos t$, so $2B=\tfrac56 \Rightarrow B=\tfrac5{12}$.

Thus
$$u_2 = E\cos t + F\sin t - \tfrac13 + \tfrac5{12}\,t\sin t + \tfrac19\cos 2t +
\tfrac1{48}\cos 3t.$$

Initial conditions:
- $u_2(0)= E - \tfrac13 + \tfrac19 + \tfrac1{48}=0 \;\Rightarrow\; E = \tfrac13-
\tfrac19-\tfrac1{48} = \tfrac{48-16-3}{144} = \tfrac{29}{144}.$
- $\dot u_2(0)= F = 0$ (all sine terms vanish at $t=0$, and the $t\sin t$ term c
ontributes $0$).

$$\boxed{u_2 = \tfrac{29}{144}\cos t - \tfrac13 + \tfrac5{12}\,t\sin t + \tfrac1
9\cos 2t + \tfrac1{48}\cos 3t.}$$

---

### Final expansion (up to $O(\varepsilon^2)$)

$$\boxed{\,u(t) = \cos t + \varepsilon\!\left(-\tfrac13\cos t + \tfrac12 - \tfra
c16\cos 2t\right) + \varepsilon^2\!\left(\tfrac{29}{144}\cos t - \tfrac13 + \tfr
ac5{12}\,t\sin t + \tfrac19\cos 2t + \tfrac1{48}\cos 3t\right) + O(\varepsilon^3
).\,}$$

**Remarks.**
- A **secular term** $\tfrac5{12}\varepsilon^2\,t\sin t$ appears at second order
, reflecting the frequency shift induced by the quadratic nonlinearity; it signa
ls the breakdown of the regular expansion on timescales $t\sim \varepsilon^{-2}$
, which is the expected validity horizon for a Poincaré expansion of this equati
on.
- The expansion satisfies the initial conditions exactly to each order: $u(0)=1+
O(\varepsilon^3)$, $\dot u(0)=0+O(\varepsilon^3)$.
