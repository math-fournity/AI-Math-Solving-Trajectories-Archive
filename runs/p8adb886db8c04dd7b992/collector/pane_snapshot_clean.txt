## Solution

The statement is **false** in general. It fails for every irreducible monic poly
nomial of degree ≥ 2. Below is a complete proof, followed by an explicit counter
example.

---

### General argument (via Chebotarev's density theorem)

Let $f \in \mathbf{Z}[X]$ be irreducible, monic, of degree $n \geq 2$. Let $K$ b
e the splitting field of $f$ over $\mathbf{Q}$ and let $G = \mathrm{Gal}(K/\math
bf{Q})$.

**Transitivity.** Since $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbf{Q}$, the group $G$ acts
 transitively on the $n$ roots of $f$. A transitive group on $n$ elements has or
der $\geq n$, so $|G| \geq n \geq 2$.

**Frobenius and splitting.** For any prime $p \nmid \mathrm{disc}(f)$, the facto
rization pattern of $f \bmod p$ corresponds to the cycle type of the Frobenius e
lement $\mathrm{Frob}_p \in G$ acting on the roots. In particular, $f$ splits co
mpletely into linear factors mod $p$ if and only if $\mathrm{Frob}_p$ fixes ever
y root, i.e., $\mathrm{Frob}_p = \mathrm{id}_G$ (since $K$ is generated by the r
oots of $f$).

**Chebotarev.** By Chebotarev's density theorem, the set of primes $p$ (unramifi
ed, i.e., $p \nmid \mathrm{disc}(f)$) with $\mathrm{Frob}_p = \mathrm{id}_G$ has
 natural density
$$\frac{1}{|G|} \leq \frac{1}{2}.$$
Therefore the set of primes $p \nmid \mathrm{disc}(f)$ for which $f$ does **not*
* split completely mod $p$ has density
$$1 - \frac{1}{|G|} \geq 1 - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2} > 0.$$
A set of primes of positive density is infinite. Adding back the finitely many p
rimes dividing $\mathrm{disc}(f)$ does not change this conclusion.

**Conclusion.** For every irreducible monic $f$ of degree $\geq 2$, there are **
infinitely many** primes $p$ such that $f$ does not split into linear factors mo
d $p$. Hence the proposed statement is false.

(The only case where the statement holds is $n = 1$: a linear polynomial is alre
ady a linear factor mod every prime, so the "bad" set is empty, hence finite.)

---

### Explicit counterexample: $f(X) = X^2 + 1$

- $f$ is monic and irreducible in $\mathbf{Z}[X]$ (no integer root).
- For an odd prime $p$, $f$ splits into linear factors mod $p$ iff $X^2 + 1 \equ
iv 0 \pmod{p}$ has a solution iff $-1$ is a quadratic residue mod $p$.
- By the first supplement to quadratic reciprocity (Euler's criterion), $-1$ is
a QR mod $p$ iff $p \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$.
- Hence $f$ does **not** split mod $p$ for every prime $p \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$.
- By Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions, there are infinit
ely many primes $p \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$.

Therefore $f(X) = X^2 + 1$ is an irreducible monic polynomial for which infinite
ly many primes $p$ fail to make $f$ split into linear factors mod $p$. This dire
ctly refutes the statement.

---

**Answer:** The statement is **not true**. For every irreducible monic polynomia
l of degree $\geq 2$, infinitely many primes $p$ fail to split it into linear fa
ctors mod $p$; the splitting primes have density $1/|\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbf{Q})|
 \leq 1/2$ by Chebotarev.
