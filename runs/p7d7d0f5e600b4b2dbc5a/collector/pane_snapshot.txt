## Solution

We compute the variance of
$$X := \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T W_t\,dt$$
where $(W_t)_{t\ge 0}$ is a standard one-dimensional Brownian motion.

**Step 1: Mean.** Since Brownian motion has $\mathbb{E}[W_t]=0$ for every $t$, b
y linearity and Fubini,
$$\mathbb{E}[X]=\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T \mathbb{E}[W_t]\,dt = 0.$$
Hence $\operatorname{Var}(X)=\mathbb{E}[X^2]$.

**Step 2: Second moment.** Using Fubini (justified since the integrand is non-ne
gative / the double integral is finite),
$$\mathbb{E}[X^2] = \frac{1}{T^2}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left(\int_0^T W_s\,ds\right)
\!\left(\int_0^T W_t\,dt\right)\right] = \frac{1}{T^2}\int_0^T\!\int_0^T \mathbb
{E}[W_s W_t]\,ds\,dt.$$

**Step 3: Covariance kernel.** For standard Brownian motion,
$$\mathbb{E}[W_s W_t] = \min(s,t).$$

**Step 4: Evaluate the double integral.** By symmetry of $\min(s,t)$ in $s,t$,
$$\int_0^T\!\int_0^T \min(s,t)\,ds\,dt = 2\int_0^T\!\int_0^t s\,ds\,dt.$$
The inner integral:
$$\int_0^t s\,ds = \frac{t^2}{2}.$$
Therefore
$$2\int_0^T \frac{t^2}{2}\,dt = \int_0^T t^2\,dt = \frac{T^3}{3}.$$

**Step 5: Assemble.**
$$\operatorname{Var}(X) = \frac{1}{T^2}\cdot\frac{T^3}{3} = \frac{T}{3}.$$

**Sanity check (Itô isometry / scaling).** The random variable $\int_0^T W_t\,dt
$ is Gaussian with mean $0$ and variance $\int_0^T\!\int_0^T \min(s,t)\,ds\,dt =
 T^3/3$, so dividing by $T$ gives variance $T/3$. This matches the well-known re
sult that the time-average of Brownian motion over $[0,T]$ has variance $T/3$.

## Result

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T W_t\,dt\right) = \frac{T}
{3}}$$
