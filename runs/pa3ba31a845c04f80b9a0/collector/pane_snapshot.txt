## Proof

Let \(x = t^B\) and \(\sigma = \dfrac{x^2}{2\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{t^{2B}}{2\sqrt{2}
}\), so that \(z = \dfrac{t^B}{2^{3/4}}\) satisfies \(z^2 = \sigma\).

The exponent is:
$$E(t) = \frac{2^{1/4}(\sqrt{\pi}-1)}{\pi}\, x\, e^{-\sigma} \;-\; \frac{2\sqrt{
2}(\sqrt{\pi}-1)\,t^{2B} + \sqrt{\pi}+2}{4\sqrt{\pi}}\;\operatorname{erfc}(z) \;
+\; \frac{1}{2}.$$

Note that \(2\sqrt{2}\cdot t^{2B} = 2\sqrt{2}\cdot 2\sqrt{2}\,\sigma = 8\sigma\)
.

---

### Case 1: \(B = 0\)

Then \(x = 1\), \(\sigma = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\), and every quantity in \(E(t)\)
 is a **finite constant** independent of \(t\). Hence \(E(t) = C\) (constant), a
nd
$$\frac{1}{t}\,e^{E(t)} = \frac{e^C}{t} \;\longrightarrow\; 0.$$

2} + \frac{3}{4z^4} - \cdots\right) = \frac{e^{-\sigma}}{z\sqrt{\pi}}\left(1 - \
frac{1}{2\sigma} + \cdots\right).$$

$$E(t) = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{2^{3/4}(-3\sqrt{\pi}+6)}{4\pi\, x}\, e^{-\sigma} +
