## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. Every locally connected, second-countable space admits a countab
le basis consisting of connected (open) sets.

**Proof.**

Let $X$ be a locally connected, second-countable space, and let $\mathcal{B} = \
{B_n\}_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ be a countable basis for the topology of $X$.

**Step 1: Each open set in $X$ has at most countably many connected components,
and each component is open.**

Since $X$ is locally connected, every connected component of an open subset of $
X$ is itself open. (Indeed, if $U \subseteq X$ is open and $C$ is a component of
 $U$, then for each $x \in C$, local connectedness gives a connected open neighb
orhood $V_x$ of $x$ with $V_x \subseteq U$; since $C$ is the component containin
g $x$, we have $V_x \subseteq C$, so $C$ is a union of open sets, hence open.)

Now the components of an open set $U$ form a pairwise disjoint family of open se
ts. In a second-countable space, every pairwise disjoint family of open sets is
countable: each member contains at least one basic open set $B_n$, and distinct
disjoint open sets must contain distinct basic open sets, giving an injection in
to $\mathbb{N}$.

**Step 2: Construct the candidate basis.**

For each $n \in \mathbb{N}$, let $\mathcal{C}_n$ be the collection of connected
components of $B_n$. By Step 1, each $\mathcal{C}_n$ is countable, and every mem
ber of $\mathcal{C}_n$ is open and connected. Define
$$\mathcal{C} = \bigcup_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mathcal{C}_n.$$
This is a countable union of countable sets, hence countable, and consists of co
nnected open sets.

**Step 3: Verify $\mathcal{C}$ is a basis.**

Let $U \subseteq X$ be open and $x \in U$. Since $\mathcal{B}$ is a basis, there
 exists $n$ with $x \in B_n \subseteq U$. Let $C$ be the connected component of
$B_n$ containing $x$. Then $C \in \mathcal{C}_n \subseteq \mathcal{C}$, and:
- $C$ is connected and open (Step 1),
- $x \in C$,
- $C \subseteq B_n \subseteq U$.

Thus for every point $x$ of every open set $U$, there is a member $C \in \mathca
l{C}$ with $x \in C \subseteq U$. This is precisely the definition of a basis.

**Conclusion.** $\mathcal{C}$ is a countable basis for $X$ consisting of connect
ed open sets. Therefore a locally connected, second-countable space always has a
 countable basis consisting of connected sets. $\blacksquare$
