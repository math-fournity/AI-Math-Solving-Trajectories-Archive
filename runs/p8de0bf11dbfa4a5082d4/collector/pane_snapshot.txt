**Claim:** $\sigma(a) = \{0, 1\}$.

**Step 1: The hypothesis reduces to idempotence.**

The condition $a^n = a$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ includes $n = 2$, so $a^2 = a
$; i.e., $a$ is idempotent. Conversely, idempotence implies $a^n = a$ for every
$n \geq 1$ by induction ($a^{n+1} = a^n \cdot a = a \cdot a = a$). Thus the hypo
thesis is exactly that $a$ is a nontrivial idempotent ($a \neq 0, 1$).

**Step 2: The spectrum is contained in $\{0, 1\}$.**

Since $a^2 = a$, the element $a$ is annihilated by the polynomial $p(x) = x^2 -
x = x(x-1)$. In a unital Banach algebra, the spectrum of any element is containe
d in the zero set of every polynomial that annihilates it: if $\lambda \in \sigm
a(a)$ and $p(a) = 0$, then $p(\lambda) \in \sigma(p(a)) = \{0\}$, so $p(\lambda)
 = 0$. Hence
$$\sigma(a) \subseteq \{\lambda \in \mathbb{C} : \lambda(\lambda - 1) = 0\} = \{
0, 1\}.$$

**Step 3: $0 \in \sigma(a)$.**

Suppose for contradiction that $a$ is invertible. Then from $a^2 = a$, left-mult
iplying by $a^{-1}$ gives $a = 1$, contradicting $a \neq 1$. Therefore $a$ is no
t invertible, so $0 \in \sigma(a)$.

**Step 4: $1 \in \sigma(a)$.**

Note that $1 - a$ is also idempotent:
$$(1-a)^2 = 1 - 2a + a^2 = 1 - 2a + a = 1 - a.$$
Suppose $1 - a$ were invertible. Then from $(1-a)^2 = 1-a$, multiplying by $(1-a
)^{-1}$ gives $1 - a = 1$, i.e., $a = 0$, contradicting $a \neq 0$. Hence $1 - a
$ is not invertible, which means $1 \in \sigma(a)$.

**Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 2–4,
$$\sigma(a) = \{0, 1\}.$$
