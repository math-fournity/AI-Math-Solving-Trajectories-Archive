## Proof

Let $A$ be the $C^*$-algebra and $x \in A$ a normal element with $\sigma(x) = \{
-1, 1\}$.

**Yes, such non-trivial projections exist.**

Since $x$ is normal, the unital $C^*$-subalgebra $C^*(x, 1)$ generated by $x$ an
d the identity is abelian. By the Gelfand–Naimark theorem for commutative $C^*$-
algebras,

$$C^*(x, 1) \cong C(\sigma(x)) = C(\{-1, 1\}) \cong \mathbb{C} \oplus \mathbb{C}
,$$

where the isomorphism sends $x$ to the identity function $t \mapsto t$ on $\{-1,
1\}$, i.e. to the pair $(-1, 1)$.

Define two continuous functions on $\sigma(x)$:
$$f = \chi_{\{1\}}, \qquad g = \chi_{\{-1\}},$$
i.e. $f(1) = 1, f(-1) = 0$ and $g(1) = 0, g(-1) = 1$. These are continuous since
 $\{-1, 1\}$ is discrete.

By the continuous functional calculus, set
$$p = f(x), \qquad q = g(x).$$

**Projections:** Since $f^2 = f$ and $\bar{f} = f$ (real-valued idempotent), fun
ctional calculus gives $p^2 = p$ and $p^* = p$, so $p$ is a projection. Likewise
 $q$ is a projection.

**Non-triviality of $p$:** The Gelfand transform of $p$ is $f$, which is not the
 zero function (it takes value $1$ at the point $1 \in \sigma(x)$). Hence $p \ne
q 0$. Also $f$ is not the constant function $1$ (it takes value $0$ at $-1 \in \
sigma(x)$), so $p \neq 1$. Thus $p$ is non-trivial. The identical argument with
$g$ shows $q$ is non-trivial.

**Orthogonality:** Since $fg = 0$ (the functions have disjoint support), functio
nal calculus gives
$$pq = (fg)(x) = 0.$$

**Bonus:** Moreover $f + g = 1$ on $\sigma(x)$, so $p + q = 1$; thus $\{p, q\}$
form a pair of complementary non-trivial orthogonal projections, giving the dire
ct sum decomposition $C^*(x,1) = pA \oplus qA \cong \mathbb{C} \oplus \mathbb{C}
$, with $x = p - q$.

Therefore, two non-trivial projections $p, q$ with $pq = 0$ do exist, constructe
d explicitly via the continuous functional calculus as the spectral projections
of $x$ associated to the two points of its spectrum.
