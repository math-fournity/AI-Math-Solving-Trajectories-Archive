## Proof

We show that the **only** function $g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}$ satisfying both c
onditions is $g=0$ a.e. We interpret "trigonometric polynomial" in the general s
ense $f(x)=\sum_{k=1}^N c_k e^{i\lambda_k x}$, $\lambda_k\in\mathbb{R}$ (this in
cludes the classical $2\pi$-periodic case $\lambda_k\in\mathbb{Z}$).

Suppose $g$ satisfies (i) $\int_0^\infty |g(x)|\,dx<\infty$ and (ii) there exist
 trigonometric polynomials $f_n$ with $\|f_n-g\|\to 0$.

### Step 1: $g$ is Stepanov almost periodic

Let $\varepsilon>0$. Choose $n$ so that $\|f_n-g\|<\varepsilon$. Since $f_n$ is
a trigonometric polynomial, it is Bohr almost periodic: there exists $L>0$ such
that every interval of length $L$ contains $h$ with $\sup_{x}|f_n(x+h)-f_n(x)|<\
varepsilon$.

For such $h$, using the translation-invariance of $\|\cdot\|$ (the substitution
$u=x+h$ shows $\|g(\cdot+h)-f_n(\cdot+h)\|=\|g-f_n\|$):

$$\|g(\cdot+h)-g\|\le \|g(\cdot+h)-f_n(\cdot+h)\|+\|f_n(\cdot+h)-f_n\|+\|f_n-g\|
.$$

The first and third terms are each $<\varepsilon$. The middle term satisfies $\|
f_n(\cdot+h)-f_n\|\le\sup_x|f_n(x+h)-f_n(x)|<\varepsilon$. Hence $\|g(\cdot+h)-g
\|<3\varepsilon$, with such $h$ relatively dense. So $g$ is Stepanov almost peri
odic.

### Step 2: $g=0$ a.e.

Fix $s\in\mathbb{R}$ and $\varepsilon>0$. Let $L$ be as in Step 1. For any $N>0$
, there exists $h\in[N,\,N+L]$ with $\|g(\cdot+h)-g\|<3\varepsilon$. In particul
ar:

$$\int_s^{s+1}|g(x+h)-g(x)|\,dx<3\varepsilon.$$

By the triangle inequality:

$$\int_s^{s+1}|g(x)|\,dx\le\int_s^{s+1}|g(x+h)|\,dx+3\varepsilon=\int_{s+h}^{s+h
+1}|g(u)|\,du+3\varepsilon.$$

Since $h\in[N,N+L]$, we have $s+h\in[s+N,\,s+N+L]$, so:

$$\int_{s+h}^{s+h+1}|g(u)|\,du\le\int_{s+N}^{s+N+L+1}|g(u)|\,du.$$

For $N$ large enough, $s+N>0$, and since $g\in L^1(0,\infty)$:

$$\int_{s+N}^{s+N+L+1}|g(u)|\,du\le\int_{s+N}^{\infty}|g(u)|\,du\;\xrightarrow{N
\to\infty}\;0.$$

Taking $N\to\infty$:

$$\int_s^{s+1}|g(x)|\,dx\le 3\varepsilon.$$

Since $\varepsilon>0$ is arbitrary, $\int_s^{s+1}|g(x)|\,dx=0$ for every $s\in\m
athbb{R}$, hence $g=0$ a.e. on $\mathbb{R}$.

### Conclusion

The only function satisfying both conditions is $g=0$. No non-zero $L^1(0,\infty
)$ function lies in the $\|\cdot\|$-closure of trigonometric polynomials. $\blac
ksquare$
