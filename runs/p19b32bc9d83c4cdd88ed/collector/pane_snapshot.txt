## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, the equality of the two factorizations forces $\omega = 1$ withi
n the splitting field. In fact, no primitive 3rd root of unity exists in any fie
ld of characteristic 3.

**Setup.** Let $F = \mathbb{F}_3(t)$ and consider $f(x) = x^3 - t \in F[x]$. Sin
ce $\operatorname{char} F = 3$, the Frobenius / "freshman's dream" identity give
s
$$(x - c)^3 = x^3 - 3cx^2 + 3c^2 x - c^3 = x^3 - c^3,$$
because $3 = 0$ in $F$.

**Step 1: The first factorization.** Let $a$ be a root of $x^3 - t$ in a splitti
ng field, so $a^3 = t$. Then
$$(x - a)^3 = x^3 - a^3 = x^3 - t.$$
Thus $x^3 - t = (x-a)^3$, and $a$ is a triple root — the polynomial is *insepara
ble*.

**Step 2: The second factorization.** Suppose we also write $x^3 - t = (x - \ome
ga a)^3$ for some element $\omega$. Expanding in characteristic 3:
$$(x - \omega a)^3 = x^3 - (\omega a)^3 = x^3 - \omega^3 a^3 = x^3 - \omega^3 t.
$$
For this to equal $x^3 - t$, we need
$$\omega^3 t = t.$$
Since $t \neq 0$ in $\mathbb{F}_3(t)$, this gives $\omega^3 = 1$.

**Step 3: $\omega^3 = 1$ forces $\omega = 1$ in characteristic 3.** In character
istic 3, again by the freshman's dream identity,
$$\omega^3 - 1 = (\omega - 1)^3.$$
So $\omega^3 = 1$ becomes $(\omega - 1)^3 = 0$. Since we are working in a **fiel
d** (the splitting field is a field, hence an integral domain with no nonzero ni
lpotent elements), $(\omega - 1)^3 = 0$ implies $\omega - 1 = 0$, i.e.,
$$\omega = 1.$$

**Step 4: No primitive 3rd root of unity exists in characteristic 3.** The polyn
omial $x^3 - 1$ factors in characteristic 3 as
$$x^3 - 1 = (x - 1)^3,$$
so $1$ is its *only* root (with multiplicity 3). There is no element $\omega \ne
q 1$ satisfying $\omega^3 = 1$ in $F$ or in any extension field of $F$. The very
 notion of a "primitive 3rd root of unity" is vacuous in characteristic 3.

**Conclusion.** The two factorizations $x^3 - t = (x-a)^3 = (x - \omega a)^3$ on
ly require $\omega^3 = 1$. In characteristic 3, this equation has the unique sol
ution $\omega = 1$ (because $x^3 - 1 = (x-1)^3$ and fields have no nilpotent ele
ments). Therefore the equality of factorizations **does** imply $\omega = 1$. Th
e inseparability of $x^3 - t$ — a consequence of $\gcd(x^3 - t, \, 3x^2) = \gcd(
x^3 - t, 0) = x^3 - t \neq 1$ — is the underlying reason: there is no distinct c
onjugate $\omega a$ to distinguish from $a$, because the cube roots of unity col
lapse to a single element in characteristic 3.
