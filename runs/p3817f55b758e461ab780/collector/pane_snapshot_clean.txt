# Berge–Fulkerson Conjecture for Bridgeless Cubic Planar Graphs

**Answer: No — the conjecture is not open for bridgeless cubic planar graphs. It
 is settled (true), as a direct consequence of the Four Color Theorem.**

## Proof

**Definitions.** The Berge–Fulkerson conjecture asserts that every bridgeless cu
bic graph $G$ admits six perfect matchings $M_1,\dots,M_6$ such that every edge
of $G$ belongs to exactly two of them. Equivalently, $G$ has a 2-fold cover by p
erfect matchings, which is equivalent to the fractional chromatic index $\chi'_f
(G)=3$.

**Key equivalence (Tait, 1880).** A cubic graph is 3-edge-colorable if and only
if its edge set can be partitioned into three perfect matchings (a 1-factorizati
on). This is immediate: in a proper 3-edge-coloring of a cubic graph, each color
 class is a 1-factor (a perfect matching), since every vertex is incident to exa
ctly one edge of each color.

**Four Color Theorem ⇒ 3-edge-colorability of bridgeless cubic planar graphs.**
By the Four Color Theorem (Appel–Haken, 1976; confirmed by Robertson–Sanders–Sey
mour–Thomas, 1997), every planar graph is 4-vertex-colorable. Tait showed that 4
-vertex-colorability of a bridgeless cubic planar graph is equivalent to 3-edge-
colorability of that graph. Concretely, given a proper 4-vertex-coloring with co
lors $\{0,1,2,3\}$, one assigns to each edge $uv$ the color $\{a \in \mathbb{Z}_
2^2 : \text{color}(u)+\text{color}(v) = a\}$ — the three nonzero elements of $\m
athbb{Z}_2^2$ give three edge colors, and this is a proper 3-edge-coloring. (The
 bridgeless condition ensures no edge is a cut-edge, which is necessary for a cu
bic graph to admit a perfect matching at all, via Petersen's theorem.)

Therefore every bridgeless cubic planar graph is 3-edge-colorable, hence admits
a 1-factorization $E = M_1 \sqcup M_2 \sqcup M_3$ into three perfect matchings.

**1-factorization ⇒ Berge–Fulkerson.** Given the 1-factorization $M_1, M_2, M_3$
, take the six perfect matchings
$$M_1, M_1, M_2, M_2, M_3, M_3.$$
Every edge lies in exactly one of $M_1, M_2, M_3$, hence in exactly two of the s
ix listed matchings. This is precisely the Berge–Fulkerson configuration. $\squa
re$

**Remark on scope.** The Berge–Fulkerson conjecture remains open in general — th
e hard cases are bridgeless cubic graphs that are *not* 3-edge-colorable (snarks
), such as the Petersen graph. The planar restriction removes all such cases bec
ause the Four Color Theorem forces 3-edge-colorability. Thus the conjecture is t
rivially true on the planar subclass and the open problem lives entirely among n
on-planar (and necessarily non-3-edge-colorable) cubic graphs.
