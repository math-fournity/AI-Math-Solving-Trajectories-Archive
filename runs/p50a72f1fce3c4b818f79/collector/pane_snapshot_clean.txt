## Proof

We evaluate $I_n = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{d^n}{dx^n} e^{-x^2}\, dx$ for $
n \neq 0$ (with $n$ a positive integer, so $n \geq 1$).

**Key observation.** For any $n \geq 1$, the $n$-th derivative is itself a total
 derivative:
$$\frac{d^n}{dx^n} e^{-x^2} = \frac{d}{dx}\!\left(\frac{d^{n-1}}{dx^{n-1}} e^{-x
^2}\right).$$

By the fundamental theorem of calculus,
$$I_n = \left[\frac{d^{n-1}}{dx^{n-1}} e^{-x^2}\right]_{-\infty}^{\,+\infty}.$$

**Claim.** For every $k \geq 0$, the function $\frac{d^k}{dx^k} e^{-x^2}$ vanish
es at $x = \pm\infty$.

*Proof of claim.* By induction on $k$. The $k$-th derivative of $e^{-x^2}$ has t
he form
$$\frac{d^k}{dx^k} e^{-x^2} = P_k(x)\, e^{-x^2},$$
where $P_k(x)$ is a polynomial of degree $k$ (this is immediate by induction: di
fferentiating $P_k(x) e^{-x^2}$ gives $(P_k'(x) - 2x P_k(x)) e^{-x^2}$, and $P_k
'(x) - 2x P_k(x)$ is a polynomial of degree $k+1$). Since $e^{-x^2}$ decays fast
er than any polynomial grows — i.e., $\lim_{|x|\to\infty} |x|^m e^{-x^2} = 0$ fo
r every $m$ — we conclude
$$\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} P_k(x)\, e^{-x^2} = 0. \qquad \square$$

Applying the claim with $k = n-1 \geq 0$, both boundary terms vanish:
$$I_n = \lim_{x\to+\infty} \frac{d^{n-1}}{dx^{n-1}} e^{-x^2} \;-\; \lim_{x\to-\i
nfty} \frac{d^{n-1}}{dx^{n-1}} e^{-x^2} = 0 - 0 = 0.$$

**Conclusion.** For every positive integer $n$ (i.e., $n \neq 0$),
$$\boxed{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{d^n}{dx^n} e^{-x^2}\, dx = 0.}$$
