## Proof

Let $H = L^2([0,1])$ and let $\mathscr{K}:H\to H$ be a bounded operator with $\o
peratorname{rank}(\mathscr{K})=1$. We show $\mathscr{K}$ has at most one non-zer
o eigenvalue.

**Step 1: Structure of a rank-1 operator.** Since $\operatorname{Im}(\mathscr{K}
)$ is one-dimensional, there exists a non-zero $v\in H$ with $\operatorname{Im}(
\mathscr{K})=\operatorname{span}\{v\}$. Hence for every $x\in H$,
$$\mathscr{K}x = \varphi(x)\,v$$
for some linear functional $\varphi:H\to\mathbb{C}$. Boundedness of $\mathscr{K}
$ forces $\varphi$ to be bounded: $|\varphi(x)|\,|\leq \|\mathscr{K}\|\,\|x\|/\|
v\|$... more directly, $\varphi$ is bounded since $\|\mathscr{K}x\|=|\varphi(x)|
\,\|v\|$, so $|\varphi(x)|\le \|\mathscr{K}\|\|x\|/\|v\|$. By the Riesz represen
tation theorem, there exists $u\in H$ such that
$$\varphi(x)=\langle x,u\rangle, \qquad \mathscr{K}x = \langle x,u\rangle\, v.$$

**Step 2: Every eigenvector for a non-zero eigenvalue lies in $\operatorname{spa
n}\{v\}$.** Suppose $\lambda\neq 0$ is an eigenvalue with eigenvector $x\neq 0$:
$$\lambda x = \mathscr{K}x = \langle x,u\rangle\, v.$$
Since $\lambda\neq 0$, we obtain $x = \frac{\langle x,u\rangle}{\lambda}\,v\in\o
peratorname{span}\{v\}$.

**Step 3: At most one non-zero eigenvalue.** Suppose $\lambda_1,\lambda_2\neq 0$
 are eigenvalues with eigenvectors $x_1,x_2\neq 0$. By Step 2, $x_i = \alpha_i v
$ for some $\alpha_i\neq 0$. Then
$$\lambda_i\, \alpha_i v = \mathscr{K}(\alpha_i v)=\alpha_i\,\mathscr{K}v=\alpha
_i\,\langle v,u\rangle\, v.$$
Dividing by $\alpha_i\neq 0$ gives $\lambda_i = \langle v,u\rangle$ for $i=1,2$.
 Hence $\lambda_1=\lambda_2$.

Therefore $\mathscr{K}$ has **at most one** non-zero eigenvalue (namely $\langle
 v,u\rangle$, which occurs iff $\langle v,u\rangle\neq 0$, with eigenvector $v$)
. $\blacksquare$
