## Proof

The **Smith–Volterra–Cantor (SVC) set** $S$ is constructed on $[0,1]$ as follows
. At step $n\geq 1$, from each of the $2^{n-1}$ remaining closed intervals, remo
ve the open middle subinterval of length $\frac{1}{4^n}$.

### Step 1 — Measure of $S$

The total length removed is
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 2^{n-1}\cdot\frac{1}{4^n}
= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{n-1}}{2^{2n}}
= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}
= \frac{1}{2}.
$$
Hence
$$
\lambda(S)=1-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{2}.
$$

### Step 2 — Symmetry of $S$ about $x=\tfrac12$

We show $S$ is symmetric about $\tfrac12$, i.e. $x\in S\iff 1-x\in S$.

The construction is invariant under the reflection $x\mapsto 1-x$:
- At step 1, the removed interval $(\tfrac38,\tfrac58)$ is centered at $\tfrac12
$.
- If after step $n$ the remaining set $S_n$ is symmetric about $\tfrac12$, then
at step $n+1$ each removed interval is the middle of a component of $S_n$. Each
component $[a,b]\subseteq S_n$ has a mirror component $[1-b,1-a]\subseteq S_n$,
and removing the middle of $[a,b]$ (centered at $\frac{a+b}{2}$) produces a mirr
or removal centered at $1-\frac{a+b}{2}=\frac{(1-b)+(1-a)}{2}$, which is exactly
 the middle of $[1-b,1-a]$.

By induction every $S_n$ is symmetric about $\tfrac12$, so $S=\bigcap_n S_n$ is
as well.

### Step 3 — Computing the integral

Since $S$ is symmetric about $\tfrac12$ and $f(x)=x$ satisfies $x + (1-x)=1$, we
 use the substitution $x\mapsto 1-x$ (which preserves Lebesgue measure on $S$ by
 symmetry):
$$
\int_S x\,d\lambda = \int_S (1-x)\,d\lambda = \lambda(S) - \int_S x\,d\lambda.
$$
Therefore
$$
2\int_S x\,d\lambda = \lambda(S)=\frac12,
$$
and
$$
\boxed{\int_S x\,d\lambda = \frac14.}
$$
