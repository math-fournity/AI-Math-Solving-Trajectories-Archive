**Answer: Yes, we can conclude that $x_n \to x$.**

**Proof.**

Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that $x_n \not\to x$. Then there exists
$\varepsilon > 0$ and a subsequence $(x_{n_k})_{k=1}^{\infty}$ such that

$$|x_{n_k} - x| \geq \varepsilon \quad \text{for all } k \geq 1.$$

Define $f \colon \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ by

$$f(t) = \max\!\left(0,\; 1 - \frac{|t - x|}{\varepsilon}\right).$$

This is a "tent" function centered at $x$ with support radius $\varepsilon$. It
is continuous (as the maximum of two continuous functions), and it takes values
in $[0,1]$, so it is bounded. Moreover:

- $f(x) = \max(0,\, 1 - 0) = 1$.
- For every $k$, since $|x_{n_k} - x| \geq \varepsilon$, we have $\frac{|x_{n_k}
 - x|}{\varepsilon} \geq 1$, so $f(x_{n_k}) = \max(0,\, 1 - \frac{|x_{n_k}-x|}{\
varepsilon}) = 0$.

By hypothesis, $f(x_n) \to f(x) = 1$ as $n \to \infty$. Since convergence of the
 full sequence implies convergence of every subsequence to the same limit, we mu
st have $f(x_{n_k}) \to 1$ as $k \to \infty$.

But $f(x_{n_k}) = 0$ for every $k$, so $f(x_{n_k}) \to 0 \neq 1$. This is a cont
radiction.

Therefore our assumption was false, and we conclude

$$x_n \to x \quad \text{as } n \to \infty. \qquad \blacksquare$$
