## Solution

I need to evaluate $\displaystyle I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\cos x}{x}\,
dx$.

### Step 1: Identify the issue at the origin

The integrand $f(x) = \frac{\cos x}{x}$ has a singularity at $x = 0$. Expanding
near the origin,
$$
\frac{\cos x}{x} = \frac{1}{x} - \frac{x}{2} + \frac{x^{3}}{24} - \cdots
$$
The leading term $1/x$ is **not integrable** near $0$. Concretely, for $0 < \var
epsilon < 1$,
$$
\int_{\varepsilon}^{1} \frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx
= \int_{\varepsilon}^{1} \frac{1}{x}\,dx + \int_{\varepsilon}^{1} \frac{\cos x -
 1}{x}\,dx
= -\ln\varepsilon + \int_{\varepsilon}^{1} \frac{\cos x - 1}{x}\,dx.
$$
Since $\frac{\cos x - 1}{x} \to 0$ as $x \to 0$, the second integral has a finit
e limit as $\varepsilon \to 0^{+}$. But $-\ln\varepsilon \to +\infty$, so
$$
\int_{\varepsilon}^{1} \frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx \xrightarrow{\varepsilon \to 0^{+}}
+\infty.
$$
By symmetry (oddness), $\int_{-1}^{-\varepsilon} \frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx \to -\inft
y$.

Therefore, as an **improper Riemann integral** (or Lebesgue integral) defined by
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{0} \frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx + \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx
,
$$
each piece diverges, so **the integral does not converge**.

### Step 2: The Cauchy principal value

The natural regularized interpretation is the **Cauchy principal value**:
$$
\text{p.v.}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx
= \lim_{\substack{R\to\infty\\ \varepsilon\to 0^{+}}}
\left(\int_{-R}^{-\varepsilon} \frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx + \int_{\varepsilon}^{R} \fr
ac{\cos x}{x}\,dx\right).
$$

Since $\cos x$ is even and $1/x$ is odd, $f(x) = \frac{\cos x}{x}$ is **odd**:
$$
f(-x) = \frac{\cos(-x)}{-x} = \frac{\cos x}{-x} = -f(x).
$$

Hence for every $R > 0$ and $\varepsilon > 0$,
$$
\int_{-R}^{-\varepsilon} f(x)\,dx \;\stackrel{u=-x}{=}\; -\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}
 f(-u)\,du = -\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}(-f(u))\,du = -\int_{\varepsilon}^{R} f(u)\,
du,
$$
so the two pieces cancel exactly:
$$
\int_{-R}^{-\varepsilon} \frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx + \int_{\varepsilon}^{R} \frac{\co
s x}{x}\,dx = 0
\quad\text{for all } R,\varepsilon.
$$

Taking the limits, the principal value exists and equals
$$
\text{p.v.}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx = 0.
$$

### Conclusion

- As a standard improper integral, $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\c
os x}{x}\,dx$ **diverges** (the singularity at $x=0$ is non-integrable).
- Its **Cauchy principal value** is
$$
\boxed{\,\text{p.v.}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx = 0\,}
$$
which follows immediately from the oddness of the integrand and the symmetric re
gularization.
