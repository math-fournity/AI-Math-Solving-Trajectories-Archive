Looking at this problem, I need to find the fundamental group of a cube with 12
points removed (at face centers).

**Note on the problem statement:** A cube has 6 faces, so "12 points at the cent
er of each face" appears to contain a discrepancy. A cube has 12 edges; the prob
lem likely intends 12 points removed from the surface (perhaps at edge midpoints
). In any case, as I will show, the fundamental group depends only on the number
 of points removed, not their positions. I proceed with **12 points removed from
 the surface of the cube**.

---

## Proof

**Step 1: The surface of a cube is homeomorphic to $S^2$.**

The boundary of a cube is a closed, orientable 2-manifold. By the classification
 of compact surfaces, any such surface homeomorphic to $S^2$ is $S^2$ itself. Co
ncretely, one can construct an explicit homeomorphism by radially projecting the
 surface of the cube (centered at the origin) onto the unit sphere: each point $
x$ on the cube surface maps to $x/\|x\| \in S^2$. This is a continuous bijection
 between compact Hausdorff spaces, hence a homeomorphism.

**Step 2: Removing 12 points from $S^2$.**

Let $p_1, \dots, p_{12}$ be the 12 removed points on the cube surface. Under the
 homeomorphism from Step 1, these correspond to 12 points $q_1, \dots, q_{12}$ o
n $S^2$. The punctured cube surface is homeomorphic to $S^2 \setminus \{q_1, \do
ts, q_{12}\}$.

**Step 3: Stereographic projection reduces to the plane.**

Apply stereographic projection from $q_{12}$ (one of the removed points). This g
ives a homeomorphism:
$$S^2 \setminus \{q_{12}\} \cong \mathbb{R}^2.$$
Under this homeomorphism, the remaining 11 points $q_1, \dots, q_{11}$ map to 11
 distinct points $r_1, \dots, r_{11} \in \mathbb{R}^2$. Therefore:
$$S^2 \setminus \{q_1, \dots, q_{12}\} \cong \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{r_1, \dots
, r_{11}\}.$$

**Step 4: $\mathbb{R}^2$ minus 11 points deformation retracts to a bouquet of 11
 circles.**

Choose a large closed disk $D$ containing all 11 points $r_1, \dots, r_{11}$ in
its interior. The space $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{r_1, \dots, r_{11}\}$ deformat
ion retracts onto $D \setminus \{r_1, \dots, r_{11}\}$ (by radially contracting
everything outside $D$ onto $\partial D$).

Now, $D \setminus \{r_1, \dots, r_{11}\}$ is a disk with 11 punctures. This defo
rmation retracts onto a wedge (bouquet) of 11 circles: connect each puncture to
the boundary by a thin "cut" (a slit), then the remaining space retracts onto a
graph with 11 independent loops—one encircling each puncture. Formally, $D \setm
inus \{r_1, \dots, r_{11}\}$ is homotopy equivalent to $\bigvee_{i=1}^{11} S^1$.

**Step 5: Compute the fundamental group.**

Since the punctured cube surface is homotopy equivalent to $\bigvee_{11} S^1$, a
nd the fundamental group of a bouquet of $n$ circles is the free group on $n$ ge
nerators (by the Seifert–van Kampen theorem, applied iteratively), we conclude:

$$\pi_1\!\left(\text{cube surface} \setminus \{12 \text{ points}\}\right) \cong
F_{11},$$

the **free group on 11 generators**.

**Remark on the solid cube interpretation.** If "cube" means the solid 3-dimensi
onal cube (homeomorphic to the closed 3-ball $B^3$) and the 12 points are on the
 boundary, the answer is the same. Indeed, $B^3 \setminus \{p_1, \dots, p_{12}\}
$ (with $p_i \in \partial B^3$) deformation retracts onto $\partial B^3 \setminu
s \{p_1, \dots, p_{12}\} \cong S^2 \setminus \{12 \text{ points}\}$: first push
all interior points with $\|x\| < \frac{1}{2}$ radially outward to $\|x\| = \fra
c{1}{2}$ (this avoids the boundary points), then radially project the annular re
gion $\{\frac{1}{2} \leq \|x\| \leq 1\} \setminus \{p_i\}$ onto $S^2 \setminus \
{p_i\}$. This again yields $\pi_1 \cong F_{11}$.

---

**Conclusion:** The fundamental group of a cube with 12 points removed is the fr
ee group on 11 generators, $F_{11}$.
