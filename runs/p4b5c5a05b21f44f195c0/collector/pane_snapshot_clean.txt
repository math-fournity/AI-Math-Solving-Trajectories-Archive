$$a^2c + ab^2 + bc^2 = 3abc. \qquad (\star)$$

We prove $v_p(abc)\equiv 0\pmod 3$ for every prime $p$, which implies $abc$ is a
 cube of an integer.

Fix a prime $p$. Let $\alpha=v_p(a),\ \beta=v_p(b),\ \gamma=v_p(c)$, and set $s=
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=v_p(abc)$. Define
$$u=\alpha-\beta,\quad v=\beta-\gamma,\quad w=\gamma-\alpha,$$
so $u+v+w=0$.

The three terms on the left of $(\star)$ have $p$-adic valuations
$$v_p(a^2c)=2\alpha+\gamma=s+u,\quad v_p(ab^2)=\alpha+2\beta=s+v,\quad v_p(bc^2)
=\beta+2\gamma=s+w,$$
while the right side has valuation $v_p(3abc)=v_p(3)+s$.

**Claim:** The minimum of $\{u,v,w\}$ cannot be attained uniquely.

*Case $p\neq 3$:* Then $v_p(\text{RHS})=s$. If the minimum $m=\min(u,v,w)$ is un
ique, then by the non-Archimedean property $v_p(\text{LHS})=s+m$. Equating gives
 $m=0$. But if $m=0$ is the unique minimum, the other two values are $>0$, so $u
+v+w>0$, contradicting $u+v+w=0$.

*Case $p=3$:* Then $v_p(\text{RHS})=1+s$. A unique minimum $m$ gives $s+m=1+s$,
i.e., $m=1>0$. Then all three values $\geq 1$, so $u+v+w\geq 3>0$, again contrad
icting $u+v+w=0$.

Therefore the minimum of $\{u,v,w\}$ is attained **at least twice**. There are t

\gamma$ and $s=3\alpha\equiv 0\pmod 3$.

s=3\gamma$, and $w=u<v$ gives $s=3\alpha$.
