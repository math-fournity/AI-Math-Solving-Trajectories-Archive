# Proof

**Claim:** Yes. If $A \in GL(n,\mathbb{C})$ and $0\notin W(A):=\{x^*Ax:\|x\|=1\}
$, then $0\notin W(A^{-1})$.

---

**Step 1. Separating-hyperplane characterization.**

The numerical range $W(A)$ is compact and convex (Toeplitz–Hausdorff). The condi
tion $0\notin W(A)$ is equivalent, by the strict separating-hyperplane theorem,
to the existence of an angle $\theta\in\mathbb{R}$ and a constant $c>0$ such tha
t

$$
\operatorname{Re}\!\big(e^{i\theta}z\big)\ge c \qquad\text{for all } z\in W(A),
$$

(or the same with $\le -c$; the two cases are exchanged by replacing $\theta$ wi
th $\theta+\pi$). Since $\operatorname{Re}(e^{i\theta}x^*Ax)=x^*\,H_\theta\,x$ w
here

$$
H_\theta \;:=\; \operatorname{Re}\!\big(e^{i\theta}A\big)\;=\;\frac{e^{i\theta}A
+e^{-i\theta}A^*}{2}
$$

is Hermitian, the inequality becomes $x^*H_\theta x\ge c\|x\|^2$ for all $x$, i.
e.

$$
\operatorname{Re}\!\big(e^{i\theta}A\big)\;\succ\;0.
$$

Conversely, $\operatorname{Re}(e^{i\theta}A)\succ 0$ immediately gives $\operato
rname{Re}(e^{i\theta}x^*Ax)>0$ for all unit $x$, hence $0\notin W(A)$. Thus

$$
0\notin W(A)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists\,\theta\text{ with }\operatorn
ame{Re}\!\big(e^{i\theta}A\big)\succ 0\text{ (or }\prec 0\text{)}.
$$

**Step 2. Hermitian part of the inverse.**

Set $B:=e^{i\theta}A$, so that $\operatorname{Re}(B)\succ 0$. Then $A=e^{-i\thet
a}B$ and $A^{-1}=e^{i\theta}B^{-1}$. Compute the Hermitian part of $B^{-1}$:

$$
\operatorname{Re}\!\big(B^{-1}\big)\;=\;\frac{B^{-1}+(B^*)^{-1}}{2}\;=\;\frac{(B
^*)^{-1}\big(B^*+B\big)\,B^{-1}}{2}\;=\;(B^{-1})^{*}\,\operatorname{Re}(B)\,B^{-
1}.
$$

Since $\operatorname{Re}(B)\succ 0$ and congruence by an invertible matrix prese
rves positive definiteness,

$$
\operatorname{Re}\!\big(B^{-1}\big)\;=\;(B^{-1})^{*}\,\operatorname{Re}(B)\,B^{-
1}\;\succ\;0.
$$

**Step 3. Apply the characterization to $A^{-1}$.**

Using $A^{-1}=e^{i\theta}B^{-1}$ with $\phi:=-\theta$,

$$
\operatorname{Re}\!\big(e^{i\phi}A^{-1}\big)\;=\;\operatorname{Re}\!\big(e^{-i\t
heta}\,e^{i\theta}B^{-1}\big)\;=\;\operatorname{Re}\!\big(B^{-1}\big)\;\succ\;0.
$$

By the characterization of Step 1 (applied to $A^{-1}$), this is exactly the con
dition $0\notin W(A^{-1})$.

---

Therefore $0\notin W(A)$ implies $0\notin W(A^{-1})$. $\blacksquare$
