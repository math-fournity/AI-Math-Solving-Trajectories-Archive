## Darboux Criterion for Riemann Integrability

**Setup.** Let $f : [a, b] \to \mathbb{R}$ be a bounded function. For a partitio
n (division) $P = \{a = x_0 < x_1 < \cdots < x_n = b\}$ of $[a, b]$, define on e
ach subinterval $[x_{i-1}, x_i]$:

$$m_i = \inf_{x \in [x_{i-1}, x_i]} f(x), \qquad M_i = \sup_{x \in [x_{i-1}, x_i
]} f(x).$$

The **lower** and **upper Darboux sums** are

$$L(P, f) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i \, \Delta x_i, \qquad U(P, f) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} M_
i \, \Delta x_i, \qquad \Delta x_i = x_i - x_{i-1}.$$

The **norm** (mesh) of the partition is

$$\|P\| = \max_{1 \le i \le n} \Delta x_i.$$

**Darboux Criterion.** The function $f$ is Riemann integrable on $[a, b]$ if and
 only if for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $\delta > 0$ such that for eve
ry partition $P$ of $[a, b]$ with

$$\|P\| < \delta,$$

the gap between the upper and lower Darboux sums satisfies

$$U(P, f) - L(P, f) < \varepsilon.$$

Equivalently, writing $S(P, f) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(t_i)\,\Delta x_i$ for any choi
ce of sample points $t_i \in [x_{i-1}, x_i]$ (a Riemann sum), the criterion says
:

$$\|P\| \to 0 \implies U(P, f) - L(P, f) \to 0,$$

i.e., the upper and lower Darboux sums can be made arbitrarily close to each oth
er by taking the partition fine enough (norm of the division sufficiently small)
.

**Remark on the quantifier structure.** The criterion is sometimes stated in two
 equivalent forms:

1. **Norm-controlled form** (stated above): $\forall \varepsilon > 0,\ \exists \
delta > 0,\ \forall P$ with $\|P\| < \delta$: $\;U(P,f) - L(P,f) < \varepsilon$.

2. **Partition-existence form**: $\forall \varepsilon > 0,\ \exists P$: $\;U(P,
f) - L(P, f) < \varepsilon$.

Both are equivalent for bounded $f$. The norm-controlled form is the one that ex
plicitly involves the norm of the division, as requested in the problem. The equ
ivalence of the two forms relies on the fact that refining a partition can only
decrease $U(P, f)$ and increase $L(P, f)$, so that once a single partition makes
 the gap small, every sufficiently fine refinement (and, by a standard argument,
 every partition of small enough norm) also makes the gap small.

**Why this characterizes integrability.** The lower and upper Darboux integrals
are

$$\underline{\int_a^b} f = \sup_P L(P, f), \qquad \overline{\int_a^b} f = \inf_P
 U(P, f).$$

By construction $\underline{\int_a^b} f \le \overline{\int_a^b} f$ always. The D
arboux criterion asserts exactly that these two coincide, which is the definitio
n of Riemann integrability, with the common value being $\int_a^b f$.
