Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every compact surface in R³
 not homeomorphic to a sphere must have a point where H = 0.

**Answer: NO.** A counterexample is a "thin" torus.

**Proof.**

Consider the standard torus $T$ parametrized by
$$X(u,v) = \big((R + r\cos v)\cos u,\; (R + r\cos v)\sin u,\; r\sin v\big), \qua
d 0 \le u,v < 2\pi,$$
with $R > 2r > 0$. This is a compact, embedded surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$, homeom
orphic to a torus (genus 1), hence **not** homeomorphic to a sphere.

**Computing the principal curvatures.** The outward unit normal is
$$\mathbf{n} = (\cos u\cos v,\; \sin u\cos v,\; \sin v).$$

The first and second fundamental forms yield:
- $E = (R+r\cos v)^2$, $F=0$, $G = r^2$,
- $L = -(R+r\cos v)\cos v$, $M = 0$, $N = -r$.

Since $F = M = 0$, the principal directions are the coordinate curves, and the p
rincipal curvatures are:
$$k_1 = \frac{L}{E} = \frac{-\cos v}{R + r\cos v}, \qquad k_2 = \frac{N}{G} = \f
rac{-1}{r}.$$

**Mean curvature:**
$$H = \frac{k_1 + k_2}{2} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{-\cos v}{R + r\cos v} - \frac
{1}{r}\right) = \frac{-(R + 2r\cos v)}{2r(R + r\cos v)}.$$

**Vanishing analysis.** $H = 0$ if and only if $R + 2r\cos v = 0$, i.e., $\cos v
 = -R/(2r)$.

Since $R > 2r$, we have $R/(2r) > 1$, so $-R/(2r) < -1$, which is **outside** th
e range of $\cos v \in [-1,1]$. Therefore $H = 0$ has **no solution**.

Moreover, $H$ has constant sign: the numerator $R + 2r\cos v \ge R - 2r > 0$, an
d the denominator $2r(R + r\cos v) > 0$ (since $R > r$ ensures $R + r\cos v \ge
R - r > 0$). Hence $H < 0$ everywhere on $T$.

**Consistency check via Gauss–Bonnet.** The Gaussian curvature is
$$K = k_1 k_2 = \frac{\cos v}{r(R + r\cos v)},$$
which is positive on the outer rim ($\cos v > 0$) and negative on the inner rim
($\cos v < 0$). Then
$$\iint_T K\, dA = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{2\pi} \frac{\cos v}{r(R+r\cos v)}\cd
ot r(R+r\cos v)\, du\, dv = 2\pi\int_0^{2\pi}\cos v\, dv = 0,$$
consistent with $\chi(T) = 0$. Thus $K$ changes sign while $H$ maintains constan
t sign — the sign change of $K$ (forced by Gauss–Bonnet for genus $\ge 1$) does
**not** force $H$ to vanish.

**Conclusion.** The thin torus ($R > 2r$) is a compact surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$
, not homeomorphic to a sphere, with $H \neq 0$ at every point. Therefore, it is
 **not** always the case that a non-spherical compact surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$
admits a point of zero mean curvature.
