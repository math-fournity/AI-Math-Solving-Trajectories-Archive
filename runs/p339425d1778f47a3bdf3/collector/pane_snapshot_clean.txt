Yes, it is necessarily true. In fact, the Borel hypothesis on $Y$ is not even ne
eded for this direction.

**Claim.** For any subspace $Y \subseteq X$ (Borel or not) with the induced topo
logy,
$$\mathcal{B}_Y = \{\,B \cap Y : B \in \mathcal{B}_X\,\}.$$
In particular, $A \in \mathcal{B}_X \Rightarrow A \cap Y \in \mathcal{B}_Y$.

**Proof.** Define $\Sigma := \{B \cap Y : B \in \mathcal{B}_X\}$.

*Step 1: $\Sigma$ is a σ-algebra on $Y$.*
- $Y = X \cap Y \in \Sigma$ since $X \in \mathcal{B}_X$.
- If $B \cap Y \in \Sigma$ with $B \in \mathcal{B}_X$, then $Y \setminus (B \cap
 Y) = (X \setminus B) \cap Y \in \Sigma$ since $X \setminus B \in \mathcal{B}_X$
.
- If $B_n \cap Y \in \Sigma$ (with $B_n \in \mathcal{B}_X$), then $\bigcup_n (B_
n \cap Y) = \left(\bigcup_n B_n\right) \cap Y \in \Sigma$ since $\bigcup_n B_n \
in \mathcal{B}_X$.

*Step 2: $\mathcal{B}_Y \subseteq \Sigma$.*
Every open set in $Y$ has the form $U \cap Y$ for some open $U \subseteq X$. Sin
ce $U \in \mathcal{B}_X$, this open set lies in $\Sigma$. As $\mathcal{B}_Y$ is
the smallest σ-algebra on $Y$ containing all open sets of $Y$, and $\Sigma$ is a
 σ-algebra on $Y$ containing them, $\mathcal{B}_Y \subseteq \Sigma$.

*Step 3: $\Sigma \subseteq \mathcal{B}_Y$.*
Define $\Sigma' := \{B \in \mathcal{B}_X : B \cap Y \in \mathcal{B}_Y\}$. We che
ck $\Sigma'$ is a σ-algebra on $X$:
- $X \in \Sigma'$ since $X \cap Y = Y \in \mathcal{B}_Y$.
- If $B \in \Sigma'$, then $(X\setminus B) \cap Y = Y \setminus (B \cap Y) \in \
mathcal{B}_Y$, so $X \setminus B \in \Sigma'$.
- If $B_n \in \Sigma'$, then $\left(\bigcup_n B_n\right) \cap Y = \bigcup_n (B_n
 \cap Y) \in \mathcal{B}_Y$, so $\bigcup_n B_n \in \Sigma'$.

Moreover $\Sigma'$ contains every open set $U \subseteq X$, since $U \cap Y$ is
open in $Y$ and hence in $\mathcal{B}_Y$. Being a σ-algebra on $X$ containing al
l open sets, $\Sigma'$ contains $\mathcal{B}_X$. Thus for every $B \in \mathcal{
B}_X$, $B \cap Y \in \mathcal{B}_Y$, i.e. $\Sigma \subseteq \mathcal{B}_Y$.

Combining Steps 2 and 3, $\Sigma = \mathcal{B}_Y$. Therefore, for any $A \in \ma
thcal{B}_X$, $A \cap Y \in \mathcal{B}_Y$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the hypothesis.** The assumption $Y \in \mathcal{B}_X$ is not used i
n this argument; the identity $\mathcal{B}_Y = \{B \cap Y : B \in \mathcal{B}_X\
}$ holds for *every* subspace. The Borel hypothesis on $Y$ is only needed for th
e *reverse* embedding — that every $\mathcal{B}_Y$-set, viewed as a subset of $X
$, lies in $\mathcal{B}_X$ (which requires $Y$ itself to be Borel). Since the qu
estion asks only the forward direction, the answer is **yes**, unconditionally.
