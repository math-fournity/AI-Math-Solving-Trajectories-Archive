## Solution

The integral $\displaystyle I = \int_{-1}^{1} \frac{dx}{\cos(\pi x)}$ has simple
 poles on the path at $x = \pm\tfrac{1}{2}$ (where $\cos(\pi x)=0$), so it must
be interpreted as a **Cauchy principal value**. We evaluate it via a rectangular
 contour and the residue theorem.

### Step 1. Identify the poles and residues

Let $f(z) = \sec(\pi z) = 1/\cos(\pi z)$. The poles are at $z = \tfrac{1}{2}+k$,
 $k\in\mathbb{Z}$, all simple. Within $[-1,1]$ the relevant poles are $z=\tfrac1
2$ and $z=-\tfrac12$.

Since $\cos(\pi z)$ has derivative $-\pi\sin(\pi z)$:

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=1/2} f = \frac{1}{-\pi\sin(\pi/2)} = -\frac{1}{\pi}, \qq
uad \operatorname{Res}_{z=-1/2} f = \frac{1}{-\pi\sin(-\pi/2)} = \frac{1}{\pi}.$
$

The sum of the two residues is $\displaystyle -\frac{1}{\pi}+\frac{1}{\pi}=0$.

### Step 2. Set up the contour

Consider the positively oriented rectangle $\mathcal{R}$ with vertices $-1,\;1,\
;1+iR,\;-1+iR$, where the bottom edge from $-1$ to $1$ is indented by small semi
circles of radius $\varepsilon$ in the **upper** half-plane around $z=-\tfrac12$
 and $z=\tfrac12$. The indents exclude both real-axis poles from the interior, s
o **no poles lie inside** $\mathcal{R}$. By Cauchy's theorem:

$$\oint_{\mathcal{R}} f(z)\,dz = 0.$$

### Step 3. Evaluate each piece as $R\to\infty$, $\varepsilon\to 0$

**Top edge** ($z=x+iR$, $x:1\to -1$): For $\operatorname{Im} z = R>0$,

$$|\cos(\pi z)| = \tfrac12|e^{i\pi z}+e^{-i\pi z}| \geq \tfrac12(e^{\pi R}-e^{-\
pi R}),$$

so $|f(z)|\leq 2/(e^{\pi R}-e^{-\pi R})\to 0$. The top integral $\to 0$.

**Right side** ($z=1+iy$, $y:0\to R$): $\cos(\pi(1+iy))=-\cosh(\pi y)$, so

$$\int_0^R \frac{i\,dy}{-\cosh(\pi y)} \;\xrightarrow{R\to\infty}\; -i\int_0^\in
fty \operatorname{sech}(\pi y)\,dy = -i\cdot\frac{1}{2},$$

using $\displaystyle\int_0^\infty \operatorname{sech}(\pi y)\,dy = \frac{1}{2}$
(verified by $t=e^{\pi y}$: $\frac{2}{\pi}\int_1^\infty \frac{dt}{t^2+1}=\frac{2
}{\pi}\cdot\frac{\pi}{4}=\frac12$).

**Left side** ($z=-1+iy$, $y:R\to 0$): $\cos(\pi(-1+iy))=-\cosh(\pi y)$ as well,
 so

$$\int_R^0 \frac{i\,dy}{-\cosh(\pi y)} \;\xrightarrow{R\to\infty}\; +i\cdot\frac
{1}{2}.$$

**Side contributions cancel**: $-i/2 + i/2 = 0$.

**Bottom edge with indents**: The straight segments give the principal value $\o
peratorname{PV}\int_{-1}^1 \sec(\pi x)\,dx$. Each clockwise semicircular indent
above a simple pole contributes $-i\pi\operatorname{Res}$:

$$\text{indent contribution} = -i\pi\!\left(\operatorname{Res}_{z=-1/2}+\operato
rname{Res}_{z=1/2}\right) = -i\pi\cdot 0 = 0.$$

### Step 4. Assemble

$$0 = \oint_{\mathcal{R}} f\,dz = \operatorname{PV}\!\int_{-1}^{1}\sec(\pi x)\,d
x \;+\; \underbrace{0}_{\text{indents}} \;+\; \underbrace{0}_{\text{sides}} \;+\

Therefore

$$\boxed{\operatorname{PV}\!\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dx}{\cos(\pi x)} = 0.}$$
