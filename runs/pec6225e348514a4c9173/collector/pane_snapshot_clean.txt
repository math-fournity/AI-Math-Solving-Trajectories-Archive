## Proof

Let $F$ be a free group of rank $n > 1$, and let $F' = [F, F]$ denote its commut
ator subgroup. We prove that the abelianization $F/F'$ is a free abelian group o
f rank $n$, hence $\operatorname{rank}(F) = \operatorname{rank}(F/F') = n$.

---

**Step 1: Setup and the map to $\mathbb{Z}^n$.**

Let $\{x_1, \dots, x_n\}$ be a free basis of $F$. Let $\mathbb{Z}^n$ denote the
free abelian group with standard basis $\{e_1, \dots, e_n\}$. By the universal p
roperty of the free group, there exists a unique group homomorphism
$$\varphi: F \to \mathbb{Z}^n, \qquad \varphi(x_i) = e_i.$$
Since $\mathbb{Z}^n$ is abelian, every commutator $[a, b] = aba^{-1}b^{-1}$ lies
 in $\ker(\varphi)$, so $F' \subseteq \ker(\varphi)$. Therefore $\varphi$ descen
ds to a well-defined homomorphism
$$\bar{\varphi}: F/F' \to \mathbb{Z}^n, \qquad \bar{\varphi}(xF') = \varphi(x).$
$

**Step 2: The inverse map.**

Since $\mathbb{Z}^n$ is free abelian on $\{e_1, \dots, e_n\}$, there is a unique
 homomorphism
$$\psi: \mathbb{Z}^n \to F/F', \qquad \psi(e_i) = x_i F'.$$

**Step 3: The two maps are inverses.**

For each generator $e_i$:
$$\bar{\varphi}(\psi(e_i)) = \bar{\varphi}(x_i F') = \varphi(x_i) = e_i.$$
Since $\{e_i\}$ generates $\mathbb{Z}^n$, we have $\bar{\varphi} \circ \psi = \m
athrm{id}_{\mathbb{Z}^n}$.

For each generator $x_i F'$ of $F/F'$:
$$\psi(\bar{\varphi}(x_i F')) = \psi(e_i) = x_i F'.$$
Since $\{x_i F'\}$ generates $F/F'$ (being the image of the basis $\{x_i\}$ unde
r the quotient map), we have $\psi \circ \bar{\varphi} = \mathrm{id}_{F/F'}$.

Therefore $\bar{\varphi}$ and $\psi$ are mutual inverses, giving an isomorphism
$$F/F' \cong \mathbb{Z}^n.$$

**Step 4: Conclusion on ranks.**

The group $F/F'$ is thus a free abelian group on the basis $\{x_1 F', \dots, x_n
 F'\}$, so $\operatorname{rank}(F/F') = n$.

The rank of a free abelian group is well-defined: if $\mathbb{Z}^m \cong \mathbb
{Z}^k$, then tensoring with $\mathbb{Q}$ gives $\mathbb{Q}^m \cong \mathbb{Q}^k$
 as $\mathbb{Q}$-vector spaces, so $m = k$ by linear algebra. Likewise, the rank
 of a free group is well-defined (this is a standard result, e.g., by abelianizi
ng: two bases of sizes $m$ and $k$ would yield $\mathbb{Z}^m \cong \mathbb{Z}^k$
, forcing $m = k$).

Since $F$ has rank $n$ by hypothesis and $F/F' \cong \mathbb{Z}^n$ has rank $n$,
 we conclude:
$$\operatorname{rank}(F) = \operatorname{rank}(F/F') = n. \qquad \blacksquare$$
