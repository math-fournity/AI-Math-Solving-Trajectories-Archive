# Problem

In a remote desert, there is a large, triangular plot of land designated as a "Solar Base," forming an equilateral triangle $ABC$ with side lengths of 4 kilometers. A single sensor, $P$, is dropped from a plane and lands at a completely random, uniform position anywhere within the interior of the triangular plot.

An engineering team needs to deploy a square solar panel array with a side length of exactly 1 kilometer. The array is designed such that one of its four corners must be anchored exactly at the sensor $P$. For the array to be operational, the team must be able to rotate the square around the anchor point $P$ into at least one orientation where the entire 1x1 kilometer square lies within the boundaries of the triangular Base $ABC$.

What is the probability that the sensor $P$ lands in a location that allows the square solar array to be rotated so it fits entirely within the boundaries of the Solar Base?
