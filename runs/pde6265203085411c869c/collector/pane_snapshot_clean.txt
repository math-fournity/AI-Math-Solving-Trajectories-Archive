# Proof

Let $n$ be fixed, and let
$$S = \{A \in \operatorname{Sym}_n(\mathbb{R}) : \text{every eigenvalue } \lambd
a \text{ of } A \text{ satisfies } |\lambda| \le 2\}.$$

The space $\operatorname{Sym}_n(\mathbb{R})$ of real symmetric $n\times n$ matri
ces is a finite-dimensional real vector space of dimension $\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$, h
ence canonically identified with $\mathbb{R}^{n(n+1)/2}$. By the Heine–Borel the
orem, a subset of this space is compact if and only if it is closed and bounded.
 I show both.

**Boundedness.** For $A \in S$, let $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n$ be its eigenvalu
es (real, since $A$ is symmetric). The spectral norm satisfies
$$\|A\|_2 = \max_i |\lambda_i| \le 2.$$
The Frobenius norm satisfies
$$\|A\|_F = \Big(\sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i^2\Big)^{1/2} \le \sqrt{4n} = 2\sqrt{n}.$
$
Since every entry is bounded by the Frobenius norm, $|a_{ij}| \le \|A\|_F \le 2\
sqrt n$ for all $i,j$. Thus $S$ is contained in a bounded box in $\mathbb{R}^{n(
n+1)/2}$, so $S$ is bounded.

**Closedness.** The set $S$ can be written as
$$S = \{A \in \operatorname{Sym}_n(\mathbb{R}) : \|A\|_2 \le 2\} = \{A \in \oper
atorname{Sym}_n(\mathbb{R}) : \rho(A) \le 2\},$$
where $\rho(A)=\max_i|\lambda_i(A)|$ is the spectral radius. For symmetric matri
ces, the eigenvalues depend continuously on the entries (this follows, e.g., fro
m the variational characterization $\lambda_i(A) = \min_{\dim V=i}\max_{x\in V,\
|x\|=1}\langle Ax,x\rangle$, or from Weyl's inequality / Hoffman–Wielandt). Henc
e the map $A\mapsto \|A\|_2=\rho(A)$ is continuous on $\operatorname{Sym}_n(\mat
hbb{R})$. Therefore
$$S = \rho^{-1}\big([0,2]\big)$$
is the preimage of the closed interval $[0,2]$ under a continuous function, and
so $S$ is closed.

**Conclusion.** $S$ is a closed and bounded subset of the finite-dimensional spa
ce $\operatorname{Sym}_n(\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}^{n(n+1)/2}$. By the Heine–Bo
rel theorem, $S$ is **compact**. $\blacksquare$
