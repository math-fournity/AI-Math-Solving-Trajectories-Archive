## Proof

We are given four distinct primes $p_1, p_2, p_3, p_4$ satisfying

$$2p_1 + 3p_2 + 5p_3 + 7p_4 = 162 \tag{1}$$
$$11p_1 + 7p_2 + 5p_3 + 4p_4 = 162 \tag{2}$$

**Step 1: Eliminate $p_3$.**

Subtracting (1) from (2):

$$(11-2)p_1 + (7-3)p_2 + (5-5)p_3 + (4-7)p_4 = 0$$
$$9p_1 + 4p_2 - 3p_4 = 0$$
$$3p_4 = 9p_1 + 4p_2 \tag{3}$$

**Step 2: Determine $p_2$.**

From (3), $p_4 = 3p_1 + \tfrac{4p_2}{3}$. Since $p_4$ is an integer, $3 \mid 4p_
2$. As $\gcd(3,4)=1$, we need $3 \mid p_2$. Since $p_2$ is prime, $p_2 = 3$.

Then $p_4 = 3p_1 + 4$.

**Step 3: Determine $p_1$.**

Substitute $p_2 = 3$ and $p_4 = 3p_1 + 4$ into (1):

$$2p_1 + 9 + 5p_3 + 7(3p_1+4) = 162$$
$$2p_1 + 9 + 5p_3 + 21p_1 + 28 = 162$$
$$23p_1 + 5p_3 = 125$$
$$p_3 = 25 - \tfrac{23p_1}{5}$$

For $p_3$ to be an integer, $5 \mid 23p_1$. As $\gcd(5,23)=1$, we need $5 \mid p
_1$. Since $p_1$ is prime, $p_1 = 5$.

**Step 4: Compute the remaining values.**

$$p_3 = 25 - \frac{23 \cdot 5}{5} = 25 - 23 = 2$$
$$p_4 = 3 \cdot 5 + 4 = 19$$

**Step 5: Verify.**

The four primes are $p_1 = 5,\ p_2 = 3,\ p_3 = 2,\ p_4 = 19$ — all distinct prim
es.

- Equation (1): $2(5) + 3(3) + 5(2) + 7(19) = 10 + 9 + 10 + 133 = 162$. ✓
- Equation (2): $11(5) + 7(3) + 5(2) + 4(19) = 55 + 21 + 10 + 76 = 162$. ✓

**Step 6: Compute the product.**

$$p_1 p_2 p_3 p_4 = 5 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 19 = \boxed{570}$$

The solution is unique, since each of $p_1$ and $p_2$ was forced to a single val
ue by the divisibility constraints.
