## Proof

**Step 1: Identify the branch points and effective cut.**

The function $f(z) = (z^2-1)^{1/2} = (z-1)^{1/2}(z+1)^{1/2}$ has branch points a
t $z = \pm 1$. The prescribed branch cut $[-2,2]$ passes through both branch poi
nts, so it is a valid cut rendering $f$ single-valued on $\mathbb{C}\setminus[-2
,2]$.

We choose the branch with $(z^2-1)^{1/2} \sim z$ as $z\to+\infty$ (i.e., $f(z)>0
$ for large positive real $z$).

**Step 2: The effective discontinuity is only on $[-1,1]$.**

For $x\in(1,2)$, approaching from above ($z=x+i0^+$): both $z-1$ and $z+1$ have
argument $0$, so $\arg(z^2-1)=0$ and $f=\sqrt{x^2-1}$. Approaching from below gi
ves the same value. Thus $f$ is **continuous** across $[1,2]$ (and similarly acr
oss $[-2,-1]$). The actual jump occurs only on $(-1,1)$, where $z^2-1<0$.

This means the integral is well-defined for all $R>1$ (even $1<R<2$, since the c
ontour crosses the real axis at $z=\pm R$ where $f$ is continuous), and by Cauch
y's theorem its value is independent of $R>1$.

**Step 3: Compute the boundary values on the cut $[-1,1]$.**

For $z = x+i0^+$ with $x\in(-1,1)$:
- $z-1$: negative real, $\arg = \pi$; $z+1$: positive real, $\arg = 0$.
- $\arg(z^2-1) = \pi$, so $f_{\text{upper}}(x) = \sqrt{1-x^2}\,e^{i\pi/2} = i\sq
rt{1-x^2}$.

For $z = x-i0^+$ with $x\in(-1,1)$:
- $z-1$: $\arg = -\pi$; $z+1$: $\arg = 0$.
- $\arg(z^2-1) = -\pi$, so $f_{\text{lower}}(x) = \sqrt{1-x^2}\,e^{-i\pi/2} = -i
\sqrt{1-x^2}$.

**Step 4: Collapse the contour onto the cut.**

The counterclockwise contour $|z|=R$ deforms to hug the cut $[-1,1]$: the upper
side is traversed from $x=1$ to $x=-1$ (right to left), and the lower side from
$x=-1$ to $x=1$ (left to right). Thus:

$$\oint_{|z|=R} f(z)\,dz = \int_{1}^{-1} f_{\text{upper}}(x)\,dx + \int_{-1}^{1}
 f_{\text{lower}}(x)\,dx$$

$$= \int_{1}^{-1} i\sqrt{1-x^2}\,dx + \int_{-1}^{1}(-i\sqrt{1-x^2})\,dx$$

$$= -i\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{1-x^2}\,dx - i\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{1-x^2}\,dx = -2i\int_{
-1}^{1}\sqrt{1-x^2}\,dx.$$

**Step 5: Evaluate.**

The integral $\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{1-x^2}\,dx$ is the area of a semicircle of radi
us 1, equal to $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

Therefore:

$$\oint_{|z|=R} (z^2-1)^{1/2}\,dz = -2i\cdot\frac{\pi}{2} = \boxed{-\pi i}.$$

**Verification via residue at infinity.** For $|z|$ large, with the chosen branc
h:

$$(z^2-1)^{1/2} = z\left(1-\frac{1}{z^2}\right)^{1/2} = z - \frac{1}{2z} - \frac
{1}{8z^3}-\cdots$$

The coefficient of $1/z$ is $-1/2$, so $\operatorname{Res}_{z=\infty} f = -(-1/2
) = 1/2$. Since $f$ is analytic outside the contour:

$$\oint_{|z|=R} f\,dz = -2\pi i\cdot\operatorname{Res}_{z=\infty} f = -2\pi i\cd
ot\frac{1}{2} = -\pi i. \quad\checkmark$$

Both methods agree.
