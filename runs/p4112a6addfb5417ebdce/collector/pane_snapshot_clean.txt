## Proof

**Setup and self-inversive structure.** Let $P(z)=a\prod_{k=1}^5(z-r_k)$ with $a
\in\mathbb{R}$ and all $|r_k|=1$. Since the coefficients are real, non-real root
s occur in conjugate pairs. Define $c=\prod_{k=1}^5 r_k$. Since the root set equ
als its conjugate set (conjugate pairs, plus real roots $\pm1$), the self-invers
ive identity gives
$$z^5P(1/z)=-c\,P(z).$$
Comparing the constant and leading coefficients: $a_0=-ca_5$ and $a_5=-ca_0$, i.
e. $a=-c\cdot a_0=-2c$ and $ac=-2$, hence $c^2=1$, so $c=\pm1$ and $a=-2c$.

**Real roots must all be $-1$.** Since $P(1)=3\neq 0$, the root $1$ cannot occur
. Real roots on the unit circle are $\pm1$, so every real root is $-1$. Let $q$
be the multiplicity of $-1$ (so $q$ is odd: $1,3,$ or $5$), and let the remainin
g $m=(5-q)/2$ conjugate pairs be $e^{\pm i\alpha_j}$, with $x_j=\cos\alpha_j\in[
-1,1]$. Then $c=(-1)^q=-1$ (since $q$ odd), and
$$a=-2c=2.$$

**Evaluating at $1$ and $2$.** Using $(1-e^{i\alpha})(1-e^{-i\alpha})=2(1-\cos\a
lpha)$ and $(2-e^{i\alpha})(2-e^{-i\alpha})=5-4\cos\alpha$:
$$P(1)=2\cdot 2^q\cdot\prod_{j=1}^m 2(1-x_j)=2^{q+m+1}\prod_{j=1}^m(1-x_j)=3,$$
$$P(2)=2\cdot 3^q\cdot\prod_{j=1}^m(5-4x_j).$$

**Case $q=5,m=0$:** $P(1)=2\cdot 2^5=64\neq 3$. Impossible.

**Case $q=3,m=1$:** $P(1)=2\cdot 8\cdot 2(1-x)=32(1-x)=3$, so $x=29/32$. Then $P
(2)=2\cdot 27\cdot(5-4\cdot\tfrac{29}{32})=54\cdot\tfrac{11}{8}=\tfrac{297}{4}=7
4.25$.

**Case $q=1,m=2$:** Set $u_j=1-x_j\in[0,2]$. Then
$$P(1)=16\,u_1u_2=3\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u_1u_2=\tfrac{3}{16},$$
$$P(2)=6(1+4u_1)(1+4u_2)=6\bigl(1+4(u_1+u_2)+16u_1u_2\bigr)=24\bigl(1+u_1+u_2\bi
gr).$$
To minimize $P(2)$ we minimize $u_1+u_2$ subject to $u_1u_2=3/16$ and $u_1,u_2\i
n[0,2]$. By AM–GM,
$$u_1+u_2\ge 2\sqrt{u_1u_2}=2\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{16}}=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2},$$
with equality at $u_1=u_2=\sqrt3/4\approx 0.433\in[0,2]$, which is feasible. Thi
s gives
$$P(2)_{\min}=24\Bigl(1+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\Bigr)=24+12\sqrt3\approx 44.78.$$

Since $a=2>0$ and $5-4x_j\ge 1>0$, we have $P(2)>0$ in every admissible configur
ation, so no smaller (negative) value is possible. Comparing the cases, $24+12\s
qrt3<74.25$.

**Verification of attainability.** Take $q=1$, $u_1=u_2=\sqrt3/4$, i.e. $x_1=x_2
=1-\sqrt3/4\in[-1,1]$. The polynomial
$$P(z)=2(z+1)\bigl(z^2-2(1-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{4})z+1\bigr)^2$$
has real coefficients, degree $5$, all roots on the unit circle (one root $-1$,
and two conjugate pairs $e^{\pm i\alpha}$ with $\cos\alpha=1-\sqrt3/4\in[-1,1]$)
, $P(0)=2\cdot1\cdot1=2$, $P(1)=16\cdot(3/16)=3$, and $P(2)=24+12\sqrt3$.

The smallest possible value of $P(2)$ is $\boxed{24+12\sqrt3}$.
