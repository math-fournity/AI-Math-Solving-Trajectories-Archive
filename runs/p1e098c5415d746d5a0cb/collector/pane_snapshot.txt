## Proof

**Claim.** Yes, there exist two different multiplicative systems $S_1 \neq S_2$
in $\mathbb{Z}$ with $\mathbb{Z}_{S_1} = \mathbb{Z}_{S_2}$.

**Construction.** Define
$$S_1 = \{2^n : n \geq 0\} = \{1, 2, 4, 8, 16, \dots\}, \qquad S_2 = \{4^n : n \
geq 0\} = \{1, 4, 16, 64, \dots\}.$$

Both are multiplicatively closed subsets of $\mathbb{Z}$ containing $1$ and not
containing $0$, so both are valid multiplicative systems. They are **distinct**:
 $2 \in S_1$ but $2 \notin S_2$.

**Equality of localizations.** We show $\mathbb{Z}_{S_1} = \mathbb{Z}_{S_2}$ (as
 subrings of $\mathbb{Q}$).

($\supseteq$) Every element of $\mathbb{Z}_{S_2}$ has the form $\frac{a}{4^n} =
\frac{a}{2^{2n}}$ with $a \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $2^{2n} \in S_1$, so $\mathbb{Z}_{
S_2} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_{S_1}$.

($\subseteq$) It suffices to show $\frac{1}{2} \in \mathbb{Z}_{S_2}$, since then
 $\frac{a}{2^n} = a \cdot \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n \in \mathbb{Z}_{S_2}$ for a
ll $a, n$. Indeed:
$$\frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{4} \in \mathbb{Z}_{S_2}.$$
Hence $\mathbb{Z}_{S_1} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_{S_2}$.

Therefore $\mathbb{Z}_{S_1} = \mathbb{Z}_{S_2}$ while $S_1 \neq S_2$. $\blacksqu
are$

**Remark.** The underlying reason is that localization depends only on the *satu
ration* $\overline{S} = \{d \in \mathbb{Z} : d \mid s \text{ for some } s \in S\
}$. Here both $S_1$ and $S_2$ have the same saturation $\overline{S_1} = \overli
ne{S_2} = \{\pm 2^n : n \geq 0\}$ (up to sign conventions), yet the systems them
selves differ.
