## Proof

**Goal.** Given $G/Z(G) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$, show $G' \cong
\mathbb{Z}_2$.

---

**Step 1. $G' \le Z(G)$.**

Since $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ is abelian, $G/Z(G)$ is abelian. By the
 standard characterization, $G/Z(G)$ is abelian if and only if $G' \le Z(G)$. He
nce $G' \le Z(G)$, and in particular $G'$ is abelian.

**Step 2. $G$ is non-abelian, so $G' \ne \{e\}$.**

If $G$ were abelian then $Z(G)=G$ and $G/Z(G)$ would be trivial, contradicting $
G/Z(G)\cong\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2$ (which has order $4$). Thus $G$ is no
n-abelian and $G'\ne\{e\}$.

**Step 3. The commutator map factors to an alternating bilinear form.**

Since $G'\le Z(G)$, every commutator is central. The commutator identities then
simplify to
$$[xy,z]=[x,z]\,[y,z], \qquad [x,yz]=[x,y]\,[x,z],$$
because the conjugation factors (e.g. $[x,z]^y$) act trivially on central elemen
ts. Replacing $g$ by $gz$ with $z\in Z(G)$ does not change $[g,h]$, so the commu
tator descends to a well-defined map
$$\beta:\; G/Z(G)\times G/Z(G)\;\longrightarrow\; G', \qquad \beta(\bar g,\bar h
)=[g,h].$$
The identities above show $\beta$ is **bilinear**; and $\beta(\bar g,\bar g)=[g,
g]=e$ shows it is **alternating**.

**Step 4. $G'$ is an elementary abelian $2$-group.**

Set $V:=G/Z(G)\cong\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2$, a $2$-dimensional vector spa
ce over $\mathbb{F}_2$. Every $\bar g\in V$ satisfies $2\bar g=0$, so by bilinea
rity
$$2\,\beta(\bar g,\bar h)=\beta(2\bar g,\bar h)=\beta(0,\bar h)=e.$$
Thus every element in the image of $\beta$ has order dividing $2$. Since $G'$ is
 generated by commutators, i.e. by $\operatorname{im}\beta$, the group $G'$ is a
n elementary abelian $2$-group: $G'\cong(\mathbb{Z}_2)^k$ for some $k\ge 0$.

**Step 5. The form is determined by one value, forcing $k=1$.**

Let $\{e_1,e_2\}$ be a basis of $V\cong\mathbb{F}_2^2$. By alternating bilineari
ty,
$$\beta(ae_1+be_2,\,ce_1+de_2)=(ad-bc)\,\beta(e_1,e_2).$$
Over $\mathbb{F}_2$ this is $(ad+bc)\,\beta(e_1,e_2)$, so the entire image of $\
beta$ is
$$\operatorname{im}\beta=\{e,\;\beta(e_1,e_2)\},$$
a subgroup of order $1$ or $2$. Since $G'=\langle\operatorname{im}\beta\rangle$
and $G'\ne\{e\}$ (Step 2), we must have $\beta(e_1,e_2)\ne e$, giving
$$G'=\{e,\,\beta(e_1,e_2)\}\cong\mathbb{Z}_2.$$

---

**Conclusion.** The derived subgroup is cyclic of order $2$:

$$\boxed{G'\cong\mathbb{Z}_2}.$$

This is consistent with the familiar examples $D_4$ and $Q_8$, both of which sat
isfy $G/Z(G)\cong V_4$ and have $G'=\{e,r^2\}\cong\mathbb{Z}_2$ (resp. $\{1,-1\}
\cong\mathbb{Z}_2$).
