## WKB Solutions of Stokes' Equation

**Problem.** Find two independent WKB solutions of
$$\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} + \lambda\, x\, y = 0, \qquad \lambda \in \mathbb{R},\ \
lambda > 0.$$

---

### Setup

The equation has a single (simple) turning point at $x = 0$: for $x > 0$ the coe
fficient $\lambda x$ is positive (oscillatory regime), for $x < 0$ it is negativ
e (exponential regime). We treat $\lambda$ as the large parameter and write the
equation in the standard WKB form
$$y'' + \lambda\, q(x)\, y = 0, \qquad q(x) = x.$$

The classical WKB ansatz for a linear second-order equation with a large paramet
er $\lambda$ is
$$y(x) \sim q(x)^{-1/4}\exp\!\Bigl(\pm\, i\sqrt{\lambda}\int^{x}\sqrt{q(t)}\,dt\
Bigr),$$
valid away from turning points (i.e. where $q(x)\neq 0$). The prefactor $q^{-1/4
}$ removes the first-order correction, leaving a residual of relative order $\la
mbda^{-1}$.

---

### Region I: $x > 0$ (oscillatory)

Here $q(x) = x > 0$, so $\sqrt{q} = \sqrt{x}$ and
$$\int \sqrt{x}\,dx = \tfrac{2}{3}\,x^{3/2}.$$
The two independent WKB solutions are therefore
$$\boxed{\;y_{\pm}(x) = \frac{1}{x^{1/4}}\exp\!\Bigl(\pm\, i\,\frac{2\sqrt{\lamb
da}}{3}\,x^{3/2}\Bigr),\qquad x > 0.\;}$$

Equivalently, in real form,
$$y_{1}(x) = \frac{1}{x^{1/4}}\cos\!\Bigl(\tfrac{2\sqrt{\lambda}}{3}x^{3/2}\Bigr
),\qquad y_{2}(x) = \frac{1}{x^{1/4}}\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{2\sqrt{\lambda}}{3}x^{3/
2}\Bigr).$$

---

### Region II: $x < 0$ (exponential / evanescent)

Write $x = -|x|$, so $q(x) = -|x| < 0$. Taking $\sqrt{q} = i\sqrt{|x|} = i\sqrt{
-x}$ (principal branch), the phase integral becomes
$$\int \sqrt{q}\,dx = i\int\sqrt{-x}\,dx = i\cdot\Bigl(-\tfrac{2}{3}(-x)^{3/2}\B
igr) = -\,\tfrac{2i}{3}(-x)^{3/2},$$
and $q^{-1/4} = (-x)^{-1/4}$. Substituting into the ansatz gives the two indepen
dent real exponential WKB solutions
$$\boxed{\;y_{\pm}(x) = \frac{1}{(-x)^{1/4}}\exp\!\Bigl(\pm\, \frac{2\sqrt{\lamb
da}}{3}\,(-x)^{3/2}\Bigr),\qquad x < 0.\;}$$

One grows and one decays as $x\to -\infty$; they are clearly linearly independen
t (their Wronskian is non-zero).

---

### Verification (residual check)

Take $y = x^{-1/4}e^{i\phi}$ with $\phi = \frac{2\sqrt{\lambda}}{3}x^{3/2}$, so
$\phi' = \sqrt{\lambda}\,x^{1/2}$ and $\phi'' = \frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{2}x^{-1/2}
$. Differentiating twice,
$$y'' = e^{i\phi}\Bigl[\underbrace{-(\phi')^{2}\,x^{-1/4}}_{-\lambda x^{3/4}} \;
+\; i\phi''\,x^{-1/4} \;-\; \tfrac{i}{2}\phi'\,x^{-5/4} \;+\; \tfrac{5}{16}x^{-9
/4}\Bigr].$$
The two $O(\sqrt{\lambda})$ imaginary terms cancel exactly:
$$i\phi''\,x^{-1/4} = \tfrac{i\sqrt{\lambda}}{2}x^{-3/4},\qquad -\tfrac{i}{2}\ph
i'\,x^{-5/4} = -\tfrac{i\sqrt{\lambda}}{2}x^{-3/4}.$$
Hence
$$y'' + \lambda x\,y = e^{i\phi}\,\frac{5}{16}\,x^{-9/4}.$$
The residual is $O(\lambda^{0}\,x^{-9/4})$, while the leading terms are $O(\lamb
da\,x^{3/4})$; the relative error is $O\!\bigl((\lambda x^{3/2})^{-1}\bigr)$, wh
ich is precisely the expected first-order WKB remainder. The identical calculati
on holds for $y_{-}$ and, by analytic continuation ($x \mapsto -x$, $\sqrt{x}\ma
psto i\sqrt{-x}$), for the two solutions in Region II. This confirms that the fo
ur expressions above are genuine WKB solutions to leading order in $\lambda$.

---

### Summary

The two independent WKB solutions of $y'' + \lambda x\,y = 0$ ($\lambda > 0$) ar
e, on each side of the turning point $x = 0$:

| Region | Solutions |
|---|---|
| $x > 0$ | $\displaystyle y_{\pm} = x^{-1/4}\exp\!\bigl(\pm\, i\,\tfrac{2\sqrt{
\lambda}}{3}\,x^{3/2}\bigr)$ |
| $x < 0$ | $\displaystyle y_{\pm} = (-x)^{-1/4}\exp\!\bigl(\pm\, \tfrac{2\sqrt{
\lambda}}{3}\,(-x)^{3/2}\bigr)$ |

These are the standard Airy-type WKB approximations (the exact solutions are $\m
athrm{Ai}(-\lambda^{1/3}x)$ and $\mathrm{Bi}(-\lambda^{1/3}x)$, whose large-$|x|
$ asymptotics reproduce the formulas above).
