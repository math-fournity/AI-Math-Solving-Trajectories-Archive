**Proof.** Define $a_n = -\log b_n$. Since $(b_n)$ is strictly decreasing and po
sitive with $b_n \to 0$, the sequence $(a_n)$ is strictly increasing, $a_n \to +

$$
\frac{b_n}{b_{n+1}} = e^{\,a_{n+1}-a_n} \to 1 \iff a_{n+1}-a_n \to 0.
$$

For $i \le j$ we have $b_i/b_j = e^{\,a_j - a_i}$, so it suffices to show that

$$
D := \{a_j - a_i : i \le j,\ i,j\in\mathbb N\}
$$

at $a_{n+1}-a_n < \varepsilon$ for all $n \ge N$. Consider the tail sequence

$$
s_j := a_j - a_N, \qquad j = N, N+1, N+2, \dots
$$

It starts at $s_N = 0$, is strictly increasing, satisfies $s_{j+1}-s_j = a_{j+1}
-a_j < \varepsilon$ for all $j \ge N$, and $s_j \to +\infty$ (because $a_j\to\in

$$
s_{j^\*} \le t < s_{j^\*+1},

$$
0 \le t - s_{j^\*} < s_{j^\*+1} - s_{j^\*} < \varepsilon.
$$

Thus $|s_{j^\*} - t| < \varepsilon$, i.e. $|a_{j^\*} - a_N - t| < \varepsilon$ w

_i \in D$ within $\varepsilon$ of $t$.

$$
