Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum total sensors $S$ such that
there are at least 3 distinct ways to arrange them in the staggered grid.

## Setting Up the Formula

The pattern begins and ends with a long row ($n$ sensors), alternating with shor
t rows ($n-1$ sensors). Since it must begin AND end with a long row, the number
of rows $r$ must be **odd** (and $\geq 3$ since there's more than one row).

For odd $r$: there are $\frac{r+1}{2}$ long rows and $\frac{r-1}{2}$ short rows,
 giving:

$$S = n\cdot\frac{r+1}{2} + (n-1)\cdot\frac{r-1}{2} = \frac{r(2n-1)+1}{2}$$

Setting $m = 2n-1$ (odd, $\geq 3$), this becomes:

$$2S - 1 = r \cdot m$$

where $r$ is odd ($\geq 3$) and $m$ is odd ($\geq 3$).

## Counting Arrangements

Let $N = 2S - 1$. Each **ordered** factorization $N = a \cdot b$ with $a, b$ bot
h odd and $\geq 3$ gives a unique valid arrangement: $r = a$ rows, $n = (b+1)/2$
. Different ordered factorizations give genuinely different $(r, n)$ pairs.

The number of ordered factorizations of $N$ into two positive factors equals $d(
N)$ (the number of divisors). We exclude the two trivial factorizations $(1, N)$
 and $(N, 1)$, so the count of valid arrangements is:

$$d(N) - 2$$

We need $d(N) - 2 \geq 3$, i.e., $d(N) \geq 5$.

## Finding the Minimum

Since $N = 2S-1$ is always odd, I need the **smallest odd** $N$ with $d(N) \geq
5$.

Checking small odd numbers:
- $N = p^2$ (e.g., 9, 25): $d = 3$. ✗
- $N = p^3$ (e.g., 27): $d = 4$. ✗
- $N = pq$ (e.g., 15, 21, 33, 35, 39): $d = 4$. ✗
- $N = p^4 = 3^4 = 81$: $d = 5$. ✓ but large.
- $N = p^2 q = 3^2 \times 5 = 45$: $d = 6$. ✓

**$N = 45$ is the smallest odd number with $d(N) \geq 5$**, giving $S = (45+1)/2
 = 23$.

All odd $N < 45$ have $d(N) \leq 4$, so $d(N)-2 \leq 2 < 3$.

## Verification for $S = 23$

$N = 45 = 3^2 \times 5$, divisors: $\{1, 3, 5, 9, 15, 45\}$, giving $d(45)-2 = 4
 \geq 3$ arrangements:

| Factorization | $r$ (rows) | $m$ | $n$ | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $3 \times 15$ | 3 | 15 | 8 | $8+7+8 = 23$ ✓ |
| $5 \times 9$ | 5 | 9 | 5 | $5+4+5+4+5 = 23$ ✓ |
| $9 \times 5$ | 9 | 5 | 3 | $5(3)+4(2) = 23$ ✓ |
| $15 \times 3$ | 15 | 3 | 2 | $8(2)+7(1) = 23$ ✓ |

All four are distinct valid arrangements (different $n$ and different row counts
), confirming at least 3 ways.

## Conclusion

The minimum total number of sensors is $\boxed{23}$.
