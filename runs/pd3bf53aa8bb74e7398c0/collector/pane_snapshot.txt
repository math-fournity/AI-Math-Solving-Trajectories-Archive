## Proof

We show that $C_0(a,b)$ is dense in $\bigl(C(a,b),\,\|\cdot\|_1\bigr)$.

**Setup.** We assume $a,b$ are finite (the argument extends to infinite endpoint
s by analogous truncation). The space $C(a,b)$ equipped with the $L^1$ norm cons
ists of those continuous functions on the open interval $(a,b)$ that are Lebesgu
e-integrable, i.e., $C(a,b)\cap L^1(a,b)$. The space $C_0(a,b)$ consists of cont
inuous $f:(a,b)\to\mathbb{R}$ with $\lim_{x\to a^+}f(x)=0$ and $\lim_{x\to b^-}f
(x)=0$.

**Key tool — absolute continuity of the integral.** If $f\in L^1(a,b)$, then for
 every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $\delta>0$ such that for every measurable $E
\subset(a,b)$ with $m(E)<\delta$, $\int_E |f|\,dx<\varepsilon$. In particular,
$$\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^+}\int_a^{a+\varepsilon}|f(x)|\,dx = 0 \quad\text{and}\
quad \lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^+}\int_{b-\varepsilon}^{b}|f(x)|\,dx = 0.$$

**Construction of the approximants.** Let $f\in C(a,b)\cap L^1(a,b)$ and let $\v
arepsilon>0$. Choose $\eta>0$ small enough that
$$\int_a^{a+\eta}|f(x)|\,dx < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \quad\text{and}\quad \int_{b
-\eta}^{b}|f(x)|\,dx < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.$$
Define a continuous cutoff $\varphi_\eta:(a,b)\to[0,1]$ by
$$\varphi_\eta(x)=\begin{cases} 0 & a < x \le a+\eta/2,\\[4pt] \dfrac{x-a-\eta/2

frac{b-\eta/2-x}{\eta/2} & b-\eta < x < b-\eta/2,\\[6pt] 0 & b-\eta/2 \le x < b.
 \end{cases}$$
This $\varphi_\eta$ is continuous on $(a,b)$, equals $0$ near both endpoints, an
d equals $1$ on $[a+\eta,\, b-\eta]$.

- $g_\eta$ is continuous on $(a,b)$ (product of continuous functions).

**Estimating the error.** Since $0\le\varphi_\eta\le 1$ and $\varphi_\eta=1$ on
