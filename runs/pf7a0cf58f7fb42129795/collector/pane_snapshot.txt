## Proof

Let $f$ be holomorphic in the open strip $B = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : a < \operator
name{Im}(z) < b\}$ and satisfy $f(z+1) = f(z)$ for all $z \in B$.

### Step 1: Reduce to a function on an annulus

Consider the map
$$\varphi : B \to \mathbb{C}^*, \qquad \varphi(z) = e^{2\pi i z}.$$

Writing $z = x + iy$ with $a < y < b$, we have
$$|\varphi(z)| = |e^{2\pi i(x+iy)}| = e^{-2\pi y} \in (e^{-2\pi b},\, e^{-2\pi a
}).$$

Thus $\varphi$ maps $B$ onto the annulus
$$A = \{w \in \mathbb{C} : e^{-2\pi b} < |w| < e^{-2\pi a}\},$$
with inner radius $r_1 = e^{-2\pi b}$ and outer radius $r_2 = e^{-2\pi a}$.

Since $f(z+1) = f(z)$, the value of $f$ depends only on $\varphi(z) = e^{2\pi i
z}$ (because $\varphi(z_1) = \varphi(z_2)$ iff $z_1 - z_2 \in \mathbb{Z}$). Ther
efore $f$ descends through $\varphi$: there is a well-defined function $g : A \t
o \mathbb{C}$ with
$$f(z) = g(e^{2\pi i z}).$$

The map $\varphi : B \to A$ is a holomorphic covering map (locally biholomorphic
), so $g$ is holomorphic on $A$.

### Step 2: Laurent expansion of $g$

By the Laurent series theorem, every function holomorphic on an annulus $r_1 < |
w| < r_2$ admits a unique expansion
$$g(w) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n\, w^n,$$
converging **absolutely and uniformly on every compact subset** of $A$, i.e., on
 every closed sub-annulus $r_1' \le |w| \le r_2'$ with $r_1 < r_1' < r_2' < r_2$
.

### Step 3: Translate back to $f$

Substituting $w = e^{2\pi i z}$:

$$\boxed{\,f(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n\, e^{2\pi i n z}\,}$$

This is the **Fourier-type (exponential) series expansion** of $f$.

### Step 4: Convergence conditions

The uniform absolute convergence on the closed sub-annulus $r_1' \le |w| \le r_2
'$ translates, via $|w| = e^{-2\pi y}$, into convergence on the corresponding cl
osed sub-strip
$$a' \le \operatorname{Im}(z) \le b', \qquad a < a' < b' < b.$$

Concretely, on such a sub-strip we have $|e^{2\pi i n z}| = e^{-2\pi n\, \operat
orname{Im}(z)}$, so the series becomes
$$\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n\, e^{-2\pi n y},$$
and the absolute convergence is governed by the two "tails":

- **Positive-index tail** $\sum_{n \ge 0} |c_n|\, e^{-2\pi n a'}$: converges bec
ause $g$ is bounded near $|w| = r_2' = e^{-2\pi a'}$ (inside the annulus), givin
g $\limsup_{n\to\infty} |c_n|^{1/n} \le 1/r_2 = e^{2\pi a}$, hence $|c_n| e^{-2\
pi n a'} \to 0$ geometrically since $a' > a$.

- **Negative-index tail** $\sum_{n \le -1} |c_n|\, e^{-2\pi n b'}$: converges be
cause $\limsup_{n\to\infty} |c_{-n}|^{1/n} \le r_1 = e^{-2\pi b}$, hence $|c_{-n
}| e^{2\pi n b'} \to 0$ geometrically since $b' < b$.

### Conclusion

A holomorphic function $f$ of period $1$ in the strip $a < \operatorname{Im}(z)
< b$ has the expansion

$$f(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n\, e^{2\pi i n z},$$

which **converges absolutely and uniformly on every closed sub-strip $a' \le \op
