$$
(\mathcal{L}f)(s) = \int_0^\infty e^{-st} f(t)\,dt, \qquad s>0.
$$

**Step 1: Reduction to convolution on $\mathbb{R}$.**

Make the substitutions $t = e^u$, $s = e^{-v}$ with $u,v\in\mathbb{R}$. Define
$$
g(u) = e^{u/2} f(e^u), \qquad h(v) = e^{-v/2}(\mathcal{L}f)(e^{-v}).
$$
Then
$$
\|g\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R})}^2 = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} |f(e^u)|^2 e^u\,du = \int_0^
\infty |f(t)|^2\,dt = \|f\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^+)}^2,
$$
and similarly $\|h\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R})}^2 = \|\mathcal{L}f\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^+)}^
2$. So $\mathcal{L}$ is unitarily equivalent to the operator $T: g\mapsto h$ on
$L^2(\mathbb{R})$.

Computing $h(v)$:
$$
h(v) = e^{-v/2}\int_0^\infty e^{-e^{-v}t}f(t)\,dt = e^{-v/2}\int_{-\infty}^{\inf

Using $f(e^u)=e^{-u/2}g(u)$:
$$
h(v) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-e^{u-v}} e^{(u-v)/2}\,g(u)\,du.
$$
Setting $w=u-v$:
$$
h(v) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} k(w)\,g(v+w)\,dw = (k * g)(v), \qquad k(w) := e^{
-e^w}e^{w/2}.
$$

$$

Since $k\in L^1(\mathbb{R})$, convolution by $k$ is bounded on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$

$$
\|\mathcal{L}\| = \|\widehat{k}\|_\infty = \sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}} |\widehat{k}(

$$

**Step 3: Computing the Fourier transform.**

$$
\widehat{k}(\xi) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-e^w}e^{w/2}e^{-i\xi w}\,dw.
$$

$$
\widehat{k}(\xi) = \int_0^\infty e^{-x}\,x^{1/2}\,x^{-i\xi}\,\frac{dx}{x} = \int

$$
