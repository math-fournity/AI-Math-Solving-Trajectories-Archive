Looking at this problem, I need to find the 1000th digit after the decimal point
 of $\sqrt{N}$ where $N = \underbrace{11\cdots1}_{1998} = \frac{10^{1998}-1}{9}$
.

## Proof

**Setup.** We have
$$\sqrt{N} = \frac{\sqrt{10^{1998}-1}}{3} = \frac{10^{999}\sqrt{1-10^{-1998}}}{3
}.$$

**Binomial expansion.** Using $\sqrt{1-x} = 1 - \frac{x}{2} - \frac{x^2}{8} - \c
dots$ with $x = 10^{-1998}$:
$$\sqrt{1-10^{-1998}} = 1 - \frac{10^{-1998}}{2} - \frac{10^{-3996}}{8} - \cdots
$$

Multiplying by $\frac{10^{999}}{3}$:
$$\sqrt{N} = \frac{10^{999}}{3} - \frac{10^{-999}}{6} - \frac{10^{-2997}}{24} -
\cdots$$

**First term.** $\frac{10^{999}}{3} = \underbrace{33\cdots3}_{999}.\overline{3}$
, i.e., 999 threes before the decimal, then all 3s repeating after.

**Second term.** $\frac{10^{-999}}{6} = \frac{1}{6}\cdot 10^{-999} = 0.\underbra
ce{00\cdots0}_{999}1\overline{6}$, since $\frac{1}{6} = 0.1\overline{6}$.

**Error bound.** The third term $\frac{10^{-2997}}{24} \approx 4.17\times 10^{-2
999}$ begins affecting at the 2999th decimal place. The total tail is bounded by
 $\frac{10^{-2997}}{24}\cdot\frac{1}{1-10^{-1998}} < 10^{-2996}$, which is utter
ly negligible at position 1000. So only the first two terms determine the 1000th
 digit.

**Subtraction.** Compute:
$$\underbrace{33\cdots3}_{999}.\underbrace{33\cdots3}_{999}333\ldots \;-\; 0.\un
derbrace{00\cdots0}_{999}1\overline{6}$$

- **Positions 1–999:** $3 - 0 = 3$ (unchanged).
- **From position 1000 onward:** we compute $0.\overline{3} - 0.1\overline{6}$ (
viewing positions 1000+ as a fresh decimal). Since $0.\overline{3} = \frac{1}{3}
$ and $0.1\overline{6} = \frac{1}{6}$:
$$\frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{6} = 0.1\overline{6}$$

So from position 1000 onward, the digits are $1, 6, 6, 6, \ldots$

**Verification with small case.** For $N = 111111$ (6 ones): $\sqrt{N} \approx 3
33.3331667\ldots$ The 4th digit after the decimal (= $n/2 + 1 = 4$) is $1$, conf
irming the pattern.

**Conclusion.** The 1000th digit after the decimal point of $\sqrt{N}$ is $\boxe
d{1}$.
