# Solution

## Step 1: Show the three definitions agree on the sphere

On the unit sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$, differentiating gives $x\,dx + y\,dy +
 z\,dz = 0$.

**Claim:** On the sphere (where the respective denominators are nonzero),
$$\frac{dy \wedge dz}{x} = \frac{dz \wedge dx}{y} = \frac{dx \wedge dy}{z}.$$

*Proof of claim.* When $x \neq 0$, solve $x\,dx = -y\,dy - z\,dz$ for $dx = -\fr
ac{y\,dy + z\,dz}{x}$. Then
$$dx \wedge dy = -\frac{z}{x}\,dz \wedge dy = \frac{z}{x}\,dy \wedge dz \implies
 \frac{dx \wedge dy}{z} = \frac{dy \wedge dz}{x}.$$

When $z \neq 0$, solve $z\,dz = -x\,dx - y\,dy$ for $dz = -\frac{x\,dx + y\,dy}{
z}$. Then
$$dz \wedge dx = -\frac{y}{z}\,dy \wedge dx = \frac{y}{z}\,dx \wedge dy \implies
 \frac{dz \wedge dx}{y} = \frac{dx \wedge dy}{z}.$$

This establishes the chain of equalities. $\square$

Denote this common 2-form on the sphere by $\omega$.

## Step 2: Relate $\omega$ to the area form

The area 2-form on the unit sphere with **outward** unit normal $\mathbf{n} = (x
, y, z)$ is
$$\sigma = x\,dy \wedge dz + y\,dz \wedge dx + z\,dx \wedge dy.$$

(Indeed $\sigma = \iota_{\mathbf{n}}(dx \wedge dy \wedge dz)$, the contraction o
f the volume form with the outward normal.)

On the sphere, using the equalities from Step 1:
$$\sigma = x^2\,\omega + y^2\,\omega + z^2\,\omega = (x^2 + y^2 + z^2)\,\omega =
 \omega.$$

Thus **$\omega$ is exactly the area form** on the unit sphere (with outward orie
ntation).

## Step 3: Compute the integral

The upper hemisphere $S$ with outward normal has total area $= \frac{1}{2}(4\pi)
 = 2\pi$.

**Direct verification.** Parametrize the upper hemisphere by spherical coordinat
es:
$$x = \sin\theta\cos\phi,\quad y = \sin\theta\sin\phi,\quad z = \cos\theta,$$
with $\theta \in [0, \pi/2]$, $\phi \in [0, 2\pi]$. Since $z = \cos\theta \geq 0
$ (zero only on the boundary circle, a measure-zero set), use $\omega = \frac{dx
 \wedge dy}{z}$:

$$dx \wedge dy = \sin\theta\cos\theta\,d\theta \wedge d\phi,$$
$$\omega = \frac{\sin\theta\cos\theta}{\cos\theta}\,d\theta \wedge d\phi = \sin\
theta\,d\theta \wedge d\phi.$$

Therefore
$$\int_S \omega = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{\pi/2} \sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi = 2
\pi\Big[-\cos\theta\Big]_0^{\pi/2} = 2\pi\,(0 - (-1)) = 2\pi.$$

## Result

$$\boxed{\int_S \omega = 2\pi}$$
