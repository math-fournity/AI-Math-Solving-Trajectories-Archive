## (a) Solution of the boundary-value problem

**Setting up the problem.** The PDE is the one-dimensional wave equation

$$Y_{tt}=Y_{xx},\qquad 0<x<2,\;t>0,$$

with boundary condition $Y(0,t)=0$, initial displacement $Y(x,0)=0.05\,x(2-x)$,
and initial velocity $Y_t(x,0)=0$.

The spatial domain is the finite interval $0<x<2$, and the prescribed initial sh
ape $f(x)=0.05\,x(2-x)$ vanishes at **both** endpoints ($f(0)=0$ and $f(2)=0$).
For the wave equation on a finite interval to be well-posed a second boundary co
ndition is required; the natural one, consistent with the given data, is

$$Y(2,t)=0.$$

(Indeed $f(2)=0$, so the data already satisfies it.) We therefore solve the clas
sical **vibrating-string problem with both ends fixed.**

---

**Separation of variables.** With $Y(0,t)=Y(2,t)=0$, the spatial eigenfunctions
are

$$X_n(x)=\sin\!\Bigl(\frac{n\pi x}{2}\Bigr),\qquad \lambda_n=\frac{n\pi}{2},\qqu
ad n=1,2,3,\dots$$

The time equation $T''_n+\lambda_n^2 T_n=0$ gives $T_n(t)=A_n\cos(\lambda_n t)+B
_n\sin(\lambda_n t)$. The zero initial velocity $Y_t(x,0)=0$ forces every $B_n=0
$, so

$$Y(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n\,\sin\!\Bigl(\frac{n\pi x}{2}\Bigr)\cos\!\Bigl(
\frac{n\pi t}{2}\Bigr).$$

---

**Fourier coefficients.** At $t=0$,

$$0.05\,x(2-x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n\sin\!\Bigl(\frac{n\pi x}{2}\Bigr),\qquad
A_n=\frac{2}{2}\int_0^2 0.05\,x(2-x)\sin\!\Bigl(\frac{n\pi x}{2}\Bigr)\,dx.$$

Set $k=n\pi/2$ and compute $I_n=\int_0^2 (2x-x^2)\sin(kx)\,dx$ by tabular integr
ation by parts ($u=2x-x^2$, $dv=\sin(kx)\,dx$):

$$\int(2x-x^2)\sin(kx)\,dx=-\frac{(2x-x^2)\cos(kx)}{k}+\frac{(2-2x)\sin(kx)}{k^2
}-\frac{2\cos(kx)}{k^3}.$$

Evaluating at the endpoints, using $\cos(2k)=\cos(n\pi)=(-1)^n$ and $\sin(2k)=\s
in(n\pi)=0$:

- at $x=2$: $\;-\dfrac{2(-1)^n}{k^3}$,
- at $x=0$: $\;-\dfrac{2}{k^3}$,

so

$$I_n=\frac{2}{k^3}\bigl(1-(-1)^n\bigr)=\frac{16}{n^3\pi^3}\bigl(1-(-1)^n\bigr).
$$

Hence

$$A_n=0.05\,I_n=\frac{8}{5n^3\pi^3}\bigl(1-(-1)^n\bigr).$$

For **even** $n$, $(-1)^n=1$ and $A_n=0$. For **odd** $n=2m+1$ ($m=0,1,2,\dots$)
, $(-1)^n=-1$ and

$$A_{2m+1}=\frac{16}{5(2m+1)^3\pi^3}.$$

---

**The solution.** Substituting back,

$$\boxed{\;Y(x,t)=\frac{16}{5\pi^3}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2m+1)^3}\,\sin\!
\Bigl(\frac{(2m+1)\pi x}{2}\Bigr)\cos\!\Bigl(\frac{(2m+1)\pi t}{2}\Bigr)\;}$$

---

**Verification of the conditions.**

- *Boundary $Y(0,t)$:* every term contains $\sin(0)=0$, so $Y(0,t)=0$. Likewise
$\sin((2m+1)\pi\cdot 2/2)=\sin((2m+1)\pi)=0$, giving $Y(2,t)=0$. ✓
- *Initial displacement:* at $t=0$ the series becomes $\frac{16}{5\pi^3}\sum_{m\
ge0}\frac{\sin((2m+1)\pi x/2)}{(2m+1)^3}$, which is exactly the Fourier sine ser
ies of $0.05\,x(2-x)$ on $[0,2]$ (only odd harmonics, because the parabolic prof
ile is symmetric about the midpoint $x=1$). ✓
- *Initial velocity:* $Y_t$ brings down a factor $\sin((2m+1)\pi t/2)$, which va
nishes at $t=0$, so $Y_t(x,0)=0$. ✓
- *PDE:* each term $\sin(\lambda_n x)\cos(\lambda_n t)$ individually satisfies $
Y_{tt}=Y_{xx}$ since $\lambda_n^2=\lambda_n^2$. ✓

---

## (b) Physical interpretation

This is the **transverse vibration of an elastic string of length $L=2$ fixed at
 both ends** ($x=0$ and $x=2$). At time $t=0$ the string is **plucked** into the
 parabolic shape

$$Y(x,0)=0.05\,x(2-x),$$

whose maximum displacement $0.05$ occurs at the midpoint $x=1$, and it is **rele
ased from rest** (zero initial velocity).

The solution is a superposition of the string's **normal modes** (standing waves
):

$$\sin\!\Bigl(\frac{(2m+1)\pi x}{2}\Bigr)\cos\!\Bigl(\frac{(2m+1)\pi t}{2}\Bigr)
,\qquad m=0,1,2,\dots$$

- The **fundamental** ($m=0$) has angular frequency $\omega_1=\pi/2$ and period
$T_1=2\pi/\omega_1=4$; the higher modes have frequencies $(2m+1)\omega_1$.
- **Only odd harmonics** appear. This is because the initial parabolic displacem
ent is *symmetric about the midpoint* $x=1$, while the even modes are antisymmet
ric there and so are not excited.
- The amplitude of the $m$-th mode falls off as $1/(2m+1)^3$, so the fundamental
 dominates; the series converges rapidly, consistent with the smooth (parabolic)
 initial shape.
- The motion is **periodic** with period $T=4$ (the fundamental period): the str
ing oscillates between the plucked parabola and its mirror image below the equil
ibrium axis, retracing its shape indefinitely — the characteristic behaviour of
an ideal lossless string released from rest.
