## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle \int_{\gamma} \frac{dz}{(z^2+4)^2}$ where $\gamma$ wi
nds once counterclockwise around $2i$ and once clockwise around $-2i$.

**Step 1: Factor and locate the poles.**

$$z^2+4 = (z-2i)(z+2i), \qquad (z^2+4)^2 = (z-2i)^2(z+2i)^2.$$

The integrand $f(z) = \dfrac{1}{(z-2i)^2(z+2i)^2}$ has poles of order $2$ at $z
= 2i$ and $z = -2i$.

**Step 2: Apply the (generalized) residue theorem with winding numbers.**

The winding numbers are $n(\gamma, 2i) = +1$ and $n(\gamma, -2i) = -1$, so

$$\int_{\gamma} f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i\Big[n(\gamma,2i)\,\operatorname{Res}(f,2i) + n
(\gamma,-2i)\,\operatorname{Res}(f,-2i)\Big].$$

**Step 3: Compute $\operatorname{Res}(f, 2i)$ — pole of order $2$.**

For a double pole at $z_0$, $\operatorname{Res}(f,z_0)=\dfrac{d}{dz}\!\left[(z-z
_0)^2 f(z)\right]\Big|_{z=z_0}$. Here

$$\operatorname{Res}(f,2i) = \frac{d}{dz}\!\left[\frac{1}{(z+2i)^2}\right]_{z=2i
} = -\frac{2}{(z+2i)^3}\Big|_{z=2i} = -\frac{2}{(4i)^3}.$$

Since $(4i)^3 = 64i^3 = -64i$,

$$\operatorname{Res}(f,2i) = -\frac{2}{-64i} = \frac{1}{32i} = -\frac{i}{32}.$$

**Step 4: Compute $\operatorname{Res}(f, -2i)$ — pole of order $2$.**

$$\operatorname{Res}(f,-2i) = \frac{d}{dz}\!\left[\frac{1}{(z-2i)^2}\right]_{z=-
2i} = -\frac{2}{(z-2i)^3}\Big|_{z=-2i} = -\frac{2}{(-4i)^3}.$$

Since $(-4i)^3 = -64i^3 = 64i$,

$$\operatorname{Res}(f,-2i) = -\frac{2}{64i} = -\frac{1}{32i} = \frac{i}{32}.$$

$$\int_{\gamma} f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i\left[(+1)\!\left(-\frac{i}{32}\right) + (-1)\!
\left(\frac{i}{32}\right)\right] = 2\pi i\left[-\frac{i}{32} - \frac{i}{32}\righ
t] = 2\pi i\!\left(-\frac{2i}{32}\right).$$

$$= 2\pi i \cdot \left(-\frac{i}{16}\right) = -\frac{2\pi i^2}{16} = -\frac{2\pi
(-1)}{16} = \frac{2\pi}{16} = \frac{\pi}{8}.$$

**Result:**

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \int_{\gamma} \frac{dz}{(z^2+4)^2} = \frac{\pi}{8}}.$$
