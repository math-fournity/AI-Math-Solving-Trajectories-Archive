## Proof

**Claim:** $H^2(U, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$ when $U$ is a disk and $\mathbb{Z}$ is the l
ocally constant integer sheaf.

**Step 1: A disk is contractible.**

Let $U$ be a disk (open or closed, in any dimension — say $U = \{x \in \mathbb{R
}^n : \|x\| < 1\}$). The map $H: U \times [0,1] \to U$ defined by $H(x,t) = (1-t
)x$ is a deformation retraction of $U$ onto the point $\{0\}$. Hence $U$ is cont
ractible.

**Step 2: A locally constant sheaf on a contractible (hence simply connected) sp
ace is constant.**

A disk is contractible, and every contractible space is simply connected. For a
simply connected space $X$, every locally constant sheaf (local system) of abeli
an groups is constant. Indeed, a locally constant sheaf of rank-$r$ local system
s corresponds to a representation $\pi_1(X) \to \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}^r)$; whe
n $\pi_1(X) = 0$, this representation is trivial, so the sheaf is the constant s
heaf $\underline{\mathbb{Z}^r}$. In our case $r = 1$, so the locally constant sh
eaf $\mathbb{Z}$ is the constant sheaf $\underline{\mathbb{Z}}$.

**Step 3: The constant sheaf on a contractible space is acyclic in positive degr
ee.**

We use Čech cohomology, which agrees with sheaf cohomology for paracompact Hausd
orff spaces (a disk is such). Consider the cover $\mathcal{U} = \{U\}$ consistin
g of the single open set $U$ itself. Since $U$ is contractible, it is acyclic fo
r the constant sheaf: $H^i(U, \underline{\mathbb{Z}}) = 0$ for $i > 0$ (this is
the standard fact that constant sheaves are acyclic on contractible open sets —
verified, e.g., by the fact that singular cohomology of a contractible space wit
h $\mathbb{Z}$-coefficients vanishes in positive degree, and singular cohomology
 computes sheaf cohomology of the constant sheaf on locally contractible spaces)
.

The cover $\{U\}$ is a good cover (its only nonempty intersection is $U$ itself,
 which is contractible). The Čech complex for this cover is:
$$0 \to \check{C}^0(\mathcal{U}, \underline{\mathbb{Z}}) = \underline{\mathbb{Z}
}(U) = \mathbb{Z} \to \check{C}^1(\mathcal{U}, \underline{\mathbb{Z}}) = 0 \to \
check{C}^2(\mathcal{U}, \underline{\mathbb{Z}}) = 0 \to \cdots$$

All higher Čech groups are zero since there are no ordered tuples of distinct in
dices of length $\geq 2$ from a single-element index set. Therefore:
$$\check{H}^i(\mathcal{U}, \underline{\mathbb{Z}}) = 0 \quad \text{for all } i >
 0.$$

By the good-cover theorem (Čech cohomology computed on a good cover agrees with
sheaf cohomology), we conclude:
$$H^i(U, \underline{\mathbb{Z}}) = 0 \quad \text{for all } i > 0.$$

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 2 and 3, the locally constant sheaf $\mathbb{Z}$ on the disk $U$
 is the constant sheaf $\underline{\mathbb{Z}}$, and its sheaf cohomology vanish
es in every positive degree. In particular:

$$\boxed{H^2(U, \mathbb{Z}) = 0.}$$
