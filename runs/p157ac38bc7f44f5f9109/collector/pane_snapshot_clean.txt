## Proof

Let $f$ be holomorphic and non-constant on the annulus $A(a,b)=\{z:a<|z|<b\}$, a
nd let

$$M(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|,\qquad a<r<b.$$

Hadamard's three-circle theorem says that $\log M(r)$ is a **convex** function o
f $\log r$; equivalently, for every $a<r_1<r_2<r_3<b$,

$$\log M(r_2)\le \frac{\log r_3-\log r_2}{\log r_3-\log r_1}\,\log M(r_1)+\frac{
\log r_2-\log r_1}{\log r_3-\log r_1}\,\log M(r_3).$$

We must show that **equality holds (for every triple, i.e. $\log M(r)$ is affine
 in $\log r$) if and only if $f(z)=cz^k$** for some $c\neq 0$ and $k\in\mathbb Z
$.

---

### Sufficiency ($\Leftarrow$)

If $f(z)=cz^k$ with $c\neq 0$, $k\in\mathbb Z$, then $|f(z)|=|c|\,|z|^k$, so

$$M(r)=|c|\,r^k,\qquad \log M(r)=\log|c|+k\log r,$$

which is affine in $\log r$. An affine function is convex with equality in every
 convexity inequality, so equality holds in the three-circle theorem. (Non-const
ancy forces $k\neq 0$, but the form is valid for every $k$.)

---

### Necessity ($\Rightarrow$)

Assume equality holds in the three-circle theorem, i.e. $\log M(r)$ is affine in
 $\log r$ on $(a,b)$: there exist real constants $\alpha,\beta$ with

$$\log M(r)=\alpha\log r+\beta,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad M(r)=C\,r^{\alpha},\quad
C:=e^{\beta}>0.\tag{1}$$

**Step 1 — reduce to a bounded auxiliary function.**
Choose a ray (slit) from the inner to the outer boundary; the slit annulus $D=A(
a,b)\setminus\text{ray}$ is simply connected, so a holomorphic branch $L(z)$ of
$\log z$ exists on $D$. Define on $D$

$$h(z):=f(z)\,e^{-\alpha L(z)}=f(z)\,z^{-\alpha}\quad(\text{branch}).$$

Then $h$ is holomorphic on $D$, and for $|z|=r$,

$$|h(z)|=\frac{|f(z)|}{r^{\alpha}}\le \frac{M(r)}{r^{\alpha}}=C.\tag{2}$$

So $h$ is **bounded by $C$** on $D$.

**Step 2 — remove the slit.**
The slit is a smooth arc (a compact set of zero area). A function bounded and ho
lomorphic on the complement of such a removable set extends holomorphically acro
ss it (Painlevé/Riemann removable-singularity theorem). Hence $h$ extends to a h
olomorphic function $H$ on the **full** annulus $A(a,b)$, still satisfying $|H|\
le C$.

**Step 3 — apply the maximum-modulus principle.**
For every $r\in(a,b)$, the maximum of $|f|$ on $|z|=r$ is $M(r)=Cr^{\alpha}$, at
tained at some point $\zeta_r$ with $|\zeta_r|=r$. At that point

$$|H(\zeta_r)|=\frac{|f(\zeta_r)|}{r^{\alpha}}=\frac{Cr^{\alpha}}{r^{\alpha}}=C.
$$

Pick any $a<r_1<r_2<b$ and any $r\in(r_1,r_2)$. The point $\zeta_r$ lies in the
**interior** of the sub-annulus $\{r_1<|z|<r_2\}$, and $|H|$ attains the value $
C$ there. Since $|H|\le C$ everywhere, $|H|$ attains its global maximum at an in
terior point. By the maximum-modulus principle, **$H$ is constant** on $\{r_1<|z
|<r_2\}$. As $r_1,r_2$ are arbitrary, $H\equiv c$ on all of $A(a,b)$, with $|c|=
C$.

**Step 4 — single-valuedness forces $\alpha\in\mathbb Z$.**
On $D$ we have $f(z)=c\,z^{\alpha}$ (branch). Since $f$ is single-valued on the
full annulus $A(a,b)$, analytic continuation of $z^{\alpha}=e^{\alpha L(z)}$ aro
und a loop encircling the origin once multiplies it by $e^{2\pi i\alpha}$; singl
e-valuedness requires $e^{2\pi i\alpha}=1$, i.e. $\alpha=k\in\mathbb Z$.

Therefore

$$\boxed{\,f(z)=c\,z^{k},\qquad c\neq 0,\; k\in\mathbb Z\,}$$

(with non-constancy of $f$ forcing $k\neq 0$). ∎
