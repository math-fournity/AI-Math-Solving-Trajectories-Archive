Looking at this problem, I need to evaluate:

$$\lim_{M \to \infty} \frac{1}{\sum_{i=0}^{\infty} \frac{M!}{(M+i)!} x^i}, \quad
 x > 0.$$

## Proof

Let $S_M = \sum_{i=0}^{\infty} \frac{M!}{(M+i)!} x^i$. I will show $S_M \to 1$ a
s $M \to \infty$, which gives the limit $1/S_M \to 1$.

**Rewriting the terms.** For $i = 0$, the term is $\frac{M!}{M!}x^0 = 1$. For $i
 \geq 1$:

$$\frac{M!}{(M+i)!} = \frac{1}{(M+1)(M+2)\cdots(M+i)}.$$

So $S_M = 1 + \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^i}{(M+1)(M+2)\cdots(M+i)}$.

**Lower bound.** Since every term in the sum is positive (as $x > 0$), we have $
S_M \geq 1$.

**Upper bound.** Each factor $(M+j) \geq (M+1)$ for $j \geq 1$, so:

$$\frac{x^i}{(M+1)(M+2)\cdots(M+i)} \leq \frac{x^i}{(M+1)^i} = \left(\frac{x}{M+
1}\right)^i.$$

Therefore, for $M+1 > x$ (which holds for all sufficiently large $M$):

$$S_M \leq \sum_{i=0}^{\infty} \left(\frac{x}{M+1}\right)^i = \frac{1}{1 - \frac
{x}{M+1}} = \frac{M+1}{M+1-x}.$$

**Squeeze.** We have:

$$1 \leq S_M \leq \frac{M+1}{M+1-x}.$$

As $M \to \infty$, the upper bound $\frac{M+1}{M+1-x} \to 1$. By the squeeze the
orem:

$$\lim_{M \to \infty} S_M = 1.$$

Since $S_M \geq 1 > 0$ for all $M$, the reciprocal is well-defined and continuou
s near the limit, so:

$$\lim_{M \to \infty} \frac{1}{S_M} = \frac{1}{\lim_{M \to \infty} S_M} = \frac{
1}{1} = 1.$$
