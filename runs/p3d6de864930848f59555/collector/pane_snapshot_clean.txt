## Proof

**Claim:** The product of a complete vector field and a bounded (smooth) functio
n on a manifold is always a complete vector field. The answer is **true**.

**Setup.** Let $M$ be a smooth manifold, $X$ a complete smooth vector field on $
M$ with flow $\phi_t$ (defined for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$), and $f: M \to \mathb
b{R}$ a bounded smooth function with $|f| \leq C$. We show that $fX$ is complete
.

**Reparametrization argument.** Let $p \in M$. We seek an integral curve $\gamma
(t)$ of $fX$ with $\gamma(0)=p$, i.e., $\dot\gamma = f(\gamma)\,X(\gamma)$.

Define a scalar function $\tau(t)$ via the ODE
$$\tau'(t) = f\!\bigl(\phi_{\tau(t)}(p)\bigr), \qquad \tau(0)=0,$$
and set $\gamma(t) := \phi_{\tau(t)}(p)$.

**Consistency.** Differentiating $\gamma(t)=\phi_{\tau(t)}(p)$ gives
$$\dot\gamma(t) = \tau'(t)\, X\!\bigl(\phi_{\tau(t)}(p)\bigr) = f(\gamma(t))\,X(
\gamma(t)),$$
so $\gamma$ is indeed an integral curve of $fX$.

**Global existence of $\tau$.** The right-hand side $g(\tau) := f(\phi_\tau(p))$
 is a smooth function of $\tau$ (smoothness of the flow in all variables) and is
 bounded: $|g(\tau)| \leq C$. For the ODE $\tau' = g(\tau)$, the bound $|\tau'(t
)| \leq C$ gives
$$|\tau(t)| \leq C\,|t|,$$
so $\tau(t)$ cannot blow up to $\pm\infty$ in finite time. Since the only obstru
ction to global existence for a smooth ODE on $\mathbb{R}$ is finite-time blowup
, $\tau(t)$ exists for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$.

**Global existence of $\gamma$.** Since $X$ is complete, $\phi_s(p)$ is defined
for every $s \in \mathbb{R}$. For each finite $t$, $\tau(t)$ is finite, so $\gam
ma(t) = \phi_{\tau(t)}(p)$ is well-defined. Thus the integral curve through $p$
exists for all $t$.

Since $p$ was arbitrary, $fX$ is complete. $\blacksquare$
