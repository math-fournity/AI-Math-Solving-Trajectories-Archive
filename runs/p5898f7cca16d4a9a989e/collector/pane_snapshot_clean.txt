**Proof.**

We prove existence and uniqueness of a function $f \colon \mathbb{R}^+ \to \math
bb{R}^+$ satisfying $f(f(x)) = 6x - f(x)$ for all $x > 0$.

**Existence.** Take $f(x) = 2x$. Then $f(f(x)) = f(2x) = 4x = 6x - 2x = 6x - f(x
)$, and $f(x) = 2x > 0$ for all $x > 0$. So such a function exists.

**Uniqueness.** Suppose $f \colon \mathbb{R}^+ \to \mathbb{R}^+$ satisfies $f(f(
x)) + f(x) = 6x$ for all $x > 0$. Fix any $x_0 > 0$ and define the orbit $x_n =
f^{(n)}(x_0)$ for $n \geq 0$, where $f^{(n)}$ denotes the $n$-fold iterate. Sinc
e $f$ maps $\mathbb{R}^+$ to $\mathbb{R}^+$, every $x_n$ is well-defined and pos
itive.

The functional equation gives $f(f(x_n)) + f(x_n) = 6x_n$, i.e.,
$$x_{n+2} + x_{n+1} = 6x_n, \qquad \text{equivalently } x_{n+2} = 6x_n - x_{n+1}
, \quad n \geq 0.$$

This is a linear recurrence with characteristic equation $r^2 + r - 6 = 0$, fact
oring as $(r-2)(r+3) = 0$, with roots $r = 2$ and $r = -3$. Hence
$$x_n = A \cdot 2^n + B \cdot (-3)^n$$
for constants $A, B$ determined by the initial values:
$$A = \frac{3x_0 + x_1}{5}, \qquad B = \frac{2x_0 - x_1}{5},$$
where $x_1 = f(x_0)$.

We claim $B = 0$. Suppose for contradiction that $B \neq 0$.

- If $B > 0$: for odd $n$, $(-3)^n < 0$, so $B(-3)^n = -B \cdot 3^n \to -\infty$
, while $A \cdot 2^n = o(3^n)$. Thus $x_n \to -\infty$ along odd $n$, so $x_n <
0$ for all sufficiently large odd $n$.
- If $B < 0$: for even $n$, $(-3)^n > 0$, so $B(-3)^n = -|B| \cdot 3^n \to -\inf
ty$, while $A \cdot 2^n = o(3^n)$. Thus $x_n \to -\infty$ along even $n$, so $x_
n < 0$ for all sufficiently large even $n$.

In either case $x_n < 0$ for some $n$, contradicting $x_n \in \mathbb{R}^+$ (sin
ce $f$ maps $\mathbb{R}^+$ to $\mathbb{R}^+$). Therefore $B = 0$.

From $B = \frac{2x_0 - x_1}{5} = 0$ we get $x_1 = 2x_0$, i.e., $f(x_0) = 2x_0$.
Since $x_0 > 0$ was arbitrary, $f(x) = 2x$ for all $x > 0$.

Thus $f(x) = 2x$ is the unique function satisfying the given conditions. $\black
square$
