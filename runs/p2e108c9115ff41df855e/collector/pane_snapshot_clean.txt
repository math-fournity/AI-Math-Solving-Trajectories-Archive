## Proof

**Answer: Yes, it is always possible.**

Let $S$ be a compact Riemann surface of genus $g$, and let $p \neq q$ be two dis
tinct points on $S$. Since $S$ is a one-dimensional complex manifold, it has inf
initely many points, so we may choose a point $r \in S \setminus \{p, q\}$.

Set $n = 2g+1$ and consider the divisor $D = n\cdot r$, so $\deg D = 2g+1$.

**Riemann–Roch setup.** Recall that for any divisor $E$ with $\deg E > 2g-2$, th
e divisor $K - E$ has negative degree, hence $l(K - E) = 0$, and the Riemann–Roc
h theorem gives
$$l(E) = \deg E + 1 - g.$$

Apply this to three divisors:

| Divisor | Degree | Condition | Dimension |
|---|---|---|---|
| $D = n\cdot r$ | $2g+1$ | $> 2g-2$ ✓ | $l(D) = g+2$ |
| $D - p = n\cdot r - p$ | $2g$ | $> 2g-2$ ✓ | $l(D-p) = g+1$ |
| $D - p - q = n\cdot r - p - q$ | $2g-1$ | $> 2g-2$ ✓ | $l(D-p-q) = g$ |

**Interpreting the spaces.** Since $r \notin \{p, q\}$, neither $p$ nor $q$ lies
 in the support of $D$. Therefore every $f \in L(D)$ is holomorphic at $p$ and a
t $q$, so evaluation at these points is well defined. Moreover:

- $L(D - p) = \{f \in L(D) : f(p) = 0\}$, since $f \in L(D-p)$ requires $\operat
orname{ord}_p(f) \geq 1$.
- $L(D - p - q) = \{f \in L(D) : f(p) = 0 \text{ and } f(q) = 0\}$.

**Counting.** The subspace of $L(D)$ consisting of functions vanishing at $p$ bu
t **not** at $q$ has dimension
$$\dim\{f \in L(D) : f(p)=0,\; f(q)\neq 0\} = l(D-p) - l(D-p-q) = (g+1) - g = 1
> 0.$$

Hence there exists a meromorphic function $f$ on $S$ with $f(p) = 0$ and $f(q) \
neq 0$.

**Remark (sanity checks).** For $g=0$ ($S \cong \mathbb{P}^1$): $n=1$, $l(D)=2$,
 $l(D-p)=1$, $l(D-p-q)=0$, giving dimension $1$ — consistent with, e.g., $f(z)=z
-p$ (poles only at $r=\infty$). For $g=1$: $n=3$, giving dimensions $3, 2, 1$, a
gain yielding a $1$-dimensional space of desired functions.
