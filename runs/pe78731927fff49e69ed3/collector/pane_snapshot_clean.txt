## Proof

We wish to count closed paths that traverse every edge of an icosahedron exactly
 once — that is, **Eulerian circuits** of the icosahedron graph — up to rotation
al symmetry.

**Key facts about the icosahedron graph:**
- Vertices: 12
- Edges: 30
- Faces: 20 (all triangular)
- **Each vertex has degree 5** (five edges meet at every vertex, since five equi
lateral triangles meet at each vertex of an icosahedron).

**Euler's Theorem (Euler, 1736):** A connected graph admits an Eulerian circuit
(a closed walk using every edge exactly once) if and only if **every vertex has
even degree**.

**Application:** The icosahedron graph is connected, but every one of its 12 ver
tices has degree 5, which is **odd**. By Euler's theorem, a connected graph with
 any vertex of odd degree possesses no Eulerian circuit.

Since all 12 vertices have odd degree, there are **no** closed paths traversing
each edge of the icosahedron exactly once. The question of identifying paths up
to rotation is therefore moot — there is nothing to count.

**The number of distinct closed paths is 0.**
