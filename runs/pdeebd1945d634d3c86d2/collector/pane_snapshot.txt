## Minimum-Cost Branching via the Primal–Dual Method (Edmonds' Algorithm)

### 1. LP formulation and its dual

Let $\delta^-(S)=\{(i,j)\in A: i\notin S,\; j\in S\}$. A set $F\subseteq A$ give
s a unique $r\to v$ path for every $v$ iff $F$ is a *spanning arborescence* root
ed at $r$. Its incidence vector $x$ satisfies $x(\delta^-(S))\ge 1$ for every $\
emptyset\ne S\subseteq V\setminus\{r\}$ (every non-root set is "entered"), and c
onversely an integral minimizer of non-negative weights over this system is an a
rborescence (a cycle avoiding $r$ would make its vertex set unentered).

**Primal (P):**
$$\min\ \sum_{(i,j)\in A} w_{ij}x_{ij}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad x(\delta^-(S))\ge 1\
 \ \forall\emptyset\ne S\subseteq V\setminus\{r\},\quad x_{ij}\ge 0.$$

**Dual (D):**
$$\max\ \sum_{\emptyset\ne S\subseteq V\setminus\{r\}} y_S\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\sum_{S:\, j\in S,\,i\notin S} y_S \le w_{ij}\ \ \forall(i,j)\in A,\quad y_S\ge
0.$$

We construct a feasible dual $y$ and an arborescence $F^*$ with $\operatorname{c
ost}(F^*)=\sum y_S$; weak duality then yields $\operatorname{cost}(F^*)=\sum y_S
\le \mathrm{OPT}_{\text{LP}}\le \operatorname{cost}(\text{any arborescence})$, p
roving $F^*$ optimal. No integrality theorem is needed.

### 2. The algorithm

Maintain a **laminar family** $\mathcal L$ of subsets of $V\setminus\{r\}$ with
dual variables $y_S\ge 0$ ($S\in\mathcal L$; all other $y_S=0$), and a set $F$ o
f *tight* arcs. The maximal members of $\mathcal L$ together with uncovered sing
letons are the current **components**. A component $C$ is **active** if $r\notin
 C$ and no arc of $F$ enters $C$ from outside $C$.

Initialize $\mathcal L=\emptyset$, $F=\emptyset$, $y=0$ (every $\{v\}$, $v\ne r$
, is an active component).

**Growth step** (while an active component exists):
- Let $\mathcal C$ be the active components. Define the *reduced cost* of an arc
 $(i,j)$ entering an active component $C\ni j$ (with $i\notin C$):
$$\bar w_{ij}=w_{ij}-\sum_{S\in\mathcal L:\, j\in S,\, i\notin S} y_S.$$
- Increase $y_C$ for every $C\in\mathcal C$ at a **uniform rate** by $\varepsilo
n\ge 0$, where
$$\varepsilon=\min\{\bar w_{ij}:\ (i,j)\in A,\ j\in C\in\mathcal C,\ i\notin C\}
.$$
(If the minimum is over an empty set, $r$ cannot reach all vertices: **infeasibl
e**.)
- Update $y_C\leftarrow y_C+\varepsilon$ for all active $C$. At least one arc en
tering an active component becomes tight ($\bar w_{ij}=0$); add **all** such new
ly-tight arcs to $F$.

**Contraction.** If the tight arcs added to $F$ form a directed cycle among curr
ent components (necessarily all active, hence none containing $r$), merge all co
mponents of that cycle into a single new component $C^\star=\bigcup_i C_i$; add
$C^\star$ to $\mathcal L$ with $y_{C^\star}=0$; the cycle arcs become internal t
o $C^\star$. $C^\star$ is active (it has no external incoming selected arc).

**Inactivation.** A component becomes inactive the moment an arc of $F$ enters i
t from outside; its $y$ is then frozen forever.

When no active component remains, every non-root component has exactly one incom
ing arc of $F$, and the contracted graph is an arborescence rooted at $r$ (cycle
s were eliminated by contraction).

### 3. Uncontraction

Expand contractions in reverse order. Each contracted cycle $C^\star$ has exactl
y one incoming selected arc $(a^\star,v^\star)$ from outside $C^\star$ (the arc
that inactivated $C^\star$). Inside $C^\star$ the cycle arcs form a directed cyc
le through the sub-components; let $B^\star$ be the sub-component containing $v^
\star$. **Delete** the single cycle arc that enters $B^\star$, and keep all othe
r cycle arcs. This breaks the cycle into a directed path while preserving in-deg
ree $1$ at every vertex. Recurse into nested contractions. The result $F^\star$
is a spanning arborescence: every $v\ne r$ has in-degree exactly $1$, and no dir
ected cycle survives (each would have been broken at its outermost contraction).

### 4. Dual feasibility

Clearly $y_S\ge 0$. The dual constraint $\sum_{S\ni j,\,i\notin S}y_S\le w_{ij}$
 is maintained because $\varepsilon$ is chosen exactly so that no reduced cost g
oes negative. We must check it is *never* violated later. The only way the sum $
\sum_{S\ni j,\,i\notin S}y_S$ can increase after an arc $(i,j)$ is tight is if s
ome **active** set $S$ with $j\in S$, $i\notin S$ grows. By laminarity, such a g
rowing set must be a component containing $j$ but not $i$. Once the component co
ntaining $j$ becomes inactive it stops growing; and any later active set contain
ing $j$ either also contains $i$ (so it does not contribute to the sum) or is co
ntained in the inactive component of $j$ (which does not grow). Hence a tight ar
c **stays tight**. So $(y,\mathcal L)$ is dual-feasible throughout.

*Key observation for cycle arcs:* when a cycle on components $C_1,\dots,C_k$ is
contracted to $C^\star$, every cycle arc has **both** endpoints inside $C^\star$
, so $y_{C^\star}$ (and any ancestor of $C^\star$) does *not* enter its reduced-
cost sum; only the already-frozen sub-component duals contribute, and those are
constant. So cycle arcs remain tight as $C^\star$ grows. The external arc that l
ater inactivates $C^\star$ becomes tight exactly when $C^\star$ stops growing, s
o it too stays tight. By induction over the contraction tree, **every arc of $F^
\star$ is tight**.

### 5. Complementary slackness and optimality

- **Dual slackness:** every $(i,j)\in F^\star$ is tight, i.e. $w_{ij}=\sum_{S\ni
 j,\,i\notin S}y_S$.
- **Primal slackness:** if $y_S>0$ then $S\in\mathcal L$ was active and grew, he
nce was inactivated by an incoming selected arc; in the final arborescence exact
ly one arc of $F^\star$ enters $S$ from outside, so $x(\delta^-(S))=1$. (This ho
lds for singleton $\{v\}$ too: $v$ has in-degree $1$ in $F^\star$, and that arc
enters $\{v\}$ from outside.)

Therefore
$$\operatorname{cost}(F^\star)=\sum_{(i,j)\in F^\star} w_{ij}=\sum_{(i,j)\in F^\
star}\sum_{S\ni j,\,i\notin S}y_S=\sum_{S}y_S\cdot x(\delta^-(S))=\sum_{S:\,y_S>
0}y_S=\sum_S y_S.$$

(The third equality swaps summations; the fourth uses $x(\delta^-(S))=1$ for $y_
S>0$ and $=0$ contribution otherwise.) Since $y$ is dual-feasible, weak duality
gives
$$\operatorname{cost}(F^\star)=\sum_S y_S\ \le\ \mathrm{OPT}_{(P)}\ \le\ \operat
orname{cost}(\text{any arborescence}).$$
But $F^\star$ is itself an arborescence, so it attains the minimum: **$F^\star$
is a minimum-cost branching**.

### 6. Running time

Each growth step either inactivates a component (one new incoming arc) or perfor
ms a contraction (merging $\ge 2$ components). There are at most $|V|-1$ inactiv
ations and at most $|V|-1$ contractions, hence $O(|V|)$ growth steps. Computing
$\varepsilon$ scans $O(|A|)$ arcs, giving $O(|V|\cdot|A|)$ total time (improvabl
e to $O(|A|\log|V|)$ with heaps/mergeable data structures). Polynomial and optim
al.

This is Edmonds' contraction algorithm (1967) cast as a primal–dual method: grow
 dual variables on unsatisfied (active) $r$-cuts uniformly, add arcs as their re
duced costs hit zero, contract directed cycles of tight arcs, and finally expand
—breaking each cycle at its external attachment point—to obtain a minimum-cost a
rborescence certified by a feasible dual of equal value.
