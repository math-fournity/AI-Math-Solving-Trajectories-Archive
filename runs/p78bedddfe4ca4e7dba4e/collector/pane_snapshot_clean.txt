## Solution

Let $k\subset K$ be a finite Galois extension with Galois group $G=\operatorname
{Gal}(K/k)$. The restriction map is
$$\operatorname{res}:Br(k)\longrightarrow Br(K),\qquad [A]\longmapsto [A\otimes_
k K].$$

### The $G$-action on $Br(K)$

Each $\sigma\in G$ acts on $K$, hence on central simple $K$-algebras by twisting
 the scalar action: $\sigma(B)$ is the $K$-algebra whose underlying ring is $B$
but with $K$-action $\lambda\cdot_{\sigma}b=\sigma(\lambda)b$. This induces an a
ction of $G$ on $Br(K)$, denoted $\sigma\cdot[B]=[\sigma(B)]$. We write
$$Br(K)^G=\bigl\{[B]\in Br(K):\sigma\cdot[B]=[B]\ \text{for all }\sigma\in G\big
r\}.$$

### Step 1. The image is contained in $Br(K)^G$

For $[A]\in Br(k)$ and $\sigma\in G$, the map
$$\mathrm{id}\otimes\sigma^{-1}:A\otimes_k K\longrightarrow A\otimes_k K$$
is a ring automorphism. To check it is $K$-linear for the twisted structure: an
element of $\sigma(A\otimes_k K)$ is acted on by $\lambda$ as $\sigma(\lambda)$,
 and
$$(\mathrm{id}\otimes\sigma^{-1})(\lambda\cdot_{\sigma}(a\otimes\mu))=(\mathrm{i
d}\otimes\sigma^{-1})(a\otimes\sigma(\lambda)\mu)=a\otimes\lambda\sigma^{-1}(\mu
)=\lambda\cdot(a\otimes\sigma^{-1}(\mu)).$$
Hence $\mathrm{id}\otimes\sigma^{-1}$ is a $K$-algebra isomorphism $\sigma(A\oti
mes_k K)\xrightarrow{\sim}A\otimes_k K$, so
$$\sigma\cdot\operatorname{res}([A])=[\sigma(A\otimes_k K)]=[A\otimes_k K]=\oper
atorname{res}([A]).$$
Thus $\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{res})\subseteq Br(K)^G$.

### Step 2. The obstruction to the reverse inclusion (Hochschild–Serre)

Fix a separable closure $k_s$ of $k$ containing $K$, and set $H=\operatorname{Ga
l}(k_s/K)\trianglelefteq G_k:=\operatorname{Gal}(k_s/k)$, so $G_k/H\cong G$. The
n
$$Br(k)=H^2(G_k,k_s^\times),\qquad Br(K)=H^2(H,k_s^\times),\qquad H^1(H,k_s^\tim
es)=0\ \text{(Hilbert 90)}.$$
The Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence $E_2^{p,q}=H^p\!\bigl(G,H^q(H,k_s^\times)
\bigr)\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(G_k,k_s^\times)$ has $E_2^{p,1}=0$ (by Hilbert 90) and
 $E_2^{0,2}=H^0(G,Br(K))=Br(K)^G$, $E_2^{2,0}=H^2(G,K^\times)=Br(K/k)$, $E_2^{3,
0}=H^3(G,K^\times)$. Since $E_2^{2,1}=0$, the only nontrivial higher differentia
l affecting $E_2^{0,2}$ is
$$d_3:E_3^{0,2}=Br(K)^G\longrightarrow E_3^{3,0}=H^3(G,K^\times).$$
The edge homomorphism $H^2(G_k,k_s^\times)\to H^2(H,k_s^\times)^G$ is precisely
$\operatorname{res}$, and its image is $E_\infty^{0,2}=\ker d_3$. This yields th
e **inflation–restriction exact sequence**
$$0\longrightarrow Br(K/k)\longrightarrow Br(k)\xrightarrow{\;\operatorname{res}
\;}Br(K)^G\xrightarrow{\;d_3\;}H^3(G,K^\times),$$
where $Br(K/k)=\ker(\operatorname{res})=H^2(G,K^\times)$ is the relative Brauer
group, and $d_3$ is the **transgression** (Teichmüller map).

### Step 3. Identification of the obstruction with the Teichmüller class (descen
t)

The transgression $d_3$ admits a concrete descent interpretation, confirming the
 exactness. Let $[B]\in Br(K)^G$ and let $D$ be the unique division $K$-algebra
with $[D]=[B]$. Since $\sigma\cdot[D]=[D]$ and both $\sigma(D)$ and $D$ are divi
sion $K$-algebras, Brauer equivalence forces $\sigma(D)\cong D$ as $K$-algebras.
 Choose $K$-algebra isomorphisms $u_\sigma:\sigma(D)\to D$.

The defect of the $u_\sigma$ from forming a strict $G$-cocycle is
$$u_\sigma\circ\sigma(u_\tau)\circ u_{\sigma\tau}^{-1}\in\operatorname{Aut}_K(D)
.$$
By the Skolem–Noether theorem $\operatorname{Aut}_K(D)=\operatorname{Inn}(D)\con
g D^\times/K^\times$, so this defines a class $\omega\in H^2(G,D^\times/K^\times
)$. The class $[B]$ descends to $k$ (i.e. lies in $\operatorname{im}\operatornam
e{res}$) iff $\omega$ lifts to $H^2(G,D^\times)$. From the exact sequence $1\to
K^\times\to D^\times\to D^\times/K^\times\to 1$, the connecting map $\delta:H^2(
G,D^\times/K^\times)\to H^3(G,K^\times)$ sends $\omega$ to
$$d_3([B])=\delta(\omega)\in H^3(G,K^\times),$$
the **Teichmüller $3$-cocycle** of $D$. By exactness, $[B]\in\operatorname{im}(\
operatorname{res})$ iff $d_3([B])=0$.

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{\operatorname{im}\bigl(Br(k)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{res}}Br(K)\bigr)
=\ker\!\bigl(d_3:Br(K)^G\longrightarrow H^3(G,K^\times)\bigr)\subseteq Br(K)^G.}
$$

Equivalently, there is an exact sequence
$$0\to Br(K/k)\to Br(k)\to Br(K)^G\xrightarrow{d_3}H^3(G,K^\times),$$
where $Br(K/k)=H^2(G,K^\times)$ is the relative Brauer group and $d_3$ is the Te
ichmüller transgression. In particular:

* the image is always contained in the $G$-invariant subgroup $Br(K)^G$;
* the image equals $Br(K)^G$ **if and only if** the Teichmüller map $d_3$ vanish
es — for instance whenever $H^3(G,K^\times)=0$ (e.g. $K$ algebraically closed, o
r $K^\times$ cohomologically trivial as a $G$-module, or $G$ cyclic with $H^3=H^
1$ and norm-surjectivity holding).
