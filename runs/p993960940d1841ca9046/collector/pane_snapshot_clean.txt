Let $X_0, X_1, \dots, X_n$ be i.i.d. $N(0,1)$, and define
$$Y_n = \frac{\sqrt{n}\, X_0}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i^2}}.$$

Set $S_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i^2$. Rewrite
$$Y_n = \frac{X_0}{\sqrt{S_n / n}}.$$

**Step 1: Law of large numbers.** Since $\{X_i^2\}_{i \geq 1}$ are i.i.d. with $
\mathbb{E}[X_1^2] = \operatorname{Var}(X_1) = 1$, the strong law of large number
s gives
$$\frac{S_n}{n} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i^2 \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \mat
hbb{E}[X_1^2] = 1 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty.$$

By the continuous mapping theorem (the square-root function is continuous on $(0
,\infty)$, and $S_n/n > 0$ a.s.),
$$\sqrt{\frac{S_n}{n}} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \sqrt{1} = 1.$$

**Step 2: Independence.** The random variable $X_0$ is independent of $\{X_1, X_
2, \dots\}$, hence independent of $S_n$ for every $n$, and therefore independent
 of the limit $\sqrt{S_n/n} \to 1$.

**Step 3: Almost-sure convergence of $Y_n$.** Since $\sqrt{S_n/n} \to 1$ a.s. an
d $X_0$ is a fixed (in $n$) random variable,
$$Y_n = \frac{X_0}{\sqrt{S_n/n}} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \frac{X_0}{1} = X_0 \
quad \text{as } n \to \infty.$$

Almost-sure convergence implies convergence in distribution, so
$$Y_n \xrightarrow{d} X_0 \sim N(0,1).$$

**Conclusion.** The limiting distribution of $Y_n$ as $n \to \infty$ is the **st
andard normal distribution $N(0,1)$**, with distribution function
$$\Phi(y) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{-\infty}^{y} e^{-t^2/2}\, dt.$$
