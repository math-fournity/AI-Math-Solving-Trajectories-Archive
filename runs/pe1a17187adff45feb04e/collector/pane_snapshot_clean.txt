## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_C \frac{2z^2-5}{(z^2+1)(z^2+4)}\
,dz$, where $C$ is the upper semicircular arc $|z|=R$, $\operatorname{Im}z>0$ (p
arametrized by $z=Re^{i\theta}$, $0\le\theta\le\pi$).

**Key estimate.** For $|z|=R\to\infty$, expand the integrand:

$$f(z)=\frac{2z^2-5}{(z^2+1)(z^2+4)}=\frac{2z^2-5}{z^4+5z^2+4}=\frac{2}{z^2}\cdo
t\frac{1-\tfrac{5}{2z^2}}{1+\tfrac{5}{z^2}+\tfrac{4}{z^4}}=\frac{2}{z^2}+O\!\lef
t(\frac{1}{z^4}\right).$$

**Split the integral.** Write

$$\int_C f(z)\,dz=\int_C \frac{2}{z^2}\,dz+\int_C O\!\left(\frac{1}{z^4}\right)d
z.$$

**Remainder term.** On $C$, $\left|O(1/z^4)\right|=O(1/R^4)$ and $|dz|=R\,d\thet
a$, so

$$\left|\int_C O\!\left(\frac{1}{z^4}\right)dz\right|\le \frac{C}{R^4}\cdot \pi
R = \frac{C\pi}{R^3}\xrightarrow{R\to\infty}0.$$

**Leading term.** With $z=Re^{i\theta}$, $dz=iRe^{i\theta}\,d\theta$:

$$\int_C \frac{2}{z^2}\,dz=\int_0^\pi \frac{2}{R^2e^{2i\theta}}\,iRe^{i\theta}\,
d\theta=\frac{2i}{R}\int_0^\pi e^{-i\theta}\,d\theta.$$

Compute the inner integral:

$$\int_0^\pi e^{-i\theta}\,d\theta=\frac{e^{-i\theta}}{-i}\bigg|_0^\pi=\frac{e^{
-i\pi}-1}{-i}=\frac{-1-1}{-i}=\frac{-2}{-i}=-2i.$$

Therefore

$$\int_C \frac{2}{z^2}\,dz=\frac{2i}{R}\cdot(-2i)=\frac{-4i^2}{R}=\frac{4}{R}\xr
ightarrow{R\to\infty}0.$$

**Conclusion.** Both pieces vanish as $R\to\infty$, hence

$$\boxed{\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_C \frac{2z^2-5}{(z^2+1)(z^2+4)}\,dz=0.}$$

*Remark.* The decay $f(z)=O(1/z^2)$ combined with arc length $O(R)$ gives the in
tegral $O(1/R)\to 0$. (Had $C$ been the *closed* contour—arc plus the real segme
nt $[-R,R]$—the limit would instead be $2\pi i$ times the sum of residues at $z=
i$ and $z=2i$, namely $-\pi/6$; but the problem specifies only the arc.)
