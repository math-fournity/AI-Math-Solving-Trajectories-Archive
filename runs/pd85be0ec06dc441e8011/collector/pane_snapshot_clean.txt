## Solution

We determine all functions $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfying

$$f(x - f(y)) = 1 - x - y \qquad \text{for all } x, y \in \mathbb{R}. \tag{P}$$

### Step 1. $f$ is injective

Suppose $f(y_1) = f(y_2)$. Then for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$, the left-hand side
s of $P(x,y_1)$ and $P(x,y_2)$ coincide:

$$f(x - f(y_1)) = f(x - f(y_2)).$$

Hence the right-hand sides are equal:

$$1 - x - y_1 = 1 - x - y_2 \implies y_1 = y_2.$$

So $f$ is injective.

### Step 2. $f$ is surjective

For any real number $t$, choose $x = 1 - y - t$ (with $y$ arbitrary, say $y = 0$
). Then $P(x, y)$ gives

$$f(x - f(y)) = 1 - x - y = t.$$

Thus every $t \in \mathbb{R}$ lies in the range of $f$, so $f$ is surjective.

### Step 3. Determine $f$

Since $f$ is surjective, there exists $c \in \mathbb{R}$ with $f(c) = 0$. Substi
tute $y = c$ into $(P)$:

$$f(x - f(c)) = f(x - 0) = f(x) = 1 - x - c.$$

Therefore

$$f(x) = (1 - c) - x \qquad \text{for all } x \in \mathbb{R}. \tag{$\ast$}$$

In particular, applying $(\ast)$ at $x = c$ and using $f(c) = 0$:

$$0 = f(c) = (1 - c) - c = 1 - 2c \implies c = \tfrac{1}{2}.$$

Substituting back into $(\ast)$:

$$\boxed{f(x) = \tfrac{1}{2} - x}.$$

### Step 4. Verification

With $f(x) = \tfrac{1}{2} - x$:

$$f(x - f(y)) = f\!\left(x - \left(\tfrac{1}{2} - y\right)\right) = f\!\left(x +
 y - \tfrac{1}{2}\right) = \tfrac{1}{2} - \left(x + y - \tfrac{1}{2}\right) = 1
- x - y. \checkmark$$

The equation is satisfied for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$, and the derivation abov
e shows this is the unique solution.
