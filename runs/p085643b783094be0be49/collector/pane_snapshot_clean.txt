## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle I = \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\sin^3(2x)}{x}\,dx$.

**Step 1: Reduce via substitution.** Let $u = 2x$, so $du = 2\,dx$, $x = u/2$. T
hen
$$
I = \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\sin^3 u}{u/2}\cdot\frac{du}{2} = \int_{0}^{\infty}
\frac{\sin^3 u}{u}\,du.
$$

**Step 2: Linearize the cube.** Using the standard identity
$$
\sin^3 \theta = \frac{3\sin\theta - \sin(3\theta)}{4},
$$
we get
$$
I = \frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{3\sin u - \sin(3u)}{u}\,du = \frac{1}{4}\
left(3\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin u}{u}\,du - \int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(3u)}{u
}\,du\right).
$$

**Step 3: Apply the Dirichlet integral.** For $a > 0$,
$$
\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\sin(au)}{u}\,du = \frac{\pi}{2},
$$
which follows from the substitution $t = au$ reducing it to $\int_0^\infty \frac
{\sin t}{t}\,dt = \frac{\pi}{2}$ (the classical Dirichlet integral, valid by, e.
g., differentiating the Laplace transform $\int_0^\infty e^{-st}\frac{\sin t}{t}
\,dt = \arctan(1/s)$ and taking $s \to 0^+$).

Thus both integrals equal $\frac{\pi}{2}$:
$$
I = \frac{1}{4}\left(3\cdot\frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{\pi}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{4}\cd
ot\pi = \frac{\pi}{4}.
$$

**Result:**
$$
\boxed{\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\sin^3(2x)}{x}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{4}}
$$
