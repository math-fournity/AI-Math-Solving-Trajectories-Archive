The statement is **TRUE**.

## Proof

Let $r = \sqrt{a^2+b^2}$.

**Case 1: $r \neq 0$ (i.e., $(a,b) \neq (0,0)$).**

Perform an orthogonal change of variables (a rotation) in the $(x,y)$-plane. Def
ine new coordinates $(u,v)$ by
$$u = \frac{ax+by}{r}, \qquad v = \frac{-bx+ay}{r}.$$
This is a rotation (the matrix $\frac{1}{r}\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ -b & a\end{p
matrix}$ is orthogonal with determinant $1$), so:
- $ax + by = r\,u$,
- the unit disk $S = \{x^2+y^2 \le 1\}$ maps to $\{u^2+v^2 \le 1\}$,
- the Jacobian is $|\det| = 1$, so $dx\,dy = du\,dv$.

Therefore
$$\iint_S f(ax+by+c)\,dx\,dy = \iint_{u^2+v^2\le 1} f(r\,u + c)\,du\,dv.$$

Now integrate out $v$. For each fixed $u \in [-1,1]$, the variable $v$ ranges ov
er $\bigl[-\sqrt{1-u^2},\,\sqrt{1-u^2}\bigr]$, giving a factor of $2\sqrt{1-u^2}
$:
$$= \int_{-1}^{1} \left(\int_{-\sqrt{1-u^2}}^{\sqrt{1-u^2}} dv\right) f(r\,u+c)\
,du = \int_{-1}^{1} 2\sqrt{1-u^2}\,f(r\,u+c)\,du.$$
Substituting $r = \sqrt{a^2+b^2}$:
$$= 2\int_{-1}^{1} \sqrt{1-u^2}\,f\!\left(u\sqrt{a^2+b^2}+c\right)du,$$
which is exactly the right-hand side.

**Case 2: $r = 0$ (i.e., $a = b = 0$).**

The left side becomes $\iint_S f(c)\,dx\,dy = \pi\, f(c)$ (area of the unit disk
 is $\pi$).

The right side becomes $2\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{1-u^2}\,f(c)\,du = 2f(c)\int_{-1}^{1
}\sqrt{1-u^2}\,du = 2f(c)\cdot\frac{\pi}{2} = \pi\,f(c)$.

Both sides agree.

## Conclusion

In all cases the identity holds. The key mechanism is that rotating coordinates
converts the linear form $ax+by$ into $u\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$, and integrating the rot
ation-invariant integrand over the cross-sectional chord of the unit disk produc
es the factor $2\sqrt{1-u^2}$.
