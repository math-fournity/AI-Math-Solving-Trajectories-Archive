## Proof

Let $\alpha(s)$ be the curve parametrized by arc length, and let $N$ denote the
unit normal vector field of $M$. The hypothesis is that $N$ is constant along $\
alpha$:

$$N(\alpha(s)) = N_0 \quad \text{for some fixed unit vector } N_0 \text{ and all
 } s.$$

---

### (1) $\alpha$ is an asymptotic curve.

Since $\alpha$ lies on $M$, its velocity $\alpha'(s)$ is tangent to $M$ at $\alp
ha(s)$, hence orthogonal to the surface normal:

$$N_0 \cdot \alpha'(s) = 0.$$

Differentiating with respect to $s$ (and using that $N_0$ is constant):

$$N_0 \cdot \alpha''(s) = 0.$$

The tangent plane $T_{\alpha(s)}M$ is precisely the set of vectors orthogonal to
 $N(\alpha(s)) = N_0$. Therefore $\alpha''(s) \in T_{\alpha(s)}M$; the accelerat
ion is everywhere tangent to $M$. By definition, $\alpha$ is an asymptotic curve
. $\checkmark$

---

### (2) A plane contains $\alpha$.

From $N_0 \cdot \alpha'(s) = 0$ and the constancy of $N_0$:

$$\frac{d}{ds}\bigl(N_0 \cdot \alpha(s)\bigr) = N_0 \cdot \alpha'(s) = 0.$$

Integrating, there exists a constant $c$ such that

$$N_0 \cdot \alpha(s) = c \quad \text{for all } s.$$

This is the equation of the plane $\Pi = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^3 : N_0 \cdot x = c\
}$, which has normal $N_0$. Hence $\alpha \subseteq \Pi$. $\checkmark$

---

### (3) The Gauss curvature of $M$ vanishes at each point of $\alpha$.

The shape operator $S_p : T_pM \to T_pM$ is defined by $S_p(v) = -dN_p(v)$, wher
e $dN_p$ is the differential of the Gauss map $N : M \to S^2$. The Gauss curvatu
re is $K(p) = \det S_p$.

Since $N$ is constant along $\alpha$, its derivative along $\alpha$ vanishes:

$$dN_{\alpha(s)}\bigl(\alpha'(s)\bigr) = \frac{d}{ds}N(\alpha(s)) = \frac{d}{ds}
N_0 = 0.$$

Therefore

$$S_{\alpha(s)}\bigl(\alpha'(s)\bigr) = -dN_{\alpha(s)}\bigl(\alpha'(s)\bigr) =
0.$$

Since $\alpha'(s) \neq 0$ (the curve is regular), the shape operator $S_{\alpha(
s)}$ has a nontrivial kernel; in particular $S_{\alpha(s)}$ is singular. Hence

$$K(\alpha(s)) = \det S_{\alpha(s)} = 0 \quad \text{for all } s,$$

so the Gauss curvature vanishes at every point of $\alpha$. $\checkmark$

---

**Remark.** The three conclusions are tightly linked: the constancy of $N$ along
 $\alpha$ simultaneously forces the acceleration to be tangent (asymptoticity),
the curve into a plane (integrating $N_0 \cdot \alpha' = 0$), and a zero eigenva
lue of the shape operator (vanishing Gauss curvature). In classical language, $\
alpha'(s)$ is a principal direction with principal curvature $0$ at each point o
f $\alpha$.
