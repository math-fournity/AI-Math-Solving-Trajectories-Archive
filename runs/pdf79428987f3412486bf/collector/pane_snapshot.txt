g on the image of $(a,b,c) \mapsto (a+1/a, b+1/b, c+1/c)$ subject to $abc=1$.

**Answer:** The polynomials are exactly the multiples of $g(x,y,z) = x^2 + y^2 +
 z^2 - xyz - 4$.

---

**Proof.**

**Step 1: The polynomial $g(x,y,z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - xyz - 4$ vanishes on the
image.**

Suppose $abc = 1$ with $a,b,c \neq 0$. Then $c = 1/(ab)$, so
$$z = c + \frac{1}{c} = ab + \frac{1}{ab}.$$
Set $x = a + 1/a$, $y = b + 1/b$. Compute:
$$xy = \left(a+\frac{1}{a}\right)\left(b+\frac{1}{b}\right) = ab + \frac{a}{b} +
 \frac{b}{a} + \frac{1}{ab} = \underbrace{\left(ab+\frac{1}{ab}\right)}_{z} + \u
nderbrace{\left(\frac{a}{b}+\frac{b}{a}\right)}_{w}.$$
So $w = xy - z$. Also $x^2 = a^2 + 2 + 1/a^2$ gives $a^2 + 1/a^2 = x^2 - 2$, and
 similarly $b^2 + 1/b^2 = y^2 - 2$. Now:
$$zw = \left(ab+\frac{1}{ab}\right)\left(\frac{a}{b}+\frac{b}{a}\right) = a^2 +
b^2 + \frac{1}{a^2} + \frac{1}{b^2} = (x^2-2)+(y^2-2) = x^2+y^2-4.$$
Substituting $w = xy - z$:
$$z(xy - z) = x^2 + y^2 - 4 \implies z^2 - xyz + x^2 + y^2 - 4 = 0.$$
Thus $g(x,y,z) = 0$. $\checkmark$

**Step 2: $g$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{R}$.**

View $g$ as a quadratic in $z$: $z^2 - (xy)z + (x^2+y^2-4)$. Its discriminant is
$$x^2y^2 - 4(x^2+y^2-4) = (x^2-4)(y^2-4) = (x-2)(x+2)(y-2)(y+2),$$
which is not a perfect square in $\mathbb{R}[x,y]$ (it has four distinct simple
factors). By Gauss's lemma, $g$ is irreducible in $\mathbb{R}[x,y,z]$.

**Step 3: The vanishing ideal of the image is exactly $(g)$.**

Since $g$ is irreducible in the UFD $\mathbb{R}[x,y,z]$, the ideal $(g)$ is prim
e. The variety $V(g)$ is an irreducible surface of dimension $2$.

Consider the parametrization $\phi: (\mathbb{R}^*)^2 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ given by
$$\phi(a,b) = \left(a+\tfrac{1}{a},\; b+\tfrac{1}{b},\; ab+\tfrac{1}{ab}\right).
$$
Its image lies in $V(g)$ by Step 1. The Jacobian of $\phi$ has rank $2$ at gener
ic points (e.g., at $a=2, b=1$: $\partial\phi/\partial a = (3/4, 0, 3/2)$, $\par
tial\phi/\partial b = (0, 0, 3/2)$, which are linearly independent). So the imag
e is a smooth $2$-dimensional submanifold of the irreducible surface $V(g)$, hen
ce contains a Euclidean-open subset of $V(g)$, and is therefore **Zariski dense*
* in $V(g)$.

Since $(g)$ is prime and $V(g)$ has smooth real points (e.g., $(2,2,2)$), the re
al Nullstellensatz gives $I(V(g)) = (g)$. As the image is Zariski dense in $V(g)
$:
$$I(\text{image}) = I(V(g)) = (g).$$

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

A polynomial $f \in \mathbb{R}[x,y,z]$ vanishes on the image if and only if $f \
in (g)$, i.e., $g \mid f$. Therefore:
$$\boxed{f(x,y,z) = \left(x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - xyz - 4\right)\cdot h(x,y,z)}$$
for an arbitrary polynomial $h \in \mathbb{R}[x,y,z]$.
