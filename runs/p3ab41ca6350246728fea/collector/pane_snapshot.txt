Looking at this problem, I need to evaluate the limit of a ratio of two integral
s as $\beta \to +\infty$, where the exponential $e^{-\beta t}$ concentrates the
integrals near $t = 0$.

## Proof

Let $f(t) = I_0(\sqrt{t(2-t)})$ and $\phi(t) = t - 1$. We seek:

$$L = \lim_{\beta \to +\infty} \frac{\int_0^2 f(t)\,\phi(t)\,e^{-\beta t}\,dt}{\
int_0^2 f(t)\,e^{-\beta t}\,dt}.$$

**Key observations.**

1. **$f$ is continuous and positive at $t=0$.** Since $I_0(0) = 1$ and $\sqrt{t(
2-t)} \to 0$ as $t \to 0$, we have $f(0) = I_0(0) = 1 > 0$. Also $f$ is continuo
us on $[0,2]$ (composition of continuous functions).

2. **The weight $e^{-\beta t}$ concentrates at $t = 0$.** As $\beta \to +\infty$
, $e^{-\beta t}$ is maximized at $t=0$ and decays exponentially for $t > 0$.

**Laplace's method (boundary minimum with nonzero derivative).**

The phase function $g(t) = t$ attains its minimum on $[0,2]$ at the boundary poi
nt $t = 0$, with $g'(0) = 1 > 0$. The standard Laplace asymptotic formula for a
boundary minimum gives:

$$\int_0^2 h(t)\,e^{-\beta t}\,dt \sim \frac{h(0)}{\beta\,g'(0)} = \frac{h(0)}{\
beta}, \qquad \beta \to +\infty,$$

for any function $h$ continuous at $t = 0$.

**Applying to the denominator** ($h = f$):

$$D(\beta) = \int_0^2 f(t)\,e^{-\beta t}\,dt \sim \frac{f(0)}{\beta} = \frac{1}{
\beta}.$$

**Applying to the numerator** ($h = f \cdot \phi$):

$$N(\beta) = \int_0^2 f(t)\,\phi(t)\,e^{-\beta t}\,dt \sim \frac{f(0)\,\phi(0)}{
\beta} = \frac{1 \cdot (0-1)}{\beta} = \frac{-1}{\beta}.$$

**Verification via direct expansion.** Near $t = 0$:
- $f(t) = I_0(\sqrt{t(2-t)}) = 1 + \frac{t(2-t)}{4} + O(t^2) = 1 + \frac{t}{2} +
 O(t^2)$,
- $f(t)\,\phi(t) = \left(1 + \frac{t}{2} + \cdots\right)(t-1) = -1 + \frac{t}{2}
 + O(t^2)$.

Extending the integration to $[0,\infty)$ (the error is exponentially small sinc
e $e^{-\beta t} \leq e^{-\beta \cdot 2}$ for $t \geq 2$):

$$D(\beta) = \int_0^\infty \left(1 + \tfrac{t}{2} + \cdots\right) e^{-\beta t}\,
dt = \frac{1}{\beta} + \frac{1}{2\beta^2} + O(\beta^{-3}),$$

$$N(\beta) = \int_0^\infty \left(-1 + \tfrac{t}{2} + \cdots\right) e^{-\beta t}\
,dt = -\frac{1}{\beta} + \frac{1}{2\beta^2} + O(\beta^{-3}).$$

**Taking the ratio:**

$$\frac{N(\beta)}{D(\beta)} = \frac{-\frac{1}{\beta} + \frac{1}{2\beta^2} + O(\b
eta^{-3})}{\frac{1}{\beta} + \frac{1}{2\beta^2} + O(\beta^{-3})} = \frac{-1 + \f
rac{1}{2\beta} + O(\beta^{-2})}{1 + \frac{1}{2\beta} + O(\beta^{-2})} \xrightarr
ow{\beta \to +\infty} -1.$$

**Interpretation.** The ratio $N(\beta)/D(\beta)$ is a weighted average of $\phi
(t) = t-1$ with weight $f(t)\,e^{-\beta t}$. As $\beta \to +\infty$, this weight
 concentrates at $t = 0$ (becoming an approximate delta function at the origin a
fter normalization). Therefore the weighted average converges to $\phi(0) = 0 -
1 = -1$.

$$\boxed{L = -1}$$
