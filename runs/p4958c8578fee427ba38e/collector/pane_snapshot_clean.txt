## Proof

The answer is **No**. We provide a counterexample.

**Counterexample: $M = L \times \mathbb{R}$, where $L$ is the open long line.**

Let $L$ denote the open long line — the linearly ordered topological space obtai
ned from $\omega_1$ copies of $[0,1)$ glued in lexicographic order (without the
initial point). $L$ is a connected, Hausdorff, 1-dimensional manifold locally ho
meomorphic to $\mathbb{R}$, but it is **not second countable**.

---

**$M$ is a connected orientable 2-manifold, Hausdorff, locally homeomorphic to $
\mathbb{R}^2$:**

- **Hausdorff:** product of Hausdorff spaces.
- **Locally Euclidean:** every $(x,y) \in M$ has a neighborhood $U \times V$ whe
re $U \subset L$ is an open interval $\cong \mathbb{R}$ and $V \subset \mathbb{R
}$ is an open interval, so $U \times V \cong \mathbb{R}^2$.
- **Connected:** product of connected spaces.
- **Orientable:** $L$ carries a natural orientation from its linear order, $\mat
hbb{R}$ is orientable, and the product of orientable manifolds is orientable.

---

**$\pi_1(M)$ is a finitely generated free group (in fact, trivial):**

**Key fact:** Every compact subset $K \subset L$ is contained in a closed interv
al $[a,b] \subset L$, which is homeomorphic to $[0,1] \subset \mathbb{R}$. This
holds because $L$ is a linearly ordered topological space: compact subsets are c
losed and order-bounded, and every bounded interval in $L$ is homeomorphic to a
real interval.

Let $f: S^1 \to M$ be any loop. Since $S^1$ is compact, $f(S^1)$ is compact in $
M$. Its projection onto $L$ is compact, hence contained in some $[a,b] \cong [0,
1]$. Therefore:

$$f(S^1) \subseteq [a,b] \times \mathbb{R} \cong [0,1] \times \mathbb{R} \hookri
ghtarrow \mathbb{R}^2.$$

Since $\mathbb{R}^2$ is simply connected, $f$ is null-homotopic in $[a,b]\times\
mathbb{R}$, hence in $M$. Every loop is null-homotopic, so $\pi_1(M) = 0$, the f
ree group $F_0$ of rank 0 — finitely generated and free. ✓

---

**$M$ is not homeomorphic to any closed surface with finitely many punctures:**

A closed surface (compact 2-manifold without boundary) is second countable. Remo
ving finitely many points preserves second countability. Thus every "closed surf
ace with finitely many punctures" is second countable.

However, $M = L \times \mathbb{R}$ is **not second countable**: $L$ contains unc
ountably many pairwise disjoint open intervals (one for each countable ordinal),
 so $L$ is not second countable, and a product with a non-second-countable facto
r is not second countable.

Therefore $M$ cannot be homeomorphic to any closed surface with finitely many pu
nctures.

---

**Conclusion:** $M = L \times \mathbb{R}$ is a connected, orientable, Hausdorff
2-manifold, locally homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^2$, with $\pi_1(M) \cong F_0$ a
finitely generated free group, yet $M$ is not homeomorphic to any closed surface
 with finitely many punctures. The answer is **No**.
