**Answer: Yes.** Such a map $f: M \to N$ exists (and is unique).

## Proof

The key ingredient is the **gluing exact sequence**: since $(s,t)=A$, for every
$A$-module $M$ the sequence
$$0 \longrightarrow M \xrightarrow{\;\alpha\;} M_s \oplus M_t \xrightarrow{\;\be
ta\;} M_{st}$$
is exact, where $\alpha(m)=(m/1,\,m/1)$ and $\beta(x,y)=x-y$ (both $x,y$ viewed
in $M_{st}$ via the natural localization maps). Equivalently, $M \cong M_s \time
s_{M_{st}} M_t$ (fiber product). We prove this, then use it to construct $f$.

### Step 1. Injectivity of $\alpha$

If $\alpha(m)=0$, then $m/1=0$ in $M_s$ and $m/1=0$ in $M_t$, so $s^p m=0$ and $
t^q m=0$ for some $p,q\ge 0$. Since $(s,t)=A$ implies $(s^p,t^q)=A$, write $1=a\
,s^p+b\,t^q$. Then
$$m=(a\,s^p+b\,t^q)\,m=a\,(s^p m)+b\,(t^q m)=0. \qquad\checkmark$$

### Step 2. $\ker\beta \subseteq \operatorname{im}\alpha$

Clearly $\beta\circ\alpha=0$. Conversely, let $(x,y)\in\ker\beta$, i.e. $x=y$ in
 $M_{st}$. Write $x=a/s^p\in M_s$, $y=b/t^q\in M_t$ with $a,b\in M$. Equality in
 $M_{st}$ gives $(st)^n(t^q a - s^p b)=0$ for some $n\ge 0$, i.e.
$$c:=s^n t^{n+q}a = s^{n+p}t^n b.$$

Since $(s,t)=A$, also $(s^{n+p},t^{n+q})=A$: write $1=u\,s^{n+p}+v\,t^{n+q}$ wit
h $u,v\in A$. **Define**
$$m:=u\,s^n a + v\,t^n b \;\in M.$$

**Check $m/1 = x$ in $M_s$:** compute
$$s^{n+p}m = s^{n+p}(u\,s^n a+v\,t^n b)=u\,s^{2n+p}a+v\,s^{n+p}t^n b = u\,s^{2n+
p}a+v\,c = u\,s^{2n+p}a+v\,s^n t^{n+q}a = s^n a\,(u\,s^{n+p}+v\,t^{n+q})=s^n a.$
$
So $s^n(s^p m - a)=s^{n+p}m - s^n a = 0$, hence $m/1 = a/s^p = x$ in $M_s$. $\ch
eckmark$

**Check $m/1 = y$ in $M_t$:** similarly,
$$t^{n+q}m = t^{n+q}(u\,s^n a+v\,t^n b)=u\,s^n t^{n+q}a+v\,t^{2n+q}b = u\,c+v\,t
^{2n+q}b = u\,s^{n+p}t^n b+v\,t^{2n+q}b = t^n b\,(u\,s^{n+p}+v\,t^{n+q})=t^n b.$
$
So $t^n(t^q m - b)=0$, hence $m/1=b/t^q=y$ in $M_t$. $\checkmark$

Thus $\alpha(m)=(x,y)$, proving $\ker\beta\subseteq\operatorname{im}\alpha$, and
 the sequence is exact. Consequently
$$M \;\cong\; M_s\times_{M_{st}} M_t, \qquad N \;\cong\; N_s\times_{N_{st}} N_t.
$$

### Step 3. Construction of $f$

For $m\in M$, set $(x,y):=\alpha(m)=(m/1,m/1)\in M_s\oplus M_t$, so $x=y$ in $M_
{st}$. Consider $(f_s(x),\,f_t(y))\in N_s\oplus N_t$. Its image under $\beta_N$
is
$$f_s(x)-f_t(y)\quad\text{in }N_{st}.$$
By the commutativity hypotheses, the image of $f_s(x)$ in $N_{st}$ equals $f_{st
}(\text{image of }x\text{ in }M_{st})$, and the image of $f_t(y)$ in $N_{st}$ eq
uals $f_{st}(\text{image of }y\text{ in }M_{st})$. Since $x=y$ in $M_{st}$, thes
e are equal, so $(f_s(x),f_t(y))\in\ker\beta_N$. By exactness (Step 2, applied t
o $N$), there is a **unique** $n\in N$ with $n/1=f_s(x)$ in $N_s$ and $n/1=f_t(y
)$ in $N_t$. **Define** $f(m):=n$.

### Step 4. $f$ is $A$-linear

If $\alpha(m_i)=(x_i,y_i)$ for $i=1,2$, then $\alpha(m_1+m_2)=(x_1+x_2,\,y_1+y_2
)$, and
$$f_s(x_1+x_2)=f_s(x_1)+f_s(x_2),\qquad f_t(y_1+y_2)=f_t(y_1)+f_t(y_2),$$
so the unique element of $N$ corresponding to $(f_s(x_1+x_2),f_t(y_1+y_2))$ is $
f(m_1)+f(m_2)$; hence $f(m_1+m_2)=f(m_1)+f(m_2)$. Likewise $f_s(a\,x)=a\,f_s(x)$
 for $a\in A$ (since $f_s$ is $A_s$-linear, hence $A$-linear), giving $f(a\,m)=a
\,f(m)$. So $f$ is an $A$-module homomorphism.

### Step 5. The diagrams commute

For $m\in M$ with $x=m/1\in M_s$: by construction $f(m)/1=f_s(x)=f_s(m/1)$ in $N
_s$, so
$$\begin{CD} M @>f>> N \\ @VVV @VVV \\ M_s @>f_s>> N_s \end{CD}$$
commutes. The same argument with $t$ gives the $t$-diagram. For the $st$-diagram
: the map $M\to M_{st}$ factors as $M\to M_s\to M_{st}$, so
$$\text{image of }f(m)\text{ in }N_{st} = \text{image of }f_s(m/1)\text{ in }N_{
st} = f_{st}(\text{image of }m/1\text{ in }M_{st}),$$
where the last equality is the given commutativity of the $s$-to-$st$ square. He
nce the $st$-diagram commutes as well.

### Step 6. Uniqueness

If $f,g:M\to N$ both make the $s$- and $t$-diagrams commute, then for every $m\i
n M$:
$$f(m)/1 = f_s(m/1) = g(m)/1 \;\text{ in } N_s, \qquad f(m)/1 = f_t(m/1) = g(m)/
1 \;\text{ in } N_t.$$
By injectivity of $N\to N_s\oplus N_t$ (Step 1), $f(m)=g(m)$. Thus $f=g$.

---

**Conclusion.** There exists a **unique** $A$-module homomorphism $f:M\to N$ mak
ing all the relevant localization diagrams commute. $\blacksquare$
