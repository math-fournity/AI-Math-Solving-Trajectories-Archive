## Proof

We prove that $K = \bar{F}$.

**Setup.** Work inside a fixed algebraic closure $\bar{F}$ of $F$. For each $f \
in F[x]$, let $K_f \subseteq \bar{F}$ denote the splitting field of $f$ over $F$
, i.e., the subfield of $\bar{F}$ generated by $F$ and all roots of $f$ in $\bar
{F}$. Set
$$K = \bigcup_{f \in F[x]} K_f \subseteq \bar{F}.$$

**Step 1: $K$ is a subfield of $\bar{F}$.** Each $K_f$ is a subfield of $\bar{F}
$, and the union of any family of subfields of a common ambient field is itself
a subfield (sums, products, inverses of elements lying in some $K_f$ stay in tha
t same $K_f$). Hence $K$ is a field with $F \subseteq K \subseteq \bar{F}$.

**Step 2: $K \subseteq \bar{F}$.** Every $K_f$ is a splitting field, hence a fin
ite (and therefore algebraic) extension of $F$. Thus every element of $K_f$ is a
lgebraic over $F$, so $K_f \subseteq \bar{F}$. Taking the union gives $K \subset
eq \bar{F}$.

**Step 3: $\bar{F} \subseteq K$.** Let $\alpha \in \bar{F}$ be arbitrary. By def
inition of algebraic closure, $\alpha$ is algebraic over $F$, so it has a minima
l polynomial $m_\alpha(x) \in F[x]$. Since $\alpha$ is a root of $m_\alpha$, and
 the splitting field $K_{m_\alpha}$ contains **all** roots of $m_\alpha$ in $\ba
r{F}$, in particular $\alpha \in K_{m_\alpha}$. Therefore
$$\alpha \in K_{m_\alpha} \subseteq \bigcup_{f \in F[x]} K_f = K.$$
Since $\alpha \in \bar{F}$ was arbitrary, $\bar{F} \subseteq K$.

**Conclusion.** Combining Steps 2 and 3,
$$K \subseteq \bar{F} \quad\text{and}\quad \bar{F} \subseteq K,$$
so $K = \bar{F}$. $\blacksquare$
