## Proof

We classify all extensions of $\mathbb{Z}$ by $\mathbb{Z}$, i.e., short exact se
quences
$$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{i} E \xrightarrow{\pi} \mathbb{Z} \to 0,$$
up to isomorphism of extensions, and then count the non-abelian ones.

### Step 1: Every extension splits.

The quotient $\mathbb{Z}$ is a free group (free on one generator). By the univer
sal property of free groups, any surjection $\pi: E \to \mathbb{Z}$ admits a gro
up-theoretic section $s: \mathbb{Z} \to E$ with $\pi \circ s = \mathrm{id}$. Hen
ce every extension of $\mathbb{Z}$ by $\mathbb{Z}$ splits, and
$$E \cong \mathbb{Z} \rtimes_\varphi \mathbb{Z}$$
for some homomorphism $\varphi: \mathbb{Z} \to \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z})$.

### Step 2: Classify the actions.

We have $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}) = \{\mathrm{id}, \mathrm{neg}\} \cong \mathbb{
Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, since the only automorphisms of $\mathbb{Z}$ are $n \mapsto n$
and $n \mapsto -n$.

A homomorphism $\varphi: \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is determined by
 $\varphi(1) \in \{0, 1\}$ (writing $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ additively). There
are exactly **two** such homomorphisms:

- **Trivial action** ($\varphi(1) = 0$): $\varphi(n) = \mathrm{id}$ for all $n$.
 This gives the direct product $E = \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$, which is **ab
elian**.

- **Nontrivial action** ($\varphi(1) = 1$): $\varphi(n) = \mathrm{neg}$ if $n$ i
s odd, $\varphi(n) = \mathrm{id}$ if $n$ is even. The generator of the quotient
acts by negation on the kernel. This gives the semidirect product $E = \mathbb{Z
} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}$, which is the infinite dihedral group $D_\infty$. This gro
up is **non-abelian**: if $a$ generates the kernel and $b$ generates the quotien
t, then $bab^{-1} = a^{-1} \neq a$.

### Step 3: These two are non-isomorphic.

The direct product $\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$ is abelian, while $\mathbb{Z}
\rtimes \mathbb{Z} \cong D_\infty$ is non-abelian, so they are not isomorphic. M
oreover, Step 1–2 shows these are the **only** two extensions up to isomorphism.

### Step 4: Count the non-abelian extensions.

Of the two extensions:
- $\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$ — abelian,
- $\mathbb{Z} \rtimes \mathbb{Z} \cong D_\infty$ — non-abelian,

exactly **one** is non-abelian.
