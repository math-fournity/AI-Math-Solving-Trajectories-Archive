## Setup

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a Lie $\mathbb{K}$-algebra of dimension $2$ with basis $\{
e_1, e_2\}$. By bilinearity and the alternating property $[x,x]=0$, the only pos
sibly-nonzero structure constant is determined by

$$[e_1, e_2] = a\,e_1 + b\,e_2, \qquad a,b\in\mathbb{K}.$$

**Jacobi is automatic in dimension $2$.** For any three elements chosen from a $
2$-dimensional space, at least two coincide, and the Jacobi identity $\sum_{\tex
t{cyc}}[x,[y,z]]=0$ vanishes trivially whenever two of $x,y,z$ are equal (using
$[x,x]=0$ and bilinearity). Hence **every** pair $(a,b)\in\mathbb{K}^2$ defines
a valid Lie algebra.

So the set of isomorphism classes is the set of orbits of $\mathbb{K}^2$ under t
he action of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{K})$ induced by basis changes.

---

## Case 1: $(a,b)=(0,0)$ — the abelian algebra

Here $[\,x,y\,]=0$ for all $x,y$. The algebra is **commutative** (abelian). This
 is a single isomorphism class, characterized by

$$[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=0, \qquad Z(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{g}\ (\dim 2).$
$

---

## Case 2: $(a,b)\neq(0,0)$ — all non-abelian algebras are isomorphic

Set $v := a\,e_1+b\,e_2\neq 0$. Since $v\neq 0$, extend it to a basis $\{v, w\}$
 of $\mathfrak{g}$, where $w = c\,e_1 + d\,e_2$ with $\det\!\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\
 b&d\end{pmatrix}=ad-bc\neq 0$. Then

$$[v,w] = [a\,e_1+b\,e_2,\; c\,e_1+d\,e_2] = (ad-bc)\,[e_1,e_2] = (ad-bc)\,v.$$

Since $ad-bc\neq 0$, rescale the second basis vector: define

$$f_1 := v, \qquad f_2 := \frac{1}{ad-bc}\,w.$$

Then

$$\boxed{[f_1, f_2] = f_1}.$$

Thus **every** non-abelian $2$-dimensional Lie $\mathbb{K}$-algebra admits a bas
is $\{f_1,f_2\}$ in which the single bracket relation is $[f_1,f_2]=f_1$. Conseq
uently all non-abelian $2$-dimensional Lie $\mathbb{K}$-algebras are mutually is
omorphic. (The rescaling only divides by the nonzero scalar $ad-bc$, so this wor
ks over **any** field $\mathbb{K}$, including characteristic $2$.)

**Invariant distinguishing this class from Case 1.** For the algebra with $[f_1,
f_2]=f_1$:

- The derived algebra is $[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}] = \mathbb{K}f_1$, which is
 $1$-dimensional.
- The center: $x = \alpha f_1 + \beta f_2$ is central iff $[x,f_1]=0$ and $[x,f_
2]=0$. Now $[x,f_1]=\beta[f_2,f_1]=-\beta f_1$ and $[x,f_2]=\alpha[f_1,f_2]=\alp
ha f_1$, so both vanish iff $\alpha=\beta=0$. Hence $Z(\mathfrak{g})=0$.

Both invariants ($\dim[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=1$, $Z(\mathfrak{g})=0$) diffe
r from the abelian case ($\dim[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=0$, $Z(\mathfrak{g})=\
mathfrak{g}$), so the two classes are **not** isomorphic.

---

## Conclusion

There are **exactly two** isomorphism classes of $2$-dimensional Lie $\mathbb{K}
$-algebras:

1. **The abelian (commutative) algebra** $\mathfrak{g}_0$: $[e_1,e_2]=0$.
2. **The unique non-abelian algebra** $\mathfrak{g}_1$: there is a basis $\{e_1,
e_2\}$ with $[e_1,e_2]=e_1$ (equivalently, $[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]$ is $1$-
dimensional and the center is trivial).

These exhaust all possibilities, since every $2$-dimensional Lie algebra is eith
er abelian (Case 1) or non-abelian (Case 2), and within each case the algebras a
re isomorphic while the two cases are distinguished by the isomorphism invariant
s $\dim[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]$ and $\dim Z(\mathfrak{g})$.
