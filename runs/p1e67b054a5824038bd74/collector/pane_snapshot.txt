## Solution

**Setup.** Each small cube has 4 orange (coated) faces and 2 uncoated faces that
 share a common edge. Under a uniformly random rotation among the 24 cube rotati
ons, the pair of uncoated faces maps to a uniformly random pair of adjacent face
s among the 12 possible adjacent face-pairs on a cube.

**Justification:** The rotation group of the cube acts transitively on the 12 ed
ges. The stabilizer of any given edge (pair of adjacent faces) has order 2 (the
identity and the 180° rotation about the axis through that edge's midpoint and t
he opposite edge's midpoint). So the orbit has size 24/2 = 12, confirming unifor
m distribution over all 12 adjacent pairs.

**Position classification in the 3×3×3 grid:**

| Position type | Count | Exterior faces |
|---|---|---|
| Corner | 8 | 3 (mutually adjacent, meeting at a vertex) |
| Edge (non-corner) | 12 | 2 (adjacent) |
| Face-center | 6 | 1 |
| Interior | 1 | 0 |

Total: 8 + 12 + 6 + 1 = 27. ✓

**Corner positions (3 exterior faces):** The 3 exterior faces meet at a corner v
ertex; the 3 *interior* faces meet at the opposite corner and are likewise mutua
lly adjacent. The uncoated pair must avoid all 3 exterior faces, so both uncoate
d faces must be among the 3 interior faces. The number of adjacent pairs among 3
 mutually adjacent faces is C(3,2) = 3. Thus:

$$P_{\text{corner}} = \frac{3}{12} = \frac{1}{4}$$

**Edge positions (2 exterior faces):** The 2 exterior faces are adjacent. The 4
non-exterior faces include one pair of opposite faces (the two faces perpendicul
ar to the edge direction), so the adjacent pairs among the 4 non-exterior faces
number C(4,2) − 1 = 5. Thus:

$$P_{\text{edge}} = \frac{5}{12}$$

**Face-center positions (1 exterior face):** The uncoated pair must avoid the si
ngle exterior face. Of the 12 adjacent pairs, exactly 4 involve the exterior fac
e (since each face is adjacent to 4 others). So 12 − 4 = 8 pairs are valid. Thus
:

$$P_{\text{face}} = \frac{8}{12} = \frac{2}{3}$$

**Interior position (0 exterior faces):** Always satisfies the condition: $P_{\t
ext{interior}} = 1$.

**Total probability:**

$$P = \left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^8 \cdot \left(\frac{5}{12}\right)^{12} \cdot \lef
t(\frac{2}{3}\right)^6 \cdot 1$$

Computing each factor:

- $\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^8 = \frac{1}{2^{16}}$
- $\left(\frac{5}{12}\right)^{12} = \frac{5^{12}}{2^{24} \cdot 3^{12}}$
- $\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^6 = \frac{2^6}{3^6}$

Combining:

$$P = \frac{5^{12} \cdot 2^6}{2^{16} \cdot 2^{24} \cdot 3^{12} \cdot 3^6} = \fra
c{5^{12}}{2^{34} \cdot 3^{18}}$$

This matches the form $\frac{p^a}{q^b \cdot r^c}$ with:

- $p = 5,\; a = 12$
- $q = 2,\; b = 34$
- $r = 3,\; c = 18$

**Final answer:**

$$a + b + c + p + q + r = 12 + 34 + 18 + 5 + 2 + 3 = \boxed{74}$$
