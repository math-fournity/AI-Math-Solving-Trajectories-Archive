## Setting up the rotation per pivot

Since the square and triangle have the same side length $a$, each side of the sq
uare exactly covers one side of the triangle. The square doesn't "roll along" a
side — it immediately reaches the next vertex and pivots.

At each vertex of the triangle, the square rotates by the **sum of the exterior
angles** of both polygons:

- Exterior angle of the equilateral triangle: $180° - 60° = 120°$
- Exterior angle of the square: $180° - 90° = 90°$
- **Rotation per pivot: $120° + 90° = 210°$**

**Verification:** The square's side lies along one triangle side. After pivoting
, the next side of the square lies along the next triangle side. The triangle's
direction changes by $120°$; the square's own side-to-side turn adds $90°$; both
 in the same rotational sense, giving $210°$.

## Tracking one "full round"

side (below $AB$):
$$A_s=(0,0),\; B_s=(a,0),\; P_s=(a,-a),\; Q_s=(0,-a).$$

**Pivot 1 (at $B$, $210°$ CCW):** $B_s$ stays at $B$; side $BP$ lands on $BC$, s
o $P_s$ goes to $C$.

**Pivot 2 (at $C$, $210°$ CCW):** $P_s$ stays at $C$; side $PQ$ lands on $CA$, s
o $Q_s$ goes to $A$.

**Pivot 3 (at $A$, $210°$ CCW):** $Q_s$ stays at $A$; side $QA$ lands on $AB$, s
o $A_s$ goes to $B$.

The vertex positions after one trip are:
$$A_s \to (a,0),\; B_s \to (a,-a),\; P_s \to (0,-a),\; Q_s \to (0,0).$$

Each vertex has **cyclically shifted by one position** ($A \to B$'s spot, $B \to

## Counting full rounds to return ($n$)

After $k$ trips, the cyclic shift is $k$ positions. All four vertices return to
their original positions when $k \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$, i.e., $k = 4$.

Equivalently: after each trip, the contact side of the square advances by 3 (mod
 4). We need $3k \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$; since $\gcd(3,4)=1$, this gives $k = 4$.

Checking intermediate trips:
- 1 trip: shift by 1 — not all back
- 2 trips: shift by 2 — not all back
- 3 trips: shift by 3 — not all back
- **4 trips: shift by 4 ≡ 0 — all back** ✓

No intermediate (mid-pivot) return is possible, since all four vertices simultan
eously at their original positions requires the square to be in exactly its orig
inal position and orientation, which only occurs at trip boundaries.

$$\boxed{n = 4}$$

## Counting total rotations ($r$)

$$\text{Rotations per trip} = 1 + \frac{m}{n} = 1 + \frac{3}{4} = \frac{7}{4}.$$
After 4 trips: $4 \times \frac{7}{4} = 7$ rotations. ✓
