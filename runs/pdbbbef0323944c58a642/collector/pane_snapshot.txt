## Solution

**Yes, such vectors exist for every $k > 0$.**

**Proof.**

Take $H = \mathbb{R}$ (a one-dimensional Hilbert space with the standard inner p
roduct). Fix any $k > 0$. Set

$$y = 1, \qquad x = T, \qquad T = \max(2,\, k).$$

Then:
- $(x, y) = T \cdot 1 = T$,
- $\|x - y\|^2 = (T - 1)^2$.

Since $T \geq 2$, we have $T - 1 \geq 1$, hence $(T-1)^2 \geq 1$. Therefore:

$$\frac{k}{\|x - y\|^2} = \frac{k}{(T-1)^2} \leq k \leq T = (x, y),$$

where the middle inequality $k \leq T$ holds by our choice $T = \max(2, k)$.

Thus $(x, y) \geq \dfrac{k}{\|x - y\|^2}$, as required. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** More generally, the quantity $(x,y)\cdot\|x-y\|^2$ can be made arbit
rarily large: with $x = T,\, y = 1$ we get $T(T-1)^2 \to \infty$ as $T \to \inft
y$, so the inequality is satisfiable for any prescribed $k > 0$.
