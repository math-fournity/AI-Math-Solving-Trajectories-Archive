**Proof.**

Given $abc = 1$ with $a, b, c > 0$, we have $bc = 1/a$, $ca = 1/b$, $ab = 1/c$.
Substituting into each term:

$$\sqrt{\frac{a^3}{1+bc}} = \sqrt{\frac{a^3}{1+1/a}} = \sqrt{\frac{a^4}{a+1}} =
\frac{a^2}{\sqrt{a+1}}.$$

Thus the desired inequality becomes

$$\sum_{\text{cyc}} \frac{a^2}{\sqrt{a+1}} \geq 2.$$

We establish the following chain of inequalities.

**Step 1 (Cauchy–Schwarz).** By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

$$\left(\sum_{\text{cyc}} \frac{a^2}{\sqrt{a+1}}\right)\!\left(\sum_{\text{cyc}}
 \sqrt{a+1}\right) \geq \left(\sum_{\text{cyc}} a\right)^{\!2},$$

so

$$\sum_{\text{cyc}} \frac{a^2}{\sqrt{a+1}} \geq \frac{\left(\sum a\right)^2}{\su
m \sqrt{a+1}}.$$

**Step 2 (Cauchy–Schwarz on the denominator).** Again by Cauchy–Schwarz,

$$\sum_{\text{cyc}} \sqrt{a+1} \leq \sqrt{3\sum_{\text{cyc}}(a+1)} = \sqrt{3\!\l
eft(\sum a + 3\right)}.$$

Combining Steps 1 and 2, and letting $S = a + b + c$:

$$\sum_{\text{cyc}} \frac{a^2}{\sqrt{a+1}} \geq \frac{S^2}{\sqrt{3(S+3)}}.$$

**Step 3 (AM–GM).** Since $abc = 1$,

$$S = a + b + c \geq 3\sqrt[3]{abc} = 3.$$

**Step 4 (Monotonicity).** Define $h(S) = \dfrac{S^2}{\sqrt{3(S+3)}}$ for $S > 0
$. Then

$$h'(S) = \frac{S(3S+12)}{2\sqrt{3}\,(S+3)^{3/2}} > 0 \quad \text{for } S > 0,$$

so $h$ is strictly increasing. Since $S \geq 3$,

$$h(S) \geq h(3) = \frac{9}{\sqrt{18}} = \frac{9}{3\sqrt{2}} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{2}
}.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\sum_{\text{cyc}} \sqrt{\frac{a^3}{1+bc}} = \sum_{\text{cyc}} \frac{a^2}{\sqrt
{a+1}} \geq \frac{3}{\sqrt{2}} > 2.$$

In particular, $\displaystyle\sum_{\text{cyc}} \sqrt{\frac{a^3}{1+bc}} \geq 2$,
as required. (The stronger bound $\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}$ is achieved at $a = b = c
= 1$, where equality holds in all three inequalities above.) $\blacksquare$
