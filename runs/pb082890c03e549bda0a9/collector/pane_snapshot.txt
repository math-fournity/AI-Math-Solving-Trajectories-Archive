## Proof

The operator is
$$(Ax)(t) = \int_{-1}^{1} t^{2} s\, x(s)\,ds = t^{2}\!\int_{-1}^{1} s\,x(s)\,ds.
$$

**Step 1. Structure of $A$.** The kernel $K(t,s)=t^{2}s$ is separable, so $A$ is
 a rank-one operator. Setting $u(t)=t^{2}$ and $v(s)=s$, we have
$$Ax = u\,\langle v, x\rangle, \qquad \langle v,x\rangle = \int_{-1}^{1} s\,x(s)
\,ds.$$

**Step 2. $A$ is nilpotent.** Compute
$$\langle v, u\rangle = \int_{-1}^{1} s\cdot s^{2}\,ds = \int_{-1}^{1} s^{3}\,ds
 = 0$$
since $s^{3}$ is odd on $[-1,1]$. Hence $Au = u\langle v,u\rangle = 0$, and for
any $x$,
$$A^{2}x = A\bigl(u\,\langle v,x\rangle\bigr) = \langle v,x\rangle\, Au = 0.$$
Thus $A^{2}=0$; $A$ is nilpotent of index $2$.

**Step 3. Spectrum.** For any $\lambda\neq 0$, the operator $A-\lambda I$ is inv
ertible, with inverse
$$(A-\lambda I)^{-1} = -\tfrac{1}{\lambda}I - \tfrac{1}{\lambda^{2}}A,$$
since
$$(A-\lambda I)\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{\lambda}I-\tfrac{1}{\lambda^{2}}A\right) = -\t
frac{A}{\lambda}-\tfrac{A^{2}}{\lambda^{2}} + I + \tfrac{A}{\lambda} = I - \tfra
c{A^{2}}{\lambda^{2}} = I.$$
Therefore no $\lambda\neq 0$ belongs to the spectrum.

For $\lambda=0$: $A$ is not invertible (it is not even injective, since $Au=0$ w
ith $u\neq 0$), so $0\in\sigma(A)$.

Hence
$$\boxed{\sigma(A)=\{0\}}.$$

**Step 4. Spectral type.** $0$ is an eigenvalue. The eigenspace is
$$\ker A = \left\{x\in L_{2}[-1,1] : \int_{-1}^{1} s\,x(s)\,ds = 0\right\},$$
which has codimension $1$ and is therefore infinite-dimensional. The range of $A
$ is $\operatorname{span}\{t^{2}\}$, which is a one-dimensional subspace contain
ed in $\ker A$ (consistent with $A^{2}=0$). There are no nonzero eigenvalues, si
nce the only candidate, $\langle v,u\rangle$, equals $0$.
