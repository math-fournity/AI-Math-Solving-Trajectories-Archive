## Proof

Let $S_N = \sum_{k=2}^{N-2} \frac{1}{\ln k \cdot \ln(N-k)}$. We show $\displayst
yle\lim_{N\to\infty} \frac{\ln^2 N}{N}\,S_N = 1$.

**Rewriting.** Set
$$f_N(t)=\frac{1}{\bigl(1+\frac{\ln t}{\ln N}\bigr)\bigl(1+\frac{\ln(1-t)}{\ln N
}\bigr)},\qquad t\in(0,1),$$
so that $\frac{\ln k}{\ln N}=1+\frac{\ln(k/N)}{\ln N}$ and
$$\frac{\ln^2 N}{N}\,S_N=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=2}^{N-2}f_N\!\Bigl(\frac{k}{N}\Bigr)
.$$

Fix $\delta\in(0,\tfrac12)$ and split the sum into **left edge** ($2\le k\le \lf
loor\delta N\rfloor$), **middle** ($\lfloor\delta N\rfloor< k<\lfloor(1-\delta)N
\rfloor$), and **right edge** ($\lfloor(1-\delta)N\rfloor\le k\le N-2$).

---

**Middle region.** For $t\in[\delta,\,1-\delta]$ we have $|\ln t|,|\ln(1-t)|\le|
\ln\delta|$, so $\frac{\ln t}{\ln N}\to0$ and $\frac{\ln(1-t)}{\ln N}\to0$ **uni
formly**. Hence $f_N(t)\to 1$ uniformly on $[\delta,1-\delta]$, and the Riemann
sum gives
$$\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=\lceil\delta N\rceil}^{\lfloor(1-\delta)N\rfloor}f_N\!\Bigl
(\frac{k}{N}\Bigr)\;\longrightarrow\;\\int_\delta^{1-\delta}1\,dt=1-2\delta.$$

---

**Left edge.** For $k\le\delta N$,
$$\frac{\ln N}{\ln(N-k)}=\frac{1}{1+\frac{\ln(1-k/N)}{\ln N}}\;\longrightarrow\;
1\quad\text{uniformly},$$
since $|\ln(1-k/N)|\le|\ln(1-\delta)|$ is bounded. Therefore
$$\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor\delta N\rfloor}f_N\!\Bigl(\frac{k}{N}\Bigr)=\fr
ac{\ln N}{N}\sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor\delta N\rfloor}\frac{1}{\ln k}\cdot\frac{\ln N}{
\ln(N-k)}=\frac{\ln N}{N}\sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor\delta N\rfloor}\frac{1}{\ln k}\cdot
\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr).$$

By integral comparison with the logarithmic integral,
$$\sum_{k=2}^{M}\frac{1}{\ln k}\sim\int_2^M\frac{dx}{\ln x}=\operatorname{li}(M)
\sim\frac{M}{\ln M}\qquad(M\to\infty).$$
Taking $M=\lfloor\delta N\rfloor$:
$$\sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor\delta N\rfloor}\frac{1}{\ln k}\sim\frac{\delta N}{\ln(\del
ta N)}\sim\frac{\delta N}{\ln N},$$
so
$$\frac{\ln N}{N}\cdot\frac{\delta N}{\ln N}\cdot\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)=\delta\bigl(
1+o(1)\bigr)\;\longrightarrow\;\delta.$$

---

**Right edge.** The substitution $k\mapsto N-k$ shows the right edge equals the
left edge, so it also contributes $\to\delta$.

---

**Combining.** For every $\delta\in(0,\tfrac12)$,
$$\frac{\ln^2 N}{N}\,S_N\;\longrightarrow\;(1-2\delta)+\delta+\delta=1.$$

Since the limit is independent of $\delta$, we conclude

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\ln^2 N}{N}\sum_{k=2}^{N-2}\frac{1}
{\ln k\cdot\ln(N-k)}=1.}$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
