## Proof

Let $\sigma$ be a nontrivial automorphism of a field $K$, and let $\phi: GL(n,K)
 \to GL(n,K)$ be the automorphism obtained by applying $\sigma$ entry-wise to ma
trices. We show that $\phi$ is necessarily an outer automorphism, i.e., not inne
r.

**Key observation: the center of $GL(n,K)$.**

The center of $GL(n,K)$ consists exactly of the nonzero scalar matrices:
$$Z(GL(n,K)) = \{\,\lambda I_n : \lambda \in K^\*\,\}.$$
(For $n \geq 2$ this is standard; for $n = 1$, $GL(1,K) = K^\*$ is abelian, so t
he center is all of $K^\*$, which is the same set of "scalar matrices.")

**Inner automorphisms fix the center pointwise.**

For any $h \in GL(n,K)$ and any central element $\lambda I_n$,
$$h(\lambda I_n)h^{-1} = \lambda\, h I_n h^{-1} = \lambda I_n.$$
So every inner automorphism acts as the identity on $Z(GL(n,K))$.

**The field automorphism $\phi$ does not fix the center.**

Since $\sigma$ is a nontrivial field automorphism, there exists $a \in K$ with $
\sigma(a) \neq a$. Because $\sigma(0) = 0$, such an $a$ must be nonzero, i.e., $
a \in K^\*$. Applying $\phi$ to the central element $a I_n$:
$$\phi(a I_n) = \sigma(a)\, I_n \neq a\, I_n,$$
since $\sigma(a) \neq a$.

**Conclusion.**

The automorphism $\phi$ moves the element $a I_n$ of the center, whereas every i
nner automorphism fixes every element of the center. Therefore $\phi$ cannot be
inner. Hence $\phi$ is an **outer** automorphism of $GL(n,K)$.

This argument is uniform: it applies for every $n \geq 1$ and every nontrivial f
ield automorphism $\sigma$, with no exceptional cases.
