## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** $\phi$ must be the restriction of $f$ to $\gamma$.

**Setup.** Since $\Omega$ is simply-connected and $0\notin\Omega$, the logarithm
 admits single-valued holomorphic branches on $\Omega$. By hypothesis $f:\Omega\
to\mathbf{C}$ is the unique such branch satisfying $f(\zeta)=\phi(0)$, so by def
inition of a logarithm branch
$$
e^{f(z)} = z \quad\text{for every } z\in\Omega. \tag{1}
$$
Also, by hypothesis on $\phi$,
$$
e^{\phi(t)} = \gamma(t) \quad\text{for every } t\in[0,1]. \tag{2}
$$

**Key comparison.** Define the auxiliary function
$$
h(t) := \phi(t) - f(\gamma(t)), \qquad t\in[0,1].
$$
Both $\phi$ and $f\circ\gamma$ are smooth ( $\phi$ by assumption, $f$ holomorphi
c hence smooth, $\gamma$ smooth), so $h$ is continuous—in fact smooth—on the con
nected interval $[0,1]$.

Using (1) with $z=\gamma(t)$ and (2), we compute
$$
e^{h(t)} = e^{\phi(t) - f(\gamma(t))} = \frac{e^{\phi(t)}}{e^{f(\gamma(t))}} = \
frac{\gamma(t)}{\gamma(t)} = 1,
$$
where the division is legitimate because $\gamma(t)\in\Omega$ and $0\notin\Omega
$, so $\gamma(t)\neq 0$. Hence
$$
h(t) \in 2\pi i\,\mathbf{Z} \quad\text{for every } t\in[0,1]. \tag{3}
$$

**Connectedness argument.** The map $h:[0,1]\to 2\pi i\,\mathbf{Z}$ is continuou
s, and $[0,1]$ is connected while $2\pi i\,\mathbf{Z}$ is discrete. A continuous
 map from a connected space into a discrete space is constant. Therefore $h$ is
constant on $[0,1]$.

**Determining the constant.** At $t=0$,
$$
h(0) = \phi(0) - f(\gamma(0)) = \phi(0) - f(\zeta) = \phi(0) - \phi(0) = 0,
$$
using the defining normalization $f(\zeta)=\phi(0)$. Since $h$ is constant, $h(t
)=0$ for all $t\in[0,1]$.

**Conclusion.** Thus
$$
\phi(t) = f(\gamma(t)) \quad\text{for all } t\in[0,1],
$$
i.e. $\phi = f\circ\gamma = f\big|_{\gamma([0,1])}$. The lift $\phi$ is necessar
ily the restriction of the unique logarithm branch $f$ to the curve $\gamma$. $\
blacksquare$
