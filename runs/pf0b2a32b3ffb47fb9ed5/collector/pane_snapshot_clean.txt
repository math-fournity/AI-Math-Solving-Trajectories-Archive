## Proof

Let $G = \mathbb{Z}/p = \langle g \rangle$ and $R = \mathbb{F}_p[G]$, where $\ma
thbb{F}_p = \mathbb{Z}/p$. The augmentation map $\varepsilon \colon R \to \mathb
b{F}_p$ is defined by $\varepsilon\!\left(\sum_{i=0}^{p-1} a_i g^i\right) = \sum
_{i=0}^{p-1} a_i$, and the augmentation ideal is $I = \ker \varepsilon$.

**Step 1: Identify the ring structure.** Set $t = g - 1 \in R$. Since $\text{cha
r}\,\mathbb{F}_p = p$, the freshman's dream gives
$$(1+t)^p = 1 + t^p.$$
But $g = 1+t$ and $g^p = 1$ in $G$, so $g^p - 1 = 0$ in $R$, i.e. $t^p = (g-1)^p
 = g^p - 1 = 0$. Hence
$$R = \mathbb{F}_p[g]/(g^p-1) \cong \mathbb{F}_p[t]/(t^p).$$
As an $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector space, $R$ has basis $\{1, t, t^2, \dots, t^{p-1}\}$
, so $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} R = p$.

**Step 2: Identify $I$ and $I^n$.** The augmentation ideal is generated by $g -
1 = t$:
$$I = (g-1) = (t).$$
Indeed, every element of $\ker\varepsilon$ is a linear combination of $\{g^i - 1
\}_{i=1}^{p-1}$, and $g^i - 1 = (g-1)(1 + g + \cdots + g^{i-1}) \in (t)$, while
conversely $\varepsilon(t) = \varepsilon(g) - \varepsilon(1) = 1 - 1 = 0$, so $(
t) \subseteq \ker\varepsilon$.

Therefore
$$I^n = (t)^n = (t^n) \subseteq \mathbb{F}_p[t]/(t^p).$$

**Step 3: Compute the dimension.** The ideal $(t^n)$ in $\mathbb{F}_p[t]/(t^p)$
is the $\mathbb{F}_p$-span of $\{t^n, t^{n+1}, \dots, t^{p-1}\}$. These elements
 are part of the standard basis $\{1, t, \dots, t^{p-1}\}$ of $R$, hence linearl
y independent, and they span $(t^n)$ since every element of $(t^n)$ is $t^n \cdo
t f(t)$ with $\deg f \leq p-1-n$, giving exactly the monomials $t^n, \dots, t^{p
-1}$.

The number of basis elements is
$$p - 1 - n + 1 = p - n.$$

**Verification of edge cases:**
- $n = 1$: $\dim I = p - 1$, which equals $\dim R - \dim \mathbb{F}_p = p - 1$ (
codimension of the trivial representation). ✓
- $n = p - 1$: $\dim I^{p-1} = 1$, spanned by $t^{p-1}$. ✓
- $n = p$ (formally): $\dim I^p = \dim(t^p) = 0 = p - p$. ✓

**Conclusion.** For $1 \leq n \leq p-1$,
$$\boxed{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} I^n = p - n.}$$
