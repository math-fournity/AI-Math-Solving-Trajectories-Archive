## Proof

Let \(P(n)=\prod_{k=1}^{n} k^{k}\) (the hyperfactorial). We need the asymptotic
behavior of

\[
\frac{P(n)}{n^{\frac{n^{2}+n}{2}+\frac{1}{12}}\,e^{-n^{2}/4}}.
\]

Take logarithms:

\[
\log P(n)=\sum_{k=1}^{n} k\log k.
\]

Apply the Euler–Maclaurin formula to \(f(x)=x\log x\) on \([1,n]\):

\[
\sum_{k=1}^{n} f(k)=\int_{1}^{n} f(x)\,dx+\frac{f(1)+f(n)}{2}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty
}\frac{B_{2m}}{(2m)!}\bigl(f^{(2m-1)}(n)-f^{(2m-1)}(1)\bigr).
\]

Compute the pieces.

- Integral:
\[
\int_{1}^{n} x\log x\,dx=\frac{n^{2}}{2}\log n-\frac{n^{2}}{4}+\frac14.
\]

- Endpoint term: \(f(1)=0\), \(f(n)=n\log n\), so
\[
\frac{f(1)+f(n)}{2}=\frac{n\log n}{2}.
\]

- Derivatives: for \(m\ge 1\),
\[
f^{(2m-1)}(x)=-\frac{(2m-2)!}{x^{2m-2}}.
\]
In particular \(f'(x)=\log x+1\), and the first Bernoulli correction is
\[
\frac{B_{2}}{2!}\bigl(f'(n)-f'(1)\bigr)=\frac{1}{12}\bigl((\log n+1)-1\bigr)=\fr
ac{1}{12}\log n.
\]

- For \(m\ge 2\), \(f^{(2m-1)}(n)=-\dfrac{(2m-2)!}{n^{2m-2}}\to 0\), while \(f^{
(2m-1)}(1)\) is a constant. Hence all higher corrections contribute only a const
ant (plus \(o(1)\)) to the sum.

Collecting the terms that grow with \(n\):

\[
\log P(n)=\frac{n^{2}}{2}\log n+\frac{n}{2}\log n+\frac{1}{12}\log n-\frac{n^{2}
}{4}+C+o(1),
\]

where \(C\) is a finite constant (related to the Glaisher–Kinkelin constant). Th
us

\[
\log P(n)=\left(\frac{n^{2}+n}{2}+\frac{1}{12}\right)\log n-\frac{n^{2}}{4}+C+o(
1).
\]

Exponentiating,

\[
P(n)\sim A\,n^{\frac{n^{2}+n}{2}+\frac{1}{12}}\,e^{-n^{2}/4}
\]

for some positive constant \(A=e^{C}\). Therefore

\[
\frac{P(n)}{n^{\frac{n^{2}+n}{2}+\frac{1}{12}}\,e^{-n^{2}/4}}\sim A\cdot n^{0}.
\]

Hence \(k=0\).
