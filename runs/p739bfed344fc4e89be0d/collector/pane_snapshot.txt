Looking at this problem, I need to find all surfaces of revolution with constant
 Gaussian curvature $K$ for $K = 0, 1, -1$.

## Setup

A surface of revolution is generated by rotating a profile curve $\gamma(s) = (r
(s), z(s))$ (parametrized by arc length $s$, so $(r')^2 + (z')^2 = 1$, with $r >
 0$) about the $z$-axis:

$$X(s,\theta) = (r(s)\cos\theta,\; r(s)\sin\theta,\; z(s)).$$

**First fundamental form:** $E = (r')^2 + (z')^2 = 1$, $F = 0$, $G = r^2$.

**Unit normal:** $N = (-z'\cos\theta,\; -z'\sin\theta,\; r')$.

**Second fundamental form:** $e = -z'r'' + r'z''$, $f = 0$, $g = rz'$.

The principal curvatures are $k_1 = e/E = -z'r'' + r'z''$ and $k_2 = g/G = z'/r$
.

From the arc-length condition, differentiating $(r')^2 + (z')^2 = 1$ gives $r'r'
' + z'z'' = 0$, so $z'' = -r'r''/z'$ (when $z' \neq 0$). Substituting:

$$k_1 = -z'r'' + r'\left(-\frac{r'r''}{z'}\right) = -\frac{r''}{z'}\left((z')^2
+ (r')^2\right) = -\frac{r''}{z'}.$$

Therefore the Gaussian curvature is:

$$\boxed{K = k_1 k_2 = \frac{-r''}{r}}, \quad \text{i.e.,} \quad r'' + Kr = 0.$$

This is the **fundamental ODE**. Since it is a second-order linear ODE, its gene
ral solution (with the constraint determining $z$ up to sign and additive consta
nt) gives all surfaces of revolution with constant $K$.

## Case (a): $K = 0$

The ODE becomes $r'' = 0$, giving $r(s) = as + b$ for constants $a, b$.

From $(r')^2 + (z')^2 = 1$: $a^2 + (z')^2 = 1$, so $z' = \pm\sqrt{1 - a^2}$ (con
stant), hence $z(s) = \pm\sqrt{1-a^2}\, s + c$.

The profile curve is a **straight line** in the $rz$-plane. The resulting surfac
es are:

- **$a = 0$**: $r = b$ (constant), $z$ varies $\longrightarrow$ **cylinder** of
radius $b$.
- **$0 < |a| < 1$**: $r = as + b$, $z = \pm\sqrt{1-a^2}\,s + c$ $\longrightarrow
$ **cone** (rotating a tilted line).
- **$|a| = 1$**: $z' = 0$, so $z = c$ (constant), $r = \pm s + b$ $\longrightarr
ow$ **plane**.

These are precisely the **developable** surfaces of revolution: the plane, the c
ylinder, and the cone.

## Case (b): $K = 1$

The ODE becomes $r'' + r = 0$, giving $r(s) = A\cos s + B\sin s = R\cos(s - \phi
)$, where $R = \sqrt{A^2 + B^2} > 0$. By shifting the parameter $s$, we take:

$$r(s) = R\cos s, \qquad r'(s) = -R\sin s.$$

Then $(z')^2 = 1 - R^2\sin^2 s$, so:

$$z(s) = \int_0^s \sqrt{1 - R^2 \sin^2 t}\, dt,$$

which is an **elliptic integral of the second kind** $E(s \mid R^2)$. The domain
 is restricted to where $r > 0$ and $z'$ is real.

**Sub-case $R = 1$ (the sphere):** $z' = \sqrt{1 - \sin^2 s} = |\cos s|$. For $s
 \in (-\pi/2, \pi/2)$, $\cos s > 0$, so $z' = \cos s$ and $z = \sin s$. The prof
ile is $(r, z) = (\cos s, \sin s)$ — a **semicircle** of radius 1. Rotating give
s the **unit sphere** $S^2$.

**Sub-case $0 < R < 1$ (smooth bulge-type surfaces):** Here $z' = \sqrt{1 - R^2\
sin^2 s} > 0$ for all $s \in [-\pi/2, \pi/2]$. At $s = \pm\pi/2$: $r \to 0$, $r'
 = \mp R \neq 0$, $z' = \sqrt{1 - R^2} > 0$. The profile curve connects two poin
ts on the axis smoothly, bulging outward to maximum radius $R$. The resulting su
rface is a **smooth closed surface of revolution**, homeomorphic to $S^2$, with
$K \equiv 1$, but **not congruent to the round sphere** (unless $R = 1$). These
form a one-parameter family of "bumpy" or elongated spheres.

**Sub-case $R > 1$ (spindle-type surfaces):** $z'$ is real only for $|\sin s| \l
eq 1/R$, i.e., $|s| \leq \arcsin(1/R) < \pi/2$. At the boundary $s = \pm\arcsin(
1/R)$: $z' = 0$, $r = \sqrt{R^2 - 1} > 0$. The profile has a horizontal tangent
(parallel to the $r$-axis) at these points, so the surface has a **circular boun
dary edge** of radius $\sqrt{R^2-1}$ and is spindle-shaped (pinched). It is **no
t complete**.

## Case (c): $K = -1$

The ODE becomes $r'' - r = 0$, giving $r(s) = Ae^s + Be^{-s}$ for constants $A,
B$.

Then $r' = Ae^s - Be^{-s}$ and $(z')^2 = 1 - (Ae^s - Be^{-s})^2$. The classifica
tion depends on the sign of $AB$:

### Sub-case 1: $B = 0$ (or $A = 0$) — the Pseudosphere (Tractricoid)

Take $r = Ae^s$ (the case $Ae^{-s}$ is analogous). Then $r' = r$ and $z' = \sqrt
{1 - r^2}$, valid for $r \leq A$. Setting $u = r$ and integrating:

$$z = \int \frac{\sqrt{1 - u^2}}{u}\, du = \sqrt{1 - u^2} + \ln\!\left(\frac{u}{
1 + \sqrt{1 - u^2}}\right) + \text{const}.$$

This is the **tractrix**. As $u \to 0$ (i.e., $s \to -\infty$): $r \to 0$ and $z
 \to -\infty$ — the surface has a **cusp** asymptotically approaching the axis.
At $u = A$ (i.e., $s = 0$): $z' = 0$ — the surface has a **circular rim** of rad
ius $A$. Rotating the tractrix gives the **pseudosphere** (tractricoid), the cla
ssic complete (away from the cusp) surface with $K = -1$. For $A = 1$, this is t
he standard pseudosphere.

*Verification:* For the standard tractrix $r = \operatorname{sech} t$, $z = t -
\tanh t$, reparametrizing by arc length ($ds = \tanh t\, dt$) gives $dr/ds = -\o
peratorname{sech} t = -r$, so $r = Ce^{-s}$, confirming $r'' = r$ and $K = -r''/
r = -1$. ✓

### Sub-case 2: $AB > 0$ — "Conical" (waist) type

Write $r(s) = R\cosh(s - s_0)$ with $R = 2\sqrt{AB} > 0$. Then $r' = R\sinh(s -
s_0)$ and:

$$z' = \sqrt{1 - R^2\sinh^2(s - s_0)},$$

valid for $|\sinh(s - s_0)| \leq 1/R$, i.e., $|s - s_0| \leq \operatorname{arcsi
nh}(1/R)$.

At $s = s_0$ (the **waist**): $r = R$ (minimum radius), $r' = 0$, $z' = 1$. At t
he boundary $|s - s_0| = \operatorname{arcsinh}(1/R)$: $z' = 0$, $r = \sqrt{R^2
+ 1}$. The surface is a **symmetric band** with a waist of radius $R$ and two ci
rcular boundary edges of radius $\sqrt{R^2+1}$. It is not complete.

### Sub-case 3: $AB < 0$ — "Spindle" type

Write $r(s) = R\sinh(s - s_0)$ with $R = 2\sqrt{|AB|} > 0$. Then $r' = R\cosh(s
- s_0)$ and:

$$z' = \sqrt{1 - R^2\cosh^2(s - s_0)},$$

valid for $\cosh(s - s_0) \leq 1/R$, which requires $R \leq 1$, on $|s - s_0| \l
eq \operatorname{arccosh}(1/R)$.

At $s = s_0$: $r = 0$, $r' = R \neq 0$, $z' = \sqrt{1 - R^2}$ — the surface **st
arts smoothly at a point** on the axis (a pole). At the boundary: $z' = 0$, $r =
 \sqrt{1 - R^2}$. The surface has a **point (pole) on one end** and a **circular
 edge** on the other. For $R = 1$, the domain degenerates to a single point.

## Summary

All surfaces of revolution with constant Gaussian curvature $K$ are obtained by
solving $r'' + Kr = 0$ with $(r')^2 + (z')^2 = 1$, and the complete classificati
on is:

| $K$ | ODE | General $r(s)$ | Surfaces |
|-----|-----|-----------------|----------|
| $0$ | $r'' = 0$ | $r = as + b$ | **Plane, cylinder, cone** (developable surfac
es) |
| $1$ | $r'' + r = 0$ | $r = R\cos s$ | **Sphere** ($R=1$); smooth bulge-type cl
osed surfaces ($0<R<1$); spindle-type surfaces with boundary ($R>1$) |
| $-1$ | $r'' - r = 0$ | $r = Ae^s + Be^{-s}$ | **Pseudosphere/tractricoid** ($A
B=0$); waist-type band surfaces ($AB>0$); spindle-type surfaces from a pole ($AB
<0$) |

In each case, $z(s) = \int \sqrt{1 - (r')^2}\, ds$ (with appropriate sign and do
main restrictions), and the profile curve is uniquely determined (up to rigid mo
tions and reparametrization) by the constants in $r(s)$.
