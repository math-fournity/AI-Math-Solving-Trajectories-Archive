## Solution

We solve the wave equation
$$u_{tt} = 25\,u_{xx},\qquad -\infty<x<\infty,\ t>0,$$
with initial data
$$u(x,0)=f(x)=\begin{cases}1,&x<0,\\0,&x>0,\end{cases}\qquad u_t(x,0)=g(x)=0.$$

**Wave speed.** Writing the equation as $u_{tt}=c^2 u_{xx}$ gives $c^2=25$, so $
c=5$.

**D'Alembert's formula.** For the Cauchy problem $u_{tt}=c^2 u_{xx}$ with data $
u(x,0)=f(x)$, $u_t(x,0)=g(x)$, the solution is
$$u(x,t)=\tfrac12\bigl[f(x+ct)+f(x-ct)\bigr]+\frac{1}{2c}\int_{x-ct}^{x+ct}g(s)\
,ds.$$

Since $g\equiv 0$, the integral term vanishes, and with $c=5$:
$$u(x,t)=\tfrac12\bigl[f(x+5t)+f(x-5t)\bigr].$$

**Evaluating $f$.** Recall $f(s)=1$ for $s<0$ and $f(s)=0$ for $s>0$. For $t>0$
we have $5t>0$, so $x-5t<x+5t$. There are three regions:

1. **$x+5t<0$ (i.e. $x<-5t$):** both $x+5t<0$ and $x-5t<0$, so $f(x+5t)=f(x-5t)=
1$, giving
$$u(x,t)=\tfrac12(1+1)=1.$$

2. **$x-5t>0$ (i.e. $x>5t$):** both arguments positive, so $f(x+5t)=f(x-5t)=0$,
giving
$$u(x,t)=\tfrac12(0+0)=0.$$

3. **$x-5t<0<x+5t$ (i.e. $-5t<x<5t$):** $f(x-5t)=1$ and $f(x+5t)=0$, giving
$$u(x,t)=\tfrac12(0+1)=\tfrac12.$$

The case $x+5t<0<x-5t$ is impossible since $x-5t<x+5t$.

**Verification.** The solution
$$u(x,t)=\begin{cases}1,&x<-5t,\\\tfrac12,&-5t<x<5t,\\0,&x>5t,\end{cases}$$
is constant in each open region, so $u_{tt}=u_{xx}=0$ there, satisfying the PDE.
 It is a superposition of the left-traveling wave $\tfrac12 f(x+5t)$ and the rig
ht-traveling wave $\tfrac12 f(x-5t)$, each traveling at speed $5$, consistent wi
th $c=5$. At $t=0$ the two halves sum to $f(x)$, recovering the initial displace
ment, and since $u$ is time-independent in each region (the discontinuities trav
el but the values are constant), $u_t(x,0)=0$ as required. Thus the initial cond
itions are satisfied.

**Interpretation.** The initial step at $x=0$ splits into two half-amplitude ste
ps: one traveling left at speed $5$ (located at $x=-5t$) and one traveling right
 at speed $5$ (located at $x=5t$). Between them the solution takes the average v
alue $\tfrac12$, which is exactly the value d'Alembert's formula assigns at a ju
mp discontinuity.
