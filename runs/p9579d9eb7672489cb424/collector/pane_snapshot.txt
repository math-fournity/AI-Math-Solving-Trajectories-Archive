## Proof

We model this as a graph-coloring problem: place a vertex at each positive integ
er, and connect two vertices $u, v$ whenever $|u-v|$ is prime. The desired minim
um is the chromatic number of this graph.

### Upper bound: 4 colors suffice

Color each positive integer $n$ by its residue $n \bmod 4$. We claim this is a v
alid coloring.

Suppose $|u - v| = p$ is prime. Then $u \not\equiv v \pmod 4$ iff $p \not\equiv
0 \pmod 4$. Now every prime satisfies $p \not\equiv 0 \pmod 4$:
- $p = 2 \equiv 2 \pmod 4$;
- every odd prime is $\equiv 1$ or $3 \pmod 4$.

Hence $u \bmod 4 \neq v \bmod 4$, so the two integers receive different colors.
Thus 4 sets suffice.

### Lower bound: 4 colors are necessary

It suffices to exhibit a clique of size 4, i.e. four positive integers whose pai
rwise differences are all prime. Consider

$$\{2,\; 4,\; 7,\; 9\}.$$

Computing all six pairwise differences:

| pair | difference | prime? |
|------|-----------|--------|
| $4-2$ | $2$ | yes |
| $7-2$ | $5$ | yes |
| $7-4$ | $3$ | yes |
| $9-2$ | $7$ | yes |
| $9-4$ | $5$ | yes |
| $9-7$ | $2$ | yes |

All pairwise differences are prime, so these four integers must lie in four *dis
tinct* sets. Therefore at least 4 sets are required.

### Conclusion

Combining the upper and lower bounds, the minimum number of sets needed is exact
ly **4**.
