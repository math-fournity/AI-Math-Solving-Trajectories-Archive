Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether convergence in probability
at time 0 implies weak convergence to the zero process in the Skorokhod space $D
([0,\infty),\mathbb{R})$.

**Answer: No.** A single time-point gives no control over the rest of the path.

## Counterexample

Define the deterministic càdlàg process
$$X_n(t) = \mathbf{1}_{[1/n,\,\infty)}(t), \qquad t \geq 0.$$

**Step 1: $X_n(0) \xrightarrow{P} 0$.**

Since $0 < 1/n$ for every $n \geq 1$, we have $X_n(0) = 0$ for all $n$. Thus $X_
n(0) \to 0$ almost surely (hence in probability).

**Step 2: Each $X_n$ lies in $D([0,\infty),\mathbb{R})$.**

$X_n$ is a step function: it equals $0$ on $[0,\,1/n)$ and $1$ on $[1/n,\,\infty
)$. It is right-continuous with left limits everywhere, so $X_n \in D([0,\infty)
,\mathbb{R})$.

**Step 3: $X_n \to \mathbf{1}$ (the constant function $1$) in the Skorokhod $J_1
$ topology.**

Consider the time changes $\lambda_n(t) = t + 1/n$, which are strictly increasin
g homeomorphisms of $[0,\infty)$.

- For every $T > 0$: $\sup_{0 \le t \le T}|\lambda_n(t) - t| = 1/n \to 0$.
- For every $t \ge 0$: $\lambda_n(t) = t + 1/n \ge 1/n$, so $X_n(\lambda_n(t)) =
 \mathbf{1}_{[1/n,\infty)}(t+1/n) = 1$.

Setting $x(t) \equiv 1$, we get $\sup_{0 \le t \le T}|X_n(\lambda_n(t)) - x(t)|
= 0$ for all $n$ and $T$. Hence $X_n \to x \equiv 1$ in $J_1$.

**Step 4: $X_n$ does not converge weakly to $0$.**

Since the limit $x \equiv 1$ is deterministic, weak convergence coincides with t
opological convergence, and the limit is unique. The constant function $1$ is no
t the zero process. Therefore $X_n \not\xrightarrow{w} 0$ in $D([0,\infty),\math
bb{R})$.

## Conclusion

We have exhibited a sequence $(X_n)$ in $D([0,\infty),\mathbb{R})$ with $X_n(0)
\to 0$ in probability, yet $X_n$ converges weakly to the constant process $\math
bf{1} \neq 0$. The implication **does not hold**.
