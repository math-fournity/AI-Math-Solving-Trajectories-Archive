**Step 1: Reduction to the itinerary space.** Let $\tilde{P}=\{\{s\in I:s_1=j\}:
j=0,\ldots,k-1\}$ be the first-coordinate partition on $I$. Since $P=\phi^{-1}(\
tilde{P})$ (up to null sets) and $\phi$ intertwines $T$ with $\sigma$, the atoms
 of $P^{(n)}$ correspond bijectively to atoms of $\tilde{P}^{(n)}$ with equal me
asures. Hence $H_\nu(P^{(n)})=H_\mu(\tilde{P}^{(n)})$ for all $n$, giving $h(T,P
)=h(\sigma,\tilde{P})$.

**Step 2: Every atom of $\mu$ is periodic.** Since $I$ is countable, $\mu$ is pu
rely atomic: $\mu=\sum_i p_i\,\delta_{s_i}$. The map $\sigma:I\to I$ preserves $
\mu$ (as $\phi$ is a factor map and $\nu$ is $T$-invariant). By the **Poincaré R
ecurrence Theorem**, for any $s_i$ with $p_i>0$, $\mu$-a.e. point in $\{s_i\}$ r
eturns to $\{s_i\}$ infinitely often. Since $\{s_i\}$ is a singleton, $s_i$ itse
lf satisfies $\sigma^n(s_i)=s_i$ for infinitely many $n$; in particular $s_i$ is
 periodic. Thus $\mu$ is supported on periodic points of $\sigma$.

**Step 3: Distinct periodic points lie in distinct fibers of $\sigma$.** Suppose
 $s,s'\in I$ are periodic with $\sigma s=\sigma s'$. Then $s$ and $s'$ agree on
all coordinates $\geq 2$. Let $s$ have (minimal) period $p$ and $s'$ have period
 $q$, and set $d=\gcd(p,q)$. The common tail $(s_2,s_3,\ldots)$ is periodic with
 period $d$. Since $d\mid p$ and $d\mid q$:
$$s_1 = s_{p+1} \quad\text{(period $p$)}, \qquad s'_1 = s'_{q+1} \quad\text{(per
iod $q$)}.$$
Both $s_{p+1}$ and $s'_{q+1}$ lie in the common tail at positions $\equiv 1\pmod
{d}$ (since $p+1\equiv 1$ and $q+1\equiv 1\pmod{d}$). As the tail has period $d$
, these are equal: $s_{p+1}=s'_{q+1}$. Therefore $s_1=s'_1$, and since $s,s'$ al
ready agree on all coordinates $\geq 2$, we get $s=s'$.

**Step 4: Conditional entropy is zero.** Since $H(\tilde{P})\leq\log k<\infty$,
the Kolmogorov–Sinai formula gives:
$$h(\sigma,\tilde{P}) = H\!\left(\tilde{P}\,\middle|\,\bigvee_{n=1}^{\infty}\sig
ma^{-n}\tilde{P}\right).$$
The sigma-algebra $\mathcal{F}=\bigvee_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma^{-n}\tilde{P}$ is ge
nerated by coordinates $2,3,\ldots$. Its atoms are the fibers $\{s'\in I:\sigma
s'=\sigma s\}$. By Step 3, each fiber contains **at most one** periodic point. S
ince $\mu$ is supported on periodic points (Step 2), $\mu$-a.s. the first coordi
nate $s_1$ is determined by the fiber (i.e., by $\sigma s$). Therefore:
$$H\!\left(\tilde{P}\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}\right)=0.$$

**Conclusion.** $h(T,P)=h(\sigma,\tilde{P})=0$. $\blacksquare$
