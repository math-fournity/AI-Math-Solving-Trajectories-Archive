**Claim:** Yes. A connected, simply connected, nilpotent $n$-dimensional Lie gro
up $G$ is homeomorphic (indeed, diffeomorphic) to $\mathbb{R}^n$.

---

**Proof.**

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be the Lie algebra of $G$, so $\mathfrak{g}$ is an $n$-dimens
ional nilpotent Lie algebra. We show the exponential map $\exp\colon \mathfrak{g
}\to G$ is a global diffeomorphism, which gives $\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathbb{R}^n\c
ong G$ as smooth manifolds.

**Step 1: $\exp$ is a local diffeomorphism everywhere.**

The differential of the exponential map is given by the standard formula
$$
(d\exp)_X = (dL_{\exp X})_e \circ \frac{1-e^{-\operatorname{ad}_X}}{\operatornam
e{ad}_X}, \qquad X\in\mathfrak{g}.
$$
Since $\mathfrak{g}$ is nilpotent, $\operatorname{ad}_X$ is a nilpotent endomorp
hism for every $X$. Writing $T=\operatorname{ad}_X$, the operator
$$
\frac{1-e^{-T}}{T} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k}{(k+1)!}T^k
$$
is a *finite* sum (polynomial in $T$, since $T$ is nilpotent) and equals $I$ mod
ulo nilpotent terms. Hence all its eigenvalues are $1$, so it is invertible. Sin
ce $(dL_{\exp X})_e$ is also invertible, $(d\exp)_X$ is invertible for every $X$
. By the inverse function theorem, $\exp$ is a local diffeomorphism at every poi
nt.

**Step 2: $\exp$ is surjective.**

The group $G$ is connected, so it is generated by any neighborhood of the identi
ty. By Step 1, $\exp$ maps a neighborhood of $0\in\mathfrak{g}$ onto a neighborh
ood of $e\in G$, so $G$ is generated by elements of the form $\exp(X)$. Thus eve
ry $g\in G$ is a finite product $\exp(X_1)\cdots\exp(X_m)$.

Because $\mathfrak{g}$ is nilpotent of class, say, $r$, the Baker–Campbell–Hausd
orff series
$$
\mathrm{BCH}(X,Y)=X+Y+\tfrac12[X,Y]+\tfrac1{12}[X,[X,Y]]-\tfrac1{12}[Y,[X,Y]]+\c
dots
$$
*terminates*: all iterated brackets of depth $>r$ vanish, so $\mathrm{BCH}(X,Y)$
 is a finite Lie polynomial. In particular
$$
\exp(X)\exp(Y)=\exp(\mathrm{BCH}(X,Y)).
$$
By induction, every finite product of exponentials is again a single exponential
. Hence every $g\in G$ equals $\exp(X)$ for some $X\in\mathfrak{g}$, proving sur
jectivity.

**Step 3: $\exp$ is injective (via the model group).**

Define a binary operation on the vector space $\mathfrak{g}$ by
$$
X * Y := \mathrm{BCH}(X,Y).
$$
Because $\mathfrak{g}$ is nilpotent, $*$ is a polynomial (hence smooth) operatio
n. It is associative (a formal consequence of the BCH identity and associativity
 of group multiplication), $0$ is the identity, and $-X$ is the inverse of $X$ (
since $\mathrm{BCH}(X,-X)=0$). Thus $(\mathfrak{g},*)$ is a Lie group, smooth an
d with underlying manifold $\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathbb{R}^n$, hence simply connect
ed. Its Lie algebra is $\mathfrak{g}$ with the original bracket (the bracket is
recovered from $*$ via $[X,Y]=\frac{d^2}{ds\,dt}\big|_{0}(sX)*(tY)*(-sX)*(-tY)$)
.

By Lie's third theorem (uniqueness of the simply connected Lie group with a give
n Lie algebra), there is a Lie group isomorphism $\varphi\colon(\mathfrak{g},*)\
xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}G$ inducing the identity on $\mathfrak{g}$.

In the model group $(\mathfrak{g},*)$, the one-parameter subgroup with tangent $
X$ at the identity is $t\mapsto tX$ (since $sX * tX = (s+t)X$ by $\mathrm{BCH}(s
X,tX)=sX+tX$, the bracket terms vanishing as they are bilinear and the arguments
 are proportional). Therefore the exponential map of $(\mathfrak{g},*)$ is the i
dentity $\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{g}}$. By naturality of $\exp$,
$$
\exp_G = \varphi\circ \exp_{(\mathfrak{g},*)}\circ \varphi_*^{-1} = \varphi\circ
\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{g}} = \varphi.
$$
Since $\varphi$ is a diffeomorphism, $\exp_G\colon\mathfrak{g}\to G$ is a diffeo
morphism — in particular bijective, giving injectivity.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 1–3, $\exp\colon\mathfrak{g}\to G$ is a bijective local diffeomo
rphism, hence a global diffeomorphism. Since $\mathfrak{g}$ is an $n$-dimensiona
l real vector space, $\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathbb{R}^n$ as smooth manifolds. Theref
ore
$$
G \;\cong_{\text{diffeo}}\; \mathfrak{g} \;\cong_{\text{diffeo}}\; \mathbb{R}^n,
$$
and in particular $G$ is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^n$. $\blacksquare$
