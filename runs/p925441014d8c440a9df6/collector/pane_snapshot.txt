# Proof

**Claim:** Yes. Every non-invertible element of a commutative von Neumann regula
r ring is a zero-divisor.

**Setup.** Let $R$ be a commutative von Neumann regular ring. Recall that this m
eans: for every $a \in R$, there exists $x \in R$ such that
$$a = a^2 x.$$
We use the standard (Atiyah–Macdonald) definition: $a \in R$ is a *zero-divisor*
 if there exists $b \in R$, $b \neq 0$, with $ab = 0$. (Under this definition $0
$ itself is a zero-divisor in any nonzero ring, since $0 \cdot 1 = 0$ with $1 \n
eq 0$.)

**Argument.** Let $a \in R$ be a non-invertible element. By von Neumann regulari
ty, choose $x \in R$ with $a = a^2 x$. Then
$$a(1 - ax) = a - a^2 x = a - a = 0.$$

It remains to show that the factor $1 - ax$ is nonzero. Suppose, for contradicti
on, that $1 - ax = 0$, i.e. $ax = 1$. Since $R$ is commutative, $xa = ax = 1$ as
 well, so $x$ is a two-sided inverse of $a$. This makes $a$ a unit — contradicti
ng the assumption that $a$ is non-invertible.

Therefore $1 - ax \neq 0$, and we have exhibited a nonzero element $b = 1 - ax$
with $ab = 0$. Hence $a$ is a zero-divisor. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The argument is uniform and covers $a = 0$ as well: when $a = 0$, an
y $x$ gives $1 - ax = 1 \neq 0$ and $0 \cdot 1 = 0$. Equivalently, the contrapos
itive gives a clean characterization: in a commutative VNR ring, every *non-zero
-divisor* (regular element) is a unit, since $a = a^2 x$ and $a(1-ax)=0$ force $
1 - ax = 0$ when $a$ is regular. Thus a commutative VNR ring coincides with its
total ring of quotients.
