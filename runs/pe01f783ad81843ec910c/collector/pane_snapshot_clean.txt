## Proof

**Yes**, the number of ends of $C(\Gamma)$ can be deduced solely from the graph
$\Gamma$. We establish explicit combinatorial criteria on $\Gamma$ that determin
e the number of ends in all cases, including the one-ended case.

### Setup

The right-angled Coxeter group $C(\Gamma)$ has presentation $\langle s \in V(\Ga
mma) \mid s^2=1,\; (st)^2=1 \text{ if } \{s,t\}\in E(\Gamma)\rangle$. The **Davi
s complex** $\Sigma(\Gamma)$ is a CAT(0) cube complex on which $C(\Gamma)$ acts
properly and cocompactly. Since the action is cocompact, the number of ends of $
C(\Gamma)$ equals the number of ends of $\Sigma(\Gamma)$, and $C(\Gamma)$ is fin
itely generated.

### Step 1: Finite subgroups are conjugate into clique subgroups

Every finite subgroup of $C(\Gamma)$ is conjugate to a subgroup of $C(K)$ for so
me **clique** $K$ (complete subgraph) of $\Gamma$. This follows from the CAT(0)
fixed-point theorem: a finite group acting on the CAT(0) complex $\Sigma(\Gamma)
$ has a fixed point, and cell stabilizers are conjugate to $C(K)$ for cliques $K
$.

### Step 2: Splittings over finite subgroups ↔ graph decompositions

- **Disconnection:** If $\Gamma = \Gamma_1 \sqcup \Gamma_2$ (disconnected), then
 $C(\Gamma) = C(\Gamma_1) * C(\Gamma_2)$, a free product (splitting over the tri
vial group).

- **Separating clique:** If $K$ is a clique and $\Gamma \setminus K$ is disconne
cted (with components inducing subgraphs $\Gamma_1, \Gamma_2$ after reattaching
$K$), then $C(\Gamma) = C(\Gamma_1) *_{C(K)} C(\Gamma_2)$, an amalgamated produc
t over the finite group $C(K) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2)^{|K|}$.

Conversely, by Step 1, any splitting of $C(\Gamma)$ over a finite subgroup is, u
p to conjugation, a splitting over some $C(K)$, which corresponds to either a di
sconnection ($K=\emptyset$) or a separating clique.

### Step 3: Stallings' theorem gives the criterion

By **Stallings' theorem**, a finitely generated group has more than one end if a
nd only if it splits over a finite subgroup. Combining with Steps 1–2:

$$C(\Gamma) \text{ has more than one end} \iff \Gamma \text{ is disconnected or
has a separating clique.}$$

### Step 4: Distinguishing 0, 2, and infinitely many ends

- **0 ends (finite group):** $C(\Gamma)$ is finite iff $\Gamma$ is complete, giv
ing $C(\Gamma) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2)^n$.

- **2 ends (virtually $\mathbb{Z}$):** $C(\Gamma)$ is virtually $\mathbb{Z}$ iff
 $\Gamma$ is a **join** $K_n \star \bar{K}_2$ (a complete graph $K_n$ joined wit
h two isolated vertices). Then $C(\Gamma) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2)^n \times D_\infty
$, which is virtually $\mathbb{Z}$. (For $n=0$: $\Gamma = \bar{K}_2$, $C(\Gamma)
 = D_\infty$.) Conversely, if $C(\Gamma)$ is virtually $\mathbb{Z}$, it has a jo
in decomposition $\Gamma = K \star \Lambda$ with $C(K)$ finite (so $K$ is a cliq
ue) and $C(\Lambda)$ virtually $\mathbb{Z}$ with no further finite join factor;
the only such $\Lambda$ is $\bar{K}_2$, giving $\Gamma = K \star \bar{K}_2$.

- **Infinitely many ends:** $C(\Gamma)$ has more than one end but is not virtual
ly $\mathbb{Z}$, i.e., $\Gamma$ is disconnected (but not $\bar{K}_2$) or has a s
eparating clique (but is not $K_n \star \bar{K}_2$).

### Step 5: The one-ended case

$C(\Gamma)$ is **one-ended** if and only if:

1. $\Gamma$ is **connected** (rules out free product splittings),
2. $\Gamma$ is **not complete** (rules out finiteness), and
3. $\Gamma$ has **no separating clique** (rules out amalgamated product splittin
gs over finite subgroups).

Note that the 2-ended case is automatically excluded: $\Gamma = K_n \star \bar{K
}_2$ with $n \geq 1$ has the separating clique $K_n$, and $\Gamma = \bar{K}_2$ i
s disconnected.

### Verification with examples

- **$C_5$ (5-cycle):** Connected, not complete, no separating clique (removing a
ny vertex leaves a connected path). → **One-ended.** ✓
- **Path $P_3$:** The middle vertex is a separating clique. → **Infinitely many
ends** (amalgam $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2 *_{\mathbb{Z}/2} (\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$). ✓
- **$\bar{K}_2$:** Disconnected, two order-2 factors. → **2 ends** ($D_\infty$).
 ✓
- **$K_n$:** Complete. → **0 ends** (finite). ✓

### Conclusion

The number of ends of $C(\Gamma)$ is completely determined by $\Gamma$ through p
urely graph-theoretic criteria:

| Number of ends | Criterion on $\Gamma$ |
|---|---|
| 0 | $\Gamma$ is complete |
| 2 | $\Gamma = K_n \star \bar{K}_2$ for some $n \geq 0$ |
| $\infty$ | $\Gamma$ disconnected (not $\bar{K}_2$) or has a separating clique
(not $K_n \star \bar{K}_2$) |
| 1 | $\Gamma$ connected, not complete, no separating clique |

In particular, **when $C(\Gamma)$ is one-ended, this is detected by the conditio
ns that $\Gamma$ is connected, not complete, and has no separating clique** — al
l of which are read off directly from $\Gamma$. $\blacksquare$
