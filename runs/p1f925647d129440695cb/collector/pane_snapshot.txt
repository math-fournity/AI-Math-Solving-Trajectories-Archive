## Construction

Let $T:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ be the **Takagi (blancmange) function**

$$
T(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\phi(2^{n}x)}{2^{n}},\qquad
\phi(x):=\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathbb{Z})=\min_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|x-k|.
$$

Two classical facts about $T$ (standard, see e.g. the original construction of T
akagi, 1903):

1. **Continuity.** Since $0\le \phi\le \tfrac12$ and $\sum 2^{-n}$ converges, th
e series converges uniformly, so $T$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$.
2. **Nowhere differentiability.** $T$ is differentiable at **no** point of $\mat
hbb{R}$. (The proof examines the difference quotients $[T(x+h_m)-T(x)]/h_m$ alon
g the dyadic sequence $h_m=\pm 2^{-m}$ with sign chosen via the binary expansion
 of $x$; the quotients grow without bound.)

Also $T(0)=\phi(0)=0$.

Define

$$
\boxed{\;f(x)=\int_{0}^{x} t\,T(t)\,dt\;},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.
$$

Equivalently, set $g(x):=x\,T(x)$ and $f(x)=\int_0^x g(t)\,dt$.

---

## Proof that $f$ has the required properties

**$f$ is $C^{1}$ on $\mathbb{R}$.**
Since $T$ is continuous, $g(x)=xT(x)$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$. By the Fund
amental Theorem of Calculus, $f$ is differentiable everywhere with

$$
f'(x)=g(x)=x\,T(x),
$$

and $f'=g$ is continuous. Hence $f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})$.

**$f''(0)$ exists.**
We have $f''(0)=g'(0)$, and

$$
g'(0)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{g(h)-g(0)}{h}
      =\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{h\,T(h)-0}{h}
      =\lim_{h\to 0}T(h)=T(0)=0,
$$

using continuity of $T$ and $T(0)=0$. Thus $f''(0)=0$ exists.

**$f''(x_{0})$ does not exist for every $x_{0}\neq 0$.**
Suppose, for contradiction, that $f''(x_{0})=g'(x_{0})$ exists at some $x_{0}\ne
q 0$. Since $x_{0}\neq 0$, the function $x\mapsto 1/x$ is differentiable at $x_{
0}$, and $x_{0}\neq 0$ ensures the denominator is nonzero near $x_{0}$. Therefor
e the quotient

$$
T(x)=\frac{g(x)}{x}
$$

would be differentiable at $x_{0}$ (as a quotient of two functions differentiabl
e at $x_{0}$ with nonzero denominator). This contradicts the nowhere-differentia
bility of $T$. Hence $g'(x_{0})$, i.e. $f''(x_{0})$, does not exist.

---

## Conclusion

The function

$$
f(x)=\int_{0}^{x} t\,T(t)\,dt,\qquad
T(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\operatorname{dist}(2^{n}t,\mathbb{Z})}{2^{n}},
$$

is of class $C^{1}$ on $\mathbb{R}$, is twice differentiable at the origin (with
 $f''(0)=0$), and is **not** twice differentiable at any other point. Thus $f$ i
s twice differentiable only at the origin.
