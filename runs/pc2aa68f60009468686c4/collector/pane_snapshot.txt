## Proof

Let
$$A = \bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}} [\,2n,\; 2n+1\,).$$

We claim that $A$ is homeomorphic to its complement $\mathbb{R}\setminus A$ (wit
h the subspace topology) and that $A$ is not open.

---

### 1. The complement of $A$

Since the intervals $[2n,2n+1)$ for $n\in\mathbb{Z}$ are pairwise disjoint and t
ile out a "half" of the real line, the complement is

$$\mathbb{R}\setminus A \;=\; \bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}} [\,2n+1,\; 2n+2\,).$$

Indeed, every real number $x$ lies in a unique half-open unit interval $[k,k+1)$
 for some $k\in\mathbb{Z}$; $x\in A$ iff $k$ is even, and $x\in\mathbb{R}\setmin
us A$ iff $k$ is odd.

---

### 2. $A$ is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}\setminus A$

Consider the translation map
$$T:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad T(x)=x+1.$$

$T$ is a homeomorphism of $\mathbb{R}$ onto itself. For each $n\in\mathbb{Z}$,

$$T\bigl([2n,\,2n+1)\bigr) = [2n+1,\,2n+2),$$

so

$$T(A) \;=\; \bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}} [2n+1,\,2n+2) \;=\; \mathbb{R}\setminus A.
$$

The restriction $T|_A : A \to \mathbb{R}\setminus A$ is a bijection. Since $T$ i
s a homeomorphism of $\mathbb{R}$, its restriction is a homeomorphism between th
e subspaces $A$ and $\mathbb{R}\setminus A$ (both endowed with the subspace topo
logy): continuity of $T|_A$ and of $(T|_A)^{-1}=T^{-1}|_{\mathbb{R}\setminus A}$
 follows directly from continuity of $T$ and $T^{-1}$ on $\mathbb{R}$.

Hence $A \cong \mathbb{R}\setminus A$.

---

### 3. $A$ is not open

The point $0\in A$ (since $0\in[0,1)\subset A$). However, for every $\varepsilon
>0$, the point $-\varepsilon/2$ lies in $[-1,0)\subset \mathbb{R}\setminus A$, s
o $(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\not\subset A$. Thus $0$ is not an interior point o
f $A$, and $A$ is not open in $\mathbb{R}$.

---

### Conclusion

The set $A=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}[2n,2n+1)$ is a subset of $\mathbb{R}$ that i
s homeomorphic to its complement (via $x\mapsto x+1$), and $A$ is not open.
