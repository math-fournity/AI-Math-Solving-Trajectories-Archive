Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there exists a measurable f
unction on ℝ² that is essentially unbounded on every measurable rectangle of pos
itive measure.

**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

**Step 1: Auxiliary function on ℝ.**

Let {rₙ}ₙ≥₁ be an enumeration of ℚ. For each n ≥ 1, set Eₙ = (rₙ − 1/(2n²), rₙ +
 1/(2n²)), so |Eₙ| = 1/n² and Σ|Eₙ| < ∞. Define

$$g(t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n \cdot \mathbf{1}_{E_n}(t).$$

This is measurable (pointwise limit of measurable partial sums). By Borel–Cantel
li, since Σ|Eₙ| < ∞, a.e. t lies in finitely many Eₙ, so g < ∞ a.e.

**Claim:** g is essentially unbounded on every open interval.

*Proof of claim.* Let I = (a,b) with a < b, and M > 0. Since ℚ is dense, infinit
ely many rₙ lie in I; pick n > M with rₙ ∈ I. Then Eₙ ∩ I contains an open inter
val around rₙ (of radius min(1/(2n²), rₙ−a, b−rₙ) > 0), so |Eₙ ∩ I| > 0. Every t
 ∈ Eₙ ∩ I satisfies g(t) ≥ n > M, giving |{t ∈ I : g(t) > M}| > 0. □

**Step 2: Define f.**

Set f(x, y) = g(x − y). This is measurable on ℝ² since g is measurable and (x,y)
 ↦ x−y is continuous.

**Step 3: f is essentially unbounded on every measurable rectangle of positive m
easure.**

Let A, B ⊂ ℝ be Lebesgue measurable with |A| > 0, |B| > 0 (equivalently, |A×B| >
 0). Fix M > 0; we show |{(x,y) ∈ A×B : f(x,y) > M}| > 0.

Choose A₀ ⊂ A, B₀ ⊂ B with 0 < |A₀|, |B₀| < ∞ (intersect with large bounded inte
rvals). Consider the convolution

$$h(t) = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbf{1}_{A_0}(t+s)\,\mathbf{1}_{B_0}(s)\,ds = |B_0
 \cap (A_0 - t)|.$$

Since 1_{A₀}, 1_{−B₀} ∈ L¹, their convolution h is **continuous** and non-negati
ve. By Fubini, ∫h = |A₀|·|B₀| > 0, so h is not identically zero. Thus U = {t : h
(t) > 0} is **open and nonempty**, hence contains an open interval J.

Let S_M = {t : g(t) > M}. By the claim, |S_M ∩ J| > 0. By Fubini with the substi
tution t = x − y:

$$|\{(x,y) \in A_0 \times B_0 : f(x,y) > M\}| = \int_{S_M} h(t)\,dt \geq \int_{S
_M \cap J} h(t)\,dt > 0,$$

since h is continuous with h > 0 on J and |S_M ∩ J| > 0.

Since A₀ × B₀ ⊂ A × B, we obtain

$$|\{(x,y) \in A \times B : f(x,y) > M\}| \geq |\{(x,y) \in A_0 \times B_0 : f(x
,y) > M\}| > 0.$$

This holds for every M > 0, so f is essentially unbounded on A × B. As A × B was
 arbitrary, the construction is complete. $\blacksquare$
