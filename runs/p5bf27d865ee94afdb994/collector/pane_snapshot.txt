## Solution

**Reformulation.** We count ordered pairs $(a,b)$ of natural numbers with $\frac
{ab}{a+b}=n$. Multiplying through:

$$ab = n(a+b) \iff ab - na - nb = 0 \iff (a-n)(b-n) = n^2.$$

**Counting solutions.** Assume natural numbers are positive integers ($a,b \geq
1$). Since $n^2 > 0$, the factors $(a-n)$ and $(b-n)$ share the same sign.

*Both positive:* $a-n \geq 1,\; b-n \geq 1$, and $(a-n)(b-n)=n^2$. Each positive
 divisor $d$ of $n^2$ gives a pair $(a,b)=(n+d,\; n+n^2/d)$. This yields exactly
 $d(n^2)$ ordered pairs, where $d(\cdot)$ is the divisor function.

*Both negative:* Write $a-n=-s,\; b-n=-t$ with $s,t\geq 1$ and $st=n^2$. We need
 $a=n-s\geq 1$ and $b=n-t\geq 1$, i.e. $s\leq n-1$ and $t\leq n-1$. But $s\leq n
-1$ forces $t=n^2/s \geq n^2/(n-1) = n + \tfrac{n}{n-1} > n$, contradicting $t\l
eq n-1$. So there are **no** negative-sign solutions for $n\geq 2$ (and for $n=1
$ the negative case gives $a=b=0$, not natural).

Therefore, for every $n\geq 1$:
$$b_n = d(n^2).$$

**Bounding the divisor function.** For any positive integer $m$, each divisor $d
\leq\sqrt{m}$ pairs with a unique divisor $m/d\geq\sqrt{m}$, so:
$$d(m) \leq 2\sqrt{m}.$$

Applying this with $m=n^2$:
$$b_n = d(n^2) \leq 2\sqrt{n^2} = 2n.$$

**The limit.** Hence for every $n$ (power of 2 or not):
$$0 \;\leq\; \frac{b_n}{n^2} \;=\; \frac{d(n^2)}{n^2} \;\leq\; \frac{2n}{n^2} \;
=\; \frac{2}{n} \;\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\; 0.$$

By the squeeze theorem, $\dfrac{b_n}{n^2}\to 0$. The restriction "$n$ is not a p
ower of $2$" only removes a subsequence; since the full sequence already tends t
o $0$, the restricted sequence does as well.

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{b_n}{n^2}=0}$$
