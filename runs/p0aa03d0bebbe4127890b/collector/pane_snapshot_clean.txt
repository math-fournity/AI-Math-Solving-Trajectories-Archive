### Step 1. Reduce to a 1D wave equation

For a radially symmetric function $u(\rho,t)$, the Laplacian in spherical coordi
nates is
$$\nabla^2 u = u_{\rho\rho} + \frac{2}{\rho}\,u_\rho .$$

Hence the PDE $u_{tt}=c^2\nabla^2 u$ becomes
$$u_{tt} = c^2\!\left(u_{\rho\rho}+\frac{2}{\rho}u_\rho\right). \tag{1}$$

Following the hint, set
$$u(\rho,t)=\frac{v(\rho,t)}{\rho}. \tag{2}$$

Compute the derivatives:
$$u_\rho = \frac{v_\rho}{\rho}-\frac{v}{\rho^2},\qquad
u_{\rho\rho} = \frac{v_{\rho\rho}}{\rho}-\frac{2v_\rho}{\rho^2}+\frac{2v}{\rho^3
},\qquad
u_{tt}=\frac{v_{tt}}{\rho}.$$

Substituting into (1):
$$\frac{v_{tt}}{\rho}=c^2\!\left[\frac{v_{\rho\rho}}{\rho}-\frac{2v_\rho}{\rho^2
}+\frac{2v}{\rho^3}+\frac{2}{\rho}\!\left(\frac{v_\rho}{\rho}-\frac{v}{\rho^2}\r
ight)\right]=c^2\,\frac{v_{\rho\rho}}{\rho}.$$

The terms in $v_\rho$ and $v$ cancel exactly, leaving
$$\boxed{\,v_{tt}=c^2\,v_{\rho\rho}\,}, \tag{3}$$
the **one-dimensional wave equation** for $v(\rho,t)$.

### Step 2. Translate the initial and boundary data

From (2) and the given initial conditions ($\rho>0$):
$$v(\rho,0)=\rho\,f(\rho),\qquad v_t(\rho,0)=\rho\,g(\rho). \tag{4}$$

Since $u$ must be regular (bounded) at the origin, $v(0,t)=\rho\,u\big|_{\rho\to
 0}=0$, i.e.
$$v(0,t)=0. \tag{5}$$

Because $f$ and $g$ depend on $\rho=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}$, they are **even** funct
ions of $\rho$. Consequently $\rho f(\rho)$ and $\rho g(\rho)$ are **odd**. Exte
nding the data in (4) to all of $\mathbb{R}$ by oddness automatically enforces (
5), so $v$ satisfies the **free-space** 1D wave equation on the whole line with
odd initial data.

### Step 3. Apply d'Alembert's formula

For (3) on $\mathbb{R}$ with data (4) (interpreted via even extension of $f,g$),
 d'Alembert's solution is
$$v(\rho,t)=\frac{1}{2}\big[(\rho+ct)\,f(\rho+ct)+(\rho-ct)\,f(\rho-ct)\big]+\fr
ac{1}{2c}\int_{\rho-ct}^{\rho+ct} s\,g(s)\,ds, \tag{6}$$
where $f(-s)=f(s)$ and $g(-s)=g(s)$.

### Step 4. Recover $u=v/\rho$

Dividing (6) by $\rho$ gives, for $\rho>0$:

$$\boxed{\;u(\rho,t)=\frac{(\rho+ct)\,f(\rho+ct)+(\rho-ct)\,f(\rho-ct)}{2\rho}+\
frac{1}{2c\rho}\int_{\rho-ct}^{\rho+ct} s\,g(s)\,ds\;} \tag{7}$$

with $f,g$ extended evenly. This is the **radial form of Kirchhoff's formula** f
or the 3D wave equation.

### Step 5. Value at the origin ($\rho=0$)

Both terms in (7) are $0/0$ at $\rho=0$. Applying L'Hôpital's rule (differentiat
ing numerator and denominator in $\rho$):

- **First term.** $\frac{d}{d\rho}\big[(\rho+ct)f(\rho+ct)+(\rho-ct)f(\rho-ct)\b
ig]\Big|_{\rho=0}=f(ct)+ct\,f'(ct)+f(ct)-ct\,f'(ct)=2f(ct)$, so the limit is $f(
ct)$.
- **Second term.** $\frac{d}{d\rho}\int_{\rho-ct}^{\rho+ct}sg(s)\,ds\Big|_{\rho=
0}=(ct)\,g(ct)-(-ct)\,g(-ct)=2ct\,g(ct)$, giving limit $t\,g(ct)$.

Hence
$$u(0,t)=f(ct)+t\,g(ct), \tag{8}$$
the expected value at the origin (consistent with Huygens' principle: only the s
phere of radius $ct$ contributes).

### Verification

- **PDE:** Direct substitution of $u=v/\rho$ showed $v_{tt}=c^2v_{\rho\rho}$, so
 (3) holds; dividing back by $\rho$ recovers (1). ✓
- **Initial data:** At $t=0$, (7) gives $\frac{\rho f(\rho)+\rho f(\rho)}{2\rho}
+\frac{1}{2c\rho}\int_{\rho}^{\rho}sg(s)\,ds=f(\rho)$, and $u_t(\rho,0)=g(\rho)$
 follows by differentiating (6) at $t=0$. ✓
- **Regularity at $\rho=0$:** $v(0,t)=0$ is guaranteed by oddness of the data, s
o $u$ is bounded at the origin. ✓

Equation (7) (with (8) at $\rho=0$) is the complete solution.
