## Proof

Define
$$I_n = \int_1^\infty \frac{1+(-1)^n e^{-nx^2}}{x^2}\,dx.$$

Since both $\frac{1}{x^2}$ and $\frac{e^{-nx^2}}{x^2}$ are integrable on $[1,\in
fty)$ (the latter is nonnegative and bounded by $\frac{1}{x^2}$), we may split t
he integral:
$$I_n = \int_1^\infty \frac{1}{x^2}\,dx + (-1)^n \int_1^\infty \frac{e^{-nx^2}}{
x^2}\,dx = 1 + (-1)^n J_n,$$
where
$$J_n = \int_1^\infty \frac{e^{-nx^2}}{x^2}\,dx.$$

**Computing the first term.** $\displaystyle\int_1^\infty \frac{1}{x^2}\,dx = \l
eft[-\frac{1}{x}\right]_1^\infty = 1.$

**Bounding $J_n$.** For $x \geq 1$ we have $x^2 \geq 1$, so $e^{-nx^2} \leq e^{-
n}$. Therefore
$$0 \leq J_n = \int_1^\infty \frac{e^{-nx^2}}{x^2}\,dx \leq e^{-n}\int_1^\infty
\frac{1}{x^2}\,dx = e^{-n}\cdot 1 = e^{-n}.$$

Since $e^{-n} \to 0$ as $n\to\infty$, by the squeeze theorem $J_n \to 0$.

**Taking the limit.** Since $|(-1)^n J_n| = J_n \to 0$,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} I_n = \lim_{n\to\infty}\bigl(1 + (-1)^n J_n\bigr) = 1 + 0 =
1.$$

The oscillating sign $(-1)^n$ is harmless because it multiplies a quantity tendi
ng to $0$.

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} \int_1^\infty \frac{1+(-1)^n e^{-nx^2}}{x^2}\,dx = 1.
}$$
