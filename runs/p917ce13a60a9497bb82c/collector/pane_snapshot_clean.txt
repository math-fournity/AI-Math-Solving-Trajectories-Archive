Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the Fourier transform of th
e indicator function of a compact convex set with $C^1$ boundary has polynomial
decay at infinity.

**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

Let $A \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ be a compact convex set with nonempty interior and
$C^1$ boundary. We use the Fourier transform convention $\widehat{f}(\xi) = \int
_{\mathbb{R}^n} f(x)\,e^{-i\xi\cdot x}\,dx$.

**Key observation.** Since $A$ is convex, its boundary $\partial A$ is locally t
he graph of a convex function, hence locally Lipschitz. The $C^1$ assumption fur
ther guarantees that the outward unit normal $n(x)$ exists and is continuous at
every point of $\partial A$. In particular, the divergence theorem applies.

**Step 1: Reduction to a boundary integral.** Fix $\xi \neq 0$ and set $v := \fr
ac{\xi}{|\xi|^2} \in \mathbb{R}^n$. Then:
$$\nabla \cdot \!\bigl(v\, e^{-i\xi\cdot x}\bigr) = (v \cdot (-i\xi))\,e^{-i\xi\
cdot x} = \frac{-i\,|\xi|^2}{|\xi|^2}\,e^{-i\xi\cdot x} = -i\,e^{-i\xi\cdot x}.$
$

Therefore $e^{-i\xi\cdot x} = i\,\nabla\cdot(v\,e^{-i\xi\cdot x})$, and integrat
ing over $A$:

$$\widehat{\mathbf{1}_A}(\xi) = \int_A e^{-i\xi\cdot x}\,dx = i\int_A \nabla\cdo
t\!\bigl(v\,e^{-i\xi\cdot x}\bigr)\,dx = i\int_{\partial A} (v\cdot n(x))\,e^{-i
\xi\cdot x}\,d\sigma(x),$$

where the last equality is the divergence theorem (valid since $\partial A$ is $
C^1$, hence Lipschitz).

**Step 2: Uniform bound.** Taking absolute values:

$$\bigl|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_A}(\xi)\bigr| \leq \int_{\partial A} |v\cdot n(x)|\,
d\sigma(x) \leq |v|\int_{\partial A} d\sigma = \frac{\operatorname{Area}(\partia
l A)}{|\xi|}.$$

Since $A$ is compact, $\operatorname{Area}(\partial A) < \infty$. Thus, for all
$\xi \neq 0$:

$$\bigl|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_A}(\xi)\bigr| \leq \frac{\operatorname{Area}(\partia
l A)}{|\xi|}.$$

**Step 3: Conclusion.** Setting $C := \operatorname{Area}(\partial A)$, we have:

$$\widehat{\mathbf{1}_A}(\xi) \ll |\xi|^{-1} \quad \text{as } |\xi| \to \infty.$
$

Taking $c = 1$ (or any $0 < c \leq 1$), this shows that $\mathbf{1}_A$ is of mod
erate growth. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The argument uses only convexity (which gives a Lipschitz boundary a
nd finite surface area). The $C^1$ hypothesis is sufficient but not necessary —
the result holds for any compact convex set with nonempty interior.
