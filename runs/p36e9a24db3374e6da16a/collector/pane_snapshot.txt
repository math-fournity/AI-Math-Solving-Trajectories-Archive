$$\Pi_{xy} = \{x = y\}, \quad \Pi_{yz} = \{y = z\}, \quad \Pi_{xz} = \{x = z\}.$
$

So $N = \mathbb{R}^3 \setminus (\Pi_{xy} \cup \Pi_{yz} \cup \Pi_{xz})$.

**Step 2: All three planes share a common line.**

Each plane contains the diagonal line $L = \{(t, t, t) : t \in \mathbb{R}\}$. To
 count the chambers, take a 2-plane transverse to $L$, namely $P = \{x + y + z =
 0\}$, which intersects $L$ at the origin. Each plane $\Pi$ intersects $P$ in a
line through the origin:

- $\Pi_{xy} \cap P$: $\{(t, t, -2t)\}$,
- $\Pi_{yz} \cap P$: $\{(-2t, t, t)\}$,
- $\Pi_{xz} \cap P$: $\{(t, -2t, t)\}$.

These are three distinct lines through the origin in $P \cong \mathbb{R}^2$, whi
ch divide $\mathbb{R}^2$ into **6 sectors**.

**Step 3: $N$ has 6 connected components, each $\cong \mathbb{R}^3$.**

By the product decomposition $\mathbb{R}^3 \cong P \times L \cong \mathbb{R}^2 \

$$C_1 = \{x < y < z\},\; C_2 = \{x < z < y\},\; C_3 = \{y < x < z\},\; C_4 = \{y
 < z < x\},\; C_5 = \{z < x < y\},\; C_6 = \{z < y < x\}.$$

Each $C_i$ is an open convex set (defined by strict linear inequalities), hence
homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^3$. Since the planes are closed and separate these c
hambers, $N$ is the **disjoint union**:

$$N = \bigsqcup_{i=1}^{6} C_i \cong \bigsqcup_{i=1}^{6} \mathbb{R}^3.$$

**Step 4: Each component is a product of 1-manifolds.**

$\mathbb{R}^3 = \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}$ is a product of

$$N \cong \bigsqcup_{i=1}^{6} \underbrace{(\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R} \times \
mathbb{R})}_{\text{product of 1-manifolds}}$$

This is a finite disjoint union (of 6 pieces) of products of one-dimensional man
ifolds.

**Therefore, $N$ is homeomorphic to a finite union of products of one-dimensiona
