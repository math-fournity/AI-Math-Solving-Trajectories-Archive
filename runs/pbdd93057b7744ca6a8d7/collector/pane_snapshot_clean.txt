# Solution

**Claim:** Yes, $\begin{bmatrix}t & 1-t \\ 1-t & t\end{bmatrix}^n = I$ in $\math
bb{Z}_n[t^{\pm 1}]/(t-1)^2$.

## Proof

Let $R = \mathbb{Z}_n[t^{\pm 1}]/(t-1)^2$ and set $u = t - 1 \in R$. By definiti
on of the quotient, $u^2 = (t-1)^2 = 0$ in $R$, and $t = 1 + u$. Note $t$ is inv
ertible in $R$ since $(1+u)(1-u) = 1 - u^2 = 1$, so $t^{-1} = 1 - u$, confirming
 the Laurent structure is preserved.

Rewrite the matrix $A$ in terms of $u$:

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1+u & -u \\ -u & 1+u \end{bmatrix} = I + u\begin{bmatrix}
1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}.$$

Let $B = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$. Observe that $B^2 = \b
egin{bmatrix} 2 & -2 \\ -2 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = 2B$, and by induction $B^k = 2^{k
-1}B$ for all $k \geq 1$.

Since $u$ is a scalar in the commutative ring $R$, it commutes with every matrix
 entry and hence with $B$ and $I$. Therefore the binomial theorem applies:

$$(I + uB)^n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k} u^k B^k.$$

Because $u^2 = 0$, every term with $k \geq 2$ vanishes (as $u^k = 0$). Only $k =
 0$ and $k = 1$ survive:

$$A^n = I + \binom{n}{1} u B = I + n\,u\,B.$$

Now $n\,u = n(t-1)$ in $R$. The coefficient $n$ lies in $\mathbb{Z}_n$, where $n
 \equiv 0 \pmod{n}$. Hence $n\,u = 0$ in $R$, giving

$$A^n = I + 0 \cdot B = I.$$

**Verification (sanity check, $n=2$):** In $\mathbb{Z}_2$, we have $u = t-1 = t+
1$ and $t^2 = 2t-1 = -1 = 1$. Then $(1+t)^2 = (t-1)^2 = 0$ and $t(1+t) = t + t^2
 = t+1$. Computing directly:
$$A^2 = \begin{bmatrix} t^2+(1+t)^2 & 2t(1+t) \\ 2t(1+t) & (1+t)^2+t^2 \end{bmat
rix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1+0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0+1 \end{bmatrix} = I,$$
since $2(t+1) = 0$ in $\mathbb{Z}_2$. This confirms the general result. $\blacks
quare$
