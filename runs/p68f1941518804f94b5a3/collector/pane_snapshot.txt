## Solution

rmalization notes. Proceeding.

Let $z = e^{i\theta}$, so that as $\theta$ runs from $0$ to $2\pi$, $z$ traverse
s the unit circle $|z|=1$ once counterclockwise. Then
$$dz = i e^{i\theta}\,d\theta = iz\,d\theta \quad\Longrightarrow\quad d\theta =
\frac{dz}{iz}.$$

Substituting into the integral:
$$\int_{0}^{2\pi} e^{e^{i\theta}}\,d\theta = \oint_{|z|=1} e^{z}\,\frac{dz}{iz}
= \frac{1}{i}\oint_{|z|=1} \frac{e^{z}}{z}\,dz.$$

The integrand $f(z) = \dfrac{e^{z}}{z}$ has a simple pole at $z=0$ with residue
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{e^{z}}{z} = \lim_{z\to 0} z\cdot\frac{e^{z}}{z}
= e^{0} = 1.$$

Equivalently, by Cauchy's integral formula applied to the entire function $g(z)=
e^{z}$ at the point $z_0=0$ (which lies inside $|z|=1$):
$$\oint_{|z|=1} \frac{e^{z}}{z}\,dz = 2\pi i\, g(0) = 2\pi i \cdot 1 = 2\pi i.$$

Therefore
$$\int_{0}^{2\pi} e^{e^{i\theta}}\,d\theta = \frac{1}{i}\cdot 2\pi i = 2\pi.$$
