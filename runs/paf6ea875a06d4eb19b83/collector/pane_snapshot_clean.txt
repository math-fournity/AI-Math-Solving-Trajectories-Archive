## Construction

**Setting.** Let $A:\ell^2\to\ell^2$ be the compact diagonal operator
$$Ae_n = \tfrac{1}{n}\,e_n,\qquad n=1,2,\dots$$
where $\{e_n\}$ is the standard orthonormal basis. $A$ is self-adjoint, positive
, and injective with $\lambda_n=1/n\to 0$.

**Exact problem.** Take the exact solution $x_0 = e_1$, so the exact data is
$$y = Ax_0 = e_1 \in R(A).$$
Since $A$ is injective, $x_0=e_1$ is the unique (hence minimum-norm) solution.

**Noisy data.** For $\delta\in(0,1/2)$, set $N(\delta):=\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor$
 (so $N\to\infty$ and $N\ge 2$) and define
$$y_\delta := e_1 + \delta\, e_{N(\delta)}.$$
Then $\|y_\delta - y\| = \delta$, satisfying the noise level assumption.

**Regularization parameter.** Choose
$$\alpha(\delta) := \delta^2.$$
Then $\alpha(\delta)\to 0$ (condition 1 of Theorem 5.1 holds), but
$$\frac{\delta^2}{\alpha(\delta)} = 1 \not\to 0,$$
so condition 2 **fails**.

---

## The regularized solution

The Tikhonov regularized solution minimizes $\|Ax-y_\delta\|^2+\alpha\|x\|^2$ an
d is given by
$$x_\alpha^\delta = (A^2+\alpha I)^{-1}A\,y_\delta = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\l
ambda_n}{\lambda_n^2+\alpha}\,\langle y_\delta,e_n\rangle\,e_n.$$

Since $y_\delta = e_1 + \delta\,e_N$, only the $n=1$ and $n=N$ components are no
nzero:
$$x_\alpha^\delta = \frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_1^2+\alpha}\,e_1 \;+\; \frac{\lambd
a_N\,\delta}{\lambda_N^2+\alpha}\,e_N = \frac{1}{1+\delta^2}\,e_1 \;+\; \frac{(1
/N)\,\delta}{1/N^2+\delta^2}\,e_N.$$

---

## Error analysis

$$\|x_\alpha^\delta - x_0\|^2 = \underbrace{\left|\frac{1}{1+\delta^2}-1\right|^
2}_{\text{Term I}} \;+\; \underbrace{\left(\frac{(1/N)\,\delta}{1/N^2+\delta^2}\
right)^2}_{\text{Term II}}.$$

**Term I** (regularization error with exact data):
$$\left|\frac{1}{1+\delta^2}-1\right|^2 = \left(\frac{\delta^2}{1+\delta^2}\righ
t)^2 \longrightarrow 0.$$

**Term II** (data-error propagation): With $N=\lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor$ we have $
\frac{1}{N+1}<\delta\le\frac{1}{N}$. Consider the function
$$f(t)=\frac{(1/N)\,t}{1/N^2+t^2},\qquad t\in\!\left(\tfrac{1}{N+1},\,\tfrac{1}{
N}\right].$$

Its derivative is
$$f'(t)=\frac{(1/N)(1/N^2-t^2)}{(1/N^2+t^2)^2},$$
which is $\ge 0$ on $(0,1/N]$, so $f$ is increasing. Therefore:

- **Maximum** at $t=1/N$: $\;f(1/N)=\dfrac{1/N^2}{2/N^2}=\dfrac{1}{2}$.
- **Minimum** at $t=1/(N+1)$:
$$f\!\left(\tfrac{1}{N+1}\right)=\frac{N(N+1)}{2N^2+2N+1}\;\longrightarrow\;\fra
c{1}{2}\quad\text{as }N\to\infty.$$

Hence Term II $\to (1/2)^2 = 1/4$ as $\delta\to 0$.

---

## Conclusion

Combining both terms:
$$\|x_{\alpha(\delta)}^\delta - x_0\|^2 \;\longrightarrow\; 0 + \tfrac{1}{4} = \
tfrac{1}{4},\qquad\text{i.e.,}\qquad \|x_{\alpha(\delta)}^\delta - x_0\| \;\long
rightarrow\; \tfrac{1}{2}\neq 0.$$

The approximate solutions **do not converge** to the exact solution $x_0=e_1$, e
ven though $\alpha(\delta)=\delta^2\to 0$.

**What went wrong.** The data error $\delta\,e_N$ was placed in the direction $e
_N$ with $\lambda_N\approx\sqrt{\alpha}=\delta$, precisely where the amplificati
on factor $\lambda_N/(\lambda_N^2+\alpha)$ is maximized. The propagated data err
or is $\sim\delta/(2\sqrt{\alpha})=\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\delta^2/\alpha}=\tfrac{1}{
2}$, which stays bounded away from $0$ because $\delta^2/\alpha=1\not\to 0$.

This demonstrates that condition $\delta^2/\alpha(\delta)\to 0$ is **necessary**
: without it, the data-error propagation term $\delta/\sqrt{\alpha}$ need not va
nish, and an adversarial choice of noise direction (concentrated at the singular
 value $\lambda\approx\sqrt{\alpha}$) prevents convergence.
