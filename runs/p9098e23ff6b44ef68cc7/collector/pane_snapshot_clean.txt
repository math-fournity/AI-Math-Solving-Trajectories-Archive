## Solution

**Answer: Not in general.** The restriction $f|_T$ need not be surjective. Below
 is a counterexample, followed by a remark that the statement *does* hold when $
G$ is connected (so the obstruction is purely the disconnectedness of $G$).

---

### Counterexample

Take
- $G = \mathrm{O}(2)$, the orthogonal group of the plane — a compact Lie group;
- $T = \mathrm{SO}(2) \cong S^1$, the maximal torus of $G$ (it is the identity c
omponent, hence the unique maximal connected compact abelian subgroup);
- $H = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, a discrete (hence $0$-dimensional) Abelian Lie g
roup;
- $f = \det \colon \mathrm{O}(2) \to \{\pm 1\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.

**Verification of the hypotheses.**

1. $\mathrm{O}(2)$ is compact (closed and bounded in $\mathrm{M}_2(\mathbb{R})$)
. ✓
2. $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is Abelian. ✓
3. $\det$ is a Lie-group homomorphism ($\det(AB)=\det A\det B$) and is surjectiv
e ($\det\!\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}=-1$). ✓
4. The maximal torus of $\mathrm{O}(2)$ is $\mathrm{SO}(2)$: every torus is conn
ected, hence lies in the identity component $\mathrm{SO}(2)$; and $\mathrm{SO}(2
)\cong S^1$ is itself a torus. ✓

**The restriction is not surjective.** Every element of $\mathrm{SO}(2)$ is a ro
tation, hence has determinant $+1$. Thus
$$f|_T = \det|_{\mathrm{SO}(2)} \equiv 1,$$
the trivial homomorphism. Its image is $\{1\}\subsetneq \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}=H
$. So $f|_T$ is **not** surjective. $\square$

---

### Remark: the connected case is true

For context, the statement *is* true when $G$ is connected. Sketch: since $H$ is
 Abelian, the commutator subgroup $[G,G]\subseteq\ker f$, so $f$ factors through
 the abelianization $G/[G,G]$. For connected compact $G$ with maximal torus $T$,
 writing $T_{\mathrm{ss}}=T\cap[G,G]$ (the maximal torus of the semisimple part)
, one has
$$G/[G,G]\;\cong\; Z(G)_0\big/\bigl(Z(G)_0\cap[G,G]\bigr)\;\cong\; T/T_{\mathrm{
ss}},$$
and $T_{\mathrm{ss}}\subseteq[G,G]\subseteq\ker f$. Hence $f|_T$ kills exactly $
T_{\mathrm{ss}}$ and induces the same surjection $G/[G,G]\twoheadrightarrow H$;
consequently $f|_T\twoheadrightarrow H$.

The counterexample above exploits precisely the component group: $\mathrm{O}(2)/
\mathrm{SO}(2)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ carries the entire image of $f$, whil
e the maximal torus sits inside the identity component and misses it.

---

**Conclusion:** No. A surjective homomorphism from a compact Lie group to an Abe
lian Lie group need not restrict to a surjection on the maximal torus; $\det\col
on\mathrm{O}(2)\to\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is an explicit counterexample.
