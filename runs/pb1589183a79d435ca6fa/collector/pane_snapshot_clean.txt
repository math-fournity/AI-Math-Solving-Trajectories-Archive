## Solution

The statement is **false**. We provide a counterexample in $\mathbb{R}^2$.

**Construction.** Let
$$U = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^2 : 1 < |x| < 2\} \quad\text{(open annulus)}, \qquad V
= \{x \in \mathbb{R}^2 : 0 < |x| < 1\} \quad\text{(punctured open disk)}.$$

Both $U$ and $V$ are bounded open subsets of $\mathbb{R}^2$.

**Step 1: $U$ and $V$ are homeomorphic.** Define $h: V \to U$ by
$$h(x) = \frac{x}{|x|}\bigl(1 + |x|\bigr).$$
If $|x| = r \in (0,1)$, then $|h(x)| = 1 + r \in (1,2)$, so $h$ maps $V$ into $U
$. The map is continuous, and its inverse $h^{-1}: U \to V$ is
$$h^{-1}(y) = \frac{y}{|y|}\bigl(|y| - 1\bigr),$$
since $|y| \in (1,2)$ gives $|y|-1 \in (0,1)$. Both maps are continuous, so $h$
is a homeomorphism $V \to U$, and $h^{-1}: U \to V$ is the desired homeomorphism
.

**Step 2: The closures are not homeomorphic.** We have
$$\overline{U} = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^2 : 1 \le |x| \le 2\}, \qquad \overline{V} =
 \{x \in \mathbb{R}^2 : 0 \le |x| \le 1\} = D^2 \quad\text{(closed unit disk)}.$
$

We distinguish these two compact spaces by their fundamental groups:

- $\overline{V} = D^2$ is **contractible** (radial deformation retract to the or
igin), so $\pi_1(\overline{V}) = 0$ (simply connected).
- $\overline{U} = \{1 \le |x| \le 2\}$ **deformation retracts** onto the circle
$\{|x| = \tfrac{3}{2}\} \cong S^1$ via the radial retraction $F_t(x) = \frac{x}{
|x|}\bigl((1-t)|x| + t\cdot\tfrac{3}{2}\bigr)$. Hence $\pi_1(\overline{U}) \cong
 \pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z} \ne 0$.

Since the fundamental group is a topological invariant, $\overline{U}$ and $\ove
rline{V}$ are **not homeomorphic**.

**Step 3: No extension can exist.** If a homeomorphism $H: \overline{U} \to \ove
rline{V}$ extending $h$ existed, then $\overline{U}$ and $\overline{V}$ would be
 homeomorphic, contradicting Step 2. $\square$

**Remark.** The obstruction is topological: $U$ and $V$ are both homeomorphic to
 $S^1 \times (0,1)$, but their closures in $\mathbb{R}^2$ "fill in" different bo
undary components — the annulus closure keeps a hole (nontrivial $\pi_1$) while
the punctured-disk closure fills the puncture (trivial $\pi_1$). No homeomorphis
m of the open sets can control which boundary component gets filled, so the exte
nsion fails.
