## Proof

**Given:** A group $(G, \cdot)$ with an element $a \in G$ such that $ax = x^4 a$
 for all $x \in G$. Equivalently,
$$axa^{-1} = x^4 \quad \text{for all } x \in G. \tag{0}$$

**Goal:** Solve $x^7 = e$.

---

### Step 1. Conjugation by $a$ is the automorphism $\phi(x) = x^4$.

Equation (0) says $\phi(x) := axa^{-1} = x^4$. Since conjugation is always an au
tomorphism, the map $\phi: G \to G$, $x \mapsto x^4$, is an automorphism. In par
ticular:

- $\phi$ is a **homomorphism**: $(xy)^4 = x^4 y^4$ for all $x, y \in G$. $\tag{1
}$
- $\phi$ is **bijective**: every element of $G$ is a fourth power.

---

### Step 2. Derive that $x^3$ commutes with every fourth power.

From (1), left-multiply $(xy)^4 = x^4 y^4$ by $x^{-1}$:

$$x^{-1}(xy)^4 = (yx)^3 y = x^3 y^4.$$

Right-multiply by $y^{-1}$:

$$(yx)^3 = x^3 y^3 \quad \text{for all } x, y. \tag{2}$$

Now compute $(yx)^4$ two ways. Using (2):

$$(yx)^4 = (yx)^3(yx) = x^3 y^3 \cdot yx = x^3 y^4 x.$$

Using (1) with $x \leftrightarrow y$:

$$(yx)^4 = y^4 x^4.$$

Equating: $x^3 y^4 x = y^4 x^4$. Left-multiply by $x^{-3}$ and right-multiply by
 $x^{-1}$:

$$y^4 = x^{-3} y^4 x^3, \quad\text{i.e.,}\quad x^3 y^4 = y^4 x^3 \quad \text{for
 all } x, y. \tag{3}$$

---

### Step 3. $x^3 \in Z(G)$ for all $x$.

Since $\phi$ is bijective, every $g \in G$ can be written as $g = y^4$ for some
$y$. Substituting into (3):

$$x^3 g = g\, x^3 \quad \text{for all } x, g \in G.$$

Therefore $x^3$ is central for every $x \in G$:
$$x^3 \in Z(G) \quad \text{for all } x \in G. \tag{4}$$

---

### Step 4. Combine with $x^7 = e$.

Let $x \in G$ satisfy $x^7 = e$. By (4), $x^3$ is central, so it commutes with $
a$:

$$a\, x^3\, a^{-1} = x^3. \tag{5}$$

But using (0) three times (or $(axa^{-1})^3 = (x^4)^3$):

$$a\, x^3\, a^{-1} = (axa^{-1})^3 = (x^4)^3 = x^{12}. \tag{6}$$

From (5) and (6): $x^3 = x^{12}$, hence
$$x^9 = e. \tag{7}$$

---

### Step 5. Conclude $x = e$.

We have $x^7 = e$ and $x^9 = e$. Since $\gcd(7, 9) = 1$, by Bézout's identity ($
4 \cdot 7 - 3 \cdot 9 = 1$):

$$x = x^1 = x^{4 \cdot 7 - 3 \cdot 9} = (x^7)^4 \cdot (x^9)^{-3} = e^4 \cdot e^{
-3} = e.$$

---

### Conclusion

The unique solution of $x^7 = e$ is

$$\boxed{x = e}.$$
