Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest positive integer $b$ such t
hat $N(b) = 20$, where $N(b)$ counts positive integers $a$ for which $x^2 + ax +
 b = 0$ has both integer roots.

## Setting Up the Counting Formula

If $x^2 + ax + b = 0$ has integer roots $r, s$, then by Vieta's formulas:
- $r + s = -a$, so $a = -(r+s) > 0$ requires $r + s < 0$
- $rs = b > 0$, so $r, s$ have the same sign

Since $r+s < 0$ and $rs > 0$, both roots must be **negative**. Setting $r = -m$,
 $s = -n$ with $m, n$ positive integers:
- $a = m + n$
- $b = mn$

So $N(b)$ equals the number of **distinct** values of $m + n$ over all ways to w
rite $b = mn$ with $m, n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.

## Distinct Sums Analysis

Each divisor $d$ of $b$ yields a pair $(d, b/d)$ with sum $d + b/d$. I claim two
 different divisors $d_1 \neq d_2$ give the same sum **only if** $d_1 d_2 = b$ (
i.e., they are complementary).

**Proof:** If $d_1 + b/d_1 = d_2 + b/d_2$, then $(d_1 - d_2) = b(d_1 - d_2)/(d_1
 d_2)$. If $d_1 \neq d_2$, dividing gives $d_1 d_2 = b$.

Therefore distinct sums correspond exactly to **unordered pairs** $\{d, b/d\}$:
- **$b$ not a perfect square:** every divisor pairs with a distinct complement,
so $N(b) = \tau(b)/2$
- **$b$ a perfect square:** the divisor $\sqrt{b}$ pairs with itself, so $N(b) =
 (\tau(b)+1)/2$

**Verification:** $b=6$: $\tau=4$, not a square, $N=2$. Divisors 1,2,3,6 → sums
7, 5. ✓ $b=4$: $\tau=3$, square, $N=2$. Divisors 1,2,4 → sums 5, 4. ✓

## Solving $N(b) = 20$

**Case 1: $b$ not a perfect square, $\tau(b) = 40$.**

I enumerate all factorizations of $40$ into parts $\geq 2$ and find the smallest
 $b$ for each (assigning largest exponents to smallest primes):

| Factorization of 40 | Exponents | Smallest $b$ |
|---|---|---|
| $\{40\}$ | $39$ | $2^{39}$ |
| $\{20,2\}$ | $19,1$ | $2^{19}\cdot 3 = 1572864$ |
| $\{10,4\}$ | $9,3$ | $2^9 \cdot 3^3 = 13824$ |
| $\{10,2,2\}$ | $9,1,1$ | $2^9 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 = 7680$ |
| $\{8,5\}$ | $7,4$ | $2^7 \cdot 3^4 = 10368$ |
| $\{5,4,2\}$ | $4,3,1$ | $2^4 \cdot 3^3 \cdot 5 = 2160$ |
| $\{5,2,2,2\}$ | $4,1,1,1$ | $2^4 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 = \mathbf{1680}$ |

The maximum number of parts is 4 (since $2^4 \cdot 5 = 40$), so no 5-prime patte
rn exists. The minimum is $b = 1680 = 2^4 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7$, with exponen
ts $(4,1,1,1)$ — not a perfect square since exponents 1 are odd. ✓

**Case 2: $b$ a perfect square, $\tau(b) = 39$.**

Since $39 = 3 \times 13$, the exponent patterns (all exponents even) are:
- $\{39\}$: exponent $38$ → $b = 2^{38}$ (huge)
- $\{13, 3\}$: exponents $12, 2$ → $b = 2^{12} \cdot 3^2 = 36864$

The smallest is $36864 \gg 1680$.

## Conclusion

The smallest $b$ with $N(b) = 20$ is:

$$b = 2^4 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 = \boxed{1680}$$

**Final check:** $\tau(1680) = 5 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 40$, $1680$ is not a
perfect square, so $N(1680) = 40/2 = 20$. ✓
