## Solution

The statement is **false**. I provide a counterexample.

**Counterexample.** Let
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B = \begin{pmatrix} 1
 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Both are real symmetric (hence Hermitian).

**Step 1: Verify $A \geq 0$.** $A$ is diagonal with positive entries, so $A \suc
ceq 0$, and thus $|A| = \sqrt{A^2} = A$.

**Step 2: Verify $A \geq B$, i.e., $A - B \succeq 0$.**
$$A - B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$
The eigenvalues are $0$ and $2$, both non-negative. So $A - B \succeq 0$. ✓

**Step 3: Verify $B \geq -A$, i.e., $A + B \succeq 0$.**
$$A + B = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$
The trace is $4 > 0$ and the determinant is $3 - 1 = 2 > 0$, so both eigenvalues
 are positive. Thus $A + B \succ 0$. ✓

So the hypothesis $A \geq B \geq -A$ is satisfied.

**Step 4: Compute $|B|$.** We have
$$B^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \
\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$
This is positive definite (trace $= 3 > 0$, determinant $= 1 > 0$). For a $2 \ti
mes 2$ positive definite matrix $M$ with $\det M > 0$, the square root is given
by
$$\sqrt{M} = \frac{M + \sqrt{\det M}\cdot I}{\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}(M) + 2\sqrt
{\det M}}}.$$
Here $\operatorname{tr}(B^2) = 3$ and $\det(B^2) = 1$, so
$$|B| = \sqrt{B^2} = \frac{B^2 + I}{\sqrt{3 + 2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\begin{pma
trix} 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

**Step 5: Check whether $|A| \geq |B|$, i.e., $A - |B| \succeq 0$.**
$$A - |B| = \begin{pmatrix} 2 - \frac{3}{\sqrt{5}} & -\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \\[4pt]
 -\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} & 1 - \frac{2}{\sqrt{5}} \end{pmatrix}.$$

The determinant is:
$$\det(A - |B|) = \left(2 - \frac{3}{\sqrt{5}}\right)\left(1 - \frac{2}{\sqrt{5}
}\right) - \frac{1}{5}.$$

Expanding:
$$\left(2 - \frac{3}{\sqrt{5}}\right)\left(1 - \frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\right) = 2 - \
frac{4}{\sqrt{5}} - \frac{3}{\sqrt{5}} + \frac{6}{5} = \frac{16}{5} - \frac{7}{\
sqrt{5}}.$$

Therefore:
$$\det(A - |B|) = \frac{16}{5} - \frac{7}{\sqrt{5}} - \frac{1}{5} = 3 - \frac{7}
{\sqrt{5}} = 3 - \frac{7\sqrt{5}}{5}.$$

Since $\sqrt{5} > 2$, we have $\frac{7\sqrt{5}}{5} > \frac{14}{5} = 2.8$. More p
recisely, $\frac{7\sqrt{5}}{5} \approx 3.130 > 3$, so
$$\det(A - |B|) = 3 - \frac{7\sqrt{5}}{5} < 0.$$

A $2 \times 2$ Hermitian matrix with negative determinant cannot be positive sem
idefinite (since the determinant equals the product of eigenvalues, at least one
 eigenvalue must be negative). Therefore $A - |B| \not\succeq 0$, which means $|
A| \not\geq |B|$.

**Conclusion.** We have exhibited Hermitian matrices $A, B$ satisfying $A \geq B
 \geq -A$ but $|A| \not\geq |B|$. The statement is **false**.
