## Solution

**Claim.** The map $f(x)=|x|^{q-1}x$ is Hölder-continuous on $\mathbb{R}^n$ with
 Hölder exponent $q$, and $q$ is optimal (no exponent $\alpha>q$ is possible).

**Notation.** For $x\neq 0$, write $x=r\hat{x}$ with $r=|x|$ and $|\hat{x}|=1$.
Then
$$f(x)=|x|^{q-1}x=|x|^{q}\hat{x}=r^{q}\hat{x},\qquad f(0)=0.$$

---

### Upper bound: $f$ is $q$-Hölder

We prove $|f(x)-f(y)|\le C\,|x-y|^{q}$ with $C=(2-q)\,2^{1-q}$.

**Case 1: $y=0$ (or $x=0$).** Then
$$|f(x)-f(0)|=|f(x)|=|x|^{q}=|x-0|^{q},$$
so the bound holds with constant $1\le C$.

**Case 2: $x,y\neq 0$.** Write $x=r\hat{x}$, $y=s\hat{y}$ with $r,s>0$ and $|\ha
t{x}|=|\hat{y}|=1$. By symmetry assume $r\ge s$ and set $t:=s/r\in(0,1]$. Then
$$|f(x)-f(y)|^{2}=r^{2q}\bigl|\hat{x}-t^{q}\hat{y}\bigr|^{2},\qquad |x-y|^{2}=r^
{2}\bigl|\hat{x}-t\hat{y}\bigr|^{2}.$$
Let $d:=|\hat{x}-t\hat{y}|\ge 0$. It suffices to show
$$\bigl|\hat{x}-t^{q}\hat{y}\bigr|\le (2-q)\,2^{1-q}\,d^{\,q}. \tag{$\ast$}$$

We use the triangle inequality:
$$\bigl|\hat{x}-t^{q}\hat{y}\bigr|\le \bigl|\hat{x}-t\hat{y}\bigr|+\bigl|t\hat{y
}-t^{q}\hat{y}\bigr|=d+(t^{q}-t),$$
since $0<q<1$ and $t\in(0,1]$ imply $t^{q}\ge t$.

**Bounding $t^{q}-t$.** Define $\phi(t)=t^{q}-t-(1-q)(1-t)=t^{q}-qt-(1-q)$. Then
 $\phi(1)=0$ and $\phi'(t)=q(t^{q-1}-1)>0$ for $t\in(0,1)$ (because $q-1<0$ make
s $t^{q-1}>1$). Hence $\phi$ is increasing on $(0,1)$ with $\phi(1)=0$, so $\phi
(t)\le 0$, i.e.
$$t^{q}-t\le (1-q)(1-t). \tag{1}$$

**Bounding $1-t$ by $d$.** By the reverse triangle inequality,
$$d=|\hat{x}-t\hat{y}|\ge \bigl||\hat{x}|-t|\hat{y}|\bigr|=|1-t|=1-t. \tag{2}$$

Combining (1) and (2):
$$t^{q}-t\le (1-q)(1-t)\le (1-q)d.$$
Therefore
$$\bigl|\hat{x}-t^{q}\hat{y}\bigr|\le d+(1-q)d=(2-q)d.$$

Finally $d\le |\hat{x}|+t|\hat{y}|=1+t\le 2$, so $d=d^{q}\cdot d^{1-q}\le 2^{1-q
}d^{q}$. Thus
$$\bigl|\hat{x}-t^{q}\hat{y}\bigr|\le (2-q)\,2^{1-q}\,d^{\,q},$$
which is ($\ast$). Multiplying by $r^{q}$ and using $|x-y|=rd$:
$$|f(x)-f(y)|\le (2-q)\,2^{1-q}\,|x-y|^{q}.$$

So $f$ is $q$-Hölder (globally, with constant $C=(2-q)2^{1-q}$).

---

### Optimality: no exponent $\alpha>q$ works

Take $y=0$ and any $x\neq 0$. If $f$ were $\alpha$-Hölder for some $\alpha>q$, t
here would exist $C_{\alpha}<\infty$ with
$$|x|^{q}=|f(x)-f(0)|\le C_{\alpha}|x|^{\alpha}\quad\forall x,$$
i.e. $|x|^{q-\alpha}\le C_{\alpha}$. But $q-\alpha<0$, so $|x|^{q-\alpha}\to\inf
ty$ as $|x|\to 0$, a contradiction. Hence no $\alpha>q$ is admissible.

---

### Conclusion

The map $f(x)=|x|^{q-1}x$ **is Hölder-continuous**, and the (sharp) Hölder expon
ent is
$$\boxed{\,\alpha=q\,}.$$
