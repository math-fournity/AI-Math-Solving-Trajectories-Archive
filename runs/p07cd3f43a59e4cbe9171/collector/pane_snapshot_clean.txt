## Proof

**Claim:** If two finite groups $A$ and $B$ share no simple subgroups in common
(i.e., there is no simple group isomorphic to a subgroup of both $A$ and $B$), t
hen $|A|$ and $|B|$ are relatively prime.

**Proof.** Suppose for contradiction that $|A|$ and $|B|$ are not relatively pri
me. Then there exists a prime $p$ dividing both $|A|$ and $|B|$.

By **Cauchy's theorem**, since $p \mid |A|$, the group $A$ contains an element o
f order $p$. The cyclic subgroup $\langle a \rangle \le A$ generated by such an
element has order $p$, hence is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_p$. A group of prime o
rder has no nontrivial proper subgroups, so $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is **simple**.

By the same reasoning, since $p \mid |B|$, the group $B$ contains a subgroup iso
morphic to $\mathbb{Z}_p$, which is also simple.

Thus $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is a simple group isomorphic to a subgroup of both $A$ and $
B$. This means $A$ and $B$ share a simple subgroup in common — contradicting the
 hypothesis.

Therefore no prime divides both $|A|$ and $|B|$, which is exactly the statement
$\gcd(|A|, |B|) = 1$. $\blacksquare$

**Conclusion:** Yes, the orders of $A$ and $B$ must be relatively prime.
