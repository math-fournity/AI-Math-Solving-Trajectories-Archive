hat every element of a compact subgroup $G$ of $GL_n(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ can be conju
gated into $GL_n(p^{-k}\mathbb{Z}_p)$.

**Answer: $k = 1$.**

## Proof

**Key Theorem (Lattice Stabilization):** Every compact subgroup $G$ of $GL_n(\ma
thbb{Q}_p)$ is conjugate to a subgroup of $GL_n(\mathbb{Z}_p)$.

*Proof of the theorem.* Since $G$ is compact in $GL_n(\mathbb{Q}_p) \subset M_n(
\mathbb{Q}_p)$, it is closed and bounded. Boundedness means there exists an inte
ger $m \geq 0$ such that every entry of every $g \in G$ and every entry of every
 $g^{-1}$ lies in $p^{-m}\mathbb{Z}_p$.

Define the $\mathbb{Z}_p$-submodule:
$$\Lambda = \sum_{g \in G} g\left(\mathbb{Z}_p^n\right) \subset \mathbb{Q}_p^n.$
$

Since all entries of all $g \in G$ lie in $p^{-m}\mathbb{Z}_p$, we have $\Lambda
 \subset p^{-m}\mathbb{Z}_p^n$, which is a free $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module of rank $n
$. Therefore $\Lambda$ is finitely generated over $\mathbb{Z}_p$. Since $e \in G
$, we have $\mathbb{Z}_p^n \subseteq \Lambda$, so $\Lambda$ has rank $n$—it is a
 $\mathbb{Z}_p$-lattice in $\mathbb{Q}_p^n$.

For any $h \in G$:
$$h \cdot \Lambda = \sum_{g \in G} hg\left(\mathbb{Z}_p^n\right) = \sum_{g' \in
G} g'\left(\mathbb{Z}_p^n\right) = \Lambda,$$
where we used that left multiplication by $h$ is a bijection $G \to G$. Thus $\L
ambda$ is $G$-stable.

Since $\Lambda$ is a lattice, there exists $y \in GL_n(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ with $\Lam
bda = y\,\mathbb{Z}_p^n$. The $G$-stability gives $g \cdot y\,\mathbb{Z}_p^n = y
\,\mathbb{Z}_p^n$ for all $g \in G$, i.e., $y^{-1}gy\,\mathbb{Z}_p^n = \mathbb{Z
}_p^n$, which means $y^{-1}gy \in GL_n(\mathbb{Z}_p)$. $\square$

**Applying the theorem.** Given any compact subgroup $G$ and any $g \in G$, the
theorem provides $y \in GL_n(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ such that $y^{-1}gy \in GL_n(\mathbb
{Z}_p)$. Since $\mathbb{Z}_p \subseteq p^{-k}\mathbb{Z}_p$ for every $k \geq 0$,
 we have:
$$y^{-1}gy \in GL_n(\mathbb{Z}_p) \subseteq GL_n(p^{-k}\mathbb{Z}_p) \quad \text
{for all } k \geq 0.$$

(Here $GL_n(p^{-k}\mathbb{Z}_p)$ denotes invertible matrices with entries in $p^
{-k}\mathbb{Z}_p$; since $y^{-1}gy$ and its inverse $y^{-1}g^{-1}y$ both lie in
$GL_n(\mathbb{Z}_p) \subseteq M_n(p^{-k}\mathbb{Z}_p)$, the membership holds in
the group sense as well.)

**Conclusion.** The condition is satisfied for $k = 0$, hence for every positive
 $k$. The smallest positive integer is:

$$\boxed{k = 1}.$$
