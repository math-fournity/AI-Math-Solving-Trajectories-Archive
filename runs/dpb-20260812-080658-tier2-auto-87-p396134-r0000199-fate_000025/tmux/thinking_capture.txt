Proof

Let $D$ be an integral domain and let $m, n$ be coprime positive integers.
Suppose $a, b \in D$ satisfy $a^m = b^m$ and $a^n = b^n$. We show $a = b$.

Case 1: $a = 0$ or $b = 0$.
If $a = 0$, then $a^m = 0 = b^m$. Since $D$ is an integral domain, $b^m = 0$
implies $b = 0$, so $a = b$. The case $b = 0$ is symmetric.

Case 2: $a \neq 0$ and $b \neq 0$.
Let $F$ be the field of fractions of $D$. Since $a, b$ are nonzero elements of
$D$, they are units in $F$. Set $u = a/b \in F^\times$. From $a^m = b^m$ and
$a^n = b^n$ we obtain $$u^m = \left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^m = \frac{a^m}{b^m} = 1,
\qquad u^n = \left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^n = \frac{a^n}{b^n} = 1.$$

Because $\gcd(m, n) = 1$, by Bézout's identity there exist integers $r, s$ with
$rm + sn = 1$. Since $u$ is a unit in $F$, the power $u^{rm+sn}$ is well
defined, and $$u = u^{rm+sn} = (u^m)^r \cdot (u^n)^s = 1^r \cdot 1^s = 1.$$

Hence $a/b = 1$, i.e. $a = b$.

In both cases $a = b$, completing the proof. $\blacksquare$
