## Solution

**Definition.** For $x > 0$, the exponential integral is
$$\mathrm{E}_1(x) = \int_x^{\infty} \frac{e^{-u}}{u}\,du.$$

We seek the asymptotic expansion of $e^x \mathrm{E}_1(x)$ as $x \to \infty$.

**Derivation by repeated integration by parts.**

Write the integral as
$$\mathrm{E}_1(x) = \int_x^{\infty} \frac{1}{u}\,e^{-u}\,du.$$

Set $f(u) = 1/u$ and $g'(u) = e^{-u}$, so $g(u) = -e^{-u}$. Integration by parts
 gives
$$\mathrm{E}_1(x) = \left[-\frac{e^{-u}}{u}\right]_{x}^{\infty} - \int_x^{\infty
}\frac{e^{-u}}{u^2}\,du = \frac{e^{-x}}{x} - \int_x^{\infty}\frac{e^{-u}}{u^2}\,
du,$$
since the boundary term at $\infty$ vanishes (exponential decay beats polynomial
 growth).

Apply integration by parts again to the remainder, with $f(u)=1/u^2$, $g(u)=-e^{
-u}$:
$$\int_x^{\infty}\frac{e^{-u}}{u^2}\,du = \frac{e^{-x}}{x^2} - 2\int_x^{\infty}\
frac{e^{-u}}{u^3}\,du.$$

Substituting back:
$$\mathrm{E}_1(x) = \frac{e^{-x}}{x} - \frac{e^{-x}}{x^2} + 2\int_x^{\infty}\fra
c{e^{-u}}{u^3}\,du.$$

Continuing inductively, after $N$ steps:
$$\mathrm{E}_1(x) = e^{-x}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\frac{(-1)^n\,n!}{x^{n+1}} + (-1)^N N!
\int_x^{\infty}\frac{e^{-u}}{u^{N+1}}\,du.$$

**Estimating the remainder.** The remainder satisfies
$$R_N(x) = (-1)^N N!\int_x^{\infty}\frac{e^{-u}}{u^{N+1}}\,du,$$
so
$$|R_N(x)| \le N!\int_x^{\infty}\frac{e^{-u}}{x^{N+1}}\,du = \frac{N!\,e^{-x}}{x
^{N+1}}.$$

Thus $R_N(x) = O\!\left(\frac{e^{-x}}{x^{N+1}}\right)$, which is exactly the ord
er of the first omitted term. Moreover the remainder is bounded in absolute valu
e by the magnitude of the first omitted term and shares its sign (since the inte
grand is positive), confirming the expansion is a proper asymptotic (in fact, al
ternating with decreasing terms for fixed $N$ and large $x$).

**Multiplying by $e^x$.** Dividing through by $e^{-x}$ (i.e. multiplying by $e^x
$):
$$e^x\mathrm{E}_1(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\frac{(-1)^n\,n!}{x^{n+1}} + O\!\left(\fr
ac{1}{x^{N+1}}\right), \qquad x\to\infty.$$

Since this holds for every fixed $N$, we obtain the full asymptotic series:

$$\boxed{\,e^x\mathrm{E}_1(x) \sim \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n\,n!}{x^{n+1}}
 = \frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{x^2} + \frac{2!}{x^3} - \frac{3!}{x^4} + \frac{4!}{x^5
} - \cdots\,} \qquad (x\to\infty).$$

**Note on divergence.** The series diverges for every fixed $x$ (since $n!/x^{n+
1}\to\infty$), so it is an asymptotic, not convergent, expansion: truncating aft
er the $N$-th term gives an error of the order of the next term, optimal truncat
ion occurring near $N\approx x$.
