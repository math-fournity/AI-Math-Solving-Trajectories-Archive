## Determination of the Null Cone for $SL_2(\mathbb{C})$ acting on $S^2(\mathbb{
C}^2)$

### Setup

Let $U = \mathbb{C}^2$ with standard basis $e_1, e_2$. The symmetric square $W =
 S^2 U$ is 3-dimensional with basis $\{e_1^2,\; e_1 e_2,\; e_2^2\}$. An element
$w \in W$ can be identified with a binary quadratic form
$$w = a\, x^2 + b\, xy + c\, y^2,$$
where we write $x = e_1, y = e_2$. The group $G = SL_2(\mathbb{C})$ acts on $U$
by the standard representation, hence on $W = S^2 U$ by substitution: for $g = \
begin{pmatrix} p & q \\ r & s \end{pmatrix} \in SL_2(\mathbb{C})$,
$$(g \cdot w)(x,y) = w(px + ry,\; qx + sy).$$

### The Invariant Ring

**Claim:** $\mathbb{C}[W]^G = \mathbb{C}[\Delta]$, where $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$ is
 the discriminant.

*Proof of claim.* The representation $S^2 U$ is the 3-dimensional irreducible re
presentation $V_2$ of $SL_2(\mathbb{C})$ (the adjoint representation). We verify
 $\Delta$ is invariant: under the substitution $x \mapsto px+ry,\; y \mapsto qx+
sy$ with $ps-qr=1$, the discriminant of the transformed form equals $(ps-qr)^2 \
cdot \Delta = \Delta$.

To see the invariant ring is generated by $\Delta$ alone: by the Hilbert–Molien
approach (or classical invariant theory of binary forms), the invariants of $SL_
2$ acting on binary quadratic forms form a polynomial ring in one generator of d
egree 2. Concretely, one checks that there are no invariants of odd degree (the
representation $S^k(S^2 U)$ contains a trivial summand only when $k$ is even, by
 weight considerations), and the degree-2 invariant space is 1-dimensional, span
ned by $\Delta$. By the structure theorem for this coregular representation, $\m
athbb{C}[W]^G = \mathbb{C}[\Delta]$. $\square$

### The Null Cone

The **null cone** $\mathcal{N}$ is defined as the common zero locus of all homog
eneous $G$-invariant polynomials of positive degree:
$$\mathcal{N} = \{w \in W : f(w) = 0 \text{ for all } f \in \mathbb{C}[W]^G_+ \}
.$$

Since $\mathbb{C}[W]^G_+ = (\Delta)$, the null cone is:
$$\boxed{\mathcal{N} = \{a\, x^2 + b\, xy + c\, y^2 \in S^2(\mathbb{C}^2) : b^2
- 4ac = 0\}.}$$

This is the **quadric cone** in $\mathbb{C}^3$ — the cone of degenerate (singula
r) binary quadratic forms.

### Direct Verification via Orbit Analysis

We confirm this by the Hilbert–Mumford criterion, showing $\mathcal{N} = \{w : 0
 \in \overline{G \cdot w}\}$.

**Case 1: $\Delta(w) = 0$ and $w \neq 0$.** Then $b^2 = 4ac$, so over $\mathbb{C
}$ the form factors as a perfect square: $w = (\alpha x + \beta y)^2$ with $(\al
pha, \beta) \neq (0,0)$. Since $SL_2(\mathbb{C})$ acts transitively on $\mathbb{
C}^2 \setminus \{0\}$, there exists $g \in G$ with $g \cdot (\alpha x + \beta y)
 = x$, so $g \cdot w = x^2$. Now apply the one-parameter subgroup $\lambda(t) =
\mathrm{diag}(t, t^{-1}) \in SL_2(\mathbb{C})$:
$$\lambda(t) \cdot x^2 = t^2 x^2 \xrightarrow{t \to 0} 0.$$
Hence $0 \in \overline{G \cdot w}$, so $w \in \mathcal{N}$.

**Case 2: $\Delta(w) \neq 0$.** The form is nondegenerate. Over $\mathbb{C}$, ev
ery nondegenerate binary quadratic form is $SL_2$-equivalent to $xy$ (e.g., $x^2
 + y^2 = (x+iy)(x-iy)$, and a further change brings it to $xy$). So $g \cdot w =
 xy$ for some $g$. Since $\Delta$ is $G$-invariant and $\Delta(xy) = -4 \neq 0$,
 every point in the orbit $G \cdot w$ has $\Delta = -4 \neq 0$. As $\Delta$ is c
ontinuous and $\Delta(0) = 0$, the orbit closure cannot contain $0$. Thus $0 \no
tin \overline{G \cdot w}$, and $w \notin \mathcal{N}$.

**Case 3: $w = 0$.** Trivially $0 \in \mathcal{N}$, and indeed $\Delta(0) = 0$.

### Conclusion

The null cone for the action of $SL_2(\mathbb{C})$ on $S^2(\mathbb{C}^2)$ is the
 quadric cone
$$\mathcal{N} = \{(a, b, c) \in \mathbb{C}^3 : b^2 - 4ac = 0\},$$
i.e., the set of degenerate binary quadratic forms — equivalently, the squares o
f linear forms (together with the origin). It is the zero set of the unique (up
to scaling) generator $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$ of the invariant ring $\mathbb{C}[S^2
(\mathbb{C}^2)]^{SL_2(\mathbb{C})} = \mathbb{C}[\Delta]$.
