## Solution

The answer is $\dim X - \dim Y$.

### Proof

Let $f: X \to Y$ be a morphism of varieties over an algebraically closed field $
k$. We prove that the minimum dimension of an irreducible component of $f^{-1}(y
)$, as $y$ ranges over closed points of $Y$, equals $\dim X - \dim Y$.

**Step 1: Reduction to the irreducible, dominant case.**

Write $X = \bigcup_{i=1}^{r} X_i$ as a union of irreducible components, with $\d
im X = \max_i \dim X_i$. Pick a component $X_{i_0}$ of maximal dimension, so $\d
im X_{i_0} = \dim X$. Let $Z_i = \overline{f(X_i)} \subseteq Y$; this is an irre
ducible subvariety. Replacing $Y$ by $Z_{i_0}$ and $f$ by $f|_{X_{i_0}}: X_{i_0}
 \to Z_{i_0}$, we reduce to a dominant morphism of irreducible varieties. The fi
ber $f^{-1}(y) = \bigcup_i (f|_{X_i})^{-1}(y)$, so the irreducible components of
 $f^{-1}(y)$ are the irreducible components of the various $(f|_{X_i})^{-1}(y)$.
 It suffices to prove the theorem for a dominant morphism $f: X \to Y$ of irredu
cible varieties with $\dim X = n$, $\dim Y = m$.

**Step 2: Lower bound — every irreducible component of $f^{-1}(y)$ has dimension
 $\geq n - m$.**

This is the **Fiber Dimension Theorem**. We prove it via Krull's Principal Ideal
 Theorem (or more precisely, its generalization).

Let $y \in f(X)$ be a closed point, and let $W$ be an irreducible component of $
f^{-1}(y)$. Since $y$ is a closed point, $\{y\}$ is defined by $m = \dim Y$ equa
tions locally (in an affine chart of $Y$, the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}_y$ is
generated by $m$ elements, since $Y$ is a variety of dimension $m$ over an algeb
raically closed field, so the local ring $\mathcal{O}_{Y,y}$ has embedding dimen
sion $m$).

The fiber $f^{-1}(y)$ is cut out in $X$ by pulling back these $m$ equations. By
Krull's height theorem (generalized principal ideal theorem), every irreducible
component of the subvariety cut out by $m$ equations in an $n$-dimensional varie
ty has dimension $\geq n - m$. Therefore:
$$\dim W \geq n - m = \dim X - \dim Y.$$

**Step 3: Upper bound — the bound $n - m$ is achieved.**

We show there exists a closed point $y \in Y$ such that $f^{-1}(y)$ is nonempty
and every irreducible component has dimension exactly $n - m$.

By generic flatness (Grothendieck), there exists a nonempty open subset $U \subs
eteq Y$ such that $f^{-1}(U) \to U$ is flat. For a flat morphism of relative dim
ension $d = n - m$ between irreducible varieties, every fiber over a point of $U
$ has pure dimension $d$ (all irreducible components have dimension exactly $d$)
.

More concretely: Since $f$ is dominant, $f$ induces an inclusion $k(Y) \hookrigh
tarrow k(X)$, making $k(X)$ a finitely generated field extension of $k(Y)$ of tr
anscendence degree $n - m$. The generic fiber $X_{k(Y)}$ is a variety over $k(Y)
$ of dimension $n - m$. Spreading out, there is a nonempty open $U \subseteq Y$
such that for all $y \in U$, the fiber $f^{-1}(y)$ has dimension $n - m$ (all co
mponents have dimension $n - m$).

Since $k$ is algebraically closed, $U$ contains closed points (by the Nullstelle
nsatz, closed points are dense). Pick any closed point $y \in U \cap f(X)$. Then
 every irreducible component of $f^{-1}(y)$ has dimension exactly $n - m$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

From Step 2, every irreducible component of every fiber has dimension $\geq \dim
 X - \dim Y$. From Step 3, there exists a closed point $y$ where the fiber has a
n irreducible component of dimension exactly $\dim X - \dim Y$. Therefore:

$$\min_{\substack{y \in Y \text{ closed} \\ W \subseteq f^{-1}(y) \text{ irr. co
mp.}}} \dim W = \dim X - \dim Y.$$

The minimum dimension of an irreducible component of $f^{-1}(y)$ for a closed po
int $y \in Y$ is:

$$\boxed{\dim X - \dim Y}$$
