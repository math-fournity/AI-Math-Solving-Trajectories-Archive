## Proof

Let $A$ be a $\mathbb{C}$-algebra and $V$ an irreducible (i.e. simple) $A$-modul
e with $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} V$ countable. We determine $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \operato
rname{End}_A(V)$.

**Step 1: Schur's lemma.** Since $V$ is irreducible, every nonzero $A$-module en
domorphism of $V$ is invertible. Hence
$$D := \operatorname{End}_A(V)$$
is a division ring. The action of $\mathbb{C}\subseteq Z(A)$ (the structural map
 $\mathbb{C}\to A$ lands in the center of $A$) commutes with every $A$-endomorph
ism, so $\mathbb{C}$ embeds into the center $Z(D)$. Thus $D$ is a division algeb
ra over $\mathbb{C}$, and $V$ is naturally a left vector space over $D$ (via eva
luation of endomorphisms), with $\dim_D V \geq 1$ since $V\neq 0$.

**Step 2: $D$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{C}$.** Suppose for contradiction that s
ome $d\in D$ is transcendental over $\mathbb{C}$. Then the subfield $\mathbb{C}(
d)\subseteq D$ is isomorphic to the rational function field $\mathbb{C}(t)$, who
se dimension as a $\mathbb{C}$-vector space is uncountable (the monomials $\{1,t
,t^2,\dots\}$ already give a countably infinite linearly independent set, and in
 fact $\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}(t)=\aleph_0$... wait — actually $\{t^{-n}:n\g
eq 0\}\cup\{t^n:n\geq 0\}$ and more; the set $\{(t-\alpha)^{-1}:\alpha\in\mathbb
{C}\}$ is linearly independent over $\mathbb{C}$, giving uncountable dimension).

Concretely: the family $\bigl\{\tfrac{1}{t-\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\bigr\}\su
bset \mathbb{C}(t)$ is linearly independent over $\mathbb{C}$, since a nontrivia
l relation $\sum_{i=1}^n \tfrac{c_i}{t-\alpha_i}=0$ would, after clearing denomi
nators, give a nonzero polynomial vanishing identically — contradiction. As $\ma
thbb{C}$ is uncountable,
$$\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \mathbb{C}(d)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}(t)\ \text{is uncou
ntable}.$$

Since $D\supseteq \mathbb{C}(d)$ and $V$ is a $D$-vector space of positive dimen
sion,
$$\dim_{\mathbb{C}} V \;\geq\; \dim_{\mathbb{C}} D \;\geq\; \dim_{\mathbb{C}} \m
athbb{C}(d)\ >\ \aleph_0,$$
contradicting the hypothesis that $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} V$ is countable. Hence **ev
ery** $d\in D$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{C}$.

**Step 3: $D=\mathbb{C}$.** Let $d\in D$. Since $d$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{C
}$ and $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraically closed, the minimal polynomial of $d$ over
$\mathbb{C}$ is linear, so $d\in\mathbb{C}\cdot 1_D$. Therefore $D=\mathbb{C}\cd
ot\mathrm{id}_V\cong\mathbb{C}$.

**Conclusion.** $\operatorname{End}_A(V)=\mathbb{C}\cdot\mathrm{id}_V$, so

$$\boxed{\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \operatorname{End}_A(V)=1.}$$
