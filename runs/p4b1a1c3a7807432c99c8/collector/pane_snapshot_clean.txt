**Answer: Yes, such a compact connected set $K'$ exists.**

**Proof.**

Since $U \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is open and connected, $U$ is polygonally connect
ed. (Indeed, fix $x_0 \in U$ and let $A$ be the set of points in $U$ that can be
 joined to $x_0$ by a polygonal path in $U$. Both $A$ and $U \setminus A$ are op
en in $U$: openness of $U$ lets us extend any path by a short segment inside a b
all contained in $U$. Since $A \neq \emptyset$ and $U$ is connected, $A = U$.)

**Step 1: Enclose $K$ in a compact set inside $U$.** For each $x \in K$, since $
U$ is open there is $r_x > 0$ with $\overline{B(x, r_x)} \subset U$. The family
$\{B(x, r_x) : x \in K\}$ is an open cover of the compact set $K$, so there is a
 finite subcover $B_1, \dots, B_m$ (with closures $\overline{B_1}, \dots, \overl
ine{B_m} \subset U$). Set
$$C := \bigcup_{i=1}^{m} \overline{B_i}.$$
Then $C$ is a finite union of compact sets, hence compact; $K \subset C$; and $C
 \subset U$.

**Step 2: $C$ has finitely many connected components.** Each $\overline{B_i}$ is
 connected. Two of them lie in the same component of $C$ exactly when they are l
inked by a chain of overlapping closed balls. This partitions the finite index s
et $\{1,\dots,m\}$ into finitely many classes, giving finitely many components $
C_1, \dots, C_p$ of $C$ (with $p \le m$).

**Step 3: Connect the components by polygonal paths in $U$.** Pick a point $x_j
\in C_j$ for each $j = 1, \dots, p$. Since $U$ is polygonally connected, for eac
h $j = 2, \dots, p$ there is a polygonal path $\gamma_j : [0,1] \to U$ with $\ga
mma_j(0) = x_1$ and $\gamma_j(1) = x_j$. Each image $\gamma_j([0,1])$ is a finit
e union of line segments, hence compact, and is contained in $U$.

**Step 4: Define $K'$ and verify the required properties.** Let
$$K' := C \cup \gamma_2([0,1]) \cup \cdots \cup \gamma_p([0,1]).$$

- *Compact:* $K'$ is a finite union of compact subsets of $\mathbb{R}^n$, hence
compact.
- *Contained in $U$:* $C \subset U$ and each $\gamma_j([0,1]) \subset U$, so $K'
 \subset U$.
- *Contains $K$:* $K \subset C \subset K'$.
- *Connected:* Each $C_j$ is connected and contains $x_j$, which lies on $\gamma
_j$. The path $\gamma_j$ also contains $x_1 \in C_1$. Hence $C_1 \cup \gamma_j([
0,1]) \cup C_j$ is connected for every $j \ge 2$, and all of these share the com
mon connected piece $C_1$. Therefore their union $K'$ is connected.

Thus $K'$ is a compact, connected subset of $U$ with $K \subset K'$. $\blacksqua
