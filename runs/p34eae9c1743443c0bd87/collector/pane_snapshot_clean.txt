## Setup

Let $\gamma(s)$ be the boundary curve parametrized by arclength, $s \in [0, L]$,
 oriented counterclockwise so that $D$ lies to the left. Let $\mathbf{T}(s) = \g
amma'(s)$ be the unit tangent and $\mathbf{n}(s)$ the **outward** unit normal. F
or a convex curve, the curvature $\kappa(s) \geq 0$ satisfies the Frenet equatio
ns:
$$\mathbf{T}'(s) = \kappa(s)\,\mathbf{N}(s), \qquad \mathbf{N}(s) = -\mathbf{n}(
s) \text{ (inward normal)},$$
so $\mathbf{n}'(s) = -\mathbf{N}'(s) = \kappa(s)\,\mathbf{T}(s)$.

**Tubular coordinates.** Near the curve, every point is uniquely $\gamma(s) + t\
,\mathbf{n}(s)$ where $t$ is the signed distance (positive outside $D$, negative
 inside). By definition $p(\gamma(s)+t\mathbf{n}(s)) = t$.

**Jacobian.**
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial s}(\gamma + t\mathbf{n}) = \mathbf{T} + t\,\mathbf{n}
' = (1 + \kappa\, t)\,\mathbf{T}, \qquad \frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\gamma + t\
mathbf{n}) = \mathbf{n}.$$
The Jacobian determinant is $1 + \kappa(s)\,t$.

*Sanity check (circle of radius $R$):* $\gamma + t\mathbf{n}$ is a circle of rad
ius $R+t$, and the Jacobian is $\frac{R+t}{R} = 1 + \frac{t}{R} = 1 + \kappa t$.
 ✓

For the region $|p|<1$ we need $1 + \kappa\, t > 0$ on $t \in [-1,1]$, i.e. the
minimum radius of curvature exceeds $1$; we assume the curve satisfies this (the
 final answer is universal).

## Computation

$$I = \int_{|p|<1} p\,dx\,dy = \int_0^L \int_{-1}^{1} t\,\bigl(1 + \kappa(s)\,t\
bigr)\,dt\,ds.$$

**Inner integral:**
$$\int_{-1}^{1} \bigl(t + \kappa\, t^2\bigr)\,dt = \underbrace{\left[\frac{t^2}{
2}\right]_{-1}^{1}}_{=\,0} + \kappa\left[\frac{t^3}{3}\right]_{-1}^{1} = \frac{2
\kappa}{3}.$$

**Outer integral:**
$$I = \int_0^L \frac{2\,\kappa(s)}{3}\,ds = \frac{2}{3}\int_0^L \kappa(s)\,ds.$$

By the **turning number theorem** (or Gauss–Bonnet) for a simple closed convex c
urve traversed once counterclockwise, the total curvature is
$$\int_0^L \kappa(s)\,ds = 2\pi.$$

Therefore
$$I = \frac{2}{3}\cdot 2\pi = \frac{4\pi}{3}.$$

## Verification (circle of radius $R$)

For the circle, $p = r - R$ and $|p|<1$ is the annulus $R-1 < r < R+1$:
$$I = \int_0^{2\pi}\int_{R-1}^{R+1} (r-R)\,r\,dr\,d\theta = 2\pi\int_{R-1}^{R+1}
(r^2 - Rr)\,dr.$$
$$= 2\pi\left[\frac{r^3}{3} - \frac{R\,r^2}{2}\right]_{R-1}^{R+1}.$$
Expanding: at $r = R+1$: $\frac{(R+1)^3}{3} - \frac{R(R+1)^2}{2}$; at $r = R-1$:
 $\frac{(R-1)^3}{3} - \frac{R(R-1)^2}{2}$. Their difference is $\frac{2}{3}$ (th
e odd-in-$(r-R)$ terms cancel, leaving $\frac{2}{3}$). So $I = 2\pi \cdot \frac{
2}{3} = \frac{4\pi}{3}$, **independent of $R$**. ✓

## Conclusion

The integral is independent of the particular convex curve and equals

$$\boxed{\dfrac{4\pi}{3}}.$$
