Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $[F:Z]$ is the same for eve
ry maximal subfield $F$ of a finite-dimensional central division algebra $D$ ove
r its center $Z$.

**Answer: Yes, $[F:Z]$ is an invariant.**

## Proof

Let $D$ be a finite-dimensional central division algebra over its center $Z$. By
 the standard structure theory of central simple algebras, $[D:Z] = n^2$ for som
e positive integer $n$ (the *degree* of $D$).

Let $F$ be any maximal subfield of $D$ (a maximal commutative subfield containin
g $Z$).

**Step 1: Apply the Double Centralizer Theorem.**

Since $F$ is a field containing $Z$, it is a simple $Z$-subalgebra of the centra
l simple algebra $D$. The Double Centralizer Theorem gives:
$$[F:Z]\cdot[C_D(F):Z] = [D:Z] = n^2,$$
where $C_D(F) = \{x \in D : xf = fx \text{ for all } f \in F\}$ is the centraliz
er of $F$ in $D$.

**Step 2: Identify the center of $C_D(F)$.**

Since $F$ is commutative, $F \subseteq C_D(F)$. The center of $C_D(F)$ is:
$$Z(C_D(F)) = C_D(F) \cap C_D(C_D(F)).$$
By the Double Centralizer Theorem, $C_D(C_D(F)) = F$, so:
$$Z(C_D(F)) = C_D(F) \cap F = F.$$
Thus $C_D(F)$ is a central division algebra over $F$, and $[C_D(F):F]$ is a perf
ect square, say $m^2$.

**Step 3: Derive the dimension relation.**

We have $[C_D(F):Z] = [C_D(F):F]\cdot[F:Z] = m^2\cdot[F:Z]$. Substituting into t
he formula from Step 1:
$$[F:Z]\cdot m^2\cdot[F:Z] = n^2 \implies [F:Z]\cdot m = n.$$

**Step 4: Use maximality of $F$ to force $m=1$.**

Suppose for contradiction that $m > 1$. Then $[C_D(F):F] = m^2 > 1$, so $C_D(F)
\supsetneq F$. Pick any $x \in C_D(F)\setminus F$. Since $x$ commutes with every
 element of $F$ (by definition of centralizer), the ring $F(x) \subseteq D$ is a
 commutative field strictly containing $F$—a contradiction to the maximality of
$F$.

Therefore $m = 1$, which gives $C_D(F) = F$ and:
$$[F:Z] = n.$$

**Conclusion.**

The value $[F:Z] = n = \sqrt{[D:Z]}$ depends only on $D$ (through its degree $n$
), not on the particular choice of maximal subfield $F$. Hence $[F:Z]$ is an inv
ariant of $D$.
