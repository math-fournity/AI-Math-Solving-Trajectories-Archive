## Proof

**Claim.** The almost-everywhere equivalence relation under which the conditiona
l expectation $\mathbb{E}(X\mid N)$ is defined (i.e., the uniqueness of its vers
ions) is taken **with respect to $N$**, not $F$. Concretely: two versions of $\m
athbb{E}(X\mid N)$ agree outside a set that belongs to $N$ and has $P$-measure z
ero.

---

**Recall the definition.** Given an integrable random variable $X$ on $(\Omega,F
,P)$ and a sub-$\sigma$-algebra $N\subseteq F$, the conditional expectation $\ma
thbb{E}(X\mid N)$ is *any* random variable $Y$ satisfying

1. $Y$ is $N$-measurable, and
2. $\displaystyle\int_A Y\,dP=\int_A X\,dP$ for every $A\in N$.

The content of the definition is that such a $Y$ exists and is **unique up to $P
$-null sets in $N$**. We prove the uniqueness and identify exactly which null se
ts appear.

---

**Uniqueness proof.** Suppose $Y$ and $Y'$ both satisfy (1)–(2). Since both are
$N$-measurable, the sets

$$B_+:=\{\omega:Y(\omega)>Y'(\omega)\},\qquad B_-:=\{\omega:Y(\omega)<Y'(\omega)
\}$$

belong to $N$ (they are preimages of Borel sets under the $N$-measurable functio
n $Y-Y'$). Applying property (2) with $A=B_+$:

$$\int_{B_+}(Y-Y')\,dP=\int_{B_+}Y\,dP-\int_{B_+}Y'\,dP=\int_{B_+}X\,dP-\int_{B_
+}X\,dP=0.$$

On $B_+$ the integrand $Y-Y'$ is strictly positive, so the only way the integral
 vanishes is $P(B_+)=0$. By the identical argument with $A=B_-$, $P(B_-)=0$. Hen
ce

$$\{Y\neq Y'\}=B_+\cup B_-\in N,\qquad P(\{Y\neq Y'\})=0.$$

So $Y=Y'$ outside a $P$-null set **that lies in $N$**. ∎

---

**Why the distinction matters.** The null set witnessing the agreement of two ve
rsions is forced to lie in $N$ because it is built from $N$-measurable functions
. In general $N\subsetneq F$, and there may exist $P$-null sets in $F$ that do *
*not** belong to $N$ (whenever $N$ is a proper sub-$\sigma$-algebra and is not $
P$-complete). Therefore:

- Equivalence "mod $F$-null sets" identifies *more* pairs of functions than equi
valence "mod $N$-null sets": two $N$-measurable functions could differ on an $F$
-null set that is not in $N$, making them equal $F$-a.e. but not $N$-a.e.
- The conditional expectation is an element of $L^0(\Omega,N,P|_N)$, the space o
f $N$-measurable functions modulo $P|_N$-null sets. Its equivalence class is tak
en in this space, i.e., **with respect to $N$**.

If one replaces $N$ by its $P$-completion $\overline{N}$, the class of $N$-measu
rable functions modulo null sets does not change (every $\overline{N}$-measurabl
e function agrees $P$-a.e. with an $N$-measurable one), so the equivalence relat
ion is unchanged in substance; it is still governed by $N$, not by the larger $\
sigma$-algebra $F$.

---

**Conclusion.** The almost-everywhere equivalence relation of $\mathbb{E}(X\mid
N)$ is with respect to **$N$**: two versions agree outside a set in $N$ of $P$-m
easure zero. It is *not* with respect to $F$.
