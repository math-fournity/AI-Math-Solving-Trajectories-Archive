**Proof.**

We use the substitution $t = x^n$. For $n \geq 1$ and any $b > 1$, the map $\phi
(x) = x^n$ is $C^1$ and strictly increasing on $[1, b]$, with $\phi(1) = 1$, $\p
hi(b) = b^n$, and $\phi'(x) = nx^{n-1}$. Since $f$ is Riemann integrable on $[1,
 b^n]$ and $\frac{1}{n}t^{1/n - 1}$ is continuous on $[1, b^n]$, the product $f(
t)\cdot\frac{1}{n}t^{1/n-1}$ is Riemann integrable on $[1, b^n]$. By the change-
of-variables theorem for Riemann integrals,

$$\int_1^b f(x^n)\,dx = \int_1^{b^n} f(t)\,\frac{1}{n}\,t^{1/n-1}\,dt.$$

**Convergence of the improper integrals.** For $t \geq 1$ and $n \geq 1$, we hav
e $1/n - 1 \leq 0$, so $t^{1/n - 1} \leq 1$. Hence

$$\left|\int_1^B f(t)\,\frac{1}{n}\,t^{1/n-1}\,dt\right| \leq \frac{1}{n}\int_1^
B |f(t)|\,dt \leq \frac{1}{n}\int_1^\infty |f(t)|\,dt < \infty,$$

so $\int_1^\infty f(t)\,\frac{1}{n}\,t^{1/n-1}\,dt$ converges absolutely. Likewi
se,

$$\left|\int_1^b f(x^n)\,dx\right| \leq \frac{1}{n}\int_1^\infty |f(t)|\,dt < \i
nfty,$$

so $\int_1^\infty f(x^n)\,dx$ converges. Passing $b \to \infty$ in the substitut
ion identity gives

$$\int_1^\infty f(x^n)\,dx = \int_1^\infty f(t)\,\frac{1}{n}\,t^{1/n-1}\,dt.$$

**The limit.** Using $t^{1/n-1} \leq 1$ for $t \geq 1$, $n \geq 1$:

$$\left|\int_1^\infty f(x^n)\,dx\right| = \left|\int_1^\infty f(t)\,\frac{1}{n}\
,t^{1/n-1}\,dt\right| \leq \frac{1}{n}\int_1^\infty |f(t)|\,dt.$$

Since $\int_1^\infty |f| < \infty$, the right-hand side is a fixed constant time

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_1^\infty f(x^n)\,dx = 0. \qquad\blacksquare$$
