The differential equation $(1-x^2)y'' - 2xy' + 6y = 0$ is the **Legendre equatio
n** of order $\ell = 2$:

$$\frac{d}{dx}\!\left[(1-x^2)y'\right] + \ell(\ell+1)\,y = 0, \qquad \ell(\ell+1
)=6.$$

## Step 1. Identify the solution space

The two linearly independent solutions on $(-1,1)$ are

- the Legendre polynomial $P_2(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}(3x^2-1)$, regular at both endpo
ints, with $P_2(1)=1$;
- the Legendre function of the second kind $Q_2(x)$, which has a logarithmic sin
gularity at $x=\pm 1$ (in fact $Q_2(x)\to -\infty$ as $x\to 1^{-}$).

Hence every solution on $(-1,1)$ is

$$y(x) = A\,P_2(x) + B\,Q_2(x).$$

## Step 2. Use the boundary condition $y(1)=2$

The condition "$y(1)=2$" requires $y$ to be **finite and well-defined** at $x=1$
. Since $Q_2$ diverges logarithmically at $x=1$, the coefficient $B$ must vanish
:

$$B = 0.$$

Then $y(1) = A\,P_2(1) = A\cdot 1 = A = 2$, so

$$y(x) = 2\,P_2(x) = 3x^2 - 1.$$

## Step 3. Compute the integral

$$\int_{-1}^{1} y^2\,dx = \int_{-1}^{1}(3x^2-1)^2\,dx = \int_{-1}^{1}\!\bigl(9x^
4 - 6x^2 + 1\bigr)\,dx.$$

By symmetry about $0$:

$$= 2\int_{0}^{1}\!\bigl(9x^4 - 6x^2 + 1\bigr)\,dx = 2\!\left[\frac{9}{5}x^5 - 2
x^3 + x\right]_{0}^{1} = 2\!\left(\frac{9}{5} - 2 + 1\right) = 2\!\left(\frac{4}
{5}\right) = \frac{8}{5}.$$

## Step 4. Verification via orthogonality

The standard orthogonality relation for Legendre polynomials gives

$$\int_{-1}^{1} P_2(x)^2\,dx = \frac{2}{2\ell+1}\Big|_{\ell=2} = \frac{2}{5}.$$

Since $y = 2P_2$,

$$\int_{-1}^{1} y^2\,dx = 4\int_{-1}^{1}P_2^2\,dx = 4\cdot\frac{2}{5} = \frac{8}
{5},$$

confirming the direct computation.

## Answer

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1} y^2\,dx = \frac{8}{5}}.$$
