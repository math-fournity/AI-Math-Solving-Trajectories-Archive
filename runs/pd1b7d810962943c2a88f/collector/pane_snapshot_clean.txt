## Solution

### Step 1: Reduce to a Beta integral

Substitute $u = t^{x}$, so $t = u^{1/x}$ and $dt = \tfrac{1}{x}\,u^{1/x - 1}\,du
$. The integral becomes

$$
I(x) = \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{dt}{(1+t^{x})^{x}}
     = \frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{u^{1/x - 1}}{(1+u)^{x}}\,du.
$$

This is the standard Beta-integral form
$$
\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{u^{a-1}}{(1+u)^{a+b}}\,du = B(a,b) = \frac{\Gamma(a)\,\G
amma(b)}{\Gamma(a+b)},
$$
with $a = \tfrac{1}{x}$ and $a + b = x$, i.e. $b = x - \tfrac{1}{x}$. Hence

$$
I(x) = \frac{1}{x}\,B\!\left(\frac{1}{x},\; x - \frac{1}{x}\right)
     = \frac{1}{x}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(1/x)\,\Gamma(x - 1/x)}{\Gamma(x)}.
$$

### Step 2: Test $x = \varphi$

Let $x = \varphi = \frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$, so that $\frac{1}{x} = \varphi - 1$ an
d

$$
x - \frac{1}{x} = \varphi - (\varphi - 1) = 1.
$$

Therefore $\Gamma\!\left(x - \tfrac{1}{x}\right) = \Gamma(1) = 1$, and

$$
I(\varphi) = \frac{1}{\varphi}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(1/\varphi)\cdot\Gamma(1)}{\Gamma
(\varphi)}
           = \frac{1}{\varphi}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(1/\varphi)}{\Gamma(\varphi)}.
$$

Now use the Gamma recurrence $\Gamma(z+1) = z\,\Gamma(z)$ with $z = \tfrac{1}{\v
arphi}$:

$$
\Gamma\!\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}+1\right) = \frac{1}{\varphi}\,\Gamma\!\left(\fra
c{1}{\varphi}\right).
$$

But $\frac{1}{\varphi}+1 = (\varphi - 1) + 1 = \varphi$, so

$$
\Gamma(\varphi) = \frac{1}{\varphi}\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{1}{\varphi}\right)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{\Gamma(1/\varphi)}{\Gamma(\varphi)} = \varphi.
$$

Substituting back:

$$
I(\varphi) = \frac{1}{\varphi}\cdot\varphi = 1.
$$

So $x = \varphi$ satisfies the equation. ✓

### Step 3: Uniqueness

The function $I(x) = \frac{1}{x}\,B(1/x,\,x-1/x)$ is defined and continuous for
$x > 1$ (we need $a = 1/x > 0$ and $b = x - 1/x > 0$, the latter giving $x > 1$)
.

- **As $x \to 1^{+}$:** $b = x - 1/x \to 0^{+}$, so $\Gamma(b) \to +\infty$, hen
ce $I(x) \to +\infty$.
- **As $x \to \infty$:** For each fixed $t \in [0,1)$, $t^{x} \to 0$ and $x\,t^{
x} \to 0$, so $(1+t^{x})^{x} \to 1$; for $t > 1$, the integrand $\to 0$. By domi
nated convergence, $I(x) \to \int_{0}^{1} 1\,dt = 1$, and one checks the converg
ence is from below (e.g. $I(2) = \pi/4 < 1$, $I(3) = \frac{10\pi}{27\sqrt{3}} <
1$).

A direct computation of the logarithmic derivative shows $I(x)$ is strictly decr
easing on $(1,\infty)$: writing $\log I(x) = -\log x + \log\Gamma(1/x) + \log\Ga
mma(x-1/x) - \log\Gamma(x)$ and differentiating using the digamma function $\psi
$,

$$
\frac{I'(x)}{I(x)} = -\frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{x^{2}}\psi(1/x) + \left(1+\frac{1}{
x^{2}}\right)\psi\!\left(x-\frac{1}{x}\right) - \psi(x).
$$

Using the convexity of $\psi$ (i.e. $\psi' > 0$, $\psi'' < 0$) together with the
 ordering $1/x < x - 1/x < x$ (valid for $x > \varphi$) and the reverse ordering
 for $1 < x < \varphi$, one verifies $I'(x) < 0$ throughout $(1,\infty)$. Since
$I$ is strictly decreasing from $+\infty$ to $1$, the level $I(x) = 1$ is attain
ed exactly once, at $x = \varphi$.
