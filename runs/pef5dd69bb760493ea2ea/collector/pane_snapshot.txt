### Step 1: Half-angle reduction

Using $1-\cos\theta = 2\sin^2\frac{\theta}{2}$,

$$1-\cos\frac{2n\pi}{101} = 2\sin^2\frac{n\pi}{101}.$$

### Step 2: The sine product identity

**Claim.** For every integer $m\ge 2$,
$$\prod_{n=1}^{m-1}\sin\frac{n\pi}{m} = \frac{m}{2^{m-1}}.$$

*Proof.* Factor $x^m - 1 = \prod_{k=0}^{m-1}\!\bigl(x - e^{2\pi i k/m}\bigr)$. E
valuating at $x=1$ and cancelling the $k=0$ factor gives
$$m = \prod_{k=1}^{m-1}\!\bigl(1 - e^{2\pi i k/m}\bigr).$$
Taking absolute values,
$$m = \prod_{k=1}^{m-1}\bigl|1 - e^{2\pi i k/m}\bigr| = \prod_{k=1}^{m-1}2\sin\f
rac{k\pi}{m} = 2^{m-1}\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}\sin\frac{k\pi}{m},$$
since $|1-e^{i\varphi}| = 2\sin(\varphi/2)$ for $0<\varphi<2\pi$. Solving yields
 the claim. $\square$

Sanity checks: $m=2$: $\sin\frac{\pi}{2}=1=\frac{2}{2}$ ✓. $m=3$: $\sin\frac{\pi
}{3}\sin\frac{2\pi}{3}=\frac{3}{4}=\frac{3}{4}$ ✓.
