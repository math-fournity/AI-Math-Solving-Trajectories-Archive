## Solution

We compute the derivative of $f(M) = \operatorname{Tr}(M^p X)$ with respect to a
 symmetric matrix $M \succeq 0$, where $X = X^T$ and $p \in \mathbb{R}\setminus\
{0\}$.

### Setup

Since $M$ is symmetric and positive semidefinite, it admits the eigendecompositi
on
$$M = U\Lambda U^T, \qquad \Lambda = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda
_n), \quad \lambda_i \ge 0,$$
with $U$ orthogonal. For $p>0$ this is well-defined for $M\succeq 0$; for $p<0$
we require $M\succ 0$ (all $\lambda_i>0$). The matrix power is
$$M^p = U\Lambda^p U^T, \qquad \Lambda^p = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1^p,\dots
,\lambda_n^p).$$

### Fréchet derivative of $M\mapsto M^p$

For a symmetric perturbation $H=H^T$, the Fréchet derivative of the spectral mat
rix function $g(t)=t^p$ is the well-known Daleckii–Krein formula:
$$d(M^p)[H] = U\bigl(L_p \circ (U^T H U)\bigr)U^T,$$
where $\circ$ is the Hadamard product and $L_p$ is the **Loewner matrix** of $g(
t)=t^p$:
$$(L_p)_{ij} = \begin{cases} \dfrac{\lambda_i^p - \lambda_j^p}{\lambda_i - \lamb
da_j}, & \lambda_i \ne \lambda_j,\\[6pt] p\,\lambda_i^{p-1}, & \lambda_i = \lamb
da_j. \end{cases}$$
Note $L_p$ is symmetric since $g$ is a real-valued function on $\mathbb{R}$.

### Computing the differential of $f$

$$df[H] = \operatorname{Tr}\bigl(d(M^p)[H]\cdot X\bigr) = \operatorname{Tr}\Bigl
(U\bigl(L_p\circ(U^T H U)\bigr)U^T X\Bigr).$$

Set $A = U^T H U$ (symmetric) and $B = U^T X U$ (symmetric). Using cyclicity of
trace:
$$df[H] = \operatorname{Tr}\bigl((L_p\circ A)\,B\bigr) = \sum_{i,j}(L_p)_{ij}\,A
_{ij}\,B_{ji}.$$

Since $B$ is symmetric, $B_{ji}=B_{ij}$, so
$$df[H] = \sum_{i,j}(L_p)_{ij}\,B_{ij}\,A_{ij} = \sum_{i,j}(L_p\circ B)_{ij}\,A_
{ij} = \operatorname{Tr}\bigl((L_p\circ B)^T A\bigr).$$

Since both $L_p$ and $B$ are symmetric, $L_p\circ B$ is symmetric, hence
$$df[H] = \operatorname{Tr}\bigl((L_p\circ B)\,A\bigr) = \operatorname{Tr}\Bigl(
U(L_p\circ B)U^T\,H\Bigr).$$

### Identifying the gradient

By definition of the gradient on the space of symmetric matrices, $\nabla_M f$ i
s the unique symmetric matrix satisfying $df[H]=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl((\nabla_M
 f)\,H\bigr)$ for all symmetric $H$. Therefore:

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \frac{\partial}{\partial M}\operatorname{Tr}(M^p X) \;=\;
 U\Bigl(L_p \circ \bigl(U^T X\, U\bigr)\Bigr)\,U^T,}$$

where $M=U\Lambda U^T$ is the eigendecomposition of $M$, $L_p$ is the Loewner ma
trix of $t\mapsto t^p$ evaluated at the eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n$,
 and $\circ$ denotes the Hadamard product.

### Verification (special cases)

- **$X=I$:** Then $U^T X U = I$, so $L_p\circ I = \operatorname{diag}(p\lambda_i
^{p-1})$, giving $\nabla_M f = U\operatorname{diag}(p\lambda_i^{p-1})U^T = p\,M^
{p-1}$. This matches the classical scalar-calculus result $\frac{d}{dM}\operator
name{Tr}(M^p)=p\,M^{p-1}$. ✓

- **Integer $p\ge 1$:** The Loewner matrix reduces to divided differences of $t^
p$, and the formula coincides with $\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}M^k X\,M^{p-1-k}$ (which is
symmetric when $M,X$ are symmetric), the standard result obtained from $d(M^p)=\
sum_{k=0}^{p-1}M^k\,dM\,M^{p-1-k}$. ✓

- **$p=1$:** $L_1$ has all entries equal to $1$, so $L_1\circ(U^TXU)=U^TXU$, giv
ing $\nabla_M f = UU^T X UU^T = X$. Indeed $\operatorname{Tr}(MX)$ has gradient
$X$. ✓

### PROOF COMPLETE
