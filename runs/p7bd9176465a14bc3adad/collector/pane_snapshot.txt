We maximize \(R(f)=\dfrac{\|f\|}{\|f'\|}\) over continuously differentiable \(f\
) on \([0,1]\) with \(f(0)=f(1)=0\), where \(\|g\|^2=\int_0^1 g(x)^2\,dx\).

**Claim.** The supremum is \(\dfrac{1}{\pi}\), attained (in the limit) by \(f(x)
=\sin(\pi x)\).

---

**Upper bound.** Extend \(f\) to \([0,2]\) by odd reflection about \(x=1\):
\[
\tilde f(x)=\begin{cases} f(x), & 0\le x\le 1,\\ -f(2-x),& 1\le x\le 2.\end{case
s}
\]
Then \(\tilde f(0)=\tilde f(2)=0\), and \(\tilde f\) is continuous and piecewise
 \(C^1\). We have
\[
\int_0^2 \tilde f^2 = 2\int_0^1 f^2,\qquad \int_0^2 (\tilde f')^2 = 2\int_0^1 (f
')^2.
\]
Expanding \(\tilde f\) in the sine basis \(\{\sin(n\pi x/2)\}_{n\ge 1}\) on \([0
,2]\) (eigenfunctions of \(-d^2/dx^2\) with Dirichlet boundary conditions, eigen
values \((n\pi/2)^2\)):
\[
\tilde f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n\sin\!\Big(\tfrac{n\pi x}{2}\Big),\qquad
\int_0^2 \tilde f^2 = \sum_n b_n^2,\quad \int_0^2 (\tilde f')^2 = \sum_n \Big(\t
frac{n\pi}{2}\Big)^2 b_n^2.
\]
Since \(\tilde f\) is odd about \(x=1\), only odd \(n\) appear, so the smallest
eigenvalue present is \((\pi/2)^2\). Hence
\[
\int_0^2 (\tilde f')^2 \ge \Big(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\Big)^2 \int_0^2 \tilde f^2.
\]
Dividing by 2:
\[
\int_0^1 (f')^2 \ge \frac{\pi^2}{4}\int_0^1 f^2,
\]
i.e. \(\|f'\|^2\ge \pi^2\|f\|^2/4\), giving \(\dfrac{\|f\|}{\|f'\|}\le \dfrac{2}
{\pi}\).

This is the standard Wirtinger bound, but it is not sharp for functions vanishin
g at **both** endpoints (the reflection argument is suboptimal here). We sharpen
 it.

**Sharp bound via direct sine expansion on \([0,1]\).** The eigenfunctions of \(
-d^2/dx^2\) on \([0,1]\) with Dirichlet conditions are \(\phi_n(x)=\sin(n\pi x)\
), eigenvalues \(\lambda_n=(n\pi)^2\). For \(f\in C^1\) with \(f(0)=f(1)=0\), ex
pand \(f=\sum a_n\phi_n\) (converging in \(L^2\)). Integration by parts (justifi
ed by density of smooth functions vanishing at endpoints) gives
\[
\int_0^1 (f')^2 = \sum_{n=1}^\infty (n\pi)^2 a_n^2 \ge \pi^2\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_
n^2 = \pi^2\int_0^1 f^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{\|f\|^2}{\|f'\|^2}\le \frac{1}{\pi^2},\qquad \frac{\|f\|}{\|f'\|}\le \frac
{1}{\pi}.
\]

**Attainment.** Equality holds iff \(a_n=0\) for all \(n\ge 2\), i.e. \(f(x)=a_1
\sin(\pi x)\). This function is \(C^1\), vanishes at both endpoints, and
\[
\|f\|^2=a_1^2\int_0^1\sin^2(\pi x)\,dx=\frac{a_1^2}{2},\quad
\|f'\|^2=a_1^2\pi^2\int_0^1\cos^2(\pi x)\,dx=\frac{a_1^2\pi^2}{2},
\]
so \(\|f\|/\|f'\|=1/\pi\).

---

\[
\boxed{\sup_{f}\frac{\|f\|}{\|f'\|}=\frac{1}{\pi}}
\]
