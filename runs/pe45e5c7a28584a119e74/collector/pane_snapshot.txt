# Proof: The Punctured Non-orientable Surface $N_g$ Deformation Retracts to a We
dge of $g$ Circles

## Setup and CW Structure

The non-orientable surface $N_g$ (the connected sum of $g$ copies of $\mathbb{RP
}^2$) is constructed from a $2g$-gon by identifying pairs of adjacent edges with
 the same orientation, giving the polygon the boundary word $a_1^2 a_2^2 \cdots
a_g^2$. This yields a CW decomposition of $N_g$:

- **One 0-cell**: All $2g$ vertices of the polygon are identified to a single po
int $v$ under the gluing (one can verify this by tracing the vertex identificati
ons induced by the word $a_1^2 a_2^2 \cdots a_g^2$).
- **$g$ 1-cells**: The $2g$ edges are paired into $g$ edges $a_1, \ldots, a_g$,
each becoming a loop based at $v$.
- **One 2-cell**: The interior of the polygon, attached via the map $S^1 \to \bi
gvee_{i=1}^g S^1$ corresponding to the word $a_1^2 a_2^2 \cdots a_g^2$.

The **1-skeleton** of this CW structure is therefore a wedge of $g$ circles, $\b
igvee_{i=1}^g S^1$, with a single 0-cell $v$ and $g$ loop edges.

## The Deformation Retraction

Let $p$ be a point in the interior of the 2-cell $e^2$ (i.e., in the open disk t
hat is the interior of the polygon, before identification). Set $X = N_g \setmin
us \{p\}$.

**Key observation**: The open 2-cell $e^2$ is an open disk $D^2$ (homeomorphic t
o $\mathbb{R}^2$). Removing the point $p$ from it gives $e^2 \setminus \{p\} \co
ng D^2 \setminus \{0\}$, which is homeomorphic to an open annulus $S^1 \times (0
,1)$.

**Standard retraction**: The punctured disk $D^2 \setminus \{0\}$ deformation re
tracts onto its boundary $S^1 = \partial D^2$ via the radial retraction
$$H(x, t) = (1-t)\,x + t\,\frac{x}{\|x\|}, \quad x \in D^2 \setminus \{0\},\ t \
in [0,1],$$
which is continuous, satisfies $H(x,0) = x$ (identity on $D^2 \setminus \{0\}$),
 and $H(x,1) = x/\|x\| \in \partial D^2$ (retraction to the boundary), with $H(x
,t) = x$ for all $x \in \partial D^2$ and all $t$.

**Lifting to the quotient**: Under the quotient map $q: \overline{D^2} \to N_g$
that identifies boundary points according to the word $a_1^2 \cdots a_g^2$, the
boundary $\partial D^2$ maps onto the 1-skeleton $\bigvee_{i=1}^g S^1$. The radi
al retraction $H$ is compatible with the quotient identification (it acts radial
ly and the boundary identifications are respected), so it descends to a deformat
ion retraction
$$\widetilde{H}: X \times [0,1] \to X$$
of $X = N_g \setminus \{p\}$ onto the 1-skeleton $\bigvee_{i=1}^g S^1$.

Concretely:
- On the 1-skeleton (the image of $\partial D^2$), $\widetilde{H}$ is the identi
ty for all $t$.
- On the punctured 2-cell $e^2 \setminus \{p\}$, $\widetilde{H}$ radially pushes
 every point toward the boundary, which under $q$ lands in the 1-skeleton.

This is continuous (it is continuous on the closed 2-cell minus the point and co
nstant on the identified boundary), fixes the 1-skeleton pointwise, and retracts
 $X$ onto it. Hence $\widetilde{H}$ is a deformation retraction.

## Verification via Invariants

**Euler characteristic**: $\chi(N_g) = 2 - g$, so $\chi(X) = \chi(N_g) - 1 = 1 -
 g$. A wedge of $g$ circles has $\chi = 1 - g$. ✓

**Fundamental group**: $\pi_1(N_g) = \langle a_1, \ldots, a_g \mid a_1^2 a_2^2 \
cdots a_g^2 = 1\rangle$. Removing a point from the interior of the 2-cell remove
s the single relation (the attaching map of the 2-cell), leaving $\pi_1(X) = F_g
$, the free group on $g$ generators—exactly $\pi_1(\bigvee_{i=1}^g S^1)$. ✓

**Homology**: $H_1(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}^g$ (free abelian of rank $g$), matching $
H_1(\bigvee_{i=1}^g S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}^g$. ✓

## Conclusion

**Yes**, the punctured non-orientable surface $X = N_g \setminus \{p\}$ deformat
ion retracts to a wedge of $g$ circles. The deformation retraction is the radial
 retraction of the punctured 2-cell onto its boundary (the 1-skeleton), which is
 a wedge of $g$ circles in the standard CW decomposition of $N_g$ from the $2g$-
gon with boundary word $a_1^2 a_2^2 \cdots a_g^2$.
