## Proof

Let $G = \{g_1, \dots, g_n\}$ be an abelian group of order $n$, and let $\wideha
t{G} = \{\chi_1, \dots, \chi_n\}$ be its set of irreducible characters. Since $G
$ is abelian, every irreducible character has degree $1$, and the number of irre
ducible characters equals $|G| = n$.

The character table of $G$ is the $n \times n$ matrix
$$X = \bigl(\chi_i(g_j)\bigr)_{1 \le i,j \le n},$$
whose rows are indexed by irreducible characters and columns by group elements.

**Column orthogonality.** For any $g, h \in G$, the column orthogonality relatio
ns give
$$\sum_{\chi \in \widehat{G}} \chi(g)\,\overline{\chi(h)} = n\,\delta_{g,h}.$$

In matrix terms this says
$$X\,X^{*} = n\,I_n,$$
where $X^{*} = \overline{X}^{\mathsf{T}}$ is the conjugate transpose.

**Taking determinants.** From $X X^{*} = n I_n$,
$$\det(X)\,\det(X^{*}) = \det(n I_n) = n^{n}.$$
Since $\det(X^{*}) = \overline{\det(X)}$, the left-hand side is
$$\det(X)\,\overline{\det(X)} = |\det X|^{2}.$$
Hence
$$|\det X|^{2} = n^{n},$$
and taking square roots (noting $|\det X| \ge 0$),
$$|\det X| = n^{\,n/2}.$$

This value is in fact attained (e.g. the character table of a cyclic group is a
Vandermonde matrix in primitive $n$-th roots of unity, whose determinant has mod
ulus exactly $n^{n/2}$), so the modulus is precisely

$$\boxed{\,n^{\,n/2}\,}.$$
