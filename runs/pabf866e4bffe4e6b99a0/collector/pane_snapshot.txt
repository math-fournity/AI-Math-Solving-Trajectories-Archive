## Solution

**Setup.** The hyperbola is $xy = c^2$. Parametrize the tangent point as $P = (c
t,\, c/t)$, where $t \neq 0$.

**The tangent to the hyperbola at $P$.** Differentiating $xy=c^2$ gives $y' = -y
/x = -1/t^2$, so the tangent line is
$$x + t^2 y = 2ct.$$

**The circle through the origin, tangent to the hyperbola at $P$.** A circle thr
ough the origin has the form
$$x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey = 0.$$

*Passing through $P$:* $\;c^2t^2 + c^2/t^2 + Dct + Ec/t = 0$.　(1)

*Tangency at $P$:* the tangent to the circle at $P$,
$$(2ct+D)\,x + (2c/t+E)\,y + Dct + Ec/t = 0,$$
must be proportional to $x + t^2 y - 2ct = 0$. Setting the proportionality const
ant $\lambda = 2ct+D$ and comparing the remaining two coefficients yields, after
 solving (1) simultaneously:
$$D = \frac{c(1-3t^4)}{2t^3}, \qquad E = \frac{c(t^4-3)}{2t}.$$

**Intersection with the hyperbola.** Substituting $y = c^2/x$ into the circle an
d multiplying by $x^2$:
$$x^4 + Dx^3 + Ec^2\,x + c^4 = 0.$$
Since $P$ is a tangent (double) point, $x = ct$ is a double root; let the other
two roots be $x_A, x_B$ (the $x$-coordinates of $A$ and $B$). Comparing coeffici
ents of $(x-ct)^2(x-x_A)(x-x_B)$ with the quartic:

- **Constant term:** $c^2t^2\, x_Ax_B = c^4 \;\Longrightarrow\; x_Ax_B = c^2/t^2
$.
- **$x^3$ coefficient:** $x_A+x_B = -D - 2ct = -\dfrac{c(t^4+1)}{2t^3}$.

**Equation of line $AB$.** With $A=(x_A, c^2/x_A)$, $B=(x_B, c^2/x_B)$, the slop
e is
$$\frac{c^2/x_B - c^2/x_A}{x_B - x_A} = -\frac{c^2}{x_Ax_B} = -t^2.$$
Using the symmetric value $S = t^2 x_A + c^2/x_A = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[t^2(x_A+x_B
) + c^2(x_A+x_B)/(x_Ax_B)\bigr] = -\dfrac{c(t^4+1)}{2t}$, the line is
$$t^2 x + y = -\frac{c(t^4+1)}{2t}, \qquad\text{i.e.,}\quad 2t^3 x + 2t\,y + c(t
^4+1) = 0.$$

**Foot of the perpendicular from the origin.** For $ax+by+d=0$ with $a=2t^3$, $b
=2t$, $d=c(t^4+1)$, the foot from $(0,0)$ is $\bigl(-ad/(a^2+b^2),\;-bd/(a^2+b^2
)\bigr)$. Since $a^2+b^2 = 4t^2(t^4+1)$:
$$x_0 = \frac{-2t^3\cdot c(t^4+1)}{4t^2(t^4+1)} = -\frac{ct}{2}, \qquad y_0 = \f
rac{-2t\cdot c(t^4+1)}{4t^2(t^4+1)} = -\frac{c}{2t}.$$

**The locus.** As $t$ ranges over all nonzero reals,
$$x_0\,y_0 = \left(-\frac{ct}{2}\right)\!\left(-\frac{c}{2t}\right) = \frac{c^2}
{4}.$$

The foot $(x_0,y_0)$ traces the hyperbola $xy = c^2/4$ (in the first and third q
uadrants, matching the sign pattern as $t$ varies over positive and negative val
ues).

$$\boxed{xy = \dfrac{c^2}{4}}$$
