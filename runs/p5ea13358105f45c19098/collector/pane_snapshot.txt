## Solution

We find the residue of $f(z) = \dfrac{z^2 e^{iz}}{(z^2+1)^2}$ at $z=i$.

**Step 1: Identify the pole.** Since $z^2+1=(z-i)(z+i)$, we have $(z^2+1)^2=(z-i
)^2(z+i)^2$. The factor $(z-i)^2$ gives a pole of order $2$ at $z=i$ (and $(z+i)
^2\neq 0$ there), so $z=i$ is a double pole.

**Step 2: Apply the residue formula for a pole of order $m=2$.**

$$\operatorname{Res}(f,i)=\lim_{z\to i}\frac{d}{dz}\Big[(z-i)^2 f(z)\Big]=\lim_{
z\to i}\frac{d}{dz}\left[\frac{z^2 e^{iz}}{(z+i)^2}\right].$$

Set $g(z)=\dfrac{z^2 e^{iz}}{(z+i)^2}=\dfrac{h(z)}{k(z)}$ with $h(z)=z^2 e^{iz}$
 and $k(z)=(z+i)^2$. Then $g'=\dfrac{h'k-hk'}{k^2}$.

**Step 3: Evaluate the pieces at $z=i$.**

- $k(i)=(2i)^2=-4$, $\;k'(z)=2(z+i)$, $\;k'(i)=4i$.
- $h(z)=z^2 e^{iz}$, $\;h'(z)=e^{iz}(2z+iz^2)$.
- $h(i)=i^2 e^{i\cdot i}=(-1)e^{-1}=-e^{-1}$.
- $h'(i)=e^{-1}\bigl(2i+i(i^2)\bigr)=e^{-1}(2i-i)=ie^{-1}$.

**Step 4: Compute $g'(i)$.**

$$g'(i)=\frac{h'(i)\,k(i)-h(i)\,k'(i)}{k(i)^2}=\frac{(ie^{-1})(-4)-(-e^{-1})(4i)
}{(-4)^2}=\frac{-4ie^{-1}+4ie^{-1}}{16}=\frac{0}{16}=0.$$

**Step 5: Verification via Laurent expansion.** Let $w=z-i$. Then $z=i+w$, $z^2=
-1+2iw+w^2$, $(z+i)^2=-4+4iw+w^2$, and $e^{iz}=e^{-1}e^{iw}=e^{-1}(1+iw-\tfrac{w
^2}{2}+\cdots)$. Writing $f=\frac{1}{w^2}\cdot\frac{z^2 e^{iz}}{(z+i)^2}$, the r
esidue is the coefficient of $w^1$ in $\frac{z^2 e^{iz}}{(z+i)^2}$.

Expanding the numerator: $z^2 e^{iz}=e^{-1}[-1+iw-\tfrac{w^2}{2}+\cdots]$, so $a
_0=-e^{-1},\;a_1=ie^{-1}$.

Expanding the reciprocal of the denominator: $\frac{1}{(z+i)^2}=\frac{-1/4}{1-iw

4}$.

The $w^1$ coefficient of the product is $a_0b_1+a_1b_0=(-e^{-1})(-\tfrac{i}{4})+
(ie^{-1})(-\tfrac14)=\tfrac{ie^{-1}}{4}-\tfrac{ie^{-1}}{4}=0$, confirming the re
