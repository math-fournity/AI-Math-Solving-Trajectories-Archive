## Proof

**Yes, such a function exists.**

### Construction

Without loss of generality (by translation and scaling, see the remark at the en
d), take $x_0 = 0$ and $R = 1$. Fix any $r$ with $0 < r < 1$.

Let
$$
\varphi(t) = \begin{cases} e^{-1/t}, & t > 0, \\ 0, & t \le 0. \end{cases}
$$
This is the standard smooth (non-analytic) function on $\mathbb{R}$: $\varphi \i
n C^\infty(\mathbb{R})$, $\varphi \ge 0$, and $\varphi(t) > 0$ exactly on $(0,\i
nfty)$.

Set $g(x) = \dfrac{1}{1-x}$, which is real-analytic on $(-\infty, 1)$ with Taylo
r series at $0$
$$
T(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^n,
$$
whose radius of convergence is $R = 1$.

Define
$$
h(x) = \varphi(x - r) + \varphi(-x - r).
$$
Then $h \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R})$, and:

- For $|x| \le r$: both $x - r \le 0$ and $-x - r \le 0$, so $h(x) = 0$.
- For $|x| > r$: at least one of $x - r$, $-x - r$ is positive, so $h(x) > 0$.

Now take $U = (-\infty, 1)$ (open, containing $(-1,1)$) and define
$$
f = g + h : U \to \mathbb{R}.
$$
Since $g$ is smooth on $U$ and $h$ is smooth on all of $\mathbb{R}$, $f \in C^\i
nfty(U)$.

### Taylor series of $f$ at $0$

Because $h \equiv 0$ on the open neighborhood $(-r, r)$ of $0$, every derivative
 satisfies $h^{(n)}(0) = 0$. Hence
$$
f^{(n)}(0) = g^{(n)}(0) \quad \text{for all } n \ge 0,
$$
so the Taylor series of $f$ at $0$ is exactly $T(x) = \sum_{n \ge 0} x^n$, with
radius of convergence $R = 1$.

### Agreement set

On the interval of convergence $(-1, 1)$, the Taylor series sums to $g(x)$. Ther
efore
$$
f(x) = T(x) \iff h(x) = 0 \iff |x| \le r.
$$
Consequently, on $(-1,1)$:

- $f = T$ on $[-r, r] \supseteq (-r, r)$;
- $f \neq T$ at every point of $(-1, -r) \cup (r, 1)$.

In particular, the **maximal open interval centered at $0$** on which $f$ equals
 its Taylor series is exactly $(-r, r)$, with $0 < r < 1 = R$. The function agre
es with its Taylor series on a proper subinterval of the convergence interval $(
-R, R)$, and disagrees with it everywhere else inside $(-R, R)$.

### General $x_0$ and $R$

For arbitrary $x_0$ and $R > 0$, replace $g$ by $\tilde g(x) = \dfrac{1}{1 - (x
- x_0)/R}$ (Taylor series $\sum_{n \ge 0} \bigl(\tfrac{x - x_0}{R}\bigr)^n$, rad
ius $R$), choose any $r$ with $0 < r < R$, and set
$$
\tilde h(x) = \varphi\!\bigl(x - x_0 - r\bigr) + \varphi\!\bigl(x_0 - x - r\bigr
),
$$
which vanishes exactly on $\{x : |x - x_0| \le r\}$ and is positive for $|x - x_
0| > r$. Then $\tilde f = \tilde g + \tilde h$ on $U = (-\infty, x_0 + R)$ is th
e desired function.

### Conclusion

Such a smooth function exists: it is obtained by adding to a real-analytic funct
ion (whose Taylor series has the prescribed radius $R$) a smooth bump that vanis
hes on $[x_0 - r, x_0 + r]$ and is strictly positive immediately outside, within
 $(x_0 - R, x_0 + R)$. The bump is invisible to the Taylor expansion at $x_0$ (a
ll its derivatives vanish there) yet perturbs the function away from its Taylor
series outside the subinterval.
