## Proof

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a semisimple Lie algebra over $\mathbb{C}$ with Cartan sub
algebra $\mathfrak{h}$, root system $\Phi$, positive roots $\Phi^+$, simple root
s $\Delta$, and Weyl group $W$. Let $w_0 \in W$ be the longest element.

**Claim.** If $V$ is the irreducible representation of highest weight $\lambda$,
 then $V^*$ is irreducible with highest weight $-w_0(\lambda)$.

---

### Step 1. The weights of $V^*$

For any $\mathfrak{g}$-module $V$, the dual $V^*$ carries the contragredient act
ion:
$$(x \cdot f)(v) = -f(x \cdot v), \qquad x \in \mathfrak{g},\ f \in V^*,\ v \in
V.$$

For $h \in \mathfrak{h}$ and $v \in V_\mu$ (a weight vector of weight $\mu$), we
 have $h \cdot v = \mu(h) v$, so
$$(h \cdot f)(v) = -f(h \cdot v) = -\mu(h) f(v).$$
Thus if $f \in V_\mu^*$ (the dual of the $\mu$-weight space), then $h \cdot f =
(-\mu)(h) f$. Therefore:

$$\text{Weights}(V^*) = \{-\mu : \mu \in \text{Weights}(V)\}.$$

### Step 2. Irreducibility of $V^*$

Since $V$ is irreducible and finite-dimensional, $V^*$ is irreducible: any submo
dule $U \subseteq V^*$ gives $U^\perp \subseteq V$ a submodule, contradicting ir
reducibility of $V$.

### Step 3. Identifying the highest weight of $V^*$

The weights of $V$ lie in the convex hull of the Weyl orbit $W\lambda$, and the
partial order on weights is defined by $\mu \leq \nu \iff \nu - \mu \in \mathbb{
Z}_{\geq 0}\Phi^+$. The highest weight $\lambda$ is maximal: $\lambda \geq \mu$
for all weights $\mu$ of $V$.

Under negation, the order reverses in a controlled way. The key fact is that the
 longest element $w_0$ satisfies $w_0(\Phi^+) = \Phi^-$, so $w_0$ sends the posi
tive chamber to the negative chamber. Equivalently, for weights $\mu, \nu$:
$$\mu \leq \nu \iff w_0(\mu) \geq w_0(\nu).$$

Now consider the set of weights of $V^*$, which is $\{-\mu : \mu \in \text{Weigh
ts}(V)\}$. We seek the maximal element under $\leq$.

**Lemma.** $-w_0(\lambda)$ is a weight of $V^*$, and it dominates every weight o
f $V^*$.

*Proof.* Since $\lambda$ is a weight of $V$, $-\lambda$ is a weight of $V^*$. Ap
plying $w_0$: $w_0(-\lambda) = -w_0(\lambda)$ is in the Weyl orbit of $-\lambda$
, so $-w_0(\lambda)$ is a weight of $V^*$ (weights are $W$-invariant as a set, a
nd negation commutes with $W$-action).

Now let $-\mu$ be any weight of $V^*$ (so $\mu$ is a weight of $V$). Since $\mu
\leq \lambda$, applying $w_0$ reverses the order: $w_0(\mu) \geq w_0(\lambda)$,
i.e., $-w_0(\mu) \leq -w_0(\lambda)$. But $-\mu$ and $-w_0(\mu)$ are in the same
 $W$-orbit (related by $w_0$), and we need to compare $-\mu$ directly with $-w_0
(\lambda)$.

More carefully: the weights of $V^*$ form a $W$-invariant set. The weight $-w_0(
\lambda)$ is dominant (since $w_0(\lambda)$ is anti-dominant, being the image of
 the dominant $\lambda$ under $w_0$ which flips the chamber). Every weight $\nu$
 of $V^*$ satisfies $\nu \leq -w_0(\lambda)$ because:

- $\nu = -\mu$ for some weight $\mu$ of $V$ with $\mu \leq \lambda$.
- The highest weight of $V^*$ must be a dominant weight (every irreducible repre
sentation has a unique dominant highest weight).
- Among the dominant weights in $\text{Weights}(V^*)$, the maximal one is the hi
ghest weight.

Since $-w_0(\lambda)$ is dominant and is a weight of $V^*$, and the highest weig
ht of an irreducible module is the unique dominant weight that dominates all oth
ers, it suffices to show $-w_0(\lambda)$ dominates all weights of $V^*$.

For any weight $-\mu$ of $V^*$ (with $\mu$ a weight of $V$, $\mu \leq \lambda$):
 we have $\lambda - \mu \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\Phi^+$. Applying $w_0$: $w_0(\la
mbda) - w_0(\mu) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\Phi^- = -\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\Phi^+$. So
 $w_0(\mu) - w_0(\lambda) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\Phi^+$, meaning $w_0(\lambda)
\leq w_0(\mu)$, i.e., $-w_0(\mu) \leq -w_0(\lambda)$.

Now, $-\mu$ and $-w_0(\mu)$ are in the same Weyl orbit, so they have the same do
minance properties relative to the dominant chamber. In particular, $-\mu \leq -
w_0(\lambda)$ because: the difference $(-w_0(\lambda)) - (-\mu) = \mu - w_0(\lam
bda)$. We know $w_0(\lambda) \leq \mu$ (from $w_0(\lambda) \leq w_0(\mu)$ and $w
_0(\mu)$ is in the orbit of $\mu$... ).

Let me use the cleanest standard argument. The set of weights of $V^*$ equals $\
{-\mu : \mu \in \text{Weights}(V)\}$. The highest weight of an irreducible modul
e is the unique weight that is dominant and greater than or equal to all other w
eights.

**$-w_0(\lambda)$ is dominant:** $\lambda$ is dominant, so $\langle \lambda, \al
pha^\vee \rangle \geq 0$ for all $\alpha \in \Phi^+$. Then $\langle -w_0(\lambda
), \alpha^\vee \rangle = -\langle \lambda, w_0^{-1}(\alpha^\vee) \rangle = -\lan
gle \lambda, (w_0\alpha)^\vee \rangle$. Since $w_0(\Phi^+) = \Phi^-$, $w_0\alpha
 \in \Phi^-$, so $(w_0\alpha)^\vee \in \Phi^{-\vee}$, and $\langle \lambda, (w_0
\alpha)^\vee \rangle \leq 0$. Thus $\langle -w_0(\lambda), \alpha^\vee \rangle \
geq 0$. ✓

**$-w_0(\lambda)$ dominates all weights of $V^*$:** Take any weight $-\mu$ of $V
^*$, where $\mu$ is a weight of $V$. Since $\mu \leq \lambda$, write $\lambda -
\mu = \sum n_i \alpha_i$ with $n_i \geq 0$, $\alpha_i \in \Delta$. Then:
$$(-w_0(\lambda)) - (-\mu) = \mu - w_0(\lambda) = \mu - \lambda + \lambda - w_0(
\lambda).$$
Hmm, let me do this directly. $\lambda - \mu = \sum n_i \alpha_i$, $n_i \geq 0$.
 Apply $w_0$:
$$w_0(\lambda) - w_0(\mu) = \sum n_i \, w_0(\alpha_i).$$
Since $w_0(\alpha_i) \in -\Phi^+$ (each $w_0(\alpha_i)$ is a negative root), wri
te $w_0(\alpha_i) = -\beta_i$ with $\beta_i \in \Phi^+$. Then:
$$w_0(\lambda) - w_0(\mu) = -\sum n_i \beta_i, \quad \text{so} \quad w_0(\mu) -
w_0(\lambda) = \sum n_i \beta_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\Phi^+.$$
This gives $w_0(\lambda) \leq w_0(\mu)$, i.e., $-w_0(\mu) \leq -w_0(\lambda)$.

Now I need $-\mu \leq -w_0(\lambda)$, not just $-w_0(\mu) \leq -w_0(\lambda)$. B
ut $-\mu$ and $-w_0(\mu)$ are in the same Weyl orbit. The issue is whether $-\mu
 \leq -w_0(\lambda)$ holds for *every* weight $-\mu$.

The resolution: the highest weight of $V^*$ is the unique dominant weight that i
s $\geq$ all weights. We've shown $-w_0(\lambda)$ is dominant and is a weight of
 $V^*$. For any weight $\nu$ of $V^*$, there exists $w \in W$ with $w(\nu)$ domi
nant (in fact $w(\nu)$ is the highest weight, since the highest weight is the un
ique dominant weight in the "upper" set). Actually, the standard fact is:

> The highest weight of an irreducible representation is the unique **dominant**
 weight, and it is $\geq$ every weight.

So I just need: $-w_0(\lambda)$ is dominant (shown ✓), $-w_0(\lambda)$ is a weig
ht of $V^*$ (shown ✓), and $-w_0(\lambda) \geq \nu$ for every weight $\nu$ of $V
^*$.

For the last point: every weight $\nu$ of $V^*$ satisfies $\nu \leq \nu^+$ where
 $\nu^+$ is the dominant weight in the Weyl orbit of $\nu$ (this is a standard f
act: the dominant representative is the largest in the orbit). And $\nu^+$ is a
weight of $V^*$ (weights are $W$-invariant as a set). So the highest weight of $
V^*$ is the maximum over dominant weights of $V^*$.

The dominant weights of $V^*$ are $\{-\mu : \mu \in \text{Weights}(V), -\mu \tex
t{ dominant}\}$. Now $-\mu$ dominant $\iff$ $\mu$ anti-dominant $\iff$ $\mu = w_
0(\rho)$ for some dominant $\rho$ (specifically $\rho = -w_0(\mu)$ dominant). Th
e dominant weights of $V^*$ are exactly $\{-w_0(\rho) : \rho \text{ dominant wei
ght of } V\}$.

Among dominant weights $\rho$ of $V$, the maximum is $\lambda$ (since $\lambda$
is the highest weight of $V$). Since $-w_0$ is order-reversing on dominant weigh
ts (it maps the dominant chamber to itself, being $-\circ\, w_0$ which sends $\P
hi^+$ to $\Phi^+$), the maximum of $\{-w_0(\rho)\}$ over dominant $\rho \leq \la
mbda$ is $-w_0(\lambda)$.

Therefore $-w_0(\lambda)$ is the highest weight of $V^*$.

---

### Conclusion

The highest weight of the dual representation $V^*$ is $\boxed{-w_0(\lambda)}$,
where $w_0$ is the longest element of the Weyl group.

**Special cases for intuition:**
- If $w_0 = -\mathrm{id}$ (e.g., type $A_1$, $B_n$, $C_n$, $D_{2k}$, $E_7$, $E_8
$, $F_4$, $G_2$), then $-w_0(\lambda) = \lambda$, so $V^* \cong V$ (self-dual).
- For type $A_n$ ($n \geq 2$), $w_0(\lambda) = -\lambda^*$ where $\lambda^*$ is
the "reversed" weight, so the dual has highest weight $\lambda^*$ (corresponding
 to the conjugate partition / transposed Young diagram).
