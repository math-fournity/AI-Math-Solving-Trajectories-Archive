## Proof

**The statement is TRUE.**

**Setup and notation.** Strong convergence $z_n \to z$ in $C_0([0,T]; W^{1,\inft
y}(\Omega))$ means
$$
\|z_n - z\|_{C([0,T];\, W^{1,\infty}(\Omega))} = \sup_{t \in [0,T]} \|z_n(t) - z
(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)} \longrightarrow 0.
$$
Since $W^{1,\infty}(\Omega) \hookrightarrow L^\infty(\Omega)$ continuously, this
 immediately implies
$$
\|z_n - z\|_{L^\infty(\Omega \times (0,T))} \le \|z_n - z\|_{C([0,T];\, W^{1,\in
fty}(\Omega))} \to 0. \tag{1}
$$

**Uniform boundedness.** From (1), the sequence $\{z_n\}$ is bounded in $L^\inft
y(\Omega \times (0,T))$: there exists $M > 0$ such that
$$
\|z_n\|_{L^\infty(\Omega \times (0,T))} \le M, \qquad \|z\|_{L^\infty(\Omega \ti
mes (0,T))} \le M \quad \text{for all } n. \tag{2}
$$

**Uniform continuity of $f$ on a compact interval.** The hypothesis $f \in C_0(\
mathbb{R})$ means $f$ is continuous and vanishes at infinity. In particular, $f$
 is continuous on the compact set $K := [-M-1,\, M+1]$, hence **uniformly contin
uous** on $K$. (Indeed, $f \in C_0(\mathbb{R})$ is uniformly continuous on all o
f $\mathbb{R}$, but uniform continuity on $K$ suffices here.)

**The approximation argument.** Let $\varepsilon > 0$. By uniform continuity of
$f$ on $K$, there exists $\delta \in (0, 1]$ such that for all $a, b \in K$,
$$
|a - b| < \delta \implies |f(a) - f(b)| < \varepsilon. \tag{3}
$$
By (1), there exists $N$ such that for all $n \ge N$,
$$
\|z_n - z\|_{L^\infty(\Omega \times (0,T))} < \delta. \tag{4}
$$
For a.e. $(x, t) \in \Omega \times (0,T)$ and $n \ge N$, by (2) and (4) we have
$$
z(x,t) \in [-M, M] \subset K, \qquad z_n(x,t) \in [-M-1, M+1] \subset K, \qquad
|z_n(x,t) - z(x,t)| < \delta.
$$
(Here we used $\delta \le 1$ together with the bound (2) to keep $z_n(x,t)$ insi
de $K$.) Applying (3) with $a = z_n(x,t)$, $b = z(x,t)$,
$$
|f(z_n(x,t)) - f(z(x,t))| < \varepsilon \quad \text{for a.e. } (x,t) \in \Omega
\times (0,T), \quad n \ge N.
$$
Taking the essential supremum over $(x,t)$,
$$
\|f(z_n) - f(z)\|_{L^\infty(\Omega \times (0,T))} \le \varepsilon \quad \text{fo
r all } n \ge N.
$$

**Conclusion.** Since $\varepsilon > 0$ was arbitrary, we have shown
$$
\|f(z_n) - f(z)\|_{L^\infty(\Omega \times (0,T))} \to 0,
$$
i.e., $f(z_n) \to f(z)$ strongly in $L^\infty(\Omega \times (0,T))$.

The key ingredients are: (i) strong convergence in $C([0,T]; W^{1,\infty})$ impl
ies uniform $L^\infty$ convergence in space-time, giving a uniform bound on $\{z
_n\}$; (ii) $f \in C_0(\mathbb{R})$ is (uniformly) continuous on the relevant co
mpact range of values; (iii) uniform continuity plus uniform convergence of the
arguments yields uniform convergence of the composition. No regularity of $f$ be
yond continuity is needed — the $W^{1,\infty}$ structure is only used to extract
 the $L^\infty$ convergence and uniform boundedness.
