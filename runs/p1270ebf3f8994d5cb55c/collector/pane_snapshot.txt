### Setup

Let $g=\gcd(a,b)$, $a=gx$, $b=gy$ with $\gcd(x,y)=1$.

**Condition 2** ($a^2/b+b^2/a\in\mathbb Z$):
$$\frac{a^2}{b}+\frac{b^2}{a}=g\cdot\frac{x^3+y^3}{xy}.$$
Since $\gcd(x,y)=1$, any prime dividing $xy$ divides exactly one of $x^3,y^3$, h
ence cannot divide $x^3+y^3$. So $\gcd(xy,x^3+y^3)=1$, forcing $xy\mid g$. Write
 $g=kxy$, giving
$$a=kx^2y,\qquad b=kxy^2,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.$$

**Condition 1** becomes
$$\frac{4a+p}{b}+\frac{4b+p}{a}=\frac{4kxy(x^2+y^2)+p(x+y)}{kx^2y^2}\in\mathbb Z
.$$
So $kx^2y^2\mid 4kxy(x^2+y^2)+p(x+y)$.

### Restricting $(x,y)$

Reducing the divisibility modulo $x$: the first term is divisible by $x$, so $x\
mid p(x+y)$, i.e. $x\mid py$. Since $\gcd(x,y)=1$, $x\mid p$. As $p$ is prime, $
x\in\{1,p\}$. Symmetrically $y\in\{1,p\}$. With $\gcd(x,y)=1$, the only options
are
$$(x,y)\in\{(1,1),(1,p),(p,1)\}.$$

### Case $(x,y)=(1,1)$: $a=b=k$

Condition 1: $8+\tfrac{2p}{k}\in\mathbb Z\iff k\mid 2p$.

- $p=3$: $k\in\{1,2,3,6\}$, $a+b=2k\in\{2,4,6,12\}$, sum $=24$.
- $p=5$: $k\in\{1,2,5,10\}$, $a+b=2k\in\{2,4,10,20\}$, sum $=36$.

### Case $(x,y)=(1,p)$: $a=kp,\ b=kp^2$

The divisibility reduces to $kp\mid 4k+p+1$, i.e. $k\mid p+1$ and $4k\equiv -1\p
mod p$.

- $p=3$: $k\mid 4$ and $k\equiv 2\pmod 3$ $\Rightarrow$ $k=2$. Pair $(6,18)$, $a
+b=24$.
- $p=5$: $k\mid 6$ and $k\equiv 1\pmod 5$ $\Rightarrow$ $k\in\{1,6\}$. Pairs $(5
,25),(30,150)$, $a+b=30,180$, sum $=210$.

### Case $(x,y)=(p,1)$: $a=kp^2,\ b=kp$

By symmetry (swapping $a,b$), identical $k$-values:
- $p=3$: $k=2$, pair $(18,6)$, $a+b=24$.
- $p=5$: $k\in\{1,6\}$, pairs $(25,5),(150,30)$, $a+b=30,180$, sum $=210$.
