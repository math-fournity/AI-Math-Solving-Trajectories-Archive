## Green's Function Representation for the Dirichlet Problem

**Problem.** Solve $-\Delta u = f$ in $\Omega$, with $u = h$ on $\partial\Omega$
.

### The Dirichlet Green's Function

Define $G(x,y)$ as the Dirichlet Green's function for $-\Delta$ on $\Omega$:

$$-\Delta_y G(x,y) = \delta(x - y), \quad x, y \in \Omega,$$
$$G(x,y) = 0, \quad y \in \partial\Omega.$$

$G$ is symmetric: $G(x,y) = G(y,x)$.

### Derivation via Green's Second Identity

Apply Green's second identity to $u(y)$ and $G(x,y)$ over $\Omega$ (in the $y$-v
ariable):

$$\int_\Omega \bigl[u(y)\,\Delta_y G(x,y) - G(x,y)\,\Delta u(y)\bigr]\,dy = \int
_{\partial\Omega}\biggl[u(y)\,\frac{\partial G}{\partial n_y}(x,y) - G(x,y)\,\fr
ac{\partial u}{\partial n}(y)\biggr]\,dS(y).$$

**Left-hand side.** Using $\Delta_y G(x,y) = -\delta(x-y)$ and $\Delta u = -f$:

$$\int_\Omega \bigl[u(y)(-\delta(x-y)) - G(x,y)(-f(y))\bigr]\,dy = -u(x) + \int_
\Omega G(x,y)\,f(y)\,dy.$$

**Right-hand side.** On $\partial\Omega$ we have $u = h$ and $G(x,y) = 0$, so:

$$\int_{\partial\Omega} h(y)\,\frac{\partial G}{\partial n_y}(x,y)\,dS(y).$$

Equating both sides:

$$-u(x) + \int_\Omega G(x,y)\,f(y)\,dy = \int_{\partial\Omega} h(y)\,\frac{\part
ial G}{\partial n_y}(x,y)\,dS(y).$$

### The Representation Formula

Solving for $u(x)$:

$$\boxed{\displaystyle u(x) = \int_\Omega G(x,y)\,f(y)\,dy \;-\; \int_{\partial\
Omega} h(y)\,\frac{\partial G}{\partial n_y}(x,y)\,dS(y)}$$

### Interpretation of the Two Terms

- **Volume (source) term** $\displaystyle\int_\Omega G(x,y)\,f(y)\,dy$: the cont
ribution of the interior source $f$, weighted by the Green's function. This is t
he particular solution to $-\Delta u = f$ with homogeneous boundary data.

- **Boundary term** $\displaystyle -\int_{\partial\Omega} h(y)\,\frac{\partial G
}{\partial n_y}(x,y)\,dS(y)$: the contribution of the non-homogeneous boundary d
ata $h$. The quantity $-\partial G/\partial n_y$ is the **Poisson kernel** $K(x,
y)$ for the domain, so this term equals $\int_{\partial\Omega} K(x,y)\,h(y)\,dS(
y)$, which is the harmonic extension of $h$ into $\Omega$.

The two terms superpose: the solution is the sum of the particular solution driv
en by $f$ (with zero boundary) and the harmonic function matching $h$ on the bou
ndary (with zero source).
