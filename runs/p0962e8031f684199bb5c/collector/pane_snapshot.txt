## Solution

**Finding the transformation.** A Möbius transformation fixing the origin has th
e form
$$w(z) = \frac{az}{cz+d}, \qquad (b=0 \text{ since } w(0)=0).$$

**Intersection points.** The circle $|z-1|=1$ meets $\operatorname{Im}(z)=\pm 1$
 where $(x-1)^2+y^2=1$ with $y=\pm1$, giving $(x-1)^2=0$, i.e. $z=1\pm i$. Simil
arly $|z+1|=1$ meets $\operatorname{Im}(z)=\pm1$ at $z=-1\pm i$. The natural cor
respondence is $1+i\mapsto -1+i$ and $1-i\mapsto -1-i$.

**Determining coefficients.** Impose $w(1+i)=-1+i$:
$$\frac{a(1+i)}{c(1+i)+d} = -1+i \implies a(1+i)=(-1+i)\big((c+d)+ci\big).$$
Expanding the right side:
$$(-1+i)\big((c+d)+ci\big)=-(c+d)-c+i\big((c+d)-c\big)=-(2c+d)+id.$$
Equating real and imaginary parts with $a+ai$:
$$a = -(2c+d), \qquad a = d.$$
Substituting $d=a$ into the first: $a=-(2c+a)\Rightarrow c=-a$. Hence
$$\boxed{w(z)=\frac{z}{1-z}}.$$

**Verification.**
- $w(0)=0$ ✓ (fixes origin).
- $w(1+i)=\frac{1+i}{-i}=-1+i$ ✓, $w(1-i)=\frac{1-i}{i}=-1-i$ ✓ (intersections m
ap correctly).
- The circle $|z-1|=1$ passes through $0$ and $2$; $w(0)=0$, $w(2)=\frac{2}{-1}=
-2$, both on $|w+1|=1$. The interior point $z=1$ maps to $w=\infty$, confirming
the **interior** of $|z-1|=1$ maps to the **exterior** of $|w+1|=1$ ✓.

**Image of $\operatorname{Re}(z)=\tfrac12$.** Parametrize $z=\tfrac12+iy$, $y\in
\mathbb{R}$. Then $1-z=\tfrac12-iy=\overline{z}$, so
$$w=\frac{z}{1-z}=\frac{z}{\overline{z}}, \qquad |w|=\frac{|z|}{|\overline z|}=1
.$$
Conversely, as $y$ ranges over $\mathbb{R}$, $w=\frac{1/2+iy}{1/2-iy}$ traces th
e entire unit circle (it is a continuous map $\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}\to S^1$ o
f degree 1). Therefore the image of the line $\operatorname{Re}(z)=\tfrac12$ is
the **unit circle**
$$\boxed{|w|=1}.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
