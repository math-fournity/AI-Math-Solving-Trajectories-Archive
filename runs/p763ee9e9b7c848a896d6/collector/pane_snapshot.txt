### Step 1: Closed form for the series

Since $\binom{k}{6}=0$ for $k<6$, substitute $j=k-6$:

$$d=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\binom{j+6}{6}}{10^{6j+42}}=\frac{1}{10^{42}}\sum_{
j=0}^{\infty}\binom{j+6}{6}\left(10^{-6}\right)^j.$$

Using the generating function $\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{j+n}{n}x^j
=\frac{1}{(1-x)^{n+1}}$ with $n=6$, $x=10^{-6}$:

$$d=\frac{1}{10^{42}}\cdot\frac{1}{(1-10^{-6})^7}=\frac{1}{10^{42}}\cdot\frac{10
^{42}}{(10^6-1)^7}=\frac{1}{999999^7}.$$

### Step 2: Factorization

$$999999 = 10^6-1 = 999\cdot 1001 = (3^3\cdot 37)(7\cdot 11\cdot 13)=3^3\cdot 7\
cdot 11\cdot 13\cdot 37.$$

So $999999^7 = 3^{21}\cdot 7^7\cdot 11^7\cdot 13^7\cdot 37^7$.

### Step 3: The period of $d$

iodic with period $T=\operatorname{ord}_{999999^7}(10)$.

By CRT, $T=\operatorname{lcm}$ of the orders modulo each prime power.
