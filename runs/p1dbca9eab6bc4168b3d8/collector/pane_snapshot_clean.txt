## Resolvent of $L_D = -\frac{d^2}{dx^2}$ on $L^2(0,1)$ with Dirichlet BCs

### Setup

We seek $(L_D - \lambda I)^{-1}g$, i.e., the solution $f$ of
$$
(L_D - \lambda I)f = g \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad -f''(x) - \lambda f(x) = g(
x), \qquad f(0)=0,\; f(1)=0.
$$
Equivalently $f'' + \lambda f = -g$. The resolvent exists precisely when $\lambd
a$ is **not** an eigenvalue of $L_D$. The eigenvalues are $\lambda_n = n^2\pi^2$
 ($n=1,2,\dots$) with eigenfunctions $\sin(n\pi x)$. So we require $\lambda \not
in \{n^2\pi^2\}$.

### Green's function construction

Set $k = \sqrt{\lambda}$ (any branch; the final formula depends only on $\lambda
$ through $\sin\sqrt\lambda$). For $\lambda \notin\{n^2\pi^2\}$ we have $\sin k
\neq 0$. The homogeneous equation $-f''-\lambda f=0$ has independent solutions $
\sin(kx)$, $\cos(kx)$.

Define the two fundamental solutions respecting the boundary conditions:
- **Left solution**: $u_L(x) = \sin(kx)$, satisfying $u_L(0)=0$.
- **Right solution**: $u_R(x) = \sin(k(1-x))$, satisfying $u_R(1)=0$.

The Green's function $G(x,\xi)$ satisfies $-G''-\lambda G = \delta(x-\xi)$, $G(0
,\xi)=G(1,\xi)=0$, is continuous at $x=\xi$, and has a derivative jump determine
d by the delta. For $x\neq\xi$ it solves the homogeneous equation, so
$$
G(x,\xi) = \begin{cases} A\,\sin(kx), & x < \xi,\\ C\,\sin(k(1-x)), & x > \xi. \
end{cases}
$$

**Continuity at $x=\xi$**: $A\sin(k\xi) = C\sin(k(1-\xi))$.

**Jump condition**: integrating $-G''-\lambda G=\delta$ across $x=\xi$ gives $G'
(\xi^-)-G'(\xi^+)=1$, i.e.
$$
Ak\cos(k\xi) + Ck\cos(k(1-\xi)) = 1.
$$

Substituting $C = A\sin(k\xi)/\sin(k(1-\xi))$:
$$
Ak\left[\cos(k\xi)+\frac{\sin(k\xi)\cos(k(1-\xi))}{\sin(k(1-\xi))}\right]
= Ak\,\frac{\cos(k\xi)\sin(k(1-\xi))+\sin(k\xi)\cos(k(1-\xi))}{\sin(k(1-\xi))}
= Ak\,\frac{\sin k}{\sin(k(1-\xi))}=1.
$$
Hence
$$
A = \frac{\sin(k(1-\xi))}{k\sin k}, \qquad C = \frac{\sin(k\xi)}{k\sin k}.
$$

### Result

$$
\boxed{\;G(x,\xi) = \frac{\sin\!\big(\sqrt{\lambda}\,x_<\big)\,\sin\!\big(\sqrt{
\lambda}\,(1-x_>)\big)}{\sqrt{\lambda}\,\sin\sqrt{\lambda}},\quad x_<=\min(x,\xi
),\; x_>=\max(x,\xi)\;}
$$

and the resolvent is the integral operator
$$
\boxed{\;\big[(L_D-\lambda I)^{-1}g\big](x)=\int_0^1 G(x,\xi)\,g(\xi)\,d\xi\;}
$$

i.e., written out explicitly,
$$
\big[(L_D-\lambda I)^{-1}g\big](x)=\frac{\sin(\sqrt{\lambda}(1-x))}{\sqrt{\lambd
a}\sin\sqrt{\lambda}}\int_0^x \sin(\sqrt{\lambda}\,\xi)\,g(\xi)\,d\xi \;+\; \fra
c{\sin(\sqrt{\lambda}\,x)}{\sqrt{\lambda}\sin\sqrt{\lambda}}\int_x^1 \sin(\sqrt{
\lambda}(1-\xi))\,g(\xi)\,d\xi.
$$

**Domain of validity**: $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}\setminus\{n^2\pi^2 : n=1,2,3,\do
ts\}$ (equivalently $\sin\sqrt{\lambda}\neq 0$).

### Verification

1. **Boundary conditions**: At $x=0$, the first integral vanishes (upper limit 0
) and $\sin(\sqrt\lambda\cdot 0)=0$, so $f(0)=0$. At $x=1$, $\sin(\sqrt\lambda(1
-1))=0$ and the second integral vanishes (lower limit 1), so $f(1)=0$. ✓

2. **Differential equation**: For $x\neq$ any $\xi$ in the support, differentiat
ing under the integral and using $-G''-\lambda G=0$ for $x\neq\xi$ together with
 the delta-source jump gives $-f''-\lambda f = g$. ✓

3. **Limit $\lambda\to 0$**: Using $\sin(kx)/k\to x$ and $\sin(k(1-\xi))/\sin k
\to (1-\xi)$, we get $G(x,\xi)\to x_<(1-x_>)$, the well-known Green's function o
f $-d^2/dx^2$ at $\lambda=0$. ✓

4. **Poles at eigenvalues**: $\sin\sqrt\lambda=0$ exactly at $\lambda=n^2\pi^2$,
 matching the spectrum of $L_D$, confirming the resolvent is meromorphic with si
mple poles at the eigenvalues. ✓
