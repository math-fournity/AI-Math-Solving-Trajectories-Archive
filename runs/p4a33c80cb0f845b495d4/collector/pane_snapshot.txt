## Proof

**Step 1: Identify the natural tiling of the torus.**

The lattice $\Lambda = \langle (4,1), (1,-4) \rangle$ has determinant $4(-4) - 1
(1) = -17$, so the torus has area 17. The integer lattice $\mathbb{Z}^2$ contain
s $\Lambda$ as a sublattice of index 17. The 17 unit squares $[i,i+1]\times[j,j+
1]$ for $(i,j) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$ tile $\mathbb{R}^2$, and modulo $\Lambda$ they
yield **17 unit squares tiling the torus**.

**Step 2: Identify the adjacency structure.**

The quotient $\mathbb{Z}^2/\Lambda \cong \mathbb{Z}_{17}$ via the map $\phi(a,b)
 = 4a + b \pmod{17}$ (since $\phi(4,1)=17\equiv 0$ and $\phi(1,-4)=0$). Label th
e 17 unit squares by elements of $\mathbb{Z}_{17}$.

Two unit squares are **adjacent** (share an edge or vertex) iff their positions
differ by one of $(\pm1,0), (0,\pm1), (\pm1,\pm1)$. Under $\phi$, these map to:
$$(1,0)\mapsto 4,\quad (0,1)\mapsto 1,\quad (1,1)\mapsto 5,\quad (1,-1)\mapsto 3
,$$
and their negatives. So squares $i$ and $j$ are adjacent iff $|i-j| \in S$ where
$$S = \{\pm 1, \pm 3, \pm 4, \pm 5\} = \{1,3,4,5,12,13,14,16\}.$$

The **non-adjacent** differences form $\bar{S} = \mathbb{Z}_{17}\setminus\{0\} \
setminus S = \{\pm 2, \pm 6, \pm 7, \pm 8\}$.

**Step 3: Count pairs.**

There are $\binom{17}{2} = 136$ pairs of tiles. Each tile has 8 neighbors, givin
g $17 \cdot 8/2 = 68$ adjacent pairs per tiling, and 68 non-adjacent pairs. Sinc
e $68 + 68 = 136$, for every pair to be adjacent in at least one of two tilings,
 the 68 adjacent pairs in tiling 2 must cover **all** 68 non-adjacent pairs from
 tiling 1. As both sets have exactly 68 elements, we need:

$$\text{adjacent pairs in tiling 2} = \text{non-adjacent pairs in tiling 1}.$$

**Step 4: Find the second tiling.**

We need a permutation $\sigma$ of $\mathbb{Z}_{17}$ such that $|i-j| \in \bar{S}
 \iff |\sigma(i)-\sigma(j)| \in S$, i.e., $\sigma$ is a graph isomorphism from t
he non-adjacency graph $\operatorname{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{17}, \bar{S})$ to the adj
acency graph $\operatorname{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{17}, S)$.

For prime-order cyclic groups (by Muzychuk's proof of Ádám's conjecture), two ci
rculant graphs are isomorphic iff their connection sets are related by a multipl
icative unit. So we need $u \in \mathbb{Z}_{17}^*$ with $u \cdot S = \bar{S}$.

**Try $u = 2$:**
$$2 \cdot S = \{2, 6, 8, 10, 24, 26, 28, 32\} \equiv \{2, 6, 8, 10, 7, 9, 11, 15
\} \pmod{17}.$$
$$\bar{S} = \{2, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 15\}.$$
$$2 \cdot S = \bar{S}. \quad \checkmark$$

**Step 5: Construct the two tilings.**

- **Pattern 1:** Place tile $i$ at position $i \in \mathbb{Z}_{17}$.
- **Pattern 2:** Place tile $i$ at position $2i \pmod{17}$.

These are distinct (since $2i \not\equiv i$ for $i \neq 0$).

**Verification:** In Pattern 1, tiles $i, j$ are adjacent iff $|i-j| \in S$. In
Pattern 2, tiles $i, j$ are adjacent iff $|2i - 2j| \in S$, i.e., $2|i-j| \in S$
, i.e., $|i-j| \in 2^{-1}S = 9S$. Computing:
$$9S = \{9, 27, 36, 45, 108, 117, 126, 144\} \equiv \{9, 10, 2, 11, 6, 15, 7, 8\
} = \bar{S} \pmod{17}.$$

So tiles $i, j$ are adjacent in Pattern 2 iff $|i-j| \in \bar{S}$.

Since $S \cup \bar{S} = \mathbb{Z}_{17}\setminus\{0\}$ and $S \cap \bar{S} = \em
ptyset$, every pair of distinct tiles is adjacent in **exactly one** of the two
patterns (hence in at least one). $\blacksquare$
