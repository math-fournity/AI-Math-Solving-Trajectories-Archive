**Answer: The minimum is $1$.**

## Proof

**Lower bound.** The identity isometry of $\mathbb{R}^3$ preserves every embedde
d surface, so the isometry group always contains at least $\{\mathrm{id}\}$. Thu
s the size is $\geq 1$.

**Upper bound (construction achieving size $1$).** We show there exists a smooth
 embedding of a genus $g \geq 2$ surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$ whose ambient isometr
y group is trivial.

**Step 1: The isometry group is finite.** Let $S \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ be a smoo
thly embedded compact surface. Its ambient isometry group $G = \{\varphi \in E(3
) : \varphi(S) = S\}$ is a closed subgroup of the Euclidean group $E(3)$. Since
$S$ is compact, $G$ is compact. Every compact subgroup of $E(3)$ fixes a point (
the centroid of $S$), so $G$ is conjugate to a subgroup of $O(3)$.

If $G$ contained a continuous subgroup, it would contain a copy of $S^1 = SO(2)$
 (rotations about some axis), making $S$ a surface of revolution. A connected su
rface of revolution in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is generated by rotating a plane curve abo
ut an axis; topologically this yields either a sphere (genus $0$, if the curve m
eets the axis) or a torus (genus $1$, if the curve is a simple closed curve avoi
ding the axis). It cannot produce genus $\geq 2$. Likewise, $SO(3)$-symmetry for
ces $S$ to be a round sphere (genus $0$). Since $g \geq 2$, $G$ must be **finite
**.

**Step 2: Bounding the possible finite groups.** By the Riemann–Hurwitz formula
applied to the covering $S \to S/G^+$ (where $G^+ = G \cap SO(3)$ is the orienta
tion-preserving subgroup), Hurwitz's theorem gives $|G^+| \leq 84(g-1)$. Hence $
|G| \leq 168(g-1)$, so only finitely many finite subgroups of $O(3)$ (up to conj
ugacy) can arise as the isometry group of a genus $g$ surface. Denote these cand
idate subgroups $H_1, \ldots, H_N$ (all nontrivial; we exclude the trivial group
).

**Step 3: Generic embeddings have trivial symmetry.** Consider the space $\mathc
al{E}$ of smooth embeddings of a genus $g$ surface into $\mathbb{R}^3$, equipped
 with the $C^\infty$ Whitney topology (a Baire space). For each nontrivial finit
e subgroup $H \subset O(3)$, define:
$$\mathcal{F}_H = \{f \in \mathcal{E} : H \text{ preserves } f(S)\}.$$
This is the set of $H$-equivariant embeddings, satisfying $f(h \cdot p) = h \cdo
t f(p)$ for all $h \in H$, $p \in S$. This is a **proper closed** subset of $\ma
thcal{E}$: it is closed (defined by continuous equations) and proper (a generic
perturbation of any $H$-symmetric embedding breaks the symmetry, since the equiv
ariance constraints have positive codimension — the constraint $f \circ h = h \c
irc f$ for a nontrivial $h$ removes degrees of freedom).

Since there are only finitely many candidate subgroups $H_1, \ldots, H_N$, by Ba
ire's theorem:
$$\mathcal{E} \setminus \bigcup_{i=1}^{N} \mathcal{F}_{H_i}$$
is **dense** (hence nonempty) in $\mathcal{E}$. Any embedding in this set has no
 nontrivial ambient isometry, so its isometry group is $\{\mathrm{id}\}$, of siz
e $1$.

**Conclusion.** The isometry group always has size $\geq 1$ (identity), and ther
e exist smooth embeddings of genus $g \geq 2$ surfaces in $\mathbb{R}^3$ with is
ometry group exactly $\{\mathrm{id}\}$. Therefore the minimum possible size is:

$$\boxed{1}$$
