## Solution

The resulting manifold $M'$ is $S^3$.

### Proof

**Setup.** We start with $M = T = S^1 \times D^2$, a solid torus. We remove $\op
eratorname{int}(A)$ where $A = S^1 \times D^2 = M$, leaving the boundary torus $
\partial M = S^1 \times S^1 = T^2$. We then glue in $B = D^2 \times S^1$ (also a
 solid torus) along $\partial B = \partial D^2 \times S^1 = S^1 \times S^1 = T^2
$.

**The gluing map.** The surgery described — removing $S^1 \times D^2$ and replac
ing with $D^2 \times S^1$ — is the standard Dehn surgery in which the two factor
 roles are exchanged. Concretely, the gluing map $\varphi: \partial B \to \parti
al A$ sends
$$\varphi: \partial D^2 \times S^1 \longrightarrow S^1 \times \partial D^2, \qqu
ad (p, q) \mapsto (q, p).$$
This swaps the **meridian** and **longitude**:
- In $A = S^1 \times D^2$: the meridian $\mu_A = \{\mathrm{pt}\} \times \partial
 D^2$ (bounds a disk in $A$) and the longitude $\lambda_A = S^1 \times \{\mathrm
{pt}\}$.
- In $B = D^2 \times S^1$: the meridian $\mu_B = \partial D^2 \times \{\mathrm{p
t}\}$ and the longitude $\lambda_B = \{\mathrm{pt}\} \times S^1$.

Under $\varphi$, we have $\varphi(\mu_B) = \lambda_A$ and $\varphi(\lambda_B) =
\mu_A$. The meridian of each solid torus is mapped to the longitude of the other
.

**Heegaard splitting of $S^3$.** It is a classical fact that $S^3$ admits a genu
s-1 Heegaard splitting:
$$S^3 = \big(S^1 \times D^2\big) \cup_{\varphi} \big(D^2 \times S^1\big),$$
where $\varphi$ is exactly the meridian–longitude swapping map above.

*Verification via the standard model.* View $S^3 = \{(z_1, z_2) \in \mathbb{C}^2
 : |z_1|^2 + |z_2|^2 = 1\}$. Decompose:
$$V_1 = \{(z_1,z_2) \in S^3 : |z_1| \le |z_2|\}, \qquad V_2 = \{(z_1,z_2) \in S^
3 : |z_1| \ge |z_2|\}.$$
- $V_1$: write $z_2 = e^{i\theta}\sqrt{|z_1|^2 + r^2}$ with $r = \sqrt{1-|z_1|^2
-|z_1|^2}\,$; more directly, fixing $\arg z_2$ and the ratio $|z_1|/|z_2| \in [0
,1]$ parameterizes $D^2$, while $\arg z_2$ gives $S^1$. So $V_1 \cong D^2 \times
 S^1$.
- $V_2$: symmetrically, $V_2 \cong S^1 \times D^2$.
- The common boundary $|z_1| = |z_2| = 1/\sqrt{2}$ is $T^2 = S^1 \times S^1$.

On this common boundary, the curve $\{|z_1| = \text{const},\, \arg z_2 \text{ va
ries}\}$ is a meridian of $V_1 \cong D^2 \times S^1$ but a longitude of $V_2 \co
ng S^1 \times D^2$, and vice versa. Hence the gluing swaps meridian and longitud
e — precisely $\varphi$.

**Conclusion.** The surgery removes $\operatorname{int}(S^1 \times D^2)$ and glu
es in $D^2 \times S^1$ via the meridian–longitude swap $\varphi$. This is exactl
y the genus-1 Heegaard splitting of $S^3$:

$$M' = \big(S^1 \times D^2\big) \cup_{\varphi} \big(D^2 \times S^1\big) \cong S^
3.$$
